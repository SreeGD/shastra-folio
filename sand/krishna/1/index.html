Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 1.1
IAST
sandarbha-trayeṇa
śrī-bhāgavataḥ
nirdharaṇāya
sandarbhaḥ
ārabhyate
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.1:

अथ पूर्वं सन्दर्भ-त्रयेण यस्य सर्व-परत्वं साधितम्, तस्य श्री-

भगवतो निर्धारणाय सन्दर्भो ऽयम् आरभ्यते.

atha pūrvaṃ sandarbha-trayeṇa yasya sarva-paratvaṃ sādhitam, tasya śrī-

bhagavato nirdhāraṇāya sandarbho 'yam ārabhyate.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

atha—now; pūrvam—previously; sandarbha-trayeṇa—by the three Sandarbhas; yasya—of whom; sarva-paratvam—the position as the ultimate phase of the absolute truth superior to everything and any other conception of God; sādhitam—conclusively demonstrated; tasya—of Him; śrī-bhāgavataḥ—the Personality of Godhead, full of all opulences and powers; nirdharaṇāya—for the specific description; sandarbhaḥ—essay; ayam—this; ārabhyate—is begun.

English translation and comments:

In the previous three sandarbhas the conception of God as the Supreme Person full of all powers and opulences is proved to be the ultimate phase of the Absolute Truth, superior to every other conception of God. This essay is written to give a specific description of that Supreme Personality of Godhead.

Sarva-samvādinī Comment atha iti nirdhāraṇam. bahuṣv ekasya nirṇayaḥ atha iti-beginning with the word atha; nirdhāraṇam-the word nirdharana; bahusu—among many; ekasya-of one; nirnayah- conclusion. By the word nirdhāraṇa it is intendied that Bhagavān is the ultimate feature of the Absolute Truth.

IAST
brahma-ādi

Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.3:

तद् एव ब्रह्मादि-त्रयं तस्य तृतीये विविच्यते. ब्रह्म त्व् इह

यत्रेमे सद्-असद्-रूपे

प्रतिशिद्धे स्व-संविदा

अविद्ययात्मनि कृते

इति तद् ब्रह्म-दर्शनम्

tad eva brahmādi-trayaṃ tasya tṛtīye vivicyate. brahma tv iha

yatreme sad-asad-rūpe

pratiśiddhe sva-saṃvidā

avidyayātmani kṛte

iti tad brahma-darśanam

[Word-by-word analysis:]

tat—that; eva—certainly; brahma-ādi—beginning with Brahman; trayam—three feature; tasya—of that; tṛtīye—in the Third Canto; vivicyate—is delineated; brahma—Brahman; tu—also; iha—here; yatra—whenever; ime—in all these; sat-asat—gross and subtle; rūpe-in the forms of pratiśiddhe—on being nullified; sva-saṃvidā-by self—realization; avidyayā—by ignorance; ātmani—in the self; krte—having been imposed; iti—thus; tat—that is; brahma-darśanam—the process of seeing the Absolute.

These three features of the Absolute are described in the Third Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. The following verse (1.3.33) describes the Lord's Brahman feature: [...] (translation not available)

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 1.4
IAST
iti-ādinā
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.4:

इत्य्-आदिना तत्र विविक्तम् अप्य् एकाकाराविर्भावतया

संशयाभावात् तत्-तन्-निर्धारणार्थम् तत्-तद्-वचनं

निद्ध्रियते.

ity-ādinā tatra viviktam apy ekākārāvirbhāvatayā

saṃśayābhāvāt tat-tan-nirdhāraṇārtham tat-tad-vacanaṃ

niddhriyate.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

iti-ādinā—by this and other verses; tatra—in this connection; viviktam—discerned; api—manifestation; saṃśaya— of doubt; abhāvāt—because of the absence; tat-tat—various; nirdharaṇa of discrimination; artham—for the purpose; tat-tat—various; vacanam—descriptions; na—not; uddhriyate—should be raised.

English translation and comments:

According to this verse and other descriptions in the Vedic literatures, the Brahman feature of the Absolute has only one unvariegated aspect, and therefore, without any doubt, it does not require an elaborate description in many words.

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 1.5
IAST
śrī-bhagavat
paramātmanoḥ
āvirbhāvatvāt
tani vacanāni
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.5:

श्री-भगवत्-परमात्मनोस् तु नानाविर्भावत्वात् तानि वचनानि तत्-तन्-

निर्धारणार्थम् उद्ध्रियन्ते.

śrī-bhagavat-paramātmanos tu nānāvirbhāvatvāt tāni vacanāni tat-tan-

nirdhāraṇārtham uddhriyante.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

śrī-bhagavat—of the all-opulent Personality of Godhead; paramātmanoḥ—and of His localized expansion as the all pervading Supersoul; tu—however; nānā—variegated; āvirbhāvatvāt—because of manifestation; tani vacanāni—many words; tat-tat—various; nirdharana—of discriminations; artham— for the purpose; uddhriyante—may be raised.

English translation and comments:

However, the Paramātmā and Bhagavān features of the Absolute manifest a great variety of qualitites, and therefore they require an elaborate description.

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 1.6
IAST
nirākāraḥ
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.6:

तत्रेश्वरो निराकारो नास्तीति प्राण् निर्णीतम्

tatreśvaro nirākāro nāstīti prāṇ nirṇītam

[Word-by-word analysis:]

tatra—inthis connection; īśvaraḥ—the Supreme Controller; nirākāraḥ—without form or qualitites; na—not; asti—is; prak—previously; nirṇītam—conclusively demonstated.

English translation and comments:

Here we may note that in the final conclusion the Supreme Controller is not without form and qualities. This has been proved in the earlier portion of this book (the previous Sandarbhas).

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 1.7
IAST
paramātmā-śabdena
sarva-antaryāmī-all
pratipāditaḥ
sandarbheṣu
tṛtīya-adhyāya
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.7:

परमात्मा-शब्देन च सर्वान्तर्यामि-पुरुषः प्रतिपादितस् तेश्व् एव

सन्दर्भेषु. तथा च सति तस्मिंस् तृतीयाध्यायारम्भे एवम् आभाष्यम्.

paramātmā-śabdena ca sarvāntaryāmi-puruṣaḥ pratipāditas teśv eva

sandarbheṣu. tathā ca sati tasmiṃs tṛtīyādhyāyārambhe evam ābhāṣyam.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

paramātmā-śabdena—by the word Paramātmā; ca—also; sarva-antaryāmī-all—pervading; purusah—Supreme Person; pratipāditaḥ—described; teṣu—in these; eva—certainly; sandarbheṣu—in the Sandarbhas; tathā—in the same way; ca— also; sati—being; tasmin—in this; tṛtīya-adhyāya—of the Third Chapter; ārambhe—in the endeavor; evam—in this way; abhāśyam—I described.

English translation and comments:

The Paramātmā, or all-pervading expansion of the Supreme Person present in everyone's heart and in every atom, I have already described in the Third Chapter (Paramātma-sandarbha) of this book.
