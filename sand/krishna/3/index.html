Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 1.16
IAST
sva-sṛjyānām
āśrayatvāt
parama-ātmā
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 1.16:

तद् एवम् यस् तद् रूपं जगृहे, स भगवान्. यत् तु तेन गृहीतम् तत् तु स्व-

सृज्यानाम् आश्रयत्वात् परमात्मेति पर्यवसितम्.

tad evam yas tad rūpaṃ jagṛhe, sa bhagavān. yat tu tena gṛhītam tat tu sva-

sṛjyānām āśrayatvāt paramātmeti paryavasitam.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

tat—that; evam—in this way; yaḥ—who; tat—that; rūpam— form; jagṛhe—accepted; saḥ—He; bhagavān—Bhagavān; yat— because; tu—indeed; tena—by Him; gṛhītam—accepted; tat— therefore; tu—certainly; sva-sṛjyānām—of that created by Him; āśrayatvāt—because of being the shelter; parama-ātmā— Paramātmā; iti—thus; paryavasitam—determined.

English translation and comments:

In conclusion, it is Bhagavān who accepts the form of the first puruṣa- incarnation (Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu) described in this verse (Text 9). When the Lord enters the universes He assumes the feature known as Paramātmā.

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 2.3
IAST
puruṣa-rūpasya
brahma-aṇḍam
śayanasya
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 2.3:

यस्य पुरुष-रूपस्य द्वितीयेन व्यूहेन ब्रह्माण्डं प्रविश्य अम्भसि

गर्भोदके शयानस्येत्य् आदि योज्यम्.

yasya puruṣa-rūpasya dvitīyena vyūhena brahmāṇḍaṃ praviśya ambhasi

garbhodake śayānasyety ādi yojyam.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

yasya—of whom; puruṣa-rūpasya—in the form of the puruṣa incarnation; dvitīyena in the second; vyūhena—expansion; brahma-aṇḍam—the material universe; praviśya—having entered; ambhasi—on the water; garbhodake—on the Garbhodaka ocean; śayanasya—resting; iti—thus; ādi—beginning; yojyam— suitable.

English translation and comments:

This verse describes how the second puruṣa-incarnation (Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu) enters the universes and lies down (śayānasya) on the Garbhodaka ocean (ambhasi).

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 2.4
IAST
tadṛśatvena
śayanasya
saṃsthanaiḥ
śrī-caraṇa
sanniveśaiḥ
viraṭ-ākāraḥ-the universal form prapañcah
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 2.4:

यस्य च तदृशत्वेन तत्र शयानस्य अवयव-संस्थानैः साक्षाच् छ्री-चरणादि-

सन्निवेशैर् लोकस्य विस्तारो विराड्-आकारः प्रपञ्चः कल्पितः.

yasya ca tadṛśatvena tatra śayānasya avayava-saṃsthānaiḥ sākṣāc chrī-caraṇādi-

sanniveśair lokasya vistāro virāḍ-ākāraḥ prapañcaḥ kalpitaḥ.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

yasya—of whom; ca—also; tadṛśatvena—by the state of being like this; tatra—there; śayanasya—resting; avayava— bodily expansions; saṃsthanaiḥ—situated in; sākṣāt—directly; śrī-caraṇa—of the lotus feet; ādi—and other parts of the Lord's transcendental form; sanniveśaiḥ—by contact; lokasya— of the material world; vistāraḥ—the expansion; viraṭ-ākāraḥ-the universal form prapañcah—the material cosmos; kalpitaḥ—imagined.

English translation and comments:

In these verses the words "śayānasya" (resting) and "avayava-saṃsthānaiḥ" (situated on the limbs of His transcendental form) show that the transcendental feet and other limbs of the Lord were spread throughout the material universe. Because of this entrance of the Lord into the universe it is sometimes considered that the entire universe is the body of the Lord (virāṭ-rūpa).

नवीनोपासकान् प्रति मनः-स्थैर्याय प्रख्यापितः, न तु वस्तुतस् तद् एव यस्य

Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha 2.6
IAST
candramāḥ
iti-thus ārabhya
hetu-bhūtaiḥ
Translation

Sanskrit text in Devanagari and Unicode transliteration of verse 2.6:

यद् वा, चन्द्रमा मनसो जातः इत्य् आरभ्य पद्भ्यां

भूमिर् दिशः श्रोत्रात् तथा लोकान् अकल्पयत् इति श्रुतेस् तैर् हेतु-भूतैर् लोक-विष्टरो

रचित इत्य् अर्थः.

yad vā, candramā manaso jātaḥ ity ārabhya padbhyāṃ

bhūmir diśaḥ śrotrāt tathā lokān akalpayat iti śrutes tair hetu-bhūtair loka-viṣṭaro

racita ity arthaḥ.

[Word-by-word analysis:]

yad vā-perhaps; candramāḥ—the moon; manasaḥ—from the mind; jātaḥ—born; iti-thus ārabhya—beginning; padbhyaṃ— with His two feet; bhūmiḥ—the earth; diśaḥ—the directions; śrotrāt—from the ear; tathā—in the same way; lokān—the varities of material manifestation; akalpayat—created; iti— thus; śruteḥ—from the Śruti śāstra (Rg Veda 10.90.13-14); taiḥ—by these; hetu-bhūtaiḥ—as the cause; loka—of the material world; vistāraḥ—the expanse; racitaḥ—created; iti— thus; arthaḥ—the meaning.

English translation and comments:

The conception of the Lord's Universal Form may also be understood in the following way: Because the different varieties of the material manifestation have emanated from the different limbs of the Lord's form, the universe may be considered a form of the Lord. This is confirmed in the Vedic hymns: "the moon is born from the mind of the Supreme Personality of Godhead." -Ṛg Veda 10.90.13 "The Supreme Personality of Godhead created the earth from His two lotus feet. He created the different directions from His sense of hearing. He created the different parts of the material world from the parts of His transcendental body." -Ṛg Veda 10.90.14
