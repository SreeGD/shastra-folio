Important Words
  • sarva-dvāreṣu — in all the gates
    Dvāra — gate. The body's gates are the senses (and according to commentators the nine apertures plus the mind). 'All' is the operative word: partial illumination is not the sign. Sattva either lights every gate or it is not yet sattva, only a partial taste.
  • prakāśaḥ — illumination
    Prakāśa is the diagnostic. Not pleasure, not productivity, not even peace alone — but light. The room becomes legible. Compare with tamas's aprakāśaḥ in verse 13: the same axis, opposite poles.
  • jñānam — knowledge
    Knowledge co-arises with illumination — not after it, not as its product, but as its very content. Sattva and jñāna are not two events; they are one. This is also why sattvic discipline tends to clarify rather than to merely calm.
  • vivṛddham — increased, developed
    Vi-vṛdh — fully grown. Sattva must mature; the early stages still leave gates unlit. The mature form, the full state, is what the verse describes. Aim for vivṛddha, not just vṛddha.
The manifestation of the mode of goodness can be experienced when all the gates of the body are illuminated by knowledge.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Diagnostic sign of dominant sattva: every gate of the body — eyes, ears, mind — opens onto clarity and right understanding. When perception is luminous and discrimination is alive, sattva is in command.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
First of three symptom-verses (14.11–13), one per guṇa. 'Sarva-dvāreṣu' alludes to the nine gates of the body (BG 5.13) — sense apertures as portals through which prakṛti-tinged cognition flows. Under sattva they transmit prakāśa (clear light); under tamas they will dim. The optative 'vidyāt' makes this a practical self-test for the sādhaka, not just a metaphysical claim.

Purport

There are nine gates in the body: two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, the mouth, the genitals and the anus. When every gate is illuminated by the symptoms of goodness, it should be understood that one has developed the mode of goodness. In the mode of goodness, one can see things in the right position, one can hear things in the right position, and one can taste things in the right position. One becomes cleansed inside and outside. In every gate there is development of the symptoms of happiness, and that is the position of goodness.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
11. When illumination in the form of knowledge arises in all gates of the body, know that sattva has increased.
Commentary

It has been stated that the prominent guṇa appears when the other two guṇas become weak of their own accord. Now the Lord speaks of the qualities of increased guṇas in three verses. When illumination or knowledge, real awareness of things through the sound of the Vedas, appears in all the doors such as the ears, one should understand there is an increase in sattva through such symptoms of knowledge. The word uta (also) indicates there will also be an appearance of happiness arising from the soul. When knowledge and happiness appear, one should understand there is an increase in sattva.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
11. When knowledge (and happiness as well) arise in all doors of knowledge, in all the senses, know that sattva has increased in the body.
Commentary

Three verses speak of the characteristics when each of the guṇas becomes prominent. When knowledge appears in the form of revealing sense objects such as sound as they actually are, through the knowledge gathering senses, such as the ear (jṇāna dvāreṣu), then, by this characteristic, knowledge, one should know there is an increase in sattva in the body. The word uta means “also.” One should know there is an increase in sattva also by the characteristic of happiness (tranquility).

(a) “All gates illuminated by knowledge” (14.11) – Implies

Perception by the senses based on knowledge, in the right position
Clean inside and out

(b) Darkness and inertia implies (14.13)

Not works according to regulative principles

(c) Illusion – Even though he has the capacity to work, he does not endeavor (14.13)

(a) Modes struggle to dominate each other

Personal Guidance
When sattva manifests, light and clarity pervade — knowledge develops; the gates of the body shine with illumination.
Practical Applications
  • Recognize the felt-sense of sattva: lightness, clarity, energy.
  • Cultivate conditions that reproduce that felt-sense.
  • Use it as a benchmark for self-evaluation.
Reflection Questions
  • When did I last feel sattvic clarity?
  • What conditions made it possible?
Analogy

A house with nine windows thrown open at dawn — every room floods with light at once. Sattva is the dawn coming through every gate of the body: eyes see clearly, ears hear truly, the mind tracks reality without distortion.

Stories
Vidura's gatehouse counsel

When Dhṛtarāṣṭra's tamasic indecision was poisoning the kingdom, Vidura — uncle, half-brother, advisor — sat in sattva and spoke the clearest counsel of the Mahābhārata. His words to Dhṛtarāṣṭra (preserved as the Vidura-nīti) are the chapter's verse made personal: a man in whom every gate of the body was illuminated, naming what every other gate-darkened man could not.

The palace was the same. The crisis was the same. Vidura's sattva made him capable of telling the truth that nobody else in that room could afford to see.
