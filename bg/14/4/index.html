It should be understood that all species of life, O son of Kuntī, are made possible by birth in this material nature, and that I am the seed-giving father.
Purport

In this verse it is clearly explained that the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is the original father of all living entities. The living entities are combinations of the material nature and the spiritual nature. Such living entities are seen not only on this planet but on every planet, even on the highest, where Brahmā is situated. Everywhere there are living entities; within the earth there are living entities, even within water and within fire. All these appearances are due to the mother, material nature, and Kṛṣṇa’s seed-giving process. The purport is that the material world is impregnated with living entities, who come out in various forms at the time of creation according to their past deeds.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
4. O son of Kuntī, prakṛti is the mother and I am the father of all forms that arise in all wombs in this world.
Commentary

Not only at the time of creation of the universe is prakṛti the mother and I the father, but at all times. Prakṛti, mahad brahma, is the place of birth (yoniḥ), the mother, of all those forms of moving and non-moving creatures which appear in all wombs from devas to grass, and I am the giver of the seed. I am the father, the giver of the seed, the giver of the jīvas.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
4. O son of Kuntī, prakṛti is the mother and I am the father of all forms that arise within all wombs in this world.
Commentary

O son of Kuntī! Of all the bodies that appear within the wombs of the creatures from Brahmā to non-moving beings, the pradhāna is the cause of arising; it is the mother. I, the Supreme Lord, am the father, who gives the jīvas, who supplies the heap of small conscious particles according to their karmas.

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
14.4 In all wombs such as those of gods, Gandharvas, Yaksas, Raksasas, men, animals, beasts, birds, serpents etc., whatever forms are generated, the brahman (Prakrti) is the 'great womb' or cause. Beginning from Mahat and ending with the five elements, Prakrti, with the mass of conscient selves imbedded by Me in it, is the cause. I am the sowing father. The meaning is that I am the imbedder of the multiplex of conscient selves according to each one's Karma. Now, He teaches the cause of continuing births as divinities etc., of those born in this manner at the beginning of a cycle of creation. It is due to the conjunction of these beings with Prakrti, in keeping with their old Karmas:
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
14.4 Sarvayonisu etc. In all the wombs (whatever gives birth to anything), the expansive Energy of the Bhagavat exists as the prime cause; and hence It is the Mother having the innate nature of giving birth to the entire world process. But I am the Father, the Energetic, the Inexplicable.

Personal Guidance
Whatever forms are produced in any species — the great material nature is their mother; I am the seed-giving father.
Practical Applications
  • Honor the cosmic parentage of all beings — material mother, spiritual father.
  • Treat every being as Kṛṣṇa's child.
  • Use this seeing in cross-species and cross-human encounters.
Reflection Questions
  • Whom do I struggle to see as Kṛṣṇa's child?
  • What does this verse ask of that struggle?
Analogy

A father plants a seed; the mother carries the form. Neither alone produces a child. Every species — ant, fish, sage, deva — is the child of this same primordial couple. We are not orphans of the cosmos; we are family of the Lord.

Stories
Kuntī recognizing the divine fatherhood

Kuntī, in her prayers in the first canto of the Bhāgavatam, addresses Kṛṣṇa as the father of all and the basis of all — not metaphorically, but with the verse-four meaning fully in hand. Her own sons had different deva-fathers, but she names Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate seed-giver behind them all.

She was not philosophizing. Having watched her children survive impossible odds, she was identifying who had actually been protecting whom. The verse becomes a daughter-in-law's bowed acknowledgement.

Important Words
  • sarva-yoniṣu — in all species of life
    Locative plural — covers every womb in every species, 8,400,000 of them according to tradition. The verse refuses to draw a line between human and non-human. The same parentage applies.
  • mūrtayaḥ — forms
    Mūrti — the embodied form. Every species' shape is a mūrti produced by this cosmic couple. The form is mother's contribution; the soul-spark inside it is the father's.
  • mahat yoniḥ — the great womb
    The chapter JSON keeps this as a single token. Mahat-prakṛti — the material totality of verse 3 — is named again as the womb of all forms. Kṛṣṇa is consistent across the two verses: matter is womb, not rival.
  • bīja-pradaḥ — the seed-giving
    Bīja-prada — literally seed-giver. The biological metaphor is now explicit, and Kṛṣṇa claims the role for Himself in first person (aham). The whole impersonalist reading of cosmic causation is closed off by this compound: there is a personal Father at the origin.
  • pitā — father
    Pitṛ — father. The Veda's tradition of cosmic parents — Dyaus-pitṛ, Pṛthivī-mātṛ — is collapsed and personalized: Kṛṣṇa Himself is the father. Not a deva intermediary; not an abstract principle. The word at the end of the verse is the most intimate one in the cosmic vocabulary.
