BG 14.1

The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Again I shall declare to you this supreme wisdom, the best of all knowledge, knowing which all the sages have attained the supreme perfection.

Essence. Again I shall declare to you the supreme wisdom — knowing which, all the sages have attained the supreme perfection.

Practical applications
  • Welcome repetition of the same teaching from different angles.
  • Honor the lineage of sages who attained perfection through this knowledge.
  • Trust the path others have walked.

BG 14.2

By becoming fixed in this knowledge, one can attain to the transcendental nature like My own. Thus established, one is not born at the time of creation or disturbed at the time of dissolution.

Essence. Becoming fixed in this knowledge, those who attain transcendental nature like My own — are not born even at creation, nor disturbed at dissolution.

Practical applications
  • Aim for the knowledge that places you beyond creation/dissolution cycles.
  • Trust that real knowledge has cosmic protective effect.
  • Don't reduce 'liberation' to inner peace alone.

BG 14.3

The total material substance, called Brahman, is the source of birth, and it is that Brahman that I impregnate, making possible the births of all living beings, O son of Bharata.

Essence. The total material substance, called Brahman, is the source of birth — I impregnate that Brahman; the birth of all beings becomes possible.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that even material existence requires Kṛṣṇa's impregnating contact.
  • Don't separate matter from divine causation.
  • Honor matter as Kṛṣṇa-touched.

BG 14.4

It should be understood that all species of life, O son of Kuntī, are made possible by birth in this material nature, and that I am the seed-giving father.

Essence. Whatever forms are produced in any species — the great material nature is their mother; I am the seed-giving father.

Practical applications
  • Honor the cosmic parentage of all beings — material mother, spiritual father.
  • Treat every being as Kṛṣṇa's child.
  • Use this seeing in cross-species and cross-human encounters.

BG 14.5

Material nature consists of three modes – goodness, passion and ignorance. When the eternal living entity comes in contact with nature, O mighty-armed Arjuna, he becomes conditioned by these modes.

Essence. Material nature consists of three modes — sattva, rajas, tamas — these bind the eternal soul to the body.

Practical applications
  • Identify which mode is currently dominant in you.
  • Recognize that all three bind, even sattva.
  • Aim for transcendence, not merely dominance of sattva.

BG 14.6

O sinless one, the mode of goodness, being purer than the others, is illuminating, and it frees one from all sinful reactions. Those situated in that mode become conditioned by a sense of happiness and knowledge.

Essence. Of these, sattva — being purest, illuminating, free from sin — binds by attachment to happiness and to knowledge.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that even attachment to happiness or knowledge binds.
  • Don't assume sattva is the goal; it's the steppingstone.
  • Watch the subtle attachments of the 'good' modes.

BG 14.7

The mode of passion is born of unlimited desires and longings, O son of Kuntī, and because of this the embodied living entity is bound to material fruitive actions.

Essence. Rajas — the mode of passion — born from desire, binds the soul to fruitive activity.

Practical applications
  • Recognize the rajasic pattern: desire → activity → more desire.
  • Notice the 'always doing something' compulsion as rajas.
  • Cultivate purposeful rest as discipline.

BG 14.8

O son of Bharata, know that the mode of darkness, born of ignorance, is the delusion of all embodied living entities. The results of this mode are madness, indolence and sleep, which bind the conditioned soul.

Essence. Tamas — born of ignorance — the delusion of all embodied beings; binds by madness, indolence, and sleep.

Practical applications
  • Recognize tamasic patterns: lethargy, escapism, willful blindness.
  • Don't justify tamas as 'rest'; it's not.
  • Use sattvic activity to break tamas.

BG 14.9

O son of Bharata, the mode of goodness conditions one to happiness; passion conditions one to fruitive action; and ignorance, covering one’s knowledge, binds one to madness.

Essence. Sattva binds to happiness, rajas to fruitive action, tamas to delusion — each mode's binding is specific.

Practical applications
  • Diagnose the specific binding of your dominant mode.
  • Apply the specific antidote.
  • Don't generalize; modes bind specifically.

BG 14.10

Sometimes the mode of goodness becomes prominent, defeating the modes of passion and ignorance, O son of Bharata. Sometimes the mode of passion defeats goodness and ignorance, and at other times ignorance defeats goodness and passion. In this way there is always competition for supremacy.

Essence. Sometimes one mode predominates and conquers the others — they alternate dominance over the soul.

Practical applications
  • Track mode-shifts through the day or week.
  • Notice which conditions promote which mode.
  • Adjust environment to favor sattva.

BG 14.11

The manifestation of the mode of goodness can be experienced when all the gates of the body are illuminated by knowledge.

Essence. When sattva manifests, light and clarity pervade — knowledge develops; the gates of the body shine with illumination.

Practical applications
  • Recognize the felt-sense of sattva: lightness, clarity, energy.
  • Cultivate conditions that reproduce that felt-sense.
  • Use it as a benchmark for self-evaluation.

BG 14.12

O chief of the Bhāratas, when there is an increase in the mode of passion the symptoms of great attachment, fruitive activity, intense endeavor, and uncontrollable desire and hankering develop.

Essence. When rajas increases — greed, restlessness, hankering, ambition, intense activity — these manifest.

Practical applications
  • Recognize the felt-sense of rajas: restlessness, hankering.
  • Don't romanticize rajas as 'productivity.'
  • Discipline rajas with regulated routine.

BG 14.13

When there is an increase in the mode of ignorance, O son of Kuru, darkness, inertia, madness and illusion are manifested.

Essence. When tamas predominates — darkness, inertia, madness, illusion — these manifest.

Practical applications
  • Recognize the felt-sense of tamas: heaviness, dullness, escape impulse.
  • Don't gaslight yourself ('I'm just tired').
  • Apply sattvic disciplines (early rising, light food, association) to break it.

BG 14.14

When one dies in the mode of goodness, he attains to the pure higher planets of the great sages.

Essence. If one dies in sattva, he attains the pure higher planets of the wise.

Practical applications
  • Cultivate sattva approaching the time of death.
  • Treat each evening's lying-down as practice.
  • Don't let tamas dominate the late hours.

BG 14.15

When one dies in the mode of passion, he takes birth among those engaged in fruitive activities; and when one dies in the mode of ignorance, he takes birth in the animal kingdom.

Essence. If one dies in rajas, he is born among those engaged in fruitive activity; in tamas, he takes birth in the animal kingdom.

Practical applications
  • Take seriously the consequences of dominant mode at death.
  • Use this verse for daily mode-vigilance.
  • Cultivate sattva or transcendence.

BG 14.16

The result of pious action is pure and is said to be in the mode of goodness. But action done in the mode of passion results in misery, and action performed in the mode of ignorance results in foolishness.

Essence. The result of pious action is pure, in the mode of goodness; rajas yields misery; tamas yields foolishness.

Practical applications
  • Trace recent results back to which mode produced them.
  • Recognize that not all results are equal — they reflect their origin.
  • Choose mode of action by anticipating its result.

BG 14.17

From the mode of goodness, real knowledge develops; from the mode of passion, greed develops; and from the mode of ignorance develop foolishness, madness and illusion.

Essence. From sattva, knowledge develops; from rajas, greed; from tamas, illusion, madness, ignorance.

Practical applications
  • Recognize knowledge, greed, illusion as products of specific modes.
  • Track which is most active in you.
  • Cultivate the mode that produces the desired fruit.

BG 14.18

Those situated in the mode of goodness gradually go upward to the higher planets; those in the mode of passion live on the earthly planets; and those in the abominable mode of ignorance go down to the hellish worlds.

Essence. Those in sattva go upward; those in rajas remain at human levels; those in tamas descend to lower forms.

Practical applications
  • Hold the trajectory implication of each mode.
  • Don't be casual about mode of life.
  • Cultivate the upward trajectory deliberately.

BG 14.19

When one properly sees that in all activities no other performer is at work than these modes of nature and he knows the Supreme Lord, who is transcendental to all these modes, he attains My spiritual nature.

Essence. When one sees that all activities are performed by the modes alone, and knows the Supreme above the modes — he attains My nature.

Practical applications
  • Cultivate the seeing: modes act, soul witnesses, Supreme presides.
  • Use this triple awareness in ordinary action.
  • Don't reduce to a single layer.

BG 14.20

When the embodied being is able to transcend these three modes associated with the material body, he can become free from birth, death, old age and their distresses and can enjoy nectar even in this life.

Essence. Transcending the three modes which bind the body, the soul, free from birth, death, old age, and misery — attains the Supreme.

Practical applications
  • Aim for transcendence, not just balance among the modes.
  • Cultivate the path that leads beyond.
  • Treat full liberation as the actual goal.

BG 14.21

Arjuna inquired: O my dear Lord, by which symptoms is one known who is transcendental to these three modes? What is his behavior? And how does he transcend the modes of nature?

Essence. Arjuna asks: by what symptoms is the soul transcendental to the modes known? How does he behave? How does he transcend?

Practical applications
  • Welcome the practical question: how to recognize the goal-state.
  • Don't just collect descriptions; ask for symptoms and behavior.
  • Bring this question to your guide.

BG 14.26

One who engages in full devotional service, unfailing in all circumstances, at once transcends the modes of material nature and thus comes to the level of Brahman.

Essence. He who serves Me with unalloyed devotion at all times — transcends the modes and qualifies for becoming Brahman.

Practical applications
  • Trust that bhakti itself transcends the modes.
  • Don't think you must master the modes before bhakti.
  • Cultivate unalloyed devotion as the most direct path.

BG 14.27

And I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman, which is immortal, imperishable and eternal and is the constitutional position of ultimate happiness.

Essence. I am the basis of impersonal Brahman, of immortality, of eternal religion, of unending happiness — I am the support of all.

Practical applications
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa as the basis even of Brahman.
  • Don't reduce Him to one stop on the cosmic ladder.
  • Approach Him as the support of all that you cherish.
