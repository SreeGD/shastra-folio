Chapter 9 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 9.1

The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: My dear Arjuna, because you are never envious of Me, I shall impart to you this most confidential knowledge and realization, knowing which you shall be relieved of the miseries of material existence.

Essence. Because you are not envious, I shall now declare to you the most confidential knowledge — knowing which, you shall be relieved of the miseries of material existence.

Practical applications
  • Cultivate non-envy as the qualification for receiving deepest teaching.
  • Notice envy as the opposing impulse and address it explicitly.
  • Don't expect confidential knowledge while envy festers.

BG 9.2

This knowledge is the king of education, the most secret of all secrets. It is the purest knowledge, and because it gives direct perception of the self by realization, it is the perfection of religion. It is everlasting, and it is joyfully performed.

Essence. This knowledge is the king of education, the most secret of secrets — the purest, perfect by direct experience, joyful, easy to perform.

Practical applications
  • Trust the description: easy to perform, joyful, perfect.
  • Don't be deceived by paths that promise difficulty as virtue.
  • Use the natural ease of bhakti as evidence of its rightness.

BG 9.3

Those who are not faithful in this devotional service cannot attain Me, O conqueror of enemies. Therefore they return to the path of birth and death in this material world.

Essence. Those without faith in this dharma do not attain Me — they return to repeated birth and death.

Practical applications
  • Recognize faith as the gateway, not as a peripheral virtue.
  • Treat unbelief seriously, not casually.
  • Cultivate faith as a discipline.

BG 9.4

By Me, in My unmanifested form, this entire universe is pervaded. All beings are in Me, but I am not in them.

Essence. By Me, in My unmanifested form, all this is pervaded — all beings are in Me, but I am not in them.

Practical applications
  • Hold the paradox: all beings in Him, He not contained by them.
  • Practice perceiving His pervading presence.
  • Don't reduce the paradox; live in it.

BG 9.5

And yet everything that is created does not rest in Me. Behold My mystic opulence! Although I am the maintainer of all living entities and although I am everywhere, I am not a part of this cosmic manifestation, for My Self is the very source of creation.

Essence. And yet beings do not rest in Me — behold My yogic mystery; My self maintains all beings, generates them, but is not in them.

Practical applications
  • Acknowledge the inconceivable nature of Kṛṣṇa's relation to creation.
  • Don't try to logic-out what is acintya (inconceivable).
  • Accept paradox as the right shape of cosmic truth.

BG 9.6

Understand that as the mighty wind, blowing everywhere, rests always in the sky, all created beings rest in Me.

Essence. As the great wind, blowing everywhere, always rests in space — so all beings rest in Me.

Practical applications
  • Use the wind/space analogy as your contemplation.
  • Practice resting your activity in the cosmic space of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Trust that your motion does not exhaust His stillness.

BG 9.7

O son of Kuntī, at the end of the millennium all material manifestations enter into My nature, and at the beginning of another millennium, by My potency, I create them again.

Essence. At the end of the millennium all beings enter My nature; at the beginning of the next, again I send them forth.

Practical applications
  • Hold cosmic cycles as the backdrop of your transient concerns.
  • Recognize that all comings and goings are Kṛṣṇa's breathing in and out.
  • Trust the rhythm.

BG 9.8

The whole cosmic order is under Me. Under My will it is automatically manifested again and again, and under My will it is annihilated at the end.

Essence. Through My nature, I create the entire cosmic manifestation again and again — they are helplessly bound by nature's modes.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that the world's rhythms are not random; Kṛṣṇa's nature operates them.
  • Stop fighting the rhythms; align with them.
  • Don't blame the world for being subject to nature's modes.

BG 9.9

O Dhanañjaya, all this work cannot bind Me. I am ever detached from all these material activities, seated as though neutral.

Essence. All these works do not bind Me — I am unattached, sitting as if neutral, while material nature acts.

Practical applications
  • Take Kṛṣṇa's neutrality-while-creating as the model for engaged-yet-free action.
  • Distinguish 'neutral' from 'indifferent' — He cares without binding.
  • Aim for that quality in your own work.

BG 9.10

This material nature, which is one of My energies, is working under My direction, O son of Kuntī, producing all moving and nonmoving beings. Under its rule this manifestation is created and annihilated again and again.

Essence. Material nature, working under My direction, produces the moving and unmoving — the world revolves by this principle.

Practical applications
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa's directorship behind apparent chaos.
  • Don't take the world to be running on its own.
  • Live with awareness of His underlying direction.

BG 9.11

Fools deride Me when I descend in the human form. They do not know My transcendental nature as the Supreme Lord of all that be.

Essence. Fools deride Me when I appear in human form — not knowing My transcendental nature as Lord of all.

Practical applications
  • Don't reduce Kṛṣṇa to His human appearance.
  • Honor the descent (avatāra) without diminishing the descender.
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa's human form as supremely transcendental.

BG 9.12

Those who are thus bewildered are attracted by demonic and atheistic views. In that deluded condition, their hopes for liberation, their fruitive activities, and their culture of knowledge are all defeated.

Essence. Such bewildered persons are attracted to demoniac and atheistic views — their hopes for liberation, their actions, their knowledge — all defeated.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that bewilderment defeats hope, action, and knowledge — all three.
  • Address bewilderment at the root.
  • Don't waste effort on superstructure when the root needs care.

BG 9.13

O son of Pṛthā, those who are not deluded, the great souls, are under the protection of the divine nature. They are fully engaged in devotional service because they know Me as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, original and inexhaustible.

Essence. But the great souls, under the protection of the divine nature, fully engage in devotional service knowing Me as inexhaustible and original.

Practical applications
  • Aspire to the protection of the divine nature, not just the divine.
  • Cultivate full engagement, not partial.
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa as inexhaustible source.

BG 9.14

Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination, bowing down before Me, these great souls perpetually worship Me with devotion.

Essence. Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination, bowing down before Me, constantly worship Me with devotion.

Practical applications
  • Adopt the explicit practices: chanting glories, determined endeavor, bowing down.
  • Make these visible elements of daily life.
  • Don't be embarrassed by overt devotion.

BG 9.15

Others, who engage in sacrifice by the cultivation of knowledge, worship the Supreme Lord as the one without a second, as diverse in many, and in the universal form.

Essence. Others, by cultivation of knowledge, worship Me as one — as the diverse — and as the universal form.

Practical applications
  • Recognize multiple valid modes of worship.
  • Find the mode that matches your nature.
  • Don't dismiss others' modes.

BG 9.16

But it is I who am the ritual, I the sacrifice, the offering to the ancestors, the healing herb, the transcendental chant. I am the butter and the fire and the offering.

Essence. I am the ritual, the sacrifice, the offering, the herb, the mantra, the clarified butter, the fire, and what is offered.

Practical applications
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa as every component of sacrifice, not just the recipient.
  • Use this in puja to deepen perception.
  • Don't fragment Him into recipient versus means.

BG 9.17

I am the father of this universe, the mother, the support and the grandsire. I am the object of knowledge, the purifier and the syllable oṁ. I am also the Ṛg, the Sāma and the Yajur Vedas.

Essence. I am the father of this universe, the mother, the supporter, the grandfather — the object of knowledge, the purifier, the syllable Om, the Veda.

Practical applications
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa in familial language — He is your true family.
  • Use the relational language to deepen intimacy.
  • Don't reduce Him to abstract principle.

BG 9.18

I am the goal, the sustainer, the master, the witness, the abode, the refuge and the most dear friend. I am the creation and the annihilation, the basis of everything, the resting place and the eternal seed.

Essence. I am the goal, the sustainer, the master, the witness, the abode, the refuge, the most dear friend — the origin and dissolution.

Practical applications
  • Trace Kṛṣṇa's many roles and choose the one that calls you most.
  • Don't think He must be one thing — He is all of these.
  • Approach Him in the role you most need today.

BG 9.19

O Arjuna, I give heat, and I withhold and send forth the rain. I am immortality, and I am also death personified. Both spirit and matter are in Me.

Essence. I provide heat; I withhold and send rain; I am immortality and personified death — being and non-being, both.

Practical applications
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa even in withholding and death.
  • Don't blame nature when withholding occurs.
  • Trust that being-and-non-being are both Him.

BG 9.20

Those who study the Vedas and drink the soma juice, seeking the heavenly planets, worship Me indirectly. Purified of sinful reactions, they take birth on the pious, heavenly planet of Indra, where they enjoy godly delights.

Essence. Those who study the Vedas and drink the soma, performing sacrifices to attain heaven, attain heaven and enjoy.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that even Vedic ritual can be aimed at heaven, not Kṛṣṇa.
  • Aim higher than heavenly enjoyment.
  • Don't confuse pious goals with the highest goal.

BG 9.21

When they have thus enjoyed vast heavenly sense pleasure and the results of their pious activities are exhausted, they return to this mortal planet again. Thus those who seek sense enjoyment by adhering to the principles of the three Vedas achieve only repeated birth and death.

Essence. Having enjoyed in heaven, when their pious credit is exhausted, they return — those who follow Vedic ritual aiming at sense pleasure repeatedly come and go.

Practical applications
  • Don't envy 'heavenly' enjoyments — they exhaust and return you.
  • Choose unending Kṛṣṇa over expiring heaven.
  • Use this verse during temptation toward 'higher' material pleasure.

BG 9.22

But those who always worship Me with exclusive devotion, meditating on My transcendental form – to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve what they have.

Essence. Those who exclusively worship Me, ever absorbed, with no other engagement — to them I give what they lack and protect what they have.

Practical applications
  • Trust this promise: yoga-kṣemam vahāmy aham.
  • Reduce other engagements; cultivate exclusivity.
  • Don't try to be Kṛṣṇa's logistics manager — He is.

BG 9.23

Those who are devotees of other gods and who worship them with faith actually worship only Me, O son of Kuntī, but they do so in a wrong way.

Essence. Those who worship other gods with faith — they too worship Me, but improperly, since I am the only enjoyer of all sacrifice.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that misdirected worship is still partial connection.
  • Don't dismiss other religious sincerity, but understand its limit.
  • Direct your own worship correctly to its true object.

BG 9.24

I am the only enjoyer and master of all sacrifices. Therefore, those who do not recognize My true transcendental nature fall down.

Essence. I am the only enjoyer and master of all sacrifices — those who do not recognize this fall down.

Practical applications
  • Make Kṛṣṇa the explicit recipient of every offering.
  • Don't generically 'send to the universe.'
  • Specificity matters in offering.

BG 9.25

Those who worship the demigods will take birth among the demigods; those who worship the ancestors go to the ancestors; those who worship ghosts and spirits will take birth among such beings; and those who worship Me will live with Me.

Essence. Those who worship demigods go to demigods; ancestors to ancestors; ghosts to ghosts; My worshipers come to Me.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that worship has destination effects.
  • Choose your destination by choosing your worship.
  • Be deliberate, not casual.

BG 9.26

If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

Essence. Whoever offers Me with love a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water — I accept it from the pure-hearted devotee.

Practical applications
  • Don't wait for grand offerings; the simplest is accepted.
  • Make a daily offering, however small.
  • Trust that pure heart trumps grand gesture.

BG 9.27

Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me.

Essence. Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer, whatever you give, whatever austerities you perform — do it as an offering to Me.

Practical applications
  • Apply offering-consciousness to every category of action.
  • Audit a day: what was offered, what was held back?
  • Increase the offering-side over time.

BG 9.28

In this way you will be freed from bondage to work and its auspicious and inauspicious results. With your mind fixed on Me in this principle of renunciation, you will be liberated and come to Me.

Essence. Thus you will be freed from the bondage of works with their good and bad results — with mind united in renunciation, you come to Me.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that offering is the freedom-mechanism.
  • Cultivate the renunciation-of-results mindset specifically.
  • Trust that 'come to Me' follows.

BG 9.29

I envy no one, nor am I partial to anyone. I am equal to all. But whoever renders service unto Me in devotion is a friend, is in Me, and I am also a friend to him.

Essence. I am equal to all beings — neither favoring nor envying anyone; but those who worship Me with love are in Me, and I in them.

Practical applications
  • Distinguish Kṛṣṇa's structural equality from relational reciprocity.
  • Don't expect favoritism; cultivate love that establishes mutual indwelling.
  • Let the indwelling be its own reward.

BG 9.30

Even if one commits the most abominable action, if he is engaged in devotional service he is to be considered saintly because he is properly situated in his determination.

Essence. Even if one with the most abominable life worships Me with full devotion, he should be considered saintly — for he is rightly situated.

Practical applications
  • Don't disqualify yourself based on past.
  • Recognize devotion as the redirective force.
  • Treat 'rightly situated' as a verdict that surrender earns.

BG 9.31

He quickly becomes righteous and attains lasting peace. O son of Kuntī, declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes.

Essence. He quickly becomes righteous and attains lasting peace — declare boldly that My devotee never perishes.

Practical applications
  • Trust the rapidity — devotion changes things quickly.
  • Speak this declaration aloud when you doubt yourself.
  • Speak it for fellow devotees who falter.

BG 9.32

O son of Pṛthā, those who take shelter in Me, though they be of lower birth – women, vaiśyas [merchants] and śūdras [workers] – can attain the supreme destination.

Essence. Those who take shelter in Me — even of low birth, even women, vaiśyas, and śūdras — also approach the supreme destination.

Practical applications
  • Recognize the universal accessibility of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Don't disqualify yourself by social category.
  • Don't disqualify others either.

BG 9.33

How much more this is so of the righteous brāhmaṇas, the devotees and the saintly kings. Therefore, having come to this temporary, miserable world, engage in loving service unto Me.

Essence. How much more then for the pious brāhmaṇas, devotees, kingly saints — having come to this miserable, temporary world, engage in My loving service.

Practical applications
  • Use your existing privileges (birth, training, opportunity) for higher service.
  • Recognize the urgency: temporary world, miserable.
  • Don't waste this rare position.

BG 9.34

Engage your mind always in thinking of Me, become My devotee, offer obeisances to Me and worship Me. Being completely absorbed in Me, surely you will come to Me.

Essence. Always think of Me, become My devotee, worship Me, offer respect to Me — being absorbed in Me, you will surely come to Me.

Practical applications
  • Adopt the four explicit practices: think, devote, worship, respect.
  • Treat them as the operating manual for the chapter.
  • Trust the 'surely come to Me' guarantee.
