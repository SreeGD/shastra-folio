Important Words
  • mahā - ātmānaḥ — the great souls
    Mahā + ātman. Not 'celebrities' but souls who have become vast — not by accumulation but by orientation. They face Kṛṣṇa, and that single facing enlarges them.
  • daivīm — divine (i.e. parā-prakṛti, Bhagavān's spiritual energy)
    Daivī here is not 'belonging to the devas' but Bhagavān's own superior energy — the parā-prakṛti named in 7.5, internally constituted as hlādinī, sandhinī, and saṁvit. The aparā (eightfold material) deludes; the daivī shelters, because she *is* Kṛṣṇa's intimate śakti. The mahātmā takes refuge not in a higher realm but in Kṛṣṇa's own internal potency — and through her, in Him.
  • āśritāḥ — having taken shelter
    Past participle of ā + √śri. The same root used in 9.11 for Kṛṣṇa āśrita-ing the human form — but now the verb runs the other way. Just as He takes shelter in mānuṣī tanu to come close, the mahātmā takes shelter in daivī prakṛti to come close.
  • bhajanti — they worship
    From √bhaj — to share, partake, serve. Bhakti at its root means sharing in. The mahātmā does not merely admire Kṛṣṇa; he shares life with Him. The verb is in active, present, third plural — a continuous shared living.
  • ananya - manasaḥ — without deviation of mind
    A- + anya (other) + manas (mind). 'No-other-minded.' The mahātmā's mind has stopped offering itself in pieces to other objects. One mind, one direction. The single most demanding qualification in the verse.
  • avyayam — inexhaustible
    A- + √vyaya (expenditure) — never spent, never used up. The mahātmā knows Kṛṣṇa as bhūta-ādi (source of beings) and avyaya — original and never-depleted. Not a god among gods, but the never-spent fountain from which all gods come.
O son of Pṛthā, those who are not deluded, the great souls, are under the protection of the divine nature. They are fully engaged in devotional service because they know Me as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, original and inexhaustible.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Contrast to verse 12. The mahātmās take shelter in daivī-prakṛti, recognise Kṛṣṇa as the imperishable origin of all beings, and worship Him with undistracted mind. Ananya-manas is the chapter's emerging keyword.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Three moves define the mahātmā: (1) refuge in daivī-prakṛti (the polar opposite of the āsurī of 9.12); (2) right knowledge of the Lord as bhūtādi avyayam; (3) ananya-bhakti. The Gītā's specific definition of mahātmā is theological, not merely moral. Cross-ref BG 7.19 (vāsudevaḥ sarvam) and the upcoming 9.22 (ananyāś cintayanto).

Purport

In this verse the description of the mahātmā is clearly given. The first sign of the mahātmā is that he is already situated in the divine nature. He is not under the control of material nature. And how is this effected? That is explained in the Seventh Chapter: one who surrenders unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, at once becomes freed from the control of material nature. That is the qualification. One can become free from the control of material nature as soon as he surrenders his soul to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. That is the preliminary formula. Being marginal potency, as soon as the living entity is freed from the control of material nature, he is put under the guidance of the spiritual nature. The guidance of the spiritual nature is called daivī prakṛti, divine nature. So when one is promoted in that way – by surrendering to the Supreme Personality of Godhead – one attains to the stage of great soul, mahātmā. The mahātmā does not divert his attention to anything outside Kṛṣṇa, because he knows perfectly well that Kṛṣṇa is the original Supreme Person, the cause of all causes. There is no doubt about it. Such a mahātmā, or great soul, develops through association with other mahātmās, pure devotees. Pure devotees are not even attracted by Kṛṣṇa’s other features, such as the four-armed Mahā-viṣṇu. They are simply attracted by the two-armed form of Kṛṣṇa. They are not attracted to other features of Kṛṣṇa, nor are they concerned with any form of a demigod or of a human being. They meditate only upon Kṛṣṇa in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. They are always engaged in the unswerving service of the Lord in Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
13. The fortunate souls, attaining deva nature, worship My human form with no distractions, knowing that My human-like body is the cause of all things and is indestructible, being fully spiritual.
Commentary

But those men who attain greatness by unpredictable mercy of My devotees attain the nature of the devas (daivīṁ prakṛtim) (rather than asuras), and worship Me in My human-like form. Their minds do not dwell on such things as desires for jṣāna or karma (ananya manasaḥ). By knowledge of My powers (mayā tatam idaṁ sarvam), they know that I am the cause of all the unlimited bodies starting with Brahmā (bhūtādim). They know that I am indestructible (avayayam), since I have a body of sat-cid-ānanda. For understanding that I am worthy of worship, they should have at least this much knowledge of Me. Knowing this (jṣātvā), they worship Me. It should be understood that this bhakti, which is not dependent on karma or jṣāna which aim at realization of ātmā (tvam), and which is exclusively centered on the Lord, is the best of all, the king of knowledge, the king of secrets.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
13. Those of profound mind, taking shelter of My spiritual svarūpa, serve My human form with no distractions, knowing that My human-like body is the cause of all things, is indestructible, and fully spiritual.
Commentary

“Then who respects You?” Those who know Me as para Brahman with a human form, whose minds have become enlarged and deepened by such faith in Me (mahātmanaḥ—big minded), through devotee association, those who have no taste for other forms of the Lord such as the one having a thousand heads, [Note: This is the description of the Lord in the Puruṣa Sūkta, or the viśva rūpa.] though those are also My forms—such persons, having taken shelter of My spiritual svarūpa (daivim prakṛtim), accepting with conviction (jṣātvā) that I, with human form, am the cause of all living entities, the humans as well as Brahmā and Śiva (bhūtādim), and that I am eternal (avyayam), serve Me (bhajanti). Their consciousness is deeply absorbed in Me in My human form alone (ananya manasaḥ).

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Those mahatmas they are 'asritah', they have taken shelter, not in 'prakrtim mohinim sritah' as the demoniac do, but in the internal energy of the Lord 'daivim prakrtih asritah'. They have taken full shelter of the internal potency of Krsna: 'lhadini, sandini and samvit', and they know everything. They worship Krsna's form. Those mahatmas although humans, are actually divine ‑ and their consciousness is fixed on Krsna without deviation. What are these mahatmas' activities?

Personal Guidance
But the great souls, under the protection of the divine nature, fully engage in devotional service knowing Me as inexhaustible and original.
Practical Applications
  • Aspire to the protection of the divine nature, not just the divine.
  • Cultivate full engagement, not partial.
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa as inexhaustible source.
Reflection Questions
  • Where is my engagement still partial?
  • What would 'full' look like this season?
Analogy

Two ships sail the same sea. One carries no flag and accepts cargo from any port; the wind tosses it everywhere. The other flies the king's pennant; storms pass, pirates yield, supplies arrive at every port without payment. The mahātmā is the second ship — daivīm prakṛtim āśritāḥ, sheltered under the divine nature. Ananya-manasaḥ — single-masted, one flag, one destination.

Stories
Dhruva Mahārāja

Five-year-old Dhruva, refused his father's lap because he was the son of the second queen, walked alone into the forest to find a higher father. Nārada tested him and then gave him the mantra; Dhruva sat in such fierce determination that the universe itself trembled.

When the Lord appeared, Dhruva — now fully aware that he was a mahātmā in the daivī prakṛti — knew he had set out for sweets and found nectar. The royal seat he had originally come for was offered to him afterwards almost as an afterthought. The mahātmā in 9.13 is a portrait Dhruva inhabits: ananya-manasaḥ, knowing Kṛṣṇa as bhūta-ādim avyayam — the original, inexhaustible source.

Prahlāda inside Hiraṇyakaśipu's house

Born into a household defined by hatred of Viṣṇu, Prahlāda was still — from infancy — āśrita to the daivī prakṛti. No threat from his father, no torture, no poisons or serpents shook the ananya-manas of the child. He served the Lord with whole heart while serpents coiled around him.

The verse's portrait of a mahātmā fits him precisely: he knew Kṛṣṇa as bhūta-ādim, the source of all beings, and bhajati with no fluctuation. Hiraṇyakaśipu, by contrast, fits the previous verse — vicetasa, mogha-āśa.
