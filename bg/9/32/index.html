Important Words
  • vyapāśritya — having taken full shelter
    Vi + apa + ā + √śri — taking shelter completely, in every direction. A stronger compound than mere āśritya. The shelter is total, not partial — the entire being placed under His protection.
  • pāpa - yonayaḥ — those of lower birth
    Pāpa (sin) + yoni (womb, source). 'Sin-wombs' — those whose birth circumstances are classified low by Vedic social schema. The verse names the category bluntly, not euphemistically, then refuses to let it exclude.
  • striyaḥ — women
    Women, named explicitly. In a society where Vedic study was sometimes restricted to twice-born men, Kṛṣṇa's verse opens the supreme destination to women — not through ritual reform but through the bhakti channel directly. The path bypasses ritual exclusion entirely.
  • vaiśyāḥ — merchants
    The third varṇa, traditionally given limited Vedic access. Named here as candidates for parām gatim through bhakti. The verse equalizes downward — the lower three categories explicitly.
  • śūdrāḥ — workers
    The fourth varṇa, traditionally given the least Vedic access. Named explicitly. Kṛṣṇa is not reforming varṇāśrama; He is opening a parallel channel that does not run through varṇāśrama at all.
  • parām — supreme
    The very topmost. Not 'a higher destination,' not 'an intermediate one' — parām. The verse refuses to settle for a middling reward; it sets the lowliest by birth on the most direct line to the highest goal.
  • gatim — destination
    From √gam — that to which one goes. Same word as gatiḥ in 9.18. The destination of the soul. The bypass of birth-class is total: even pāpa-yoni-born, through vyapāśritya, reach this gati.
O son of Pṛthā, those who take shelter in Me, though they be of lower birth – women, vaiśyas [merchants] and śūdras [workers] – can attain the supreme destination.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Extending 9.30, Kṛṣṇa names the social categories that birth-society excluded from ritual liberation: women, vaiśyas, śūdras. By taking refuge (vyapāśritya) in Him, even they reach parā gati. Bhakti is birth-blind.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The verse must be read in the social context of its century, when ritual paths were gated by birth and gender. Bhakti, the Gītā insists, has no such gates. 'Pāpa-yonayaḥ' here reflects classical varṇa-discourse rather than ethical judgment; the verse subverts that very framework. Cross-ref Bhāgavata 2.4.18 (kirāta-hūṇāndhra-pulinda…).

Purport

It is clearly declared here by the Supreme Lord that in devotional service there is no distinction between the lower and higher classes of people. In the material conception of life there are such divisions, but for a person engaged in transcendental devotional service to the Lord there are not. Everyone is eligible for the supreme destination. In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (2.4.18) it is stated that even the lowest, who are called caṇḍālas (dog-eaters), can be purified by association with a pure devotee. Therefore devotional service and the guidance of a pure devotee are so strong that there is no discrimination between the lower and higher classes of men; anyone can take to it. The most simple man taking shelter of the pure devotee can be purified by proper guidance. According to the different modes of material nature, men are classified in the mode of goodness ( brāhmaṇas ), the mode of passion ( kṣatriyas, or administrators), the mixed modes of passion and ignorance ( vaiśyas, or merchants), and the mode of ignorance ( śūdras, or workers). Those lower than them are called caṇḍālas, and they are born in sinful families. Generally, the association of those born in sinful families is not accepted by the higher classes. But the process of devotional service is so strong that the pure devotee of the Supreme Lord can enable people of all the lower classes to attain the highest perfection of life. This is possible only when one takes shelter of Kṛṣṇa. As indicated here by the word vyapāśritya, one has to take shelter completely of Kṛṣṇa. Then one can become much greater than great jñānīs and yogīs.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

32. Women, vaiśyas and śūdrās, and even the outcastes, if they surrender to Me, attain the supreme goal.
Commentary

Is it so remarkable that My bhakti does not consider the accidental faults arising by actions of My misbehaving devotee? Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
32. Even those born in sinful situations, such as women, vaiśyas and śūdas, and even the outcastes, if they surrender to Me, attain Me, the supreme goal.
Commentary

“Going to the assembly of disputers with the loud sound of kettle drums, raising your arms, without fear, declare that I, the Supreme Lord, will purify My exclusive devotees of incidental sins.” “What is so remarkable about purifying those devotees who commit accidental or occasional sin?” “Even those who are the most sinful (and are not My devotees), by devotee association, become completely freed of ignorance, and become specially liberated.” That is expressed in this verse. “Even those of very sinful birth, outcastes (papa yonayaḥ), who have a natural inclination to sin, taking shelter of Me, the Lord of all, the son of Vasudeva, by association with My devotees, attain the supreme state (gatim). They attain Me, which is rare even for yogīs (param). This is certain.” Śukadeva says: kirāta-hūṇāndhra-pulinda-pulkaśā ābhīra-śumbhā yavanāḥ khasādayaḥ ye’nye ca pāpā yad-apāśrayāśrayāḥ śudhyanti tasmai prabhaviṣṇave namaḥ Kirāta, Hūṇa, Āndhra, Pulinda, Pulkaśa, Ābhīra, Śumbha, Yavana, members of the Khasa races and even others addicted to sinful acts can be purified by taking shelter of the devotees of the Lord, due to His being the supreme power. I beg to offer my respectful obeisances unto Him. SB 2.4.18 In mentioning women, śūdras and vaiśyas, the Lord indicates that all those who are impure or have disagreeable qualities (by nature), by taking to devotional service, will attain the Lord.

Personal Guidance
Those who take shelter in Me — even of low birth, even women, vaiśyas, and śūdras — also approach the supreme destination.
Practical Applications
  • Recognize the universal accessibility of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Don't disqualify yourself by social category.
  • Don't disqualify others either.
Reflection Questions
  • Where have I implicitly accepted social spiritual hierarchies that Kṛṣṇa rejects?
  • What does His universality require of me?
Analogy

The kingdom's main road and a forest footpath both run east. The royal carriage on the main road and the woman with a basket on the footpath are heading to the same capital. Birth-class is a road; bhakti is the direction. Whoever turns east — vyapāśritya Mām — reaches the capital, regardless of which path his birth happened to set him on. Param gatim does not check the path.

Stories
Śabarī of the forest

Śabarī, a tribal woman of the Niṣāda community — classed as below the four varṇas by the social mind of her age — lived in the forest waiting for Rāma. Her guru Mātaṅga had told her that Rāma would one day pass that way. For decades she swept the path daily and tasted each berry to set aside only the sweetest for Him.

When Rāma finally arrived, He ate the half-bitten berries with delight and gave her mokṣa on the spot. Pāpa-yoni in social classification, parām gatim in destination. The verse 9.32 is Śabarī's biography in seven words.

Mīrābāī and Sajjana Bāī

Mīrā, born into Rajput royalty but in the verse's classification 'a woman,' was among the great bhakti-paramparā saints of medieval India. Her songs travel today through schools that her caste was never admitted to. Around her gathered women of every social tier, including Sajjana Bāī and other devotee-companions who would have been excluded from Vedic learning by birth.

The verse 9.32 makes their reception scriptural, not exceptional. Striyaḥ — women — are named explicitly. Kṛṣṇa Himself shuts the door against the snobbery the verse's audience might have brought.
