Bhagavad-gītā 18.72

Important Words
  • kaccit — whether
    Interrogative particle. Soft, careful. The verse asks a yes-or-no question that allows the student to answer truly. Kṛṣṇa does not assume comprehension; he checks.
  • eka - agreṇa — with full attention
    Compound instrumental. Eka-agra — single-tipped. The verse asks not merely whether Arjuna heard but whether he heard with *concentrated* attention. Quality of listening is at issue.
  • sammohaḥ — the illusion
    From sam-muh — to be thoroughly bewildered. The specific moha — the bewilderment of 1.46 territory that opened the Gītā. The verse asks whether the original problem has been resolved.
  • praṇaṣṭaḥ — is dispelled
    Past participle — pra-naṣṭa, utterly destroyed. Kṛṣṇa wants to know whether the illusion has been *destroyed*, not merely shaken. The threshold is high.
  • dhanam-jaya — O conqueror of wealth
    Address. Kṛṣṇa uses Arjuna's victory-name precisely at the moment of testing whether the inner victory has occurred. The address itself is gentle encouragement.
O son of Pṛthā, O conqueror of wealth, have you heard this with an attentive mind? And are your ignorance and illusions now dispelled?
Purport

The Lord was acting as the spiritual master of Arjuna. Therefore it was His duty to inquire from Arjuna whether he understood the whole Bhagavad-gītā in its proper perspective. If not, the Lord was ready to re-explain any point, or the whole Bhagavad-gītā if so required. Actually, anyone who hears Bhagavad-gītā from a bona fide spiritual master like Kṛṣṇa or His representative will find that all his ignorance is dispelled. Bhagavad-gītā is not an ordinary book written by a poet or fiction writer; it is spoken by the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Any person fortunate enough to hear these teachings from Kṛṣṇa or from His bona fide spiritual representative is sure to become a liberated person and get out of the darkness of ignorance.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
72. O son of Pṛthā, have you heard this with close attention? O conqueror of wealth, have your ignorance and bewilderment been destroyed?
Commentary

The Lord speaks this verse with the intention of teaching it again to Arjuna, if he were unable to grasp the meaning fully.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
72. O son of Pṛthā, have you heard this with close attention? O conqueror of wealth, have your ignorance and bewilderment been destroyed?
Commentary

Thus the scripture and glorification of speaking and hearing it have been spoken. The Lord now asks about the comprehension of the meaning of the scripture and the realization of the scripture. kaccid is an indeclinable particle indicating a question. If you have not completely understood, then I will again teach it. That is the intention of the question.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

If Krsna, the teacher, is asking Arjuna, His student: "Do you understand now what you have to do? Have you heard properly?" And why is Krsna asking this? ‑ Because, out of His great patience and affection for His student Arjuna who was surrender to Him, if Arjuna haven't been able to understand, Krsna was fully prepared to explain the entire Bhagavad‑gita again.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

Krishna asks if, Bhagavad-gita has freed Arjuna from ignorance and illusion

Focus of Learning

(a) Why Krishna asks this question as spiritual master  As spiritual master it was His duty to know, if Arjuna understood Bhagavad-gita in proper perspective (b) Expected result of hearing (As per Krishna’s expectations)  Ignorance and illusion dispelled (c) Bhagavad-gita is not an ordinary book – So what is expected  Any person fortunate to hear from a bona-fide representative is sure to become liberated and get out of the darkness of ignorance

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
18.72 O Partha, kaccit etat, has this that has been said by Me; been srutam, listened to, grasped through hearing; ekagrena, with a none-pointed; cetasa, mind? Or have you been inattentive? O Dhananjaya, kaccit, has; te, your; ajnana-sammohah, delusion caused by ignorance, bewilderment, natural indiscrimination; been pranastah, destroyed, for which purpose has there been this effort on your part for hearing the Scripture, and on My part, the effort of being a teacher?
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
18.72 O Arjuna, have you heard attentively all that I have taught? Has your delusion caused by ignorance, been dispelled - that ignorance deluded by which you said, 'I shall not fight'?
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
18.68-72 Ya idam etc. upto Dhananjaya. The very act of explaining this among My devotees is itself an act of cultivating devotion towards Me. Whosoever shall declare etc : Whosoever directs the mind [of the devotees] by face to face method, following the best procedure prescribed in the scriptures; i.e., he who shall impart [this knowledge] - he becomes one with Me. This is an injunction [concerning the result] and not a sentence of praise. Likewise is [the case] in other places also.

Personal Guidance
Have you, with concentrated mind, heard this? Has your illusion of ignorance been dispelled, O Dhanañjaya?
Practical Applications
  • Periodically check yourself: has my own confusion been dispelled by what I've read?
  • If not, re-read.
  • Don't pretend dispelling that hasn't happened.
Reflection Questions
  • What confusion has the Gītā dispelled in me?
  • What confusion remains?
Analogy

A teacher who has poured out a long instruction pauses to check: has the cup actually filled? Or has the listener been holding the cup at an angle? Kṛṣṇa's question is gentle but precise — he wants the student's own verification before they part.
