Bhagavad-gītā 18.46

यत: प्रवृत्तिर्भूतानां येन सर्वमिदं ततम् । स्वकर्मणा तमभ्यर्च्य सिद्धिं विन्दति मानव: ॥ ४६ ॥
yataḥ pravṛttir bhūtānāṁ
[yatah pravrittir bhootaanaam]
yena sarvam idaṁ tatam
[yena sarvam idam tatam]
sva-karmaṇā tam abhyarcya
[sva-karmanaa tam abhyarchya]
siddhiṁ vindati mānavaḥ
[siddhim vindati maanavah]

By worship of the Lord, who is the source of all beings and who is all-pervading, a man can attain perfection through performing his own work.
Purport

As stated in the Fifteenth Chapter, all living beings are fragmental parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord. Thus the Supreme Lord is the beginning of all living entities. This is confirmed in the Vedānta-sūtra – janmādy asya yataḥ. The Supreme Lord is therefore the beginning of life of every living entity. And as stated in the Seventh Chapter of Bhagavad-gītā, the Supreme Lord, by His two energies, His external energy and internal energy, is all-pervading. Therefore one should worship the Supreme Lord with His energies. Generally the Vaiṣṇava devotees worship the Supreme Lord with His internal energy. His external energy is a perverted reflection of the internal energy. The external energy is a background, but the Supreme Lord by the expansion of His plenary portion as Paramātmā is situated everywhere. He is the Supersoul of all demigods, all human beings, all animals, everywhere. One should therefore know that as part and parcel of the Supreme Lord one has his duty to render service unto the Supreme. Everyone should be engaged in devotional service to the Lord in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness. That is recommended in this verse. Everyone should think that he is engaged in a particular type of occupation by Hṛṣīkeśa, the master of the senses. And by the result of the work in which one is engaged, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, should be worshiped. If one thinks always in this way, in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, then, by the grace of the Lord, he becomes fully aware of everything. That is the perfection of life. The Lord says in Bhagavad-gītā (12.7), teṣām ahaṁ samuddhartā. The Supreme Lord Himself takes charge of delivering such a devotee. That is the highest perfection of life. In whatever occupation one may be engaged, if he serves the Supreme Lord he will achieve the highest perfection.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
46. Men achieve success by worshiping the Lord who is the origin of all entities and is spread everywhere, through mentally offering their prescribed duties.
Commentary

Humans attain perfection by worshipping (abhyarcya) through their prescribed work Him alone, the Supreme Lord, from whom arise the activities of all living entities. The worship consists of offering the work in the mind, thinking, “By this work may the Lord be satisfied.”

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
46. Men achieve steadiness in jṣāna by worshiping the Lord who is the origin of all entities and is spread everywhere, through mentally offering their prescribed duties.
Commentary

From the Lord arise (pravṛttiḥ) all the living entities. By Him the whole universe is pervaded (tatam). Worshipping that Lord who is situated as the soul of all the devatās such as Indra by ones activities— offering the act to Him after performing them by thinking “May the Lord be satisfied with the act,” men attain steadiness in jṣāna (siddhim).

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
18.46 He from whom arise all activities as origination of all beings and by whom all this is pervaded, by worshipping Him, i.e., Myself, who abide in Indra and other divinites as the Inner Ruler, man attains perfection, consisting in the attainment of Myself by My grace. It has been told before that everything originates from Me and all this is pervaded by Me, in texts like the following: 'I am the origin and dissolution of the whole universe' (7.6), 'There is nothing higher than Myself, O Arjuna' (7.7), 'This entire universe is pervaded by Me in an unmanifest form' (9.4), 'Under My supervision, the Prakrti gives birth to all mobile and immobile entities' (9.10) and 'I am the origin of all; from Me proceed everything' (10.8).

Personal Guidance
By worshiping Him from whom all beings come, who pervades all — by his own work, a man attains perfection.
Practical Applications
  • Treat your work as worship of the Lord who pervades all.
  • Frame the offering deliberately.
  • Don't separate work from worship.
Reflection Questions
  • How does my work currently relate to worship?
  • What integration is being asked?
Analogy

Every river finds the ocean. The plowman, the warrior, the merchant, the servant — each pours his work back into the source of all beings, and the same ocean receives them all. The vehicle differs; the destination is one.

Stories
Tulādhāra the merchant

In the Mahābhārata, the sage Jājali learns from the simple merchant Tulādhāra of Vārāṇasī that his weighing-scale, kept perfectly honest and offered to dharma, is itself a path to the highest. Tulādhāra never left his shop.

By worshiping the Lord through impeccable sva-karma — fair weights, honest pricing, no exploitation — he reached what the most austere sages reached. The verse 18.46 is precisely the principle Jājali was taught.

Vyādha the butcher

The Mahābhārata also recounts how the brāhmaṇa Kauśika is sent to learn from a butcher in Mithilā. The butcher performs his prescribed duty without pride and without shirking, serves his parents with full devotion, and worships the Lord through his work itself.

The brāhmaṇa, who had become proud of his recitation, learns that the butcher's sva-karma offered to the Lord is the same perfection-path that he had sought through study. Sva-karmaṇā tam abhyarcya.

Important Words
  • pravṛttiḥ — the emanation
    From pra-vṛt — to come forth. The Lord is the source — *from* whom the manifestation of beings flows. The verse roots itself first in His role as origin (pravṛtti).
  • tatam — is pervaded
    Past participle of tan — to stretch, pervade. The Lord is also the all-pervader. Both manifest source and immanent presence — origin and field together. Worship is offered to *this* Lord.
  • sva-karmaṇā — by one's own work
    Instrumental. The *means* of worship is precisely the sva-karma of verse 45. The verse converts varṇa-karma into Lord-worship — its theological pivot.
  • abhyarcya — by worshiping
    Gerund of abhi-arc — to worship fully. Sva-karma is not merely 'permitted'; it is *the very act of worship*. Work becomes pūjā.
  • vindati — achieves
    From vid — to find. With siddhim: attains perfection. Same verb as verse 45 — but now its mechanism is explicit. Perfection comes not just from doing one's work but from doing it as worship.
