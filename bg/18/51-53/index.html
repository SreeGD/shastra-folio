Bhagavad-gītā 18.51-53

Important Words
  • viśuddhayā — fully purified
    Instrumental, with buddhyā. The whole brahma-niṣṭhā depends on a *clean* buddhi — the kind described in verse 30 as sāttvika. Without that base, the rest of the list is decorative.
  • vivikta-sevī — living in solitude
    Compound. Vivikta — separate, secluded. The sādhaka actively *resorts to* (sevī) solitary places. Solitude is not incidental; it is sought.
  • laghu-āśī — eating little
    Compound. Laghu — light; āśin — eater. Body kept light. Heavy food drags the buddhi into rajas and tamas; the brahma-aspirant tapers his intake.
  • dhyāna-yoga-paraḥ — absorbed in meditational yoga
    Compound — para meaning 'devoted to.' The sādhaka is *given over* to dhyāna-yoga. Not an item on the to-do list — the central organizing activity of life.
  • samupāśritaḥ — having taken shelter of
    Past participle — sam-upa-āśrita. With vairāgyam: taking shelter of detachment. Vairāgya is here a *shelter* — not an attainment to display but a refuge to inhabit. One *enters* vairāgya rather than performing it.
  • parigraham — possessiveness
    Pari-grah — gripping all around. The sixth in the list of inner obstacles (ahaṅkāra, bala, darpa, kāma, krodha, parigraha). Each is a knot; the sādhaka is vimucya — released — from all six.
  • nirmamaḥ — without 'mine'
    Nis-mama — without 'mine.' Proprietorship dissolved. With śāntaḥ: the two end-state diagnostic marks. These are the signs that the sādhaka has arrived where the verse points.
  • kalpate — is qualified
    Present indicative. With brahma-bhūyāya: is qualified for becoming Brahman. The sādhaka does not merely study Brahman — he is *fit to become* it. Kalpate names the qualification.
Being purified by his intelligence and controlling the mind with determination, giving up the objects of sense gratification, being freed from attachment and hatred, one who lives in a secluded place, who eats little, who controls his body, mind and power of speech, who is always in trance and who is detached, free from false ego, false strength, false pride, lust, anger, and acceptance of material things, free from false proprietorship, and peaceful – such a person is certainly elevated to the position of self-realization.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
A single sustained portrait of the brahma-bhūta yogī — purified intellect, self-restraint, sense-withdrawal, equanimity, solitude, moderation, mental discipline, meditation, dispassion, release of the six inner foes and possessiveness. He becomes fit to merge into Brahman.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
These three verses form one syntactic unit — a chain of absolutives and bahuvrīhis culminating in the main predicate 'brahma-bhūyāya kalpate'. The catalogue spans intellectual purity (buddhi viśuddhā), psycho-physical regimen (vivikta-sevī, laghv-āśī, yata-vāk-kāya-mānasaḥ), meditative absorption (dhyāna-yoga-paraḥ), affective renunciation (rāga-dveṣau vyudasya), and the release of the ṣaḍ-ūrmi-like sextet (ahaṅkāra, bala, darpa, kāma, krodha, parigraha). The result, nirmamaḥ śāntaḥ, mirrors 2.71's classic peace-formula. Brahma-bhūya is not annihilation but qualification: 'kalpate' (becomes fit) marks readiness, which v. 54 fulfills.

Purport

When one is purified by intelligence, he keeps himself in the mode of goodness. Thus one becomes the controller of the mind and is always in trance. He is not attached to the objects of sense gratification, and he is free from attachment and hatred in his activities. Such a detached person naturally prefers to live in a secluded place, he does not eat more than what he requires, and he controls the activities of his body and mind. He has no false ego because he does not accept the body as himself. Nor has he a desire to make the body fat and strong by accepting so many material things. Because he has no bodily concept of life, he is not falsely proud. He is satisfied with everything that is offered to him by the grace of the Lord, and he is never angry in the absence of sense gratification. Nor does he endeavor to acquire sense objects. Thus when he is completely free from false ego, he becomes nonattached to all material things, and that is the stage of self-realization of Brahman. That stage is called the brahma-bhūta stage. When one is free from the material conception of life, he becomes peaceful and cannot be agitated. This is described in Bhagavad-gītā (2.70) : āpūryamāṇam acala-pratiṣṭhaṁ samudram āpaḥ praviśanti yadvat tadvat kāmā yaṁ praviśanti sarve sa śāntim āpnoti na kāma-kāmī “A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Commentary

In his Purport, Srila Prabhupada says: "When one is purified by intelligence, he keeps himself in the mode of goodness. Thus one becomes the controller of the mind and is always in trance. He is not attached to the objects of sense gratification, and he is free from attachment and hatred in his activities. Such a detached person naturally prefers to live in a secluded place, he does not eat more than what he requires, and he controls the activities of his body and mind. He has no false ego because he does not accept the body as himself. Nor has he a desire to make the body fat and strong by accepting so many material things. Because he has no bodily concept of life, he is not falsely proud. He is satisfied with everything that is offered to him by the grace of the Lord, and he is never angry in the absence of sense gratification. Nor does he endeavor to acquire sense objects. Thus when he is completely free false ego, he becomes nonattached to all material things, and that is the stage of self‑realization of Brahman. That stage is called the brahma‑ bhuta stage. When one is free from the material conception of life, he becomes peaceful and cannot be agitated. This is described in Bhagavad‑gita (2.70):

apuryamanam acala‑pratistham tadvat kama yam pravisanti sarve sa santimn apnoti na kama‑kami

``A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires‑‑that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still‑‑can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires.''"

Theme

COMMON THEME: One achieves supreme perfection – ‘Brahma-bhuta’ platform and ‘peacefulness’ by:

1. Controlling the mind with determination

2. Purified by intelligence

3. Giving up sense objects

4. Freedom from attachment and hatred

5. Living in a secluded place

6. Eats little and controls his body, mind and power of speech

7. Freedom from all bad qualities born of material conceptions – False ego, false strength, false pride,

lust, anger and acceptance of material things and false proprietorship

8. Always in trance and detached – Thus one achieves Supreme peace

Analogy for peace (Ref. Bg. 2.70) – Rivers flowing into ocean are compared to incessant flow of desires (purport)

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
18.51 - 18.53 'Endowed with a purified understanding' means endowed with the Buddhi capable of understanding the self as it is in reality; 'subduing the mind by steadiness' means making the mind fit for meditation by turning away from external and internal objects; 'relinishing sound and other objects of senses' means keeping them far away, casting aside love and hate occasioned by them (i.e., the sense objects). 'Resorting to solitude' means living in a lonely place free from hindrances to meditation; 'eat but little' means eating neither too much nor too little; 'restraining speech, body and mind' means directing the operations of body, speech and mind to meditation; 'ever engaged in the Yoga of meditation' means being like this, i.e., constantly engaged in the Yoga of meditation day after day until death; 'taking refuge in dispassion' means developing aversion to all objects except the one entity to be meditated upon, by considering the imperfections of all objects and thus cultivating detachment to everything. Forsaking 'egoism' means abandoning the tendency to consider what is other than the self, as well as neutralising the power of forcible Vasnas (tendencies) which nourish (egoism), and the resulting pride, desire, wrath and possessiveness. 'With no feeling of mine' means free from the notion that what does not belong to oneself belongs to oneself; 'Who is tranil' means, who finds sole happiness in experiencing the self. One who has become like this and performs the Yoga of meditation becomes worthy for the state of Brahman. The meaning is that, freed from all bonds, he experiences the self as It really is.

Personal Guidance
Purified intelligence, controlled determination, giving up sense-objects, living in seclusion, eating little, controlling body and tongue, freed from false ego, false strength, lust, anger, possessiveness — qualified for Brahman-realization.
Practical Applications
  • Use this list as advanced curriculum after the foundational chapters.
  • Don't expect to do all at once; deepen one or two.
  • Honor the maturity required.
Reflection Questions
  • Which of these qualifications is least developed?
  • What is my next step toward it?
Analogy

A garden walled, weeded, watered, and pruned. The walls are buddhi viśuddhā and dhṛti; the weeding is the dropping of viṣayas and rāga-dveṣa; the watering is solitude and light eating; the pruning is the removal of ahaṅkāra, bala, darpa, kāma, krodha, parigraha. What is left at the center is the brahma-bhūta sādhaka — single-pointed, peaceful, the soul finally visible.

Stories
The Six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana

Rūpa, Sanātana, Raghunātha dāsa, Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa, Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, and Jīva embodied this composite verse precisely. They lived vivikta-sevī — secluded in Vraja; laghu-āśī — eating minimally; yata-vāk-kāya-mānasa — controlled in speech, body, and mind; dhyāna-yoga-paraḥ — absorbed in mood-meditation around Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa.

Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura's song lists them this way: kṛṣṇotkīrtana-gāna-nartana-parau, premāmṛtāmbho-nidhī. Every quality the verse asks for — vairāgya, ahaṅkāra-vimocana, nirmama, śānta — is independently attested in their lives.

Haridāsa Ṭhākura's seclusion

Haridāsa lived in a small hut chanting three lakh names a day, eating once a day or less, conversing with no one but Mahāprabhu, untouched by the seductions sent to break him. He embodied vivikta-sevī, laghu-āśī, yata-vāk-kāya-mānasaḥ, and dhyāna-yoga-paraḥ in his very body.

His state at death — Mahāprabhu cradling him as he chanted his last name — was the brahma-bhūyāya kalpate of verse 53 in flesh.
