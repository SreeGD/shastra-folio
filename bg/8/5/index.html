Important Words
  • anta - kāle — at the end of life
    Anta (end) + kāla (time). The final moment, the closing seal. The Gītā teaches that this moment is uniquely consequential — not because Kṛṣṇa is partial to it but because the soul's accumulated bhāva surfaces there with peculiar clarity.
  • mām — Me
    Accusative of aham. Personal. Not Brahman in the abstract, not the impersonal light — Kṛṣṇa Himself. The whole devotional weight of the chapter rests on this short word: remembrance is directed at a Person, not a principle.
  • smaran — remembering
    Present participle from smṛ — to remember, hold in mind, recollect. Not visualizing, not imagining — calling back to mind what one has already known. Implies prior cultivation: you can only remember what was once present.
  • muktvā — quitting
    Absolutive of muc — to release. The same root as mokṣa. The body is not taken from the soul; the soul lets it go. The dying person is grammatically the agent of the release, not its victim — at least for those who have prepared.
  • mat - bhāvam — My nature
    Mat (mine) + bhāva (nature, state of being). What the verse promises is not merely Kṛṣṇa's planet or His association — it is participation in His own bhāva, His mode of being. The strongest possible attainment, compressed into one compound.
  • saṁśayaḥ — doubt
    From sam + śī — 'lying together', i.e. two possibilities lying side by side. Kṛṣṇa explicitly closes the door: na asti atra saṁśayaḥ — there is no double possibility here. The certainty is unusually emphatic for the Gītā.
And whoever, at the end of his life, quits his body remembering Me alone at once attains My nature. Of this there is no doubt.
Purport

In this verse the importance of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is stressed. Anyone who quits his body in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is at once transferred to the transcendental nature of the Supreme Lord. The Supreme Lord is the purest of the pure. Therefore anyone who is constantly Kṛṣṇa conscious is also the purest of the pure. The word smaran (“remembering”) is important. Remembrance of Kṛṣṇa is not possible for the impure soul who has not practiced Kṛṣṇa consciousness in devotional service. Therefore one should practice Kṛṣṇa consciousness from the very beginning of life. If one wants to achieve success at the end of his life, the process of remembering Kṛṣṇa is essential. Therefore one should constantly, incessantly chant the mahā-mantra – Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. Lord Caitanya has advised that one be as tolerant as a tree ( taror api sahiṣṇunā ). There may be so many impediments for a person who is chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa. Nonetheless, tolerating all these impediments, one should continue to chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare, so that at the end of one’s life one can have the full benefit of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
5. At the point of death, he who leaves the body while knowing Me in truth, attains a nature similar to Mine. Of this there is no doubt.
Commentary

This verse answers the question “How are you to be known at the time of death?” Smaran means remembering Me, knowing Me in truth, not like knowing pots or cloth. The method of knowing or remembering the Lord is now explained in fourteen verses.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
5. At the point of death he who leaves the body remembering Me attains a nature like Mine. Of this there is no doubt.
Commentary

This verse answers the question “How can one know you at the time of dying?” I , being known at that time by knowledge in the form of remembrance, bestow the result—your attainment of My nature (mad bhāvam). Mad bhāvam in this verse means “My nature.” He who remembers Me attains a nature like Mine, with eight qualities: he becomes sinless, ageless, deathless, without grief, without hunger, without thirst, blissful, and his desires are instantly fulfilled.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Krsna answers to the question of Arjuna: 'How can we know You at the time of death?' Krsna says "By remembering ", because remembering is devotional service. And one who knows Krsna as the adhidaiva, adhiyajna and adhibhuta, can thus remember Krsna at the time of death.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

This verse stresses the importance of Krishna Consciousness – Whoever remembers the Supreme

(Krishna) at the point of death will attain the Supreme destination (attains “My Nature”)

Focus of Learning

(a) Krishna consciousness means being ‘Purest of the Pure’ – because of constant remembrance of

the Supreme Lord who is the ‘Purest of the Pure’

(b) Smaran (Remembrance)

It is not possible for an impure soul
Purify yourself by practicing remembrance
If not practiced, one cannot remember Krishna at the end of his life

PRACTICAL APPLICATION OF SECTION II

To attain Krishna, one must remember Him at the time of death. This remembrance is more than a mental trick. The word used in Text 8.5 – 8.6 is ‘bhava’ which means mood. At death, one will automatically remember the mood he cultivated throughout his life
The most effective means for fixing the mind on Krishna is ‘Harinama sankirtana’

(a) Adhibhuta – Constantly changing physical nature which manifests as six changes of body: birth,

growth, sustenance, by-products, dwindling and death

(b) Adhidaivata – Universal form

Contemplated by neophytes who cannot approach the Supreme Lord in manifestation as Supersoul
Contemplated as follows:
Legs – lower planets
Head – upper plants
Eyes – sun and moon

(c) Adhiyajna – Function of Supersoul

Witness of soul’s activities
Source of soul’s various types of consciousness
Gives individual soul – free independence

(d) “Eva” – Stresses that Paramatma is non-different from Supreme Lord Krishna Himself

(e) Who understands the functions of all these manifestations of Supreme Personality of Godhead

clearly – only a pure Krishna conscious devotee

Personal Guidance
Whoever, at the time of death, remembers Me alone — leaving the body, attains My nature; of this there is no doubt.
Practical Applications
  • Treat this verse as the central promise of the Gītā for moments of departure.
  • Practice remembrance now so it's available later.
  • Read this verse to dying loved ones, with mercy.
Reflection Questions
  • How available is Kṛṣṇa-remembrance to me right now?
  • What practice keeps Him most accessible?
Analogy

The last thought is like the final note a musician sounds before the instrument is set down — it lingers in the air and tells you what the whole evening's music meant. Kṛṣṇa promises: end on the note of Kṛṣṇa, and the music does not end at all; it joins the eternal raga.

Stories
Ajāmila's final cry

Ajāmila had spent the latter half of his life sunk in sin — drinking, gambling, attached to a low-born woman. At the moment of death, with Yamadūtas already approaching to drag him to hell, he called out the name 'Nārāyaṇa' — meaning only to summon his youngest son, whom he had affectionately named after the Lord.

The Bhāgavatam records that the Viṣṇudūtas immediately intervened, stopped the Yamadūtas, and granted Ajāmila release. The very name, spoken at the moment of death — even without conscious devotion — carried such force that it overrode the karma of a lifetime.

Kṛṣṇa's promise in 8.5 — 'remembering Me alone, attains My nature, of this there is no doubt' — is the principle; Ajāmila is the demonstration. The Bhāgavatam expands what the Gītā compresses.

Bharata Mahārāja and the deer

The great king Bharata renounced his throne and went to the forest for bhakti. There he rescued an orphaned fawn and grew attached to it, tending it, worrying over it, thinking of it constantly. At the moment of his death, his mind rested not on Kṛṣṇa but on the deer.

In his next life he was born as a deer — kept his memory, learned the lesson, and in the life after that returned as the renounced sage Jaḍa Bharata. The negative side of 8.6 made visible: whatever you remember at the end, you attain. Bharata recovered, but the detour cost him a body.
