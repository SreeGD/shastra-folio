Important Words
  • vismaya - āviṣṭaḥ — overcome with wonder
    Compound: vismaya (wonder) + āviṣṭa (entered, possessed). Arjuna is not wondering — wonder has entered him. The grammar of devotional emotion is always passive.
  • hṛṣṭa - romā — hair standing on end
    One of the eight sāttvika-bhāvas in classical rasa-theory: romāñca, the involuntary bristling of body-hair in extreme emotion. The Gītā here records a textbook sāttvika sign.
  • praṇamya — having bowed
    Gerund of pra-nam. Sequential — first the bowing, then the speaking. Praise spoken from a standing posture is not the same praise.
  • śirasā — with the head
    Instrumental. Not a nod — the head itself goes to the ground. Body-language is theology in the Gītā's grammar.
  • kṛta - añjaliḥ — with folded hands
    Compound: kṛta (made) + añjali (the hollow of joined palms). The añjali is the universal Indic gesture of receiving — Arjuna's hands are formed to receive what is about to be spoken and seen.
Then, bewildered and astonished, his hair standing on end, Arjuna bowed his head to offer obeisances and with folded hands began to pray to the Supreme Lord.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Sañjaya describes Arjuna's bodily response before any word: wonder, gooseflesh, bowed head, joined palms. The body marks the moment of darśana sacramentally before the mouth can compose a stotra.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The physiological signs (vismaya, hṛṣṭa-roma) are technical markers of sāttvika-bhāva in Sanskrit aesthetics — involuntary responses to overwhelming presence. The verse functions as a stage direction setting up the long Arjuna-stotra of 11.15-31. Hands and head, the two main organs of namaskāra, are both engaged.

Purport

Once the divine vision is revealed, the relationship between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna changes immediately. Before, Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna had a relationship based on friendship, but here, after the revelation, Arjuna is offering obeisances with great respect, and with folded hands he is praying to Kṛṣṇa. He is praising the universal form. Thus Arjuna’s relationship becomes one of wonder rather than friendship. Great devotees see Kṛṣṇa as the reservoir of all relationships. In the scriptures there are twelve basic kinds of relationships mentioned, and all of them are present in Kṛṣṇa. It is said that He is the ocean of all the relationships exchanged between two living entities, between the gods, or between the Supreme Lord and His devotees. Here Arjuna was inspired by the relationship of wonder, and in that wonder, although he was by nature very sober, calm and quiet, he became ecstatic, his hair stood up, and he began to offer his obeisances unto the Supreme Lord with folded hands. He was not, of course, afraid. He was affected by the wonders of the Supreme Lord. The immediate context is wonder; his natural loving friendship was overwhelmed by wonder, and thus he reacted in this way.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
14. Then, overcome with wonder, hair standing on end, Arjuna, bowing his head before the Lord and folding his hands in prayer, spoke.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
14. Then, overcome with wonder, hair standing on end, Arjuna, bowing his head before the Lord and folding his hands in prayer, spoke.
Commentary

Arjuna, who knew Kṛṣṇa, then experienced the rasa of adbhuta (astonishment) on seeing at that time the form with a thousand heads which he understood actually existed within Kṛṣṇa. Looking at Kṛṣṇa who had furnished this form, he spoke. Arjuna was the conqueror of all wealth (dhanaṁjayaḥ), and thus very steady, but with hairs standing on end, overcome with astonishment, after offering his respects to Kṛṣṇa by touching his head to the earth, he spoke with folded hands. It is hinted that he did not cover up his eyes out of fear, but displayed openly great astonishment. The Lord is the ālambana (the viṣaya) or object invoking rasa. [Note: This is part of vibhāva. In rasa there are five components: sattvika bhāva, anubhāva, saṣcari bhāva, vibhāva, and sthāyi bhāva. Vibhāva (preliminary factors) has two components: alambana (persons involved-viṣaya, object of love and āśraya, the experiencer) and uddīpana (stimulants).] When Arjuna looked at Him again and again, the gazing became the uddīpana, or instigation for rasa. Offering respects and folding hands are anubhāvas. Standing of hairs on end is the sāttvika bhāva. Mind overwhelmed by these elements, joy and steadiness are saṣcari bhāvas. By these four components, the fifth component, the sthāyī bhāva called viṣmaya became nourished, and the five together became adbhuta rasa.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya

Now Arjuna is seeing what previously he had heard about. He was astonished and bewildered but because he is a great ksatryia he didn't run away. But still, he put his head down in great astonishment and bewilderement. All the primary rasas and all, for except one, of the secondary rasas will be exhibited in this Chapter to Arjuna.

Personal Guidance
Bewildered with hairs standing on end, Arjuna bowed his head and offered prayers with folded hands.
Practical Applications
  • Honor the body's response to spiritual reality (tears, hairs standing).
  • Don't suppress reverent body-response.
  • Pair body and prayer — bow physically when bowing internally.
Reflection Questions
  • When did I last let my body fully respond to spiritual encounter?
  • What body-practice would honor those moments?
Analogy

When the temple doors finally open at the auspicious hour, the pilgrim does not declaim — he falls, hands joined, head down. Arjuna, the master archer, drops his stance and becomes a body of obeisance. The hair-on-end (hṛṣṭa-romā) is the body's own honest signal that the soul has met something larger.

Stories
Akrūra in the Yamunā

When Akrūra was driving Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā (SB 10.39), he stepped into the Yamunā to bathe and there beheld the four-armed form of Viṣṇu with conch, disc, mace, and lotus, attended by all the great sages. His hair stood on end, tears poured forth, and he could only fold his hands and pray.

Arjuna's response in 11.14 is identical: praṇamya śirasā kṛta-añjaliḥ. Different devotees, different settings — same bodily grammar of darśana.
