Important Words
  • bhava — appearance
    Bhava and apyaya together cover the whole life of every entity. Arjuna is signalling that he has heard Kṛṣṇa's cosmology (chapters 7–10) and is ready to see its source.
  • apyayau — disappearance
    Dual locative. Birth and dissolution treated as a pair — the same Lord presides over both. The dual form refuses to split creation from destruction.
  • kamala - patra - akṣa — O lotus-petal-eyed one
    A vocative of intimate praise. Even as Arjuna is about to ask for the awful viśva-rūpa, he addresses Kṛṣṇa first by His lotus-eyed sweetness — the request for the terrible is framed by the tender.
  • māhātmyam — glories
    Not mere mahattva (greatness) but māhātmya — glory as ontological status. Arjuna has heard not Kṛṣṇa's adjectives but His being.
  • avyayam — inexhaustible
    A favourite Gītā word for the Lord and the self. Glory that cannot be spent — what Arjuna has heard cannot be exhausted by hearing alone, which is precisely why he must now ask to see.
O lotus-eyed one, I have heard from You in detail about the appearance and disappearance of every living entity and have realized Your inexhaustible glories.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Arjuna recalls the cosmological survey of ch. 7-10: he has heard from Kṛṣṇa Himself about how all beings emerge and dissolve, and about the unending divine greatness. The vocative 'lotus-eyed' tenderizes the address — gratitude precedes the daring request that follows.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Two technical terms recap what was taught: bhava-apyayau (emergence-dissolution, the cycle of manifestation handled in ch. 7-10) and avyaya māhātmya (the inexhaustible glory exhibited in the vibhūti-yoga of ch. 10). Arjuna's emphasis on 'from You' (tvattaḥ) underlines that this is direct, not borrowed, knowledge — a precondition for asking to see what he has heard.

Purport

Arjuna addresses Lord Kṛṣṇa as “lotus-eyed” (Kṛṣṇa’s eyes appear just like the petals of a lotus flower) out of his joy, for Kṛṣṇa has assured him, in a previous chapter, ahaṁ kṛtsnasya jagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayas tathā: “I am the source of the appearance and disappearance of this entire material manifestation.” Arjuna has heard of this from the Lord in detail. Arjuna further knows that in spite of His being the source of all appearances and disappearances, He is aloof from them. As the Lord has said in the Ninth Chapter, He is all-pervading, yet He is not personally present everywhere. That is the inconceivable opulence of Kṛṣṇa which Arjuna admits that he has thoroughly understood.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
2. O lotus-eyed one, in detail I have heard about the creation and dissolution of all beings arising through You, as well as how You remain unaffected as well.
Commentary

I have heard plenty about the creation and destruction of the living entities coming from You (tvattaḥ) in the middle six chapters, in such statements as ahaṁ kṛtsnasya jagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayas tathā: I am the creation and destruction of the universe. And I have also heard how You remain aloof and unaffected in spite of being the creator and destroyer (māhātmyam avyayam), in such verses as mayā tatam idaṁ sarvam: I am pervading all this universe; and na ca māṁ tāni karmāṇi nibadhnanti: these actions do not bind Me.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
2. O lotus-eyed one, many times I have heard from You about the creation and dissolution of all beings arising through You, about Your great powers, as well as how You remain unaffected.
Commentary

O louts-eyed Lord! Those two eyes are long and reddish at the tips like lotus petals, and their beauty is intensified by Your intense prema for Your devotees! I have heard from You directly (tvattaḥ) many times (vistaraśaḥ) about the creation and dissolution of all beings caused by You (tvattaḥ). I have heard about Your eternal powers (avyayam māhātymam) in such words as ahaṁ kṛtsnasya jagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayas tathā: I am the creation and destruction of the whole universe. (BG 7.6) And I have heard from You also that, though You are the creator of everything, You do not undergo change (avyayam); though You are the controller of all, You do not associate with and become affected by the controlled entities. This You have stated in such verses as mayā tatam idaṁ sarvam: I pervade the whole universe; all beings are in Me, but I am not in them. (BG 9.4)

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

In the first six chapters of Bhagavad‑gita it was described about every living entity. What is the body? What is the soul? How to remove sorrow due to grieving for what is not worthy of grief? And in the second six chapters up to this point, Arjuna heard Krsna's amazing glories. That He is the origin and dissolution of the creation of the all universe. He has heard that Krsna is inexhaustible, the ruler and all prakrti is under Him. He has heard all these things.

Personal Guidance
Arjuna acknowledges that he has heard about Kṛṣṇa's appearance and disappearance and inexhaustible glory.
Practical Applications
  • Cultivate explicit acknowledgment of what has been received in scripture.
  • Speak back what you have heard, like Arjuna recapping.
  • Don't hold teachings silently.
Reflection Questions
  • What teaching has been so quietly received that I haven't spoken it back?
  • What would speaking it back deepen?
Analogy

A pilgrim who has read every map of the mountain now asks for one glimpse of the peak. The maps were not wasted — they brought him to the trailhead — but at the trailhead the eye begins to ache for what the ear has memorised.

Stories
Brahmā in the Second Canto

In the Bhāgavatam (SB 2.9), Brahmā receives the four essential verses of the catuḥ-ślokī after long austerity. Kṛṣṇa first gives him the description — the words about His own nature — and only afterward does the form become available to his meditation.

Arjuna's request follows the same order. Hearing about the appearance and disappearance of beings precedes the wish to see; the description prepares the eye.
