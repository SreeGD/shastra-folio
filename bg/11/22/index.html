Bhagavad-gītā 11.22

रुद्रादित्या वसवो ये च साध्या विश्वेऽश्विनौ मरुतश्चोष्मपाश्च । गन्धर्वयक्षासुरसिद्धसङ्घा वीक्षन्ते त्वां विस्मिताश्चैव सर्वे ॥ २२ ॥

Important Words
  • rudra — the Rudras
    Eleven forms of Śiva. Their inclusion at the head of the list signals that the form Arjuna sees outranks even the destroyer-aspect of the cosmos.
  • sādhyāḥ — the Sādhyas
    A class of celestial beings associated with sacrificial perfection. Their presence indicates that even the deities of yajña are subordinate to the form.
  • gandharva — the Gandharvas
    Celestial musicians. Even music — the divine register most likely to imagine itself adequate to praise — has paused to behold.
  • asura — the demons
    Notably included. The Lord's display does not respect the asura/sura partition — both behold. Surrender will divide them later (11.36); here, wonder unites them.
  • vīkṣante — they behold
    Third-person plural present of vi-īkṣ, 'to look intently.' Not casual seeing — intent, fixed gaze. Every being on the list is locked onto the form.
  • vismitāḥ — astonished
    Past participle of vi-smi — overcome with wonder. The same emotional register Arjuna entered in 11.14; now he sees that emotion shared across the cosmos.
All the various manifestations of Lord Śiva, the Ādityas, the Vasus, the Sādhyas, the Viśvedevas, the two Aśvīs, the Maruts, the forefathers, the Gandharvas, the Yakṣas, the Asuras and the perfected demigods are beholding You in wonder.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
22. The Rudras, Ādityas, Vasus, Sadhyas, Viśvas, Aśvins, Maruts, Pitṛs, Gandharvas, Yakṣas, Asuras, and Siddhas all look at You in astonishment.
Commentary

Uṣma-pā refers to those who drink hot vapor of food. That refers to the Pitṛs. The śruti says ūṣmabhāgā hi pitaraḥ: the Pitṛs eat the steam. (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa, Yajur Veda, 1.3.10.6)

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
22. The Rudras, Ādityas, Vasus, Sadhyas, Viśvas, the Aśvins, Maruts, Pitṛs, Gandharvas, Yakṣas, Asuras, and Siddhas all look at You in astonishment.
Commentary

This verse is clear in meaning. Uṣmapāḥ refers to the pitṛs who drink steam (uṣma). Śruti says uṣmabhāgā hi pitaraḥ: the pitṛs take the steam. (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa, Yajur Veda, 1.3.10.6)

Concepts
Rasa
Personal Guidance
Rudras, Ādityas, Vasus, Sādhyas, Maruts, ancestors — all are awestruck, beholding You.
Practical Applications
  • Recognize the universal awe; you are not alone in being humbled.
  • Use this verse during personal humbling moments.
  • Don't perform false composure when awe is appropriate.
Reflection Questions
  • Where have I performed composure instead of feeling awe?
  • What honest awe is being asked?
Analogy

Imagine an opera in which the entire orchestra, every chorus, every soloist has gone silent and is staring at the stage. That is the moment of 11.22 — every category of celestial being, even the asuras, has stopped its function to behold one form.

Stories
The Reverence of Even the Asuras

When Lord Nṛsiṁha appeared from the pillar (SB 7.8), even the demons who served Hiraṇyakaśipu fell silent, struck with wonder. Their hostility did not dissolve — Hiraṇyakaśipu still rushed at the Lord — but the spectacle itself momentarily suspended the categories.

Arjuna's catalogue in 11.22 includes asura-siddha-saṅghāḥ alongside the devas and sages. Even the demons are vīkṣante vismitāḥ — beholding in wonder. The viśva-rūpa silences hostility long enough to be seen.
