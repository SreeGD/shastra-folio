Bhagavad-gītā 11.21

अमी हि त्वां सुरसङ्घा विशन्ति केचिद्भ‍ीता: प्राञ्जलयो गृणन्ति । स्वस्तीत्युक्त्वा महर्षिसिद्धसङ्घा: स्तुवन्ति त्वां स्तुतिभि: पुष्कलाभि: ॥ २१ ॥
amī hi tvāṁ sura-saṅghā viśanti

Important Words
  • sura - saṅghāḥ — hosts of demigods
    Compound: sura (demigod) + saṅgha (host). The devas are in groups, not isolated. Even the gods have communities — and the communities surrender together.
  • viśanti — they enter
    Third-person plural present. The verb is the chapter's terrible echo — viśanti will reappear in 11.27–29 for the warriors entering the mouths. Here, surrender is entering; there, destruction is entering.
  • prāñjalayaḥ — with folded hands
    Nominative plural of prāñjali (pra + añjali). The same gesture Arjuna made in 11.14 — Arjuna sees that his bodily response is being mirrored by the devas inside the form.
  • mahā - ṛṣi — great sages
    Compound: mahā + ṛṣi. The Saptarṣis and other great seers — the highest tier of human spiritual attainment — are also chanting praises, not analysing.
  • stutibhiḥ — with prayers
    Instrumental plural of stuti — 'praise-hymn.' The technical Vedic word for formal Sanskrit stotras. The mahā-ṛṣis are not improvising; they are reciting in the established register.
  • puṣkalābhiḥ — abundant, full
    Instrumental plural of puṣkala — 'full, complete.' The praise is fully-stocked, not a token gesture. Paired with stutibhiḥ, the phrase names the formal stotra-tradition given in its plenitude.
All the hosts of demigods are surrendering before You and entering into You. Some of them, very much afraid, are offering prayers with folded hands. Hosts of great sages and perfected beings, crying “All peace!” are praying to You by singing the Vedic hymns.
Purport

The demigods in all the planetary systems feared the terrific manifestation of the universal form and its glaring effulgence and so prayed for protection.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
21. This host of devatās enters into You. Some in fear praise You with folded hands. Shouting “Svasti!” the host of great sages and Siddhas praise You with excellent verses.
Commentary

Tvā stands for tvām.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
21. This host of devatās take shelter of You. Some in fear praise You with folded hands. Shouting “svasti!” the host of great sages and siddhas praise You with excellent verses.
Commentary

This host of devatās resort to You, take shelter of You (tvā viśanti). Among the devatās, some, being afraid, are standing at a distance with folded hands, praying “Please protect us, master!” The host of great sages and siddhas, seeing the great danger, (in order to pacify You), praise You, saying, “Let there be auspiciousness for the universe!”

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
11.21 Ami hi, those very; sura-sanghah, groups of gods, the soldiers engaged in battle-groups of gods such as the Vasus who have descended here in the form of human beings for eliminating the burden of the earth; visanti, enter-are seen to be entering; tvam, You. Bhitah, struck with fear, and unable to flee; kecit, some among them; grnanti, extol You; pranjalayah, with their palms joined. Maharsi-siddha [Siddha: A semi-divine being supposed to be of great purity and holiness, and said to be particularly characterized by eight supernatural faculties called siddhis.-V.S.A.]-sanghah, groups of great sages and perfected beings; seeing protents foroding evil, etc. as the battle became imminent; stuvanti, praise; tvam, You; puskalabhih, with elaborate, full; stutibhih, hymns; uktva, saying; 'svasti iti, May it be well!' Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
11.21 These hosts of superior Devas beholding You as the foundation of the universe, rejoice and move towards You. Among them, some in fear, on seeing Your extremely terrible and wonderful form, 'extol,' namely pronounce sentences in the form of praise, according to their knowledge. Others, the bands of seers and Siddhas, knowers of the truth, higher and lower, saying 'Hail,' glorify You in hymns of abounding praise which are suitable to the Lord.

Personal Guidance
All the demigods are surrendering to You; some, fearful, fold hands and offer prayers — the great sages chant Your glory.
Practical Applications
  • Recognize that even demigods surrender to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Trust the testimony of the highest beings.
  • Don't think your skepticism is more sophisticated than their surrender.
Reflection Questions
  • Where is my sophistication actually delusion?
  • What does demigod-level surrender teach?
Analogy

When the king himself enters the throne room, even his ministers and generals fold their hands. Some are afraid, some recite the formal greeting, but no one stands as a peer. The devas, full of their own power, become petitioners when the Lord's full form is exposed.

Stories
The Demigods at the Churning of the Ocean

When the halāhala poison rose from the ocean during the churning (SB 8.6–7), the demigods, terrified, fled to Lord Śiva — and to Lord Viṣṇu through Brahmā — folding their hands and chanting prayers (stutibhiḥ puṣkalābhiḥ). The Bhāgavatam records the great sages and siddhas joining the chorus.

This is the exact tableau Arjuna sees in 11.21: sura-saṅghāḥ entering, kecit bhītāḥ prāñjalayaḥ, mahā-ṛṣi-siddha-saṅghāḥ chanting svasti. The viśva-rūpa scene contains, in one frame, every such moment in cosmic history.
