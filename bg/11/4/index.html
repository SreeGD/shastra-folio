Important Words
  • manyase — You think
    Second-person singular present middle of man. Arjuna places the decision squarely with Kṛṣṇa. The grammar is the practice: surrender means letting the Lord judge one's capacity.
  • yadi — if
    A conditional that quietly admits the request may be refused. Real petitionary prayer always carries this 'if' — it asks without demanding.
  • śakyam — able
    Verbal adjective of śak. Not a claim of strength but an open question about it. Arjuna does not say 'I am capable'; he asks whether he is.
  • prabho — O my Lord
    Vocative of prabhu — the master who has authority. Arjuna addresses precisely the function he is invoking: the authority to grant or withhold darśana.
  • yoga - īśvara — O master of all mystic power
    The viśva-rūpa will be a display of yoga (mystic potency) — Arjuna names this title because only the yoga-īśvara can show what he is asking to see.
  • avyayam — inexhaustible
    Same word as 11.2 — Arjuna asks specifically for the imperishable Self, not a transient vision. The repetition signals that hearing has produced precise desire.
If You think that I am able to behold Your cosmic form, O my Lord, O master of all mystic power, then kindly show me that unlimited universal Self.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Arjuna hedges his bold request with humility: 'if You judge me capable.' He calls Kṛṣṇa yoga-īśvara — the one who alone decides what mystic vision a soul can bear — and waits for permission rather than demanding the showing.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The conditional 'manyase yadi' is the verse's hinge: revelation depends not on the seeker's eagerness but on the Lord's estimate of his fitness. The title yogeśvara prepares the technical sense of yoga as the Lord's power to manifest forms (answered in 11.8's 'paśya me yogam aiśvaram'). 'Avyaya' (imperishable) is a deliberate request — Arjuna wants not a trick but the true Self.

Purport

It is said that one can neither see, hear, understand nor perceive the Supreme Lord, Kṛṣṇa, by the material senses. But if one is engaged in loving transcendental service to the Lord from the beginning, then one can see the Lord by revelation. Every living entity is only a spiritual spark; therefore it is not possible to see or to understand the Supreme Lord. Arjuna, as a devotee, does not depend on his speculative strength; rather, he admits his limitations as a living entity and acknowledges Kṛṣṇa’s inestimable position. Arjuna could understand that for a living entity it is not possible to understand the unlimited infinite. If the infinite reveals Himself, then it is possible to understand the nature of the infinite by the grace of the infinite. The word yogeśvara is also very significant here because the Lord has inconceivable power. If He likes, He can reveal Himself by His grace, although He is unlimited. Therefore Arjuna pleads for the inconceivable grace of Kṛṣṇa. He does not give Kṛṣṇa orders. Kṛṣṇa is not obliged to reveal Himself unless one surrenders fully in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and engages in devotional service. Thus it is not possible for persons who depend on the strength of their mental speculations to see Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
4. If You think that I am qualified to see that form, O master, O Lord of qualification,
Commentary

then show to me that indestructible form. The word yogeśvara indicates that though Arjuna is unqualified (ayogya), the Lord with His power of qualification (yoga), can make him qualified to see that form.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
4. If You think that I am qualified to see that form, O master, O Lord of qualification,
Commentary

then show to me that indestructible form. In this verse, Arjuna receives permission from the Lord to see that form of power. If You think, if You know, if You desire, that I am qualified to see that form, O master of all (prabho), show that indestructible form to me. O lord of qualification (yogeśvara), if I am not qualified (ayogya) to see You, Your śakti will make me qualified.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Srila Prabhupada explains in his Purport: "Every living entity is only a spiritual spark; therefore it is not possible to see or to understand the Supreme Lord. Arjuna, as a devotee, does not depend on his speculative strength; rather, he admits his limitations as a living entity and acknowledges Krsna's inestimable position. Arjuna could understand that for a l entity it is not possible to understand the unlimited infinite. If the infinite reveals Himself, then it is possible to understand the nature of the infinite by the grace of the infinite. The word yogesvara is also very significant here because the Lord has inconceivable power. If He likes, He can reveal Himself by His grace, although He is unlimited. Therefore Arjuna pleads for the inconceivable grace of Krsna. He does not give Krsna orders. Krsna is not obliged to reveal Himself unless one surrenders fully in Krsna consciousness and engages in devotional service. Thus it is not possible for persons who depend on the strength of their mental speculations to see Krsna." Arjuna knows that Krsna is not obliged to show His universal form, that's why he says: "If You think that I am qualified, then show me". Now Arjuna will be able to see the opulences which Krsna has previously spoken of.

Personal Guidance
If You think it possible for me to behold it, then please show — Arjuna leaves the decision to Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Voice your request, then leave the decision to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Don't try to manipulate the answer.
  • Practice the posture: ask, then trust His judgment.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I trying to manipulate Kṛṣṇa's response?
  • What does pure asking-without-pressure look like?
Analogy

A student asks the teacher for the highest text only after the teacher has agreed he is ready. Arjuna does not assume his own qualification — he hands the verdict back to Kṛṣṇa, the way an honest patient hands the prognosis back to the physician.

Stories
Uddhava at Kṛṣṇa's departure

When Kṛṣṇa was preparing to leave the world, Uddhava clasped His feet and asked to be given the highest knowledge before parting (Bhāgavatam 11.6–7). He did not presume; he placed the request and let the Lord judge what he could carry. Kṛṣṇa then spoke the long Uddhava-gītā, opening one mystery after another because the disciple had asked in the right mood.

Arjuna's manyase yadi tat śakyam — 'if You think me capable' — is in this lineage. The disciple asks; Bhagavān decides what the disciple can hold.
