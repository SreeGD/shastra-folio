Bhagavad-gītā 11.9

Important Words
  • mahā - yoga - īśvaraḥ — the master of all mystic power
    Compound: great + yoga + lord. Sañjaya stacks the title to forestall any reduction. The being now displaying the universal form is not a yogi who has accumulated siddhi; He is the Lord of yoga itself.
  • hariḥ — the Personality of Godhead
    A Bhāgavata-tradition name. Hari is 'he who removes' — sins, attachments, illusions. Sañjaya quietly invokes the name of removal at the moment Arjuna's last illusions are about to be removed by sight.
  • darśayām āsa — displayed
    Causative perfect of dṛś — 'caused to be seen.' Kṛṣṇa is the agent of the seeing; the form does not impose itself, it is shown.
  • pārthāya — to Arjuna
    Dative — for the benefit of Pārtha. The vision is given to a specific person at a specific moment. Kṛṣṇa does not broadcast; He addresses.
  • paramam — supreme
    Adjective qualifying rūpam. Not one form among many — the highest of the aiśvarya-forms, the one Arjuna asked for in verse 3.
Sañjaya said: O King, having spoken thus, the Supreme Lord of all mystic power, the Personality of Godhead, displayed His universal form to Arjuna.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Sañjaya re-enters as narrator, addressing Dhṛtarāṣṭra. He calls Kṛṣṇa Hari — 'the one who takes away' — and mahā-yogeśvara: the act of displaying the cosmic form is itself the supreme yogic feat.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The narration-frame matters: ch. 11 is technically Sañjaya's report, granted divine sight by Vyāsa. His vocative 'rājan' addresses the blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra, who cannot see the form even by report. The name 'Hari' — beyond the friend-name 'Kṛṣṇa' — anticipates the awe-tone of what is about to be shown.

Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
9. Saṣjaya said: O King, having spoken thus, Kṛṣṇa, the master of great yoga, showed to Arjuna His supreme, majestic form.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
9. Saṣjaya said: O King, having spoken thus, Kṛṣṇa, the master of great yoga, showed to Arjuna the supremely powerful form.
Commentary

Having spoken, the Lord showed the universal form to Arjuna. Seeing that form, Arjuna described it to the Lord. Saṣjaya relates what Arjuna said in six verses (9-14). Then (tataḥ), after giving Arjuna the celestial eyes, O Dhṛtarāṣṭra (rajan), the Lord, the great master of yoga, showed that supreme form of power to Arjuna.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Now, at this point, Sanjaya is describing what Arjuna saw.

Personal Guidance
Sañjaya describes: having said this, the great Lord, the master of all yogic mysteries, displayed His universal form to Arjuna.
Practical Applications
  • Receive Sañjaya's narration as the lens through which we still see this scene.
  • Honor the messenger who relays the scene.
  • Don't skip narrators in your reading.
Reflection Questions
  • What narrators of the spiritual scene am I ignoring?
  • What does honoring them open?
Analogy

When the curtain rises, the narrator's voice fills in what no actor can show: who is speaking, in what register, to whom. Sañjaya is that narrator, and his single sentence — evam uktvā, having spoken thus — frames the entire vision that follows.

Stories
Sañjaya's Reporting Voice

Throughout the Bhagavad-gītā, Sañjaya appears only at hinge moments — opening verse, the divya-cakṣus granted at the start, and again here at the unveiling of the universal form (Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma-parva).

His voice in 11.9 — mahā-yogeśvaro hariḥ — is doing theological work. He names Kṛṣṇa as both Hari and the great Lord of yoga, telling Dhṛtarāṣṭra (and us) the identity of the speaker before the form is described. Without this introduction, the wonders that follow could be misread as deva-līlā.
