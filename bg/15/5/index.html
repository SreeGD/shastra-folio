Bhagavad-gītā 15.5

Important Words
  • māna — false prestige
    The first thing the verse names — and the first thing renunciants underestimate. A sannyāsī can shed wealth and family and still bear the heavier load of being known as a sannyāsī. Māna outlives almost every other attachment.
  • saṅga — association
    The hinge word of the chapter's earlier verses returns here. Not all saṅga, but saṅga-doṣāḥ — the *fault* of company — must be conquered. The seeker is not asked to become inhuman, only to be done with the wrong companions.
  • kāmāḥ — from lust
    Vinivṛtta-kāmāḥ — 'with lusts turned all the way back.' Not suppressed, not redirected: turned around so completely they no longer face outward. The grammar describes a 180-degree rotation of the desiring faculty.
  • dvandvaiḥ — from the dualities
    The pairs — heat and cold, praise and blame, gain and loss. The seeker is liberated *from* them (vimuktāḥ), not equalized within them. The duality itself loses its grip on his nervous system.
  • amūḍhāḥ — unbewildered
    A-mūḍha — not stupefied. The list's climax: even after the four prior conditions are met, the soul must still see clearly. Many renunciants achieve detachment and then sit confused; this verse forbids that outcome.
  • padam — situation
    Padam avyayam — 'the imperishable abode.' The same word reappears from 15.3-4. Kṛṣṇa is now naming the destination he earlier asked Arjuna to search for, and listing exactly who walks in.
Those who are free from false prestige, illusion and false association, who understand the eternal, who are done with material lust, who are freed from the dualities of happiness and distress, and who, unbewildered, know how to surrender unto the Supreme Person attain to that eternal kingdom.
Purport

The surrendering process is described here very nicely. The first qualification is that one should not be deluded by pride. Because the conditioned soul is puffed up, thinking himself the lord of material nature, it is very difficult for him to surrender unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead. One should know by the cultivation of real knowledge that he is not lord of material nature; the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the Lord. When one is free from delusion caused by pride, he can begin the process of surrender. For one who is always expecting some honor in this material world, it is not possible to surrender to the Supreme Person. Pride is due to illusion, for although one comes here, stays for a brief time and then goes away, he has the foolish notion that he is the lord of the world. He thus makes all things complicated, and he is always in trouble. The whole world moves under this impression. People are considering the land, this earth, to belong to human society, and they have divided the land under the false impression that they are the proprietors. One has to get out of this false notion that human society is the proprietor of this world. When one is freed from such a false notion, he becomes free from all the false associations caused by familial, social and national affections. These faulty associations bind one to this material world. After this stage, one has to develop spiritual knowledge. One has to cultivate knowledge of what is actually his own and what is actually not his own. And when one has an understanding of things as they are, he becomes free from all dual conceptions such as happiness and distress, pleasure and pain. He becomes full in knowledge; then it is possible for him to surrender to the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
5. Those who are free from the delusion of pride, who are devoid of attachment, who are absorbed in reflecting on Paramātmā, who have renounced material desire, who are free of dualities of happiness and distress, and who are without bewilderment, attain that indestructible goal.
Commentary

Performing bhakti, what qualities do the people attain, so they can attain that goal? This verse explains. Adhyātma-nitya means “those who make a rule of constantly considering about adhyātma; those who are absorbed in reflecting on Paramātmā.”

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
5. Those who are free from the delusion of pride, devoid of attachment, who are absorbed in reflecting on paramātmā, who have renounced material desire, who are free of dualities of happiness and distress, and who are without bewilderment, attain that indestructible goal.
Commentary

What type of people attain the goal, having surrendered to the Lord? They should be without pride (nirmāna) which is generated from respectful treatment. They should be free of absorption in what is false (nirmoha). They should be free of affection for wife and others (jita saṅga doṣā). They should daily consider the nature of their own ātmā and paramātmā (adhyātma nityā). (They should be free of material desires.) They should be tolerant of or free of disturbance (vimuktāḥ) from opposites such as heat and cold which are the cause of the dualities of happiness and distress. They should be knowledgeable of the process of surrender (amūḍhāḥ).

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
15.5 Thus, when they have taken refute in Me, become free from 'perverse notions conerning the self', namely, become free from the delusion in the form of misconceiving the non-self (body) as the self; 'victorious over the evil of attachment', namely, victorious over the evil known as attachment to sense-objects consisting of the Gunas; 'ever devoted to self', namely completely absorbed in the knowledge of the self which is called Adhyatma or knowledge about the self; when they have 'turned away from desires' other than this self-knowledge; when they are liberated from 'dualities called pleasure and pain' - such 'undeluded souls', namely, those who are able to discern the natures of self and non-self, attain to that 'imperishable status'. They attain the self as It is, in the form of infinite knowledge. Conseently for those who seek refuge in Me, all actions become easy of performance till perfection is attained by My grace.

Personal Guidance
Free from pride and illusion, conquered the evil of association, established in eternal Self, free from desire, equal in dualities — such persons attain the eternal abode.
Practical Applications
  • Audit five conditions: no false prestige, no illusion, conquered bad association, no lust, equal in dualities.
  • Address the weakest condition.
  • Treat the list as cumulative, not optional.
Reflection Questions
  • Which condition is weakest in me?
  • What single discipline addresses it?
Analogy

A weary traveler, finally arrived at the gates of an eternal city, is asked to set down what he carries before entering. He empties his pockets — pride, illusion, the friends who pulled him aside, the cravings he could not name, the chronic ledger of pleasure and pain. Each thing put down is a clasp loosened. The gate opens only to those whose hands are empty enough to fold them.

Stories
Dhruva at the moment of departure

When the Lord's airplane came to take Dhruva to Vaikuṇṭha, his old fixations had no further grip. The boy who once climbed onto his stepmother's lap and was pushed off, who once wanted a kingdom greater than his father's, stood by the chariot of Viṣṇu purified of every one of those demands.

He placed his foot on his deceased mother's body in remembrance, then stepped into the airplane that goes to the place of no return — māna, moha, saṅga, kāma all set down at the threshold.

The Bhāgavatam's report of his ascent is a direct illustration of this verse: the eternal abode is entered, in literal procession, by one in whom the conditions Kṛṣṇa lists here had been met.
