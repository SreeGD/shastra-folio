Important Words
  • bījam — seed
    The verse's central image — Kṛṣṇa as the bīja, the originating seed of every being. Anticipates 14.4 ('I am the seed-giving father') and 7.10 ('I am the eternal seed of all beings'). A recurring Gītā metaphor.
  • vinā — without
    'Apart from, in the absence of.' The verse's logical pivot: 'there is no being that exists apart from Me.' Closes every conceivable exception clause. Universal range — moving and non-moving alike.
  • bhūtam — created being
    From bhū — 'to be.' The general word for any existing entity. Combined with cara-acaram (moving and non-moving), the verse covers literally everything that exists in any mode.
  • cara - acaram — moving and nonmoving
    Compound: cara (moving) + acara (not moving). Standard Vedic exhaustive pair — animals and plants, beings and stones. The verse closes both halves of existence under Kṛṣṇa's seed-authorship.
Furthermore, O Arjuna, I am the generating seed of all existences. There is no being – moving or nonmoving – that can exist without Me.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Kṛṣṇa now condenses the catalogue: He is the seed of every being whatsoever, and no creature — animate or inanimate — exists apart from Him. The opulences listed are illustrative; the principle is universal.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Bīja (seed) compresses Sāṅkhya-style causality into one image. The negative formulation (na tad asti vinā mayā) matches Upaniṣadic style: cf. Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.7.3 onwards on the antaryāmin as the inner controller of all. Cross-ref BG 7.10 (bījaṁ māṁ sarva-bhūtānāṁ) and 9.18.

Purport

Everything has a cause, and that cause or seed of manifestation is Kṛṣṇa. Without Kṛṣṇa’s energy, nothing can exist; therefore He is called omnipotent. Without His potency, neither the movable nor the immovable can exist. Whatever existence is not founded on the energy of Kṛṣṇa is called māyā, “that which is not.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
39. I am that seed which causes all living entities to manifest, O Arjuna. No moving or non-moving entities can exist without Me.
Commentary

I am the seed of all beings, the cause of their sprouting, because without Me, moving and non-moving creatures would not exist. This means they would be false things.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
39. I am that seed which causes all living entities to manifest, O Arjuna. No moving or non-moving entities can exist without Me.
Commentary

I am the cause of all entities coming into being. I am the seed of all entities. The reason is given. Whatever moving or non-moving entities exist (bhūtam), which are real, would not exist, or would be false, without Me, the Supreme Lord, possessor of all powers.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Krsna is explaining how one can actually think about Him and meditate on Him and see Him everywhere. Because nothing moving or non‑moving can exist without Krsna. Anything we see around us is dependent on Krsna and cannot exist without Him.

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
10.39 Ca, moreover; O Arjuna, yat api, whatsoever; is the bijam, seed, the source of growth ; sarva-bhutanam, of all beings; tat, that I am. As a conclusion of the topic the Lord states in brief His divine manifestations: Na tat asti bhutam, there is no thing; cara-acaram, moving or non-moving; yat, which; syat, can exist; vina maya, without Me. For whatever is rejected by Me, from whatever I withdraw Myself will have no substance, and will become a non-entity. Hence the meaning is that everything has Me as its essence.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
10.39 Of all beings, in whatever condition they may exist, whether manifest or not, I alone am that state. Whatever host of beings are said to exist, they do not exist without Me as their Self. In the statement, 'Nothing that moves or does not move exists without Me', it is taught that the Lord exists as the Self, as said in the beginning: 'I am the Self, seated in the hearts of all beings' (10.20). The purport is that the entire host of beings in every state, is united with Me, their Self. By this He makes it clear that He, being the Self of all things, is the ground for His being denoted by everything in co-ordinate predication.

Personal Guidance
I am the seed of all existences — there is no being, moving or unmoving, that can exist without Me.
Practical Applications
  • Recognize Him as the seed of all being.
  • Look at any being and see the seed.
  • Use this seeing as portable contemplation.
Reflection Questions
  • Where did I last see the seed in a being?
  • Where am I missing it?
Analogy

Every grain you have ever seen — wheat, rice, mango stone, mustard — was once a seed. Behind every seed, another seed. Trace the regress and Kṛṣṇa says: I am that ultimate generating seed of every existence, moving or still. Nothing exists that is not seeded by Me.
