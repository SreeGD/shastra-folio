Important Words
  • pavanaḥ — the wind
    From the root pū — 'to purify.' The wind purifies by moving — clearing air, carrying away staleness. Kṛṣṇa picks the natural purifier-in-motion as the chief of purifying agents.
  • rāmaḥ — Rāma
    Among śastra-bhṛts (weapon-bearers), Rāma is supreme. The avatāra-king who killed Rāvaṇa with the bow. Kṛṣṇa names His own previous-yuga form here — a subtle internal cross-reference.
  • makaraḥ — the shark, the great sea-creature
    Among jhaṣāḥ (fish, aquatic creatures), the makara is the largest, the predator who rules the depths. Also the vehicle of Varuṇa and the symbol on Kandarpa's banner. Kṛṣṇa picks the apex of the aquatic order.
  • jāhnavī — the river Ganges
    Named for the sage Jahnu, who once drank her and released her from his ear. Among rivers, the supreme — purifier of the dead, descended by Bhagīratha's austerity. Kṛṣṇa's river-vibhūti is the river that washes karmic load.
Of purifiers I am the wind, of the wielders of weapons I am Rāma, of fishes I am the shark, and of flowing rivers I am the Ganges.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Wind among purifiers, Rāma among weapon-bearers, makara among fishes, Gaṅgā among rivers. Kṛṣṇa picks instances of irresistible motion and sanctifying flow — purifying air and sacred water flank martial perfection.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Rāma here is Dāśarathi Rāma, the warrior-king of Ayodhyā. Jāhnavī (Gaṅgā) is named via the patronymic from Jahnu, evoking the descent-myth (gaṅgāvataraṇa). The Makara is both fish-lord and Varuṇa's mount. Together these four highlight the divine inside flowing, cleansing, and decisively acting agencies — cross-ref BG 11.39 (vāyur yamo'gniḥ).

Purport

Of all the aquatics the shark is one of the biggest and is certainly the most dangerous to man. Thus the shark represents Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
31. Among purifiers and the swift, I am the wind. Among holders of weapons, I am Paraśurāma. Among the fishes, I am the makara. Among rivers, I am the Gaṅgā.
Commentary

Of moving or purifying agents, I am the wind. Among bearers of weapons, I am Paraśurāma. He is suitable as a vibhūti because He is an aveśāvatāra, and because He is an outstanding jīva among those who have been given powers by the Lord. [Note: Rāma is taken as Paraśurāma rather than Rāmacandra, since he is a jīva . A jīva can be a vibhūti, but not the Lord Himself.] Padma Purāṇa, quoted in Bhagavatāmṛta says “O devi, I have recited to you the story of Paraśurāma, a śaktyaveśa avatāra of the Lord.” Also it says, “He took birth as an empowered jīva.” The characteristics of the aveśāvatāra are also mentioned in the Bhagavatāmṛta. “Where a jīva is filled with portions of jṣāna-śakti or other śakti of the Lord, he is called aveśāvatāra.” (Laghu Bhagavatāmṛta 1.4 .39, 1.4.38, 1.1.18) Among fish (jhaṣāṇām), I am the makara, a special type of fish. Among rivers (srotasām), I am the Gaṅgā.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
31. Among purifiers and the swift, I am the wind. Among holders of weapons, I am Paraśurāma. Among the fishes, I am the makara. Among rivers, I am the Gaṅgā.
Commentary

Among purifiers or among swift things, I am the wind. I am Paraśurāma (rāmaḥ) among wielders of weapons. Among fish I am the makara, a special variety of fish. Among flowing waters, I am the Gaṅgā.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

Śrīla Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa comments that the Rāma referred to here is Paraśurāma because

Kṛṣṇa is explaining His vibhūtis, His opulences, not His incarnations. Paraśurāma is a living entity

empowered (śakty-āveśa) to wield weapons.

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
10.31 Of moving things, namely, of things whose nature is to move, I am the wind. Of those who bear weapons, I am Rama. Here the ality of bearing weapons is the Vibhuti, as no other sense is possible. Aditya etc., being individual selves, constitute attributes of the Lord, who is their Self as they constitute His body. Therefore they stand in the same position of the attribute as that of bearing weapons.

Personal Guidance
Of purifiers I am the wind; of warriors I am Rāma; of fishes I am the shark; of flowing rivers I am the Ganges.
Practical Applications
  • Bathe in or remember the Ganges as a purification practice.
  • Honor Rāma as warrior-Lord, not just gentle prince.
  • Recognize the wind, rivers, and creatures as specific Kṛṣṇa-presences.
Reflection Questions
  • When did I last connect with sacred water?
  • What practice could renew that?
Analogy

Among purifiers, the wind that carries fragrance and clears stagnation; among warriors, the avatāra who teaches dharma with the bow; among fish, the predator who masters the depths; among rivers, the one whose touch washes lifetimes. Kṛṣṇa picks the agents that move, cleanse, and carry across.

Stories
Rāma's vow of one word, one wife, one arrow

Rāma, named here as the chief among wielders of weapons, was renowned not only for his archery but for his single-pointed dharma. One word given was kept; one wife alone was married; one arrow drawn was enough.

When Rāvaṇa abducted Sītā, Rāma did not unleash mass destruction — he assembled an army of monkeys, observed every protocol of war, and finally killed Rāvaṇa with one well-placed shaft. Kṛṣṇa names Rāma as His weapon-bearer-vibhūti because among śastra-bhṛts, none combined skill and restraint so completely.

Bhagīratha bringing the Ganges

King Bhagīratha did austerities for years so that the heavenly Ganges would descend and purify his ancestors' ashes. Ganges agreed but warned her fall would shatter the earth. Śiva caught her in his hair; she emerged as a softer stream and reached the plains.

Kṛṣṇa names her here as His river-vibhūti — Jāhnavī, named for the sage Jahnu who later swallowed and re-released her. The whole story is one of purification through cosmic cooperation. The verse's word pavatām — 'of purifiers' — applies equally to the wind (named in this verse) and to her.
