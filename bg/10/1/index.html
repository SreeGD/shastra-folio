Important Words
  • bhūyaḥ — again
    The opening note of the whole chapter. Kṛṣṇa has already spoken nine chapters; 'again' is not redundancy but mercy. The Gītā's central truths must be heard repeatedly because the conditioned ear is leaky.
  • paramam — supreme
    What follows is not one more topic among many. 'Paramam vacaḥ' — 'the supreme word' — flags that chapters 10 and 11 carry the highest knowledge yet given: the Person behind every excellence.
  • prīyamāṇāya — thinking you dear to Me
    A present participle, dative case. The reason for the speech is not Arjuna's qualification but Kṛṣṇa's affection. Confidential teaching flows from love, not from merit-checking.
  • hita - kāmyayā — for your benefit
    Compound: hita (welfare) + kāmyayā (with desire for). Kṛṣṇa is not displaying knowledge; He is doing welfare-work on a friend. Every word is for Arjuna's good, not for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure of speaking.
  • mahā - bāho — O mighty-armed
    Vocative. The address is not flattery — it reminds the warrior that this transcendent knowledge is being given to one who must still fight a war. Vibhūti-yoga is for actors in the world, not for renunciates in caves.
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Listen again, O mighty-armed Arjuna. Because you are My dear friend, for your benefit I shall speak to you further, giving knowledge that is better than what I have already explained.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Kṛṣṇa opens Chapter 10 by promising Arjuna a fresh, deeper revelation. He frames the disclosure as a gift of affection — given precisely because Arjuna listens with delight rather than doubt. Reception conditions the depth of teaching.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The chapter on Vibhūti-yoga is introduced with the keyword bhūyaḥ ('once more'), signalling a renewal of confidential instruction that began in Chapters 7 and 9. Kṛṣṇa specifies two prerequisites built into the verse: Arjuna's affectionate attentiveness (prīyamāṇāya) and Kṛṣṇa's own desire for his welfare (hita-kāmyayā). Together these make the speaker–hearer relationship a vessel for opulence-knowledge.

Purport

The word bhagavān is explained thus by Parāśara Muni: one who is full in six opulences, who has full strength, full fame, wealth, knowledge, beauty and renunciation, is Bhagavān, or the Supreme Personality of Godhead. While Kṛṣṇa was present on this earth, He displayed all six opulences. Therefore great sages like Parāśara Muni have all accepted Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Now Kṛṣṇa is instructing Arjuna in more confidential knowledge of His opulences and His work. Previously, beginning with the Seventh Chapter, the Lord has already explained His different energies and how they are acting. Now in this chapter He explains His specific opulences to Arjuna. In the previous chapter He has clearly explained His different energies to establish devotion in firm conviction. Again in this chapter He tells Arjuna about His manifestations and various opulences. The more one hears about the Supreme God, the more one becomes fixed in devotional service. One should always hear about the Lord in the association of devotees; that will enhance one’s devotional service. Discourses in the society of devotees can take place only among those who are really anxious to be in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Others cannot take part in such discourses. The Lord clearly tells Arjuna that because Arjuna is very dear to Him, for his benefit such discourses are taking place.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
1. The Lord said: Again, O Mighty-armed one, hear My supreme teachings, which I will speak to you, who are qualified with prema, because I desire to give you benefit.
Commentary

Starting from the seventh chapter, after revealing His powers, Kṛṣṇa spoke of bhakti. Now in the tenth chapter, He speaks of the confidential elements of bhakti along with a description of His vibhūtis. By knowing the powers of the Lord, one understands that the Lord is worthy of worship. These powers have been described starting from the seventh chapter. Now the Lord will explain the powers in detail for the pleasure of those who have devotion. According to the rule that the ṛṣis speak indirectly and the Lord is fond of such indirect statements, and thus, because this knowledge is somewhat difficult to comprehend, the Lord speaks again (bhūyaḥ). parokṣa-vādā ṛṣayaḥ parokṣaṁ ca mama priyam The Vedic seers and mantras deal in esoteric terms, and I also am pleased by such confidential descriptions. SB 11.21.35 “Again (bhūyaḥ), hear My words.” This means, “Again I will speak about the king of knowledge, the king of secrets.” “O Mighty-armed one, you display your supreme power of your arms. This power is greater than that of all others. In the same way, you should display the supreme power of your intelligence in this topic. Listen to the words I speak.” This implies that Arjuna should limit his attention to what is spoken, though he is already listening. “These words are even superior to what I have spoken previously (paramaṁ vacaḥ). I speak these words to you, in order to cause you astonishment.” Use of the dative case (te) with a verb (vakṣyāmi) indicates a suppressed infinitive of purpose, by the rule kriyārthopapadasya ca. (Aṣṭādhyāyī, Pāṇini 2.3.14) [Note: Use of te (dative case) with the verb vakṣyāmi indicates there is a hidden infinite which should be understood. Thus, aham te vakṣyāmi, instead of simply meaning “I speak to you” means “I speak to you in order to amaze you, impress you, please you etc.”] The reason for speaking to Arjuna is then given: “I will speak to you because you are filled with prema (priyamāṇāya).”

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
1. The Lord said: Again, O Mighty-armed one, hear My supreme teachings, which I will speak in order to astonish you, desiring to give you benefit.
Commentary

The Lord’s aiśvarya, a cause of bhakti, which was discussed in the seventh, eighth and ninth chapters, is further supported in the tenth chapter with a description of the Lord’s vibhūtis. In the previous several chapters bhakti has been described along with intermittent descriptions of the Lord’s powers. Now in this chapter, in order to manifest and increase that bhakti, the Lord will describe in detail His extraordinary vibhūtis which were previously briefly discussed. [Note: These were discussed in chapter 7.8-12 and chapter 9.16-19.] “O Mighty-armed Arjuna! Again (bhūya) hear My auspicious (paramam) words.” Kṛṣṇa commands Arjuna, who is already hearing, to listen, in order to make him alert to what will be taught. “With a desire for benefiting you, I will speak to you words concerning My transcendental powers or vibhūtis.” Dative case (te—to you) is used according to the rule kriyārthopapadasya karmaṇi sthāninaḥ (Pāṇini 2.3.14) to denote the object of a missing verb element. Thus the actual meaning is “I will speak knowledge to astonish you, who are already knowledgeable, who will take pleasure in My words (prīyamānāya) as if drinking nectar. I will speak with a desire for your welfare in the form of producing and increasing your devotion for Me (hita kāmyayā).”

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

Kṛṣṇa has just established that devotion to Him is the goal of life, and He now discusses His opu-

lences to increase Arjuna’s devotion. How does knowing Kṛṣṇa’s opulences increase one’s devo-

tion? Imagine discovering that an intimate friend is really a famous billionaire. He is so powerful that

when he snaps his fingers, people line up in their eagerness to serve him. We then realize, “What a

wonderful friend we have!” Our understanding his opulence and power has naturally enhanced our

appreciation of him. We feel fortunate to be friends with someone so great. Similarly, when a

devotee understands Kṛṣṇa’s greatness in detail, he feels increased gratitude for his good fortune of

being able to reciprocate intimately with such a great and powerful Lord.

Kṛṣṇa’s opulence is simultaneously knowable and unknowable. Is this a contradiction? No, because

we can know the unknowable to some degree, not through our own limited abilities, but when

Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself to us. Even a fraction of knowledge about Kṛṣṇa’s otherwise unknowable po-

sition inspires us to serve Him.

Personal Guidance
Hear again My supreme word, which I speak for your benefit, dear friend — knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is repeated for those who can receive it.
Practical Applications
  • Welcome repetition as kindness, not redundancy.
  • Receive the same teaching twice with greater openness.
  • Don't dismiss familiar verses; receive them fresh.
Reflection Questions
  • What familiar verse have I stopped really hearing?
  • What would fresh hearing reveal?
Analogy

A father teaches his child the same lesson many times, not because the child is dull but because love repeats. Kṛṣṇa says 'bhūyaḥ' — 'again' — and the word itself is the affection. The same nectar is poured a second time so the cup, which leaked the first, may catch a drop more.

Stories
Nārada returns to Vyāsa

After Vyāsa compiled the four Vedas, the Mahābhārata, and the Vedānta-sūtra, he sat by the Sarasvatī still uneasy. Nārada arrived and told him plainly: you have not yet spoken of the Lord's pastimes — repeat the teaching, this time with Kṛṣṇa at the center.

Out of that loving repetition came the Bhāgavatam. Vyāsa did not resent being told to say it again; he received the instruction as kindness, and the world received its sweetest scripture. Kṛṣṇa's 'bhūyaḥ' to Arjuna belongs to the same lineage of merciful repetition.
