Important Words
  • aśvatthaḥ — the banyan tree
    The pippala / aśvattha — sacred to many traditions, central to chapter 15's metaphor of the cosmic tree. Long-lived, multi-trunked, shading. Kṛṣṇa picks the tree whose form will recur as scripture's most extended metaphor.
  • nāradaḥ — Nārada
    The devarṣi — sage among the demigods. Wanderer, vīṇā-player, kīrtana-maker. Kṛṣṇa picks the sage who never sits still, the one whose whole mode of existence is glorification.
  • citrarathaḥ — Citraratha
    Chief of the Gandharvas — celestial musicians. His name means 'one of the wonderful chariot.' Music and motion combined. Kṛṣṇa picks the foremost singer of the heavenly realms.
  • kapilaḥ muniḥ — Kapila Muni
    The avatāra-form of Viṣṇu who taught Sāṅkhya to his mother Devahūti. Note: the Vaiṣṇava Kapila of the Bhāgavatam, not the later atheistic compiler. Kṛṣṇa identifies with the original, devotional Sāṅkhya teacher.
Of all trees I am the banyan tree, and of the sages among the demigods I am Nārada. Of the Gandharvas I am Citraratha, and among perfected beings I am the sage Kapila.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
The chosen tree is the cosmic aśvattha; the chosen divine sage is Nārada the travelling teacher; the chosen Gandharva is Citraratha; the chosen siddha is Kapila — founder of Sāṅkhya. Each pick is iconic of its class.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Aśvattha returns explicitly in BG 15.1 as the symbol of saṁsāra. Nārada will reappear named in 10.13 (already invoked) and at 11.37. Kapila here is the divine ṛṣi-author of Sāṅkhya — Vaiṣṇava tradition identifies him as Kṛṣṇa's own avatāra (Bhāgavatam 3.24). Cross-ref BG 2.39 where Sāṅkhya was already named as a precursor system.

Purport

The banyan tree ( aśvattha ) is one of the highest and most beautiful trees, and people in India often worship it as one of their daily morning rituals. Amongst the demigods they also worship Nārada, who is considered the greatest devotee in the universe. Thus he is the representation of Kṛṣṇa as a devotee. The Gandharva planet is filled with entities who sing beautifully, and among them the best singer is Citraratha. Amongst the perfect living entities, Kapila, the son of Devahūti, is a representative of Kṛṣṇa. He is considered an incarnation of Kṛṣṇa, and His philosophy is mentioned in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Later on another Kapila became famous, but his philosophy was atheistic. Thus there is a gulf of difference between them.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
26. Among the trees, I am the pippala. [Note: This tree is regards as a representative of Viṣṇu.] Among the divine sages, I am Nārada. Among the Gandharvas, I am Citraratha. Among perfected beings, I am Kapila.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
26. Among the trees, I am the pippala. [Note: This tree is regards as a representative of Viṣṇu.] Among the divine sages, I am Nārada. Among the gandharvas, I am Citraratha. Among perfected beings, I am Kapila.
Commentary

Among all trees worthy of worship, I am the excellent pippala tree. Among divine sages, I am Narada, outstanding because of his outstanding level of bhakti. Among Gandharvas, I am Citraratha, outstanding because of his excellent singing ability. Among the Siddhas, who have natural mystic powers like becoming small, I am the muni Kapila, son of Kardama.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

'Nara' means God and 'ada' means 'giver'. Narada is called like that because he gives God to everyone.

Personal Guidance
Of trees I am the banyan; of demigod sages I am Nārada; of Gandharvas I am Citraratha; of perfected beings I am Kapila.
Practical Applications
  • Honor Nārada Muni as the model of the wandering devotee.
  • Read Nārada's pastimes regularly.
  • Receive Bhakti-sūtra-style teachings as Kṛṣṇa-direct.
Reflection Questions
  • What model of devotee-life calls me?
  • Whose example am I underusing?
Analogy

Among trees, the long-lived banyan whose aerial roots become new trunks; among devarṣis, the wandering Nārada who carries Kṛṣṇa's name from world to world; among Gandharvas, the celestial musician; among the perfected, the sage who taught his own mother. Kṛṣṇa picks the spreaders — those who do not stay in one shape or one place.

Stories
Nārada in his previous life

In a previous life Nārada was the son of a maidservant who served visiting Vaiṣṇavas. Eating their remnants, hearing their kīrtana, he received a single seed of bhakti. When his mother died, he wandered into the forest and there had direct darśana of the Lord — once, briefly.

From that single touch he became the wandering preacher of three worlds, named here as the chief among devarṣis. The Bhāgavatam (canto 1) records the story so the reader understands why Kṛṣṇa names Nārada — not for cosmic status but for the constancy of his mission of spreading Hari-kathā.

Kapila instructing Devahūti

Kapila Muni, named here as the chief of the perfected (siddhas), was born to Devahūti as her son. When Devahūti at the right age asked for spiritual instruction, Kapila — though her son — became her guru. The Bhāgavatam preserves the entire transmission.

Kṛṣṇa picks Kapila as His siddha-vibhūti because the perfection here is not magical power but the perfection of teaching one's own kin into liberation. The choice tells us what siddhi truly means in Kṛṣṇa's reckoning.
