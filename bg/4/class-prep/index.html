Chapter 4 · Class prep

BG 4.1

The Personality of Godhead, Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, said: I instructed this imperishable science of yoga to the sun-god, Vivasvān, and Vivasvān instructed it to Manu, the father of mankind, and Manu in turn instructed it to Ikṣvāku.

Essence. Knowledge descends through paramparā — Kṛṣṇa first taught the Sun-god, who passed it down; legitimate knowledge always arrives by lineage, not invention.

Practical applications
  • Verify the source of any spiritual teaching you receive — does it descend through a recognized line?
  • Identify your own paramparā connection and honor it.
  • Be wary of teachings that begin with 'I personally believe' rather than 'as it has been taught.'

BG 4.2

This supreme science was thus received through the chain of disciplic succession, and the saintly kings understood it in that way. But in course of time the succession was broken, and therefore the science as it is appears to be lost.

Essence. By the long passage of time the disciplic succession was broken; therefore the science is now received as it was originally given.

Practical applications
  • Accept that traditions can be corrupted and need re-establishment.
  • Trust authentic re-transmissions (like Prabhupāda's restoration of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism).
  • Study the original texts under guidance, not modern reinterpretations.

BG 4.3

That very ancient science of the relationship with the Supreme is today told by Me to you because you are My devotee as well as My friend and can therefore understand the transcendental mystery of this science.

Essence. That same ancient science I am telling you today, because you are My devotee and friend; it is the supreme secret.

Practical applications
  • Cultivate the qualifications — devotion and friendship to Kṛṣṇa — that make confidential knowledge accessible.
  • Don't expect deep teaching without the relational ground.
  • Approach scripture as friend would approach friend.

BG 4.4

Arjuna said: The sun-god Vivasvān is senior by birth to You. How am I to understand that in the beginning You instructed this science to him?

Essence. Arjuna asks honestly: how could You have taught the Sun-god, who lived long ago? — the sincere skeptic's question deserves a clear answer.

Practical applications
  • Welcome honest doubts; they sharpen understanding.
  • Bring rational objections to your guide — don't suppress them.
  • Refuse to adopt a teaching you haven't honestly questioned.

BG 4.5

The Personality of Godhead said: Many, many births both you and I have passed. I can remember all of them, but you cannot, O subduer of the enemy!

Essence. Many lives have passed for both you and Me — I remember them all; you do not.

Practical applications
  • Hold humility about what you don't remember; the Lord remembers everything.
  • Trust that Kṛṣṇa knows your story across lives even when you don't.
  • Take refuge in His memory when yours fails.

BG 4.6

Although I am unborn and My transcendental body never deteriorates, and although I am the Lord of all living entities, by My internal energy I still appear in every millennium in My original transcendental form.

Essence. Although I am unborn and imperishable, the Lord of all beings, I appear in every age by My own internal energy.

Practical applications
  • Distinguish between Kṛṣṇa's appearance (svayam-kṛpā) and ordinary birth (karma-driven).
  • Don't reduce avatāra to mythology; receive it as personal mercy.
  • Honor the descent — His coming is a gift, not a duty.

BG 4.7

Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion – at that time I descend Myself.

Essence. Whenever and wherever there is a decline of dharma and a rise of adharma, I appear Myself — the great promise.

Practical applications
  • When the world looks dark, remember the promise — the Lord is poised to descend.
  • Be a small instrument of dharma's recovery in your own circle.
  • Read this verse during despair about world conditions.

BG 4.8

To deliver the pious and to annihilate the miscreants, as well as to reestablish the principles of religion, I Myself appear, millennium after millennium.

Essence. To deliver the pious, annihilate the miscreants, and re-establish dharma — I appear millennium after millennium.

Practical applications
  • Stand on the side of dharma — Kṛṣṇa is already there.
  • Trust that protection of devotees is His personal commitment.
  • Do not fear opposition; remember on whose side you stand.

BG 4.9

One who knows the transcendental nature of My appearance and activities does not, upon leaving the body, take his birth again in this material world, but attains My eternal abode, O Arjuna.

Essence. One who knows the transcendental nature of My appearance and activities does not take birth again — that one comes to Me.

Practical applications
  • Study Kṛṣṇa's pastimes (līlā) seriously — not as legend but as transcendental fact.
  • Hear daily about His activities; this hearing itself is liberation.
  • Replace material entertainment with hearing Kṛṣṇa-līlā.

BG 4.10

Being freed from attachment, fear and anger, being fully absorbed in Me and taking refuge in Me, many, many persons in the past became purified by knowledge of Me – and thus they all attained transcendental love for Me.

Essence. Freed from attachment, fear, and anger, fully absorbed in Me — taking refuge in Me — many have been purified by knowledge of Me and attained love of Me.

Practical applications
  • Identify which of the three — attachment, fear, anger — most binds you, and bring it to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Use absorption in Kṛṣṇa's name as the alchemy.
  • Trust that the same purification offered to past devotees is offered to you.

BG 4.11

As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly. Everyone follows My path in all respects, O son of Pṛthā.

Essence. As they surrender to Me, I reciprocate accordingly — all paths in the end approach My path.

Practical applications
  • Examine the texture of your surrender — is it conditional, partial, full?
  • Trust that Kṛṣṇa meets you exactly at the level of your offering.
  • Approach Him with your full self, not your edited self.

BG 4.12

Men in this world desire success in fruitive activities, and therefore they worship the demigods. Quickly, of course, men get results from fruitive work in this world.

Essence. In this world, those desiring success in fruitive activity worship the demigods — quickly indeed do results follow.

Practical applications
  • Notice the temptation to bargain with secondary deities for short-term results.
  • Distinguish between quick material results and slow spiritual fruit.
  • Choose the latter even when impatience pushes you toward the former.

BG 4.13

According to the three modes of material nature and the work associated with them, the four divisions of human society are created by Me. And although I am the creator of this system, you should know that I am yet the nondoer, being unchangeable.

Essence. I created the four divisions of human society according to qualities and work; though I am the creator, know Me as the non-doer.

Practical applications
  • Honor the natural divisions of work and temperament; don't force everyone into one shape.
  • Find your own varṇa-aligned service.
  • Recognize Kṛṣṇa as creator who is yet beyond what He creates.

BG 4.14

There is no work that affects Me; nor do I aspire for the fruits of action. One who understands this truth about Me also does not become entangled in the fruitive reactions of work.

Essence. Activity does not bind Me; I have no desire for fruits — one who understands this is also not bound by his activities.

Practical applications
  • Imitate the inner stance of the Lord — full activity, no grasping.
  • Notice the difference in feel between desire-driven and desire-free action.
  • Use Kṛṣṇa's example as your reference, not your own habits.

BG 4.15

All the liberated souls in ancient times acted with this understanding of My transcendental nature. Therefore you should perform your duty, following in their footsteps.

Essence. All the liberated souls of antiquity acted with this understanding — therefore perform your prescribed duty as they did.

Practical applications
  • Take liberated souls as your action-models, not contemporary opinion-leaders.
  • Read biographies of mahājanas regularly.
  • Imitate them in stance even when you can't yet match them in realization.

BG 4.16

Even the intelligent are bewildered in determining what is action and what is inaction. Now I shall explain to you what action is, knowing which you shall be liberated from all misfortune.

Essence. Even the wise are bewildered about action and inaction — I shall now explain what action is, knowing which you shall be liberated.

Practical applications
  • Treat the action/inaction puzzle as worth deep study, not casual opinion.
  • Make this passage of the Gītā a contemplation focus for a week.
  • Hold off concluding too quickly on these subtle topics.

BG 4.17

The intricacies of action are very hard to understand. Therefore one should know properly what action is, what forbidden action is and what inaction is.

Essence. Action, inaction, and prohibited action — these three categories must all be understood, for the path of action is intricate.

Practical applications
  • Distinguish prohibited acts (vikarma) from prescribed acts and from non-action.
  • Audit your activities into these three categories regularly.
  • Eliminate vikarma first; then refine the other two.

BG 4.18

One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is intelligent among men, and he is in the transcendental position, although engaged in all sorts of activities.

Essence. One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is intelligent among men — yogic vision sees beyond the obvious.

Practical applications
  • Notice when your busy 'doing' is spiritually idle (action that is inaction).
  • Notice when your visible 'rest' is full of contemplative engagement (inaction that is action).
  • Trust this paradox in evaluating your spiritual life.

BG 4.19

One is understood to be in full knowledge whose every endeavor is devoid of desire for sense gratification. He is said by sages to be a worker for whom the reactions of work have been burned up by the fire of perfect knowledge.

Essence. He whose every undertaking is free from desire and selfish purpose, his work burned by the fire of perfect knowledge — him the wise call learned.

Practical applications
  • Aspire to actions whose backward-trace shows no selfish thread.
  • Examine your intentions before, during, and after — not just before.
  • Allow knowledge to burn the karmic residue of past selfish acts.

BG 4.20

Abandoning all attachment to the results of his activities, ever satisfied and independent, he performs no fruitive action, although engaged in all kinds of undertakings.

Essence. Free from attachment to results, ever satisfied and independent — though engaged in action, he does nothing at all.

Practical applications
  • Cultivate self-satisfaction (nitya-tṛpta) as a defense against result-anxiety.
  • Reduce your dependence on others' praise to fuel work.
  • Find the 'doing nothing' inside the doing.

BG 4.21

Such a man of understanding acts with mind and intelligence perfectly controlled, gives up all sense of proprietorship over his possessions and acts only for the bare necessities of life. Thus working, he is not affected by sinful reactions.

Essence. Without expectations, controlled in mind and self, abandoning all sense of proprietorship — performing only bodily action — he incurs no sin.

Practical applications
  • Drop proprietorship over your tools, your time, your influence.
  • Maintain only bodily action — feed and shelter the body — without ego inflation.
  • Refuse the 'mine' that swells around even small possessions.

BG 4.22

He who is satisfied with gain which comes of its own accord, who is free from duality and does not envy, who is steady in both success and failure, is never entangled, although performing actions.

Essence. Satisfied with what comes by itself, beyond duality, without envy, equipoised in success and failure — though acting, he is not bound.

Practical applications
  • Receive what comes without complaint; let go of what doesn't.
  • Notice envy as the diagnostic — its presence reveals attachment.
  • Treat success and failure as functionally equivalent for the soul.

BG 4.23

The work of a man who is unattached to the modes of material nature and who is fully situated in transcendental knowledge merges entirely into transcendence.

Essence. Liberated and absorbed in transcendental knowledge, the work of one performed as sacrifice merges entirely into transcendence.

Practical applications
  • Frame each significant work explicitly as yajña.
  • Cultivate transcendental knowledge alongside your action.
  • Let your work merge into Kṛṣṇa rather than accumulating around you.

BG 4.24

A person who is fully absorbed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is sure to attain the spiritual kingdom because of his full contribution to spiritual activities, in which the consummation is absolute and that which is offered is of the same spiritual nature.

Essence. The offering, the offered, the fire, the offerer — all is Brahman; one absorbed in Brahman attains Him by such absorption.

Practical applications
  • See every element of an offering as Brahman — not just the abstract idea.
  • Cultivate the perception that all acts of worship are pervaded by the Supreme.
  • Let the seeing itself become absorption.

BG 4.25

Some yogīs perfectly worship the demigods by offering different sacrifices to them, and some offer sacrifices in the fire of the Supreme Brahman.

Essence. Some yogis worship the demigods perfectly, while others offer the self into the fire of Brahman — both are sacrifices of different orientation.

Practical applications
  • Honor multiple paths even as you walk one specific path.
  • Distinguish demigod worship from impersonal absorption from devotional offering.
  • Choose the path that fits your ripeness.

BG 4.26

Some [the unadulterated brahmacārīs] sacrifice the hearing process and the senses in the fire of mental control, and others [the regulated householders] sacrifice the objects of the senses in the fire of the senses.

Essence. Some sacrifice their hearing into the fire of restraint; others sacrifice sense-objects into the fire of senses — many varieties of sacrifice exist.

Practical applications
  • Identify your specific 'sacrifice' — what you regularly offer in restraint or service.
  • Experiment with one new form of sacrifice this season.
  • Recognize that sacrifice has many forms beyond the ritual.

BG 4.27

Others, who are interested in achieving self-realization through control of the mind and senses, offer the functions of all the senses, and of the life breath, as oblations into the fire of the controlled mind.

Essence. Others sacrifice all activities of senses and life into the fire of self-knowledge — kindled by knowledge.

Practical applications
  • Use knowledge as the kindling for inner sacrifice.
  • Approach scripture-study itself as sacrifice — not just learning.
  • Let study fuel inner offering, not intellectual collection.

BG 4.28

Having accepted strict vows, some become enlightened by sacrificing their possessions, and others by performing severe austerities, by practicing the yoga of eightfold mysticism, or by studying the Vedas to advance in transcendental knowledge.

Essence. Material possessions, austerity, yoga, study, knowledge — there are many forms of sacrifice for those of strict vows.

Practical applications
  • Recognize that even vows themselves can be a sacrifice.
  • Identify the sacrifice your nature can sustain.
  • Don't envy a sacrifice that isn't yours; perform your own faithfully.

BG 4.29

Still others, who are inclined to the process of breath restraint to remain in trance, practice by offering the movement of the outgoing breath into the incoming, and the incoming breath into the outgoing, and thus at last remain in trance, stopping all breathing. Others, curtailing the eating process, offer the outgoing breath into itself as a sacrifice.

Essence. Some perform prāṇāyāma — offering outgoing breath into incoming and incoming into outgoing — to enter the trance of breath suspension.

Practical applications
  • Honor the body's breath as a vehicle of practice, not just survival.
  • Even ordinary mindful breathing during work is a small offering.
  • Approach prāṇāyāma under guidance, not as DIY experiment.

BG 4.30

All these performers who know the meaning of sacrifice become cleansed of sinful reactions, and, having tasted the nectar of the results of sacrifices, they advance toward the supreme eternal atmosphere.

Essence. All who know the meaning of sacrifice become cleansed of sin, and tasting the nectar of sacrifice's remnants, attain the eternal.

Practical applications
  • Eat prasāda (the remnants of offering) regularly.
  • Recognize sacrifice as cleansing, not as deprivation.
  • Trust that 'remnant nectar' carries its own grace.

BG 4.31

O best of the Kuru dynasty, without sacrifice one can never live happily on this planet or in this life: what then of the next?

Essence. Even this world is not for those who do not perform sacrifice — what to speak of the next?

Practical applications
  • Don't expect even basic worldly stability without some structure of offering.
  • Establish at least one daily sacrifice — small but unbroken.
  • Resist the modern delusion that you can have without giving.

BG 4.32

All these different types of sacrifice are approved by the Vedas, and all of them are born of different types of work. Knowing them as such, you will become liberated.

Essence. Many varieties of sacrifice are described in the Vedas — knowing them all to arise from action, you shall be liberated.

Practical applications
  • Study the diversity of yajña historically; don't reduce sacrifice to one form.
  • Trust the breadth of scripture rather than one modern interpretation.
  • Approach Vedic literature with an open and devout mind.

BG 4.33

O chastiser of the enemy, the sacrifice performed in knowledge is better than the mere sacrifice of material possessions. After all, O son of Pṛthā, all sacrifices of work culminate in transcendental knowledge.

Essence. Sacrifice in knowledge is superior to sacrifice in matter — all action culminates in knowledge.

Practical applications
  • Prioritize knowledge-sacrifice (study, hearing, contemplation) over mere ritual.
  • Let your rituals be informed by deepening understanding.
  • Trust that knowledge will refine, not replace, devotional practice.

BG 4.34

Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth.

Essence. Approach a self-realized soul; inquire submissively, render service — the wise will impart knowledge unto you.

Practical applications
  • Identify a real teacher in your life and approach with all three: humility, inquiry, service.
  • Notice when one of the three is missing.
  • Trust the formula: submission + inquiry + service = transmission.

BG 4.35

Having obtained real knowledge from a self-realized soul, you will never fall again into such illusion, for by this knowledge you will see that all living beings are but part of the Supreme, or, in other words, that they are Mine.

Essence. Having received that knowledge, you will not be deluded again — you will see all beings in your self and in Me.

Practical applications
  • Trust that real knowledge produces visible change in vision.
  • Notice the difference between intellectual knowledge and the kind that 'changes seeing.'
  • Wait for and watch for the shift.

BG 4.36

Even if you are considered to be the most sinful of all sinners, when you are situated in the boat of transcendental knowledge you will be able to cross over the ocean of miseries.

Essence. Even if you are the worst of sinners, with the boat of knowledge you will cross over the ocean of misery.

Practical applications
  • Don't disqualify yourself based on past sins — Kṛṣṇa says even the worst can cross.
  • Embark on the boat of knowledge today, not after you 'fix' yourself.
  • Treat self-condemnation as another obstacle, not a virtue.

BG 4.37

As a blazing fire turns firewood to ashes, O Arjuna, so does the fire of knowledge burn to ashes all reactions to material activities.

Essence. As blazing fire turns wood to ashes, so the fire of knowledge burns to ashes all reactions to material activities.

Practical applications
  • Trust that real knowledge has the power to burn karmic residue.
  • Don't anxiously try to 'work off' karma manually; let knowledge burn it.
  • Cultivate the fire steadily — small flame burns slowly, big flame fast.

BG 4.38

In this world, there is nothing so sublime and pure as transcendental knowledge. Such knowledge is the mature fruit of all mysticism. And one who has become accomplished in the practice of devotional service enjoys this knowledge within himself in due course of time.

Essence. There is nothing as sublime and pure as transcendental knowledge — one perfect in yoga finds it within himself in time.

Practical applications
  • Treat knowledge of the soul as the highest treasure.
  • Cultivate yoga as the ground in which knowledge ripens.
  • Be patient — knowledge unfolds in time, not on demand.

BG 4.39

A faithful man who is dedicated to transcendental knowledge and who subdues his senses is eligible to achieve such knowledge, and having achieved it he quickly attains the supreme spiritual peace.

Essence. Faith, devotion to that knowledge, control of the senses — by these one obtains transcendental knowledge and the supreme peace.

Practical applications
  • Audit all three: faith, devotion, sense control.
  • Strengthen the weakest of the three.
  • Trust that the supreme peace follows from this triple foundation.

BG 4.40

But ignorant and faithless persons who doubt the revealed scriptures do not attain God consciousness; they fall down. For the doubting soul there is happiness neither in this world nor in the next.

Essence. The ignorant and faithless who doubts the scriptures perishes — neither this world nor the next, nor happiness, is for the doubter.

Practical applications
  • Recognize chronic doubt as itself a spiritual condition needing care.
  • Bring doubts into honest inquiry rather than letting them harden.
  • Refuse doubt as identity; treat it as a stage.

BG 4.41

One who acts in devotional service, renouncing the fruits of his actions, and whose doubts have been destroyed by transcendental knowledge, is situated factually in the self. Thus he is not bound by the reactions of work, O conqueror of riches.

Essence. One whose doubts are destroyed by knowledge, who has renounced the fruits of action, is firmly self-situated and not bound by his actions.

Practical applications
  • Use knowledge to destroy doubts — not to merely manage them.
  • Renounce the fruits of action explicitly each day.
  • Aim at being 'self-situated,' not externally validated.

BG 4.42

Therefore the doubts which have arisen in your heart out of ignorance should be slashed by the weapon of knowledge. Armed with yoga, O Bhārata, stand and fight.

Essence. Therefore, with the sword of knowledge, sever the doubts in your heart born of ignorance — arise, take to yoga, and fight, O Bhārata.

Practical applications
  • Cut doubts decisively when knowledge has shown the way.
  • Don't perpetually weigh; at some point, decide and act.
  • Stand and engage your specific 'fight' (your dharma).
