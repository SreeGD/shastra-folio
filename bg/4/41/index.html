Important Words
  • sannyasta — renounced
    Past passive participle of sam + ni + √as — 'thrown down completely.' Paired with yoga and karmāṇam to form yoga-sannyasta-karman: actions renounced *through* yoga, not renunciation *of* yoga. Yoga is the instrument by which karma's claim is set aside.
  • sañchinna — thoroughly cut
    Past passive participle of sam + √chid — 'completely severed.' Paired with jñāna and saṁśayam: doubts cut by knowledge. The verb anticipates 4.42's chittvā — both verses use the sword imagery. Knowledge as the cutting edge.
  • ātma - vantam — self-possessed
    Compound: ātma + vantam (possessing). Literally 'one who possesses himself.' Not merely self-aware but self-owning. The Gītā's idea of inner sovereignty.
  • nibadhnanti — bind
    Ni + √bandh — 'bind down.' Negated by na: 'do not bind.' The negation is structural — actions *cannot* bind such a one. Same teaching as 4.14 (limpanti negated), now applied to the devotee rather than the Lord.
  • dhanam - jaya — O conqueror of riches
    Arjuna's epithet — 'winner of wealth.' Kṛṣṇa addresses him by his most worldly title precisely when describing the renunciation of works. The juxtaposition is deliberate: even the conqueror of riches can be the renouncer of fruits.
One who acts in devotional service, renouncing the fruits of his actions, and whose doubts have been destroyed by transcendental knowledge, is situated factually in the self. Thus he is not bound by the reactions of work, O conqueror of riches.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
The chapter's summary portrait: action renounced via yoga, doubt severed by knowledge, and self-possession secured — such a person is immune to karmic bondage. The triad karma + jñāna + ātma-niṣṭhā becomes the formula.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Three bahuvrīhi compounds compress the entire chapter: yoga-sannyasta-karmā (4.20–22), jñāna-sañchinna-saṁśayam (4.34–38), and ātmavantam (4.39). The phrase ज्ञान-सञ्छिन्न-संशय is technical: doubt has been severed (not merely doubted away), implying an experiential cut. Sets up the climactic command of 4.42.

Purport

One who follows the instruction of the Bhagavad-gītā, as it is imparted by the Lord, the Personality of Godhead Himself, becomes free from all doubts by the grace of transcendental knowledge. He, as a part and parcel of the Lord, in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, is already established in self-knowledge. As such, he is undoubtedly above bondage to action.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
41. Actions do not bind the person who has renounced all action through niṣkāma-karma-yoga, who has then cut all doubts through jṣāna-yoga, and who has then attained directly realization of the ātmā.
Commentary

In such a state, a person will reach a state of no karma (naiṣkarmyam). Having renounced all actions by sannyāsa, after performance of niṣkāma-karma-yoga, and having eradicated all doubts through practice of jṣāna, actions do not bind that person, who has attained realization of the soul.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
41. Actions do not bind the person whose actions are transformed into jṣāna through niṣkāma karma yoga, who has then cut all doubts through jṣāna yoga, and who has then attained directly realization of the ātmā.
Commentary

Such a person will attain the perfection of stopping all actions. Actions do not bind that person who has renounced all actions by yoga. This means that the actions have become jṣāna, being executed without attachment, by niṣkāma karma yoga, expressed by words like yoga-sthaḥ kuru karmāṇi: perform your duty with composure, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. (BG 2.48). Actions do not bind the person who has vanquished all doubts by the knowledge spoken by Me and who is seeing the ātmā (ātmavantam). The actions do not bind because they have been destroyed by knowledge.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

This is niskama karma knowledge applied to work. Acts accordingly to one's duty in devotional service or just even prescribed duties, renunciation of the fruits of one's actions, no doubts due to one's deep, intense and realized transcendental knowledge ‑ he is actually situated in the self. That means he knows that he is not this body. Therefore, even though working, but in this consciousness, with this knowledge ‑ he is not bound by the reactions of work. And what is the knowledge? That ultimately we are all parts and parcels of Krsna (already explained in text 35).

[ 5 . Conclusion: Slash with knowledge all doubts born of ignorance. "Arjuna, armed with yoga, stand and fight!" (42) ]

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
4.41 Yoga-sannyasta-karmanam, one who has renounced actions through yoga: that person who is a knower of the supreme Goal, by whom actions called righteous or unrighteous have been renounced through the yoga characterized as the Knowledge of the supreme Goal. How does one become detached from actions through yoga? The Lord says: He is jnana-samchinna-samsayah, one whose doubts (samsaya) have been fully dispelled (samchinna) by Knowledge (jnana) characterized as the realization of the identity of the individual Self and God. O Dhananjaya, he who has thus renounced actions through yoga, atmavantam, who is not inadvertent, not careless; him, karmani, actions, seen as the activities of the gunas (see 3.28); na nibadhnanti, do not bind, (i.e.) they do not produce a result in the form of evil etc. Since one whose doubts have been destroyed by Knowledge-arising from the destruction of the impurities (of body, mind, etc.) as result of the practise of Karma-yoga-does not get bound by acitons owing to the mere fact of his actions having been burnt away by Knowledge; and since one who has doubts with regard to the practice of the yogas of Knowledge and actions gets ruined-

Personal Guidance
One whose doubts are destroyed by knowledge, who has renounced the fruits of action, is firmly self-situated and not bound by his actions.
Practical Applications
  • Use knowledge to destroy doubts — not to merely manage them.
  • Renounce the fruits of action explicitly each day.
  • Aim at being 'self-situated,' not externally validated.
Reflection Questions
  • Which doubt is overdue for destruction by knowledge?
  • What does 'self-situated' look like in my life?
Analogy

A swordsman with a sharpened blade in hand walks through the field unbound by what he cuts. Knowledge is the sword; renounced karma is the freedom; ātma-vān — self-possessed — is the swordsman. The verse names the integrated state: action, knowledge, self-possession together.
