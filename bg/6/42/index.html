Important Words
  • yoginām — of learned transcendentalists
    Genitive plural — 'of yogīs.' The family the bhraṣṭa enters is itself a family of yogīs. Not merely pious or wealthy but actively spiritual. The next life is among one's true peers.
  • dhī-matām — of those endowed with great wisdom
    Dhī — 'intellect, wisdom'; mat — 'possessor.' The family is not only spiritual but wise. The double qualification protects against the misreading that any religious family qualifies.
  • durlabha-taram — very rare
    Dur-labha — 'hard to attain'; -taram — comparative suffix. 'Even rarer.' The previous verse named one option as the destination; this verse names a second, rarer one. The grading is deliberate.
  • loke — in this world
    Locative of loka — 'world.' Such a birth is rare in the loka. The verse acknowledges that this circumstance, were it common, would empty the world of seekers. Its very rarity is part of how the system works.
  • janma yat īdṛśam — birth such as this
    Janma — birth; yat — which; īdṛśa — 'of-this-kind.' The grammar lingers on the demonstrative: a birth like this one. The verse points at it carefully, signaling the high stakes.
Or [if unsuccessful after long practice of yoga] he takes his birth in a family of transcendentalists who are surely great in wisdom. Certainly, such a birth is rare in this world.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
An even rarer option: instead of detour through heaven, birth directly into a family of dhīmant yogīs. Such a household is the ideal continuing school. The verse explicitly says: this kind of birth is the rarest of all.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Yogināṁ kule — a family of yogīs — is the chapter's quiet vindication of spiritual lineage. Not 'śrīmant' this time but 'dhīmant' (wise): wealth is not necessary, wisdom is. This passage is later read by Vaiṣṇavas as forecasting birth into a devotee family (cf. Bhāgavata 1.5.17).

Purport

Birth in a family of yogīs or transcendentalists – those with great wisdom – is praised herein because the child born in such a family receives a spiritual impetus from the very beginning of his life. It is especially the case in the ācārya or gosvāmī families. Such families are very learned and devoted by tradition and training, and thus they become spiritual masters. In India there are many such ācārya families, but they have now degenerated due to insufficient education and training. By the grace of the Lord, there are still families that foster transcendentalists generation after generation. It is certainly very fortunate to take birth in such families. Fortunately, both our spiritual master, Oṁ Viṣṇupāda Śrī Śrīmad Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Gosvāmī Mahārāja, and our humble self had the opportunity to take birth in such families, by the grace of the Lord, and both of us were trained in the devotional service of the Lord from the very beginning of our lives. Later on we met by the order of the transcendental system.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
42. Or, if he has practiced a long time, he is born in the family of intelligent yogīs. Such a birth is more difficult to obtain in this world.
Commentary

The destination of yogīs who fell after doing a little practice has been described in the previous verse. This verse describes the different destination of yogīs who fell after practicing for a long time. They are born in the families of yogīs such as Nimi.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
42. Or, if he has practiced a long time, he is born in the family of skilful yogīs. Such a birth is more difficult to obtain in this world.
Commentary

This verse describes the result of the yogī who fell after practicing a long time. He is born in a family of yoga teachers (dhīmatām), in a family which practices yoga (yogīnām). He then praises these two types of birth. Such a birth in a family suitable for yoga (previous verse) or in a family practicing yoga, because of the strength of impressions of previous yoga practice, is very rare for normal men.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Translation
6.42 If one swerves from the right path at an advanced stage of Yoga, he will be born in a family of wise Yogins who practise Yoga and are themselves capable of teaching Yoga. Thus, these two types of birth - one in the family of those who are fit to practise Yoga and the other in that of accomplished Yogins - are hardly met with among common people in this world. Personal Guidance
Or one is born in a family of transcendentalists with great wisdom — such birth is even more difficult to obtain.
Practical Applications
  • If born into devotional family, recognize the rare grace.
  • Don't take such birth for granted.
  • Use it accordingly — with seriousness.
Reflection Questions
  • Have I appreciated the grace of my circumstances?
  • What seriousness does that grace require?
Analogy

A scholar who could not finish his book in one lifetime is reborn into a family of scholars — the library is already open when he arrives. The yoga-bhraṣṭa born into a family of transcendentalists is that scholar. The unfinished manuscript is on the desk. The dictionary is already at hand. The verse calls such a birth rarer than rare for exactly this reason.

Stories
Vyāsadeva's Lineage

Śukadeva, the speaker of the Bhāgavatam, was born to Vyāsadeva — a literal birth in a family of transcendentalists 'great in wisdom.' Vyāsa was the compiler of the Vedas; his son walked out of the womb as a paramahaṁsa, requiring no instruction, only the right setting for his pre-existing realization to manifest.

The Bhāgavatam (1.4) frames Śukadeva's birth precisely as the durlabha-taram of this verse — a kind of birth so rare that even the Vedas catalogue it as exceptional. Yoga-bhraṣṭa from a previous life, reactivated in the most fertile possible soil.
