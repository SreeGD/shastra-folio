Important Words
  • śucau — in a sanctified land
    Locative of śuci — 'pure, clean, bright.' Not merely physically clean but ritually pure. The outer place mirrors the inner place that is being prepared.
  • sthiram — firm
    From √sthā, 'to stand.' Stability of seat anticipates stability of mind. The same root will return in 6.19's nivāta-sthaḥ and elsewhere — the verse is laying down a foundation of sthairya.
  • ati — too
    Repeated four times across the two-verse block — too high, too low. Kṛṣṇa builds yukta-āhāra-vihāra (6.17) into the seat itself: moderation is not philosophical balance but the disciplined regulation of body and senses so that sādhana for Bhagavān can proceed unobstructed. Extremes break the practice; the middle keeps the offering alive.
  • kuśa — kuśa grass
    The sacred grass used in Vedic ritual — itself considered purifying. The base of the seat is consecrated. The detail tells us that this is not secular concentration practice; it is sacrifice in another posture.
  • eka-agram — with one attention
    Eka — one, agra — point. The mind brought to a single point. The compound names the entire art: not emptying the mind, but pointing it.
  • viśuddhaye — for clarifying
    Dative of viśuddhi — 'thorough purification.' The seat, the posture, the breath are all in service of one goal: the cleansing of the inner heart.
To practice yoga, one should go to a secluded place and should lay kuśa grass on the ground and then cover it with a deerskin and a soft cloth. The seat should be neither too high nor too low and should be situated in a sacred place. The yogī should then sit on it very firmly and practice yoga to purify the heart by controlling his mind, senses and activities and fixing the mind on one point.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
The locus classicus of meditative āsana: a clean spot, a stable seat of just the right height, the triple-layer cushion, and an upright sitting posture. The outer arrangement is the scaffolding for the inner work — concentrating mind to one point, restraining senses, all aimed at purifying the self.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The three-layer cushion (cailājina-kuśottara) is symbolic as well as practical: kuśa as base for ritual purity, deerskin for sattva-conducing insulation, cloth for everyday comfort. Patañjali's sūtra sthira-sukham āsanam (YS 2.46) reduces the same instruction to two words. The aim — ātma-viśuddhi — is named explicitly: not levitation, not powers, but cleaning the instrument.

Purport

“Sacred place” refers to places of pilgrimage. In India the yogīs – the transcendentalists or the devotees – all leave home and reside in sacred places such as Prayāga, Mathurā, Vṛndāvana, Hṛṣīkeśa and Hardwar and in solitude practice yoga where the sacred rivers like the Yamunā and the Ganges flow. But often this is not possible, especially for Westerners. The so-called yoga societies in big cities may be successful in earning material benefit, but they are not at all suitable for the actual practice of yoga. One who is not self-controlled and whose mind is not undisturbed cannot practice meditation. Therefore, in the Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa it is said that in Kali-yuga (the present yuga, or age), when people in general are short-lived, slow in spiritual realization and always disturbed by various anxieties, the best means of spiritual realization is chanting the holy name of the Lord. harer nāma harer nāma harer nāmaiva kevalam kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā “In this age of quarrel and hypocrisy the only means of deliverance is chanting the holy name of the Lord. There is no other way. There is no other way. There is no other way.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

“Rules of celibacy” by Yajnavalkya (6.13 – 6.14) – The vow of brahmacharya is meant to help one completely abstain from sex indulgence in work, words and mind – at all times, under all circumstances and in all places
Role of celibacy in bhakti-yoga (6.14 purport)
Strict celibacy is a must for jnana and dhyana schools; but only in bhakti school the conception of householder brahmacharya is allowed
Cult of Bhakti-yoga is so powerful that one automatically loses sexual attraction, being engaged in the superior service of the Lord and experiences higher taste (Ref. Bg. 2.59 “visaya vinivartante...”)

Personal Guidance
Establishing a clean place, a moderate seat, neither too high nor too low — the yogi practices fixing the mind on one object for purification.
Practical Applications
  • Establish a clean, simple, dedicated space for practice — even a corner.
  • Don't overcomplicate the setup; balance, not extremity.
  • Honor the place as set apart for the Lord.
Reflection Questions
  • Where is my dedicated practice space?
  • What clutter or extreme have I let creep into it?
Analogy

A potter chooses the place for his wheel with care — level ground, neither in mud nor on a heap, screened from the wind. The yogī's seat is that wheel-place. The verse spends two ślokas on what looks like furniture because the inner work is also a craft: the wheel of the mind cannot turn true on uneven ground.
