Important Words
  • yuñjan — engaging in yoga practice
    Present participle of √yuj. Same word as 6.15 — the chapter's drumbeat: yuñjan, yuñjan. Yoga is the continuous yoking; the result follows the continuity.
  • sadā — always
    Echoes 6.10's satatam and 6.15's sadā. The chapter will not let us forget — sadā, sadā. Constancy is the path's central rule.
  • vigata — freed from
    Vi-gata, 'gone away.' Past participle of vi-√gam. The kalmaṣa has departed — left, not been held off. The verse names a state of accomplished cleanliness.
  • kalmaṣaḥ — material contamination
    Same word as the akalmaṣam of 6.27, here in the noun form. The two verses are paired: 27 names the state, 28 describes how it is attained — by constant engagement.
  • brahma-saṁsparśam — constant touch with the Supreme
    Sam-sparśa — 'thorough contact.' Brahma is touched, not merely contemplated. The Gauḍīya reading: brahma here means the personal Absolute, Kṛṣṇa. The contact is intimate.
  • atyantam — the highest
    Ati-anta, 'beyond all end.' Endless, ultimate. Modifies sukham — the happiness has no terminus. Once gained, it does not deplete.
Thus the self-controlled yogī, constantly engaged in yoga practice, becomes free from all material contamination and achieves the highest stage of perfect happiness in transcendental loving service to the Lord.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
What was struggle now becomes 'sukhena' — by ease. The same yoking that once felt impossible now yields brahma-saṁsparśa, the touch of Brahman, automatically. Continuous practice converts effort into spontaneity.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Note the play: sukhena … sukham — by happiness, happiness. Saṁsparśa ('contact') is gentler than aikya ('union'); the verse leaves room for both monist and theistic readings. This rounds out the 6.15-28 promise sequence before the famous sama-darśin verses begin.

Purport

Self-realization means knowing one’s constitutional position in relationship to the Supreme. The individual soul is part and parcel of the Supreme, and his position is to render transcendental service to the Lord. This transcendental contact with the Supreme is called brahma-saṁsparśa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
28. Thus constantly engaging his mind, the yogī, free of all sin, easily attains the intense bliss of contact with Brahman as a jīvan-mukta.
Commentary

Then, he attains success. Attaining bliss means he becomes jīvan-mukta, liberated even in this life.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
28. Thus constantly experiencing ātmā, the yogī, free of all sin, easily attains the unlimited bliss of experiencing paramātmā.
Commentary

After directly realizing his own ātmā, he then directly realizes the paramātmā. Experiencing his own ātmā by the yoga method described above (ātmānam yuṣjan), with all faults burned up by this method (vigata kalmasaḥ), the yogī easily (sukhena) attains the unlimited happiness (sukham), the realization of paramātmā (brahma saṁsparśam).

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

'Brahma‑samsparsam' ‑ being in constant touch with the Supreme. 'Atyantam' ‑ the highest. 'Sukham' ‑ happiness.'Asnute'‑ he attains. By practicing, free from mental, sensual engagement, other than in Krsna consciousness, in goodness, one easily contacts the Supersoul, depending upon one's desire and in this state he becomes a 'jivan‑mukta'.

Personal Guidance
Constantly engaged thus, free from contamination, the yogi easily attains contact with the Supreme — supreme happiness.
Practical Applications
  • Trust that consistency, not intensity, brings contact.
  • Reduce contamination (asat-saṅga, vikarma) as practice ground.
  • Don't strain — easy contact follows from steady practice.
Reflection Questions
  • What contamination has been hardest to reduce?
  • Where am I straining when I should be steady?
Analogy

A copper vessel left long in the Ganges turns golden — slowly, without striving, by the river's constant touch. The constantly engaged yogī is that copper. The contact with Brahman, brahma-saṁsparśam, does the alchemy. The yogī does not have to scrub at himself; he has only to stay in the river.

Stories
Ambarīṣa Mahārāja's Daily Round

King Ambarīṣa, the great devotee described in Bhāgavatam 9.4, kept his mind, words, body, and senses constantly engaged: mind on Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, voice in describing His glories, hands in cleaning His temple, ears in hearing of Him, eyes in seeing the deity, body in embracing devotees, and so on through every faculty.

The Bhāgavatam concludes that by this yuñjan evam sadā discipline he attained brahma-saṁsparśam — constant touch with the Supreme — to such a degree that even the brāhmaṇa Durvāsā, attacking him in pride, was reduced to begging his forgiveness. The contamination of pride could not hold against a man already in contact.
