Important Words
  • pūjanam — worship
    From √pūj — to honour. The keystone of the verse's opening compound (deva-dvija-guru-prājña-pūjanam): worship of the Supreme Lord, brāhmaṇas, the guru, and worshipable persons. Note the order — Lord first, then His representatives in descending immediacy. Bodily austerity begins by physically honouring those higher than oneself.
  • śaucam — cleanliness
    From √śuc — to gleam, to be radiant. Cleanliness is not negative (absence of dirt) but positive (radiance). The body is to gleam, not merely be unfilthy.
  • ārjavam — simplicity, straightness
    From ṛju — straight, direct. The bodily form of honesty: no curving postures of concealment, no theatrical comportment. The body itself becomes a straight line of intent.
  • brahmacaryam — celibacy
    Compound: brahma (the Absolute) + caryā (conduct, walking). Literally 'walking in the Absolute.' Celibacy here is not mere abstinence but the redirection of generative energy upward — śakti gathered for higher work.
  • ahiṁsā — nonviolence
    Privative a- + hiṁsā (injury). The fifth element. Note: in the kṣatriya's case (Arjuna), this does not mean refusal to fight; it means refusal to injure outside one's dharmic duty. The chapter's logic places ahiṁsā under bodily tapas because it is performed first by the limbs.
Austerity of the body consists in worship of the Supreme Lord, the brāhmaṇas, the spiritual master, and superiors like the father and mother, and in cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy and nonviolence.
Purport

The Supreme Godhead here explains the different kinds of austerity and penance. First He explains the austerities and penances practiced by the body. One should offer, or learn to offer, respect to God or to the demigods, the perfect, qualified brāhmaṇas and the spiritual master and superiors like father, mother or any person who is conversant with Vedic knowledge. These should be given proper respect. One should practice cleansing oneself externally and internally, and he should learn to become simple in behavior. He should not do anything which is not sanctioned by the scriptural injunctions. He should not indulge in sex outside of married life, for sex is sanctioned in the scripture only in marriage, not otherwise. This is called celibacy. These are penances and austerities as far as the body is concerned.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
14. Sattvic austerity of the body consists of worship of devas, brāhmaṇas, guru and the wise, as well as purity, sincerity, celibacy and non-violence.
Commentary

Speaking of three types of austerity, first the Lord speaks of three types of sattvic austerity in three verses.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
14. Austerity of the body consists of worship of devas, brāhmaṇas, guru and the wise; purity, sincerity, celibacy and non-violence.
Commentary

The Lord next in order to explain the austerity in the three modes, speaks first of the three types of austerity (body, words and mind) in three verses. There should be worship of the devas such as Vasu and Rudra; of the dvijās, the best among the brāhmaṇas; of the gurus, such as mother, father and teacher; and of the learned, who know the Vedas and its ancillary portions. The sequence is from superior to inferior. There should be cleanliness (śaucam) of the two types mentioned previously (internal and external). Ārjava, sincerity in terms of bodily austerity, means that one should equally carry out what is prescribed and avoid what is forbidden. Brahmacaryam means sex life according to scriptural injunction. These are the austerities to be accomplished by the body (śārīram).

Personal Guidance
The austerity of the body — worship of the Supreme, the brāhmaṇas, the spiritual master, elders; cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy, nonviolence.
Practical Applications
  • Audit each of these bodily austerities.
  • Pick one to deepen this season.
  • Don't reduce 'austerity' to fasting alone.
Reflection Questions
  • Which bodily austerity is most under-practiced?
  • What deepening is feasible?
Analogy

Bodily austerity is like a gardener tending the outer hedges of a great estate. The hedges are not the estate's purpose, but a neglected hedge announces a neglected garden. Worship of the worshipable, cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy, nonviolence — these are the visible edges by which the inner garden is read.

Stories
Haridāsa Ṭhākura's bodily austerity

Haridāsa Ṭhākura kept three lakhs of names of Kṛṣṇa daily — but his śārīra-tapas was equally rigorous. He honoured Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Advaita Ācārya with full bodily worship, maintained spotless personal cleanliness even when living in caves and grass huts, kept brahmacarya through every test (including the famous one with Māyā in the form of a courtesan), and was perfectly nonviolent — even refusing to swat the snake that lived in his bhajana-kuṭīra.

Caitanya-caritāmṛta presents Haridāsa as the living textbook of this verse. Every clause — deva, dvija, guru-pūjana, śauca, ārjava, brahmacarya, ahiṁsā — is annotated by some incident of his life.
