Important Words
  • manaḥ - prasādaḥ — satisfaction of the mind
    Compound: manas (mind) + prasāda (clarity, settled satisfaction, grace). The same word used for the Lord's mercy. The mind's prasāda is not enforced calm but the natural settling of muddy water when not stirred.
  • saumyatvam — being without duplicity, gentleness
    From soma — the moon, by extension cool, gentle. The mind that has the moon's quality: lit but not burning. Not the harshness of severe willpower but the gentleness of a settled inner climate.
  • maunam — gravity, silence
    From muni — the silent sage. Maunam is not merely closing the mouth; it is the inner silence from which any speech, when it comes, comes weighted. The Gītā places it inside mānasa-tapas, not vāk-tapas — significant: the silence is mental first.
  • vinigrahaḥ — control
    From vi + ni + √grah — firm grip, total restraint. The fourth element of mental austerity, paired in the verse with ātma. Note the metaphor: the self/mind is grasped, like reins. Without this vinigraha the other three elements scatter — the mind cannot be its own master without first being seized.
  • saṁśuddhiḥ — purification
    From sam + √śudh — thorough, all-around purification. The deepest of the five, paired with bhāva. Not control of thoughts but rectification of the underlying bhāva that produces them. Cure the spring, not the river. Saṁśuddhi is the chapter's most ambitious mental-tapas goal.
And satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control and purification of one’s existence are the austerities of the mind.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Mind-tapas is the deepest of the three: keep the mind clear, the temper gentle, the tongue and thought-stream silent, the self under restraint, and motives purified. Without this interior discipline, body and speech tapas collapse into outer performance.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The triad of bodily, vocal, and mental tapas (14-16) is concluded here at the subtlest level. Bhāva-saṁśuddhi — purification of the inner emotional-volitional state — is what really pulls ritual into the mode of goodness. Maunam here is not the absence of speech but the muni-state of a settled inner self (cf. 2.69, 12.19). Cross-ref Yoga-sūtra 1.20's śraddhā-vīrya-smṛti-samādhi as parallel interior cultivation.

Purport

To make the mind austere is to detach it from sense gratification. It should be so trained that it can be always thinking of doing good for others. The best training for the mind is gravity in thought. One should not deviate from Kṛṣṇa consciousness and must always avoid sense gratification. To purify one’s nature is to become Kṛṣṇa conscious. Satisfaction of the mind can be obtained only by taking the mind away from thoughts of sense enjoyment. The more we think of sense enjoyment, the more the mind becomes dissatisfied. In the present age we unnecessarily engage the mind in so many different ways for sense gratification, and so there is no possibility of the mind’s becoming satisfied. The best course is to divert the mind to the Vedic literature, which is full of satisfying stories, as in the Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata. One can take advantage of this knowledge and thus become purified. The mind should be devoid of duplicity, and one should think of the welfare of all. Silence means that one is always thinking of self-realization. The person in Kṛṣṇa consciousness observes perfect silence in this sense. Control of the mind means detaching the mind from sense enjoyment. One should be straightforward in his dealings and thereby purify his existence. All these qualities together constitute austerity in mental activities.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
16. Sattvic austerity of the mind consists of tranquility, gentleness, silence, control of the mind, and purity of the heart.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
16. Austerity of the mind consists of purity, gentleness, contemplation of ātmā, withdrawal from sense objects, and purity of the heart.
Commentary

The mind should be pure, without agitation from remembrance of sense objects (manaḥ prasādaḥ). It should be without cruelty, desiring everyone’s happiness (saumyatvam). It should be comtemplating the ātmā (maunam). The mind should withdraw from sense objects (ātma vinigrahaḥ). It should be free from deceit in dealings with others (bhāva saṁśuddhiḥ). These are the austerities to be achieved by the mind.

Personal Guidance
Austerity of the mind — satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control, purity of thought.
Practical Applications
  • Cultivate satisfaction over chronic dissatisfaction.
  • Practice gravity over flippancy.
  • Honor mental austerity equal with bodily and verbal.
Reflection Questions
  • Which mental austerity is hardest for me?
  • What discipline would build it?
Analogy

Mental austerity is like polishing a mirror behind which no one sees you work. The body's tapas is visible; speech-tapas is audible; but mānasa-tapas is performed in a room with no witnesses. The mirror gradually becomes clear and the Lord can be seen in it — but only the polisher knows the polishing happened.
