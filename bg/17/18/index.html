Important Words
  • sat - kāra — respect
    Compound: sat (the noble, the honourable) + kāra (the doing). Receiving the salutation of others. The first of the three rajasic motives. The austerity is real, but its aim is the response of the social field.
  • māna — honor
    From √man — to think well of. Public recognition as a great person. The second motive — slightly more interior than sat-kāra. The austerity is fueled by the wish to occupy a position in others' minds.
  • pūjā — worship
    The strongest of the three — to be worshiped by others. The grammar of rajas climbs through the verse: from respect, to honour, to worship. The same hunger in three intensities.
  • dambhena — with pride
    Instrumental of dambha. Note the repetition from verse 5 — dambha is the chapter's recurring marker of false austerity. Same word, same poison, different garments.
  • adhruvam — temporary
    Privative a- + dhruva (firm — the very word that is the name of the great devotee Dhruva). Note the inversion: while Dhruva Mahārāja became dhruva by redirecting his tapas to the Supreme, rajasic tapas performed for honour and worship produces the opposite — the adhruva, the unstable. Same syllable, opposite outcome.
  • calam — flickering
    From √cal — to shake, to tremble, to flicker. Paired with adhruvam at the verse's end. Calam captures the moment-to-moment instability; adhruvam the long-term impermanence. Rajasic tapas fails both at the candle-flicker scale and at the lifetime scale.
Penance performed out of pride and for the sake of gaining respect, honor and worship is said to be in the mode of passion. It is neither stable nor permanent.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Rājasika austerity is performed for social capital — to be honored, esteemed, worshiped — and displayed for show. Its fruits, even if they come, are flickering and brittle: status earned by performance evaporates as soon as performance falters.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Calam and adhruvam are diagnostic for all rajas-rooted attainments — temporary, dependent on external confirmation. The triad satkāra-māna-pūjā is a precise sociology of religious display: respect from peers, esteem from elders, worship from followers. Cf. Manu 2.162-163 on tolerating asat-kāra and avoiding pūjā. The verse exposes how a vibhūti-display can co-opt the very practices designed to dissolve ahaṅkāra.

Purport

Sometimes penance and austerity are executed to attract people and receive honor, respect and worship from others. Persons in the mode of passion arrange to be worshiped by subordinates and let them wash their feet and offer riches. Such arrangements artificially made by the performance of penances are considered to be in the mode of passion. The results are temporary; they can be continued for some time, but they are not permanent.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
18. Austerity undertaken for praise from others and for respect from others, or undertaken for show alone, is said to be rajasic. It is temporary and uncertain.
Commentary

The austerity performed with ostentation, for the purpose of verbal respect by others who will say he is a great man, for the purpose of gaining bodily respect from others by having them stand up when he approaches, and for the purpose of gaining mental respect from others which will manifest in the future as gifts of money and other things, is rajasic austerity. Its results are very temporary (calam) if they appear at all, and it is uncertain (adhruvam) that they will appear.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
18. Austerity undertaken with the aim of getting verbal praise, bodily respect, and offerings from others, and for show alone, is said to be rajasic. It is temporary and uncertain.
Commentary

Sat kāra means to earn praise from others (words), such as “He is a great ascetic.” Māna means receiving respect using the body, such as rising up when the person approaches. Pūja means worship in the form of such things as getting ones feet washed or receiving gifts of money. Austerity performed for those purposes, and the ostentation (dambhena) is called austerity in the mode of passion. It is temporary (calam) and its results, such as getting praise, are uncertain (adhruvam). One may or may not get those results.

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
17.18 'Respect' means recognition by others. 'Praise' means verbal adulation. 'Reverence' means corporeal actions such as prostration etc. That austerity, practised with expectation of rewards like respect, etc., mentioned above - it is here said to be Rajasa. It is unsteady and impermanent, because of the temporary nature of its rewards like heaven etc.; 'unsteadiness' is the result of the fear of falling. 'Impermanent' means the tendency to perish.

Personal Guidance
Austerity performed for praise, honor, or worship — out of pride — is rajasic; unsteady, transient.
Practical Applications
  • Notice when austerity is for an audience.
  • Reduce or hide the practice that's becoming public-facing.
  • Cultivate hidden austerity.
Reflection Questions
  • Where is my austerity public when it should be private?
  • What hiddenness would purify it?
Analogy

Rajasic austerity is like a candle burning brightly in a shop window — meant to be seen. The flame is real; it costs the wax just the same. But its purpose is the eyes of passers-by, and when the audience leaves, the candle finds no reason to keep burning. Calam adhruvam — flickering, impermanent.
