Bhagavad-gītā 17.19

Important Words
  • mūḍha — foolish
    From √muh — to be bewildered, deluded. Tamasic austerity is not lazy — it is, paired in the verse with grāheṇa, fanatically gripped. The defect is not lack of energy but lack of understanding. Mūḍha is the chapter's recurring mark of tamas: the obstinate grip on a wrong direction.
  • grāheṇa — with endeavor
    Instrumental of grāha — grasp, obstinate hold, even the seizure of a crocodile. Paired with mūḍha, the verse pictures a deluded clutching. The grip itself is the problem: the harder the tamasic austerity grasps its wrong target, the deeper the damage.
  • pīḍayā — by torture
    Instrumental of pīḍā — pressure, torment, deliberate pain-infliction. Crucial distinction: the body's natural strain in dharmic effort is not pīḍā; deliberate self-inflicted harm is. Pīḍā has malice in it — and the verse pairs it with ātmanaḥ to make the malice reflexive.
  • utsādana - artham — for the sake of causing annihilation
    Compound: utsādana (uprooting, destruction) + artham (for the sake of). The verse pairs it with parasya: austerity performed *for the destruction of another*. This is the path of Hiraṇyakaśipu and Rāvaṇa fully developed — tapas weaponised.
  • udāhṛtam — is said to be
    From ud + ā + √hṛ — 'is brought forth, is declared.' The verse's formal classification verb: Kṛṣṇa is not insulting such tapasvīs, He is naming them precisely so that future seekers can identify and avoid the category. The verb makes the classification authoritative.
Penance performed out of foolishness, with self-torture or to destroy or injure others, is said to be in the mode of ignorance.
Purport

There are instances of foolish penance undertaken by demons like Hiraṇyakaśipu, who performed austere penances to become immortal and kill the demigods. He prayed to Brahmā for such things, but ultimately he was killed by the Supreme Personality of Godhead. To undergo penances for something which is impossible is certainly in the mode of ignorance.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
19. Austerity performed out of foolishness, inflicting pain on oneself, or for the purpose of harming others, is tamasic austerity.
Commentary

Mūḍha-graheṇa means to be performed out of foolishness. Parasya utsadana artha m means performed with the intention of harming others.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
19. Austerity performed out of blind attachment, inflicting pain on oneself, or for the purpose of harming or destroying others, is tamasic austerity.
Commentary

Muda grahena means the austerity is performed with stubborn attachment to material enjoyment because of no discernment. Austerity performed in that way, and with pain for the body and senses (ātmanaḥ), or with the intention of harming or destroying others (parasya utsādana artham), is austerity in the mode of ignorance.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

Austerity in ignorance

Out of foolishness
With self-torture
Or to destroy or injure others
e.g. Hiranyakashipu praying to Brahma for immortality
Focus of Learning

(a) Basic principle to practice this austerity of mind – To make mind austere, one must detach from

sense gratification and train the mind to always think good for others

(b) Gravity in thought – Best training for the mind –

Not deviate from Krishna consciousness
Always avoid sense gratification

(c) Satisfaction

Obtained by taking mind away from thoughts of sense enjoyment
Best course to divert the mind
Not by the present age engagements
But by Vedic literatures (Satisfying stories like Puranas and Mahabharata)

(d) Simplicity – Devoid of duplicity and think of others welfare

(e) Self-control

Control of mind implies, detachment from sense gratification
Silence implies always thinking of self-realisation

(f) Purification of one’s existence

By straightforward dealings
Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
17.19 Yat, that; tapah, austerity; which is kriyate, under-taken; mudha-grahena, with a foolish intent, with a conviction arising out of non-discriminating; pidaya, causing pain; atmanah, to oneself (to one's body etc.); va, or; utsadanartham, for the destruction; parasya, of another; tat, that; is udahrtam, said to be; an austerity tamasam, born of tamas. Now the classification of charity is being spoken of:
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
17.19 Deluded persons are those who lack correct understanding. That austerity which is practised from deluded notion, viz., from the obstinate determination by deluded persons, by self-torture regardless of one's own capacity or which is performed for causing sufferings to others - that is said to be Tamasika.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
17.17-19 Sraddhaya etc. upto tamasam udahrtam. There is faith in all the three-fold austerity. the faith of a man of the Sattva is full of austerity itself. The faith of a man of the Rajas is in the Rajas i.e, showing (or hyprocricy) etc. But, the faith (or desire) of a man well established in the Tamas is merely in ruining others. Thus the sage speaks of all the three-fold austerity practised with faith.
Swami Sivananda Classical
Translation
17.19 That austerity which is practised out of a foolish notion, with self-torture, or for the purpose of destroying another, is declared to be Tamasic.
Commentary

17.19 मूढग्राहेण out of a foolish notion? आत्मनः of the self? यत् which? पीडया with torture? क्रियते is practised? तपः austerity? परस्य of another? उत्सादनार्थम् for the purpose of destroying? वा or? तत् that? तामसम् Tamasic? उदाहृतम् is declared.Commentary Some burn sulphur in a pot and place it on their head. Some thrust hooks of iron into their flesh. Some hang themselves with their head downwards over fire and swallow smoke. Some stand in cold water immersed up to the neck. Some torture the body by lighting fires on the four sides (with the sun as the fifth fire -- this is known as the Panchagni Tapas). Some sit in the centre of a circle of fire. Such austerities are Tamasic. These will not help one to attain knowledge of the Self.

Personal Guidance
Austerity performed out of foolishness, with self-torture or to harm others — is tamasic.
Practical Applications
  • Refuse self-tortuous or other-harming practice.
  • Don't romanticize masochistic asceticism.
  • Honor the body as Kṛṣṇa's temple.
Reflection Questions
  • Where do I tolerate self-tortuous practice as 'spiritual'?
  • What body-honoring shift is appropriate?
Analogy

Tamasic austerity is like a man hammering his own hand because he was told hammering is sacred. The action is severe, the pain real, the resolve immense — yet the target is wrong. The harm flows back to the body and outward to others; nothing is offered up, only down.

Stories
Vena Mahārāja

Bhāgavatam describes King Vena, who having descended into tamas, performed practices designed to harm others — forbidding sacrifice, persecuting brāhmaṇas, and exalting himself as the Lord. His resolution was iron; his discipline was real; his target was destruction.

The ṛṣis eventually struck him down with kuśa grass charged by mantra. Vena's reign is the Purāṇic prototype of tāmasa-tapas: the same energy that could have built dharma turned to dismantle it, ending in self-ruin.
