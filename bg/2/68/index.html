Bhagavad-gītā 2.68

तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ६८ ॥

Important Words
  • mahā - bāho — mighty-armed one
    Vocative. Used at moments of strong instruction (cf. 2.26). The hero is reminded of his strength precisely when the instruction calls for the highest discipline. The arm that bends the Gāṇḍīva is the arm that can hold the senses.
  • nigṛhītāni — fully controlled
    Past participle of ni + √grah — to grasp downward, to seize and hold under. *Held down*. Stronger than saṁyamya — not just restrained but firmly clasped beneath.
  • sarvaśaḥ — in every way
    Adverbial. The control is *total* — every direction, every sense, every occasion. The verse closes the loopholes that verse 67 opened: not one wandering sense survives.
  • indriya - arthebhyaḥ — from the sense-objects
    Ablative plural — *away from sense-objects*. Echoes verse 58. The senses are *pulled back from* the objects; that is the action of nigṛhīta.
  • pratiṣṭhitā — fully established
    The chapter's recurring word (cf. 2.57, 58, 61). Repeated as the final refrain. Each verse in this stretch ends with prajñā pratiṣṭhitā — the same image, approached from a different angle each time.
Therefore, O mighty-armed, one whose senses are restrained from their objects is certainly of steady intelligence.
Purport

One can curb the forces of sense gratification only by means of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, or engaging all the senses in the transcendental loving service of the Lord. As enemies are curbed by superior force, the senses can similarly be curbed, not by any human endeavor, but only by keeping them engaged in the service of the Lord. One who has understood this – that only by Kṛṣṇa consciousness is one really established in intelligence and that one should practice this art under the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master – is called a sādhaka, or a suitable candidate for liberation.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
68. He who completely restricts the senses from the sense objects, O mighty-armed one, is fixed in prajṣa.
Commentary

He who controls his mind restricts his senses from sense objects. He is fixed in intelligence. O might-armed one (mahā-bāho), just as you control your enemies with your strength, you should also control your mind.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
68. He who completely restricts the senses from the sense objects (by fixing the mind on Me), O mighty-armed one, is fixed in prajṣa.
Commentary

One whose mind is fixed on Me can become established in ātmā (prajṣā pratiṣṭhitā). O mighty armed one, just as you conquer the enemies, you should conquer the senses. From these verses it should be understood that conquest of the senses takes place spontaneously for the perfected sthita prajṣa by absorbing the mind in bhagavān. But it will arise from practice for the person aspiring to attain the state of sthita prajṣa.

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
2.68 Since the evils arising from the activities of the organs have been described, tasmat, therefore; mahabaho, O mighty-armed one; tasya, his, the sannyasin's; prajna, wisdom; pratisthita, becomes established; yasya, whose; indriyani, organs; sarvasah, in all their varieties, differentiated as mind etc.; nigrhitani, are withdrawn; indriya-arthhyah, from their objects such as sound etc. In the case of a man of steady wisdom in whom has arisen discriminating knowledge, those which are these ordinary and Vedic dealings cease on the eradication of ignorance, they being effects of ignorance. And ignorance ceases because it is opposed to Knowledge. For clarifying this idea, the Lord says:
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
2.68 Therefore, in the way described above, he whose mind is focussed on Me the auspicious object for meditation, and whose senses are thery restrained from sense-objects in everyway, in his mind alone wisdom is firmly set. Sri Krsna now speaks of the state of attainment by one whose senses are subdued and whose mind is serene.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
2.64-68 Raga-dvesa-etc. upto pratisthita. Here the purport is this : He, who controls his mind, is not tossed by the waves of wrath etc., even while he is enjoying the sense-objects; hence he alone is a man of Yoga, a man-of-stabilized-intellect. Extraordinary is the man of Yoga, even while he is attending to the worldly business. While examining this point, the characteristics mark of his (man of Yoga), is briefly related by the Supreme Lord-
Swami Sivananda Classical
Translation
2.68 Therefore, O mighty-armed Arjuna, his knowledge is steady whose senses are completely restrained from sense-objects.
Commentary

2.68 तस्मात् therefore? यस्य whose? महाबाहो O mightyarmed? निगृहीतानि restrained? सर्वशः completely? इन्द्रियाणि the senses? इन्द्रियार्थेभ्यः from the senseobjects? तस्य his? प्रज्ञा knowledge? प्रतिष्ठिता is steady.Commentary When the senses are completely controlled? the mind cannot wander wildly in the sensual grooves. It becomes steady like the lamp in a windless place. The Yogi is now established in the Self and his knowledge is steady. (Cf.III.7).

Swami Gambhīrānanda Classical
Translation
2.68 Therefore, O mighty-armed one, this wisdom becomes established whose organs in all their varieties are withdrawn from their objects.
Shri Purohit Swami Classical
Translation
2.68 Therefore, O Might-in-Arms, he who keeps his senses detached from their objects - take it that his reason is purified.

Personal Guidance
He whose senses are fully withdrawn from sense objects has firm intelligence — this is the state to cultivate.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one sense at a time and bring it under fuller restraint over a season.
  • Track your buddhi's firmness against your senses' restraint — they correlate.
  • Refuse the modern lie that maximum sensory engagement is freedom.
Reflection Questions
  • Which sense, if more restrained, would most strengthen my buddhi?
  • Where am I confusing sensory abundance with spiritual freedom?
Analogy

A king whose army is fully under his command — not skirmishing on its own, not deserting, but standing in formation — is a king at peace. The mahā-bāhu with senses nigṛhīta on every side is this king. His prajñā does not need to fight; it stands.
