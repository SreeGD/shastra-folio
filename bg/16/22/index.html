Bhagavad-gītā 16.22

Important Words
  • vimuktaḥ — being liberated
    Past passive participle of vi-muc — to release. Vi is the intensive: completely freed. Not 'managing the gates' but freed from them. The Gītā promises a real, observable freedom, not merely better coping.
  • kaunteya — O son of Kuntī
    Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna by his mother's name. Kuntī was famously dharmic, brave, devotional. Each time Kṛṣṇa says 'kaunteya' He is reminding Arjuna of the line he carries — relevant when promising elevation.
  • tamaḥ - dvāraiḥ — from the gates of ignorance
    Compound: tamas (darkness, ignorance, the lowest guṇa) + dvāra (gate). The three gates of the previous verse are here named gates of tamas. Crossing them is exit from the mode of ignorance itself.
  • ācarati — performs
    From ā-car — to conduct, to practice. Ā-cāra — conduct. The verse uses the verb form: the freed soul ācarati — conducts himself toward self-benefit. Conduct, not philosophy, is the measure.
  • śreyaḥ — benediction
    Śreyas — that which is genuinely good, as opposed to preyas (the merely pleasing). The freed soul's actions automatically aim at the real welfare of the ātman, paired here with the genitive ātmanaḥ.
  • parām — to the supreme
    Accusative feminine — 'supreme' qualifying gatim. The same parām that 16.20's demoniac fail to approach is here within reach. The word ties verse 22 directly to verse 20 as its opposite.
The man who has escaped these three gates of hell, O son of Kuntī, performs acts conducive to self-realization and thus gradually attains the supreme destination.
Purport

One should be very careful of these three enemies to human life: lust, anger and greed. The more a person is freed from lust, anger and greed, the more his existence becomes pure. Then he can follow the rules and regulations enjoined in the Vedic literature. By following the regulative principles of human life, one gradually raises himself to the platform of spiritual realization. If one is so fortunate, by such practice, to rise to the platform of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, then success is guaranteed for him. In the Vedic literature, the ways of action and reaction are prescribed to enable one to come to the stage of purification. The whole method is based on giving up lust, greed and anger. By cultivating knowledge of this process, one can be elevated to the highest position of self-realization; this self-realization is perfected in devotional service. In that devotional service, the liberation of the conditioned soul is guaranteed. Therefore, according to the Vedic system, there are instituted the four orders of life and the four statuses of life, called the caste system and the spiritual order system. There are different rules and regulations for different castes or divisions of society, and if a person is able to follow them, he will be automatically raised to the highest platform of spiritual realization. Then he can have liberation without a doubt.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
22. The person who is free from these three gates leading to darkness acts in the best interest of the self, and then attains the supreme goal.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
22. The person who is free from these three gates leading to darkness acts in the best interest of the self, and then attains liberation.
Commentary

The result of giving up these three items is described in this verse. He performs the most beneficial practices, which are actions according to his āśrama, and attains liberation (parām gatim).

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

While transmigrating in the tree of this material world we have some choices in human life: whether one wants to go up or he wants to go down. Krsna has given us this choice. If we walk through the gates leading to hell, Krsna gives us directions. But for a sane man Krsna advises to give up those gates leading to hell: lust, anger and greed. Every sane man should give them up. Contemporary civilization being demoniac cultivate lust and greed. And how to perform acts conducive to self‑realization?

[2. To obtain that supreme destination, one's activities should all be in accord with sastra. (23‑24) ]

Personal Guidance
He who has escaped these three gates engages in works for self-elevation — gradually attains the supreme goal.
Practical Applications
  • Trust that escaping these three is itself elevation.
  • Don't underestimate the foundational work.
  • Recognize gradual progress as real progress.
Reflection Questions
  • Where have I underestimated foundational discipline?
  • What renewed appreciation is needed?
Analogy

A traveler who escapes a thorn-thicket walks more freely the rest of the journey. He may not yet be home, but every step now serves arrival. Once the three gates are closed behind him, ordinary action becomes śreyas — a means of self-elevation — automatically.

Stories
Bharata Mahārāja's Forest Years

Having renounced his kingdom and family, Bharata in the Bhāgavatam shows what life looks like after the three gates are shut. He performed worship by the riverbank, ate fallen fruit, kept silence. The verse describes him: vimuktaḥ — liberated from these — and now ācarati ātmanaḥ śreyaḥ — performing acts conducive to self-realization.

He did stumble — the attachment to a deer was an unanticipated knot — but the larger arc of his life is the verse's affirmation: once kāma, krodha, lobha are released, the supreme destination is gradually attained.
