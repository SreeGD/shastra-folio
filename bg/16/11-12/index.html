Important Words
  • cintām — fears and anxieties
    Cintā — anxious worry. Not occasional concern but the chronic condition of the lust-driven mind. Aparimeyām — immeasurable. The verse weighs the demoniac inner life and finds it unweighable.
  • pralaya - antām — unto the point of death
    Pralaya — dissolution, here personal death. Anta — end. The anxiety extends 'up to the end' — never resolved, only stopped by mortality. Even then, the next birth picks up where it left off.
  • kāma - upabhoga — sense gratification
    Upa-bhoga — enjoyment that approaches and consumes. The demoniac philosophy reduces life's purpose (paramāḥ — the highest goal) to this. Once that reduction is made, every other vice follows logically.
  • āśā - pāśa — entanglements in a network of hope
    Āśā — hope, expectation. Pāśa — noose, rope. Hope as a noose — only the Gītā would dare the inversion. The hope of the demoniac is itself the rope that binds them.
  • kāma — of lust
    Listed twice in this verse and again later — Kṛṣṇa is naming the root. Kāma-krodha-parāyaṇāḥ: those who have taken refuge in lust and anger. The phrasing makes lust and anger their iṣṭa-devatā, their chosen deity.
  • anyāyena — illegally
    An-nyāya — not according to nyāya, not by just means. Instrumental case. The means become invisible to the demoniac; only the goal of wealth matters. Process indifference is itself a symptom.
They believe that to gratify the senses is the prime necessity of human civilization. Thus until the end of life their anxiety is immeasurable. Bound by a network of hundreds of thousands of desires and absorbed in lust and anger, they secure money by illegal means for sense gratification.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Two verses paint the inner life of the āsura: a chronic anxiety that ends only at death, a single conviction that sense-enjoyment is everything, hundreds of fragile hopes tied around the heart, and — in service of all that — wealth pursued by any means that work. The portrait is uncomfortably modern.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The pairing is deliberate: v.11 names the inner state (immeasurable cintā, fixed conviction); v.12 names the consequent outward behavior (illicit wealth accumulation). 'Pralayāntām' is grim — the worry cannot end while the body lasts. 'Āśā-pāśa' (hopes as nooses) is one of the Gītā's most quoted metaphors. 'Anyāyena' confirms the means are not merely competitive but śāstra-violating; cf. BG 3.16.

Purport

The demoniac accept that the enjoyment of the senses is the ultimate goal of life, and this concept they maintain until death. They do not believe in life after death, and they do not believe that one takes on different types of bodies according to one’s karma, or activities in this world. Their plans for life are never finished, and they go on preparing plan after plan, all of which are never finished. We have personal experience of a person of such demoniac mentality who, even at the point of death, was requesting the physician to prolong his life for four years more because his plans were not yet complete. Such foolish people do not know that a physician cannot prolong life even for a moment. When the notice is there, there is no consideration of the man’s desire. The laws of nature do not allow a second beyond what one is destined to enjoy. The demoniac person, who has no faith in God or the Supersoul within himself, performs all kinds of sinful activities simply for sense gratification. He does not know that there is a witness sitting within his heart. The Supersoul is observing the activities of the individual soul. As it is stated in the Upaniṣads, there are two birds sitting in one tree; one is acting and enjoying or suffering the fruits of the branches, and the other is witnessing. But one who is demoniac has no knowledge of Vedic scripture, nor has he any faith; therefore he feels free to do anything for sense enjoyment, regardless of the consequences.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
16.11 Those who are sure to die today or tomorrow 'obsess themselves with cares' in regard to objects the attainment of which is not possible even by the time of death. Likewise, they look upon 'enjoyment of desires' as their highest aim, viz., they regard the satisfaction of sensual enjoyments as the highest aim of human life. They are convinced that this is all, viz., they are assured that there is no value in human life greater than this.

Personal Guidance
Bound by hundreds of desires until death, satisfying lust, anger as their refuge — they unjustly amass wealth for sense gratification.
Practical Applications
  • Notice when the spirit of 'amassing for myself' creeps in.
  • Distinguish need-meeting from amassing.
  • Refuse anger as a refuge.
Reflection Questions
  • Where is amassing-for-myself active in my life?
  • What practice replaces it?
Analogy

A net thrown over a swimmer. Each knot is a hope, each rope an anxiety. The more he thrashes for one fish, the tighter the net binds his arms. Hundreds of thousands of knots — āśā-pāśa-śataiḥ — and beneath them the swimmer no longer remembers he is a swimmer.

Stories
Aśvatthāmā's Pursuit

On the night the Pāṇḍava sons slept, Aśvatthāmā — driven by anger at Duryodhana's death and the schemes of vengeance — entered their tents and killed the five Upapāṇḍavas in their beds. The act bound him to a curse from Kṛṣṇa: to wander the earth for thousands of years, festering with an unhealable wound, denied even the release of death.

Kāma-krodha-parāyaṇāḥ — situated in the mentality of lust and anger — yields exactly such a fate. The wound he carries is the verse made flesh.

Duḥśāsana's Hands

Bound by the network of his brother's schemes and his own lust for power and humiliation of others, Duḥśāsana dragged Draupadī by the hair into the Kuru assembly. The Mahābhārata makes him the verse's case study: anyāyena artha-sañcayān — accumulating by illegal means — and parāyaṇāḥ — entirely given over to lust and anger.

Bhīma's vow to drink the blood of those hands, fulfilled at Kurukṣetra, completes the arc the verse predicts.
