Bhagavad-gītā 7.29

Important Words
  • jarā — from old age
    From √jṝ (to grow old, to decay). Paired in the verse with maraṇa (death) — the two undeniable conditions of embodiment. The verse identifies the right motive for taking refuge: not material gain, but liberation from the body's terminal arc.
  • mokṣāya — for the purpose of liberation
    Dative of mokṣa — for the sake of liberation. The verse uses the technical term for liberation as the operative goal. Note: the seeker comes to Kṛṣṇa for mokṣa — and gets more, as 8.16 will reveal.
  • āśritya — taking shelter of
    Gerund of ā + √śri (to take refuge). 'Having taken shelter.' Same root as the āśrayaḥ of 7.1 — the chapter began with mat-āśrayaḥ and now closes its arc with the same teaching.
  • brahma — Brahman
    The Absolute. The verse says such a person 'knows Brahman entirely' — meaning Kṛṣṇa's bhakta knows Brahman as a feature of His person, not as a rival ultimate. The personal includes the impersonal.
  • adhyātmam — transcendental, of the self
    adhi (over, concerning) + ātman (self). The science of the self. Together with karma in the same line, the verse names three knowledge-domains — Brahman, adhyātma, karma — all of which the Kṛṣṇa-refugee comes to know in full. Chapter 8 will begin by unpacking these very terms.
Intelligent persons who are endeavoring for liberation from old age and death take refuge in Me in devotional service. They are actually Brahman because they entirely know everything about transcendental activities.
Purport

Birth, death, old age and diseases affect this material body, but not the spiritual body. There is no birth, death, old age and disease for the spiritual body, so one who attains a spiritual body, becomes one of the associates of the Supreme Personality of Godhead and engages in eternal devotional service is really liberated. Ahaṁ brahmāsmi: I am spirit. It is said that one should understand that he is Brahman, spirit soul. This Brahman conception of life is also in devotional service, as described in this verse. The pure devotees are transcendentally situated on the Brahman platform, and they know everything about transcendental activities. Four kinds of impure devotees who engage themselves in the transcendental service of the Lord achieve their respective goals, and by the grace of the Supreme Lord, when they are fully Kṛṣṇa conscious, they actually enjoy spiritual association with the Supreme Lord. But those who are worshipers of demigods never reach the Supreme Lord in His supreme planet. Even the less intelligent Brahman-realized persons cannot reach the supreme planet of Kṛṣṇa known as Goloka Vṛndāvana. Only persons who perform activities in Kṛṣṇa consciousness ( mām āśritya ) are actually entitled to be called Brahman, because they are actually endeavoring to reach the Kṛṣṇa planet. Such persons have no misgivings about Kṛṣṇa, and thus they are factually Brahman. Those who are engaged in worshiping the form or arcā of the Lord, or who are engaged in meditation on the Lord simply for liberation from material bondage, also know, by the grace of the Lord, the purports of Brahman, adhibhūta, etc., as explained by the Lord in the next chapter.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
29. Those yogīs who, taking shelter of Me, strive for freedom from birth and death, know Brahman, the jīva, and rebirth of the jīva due to the influence karma.
Commentary

It has been said that the three types of sakāma-bhaktas who worship Me become successful, but the worshippers of devatās (also sakāma) fail. Those who are not qualified for worshipping the Lord have also been described. Now the Lord speaks about a fourth type of sakāma-bhakta. Those yogīs who strive to destroy old age and death (jarā-maraṇa-mokṣāya yatanti), who desire liberation (mokṣa-kāmī), and consequently worship Me, know all about the famous Brahman (the Lord). [Note: Brahman will be defined as akṣara, in verse 3 of the next chapter, and akṣara will be defined as Nārāyaṇa in verse 21.] They know about the jīva (adhyātmam), present as the controller (adhi) of the body (ātmānam), and the repeated bodies of the jīva generated from all types of actions (karma akhilam), due to the power of devotion to Me.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
29. Those yogis striving for freedom from birth and death who take shelter of Me know the jīva and the impressions which stick to the ātmā and the force of karma.
Commentary

Thus, it has been stated that the three types of devotees with desires (sakāma bhaktas) having experienced material pleasures, surrender to Me and attain Me, whereas the devotees of the devatās continue to take birth. There is also another type of sakāma bhakta. This is stated in this verse. Desiring only freedom from birth and death, they take shelter of Me and make efforts to engage in worshipping the deity of Me, by offering respects and other actions to Me. They know the famous brahman (jīva) along with things connected to it (kṛtsnam). They also know about the impressions which cause the jīva to act (adhyātmam) and all about karma. The words brahman, adhyātma and akhila karma and the words mentioned in the next verse will be explained in the next chapter by the Lord. By worshipping My form, they realize their object of knowledge, and attain liberation, but they do not bring Me under control or become objects of My affection. The smṛti says: sakṛd yad aṅga pratimāntar-ahitā mano-mayīṁ bhāgavatīṁ dadau gatim If even only once or even by force one brings the form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead into one’s mind, one can attain the supreme salvation by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa. Personal Guidance
Those who strive for liberation from old age and death take refuge in Me — they understand Brahman, the spiritual self, and karma in full.
Practical Applications
  • Recognize liberation as the goal worth striving for.
  • Use Kṛṣṇa as the means.
  • Cultivate full understanding of Brahman + self + karma.
Reflection Questions
  • Have I given up striving for ultimate liberation?
  • What would full striving look like?
Analogy

A scholar who studies the mountain from books, the map, the gradient charts and the local botany — knows the mountain, but has not stepped on it. Liberation-seekers who take refuge in Kṛṣṇa step on the mountain. The brahman, adhyātma, karma the verse names are no longer subjects; they become terrain underfoot.

Stories
Maitreyī's question to Yājñavalkya

When Yājñavalkya, ready to renounce the world, offered to divide his wealth between his two wives, Maitreyī refused her share with the question: yenāhaṁ nāmṛtā syām, kim ahaṁ tena kuryām — what shall I do with that by which I cannot become immortal?

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka records the teaching that followed. But the Bhāgavatam is the ripened fruit of that same Upaniṣadic tree, and its answer to Maitreyī's question is unambiguous: amṛtatva is not impersonal merger — it is taking refuge in Kṛṣṇa. Her instinct was the verse's instinct — jarā-maraṇa-mokṣāya yatanti — but the verse completes it where the Upaniṣad pauses: mām āśritya. Refuge in the Person. Mokṣa from old age and death is granted only there.
