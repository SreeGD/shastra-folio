Bhagavad-gītā 7.2

Important Words
  • jñānam — phenomenal knowledge
    From √jñā, to know. Theoretical, conceptual knowledge — what a thing is. The first half of the package.
  • vijñānam — numinous knowledge, realized knowledge
    vi- (specially, fully) + jñāna. Not 'more information' but knowledge made real in experience. Prabhupāda renders it 'numinous' to mark its non-theoretical edge. Knowing without vijñāna is half-knowing.
  • aśeṣataḥ — in full, without remainder
    Privative: a- (no) + śeṣa (remainder). The same word used for Ananta-Śeṣa, the unending serpent. Kṛṣṇa promises no left-over corner of reality outside this disclosure.
  • jñātavyam — knowable, what remains to be known
    Gerundive of √jñā: 'that which is to be known.' The verse's astonishing claim is that once this is heard, this category empties out.
  • avaśiṣyate — remains
    From ava + √śiṣ (to leave over). Paired with na — nothing is left over. Kṛṣṇa is not exaggerating; He is naming the structural sufficiency of knowing Him.
I shall now declare unto you in full this knowledge, both phenomenal and numinous. This being known, nothing further shall remain for you to know.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Kṛṣṇa frames the impending teaching as exhaustive: jñāna (conceptual knowledge) plus vijñāna (its experiential realization). Together they form a self-contained answer — once known, nothing further needs investigation. The phrasing dignifies the rest of the chapter as climactic disclosure.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The pair jñāna / vijñāna recurs in Bhāgavatam and Upaniṣads as theory-and-realization or general-and-specific. Here it points to the difference between knowing about Kṛṣṇa (jñāna) and tasting Him (vijñāna). The exhaustive claim (na anyaj jñātavyam) is not a denial of other sciences but a doctrinal assertion of Kṛṣṇa as the integrating ground of all knowables — cf. Muṇḍaka 1.1.3, 'kasmin nu bhagavo vijñāte sarvam idaṁ vijñātaṁ bhavati'.

Purport

Complete knowledge includes knowledge of the phenomenal world, the spirit behind it, and the source of both of them. This is transcendental knowledge. The Lord wants to explain the above-mentioned system of knowledge because Arjuna is Kṛṣṇa’s confidential devotee and friend. In the beginning of the Fourth Chapter this explanation was given by the Lord, and it is again confirmed here: complete knowledge can be achieved only by the devotee of the Lord in disciplic succession directly from the Lord. Therefore one should be intelligent enough to know the source of all knowledge, who is the cause of all causes and the only object for meditation in all types of yoga practice. When the cause of all causes becomes known, then everything knowable becomes known, and nothing remains unknown. The Vedas ( Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1.3) say, kasminn u bhagavo vijñāte sarvam idaṁ vijñātaṁ bhavatīti.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
2. I will explain to you knowledge of My powers (jṣāna) along with knowledge of My sweetness (vijṣāna), knowing which nothing else remains to be known.
Commentary

Before attaining the stage of attachment to Me, the devotee understands (jṣānam) about My powers. After that, he realizes (vijṣānam) My sweetness. Hear about both of these. Knowing this, nothing else remains to be known, for knowledge and realization of My impersonal aspect are included in it.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
2. I will explain to you knowledge of My powers (jṣāna) along with knowledge of My sweetness (vijṣāna), knowing which nothing else remains to be known.
Commentary

The Lord praises the knowledge that He is about to explain. Jṣāna concerns the svarūpa of the Lord relating to his spiritual and material energies (jīva and material prakṛti). I will speak or teach this knowledge in fullness (aśeṣataḥ) along with vijṣāna, knowledge of My svarūpa apart from those energies, [Note: This indicates his form and pastimes not related to material creation—the form and pastimes in the spiritual world.] to you who are surrendered. Understanding this knowledge which I have promised to speak, knowledge of both of these topics concerning My svarūpa, the cause of everything, which should be the object of your meditation, nothing else will remain to be known by you, who are inquisitive about and absorbed in the highest path, since everything else is included in this knowledge.

Personal Guidance
I shall now declare in full this knowledge, both phenomenal and noumenal — knowing which, nothing further remains to be known.
Practical Applications
  • Treat the Gītā's claim seriously — knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is sufficient.
  • Don't endlessly chase additional teachings; deepen the one.
  • Trust the sufficiency.
Reflection Questions
  • What other teachings am I chasing instead of deepening this one?
  • What would 'sufficient' feel like?
Analogy

A man searching for treasure can spend his life turning over pebbles, or he can be handed the one stone beneath which it lies. Kṛṣṇa offers the second. Jñāna with vijñāna — the map and the territory together — is the stone that, once lifted, leaves nothing further to lift.

Stories
Naciketas at Yama's door

Young Naciketas, sent by his father to Yama, refused the king of death's offers of wealth, kingdoms, and long life. He asked instead for the one knowledge after which nothing remained to be asked.

Yama tested him, deferred, tempted — and finally, seeing the boy's resolve, taught him the science of the immortal self. The Kaṭha Upaniṣad records that conversation. Naciketas' instinct was exactly Kṛṣṇa's promise here: refuse the many partial knowledges; demand the one after which nothing further remains.
