Important Words
  • puṇyaḥ — original, pure, sacred
    puṇya usually means 'meritorious' but here qualifies gandha — 'the original, unspoiled fragrance.' Kṛṣṇa is the earth's perfume before any decay, the type-fragrance behind every particular smell.
  • gandhaḥ — fragrance
    The defining quality of pṛthivī (earth) in sāṅkhya. Each gross element has one distinctive sense-quality; earth's is smell. Kṛṣṇa identifies precisely with the element's signature.
  • jīvanam — life
    The principle that animates. Not life-form but life-as-such. Kṛṣṇa is what is alive in everything alive — a teaching with vast pastoral consequences for how a devotee treats other beings.
  • tapaḥ — penance, austerity
    From √tap (to heat, to burn). The inner fire of self-discipline. Kṛṣṇa is not merely rewarding the ascetic — He is the ascetic's heat. The austerity is His presence.
  • tapasviṣu — in those who practice penance
    Locative plural. The verse keeps locating Kṛṣṇa within — not above, not behind, but in. The grammar is theological: He is interior to the practice itself.
I am the original fragrance of the earth, and I am the heat in fire. I am the life of all that lives, and I am the penances of all ascetics.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Continuing the meditative inventory: Kṛṣṇa is the auspicious original fragrance of earth, the heat of fire, the life-force within every creature, and the austerity that sustains the tapasvī's discipline. The list elevates everyday phenomena into windows onto the divine.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Pṛthivyāṁ puṇyo gandhaḥ — Kṛṣṇa is the 'original' (puṇya) fragrance, not just any smell, suggesting an archetypal purity from which sensory experiences derive. Tapaḥ in tapasvinaḥ recasts ascetic effort itself as a divine quality, not a human achievement — the energy behind the discipline is Bhagavān, anticipating BG 9.27's command to dedicate every austerity to Him.

Purport

Puṇya means that which is not decomposed; puṇya is original. Everything in the material world has a certain flavor or fragrance, as the flavor and fragrance in a flower, or in the earth, in water, in fire, in air, etc. The uncontaminated flavor, the original flavor, which permeates everything, is Kṛṣṇa. Similarly, everything has a particular original taste, and this taste can be changed by the mixture of chemicals. So everything original has some smell, some fragrance and some taste. Vibhāvasu means fire. Without fire we cannot run factories, we cannot cook, etc., and that fire is Kṛṣṇa. The heat in the fire is Kṛṣṇa. According to Vedic medicine, indigestion is due to a low temperature in the belly. So even for digestion fire is needed. In Kṛṣṇa consciousness we become aware that earth, water, fire, air and every active principle, all chemicals and all material elements are due to Kṛṣṇa. The duration of man’s life is also due to Kṛṣṇa. Therefore by the grace of Kṛṣṇa, man can prolong his life or diminish it. So Kṛṣṇa consciousness is active in every sphere.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
9. I am the fragrance of the earth element, the heat in fire, the life in all entities, the austerity in the tapasvī.
Commentary

Puṇya means “pleasing” or “natural” according to Amara Kośa. I am the natural fragrance of earth element. Ca indicates that this statement applies to all the elements. Thus, I am the pleasing taste of water, the pleasing form of fire as well as the natural fragrance of the earth element. I am the power of fire, the essence—that which can burn, illuminate, and relieve cold in all things. I am the life span of all entities—their essence. I am the toleration of suffering, the essence of those performing austerities. [Note: In this verse the Lord is the essence of all items, except the fragrance of earth. Fragrance is the cause of earth.]

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
9. I am the fragrance of the earth element, the heat in fire, the life in all entities, and the endurance in the tapasvī.
Commentary

I am the sense object of natural (existing before transformation) fragrance (puṇyaḥ gandhaḥ) in the earth. The word ca is employed to indicate that the word puṇyaḥ applies to all the other sense objects in all other elements as well. I am the energy in fire—the form (rūpa) which enables all things to be cooked and illuminated. [Note: Form or rūpa is the sense object sustaining the element fire. Tejas is here regarded as equivalent to rūpa.] The word ca indicates that in air I am the natural sense object known as touch, which gives pain on touching something hot. [Note: In this way all the five elements are covered.] I am the life span of the living entities. I am the ability to endure the dualities such as heat and cold in the performers of austerities.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Bhumir, the earth, is one of Krsna's energies. All the fragrances come from the earth ‑ so they come from Krsna. We plant the seed of a rose, the rose comes up. All we do is adding water. And the flower smells like a rose. Then we plant another type of seed, all we do is adding water, which has taste but no fragrance, and then...a campaka flower comes up, or a jasmine flower... all these different fragrances they are being extracted from the earth. How wonderful it is that Krsna has put the fragrances in the earth. Fire is another of Krsna's energies. And Krsna is the heat in fire, which we all appreciate (specially in winter). What is the life of all that lives? ‑ The soul. And Krsna explains 'apareyam tv anyan..', that the soul is one of His energies, non‑different from Him.

Personal Guidance
I am the original fragrance of the earth, the heat in fire, the life in all that lives, the austerity of ascetics.
Practical Applications
  • Smell, warm yourself, breathe — and know Him as that quality.
  • Don't treat sense experience as profane; it is shot through with Him.
  • Use perception itself as remembrance.
Reflection Questions
  • Which sense most naturally connects me to Him?
  • Which most rarely?
Analogy

Catch fragrance from a flower and you cannot say where in the petal it lives — it is the petal's perfume, not a separable contents. So Kṛṣṇa is the fragrance of earth, the heat in fire, the life in life. Not located in them; constituting their particular quality.

Stories
Haridāsa Ṭhākura's three hundred thousand names

Cast out, beaten through twenty-two marketplaces, refused entry to the temple because of his birth — Haridāsa Ṭhākura responded by chanting three lakhs of holy names each day in a cave, his life literally a tapasya unbroken until death.

Caitanya Mahāprabhu declared him Nāmācārya. Where did the strength come from? Kṛṣṇa says tapaḥ … asmi tapasviṣu — I am the penance in those who do penance. Haridāsa's austerity was not his own performance; it was the Lord, living in him as the fire of his vow.
