Important Words
  • samaḥ — equal, equipoised
    Repeated twice in the verse — first toward friends and enemies, then toward heat, cold, pleasure, pain. The hinge word of the chapter's character sketch. Sameness is not indifference; it is the devotee's even ground under shifting feet.
  • māna — in honor
    Paired in the verse with apamānayoḥ. The most tempting half of the duality for the spiritually accomplished — being respected. The devotee receives it as weather.
  • apamānayoḥ — and dishonor
    Locative dual of apamāna. The dual ending shows the verse explicitly pairing māna with its opposite. The devotee receives both as one weather.
  • saṅga - vivarjitaḥ — free from contaminating association
    Compound: saṅga (attachment, company) + vivarjita (avoided). The whole Gītā's diagnosis (saṅga in 2.62) and prescription in two words. The devotee keeps no company that pulls his mind sideways.
  • maunī — silent
    From mauna — 'silence,' especially the deliberate restraint of speech. Not muteness — disciplined economy. The bhakta speaks of Kṛṣṇa, and is otherwise still.
  • aniketaḥ — without fixed residence
    Negation of niketa (dwelling). Not homeless by misfortune — homeless by choice, because no place on earth has been allowed to become 'mine.'
  • sthira — fixed
    Paired in the verse with matiḥ. From sthā, 'to stand.' The character traits do not float free — they stand on settled ground.
  • matiḥ — determination, intelligence
    From man, 'to think.' Mati is the considered judgment of the buddhi. Sthira-mati is settled intelligence about Kṛṣṇa, without which all the equanimity collapses into mere stoicism.
  • bhakti - mān — possessing devotion
    Repeated from v.17. The whole list is held together by active bhakti — without it the qualities resemble Buddhist or Sāṅkhya equanimity. With it, they are the Vaiṣṇava signature.
One who is equal to friends and enemies, who is equipoised in honor and dishonor, heat and cold, happiness and distress, fame and infamy, who is always free from contaminating association, always silent and satisfied with anything, who doesn’t care for any residence, who is fixed in knowledge and who is engaged in devotional service – such a person is very dear to Me.
Purport

A devotee is always free from all bad association. Sometimes one is praised and sometimes one is defamed; that is the nature of human society. But a devotee is always transcendental to artificial fame and infamy, distress or happiness. He is very patient. He does not speak of anything but the topics about Kṛṣṇa; therefore he is called silent. Silent does not mean that one should not speak; silent means that one should not speak nonsense. One should speak only of essentials, and the most essential speech for the devotee is to speak for the sake of the Supreme Lord. A devotee is happy in all conditions; sometimes he may get very palatable foodstuffs, sometimes not, but he is satisfied. Nor does he care for any residential facility. He may sometimes live underneath a tree, and he may sometimes live in a very palatial building; he is attracted to neither. He is called fixed because he is fixed in his determination and knowledge. We may find some repetition in the descriptions of the qualifications of a devotee, but this is just to emphasize the fact that a devotee must acquire all these qualifications. Without good qualifications, one cannot be a pure devotee. Harāv abhaktasya kuto mahad-guṇāḥ: one who is not a devotee has no good qualification. One who wants to be recognized as a devotee should develop the good qualifications. Of course he does not extraneously endeavor to acquire these qualifications, but engagement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and devotional service automatically helps him develop them.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Personal Guidance
Equal to friends and enemies, equal in honor and dishonor, in heat and cold, free from contamination and possessiveness, content with whatever happens — silent, taking shelter — is dear to Me.
Practical Applications
  • Test equality across the dualities: friend/enemy, honor/dishonor, heat/cold.
  • Cultivate content acceptance.
  • Honor silence and shelter-taking as practices.
Reflection Questions
  • Which duality most consistently breaks my equality?
  • What practice would steady it?
Analogy

A traveler on a long road carries no attachment to any inn, befriends those he meets without resenting those who pass him by, eats whatever the village offers, speaks little, and walks on. Honor and dishonor are roadside towns; heat and cold are weather; his destination is steady. Such a traveler, fixed in knowledge and engaged in bhakti, is dear to Kṛṣṇa.

Stories
Rūpa and Sanātana under the trees

Rūpa Gosvāmī and his brother Sanātana, once wealthy ministers, spent their later years in Vṛndāvana with no fixed residence. One night they slept beneath one tree; the next, beneath another. Friends and enemies, honor (the Mughal court had once bowed to them) and dishonor (Sanātana was once jailed by his old master), heat and cold — all became one weather.

They were maunī when not glorifying Kṛṣṇa, santuṣṭa with whatever mādhukarī the day brought, aniketa with no house of their own, and sthira-mati on Kṛṣṇa's līlā in Vraja. Caitanya Mahāprabhu loved them as Kṛṣṇa loves the man this verse describes.

Sudāmā walking to Dvārakā

Sudāmā the brāhmaṇa, Kṛṣṇa's childhood friend, walked to Dvārakā in rags, carrying only a small bundle of flat rice his wife had borrowed from neighbors. He cared neither for his own poverty nor for the splendor of Kṛṣṇa's palace when he arrived. He sought no honor and did not flinch at the guards' surprise; he asked for nothing and accepted whatever came.

When he returned home and found his hut transformed into a palace, his evenness did not change. Sama in distress, sama in fortune, mauni about his own deserts, santuṣṭa with whatever Kṛṣṇa arranged — Sudāmā is this verse walking on the road.
