    Arjuna inquired: Which are considered to be more perfect, those who are always properly engaged in Your devotional service or those who worship the impersonal Brahman, the unmanifested?
    Purport

    Kṛṣṇa has now explained about the personal, the impersonal and the universal and has described all kinds of devotees and yogīs . Generally, the transcendentalists can be divided into two classes. One is the impersonalist, and the other is the personalist. The personalist devotee engages himself with all energy in the service of the Supreme Lord. The impersonalist also engages himself, not directly in the service of Kṛṣṇa but in meditation on the impersonal Brahman, the unmanifested. We find in this chapter that of the different processes for realization of the Absolute Truth, bhakti-yoga, devotional service, is the highest. If one at all desires to have the association of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, then he must take to devotional service. Those who worship the Supreme Lord directly by devotional service are called personalists. Those who engage themselves in meditation on the impersonal Brahman are called impersonalists. Arjuna is here questioning which position is better. There are different ways to realize the Absolute Truth, but Kṛṣṇa indicates in this chapter that bhakti-yoga, or devotional service to Him, is the highest of all. It is the most direct, and it is the easiest means for association with the Godhead. In the Second Chapter of Bhagavad-gītā, the Supreme Lord explained that a living entity is not the material body; he is a spiritual spark. And the Absolute Truth is the spiritual whole. In the Seventh Chapter He spoke of the living entity as being part and parcel of the supreme whole and recommended that he transfer his attention fully to the whole. Then again in the Eighth Chapter it was said that anyone who thinks of Kṛṣṇa at the time of quitting his body is at once transferred to the spiritual sky, to the abode of Kṛṣṇa. And at the end of the Sixth Chapter the Lord clearly said that of all yogīs, one who always thinks of Kṛṣṇa within himself is considered the most perfect. So in practically every chapter the conclusion has been that one should be attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa, for that is the highest spiritual realization. Nevertheless, there are those who are not attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa. They are so firmly detached that even in the preparation of commentaries to Bhagavad-gītā they want to distract other people from Kṛṣṇa and transfer all devotion to the impersonal brahma-jyotir . They prefer to meditate on the impersonal form of the Absolute Truth, which is beyond the reach of the senses and is not manifest. And so, factually, there are two classes of transcendentalists. Now Arjuna is trying to settle the question of which process is easier and which of the classes is most perfect. In other words, he is clarifying his own position because he is attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa. He is not attached to the impersonal Brahman. He wants to know whether his position is secure. The impersonal manifestation, either in this material world or in the spiritual world of the Supreme Lord, is a problem for meditation. Actually, one cannot perfectly conceive of the impersonal feature of the Absolute Truth. Therefore Arjuna wants to say, “What is the use of such a waste of time?” Arjuna experienced in the Eleventh Chapter that to be attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa is best because he could thus understand all other forms at the same time and there was no disturbance to his love for Kṛṣṇa. This important question asked of Kṛṣṇa by Arjuna will clarify the distinction between the impersonal and personal conceptions of the Absolute Truth.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    1. Arjuna said: Who are the best knowers of yoga— those devotees who worship You as just described, with desire to constantly associate with You, or those who worship the unmanifest Brahman?
    Commentary

    In the twelfth chapter, the superiority of all types of devotees to the jṣānīs is described, and among the devotees those who are endowed with wonderful qualities like lack of hatred are praised. Arjuna heard the great superiority of bhakti at the beginning of the topic of bhakti in the following words: yoginām api sarveṣāṁ mad-gatenāntar-ātmanā śraddhāvān bhajate yo māṁ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ But I consider he who worships Me with faith, with mind attached to Me, to be greater than all types of yogīs. BG 6.47 Arjuna, desiring to hear about the superiority of bhakti, even if it is in summary, asks a question. The devotees described as being constantly engaged, worship You as Śyāmasundara, mentioned at the end of the last chapter with - mat-karma-kṛn mat-paramaḥ (BG 11.55). Others worship the Brahman (akṣara) with no qualities (avyakta means nirviśeṣa) described in the śrutis with statements such as etad vai tad akṣaraṁ gārgi brāhmaṇā abhivadanty asthūlam anaṇv ahrasvam: O Gārgī, that akṣara, which is not wide, not small and not short, is called Brahman. (Bṛhad Āraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.8.9) Among these two types of persons who know yoga, who are the best knowers of yoga (yoga-vittamaḥ)? Who know the best process for attaining You? Or, do they not know the process for attaining You? In a comparison involving two items, usually the form yoga-vittara (which of the two types know yoga better) would be used. The word yoga-vittama should be understood to suggest that among the better of the two types of knowers of yoga (yoga-vittarā), who are the best knowers of yoga (yoga-vittamā)?

    Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
    Translation
    1. Arjuna said: Who are the best knowers of yoga— those devotees who worship You as just described with desire to constantly associate with You, or those who first worship the ātmā?
    Commentary

    In the twelfth chapter, the Lord says that among all processes, pure bhakti, having the greatest power, enables one to quickly attain the Lord. In the second and consequent chapters, one particular path was described, in which after one understands about and realizes the jīvātmā, one then meditates on the Lord, the aṁśī. This is illustrated in verses like avināśi tu tad viddhi. (BG 2.17) A second path was shown in the seventh and consequent chapters, in which, after understanding that the jīva is an aṁśa of the Lord, one meditates upon the source of the aṁśa, the aṁśī, the Supreme Lord, by the process of bhakti with hearing and other activities. Verse such as mayy āsakta-manāḥ pārtha (BG 7.1) illustrate this. In those chapters, bhakti mixed with yoga was described in such verses as prayāṇa-kāle. (BG 8.10) Jṣāna mixed in the process of bhakti was described in such verses as jṣāna-yajṣena cāpy anye. (BG 9.15) At the end of the sixth chapter, before the middle six chapters, with the verse yoginām api sarveṣāṁ (BG 6.47) the pure bhakti of those exclusively attached to the Lord, who were also the best among all, was described. Arjuna now asks a question. There are those desiring paramātmā who, being constantly absorbed in You by the processes thus described (evam) in verses such as mayy āsakta-manāḥ pārtha (BG 7.1), worship You, Śyāmasundara Kṛṣṇa, with everything, such as body, senses and mind. There are also those desiring paramātmā who worship the jīva’s svarūpa, the akṣaram, invisible to the material senses and endeavor to realize the jīva akṣara by dhāraṇa, dhyāna and samādhi. Of these two types of people, which persons have the quickest process of reaching the goal (yoga vittamāḥ)?

    The meaning is this. One choice is to attain the Lord by first realizing ātmā and then meditating on the Lord. Since the jīva is the source of bondage, by realizing ātmā one becomes free of obstacles, and can then attain the Lord. But because of the difficulty in meditating on the jīvātma which is very small and formless, can the goal be accomplished by meditation on the ātma? The other process is exclusive bhakti to the Lord, which quickly destroys all obstacles and yields realization of the Lord. They are completely absorbed in that bhakti. What is the best process of all the processes of these two types of persons (teṣām)? Please tell me.

    Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    From the Purport: "Krsna has now explained about the personal, the impersonal and the universal and has described all kinds of devotees and yogis." This question of Arjuna may also be understood that after the universal form was seen, although it is not exactly an impersonal form but still is not exactly a personal form, Arjuna is also making sure that no one becomes confused and thinks that this universal form is Supreme. Therefore he asks Krsna again, which is considered to be the more perfect. What is his question? Which realization is higher or which is the better process? The question is: "Which is the better process and also which is the better realization?" Srila Prabhupada says in his Purport: "Krsna has now explained about the personal, the impersonal and the universal and has described all kinds of devotees and yogis. Generally, the transcendentalists can be divided into two classes. One is the impersonalist, and the other is the personalist. The personalist devotee engages himself with all energy in the service of the Supreme Lord. The impersonalist also engages himself, not directly in the service of Krsna but in meditation on the impersonal Brahman, the unmanifested. They prefer to meditate on the impersonal form of the Absolute Truth, which is beyond the reach of the senses and is not manifest." When the universal form came out, it actually disturbed Arjuna's love for Krsna. But if one actually knows Krsna, then he has love for Krsna and Paramatma simultaneously. The two: being merged in the Brahman and serving Krsna with great 'priti' or affection, they are contradictory.

    Translation
    12.1 Arjuna said These are two types of spiritual aspirants who are contrasted thus: (1) On the one hand there are those devotees who adore You 'thus'; namely, in the way taught in such text as 'Whosoever works for Me' (11.55), and who are desirous of being ever 'integrated' with You, namely, considering You as the supreme end. They adore You in utter devotion - You, the ocean of boundless attributes of limitless excellence like grace, affability, omniscience, true resolve etc., and endowed with all glory. (2) On the other hand there are those who meditate on the 'Imperishable', (Aksara) namely, the individual self in Its true nature, which is the same as the 'Unmanifest' (Avyakta), namely that whose nature cannot be grasped by organs such as the eye etc. The estion posed is: Which of these two classes of devotees have greater knowledge of Yoga? Who would reach their respective goals sooner? Such is the meaning of the estion. Sri Krsna clearly states later on, 'O Arjuna, I become before long their redeemer from the fatal sea of recurring births and deaths' (12.7), with reference to the speed with which the latter kind of devotees reach Him.

    Personal Guidance
    Arjuna asks the perennial question: which is better — worship of Your personal form, or of the impersonal unmanifest?
    Practical Applications
    • Welcome the question rather than predetermining the answer.
    • Notice your own pull toward personal vs. impersonal worship.
    • Bring the question to scripture, not just preference.
    Reflection Questions
    • Which way does my temperament tilt — personal or impersonal?
    • What does scripture's answer ask of that tilt?
    Analogy

    Two travelers ask the wise man at the crossroads: one points to a lamp shining in a near window; the other points to the unmapped sky. Both have come a long way, both are sincere — but Arjuna wants to know which road the road-maker himself prefers. The question itself is the lamp held up to the choice.

    Stories
    Saunaka's question at Naimiṣāraṇya

    When the sages of Naimiṣāraṇya gathered around Sūta Gosvāmī, they did not begin with their own answers. They asked: of all that has been spoken, what is the essential good for humanity in this age?

    Like Arjuna here, they trusted that the right question, asked of the right authority, brings the right answer. The Bhāgavatam's twelve cantos flow from that one humble interrogation.

    Important Words
    • satata — always, continuously
      Arjuna does not ask about occasional worshipers. He frames both options at their peak: continuous, unbroken engagement. The comparison is fair because the intensity is matched on both sides.
    • bhaktāḥ — devotees
      First time in the chapter the word appears, and it is set against akṣaram-upāsakas. The chapter will spend twenty verses unfolding the difference between bhakti as relation and brahman-meditation as absorption.
    • akṣaram — the imperishable, beyond the senses
      Technical term for the impersonal Absolute — that which does not perish, does not manifest in form. Arjuna names it precisely to make the dichotomy unambiguous.
    • avyaktam — the unmanifested
      From a-vi-añj — 'not made visible.' Pairs with akṣaram. Together the two words specify the niṣguṇa-brahman target, distinguishing it from any sa-guṇa form of the Lord.
    • paryupāsate — properly, completely worship
      Pari- prefix: 'all around, completely.' Arjuna uses it for the impersonalists too, conceding that their worship is also total. He is asking Kṛṣṇa to judge between two sincere wholehearted disciplines.
    • yoga - vit - tamāḥ — the most perfect knowers of yoga
      Superlative -tama. Arjuna does not ask 'which is acceptable' — he asks 'which is the most.' The whole chapter is Kṛṣṇa's answer to that superlative.

    The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Those who fix their minds on My personal form and are always engaged in worshiping Me with great and transcendental faith are considered by Me to be most perfect.
    Purport

    In answer to Arjuna’s question, Kṛṣṇa clearly says that he who concentrates upon His personal form and who worships Him with faith and devotion is to be considered most perfect in yoga. For one in such Kṛṣṇa consciousness there are no material activities, because everything is done for Kṛṣṇa. A pure devotee is constantly engaged. Sometimes he chants, sometimes he hears or reads books about Kṛṣṇa, or sometimes he cooks prasādam or goes to the marketplace to purchase something for Kṛṣṇa, or sometimes he washes the temple or the dishes – whatever he does, he does not let a single moment pass without devoting his activities to Kṛṣṇa. Such action is in full samādhi.

    Personal Guidance
    Those who fix their minds on My personal form, with deep faith, ever engaged in worship — I consider them most perfect.
    Practical Applications
    • Choose Kṛṣṇa's personal form as the explicit object.
    • Cultivate fixity over rotation of objects.
    • Trust Kṛṣṇa's verdict: this is the most perfect path.
    Reflection Questions
    • Where do I rotate objects of meditation instead of fixing on Him?
    • What single fixity could I commit to?
    Analogy

    A flame fed by a steady hand burns true; a flame whose wick the wind keeps catching gutters and recovers and gutters again. Kṛṣṇa says: those whose minds, day after day, are placed on My form as the hand places oil on a wick — they are yukta-tama, most yoked. Faith is the oil; fixity is the steady hand.

    Stories
    Ambarīṣa Mahārāja's mind on Kṛṣṇa's feet

    Mahārāja Ambarīṣa, though emperor of the earth, fixed his mind on Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, his words on Kṛṣṇa's qualities, his hands on the temple service, his ears on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, his eyes on the Deity, his body on the devotees — every faculty channeled to a single personal Object.

    When Durvāsā Muni cursed him, the Sudarśana cakra defended him without his asking. Bhāgavatam offers him as the living portrait of the yukta-tama Kṛṣṇa praises here — mind fixed, faith total, worship continuous.

    Important Words
    • mayi — upon Me
      Locative of aham. Kṛṣṇa places Himself — personal, present, addressable — as the substrate of meditation. Not on brahman, not on the void: on Me.
    • āveśya — fixing, causing to enter
      From ā-viś, 'to enter into.' Absolutive form: 'having caused [the mind] to enter [Me].' The mind is not merely turned toward Kṛṣṇa — it is settled inside Him.
    • śraddhayā — with faith
      Instrumental case: faith is the means by which the engagement is sustained. Without śraddhā, fixity collapses into routine.
    • parayā — transcendental, supreme
      Qualifies śraddhayā. Not ordinary belief in something agreeable — para śraddhā is faith that has tasted its object enough to choose it over every alternative.
    • yukta - tamāḥ — most perfect in yoga
      Direct answer to Arjuna's question of verse 1. Yukta is 'yoked'; -tama is 'most.' Kṛṣṇa returns Arjuna's exact superlative back to him, settling the comparison.
    • matāḥ — are considered
      From man, 'to think.' Past participle: 'considered [by Me].' This is not a survey result — it is Kṛṣṇa's own verdict. The case is closed by His opinion.

    But those who fully worship the unmanifested, that which lies beyond the perception of the senses, the all-pervading, inconceivable, unchanging, fixed and immovable – the impersonal conception of the Absolute Truth – by controlling the various senses and being equally disposed to everyone, such persons, engaged in the welfare of all, at last achieve Me.
    Purport

    Those who do not directly worship the Supreme Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, but who attempt to achieve the same goal by an indirect process, also ultimately achieve the same goal, Śrī Kṛṣṇa. “After many births the man of wisdom seeks refuge in Me, knowing that Vāsudeva is all.” When a person comes to full knowledge after many births, he surrenders unto Lord Kṛṣṇa. If one approaches the Godhead by the method mentioned in this verse, he has to control the senses, render service to everyone and engage in the welfare of all beings. It is inferred that one has to approach Lord Kṛṣṇa, otherwise there is no perfect realization. Often there is much penance involved before one fully surrenders unto Him. In order to perceive the Supersoul within the individual soul, one has to cease the sensual activities of seeing, hearing, tasting, working, etc. Then one comes to understand that the Supreme Soul is present everywhere. Realizing this, one envies no living entity – he sees no difference between man and animal because he sees soul only, not the outer covering. But for the common man, this method of impersonal realization is very difficult.

    Personal Guidance
    Those who worship the unmanifest, indefinable, all-pervading, immutable — controlled, equal-minded, engaged in welfare — they too attain Me, but with greater difficulty.
    Practical Applications
    • Honor the impersonalist path while choosing the easier personal one.
    • Don't dismiss either; recognize both lead to Him with different difficulty.
    • Choose the path most suited to your nature, knowing convergence.
    Reflection Questions
    • Where do I dismiss one path to validate my own?
    • What does charitable view of both paths require?
    Analogy

    A traveler sets out to reach the sky. He climbs every mountain, controls his every step, refuses every detour, treats every region with equal kindness — and at the end of his long road he too arrives. The destination is the same; the road is brutal. Kṛṣṇa grants the impersonalist his goal but does not pretend his road is easy.

    Stories
    The Four Kumāras' turn

    Sanaka, Sanātana, Sanandana, and Sanat-kumāra were born brahma-jñānīs — naked boys with the impersonal Absolute already in their grip. They wandered as paramahaṁsas, equal to all, restraining every sense.

    Yet in the Bhāgavatam it is precisely they who, upon smelling the tulasī offered to Lord Viṣṇu's lotus feet at Vaikuṇṭha's gate, melted from impersonal absorption into personal devotion. Kṛṣṇa's words here — 'they also reach Me' — are literally fulfilled in their story: the imperishable path delivered them to the Person at last.

    Important Words
    • anirdeśyam — indefinable
      Negative gerundive: 'not able to be pointed at, not able to be predicated.' Where Kṛṣṇa is nāma-rūpa-guṇa-rich, the akṣara is nāma-rūpa-vivarjita. This is the precise difficulty Kṛṣṇa is about to flag in v.5.
    • acintyam — inconceivable
      Negation of cintā — beyond what thought can frame. The mind has no handle on it, which is exactly why fixing the mind there is so 'troublesome.'
    • kūṭa - stham — unchanging, fixed at the summit
      Kūṭa = peak / anvil; stha = standing. A technical Vedāntic term for that which stands above all change — yet also above all relation. Unmoving, untouched, unembraceable.
    • sanniyamya — fully controlling
      Saṁ + ni + yam — triple intensification of 'restraint.' The impersonal path demands total sense-restraint, not partial. This is why Kṛṣṇa next calls it kleśa.
    • sama - buddhayaḥ — equally disposed
      Their cognition (buddhi) treats all beings the same. Because they see only the unmanifest substrate, no being is more dear, none less. Compare 13-14: the bhakta is also samaḥ, but his sameness flows from love of Kṛṣṇa, not from collapse of distinction.
    • sarva - bhūta - hite — in the welfare of all beings
      Compound: sarva (all) + bhūta (beings) + hita (welfare). Kṛṣṇa concedes — even this austere path is not selfish. The impersonalist who finishes his road is a friend to every creature.
    • prāpnuvanti — they attain
      Active present, 3rd plural, of pra-āp — 'they reach forth and attain.' Not 'they fail.' Kṛṣṇa is generous: the road is hard, but it ends at Him.

    For those whose minds are attached to the unmanifested, impersonal feature of the Supreme, advancement is very troublesome. To make progress in that discipline is always difficult for those who are embodied.
    Purport

    The group of transcendentalists who follow the path of the inconceivable, unmanifested, impersonal feature of the Supreme Lord are called jñāna-yogīs, and persons who are in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, engaged in devotional service to the Lord, are called bhakti-yogīs. Now, here the difference between jñāna-yoga and bhakti-yoga is definitely expressed. The process of jñāna-yoga, although ultimately bringing one to the same goal, is very troublesome, whereas the path of bhakti-yoga, the process of being in direct service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is easier and is natural for the embodied soul. The individual soul is embodied since time immemorial. It is very difficult for him to simply theoretically understand that he is not the body. Therefore, the bhakti-yogī accepts the Deity of Kṛṣṇa as worshipable because there is some bodily conception fixed in the mind, which can thus be applied. Of course, worship of the Supreme Personality of Godhead in His form within the temple is not idol worship. There is evidence in the Vedic literature that worship may be saguṇa or nirguṇa – of the Supreme possessing or not possessing attributes. Worship of the Deity in the temple is saguṇa worship, for the Lord is represented by material qualities. But the form of the Lord, though represented by material qualities such as stone, wood or oil paint, is not actually material. That is the absolute nature of the Supreme Lord. A crude example may be given here. We may find some mailboxes on the street, and if we post our letters in those boxes, they will naturally go to their destination without difficulty. But any old box, or an imitation which we may find somewhere but which is not authorized by the post office, will not do the work. Similarly, God has an authorized representation in the Deity form, which is called arcā-vigraha. This arcā-vigraha is an incarnation of the Supreme Lord. God will accept service through that form. The Lord is omnipotent, all-powerful; therefore, by His incarnation as arcā-vigraha He can accept the services of the devotee, just to make it convenient for the man in conditioned life. So for a devotee there is no difficulty in approaching the Supreme immediately and directly, but for those who are following the impersonal way to spiritual realization the path is difficult. They have to understand the unmanifested representation of the Supreme through such Vedic literatures as the Upaniṣads, and they have to learn the language, understand the nonperceptual feelings, and realize all these processes. This is not very easy for a common man. A person in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, engaged in devotional service, simply by the guidance of the bona fide spiritual master, simply by offering regulative obeisances unto the Deity, simply by hearing the glories of the Lord, and simply by eating the remnants of foodstuffs offered to the Lord, realizes the Supreme Personality of Godhead very easily. There is no doubt that the impersonalists are unnecessarily taking a troublesome path with the risk of not realizing the Absolute Truth at the ultimate end. But the personalist, without any risk, trouble or difficulty, approaches the Supreme Personality directly. A similar passage appears in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. It is stated there that if one ultimately has to surrender unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead (this surrendering process is called bhakti ), but instead takes the trouble to understand what is Brahman and what is not Brahman and spends his whole life in that way, the result is simply troublesome. Therefore it is advised here that one should not take up this troublesome path of self-realization, because there is uncertainty in the ultimate result. A living entity is eternally an individual soul, and if he wants to merge into the spiritual whole, he may accomplish the realization of the eternal and knowledgeable aspects of his original nature, but the blissful portion is not realized. By the grace of some devotee, such a transcendentalist, highly learned in the process of jñāna-yoga, may come to the point of bhakti-yoga, or devotional service. At that time, long practice in impersonalism also becomes a source of trouble, because he cannot give up the idea. Therefore an embodied soul is always in difficulty with the unmanifest, both at the time of practice and at the time of realization. Every living soul is partially independent, and one should know for certain that this unmanifested realization is against the nature of his spiritual, blissful self. One should not take up this process. For every individual living entity the process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, which entails full engagement in devotional service, is the best way. If one wants to ignore this devotional service, there is the danger of turning to atheism. Thus the process of centering attention on the unmanifested, the inconceivable, which is beyond the approach of the senses, as already expressed in this verse, should never be encouraged at any time, especially in this age. It is not advised by Lord Kṛṣṇa.

    Personal Guidance
    Those whose minds are attached to the unmanifest progress with great difficulty — for the embodied being cannot easily reach the unmanifest.
    Practical Applications
    • Recognize the natural difficulty of impersonal absorption for embodied beings.
    • Don't punish yourself for needing form; form is appropriate to embodiment.
    • Receive form-worship as compassionate accommodation.
    Reflection Questions
    • Have I felt embarrassed about needing personal form?
    • How does this verse free me?
    Analogy

    Tell an embodied soul to embrace the unmanifest, and you are telling a person with hands to clap with no hands. The body is a form-shaped instrument; it knows how to bow, how to taste, how to listen. Asking it to grasp the formless is not impossible — it is just exquisitely awkward, like trying to hug the sky.

    Stories
    Śukadeva Gosvāmī's turning

    Śukadeva left the womb already a brahma-jñānī, walking past Vyāsa's calls without looking back. He was the very portrait of one whose mind was attached to the avyakta.

    Yet Vyāsa, his father, knew the kleśa Kṛṣṇa names here. He instructed disciples to sing a few verses about Kṛṣṇa's pastimes where Śukadeva would hear them. The form-rich sound caught the formless absorbed boy. Śukadeva turned, came back, and became the speaker of the Bhāgavatam — the impersonalist whose embodiment finally found its proper rest in personal līlā.

    Important Words
    • kleśaḥ — trouble, affliction
      The same word Patañjali uses for the five afflictions of the conditioned mind. Kṛṣṇa is not gentle here — He calls this path afflicting, not just challenging. Diagnosis, not insult.
    • adhika - taraḥ — very much greater
      Comparative -tara. Greater than what? Than the kleśa of the personal path, which has its own difficulties. Kṛṣṇa is making a relative judgment, not denying that bhakti has effort.
    • avyakta — to the unmanifested
      Picked up from verses 1 and 3-4 — the keyword of the rejected path. The whole verse hinges on the mismatch between this object and the embodied subject.
    • āsakta — attached
      Past participle of ā-sañj. Their minds cling to the avyakta — which, having nothing to grip, leaves the clinging mind exhausted. Attachment to the unattachable is the precise definition of kleśa.
    • gatiḥ — progress, going
      From gam, 'to go.' Spiritual gati is the journey itself. Kṛṣṇa says the journey, not just its end, is duḥkha for this class of practitioners.
    • deha - vadbhiḥ — by the embodied
      Compound: deha (body) + vat (possessing) + bhiḥ (by). The verse's quiet center. So long as one has a body, one's faculties are body-shaped — and a body-shaped instrument is built to relate, not to dissolve.

    But those who worship Me, giving up all their activities unto Me and being devoted to Me without deviation, engaged in devotional service and always meditating upon Me, having fixed their minds upon Me, O son of Pṛthā – for them I am the swift deliverer from the ocean of birth and death.
    Purport

    It is explicitly stated here that the devotees are very fortunate to be delivered very soon from material existence by the Lord. In pure devotional service one comes to the realization that God is great and that the individual soul is subordinate to Him. His duty is to render service to the Lord – and if he does not, then he will render service to māyā. As stated before, the Supreme Lord can be appreciated only by devotional service. Therefore, one should be fully devoted. One should fix his mind fully on Kṛṣṇa in order to achieve Him. One should work only for Kṛṣṇa. It does not matter in what kind of work one engages, but that work should be done only for Kṛṣṇa. That is the standard of devotional service. The devotee does not desire any achievement other than pleasing the Supreme Personality of Godhead. His life’s mission is to please Kṛṣṇa, and he can sacrifice everything for Kṛṣṇa’s satisfaction, just as Arjuna did in the Battle of Kurukṣetra. The process is very simple: one can devote himself in his occupation and engage at the same time in chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. Such transcendental chanting attracts the devotee to the Personality of Godhead. The Supreme Lord herein promises that without delay He will deliver a pure devotee thus engaged from the ocean of material existence. Those who are advanced in yoga practice can willfully transfer the soul to whatever planet they like by the yoga process, and others take the opportunity in various ways, but as far as the devotee is concerned, it is clearly stated here that the Lord Himself takes him. The devotee does not need to wait to become very experienced in order to transfer himself to the spiritual sky. In the Varāha Purāṇa this verse appears: nayāmi paramaṁ sthānam arcir-ādi-gatiṁ vinā garuḍa-skandham āropya yatheccham anivāritaḥ The purport of this verse is that a devotee does not need to practice aṣṭāṅga-yoga in order to transfer his soul to the spiritual planets. The responsibility is taken by the Supreme Lord Himself. He clearly states here that He Himself becomes the deliverer. A child is completely cared for by his parents, and thus his position is secure. Similarly, a devotee does not need to endeavor to transfer himself by yoga practice to other planets. Rather, the Supreme Lord, by His great mercy, comes at once, riding on His bird carrier Garuḍa, and at once delivers the devotee from material existence. Although a man who has fallen in the ocean may struggle very hard and may be very expert in swimming, he cannot save himself. But if someone comes and picks him up from the water, then he is easily rescued. Similarly, the Lord picks up the devotee from this material existence. One simply has to practice the easy process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness and fully engage himself in devotional service. Any intelligent man should always prefer the process of devotional service to all other paths. In the Nārāyaṇīya this is confirmed as follows: yā vai sādhana-sampattiḥ puruṣārtha-catuṣṭaye tayā vinā tad āpnoti naro nārāyaṇāśrayaḥ The purport of this verse is that one should not engage in the different processes of fruitive activity or cultivate knowledge by the mental speculative process. One who is devoted to the Supreme Personality can attain all the benefits derived from other yogic processes, speculation, rituals, sacrifices, charities, etc. That is the specific benediction of devotional service. Simply by chanting the holy name of Kṛṣṇa – Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare – a devotee of the Lord can approach the supreme destination easily and happily, but this destination cannot be approached by any other process of religion. The conclusion of Bhagavad-gītā is stated in the Eighteenth Chapter: sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ One should give up all other processes of self-realization and simply execute devotional service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. That will enable one to reach the highest perfection of life. There is no need for one to consider the sinful actions of his past life, because the Supreme Lord fully takes charge of him. Therefore one should not futilely try to deliver himself in spiritual realization. Let everyone take shelter of the supreme omnipotent Godhead, Kṛṣṇa. That is the highest perfection of life.

    Personal Guidance
    Those who renounce all activities into Me, take Me as their supreme goal, worship with undeviating devotion — for them I am quickly the deliverer from the ocean of birth and death.
    Practical Applications
    • Renounce the doership of activities — offer them to Kṛṣṇa explicitly.
    • Make Him the supreme aim, not merely one among many.
    • Trust the 'quickly deliverer' promise.
    Reflection Questions
    • What activity have I been doing 'for myself' that I should now offer?
    • What is my actual supreme aim?
    Analogy

    Two swimmers fall into the same ocean. One strikes out for the far shore with nothing but his own arms. The other has thrown all his weight onto a swift boat, and the boat's captain steers, rows, and bails. Kṛṣṇa says: throw every karma into Me, and I will be the boat.     Draupadī's sārī

    When Duḥśāsana dragged her into the Kuru sabhā and began to disrobe her, Draupadī first clutched her sārī with one hand. The sārī gave way. Only when she lifted both hands above her head — surrendering all activity, including the activity of self-defense — and called out, 'Govinda! Dvārakāvāsin! Hari! O Lord of the Yadus!' did Kṛṣṇa multiply the cloth without end.

    The verse's two-handed surrender is Draupadī's exact posture: sarvāṇi karmāṇi mayi sannyasya. She gave up even the activity of protecting herself, and Kṛṣṇa became samuddhartā — the swift uplifter — na cirāt, not after a long time.

    Gajendra's prayer

    The elephant-king Gajendra, gripped by the crocodile for a thousand celestial years, exhausted every personal strategy. At the breaking point he lifted a lotus and called out — not to any specific form he had seen, but to the Lord of all who lift the fallen. Viṣṇu came at once, faster than any of His earlier incarnations had arrived for anyone.

    The Bhāgavatam glosses His haste: a devotee who has thrown all his activities upon the Lord cannot be left to perish. This verse's na cirāt — 'not after long' — is Gajendra's deliverance in five syllables.

    Important Words
    • sannyasya — giving up, fully renouncing
      Absolutive of sam-ni-as — 'casting down completely.' The same root as sannyāsa. Here it is not a robe one takes but an inner act of throwing every karma onto Kṛṣṇa as one casts a stone into a deep well.
    • mat - parāḥ — having Me as supreme
      Compound: mat (Me) + para (highest). The whole hierarchy of one's life is collapsed into one apex — Kṛṣṇa. Without this para-positioning, the casting of karmas would have nowhere to land.
    • ananyena — without division, without another
      Instrumental of an-anya. The yoga is undivided: no other object competes. Pair this with v.2's parayā śraddhayā — same intensity, different angle.
    • samuddhartā — the deliverer, the one who lifts up
      Sam + ud + hṛ — 'completely lifting up.' Future participle: 'I become the one who shall lift them out.' Kṛṣṇa names His own role: not coach, not witness — rescuer.
    • saṁsāra — material existence
      Literally 'flowing together' — the endless current of birth and death. Here paired with sāgarāt (from the ocean) and mṛtyu (death). The geography of bondage in three words.
    • cirāt — after a long time
      Ablative of cira, 'long.' Negated in the verse (na cirāt) — two short syllables that overturn every doctrine of slow self-effort. Kṛṣṇa makes the rescue swift; the bhakta is not condemned to a karmic ledger.

    Personal Guidance
    Just fix your mind upon Me; let your intelligence be absorbed in Me — thus you will live in Me always; do not doubt this.
    Practical Applications
    • Apply the simple instruction: fix the mind, absorb the intelligence.
    • Don't add complexity; the verse is direct.
    • Treat 'do not doubt' as Kṛṣṇa's specific reassurance.
    Reflection Questions
    • Where have I added complexity to a simple instruction?
    • What does 'just fix your mind' actually look like today?
    Analogy

    A child setting up house in his father's mansion does not need to negotiate a lease or memorize the deeds. He simply walks in, lays down his head, and lives there. Kṛṣṇa says the mind is exactly such a tenant: place it in Me, place the intelligence beside it, and from then on you live in Me. That is not metaphor; that is residency.

    Stories
    Prahlāda in his father's hall

    Whether Hiraṇyakaśipu's guards were beating him, snakes were biting him, or elephants were trampling him, Prahlāda's mind never left Viṣṇu. School lessons could not pull it away; threats could not pull it away; even the moment of his father's death could not pull it away.

    This is mayi eva manaḥ ādhatsva in lived form: the mind so placed in the Lord that no external pressure can dislodge it. Prahlāda did not 'visit' Kṛṣṇa in meditation — he resided there, exactly as this verse promises: nivasiṣyasi mayi eva.

    Important Words
    • ādhatsva — fix, place
      Imperative middle of ā-dhā, 'to place upon, set down.' Same root as ādhāna — the laying of the sacred fire. The mind is to be installed in Kṛṣṇa the way a Vedic flame is installed on an altar: deliberately, ritually, once.
    • niveśaya — apply, settle, cause to enter
      Causative imperative of ni-viś — 'cause to dwell.' Same root as niveśa (settlement). The intelligence is not visiting Kṛṣṇa; it is being settled in Him as a colonist settles in new land.
    • buddhim — intelligence
      Distinct from manas (mind). Kṛṣṇa names both faculties separately — the mind to fix, the intelligence to absorb — because each binds the soul to a different layer of the inner instrument.
    • nivasiṣyasi — you will live
      Future of ni-vas, 'to dwell.' Same root as nivāsa (residence). Place mind and intelligence in Him, and you will not merely think of Him — you will reside in Him. The verse promises a change of address.
    • ataḥ ūrdhvam — thereafter, from this point upward
      Ataḥ (from this) + ūrdhvam (upward). A temporal vector: from the moment of fixing, time flows upward. The phrase brackets the act of placement as a watershed event.
    • saṁśayaḥ — doubt
      From sam-śī, 'to lie on both sides at once.' Negated in the verse (na saṁśayaḥ) — Kṛṣṇa's signature seal of certainty, used at the close of many crucial instructions in the Gītā. He does not soften this with 'perhaps.' The verse is short because nothing further needs to be added.

    Purport

    In this verse, two different processes of bhakti-yoga are indicated. And the other is for one who has not developed an attachment for the Supreme Person by transcendental love. For this second class there are different prescribed rules and regulations one can follow to be ultimately elevated to the stage of attachment to Kṛṣṇa. Bhakti-yoga is the purification of the senses. At the present moment in material existence the senses are always impure, being engaged in sense gratification. But by the practice of bhakti-yoga these senses can become purified, and in the purified state they come directly in contact with the Supreme Lord. In this material existence, I may be engaged in some service to some master, but I don’t really lovingly serve my master. I simply serve to get some money. That stage of love can be achieved by practice of devotional service, performed with the present senses. This love of God is now in a dormant state in everyone’s heart. Now the heart has to be purified of the material association, and that dormant, natural love for Kṛṣṇa has to be revived. That is the whole process. To practice the regulative principles of bhakti-yoga one should, under the guidance of an expert spiritual master, follow certain principles: one should rise early in the morning, take bath, enter the temple and offer prayers and chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, then collect flowers to offer to the Deity, cook foodstuffs to offer to the Deity, take prasādam, and so on.     Personal Guidance
    If you cannot fix the mind on Me without deviation, follow the regulative principles of bhakti-yoga — by which the desire to attain Me develops.
    Practical Applications
    • Honor the gradation; if direct fixity fails, take the next step down.
    • Use regulated bhakti-yoga (chanting, deity-worship, hearing) as the means.
    • Don't despair at not yet being able to fix directly.
    Reflection Questions
    • Where do I despair instead of stepping down to a gentler practice?
    • What regulated practice am I underusing?
    Analogy

    If a child cannot yet write the letter in one sweep of the pen, the teacher hands him the dotted outline and tells him to trace it daily. The straight line will come, in time, from the patient retracing. Kṛṣṇa hands Arjuna the dotted outline of bhakti — its daily regulations — when the free hand is not yet steady.

    Stories
    Nārada Muni's previous life

    In a former birth, the boy who would become Nārada was the son of a maidservant. When wandering Vaiṣṇavas spent the rainy season in his master's house, he served them their remnants and listened to their kīrtana. He could not 'fix his mind' on Kṛṣṇa freely — but the daily regulation of serving and hearing did the fixing for him.

    By the next life he was Nārada, traveling the three worlds with the vīṇā. The Bhāgavatam tells the story precisely as Kṛṣṇa prescribes this verse: abhyāsa-yoga lifted a boy who could not yet hold the mind into the very Lord he could not yet see.

    Important Words
    • cittam — mind, consciousness
      Distinct from manas and buddhi — citta is the deeper substrate where impressions (saṁskāras) accumulate. Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna at the level where habit, not just thought, is formed.
    • samādhātum — to fix, to settle in samādhi
      Infinitive of sam-ā-dhā. Same root as samādhi. Kṛṣṇa is not asking for casual attention — He names the high state that v.8 demanded, then accepts that Arjuna may not yet be capable of it.
    • sthiram — steadily, fixedly
      Adverb. The fixing must be steady; flickering will not do. The verse's whole logic is built around this missing steadiness — and the remedy supplied below.
    • abhyāsa - yogena — by the practice of devotional service
      Compound: abhyāsa (repeated practice) + yoga. Echoes 6.35 where Arjuna heard the same word for mind-control. Here abhyāsa is specifically the regulated daily program of bhakti — chanting, hearing, deity service — repeated until habit becomes nature.
    • icchā — desire
      Striking word. Kṛṣṇa does not say 'attain Me' but 'develop the desire to attain Me.' Even the wanting is a fruit of practice. First we practice; then we want; then we attain.
    • dhanam - jaya — winner of wealth, O Arjuna
      Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna by an epithet of conquest — the one who won wealth at the Rājasūya. The implication: the same disciplined practice that brought you military and material victory will work for spiritual victory too.

    If you cannot follow regulative principles, work for Me — by performing work for Me, you will come to perfection.
    Practical Applications
    • Honor the next gradation: working for Kṛṣṇa.
    • Make even ordinary work an offering when meditation is hard.
    • Don't disqualify yourself; there is always a level open.
    Reflection Questions
    • What work could I most easily reframe as 'for Kṛṣṇa'?
    • What stops me from doing so?
    Analogy

    A servant who cannot yet sit at his master's feet and contemplate his face can still polish his shoes, sweep his courtyard, prepare his food. The work itself, done for the master, draws the mind toward him. Kṛṣṇa lowers the rung again: if regulated practice is beyond reach, just work — for Me.

    Stories
    Hanumān's bridge

    While Rāma sat in council and Lakṣmaṇa stood watch, Hanumān leapt the ocean, scouted Laṅkā, and led the vānaras who hauled boulders for the bridge. He did not meditate his way to Rāma; he carried stones for Him.

    Every rock placed in the sea was japa for Hanumān. By the time the bridge was complete, his mind had no other content. This is mat-karma-paramaḥ in stone and sweat — perfection achieved not in spite of the work but precisely through the work, done for the Lord's sake.

    Arjuna himself

    Arjuna is being prescribed his own future on the battlefield. He cannot, this morning, fix his mind in samādhi; he cannot follow the full regulations of bhakti-yoga in the middle of an army. But he can fight for Kṛṣṇa — and that, Kṛṣṇa says, is enough for perfection.

    The Gītā's whole denouement at 18.73 — 'I will do as You say' — is the ripening of this single verse's seed.

    Important Words
    • abhyāse — in regulated practice
      Picks up the word from v.9 only to set it aside. Kṛṣṇa systematically descends a staircase of methods; each step names the previous and concedes that some hearers will not be able to climb it.
    • asamarthaḥ — unable
      Negation of samartha, 'capable.' Kṛṣṇa keeps acknowledging incapacity — and keeps offering a lower rung. The structure of the chapter is an act of mercy in grammar.
    • mat - karma — work for Me
      Compound: mat (Me, genitive sense) + karma (work). Not karma in general, but karma whose explicit recipient is Kṛṣṇa. The work is unchanged; the dative changes everything.
    • paramaḥ — dedicated, holding as supreme
      Same root as v.6-7's mat-parāḥ. The work must be one's supreme orientation — not a hobby alongside other allegiances. One supreme object, many possible activities.
    • mat - artham — for My sake
      Compound with -artham suffix, 'for the purpose of Me.' Sanskrit's clean way of pointing the action's purpose-arrow at Kṛṣṇa. The work is identified by where its arrow points.
    • siddhim — perfection
      From sidh — the same word used for the eight yogic siddhis and for liberation itself. Kṛṣṇa uses it without qualification: by working for Him, you will achieve siddhi. Not 'a kind of progress' — perfection.

    Translation
    11. If you cannot do this, take shelter of dedicating all prescribed actions to Me. While controlling your mind, act with detachment from the results of the work.
    Commentary

    If you are unable to do this, then taking shelter of the process of offering all your actions to Me (mad-yogam āśritaḥ), give up all the results of your actions as described in the first six chapters (karma-yoga). The meaning is this. In the first six chapters, the Lord spoke of niṣkāma-karma-yoga offered to the Lord, a method to attain liberation. In the second six chapters, the Lord spoke of bhakti-yoga, the method of gaining the Lord Himself. Bhakti-yoga has two types: being fixed in bhakti through internal senses, and being fixed in bhakti through external senses. The first type of bhakti, using internal senses, has three sub categories: smaraṇa ātmika and manana ātmika (mentioned in verse 8, using mind and intellect), and for those who desire remembrance, but cannot do continual remembrance, practice of the above, called abhyāsa-rūpa (verse 9). These three are difficult for a person with poor intelligence, and easy to perform for one with good intellect and no offenses. The second type, relying on external senses, is composed of hearing and chanting (verse 10). It is easy to perform for everyone.     If you cannot work for Me, then take refuge in Me — renouncing the fruits of your work, with the self in equilibrium.
    Practical Applications
    • Honor the most accessible step: renouncing fruits.

    Analogy

    A merchant who cannot yet hand his entire shop to the Lord can at least stop checking his ledger at midnight. Begin by not pocketing tomorrow's outcome. Kṛṣṇa drops another rung: if you cannot work for Me, at least drop the fruits — turn the same activity into an offering by releasing what comes back.

    Stories
    Yudhiṣṭhira at the Aśvamedha

    After the war, when Yudhiṣṭhira's brothers urged him to perform the Aśvamedha sacrifice, he had little appetite for kingdom or glory; the dead were still on his conscience. He performed the great yajña with all its splendor — and claimed nothing of its renown for himself.

    The Bhāgavatam reports the famous mongoose at the close, whose half-golden body remained unchanged even by Yudhiṣṭhira's massive yajña, because earlier a poor brāhmaṇa family had given their last meal to a guest. The story illustrates this verse exactly: external scale matters less than internal phala-tyāga.

    Important Words
    • aśaktaḥ — unable
      Same semantic field as v.10's asamarthaḥ. Kṛṣṇa is willing to use synonym after synonym for 'unable,' because the staircase of mercy must accommodate every grade of hearer.
    • yogam — devotional service unto Me
      Paired in the verse with mat ('unto Me') — Kṛṣṇa-yoga, not yoga abstractly. The verse acknowledges that even working-for-Me may be beyond a hearer, and offers a still-lower entry point.
    • āśritaḥ — taking refuge in
      Past participle of ā-śri, 'to take shelter.' Even at this lower rung, refuge is the operative word. The renunciation of fruits is itself a kind of shelter — one has stopped trying to be the one who guarantees outcomes.
    • sarva - karma — of all activities
      Compound: sarva (all) + karma (activities). The 'all' is essential. Pairs with phala in the verse: every fruit of every action is the object of tyāga — not selectively the bitter ones, not selectively the sweet.
    • phala — of the results
      The verse's hinge object. The activities (karma) continue; their phala is what gets renounced. Right placement of tyāga: not on the doing, but on the harvest.
    • tyāgam — renunciation
      Same root as tyāga, the operative word of chapter 18. Note the precise object: tyāga of phala, not tyāga of karma. The activity continues; only the proprietorship over its harvest is released.
    • yata - ātma - vān — self-controlled, possessing a restrained self
      Compound: yata (restrained) + ātma (self) + vat (possessing). The fruit-renouncer is not careless — he is self-restrained, because letting go of phala requires real inner work. This is not laziness in disguise.

    If you cannot take to this practice, then engage yourself in the cultivation of knowledge. Better than knowledge, however, is meditation, and better than meditation is renunciation of the fruits of action, for by such renunciation one can attain peace of mind.
    Purport

    As mentioned in the previous verses, there are two kinds of devotional service: the way of regulative principles and the way of full attachment in love to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. For those who are actually not able to follow the principles of Kṛṣṇa consciousness it is better to cultivate knowledge, because by knowledge one can be able to understand his real position. Gradually knowledge will develop to the point of meditation. By meditation one can be able to understand the Supreme Personality of Godhead by a gradual process. In the cultivation of knowledge there are processes which make one understand that one himself is the Supreme, and that sort of meditation is preferred if one is unable to engage in devotional service. If one is not able to meditate in such a way, then there are prescribed duties, as enjoined in the Vedic literature, for the brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras, which we shall find in the last chapter of Bhagavad-gītā. But in all cases, one should give up the result or fruits of labor; this means to employ the result of karma for some good cause. In summary, to reach the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the highest goal, there are two processes: one process is by gradual development, and the other process is direct. Devotional service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the direct method, and the other method involves renouncing the fruits of one’s activities. Then one can come to the stage of knowledge, then to the stage of meditation, then to the stage of understanding the Supersoul, and then to the stage of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. One may take either the step-by-step process or the direct path. The direct process is not possible for everyone; therefore the indirect process is also good. It is, however, to be understood that the indirect process is not recommended for Arjuna, because he is already at the stage of loving devotional service to the Supreme Lord. It is for others, who are not at this stage; for them the gradual process of renunciation, knowledge, meditation and realization of the Supersoul and Brahman should be followed. But as far as Bhagavad-gītā is concerned, it is the direct method that is stressed. Everyone is advised to take to the direct method and surrender unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa.

    Personal Guidance
    Knowledge is better than mechanical practice; meditation is better than knowledge; renunciation of the fruits of action is better than meditation — peace immediately follows.
    Practical Applications
    • Honor the gradation but recognize fruit-renunciation as the immediate peace-bringer.
    • Treat fruit-renunciation as primary, not advanced.
    • Cultivate peace today by releasing fruits today.
    Reflection Questions
    • Where do I assume peace requires more advanced practice?
    • What fruit-renunciation today brings peace today?
    Analogy

    A gardener can study every book on roses, can practice his daily pruning, can sit beside the bush and contemplate it for hours — but the bush will not bloom in his hands until he stops weighing each rose for the market. Kṛṣṇa ranks the rungs and then says: of them all, fruit-renunciation is what brings peace immediately. The gardener exhales; the rose blooms.

    Stories
    King Janaka at his court

    Mithilā's affairs passed across Janaka's table all day — military reports, marriages, taxes, judicial disputes. He gave each matter its full attention and then released the outcome the moment the decision left his hand. Sages who came to test him for spiritual depth found a king already at peace.

    Kṛṣṇa's gradation here — knowledge, meditation, fruit-renunciation — collapses in Janaka into one practice. He is not contemplating the Absolute on a forest seat; he is renouncing fruits at the throne. The verse's anantaram, 'immediately,' was the air of his court.

    Important Words
    • śreyaḥ — better, more beneficial
      Comparative of śrī. The verse uses the word three times implicitly, ascending a ladder. Not 'good' but 'better' — the verse is built on relative ranking.
    • jñānam — knowledge
      First rung above mere mechanical practice. Better to understand what you are doing than to repeat it blindly. But Kṛṣṇa will pass it by.
    • dhyānam — meditation
      Second rung. Better to contemplate the known than to merely know it. Kṛṣṇa will pass this by too. The classical eight-limbed peak is being relativized.
    • karma - phala - tyāgaḥ — renunciation of the fruits of action
      Compound: karma + phala + tyāga. The verse's destination. Higher than meditation, because meditation can be inward absorption with the ego still pocketing its results; phala-tyāga reaches the ego directly.
    • tyāgāt — from renunciation
      Ablative — 'from such renunciation, peace.' Cause-and-effect is specified by case. Peace does not come from meditation alone; it follows the act of release.
    • śāntiḥ — peace
      The verse's promised harvest. Same word that closes the Upaniṣadic mantras and the Gītā itself (18.78). Here Kṛṣṇa makes peace the direct fruit of phala-tyāga — no other prerequisite remains.
    • anantaram — thereafter, with no interval
      From an-antara, 'without an inside-gap.' The instant the fruits are released, peace arrives — no waiting period, no advanced course required. The verse's hammer-blow word.

    One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities, who does not think himself a proprietor and is free from false ego, who is equal in both happiness and distress, who is tolerant, always satisfied, self-controlled, and engaged in devotional service with determination, his mind and intelligence fixed on Me – such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me.
    Purport

    Coming again to the point of pure devotional service, the Lord is describing the transcendental qualifications of a pure devotee in these two verses. A pure devotee is never disturbed in any circumstances. Nor is he envious of anyone. Nor does a devotee become his enemy’s enemy; he thinks, “This person is acting as my enemy due to my own past misdeeds. So it is better to suffer than to protest.” In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (10.14.8) it is stated: tat te ’nukampāṁ su-samīkṣamāṇo bhuñjāna evātma-kṛtaṁ vipākam. Whenever a devotee is in distress or has fallen into difficulty, he thinks that it is the Lord’s mercy upon him. He thinks, “Thanks to my past misdeeds I should suffer far, far greater than I am suffering now. So it is by the mercy of the Supreme Lord that I am not getting all the punishment I am due. I am just getting a little, by the mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.” Therefore he is always calm, quiet and patient, despite many distressful conditions. A devotee is also always kind to everyone, even to his enemy. Nirmama means that a devotee does not attach much importance to the pains and trouble pertaining to the body because he knows perfectly well that he is not the material body. He does not identify with the body; therefore he is freed from the conception of false ego and is equipoised in happiness and distress. He is tolerant, and he is satisfied with whatever comes by the grace of the Supreme Lord. He does not endeavor much to achieve something with great difficulty; therefore he is always joyful. He is a completely perfect mystic because he is fixed in the instructions received from the spiritual master, and because his senses are controlled he is determined. He is not swayed by false arguments, because no one can lead him from the fixed determination of devotional service. He is fully conscious that Kṛṣṇa is the eternal Lord, so no one can disturb him. All these qualifications enable him to fix his mind and intelligence entirely on the Supreme Lord. Such a standard of devotional service is undoubtedly very rare, but a devotee becomes situated in that stage by following the regulative principles of devotional service. Furthermore, the Lord says that such a devotee is very dear to Him, for the Lord is always pleased with all his activities in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

    Personal Guidance
    One who is non-envious, friendly to all, free from possessiveness, equal in pleasure and pain, forgiving, content, self-controlled, with mind and intelligence fixed on Me — that devotee is dear to Me.
    Practical Applications
    • Audit each quality in this list as your weekly self-review.
    • Address the weakest quality with deliberate practice.
    • Treat 'dear to Me' as a real bond, not metaphor.
    Reflection Questions
    • Which quality from this list is weakest in me?
    • What single discipline addresses it?
    Analogy

    Picture a still pond at dawn: no envy ripples its surface, no claim of ownership darkens its bed, no swing of pleasure and pain stirs it from below. A bird lands; a bird leaves; the pond reflects each and holds neither. Such a pond, Kṛṣṇa says, is the bhakta's heart — and that heart is dear to Him because His own face is reflected there without distortion.

    Stories
    Vidura's refusal

    When Duryodhana invited Kṛṣṇa to a palace feast on the eve of war, Kṛṣṇa walked past the royal kitchens and went instead to Vidura's modest house, where he ate plain greens. Vidura, born of a maidservant, owned nothing, claimed nothing, was equal in honor and dishonor, friendly to every being including the cousins who had insulted him.

    Kṛṣṇa's choice that night is His own commentary on this verse. Adveṣṭā, nirmamaḥ, nirahaṅkāraḥ, sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ — every qualifier the verse lists, Vidura embodied. So Kṛṣṇa came and ate at his table.

    Haridāsa Ṭhākura under the lash

    When the Kazi of Phulia ordered Haridāsa Ṭhākura beaten across twenty-two market places for chanting the holy name, Haridāsa felt no enmity. As the rods came down, he prayed for the soldiers and the Kazi himself, asking the Lord not to hold their offense against them.

    Forgiving (kṣamī), nonenvious (adveṣṭā), friendly (maitra) even to executioners, equal in distress and happiness, his mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa with determination — Haridāsa is this verse standing upright in the marketplace. Caitanya Mahāprabhu would later name him nāmācārya for the same reason Kṛṣṇa here names such a soul 'priyaḥ.'

    Important Words
    • adveṣṭā — non-envious
      Negative agent-noun: 'one who does not hate.' The list opens not with positive virtue but with the removal of dveṣa. Envy is the first weed; nothing else grows until it is pulled.
    • maitraḥ — friendly
      Same root as maitrī. Friendliness is the active counterpart of non-enmity. Not neutrality — warm regard, extended to all beings (sarva-bhūtānām).
    • nirmamaḥ — without 'mine'-ness
      Negation of mama (my). Possessiveness pulled out at the root. The bhakta still uses things, still has relationships, but the silent label 'mine' has been peeled off.
    • nirahaṅkāraḥ — without false ego
      Negation of ahaṅkāra (I-maker). Paired with nirmama as the twin disease. Together they form the deepest knot of conditioned identity. Both must go.
    • kṣamī — forgiving, tolerant
      From kṣam, 'to be patient, to forgive.' Same root as kṣamā, the earth's signature virtue. The bhakta absorbs offense the way the earth absorbs the plow.
    • santuṣṭaḥ — always satisfied
      Sam + tuṣ — 'completely contented.' The chronic dissatisfaction that drives the conditioned soul has been extinguished; whatever comes is enough.
    • dṛḍha - niścayaḥ — with firm determination
      Compound: dṛḍha (firm) + niścaya (resolve). The list is not a soft character sketch — it is held together by an iron determination, without which the rest collapses.
    • priyaḥ — dear
      The chapter's signature word from here onward. Repeated like a refrain in vv. 13-20. Kṛṣṇa is not merely describing devotees; He is naming whom He loves. Affection, not approval.

    He by whom no one is put into difficulty and who is not disturbed by anyone, who is equipoised in happiness and distress, fear and anxiety, is very dear to Me.
    Purport

    A few of a devotee’s qualifications are further being described. No one is put into difficulty, anxiety, fearfulness or dissatisfaction by such a devotee. Since a devotee is kind to everyone, he does not act in such a way as to put others into anxiety. At the same time, if others try to put a devotee into anxiety, he is not disturbed. It is by the grace of the Lord that he is so practiced that he is not disturbed by any outward disturbance. Actually because a devotee is always engrossed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and engaged in devotional service, such material circumstances cannot move him. Generally a materialistic person becomes very happy when there is something for his sense gratification and his body, but when he sees that others have something for their sense gratification and he hasn’t, he is sorry and envious. When he is expecting some retaliation from an enemy, he is in a state of fear, and when he cannot successfully execute something he becomes dejected. A devotee who is always transcendental to all these disturbances is very dear to Kṛṣṇa.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

    15. He who does not agitate others, nor is agitated by others, he who is free from material emotions such as joy, jealousy, fear and anxiety, is dear to Me.
    Commentary

    From him no one receives disturbance due to fear. Because he is compassionate to all, he does not act to cause distress to others. Also, others do not act to cause distress to him, because they know he is not hostile to anyone. Joy, jealousy, fear and anxiety, causes of disturbance, give him up.     Personal Guidance
    He by whom the world is not disturbed and who is not disturbed by the world — free from joy, envy, fear, anxiety — is dear to Me.
    Practical Applications
    • Audit the bidirectional flow: do I disturb others; am I disturbed by others?
    • Address whichever direction is more problematic.
    • Treat both as part of the same imbalance.
    Reflection Questions
    • Whom in my life do I disturb without realizing?
    • Whose disturbances unsettle me?
    Analogy

    A great rock in a river: the water bends around it without harm, and the rock is not moved by the water. So the devotee — neither he troubles the world, nor does the world's turbulence shake him. Fear, anxiety, elation, resentment all flow past; he stands. Kṛṣṇa says: such a rock in My river is dear to Me.

    Stories
    Bhīṣma on the bed of arrows

    Pierced by his own grand-disciple's arrows, lying on the field for fifty-eight days waiting for the auspicious moment to die, Bhīṣma neither cursed Arjuna nor lamented his fate. He spoke at length to Yudhiṣṭhira on dharma; he conversed with the assembled sages; he greeted Kṛṣṇa with steady eyes.

    He disturbed no one; nothing disturbed him. Harṣa (elation at his great life), amarṣa (resentment at his wounds), bhaya (fear of death), udvega (anxiety about the future) — all four had released him. Kṛṣṇa Himself stood at his side as he departed, evidence enough of the priyaḥ this verse promises.

    Important Words
    • udvijate — is agitated, is disturbed
      From ud-vij, 'to be unsettled, to start in alarm.' Used twice in the verse — both passively (no one is disturbed by him) and actively (he is not disturbed by anyone). The verb defines the verse's two-sided peace.
    • harṣa — elation, excitement
      From hṛṣ, 'to thrill.' First of the four disturbances named. Even pleasant excitement is a wave; the devotee is past wave-motion.
    • amarṣa — resentment, intolerance
      Negation of marṣa (forbearance). The grudge that festers when forbearance fails. The devotee has no such residue.
    • bhaya — fear
      The fundamental contraction of the conditioned soul. The Upaniṣads define mokṣa itself partly as abhayam — fearlessness. Here the bhakta has crossed into that condition.
    • udvegaiḥ — by anxieties
      Plural instrumental. Not one anxiety but the whole class — relational, financial, mortal. From all of them, muktaḥ.
    • muktaḥ — liberated, freed
      Past participle of muc, 'to release.' Same word the Upaniṣads use for the liberated soul. Kṛṣṇa applies the technical term not to a forest renunciate but to one who is simply free of these four agitations.

    Personal Guidance
    He who is impartial, pure, expert, unconcerned with worldly matters, free from worry, who has renounced personal undertakings — is dear to Me.
    Practical Applications
    • Cultivate impartiality and renunciation of personal undertakings.
    • Reduce the number of self-initiated projects; serve Kṛṣṇa's projects instead.
    • Treat unconcern with worldly matters as a freedom, not aloofness.
    Reflection Questions
    • What 'personal undertaking' could I lay down?
    • What would Kṛṣṇa's project for me be?
    Analogy

    A skilled lamp-trimmer does his work in the temple expertly, takes no commission, expects no notice, and feels no sting when his name is omitted from the day's records. The lamps burn; he is content. Kṛṣṇa says: such a one — pure, expert, undisturbed, not striving for any return — is dear to Me.

    Stories
    Sanātana Gosvāmī at Vṛndāvana

    Once a high minister of the Bengal sultanate, Sanātana Gosvāmī fled to Vṛndāvana on Caitanya Mahāprabhu's order and lived under trees, begging mādhukarī each day. He was anapekṣa — needing nothing from the ordinary order; dakṣa — supremely expert (his Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta and Hari-bhakti-vilāsa testify); udāsīna — without care for himself; sarva-ārambha-parityāgī — refusing every fresh enterprise, even his own former lordship.

    When the Lord wished to embrace him, Sanātana shrank back, afraid the Lord's pure body would touch his diseased skin. The Lord embraced him anyway. This verse names what Mahāprabhu held in His arms.

    Important Words
    • anapekṣaḥ — not depending, neutral
      Negation of apekṣā, 'expectation, regard for.' The devotee does not depend on the usual social or material apparatus to feel secure. The hinge of the verse — without this, the rest is performance.
    • śuciḥ — pure
      Internal and external purity together. Same word used in śuci-smita (pure-smiling) for Lakṣmī. Not the asceticism of avoidance but the cleanness of one who has stopped collecting residues.
    • dakṣaḥ — expert, skilled
      From dakṣ, 'to be able, to be competent.' The bhakta is not vague or impractical — he is sharp. Kṛṣṇa pairs spiritual qualification with hard competence.
    • udāsīnaḥ — indifferent, sitting above
      From ud-ās, 'to sit aloft.' Not coldness — elevation. The devotee sits above the petty currents that drag others; he is not dragged because he has climbed up.
    • gata - vyathaḥ — free from distress
      Compound: gata (gone) + vyathā (pain). The pain has departed — not been suppressed. Past participle paints completion, not effort.
    • sarva - ārambha — of all endeavors
      Compound: sarva (all) + ārambha (initiation, undertaking). Paired in the verse with parityāgī. The bhakta starts no new enterprises for his own sake; the engine of self-driven projects has been switched off.
    • parityāgī — renouncer
      Agent-noun from pari-tyaj, 'to release completely.' Pari- gives totality. Whatever needs doing for Kṛṣṇa, he does — but no fresh ārambha for himself.

    One who neither rejoices nor grieves, who neither laments nor desires, and who renounces both auspicious and inauspicious things – such a devotee is very dear to Me.
    Purport

    A pure devotee is neither happy nor distressed over material gain and loss, nor is he very much anxious to get a son or disciple, nor is he distressed by not getting them. If he loses anything which is very dear to him, he does not lament. Similarly, if he does not get what he desires, he is not distressed. He is transcendental in the face of all kinds of auspicious, inauspicious and sinful activities. He is prepared to accept all kinds of risks for the satisfaction of the Supreme Lord. Nothing is an impediment in the discharge of his devotional service. Such a devotee is very dear to Kṛṣṇa.

    Personal Guidance
    He who neither rejoices nor laments, neither hankers nor desires, who renounces both auspicious and inauspicious things — is dear to Me.
    Practical Applications
    • Cultivate equipoise toward both auspicious and inauspicious.
    • Refuse to be jerked between rejoicing and lamenting.
    • Treat the equipoise itself as the practice.
    Reflection Questions
    • Where does my rejoicing-and-lamenting consume my energy?
    • What does the equipoise feel like by contrast?
    Analogy

    A weaver at his loom does not cheer when the shuttle goes right nor weep when it goes left. He weaves. The cloth is the Lord's. The devotee likewise neither rejoices over fortune nor grieves over misfortune, neither yearns ahead nor mourns behind — and from both auspicious and inauspicious he stands at one remove. Such an even-handed weaver, Kṛṣṇa says, is dear.

    Stories
    Bharata Mahārāja in his three lives

    First an emperor — he relinquished the throne without grief and entered the forest without rejoicing. Then a deer — born so because of a moment's attachment to a fawn, he carried the memory of his fall without lament. Then a slow-witted brāhmaṇa — Jaḍa Bharata — who endured insults and praise alike without flicker, who walked when ordered and stopped when ordered, in whom auspicious and inauspicious had collapsed into one steady flow toward the Lord.

    The entire three-life arc of Bharata is the verse in narrative form: na hṛṣyati, na dveṣṭi, na śocati, na kāṅkṣati.

    Important Words
    • hṛṣyati — rejoices, thrills
      From hṛṣ — the same root as harṣa in v.15. The devotee has crossed past the involuntary surge of pleasure at fortune.
    • dveṣṭi — hates, recoils
      From dviṣ — the same root as adveṣṭā in v.13. Recoiling from misfortune is the mirror of clinging to fortune; both are removed.
    • śocati — laments
      From śuc — 'to grieve over what has passed.' The mind's backward pull is cut.
    • kāṅkṣati — desires, yearns
      From kāṅkṣ — 'to long for what has not yet come.' The mind's forward pull is also cut. Together with śocati, the entire time-axis of attachment is severed.
    • śubha — the auspicious
      Paired with aśubha and parityāgī. The devotee does not chase the auspicious — the secret root of much religious life. Even the 'good' is released.
    • aśubha — the inauspicious
      Negation of śubha. The devotee does not flee the inauspicious either. Both belong to the dualistic frame he has left behind.
    • parityāgī — renouncer
      Pari-tyaj — total release. Same word as in v.16. Here the object is the entire śubha/aśubha pair, the dualistic axis under which most life is organized.
    • bhakti - mān — possessing devotion
      Compound with the -mat suffix: 'one in whom bhakti dwells.' The dispassion of the verse is not the dry detachment of an impersonalist — it is detachment held inside an active bhakti.

    A devotee is always free from all bad association. Sometimes one is praised and sometimes one is defamed; that is the nature of human society. But a devotee is always transcendental to artificial fame and infamy, distress or happiness. He is very patient. He does not speak of anything but the topics about Kṛṣṇa; therefore he is called silent. Silent does not mean that one should not speak; silent means that one should not speak nonsense. One should speak only of essentials, and the most essential speech for the devotee is to speak for the sake of the Supreme Lord. A devotee is happy in all conditions; sometimes he may get very palatable foodstuffs, sometimes not, but he is satisfied. Nor does he care for any residential facility. He may sometimes live underneath a tree, and he may sometimes live in a very palatial building; he is attracted to neither. He is called fixed because he is fixed in his determination and knowledge. We may find some repetition in the descriptions of the qualifications of a devotee, but this is just to emphasize the fact that a devotee must acquire all these qualifications. Without good qualifications, one cannot be a pure devotee. Harāv abhaktasya kuto mahad-guṇāḥ: one who is not a devotee has no good qualification. One who wants to be recognized as a devotee should develop the good qualifications. Of course he does not extraneously endeavor to acquire these qualifications, but engagement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and devotional service automatically helps him develop them.

    Personal Guidance
    Equal to friends and enemies, equal in honor and dishonor, in heat and cold, free from contamination and possessiveness, content with whatever happens — silent, taking shelter — is dear to Me.
    Practical Applications
    • Test equality across the dualities: friend/enemy, honor/dishonor, heat/cold.
    • Cultivate content acceptance.
    • Honor silence and shelter-taking as practices.
    Reflection Questions
    • Which duality most consistently breaks my equality?
    • What practice would steady it?
    Analogy

    A traveler on a long road carries no attachment to any inn, befriends those he meets without resenting those who pass him by, eats whatever the village offers, speaks little, and walks on. Honor and dishonor are roadside towns; heat and cold are weather; his destination is steady. Such a traveler, fixed in knowledge and engaged in bhakti, is dear to Kṛṣṇa.

    Stories
    Rūpa and Sanātana under the trees

    Rūpa Gosvāmī and his brother Sanātana, once wealthy ministers, spent their later years in Vṛndāvana with no fixed residence. One night they slept beneath one tree; the next, beneath another. Friends and enemies, honor (the Mughal court had once bowed to them) and dishonor (Sanātana was once jailed by his old master), heat and cold — all became one weather.

    They were maunī when not glorifying Kṛṣṇa, santuṣṭa with whatever mādhukarī the day brought, aniketa with no house of their own, and sthira-mati on Kṛṣṇa's līlā in Vraja. Caitanya Mahāprabhu loved them as Kṛṣṇa loves the man this verse describes.

    Sudāmā walking to Dvārakā

    Sudāmā the brāhmaṇa, Kṛṣṇa's childhood friend, walked to Dvārakā in rags, carrying only a small bundle of flat rice his wife had borrowed from neighbors. He cared neither for his own poverty nor for the splendor of Kṛṣṇa's palace when he arrived. He sought no honor and did not flinch at the guards' surprise; he asked for nothing and accepted whatever came.

    When he returned home and found his hut transformed into a palace, his evenness did not change. Sama in distress, sama in fortune, mauni about his own deserts, santuṣṭa with whatever Kṛṣṇa arranged — Sudāmā is this verse walking on the road.

    Important Words
    • samaḥ — equal, equipoised
      Repeated twice in the verse — first toward friends and enemies, then toward heat, cold, pleasure, pain. The hinge word of the chapter's character sketch. Sameness is not indifference; it is the devotee's even ground under shifting feet.
    • māna — in honor
      Paired in the verse with apamānayoḥ. The most tempting half of the duality for the spiritually accomplished — being respected. The devotee receives it as weather.
    • apamānayoḥ — and dishonor
      Locative dual of apamāna. The dual ending shows the verse explicitly pairing māna with its opposite. The devotee receives both as one weather.
    • saṅga - vivarjitaḥ — free from contaminating association
      Compound: saṅga (attachment, company) + vivarjita (avoided). The whole Gītā's diagnosis (saṅga in 2.62) and prescription in two words. The devotee keeps no company that pulls his mind sideways.
    • maunī — silent
      From mauna — 'silence,' especially the deliberate restraint of speech. Not muteness — disciplined economy. The bhakta speaks of Kṛṣṇa, and is otherwise still.
    • aniketaḥ — without fixed residence
      Negation of niketa (dwelling). Not homeless by misfortune — homeless by choice, because no place on earth has been allowed to become 'mine.'
    • sthira — fixed
      Paired in the verse with matiḥ. From sthā, 'to stand.' The character traits do not float free — they stand on settled ground.
    • matiḥ — determination, intelligence
      From man, 'to think.' Mati is the considered judgment of the buddhi. Sthira-mati is settled intelligence about Kṛṣṇa, without which all the equanimity collapses into mere stoicism.
    • bhakti - mān — possessing devotion
      Repeated from v.17. The whole list is held together by active bhakti — without it the qualities resemble Buddhist or Sāṅkhya equanimity. With it, they are the Vaiṣṇava signature.

    Those who follow this imperishable path of devotional service and who completely engage themselves with faith, making Me the supreme goal, are very, very dear to Me.
    Purport

    In this chapter, from verse 2 through the end – from mayy āveśya mano ye mām (“fixing the mind on Me”) through ye tu dharmāmṛtam idam (“this religion of eternal engagement”) – the Supreme Lord has explained the processes of transcendental service for approaching Him. Such processes are very dear to the Lord, and He accepts a person engaged in them. The question of who is better – one who is engaged in the path of impersonal Brahman or one who is engaged in the personal service of the Supreme Personality of Godhead – was raised by Arjuna, and the Lord replied to him so explicitly that there is no doubt that devotional service to the Personality of Godhead is the best of all processes of spiritual realization. In other words, in this chapter it is decided that through good association one develops attachment for pure devotional service and thereby accepts a bona fide spiritual master and from him begins to hear and chant and observe the regulative principles of devotional service with faith, attachment and devotion and thus becomes engaged in the transcendental service of the Lord. This path is recommended in this chapter; therefore there is no doubt that devotional service is the only absolute path for self-realization, for the attainment of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The impersonal conception of the Supreme Absolute Truth, as described in this chapter, is recommended only up to the time one surrenders himself for self-realization. In other words, as long as one does not have the chance to associate with a pure devotee, the impersonal conception may be beneficial. In the impersonal conception of the Absolute Truth one works without fruitive result, meditates and cultivates knowledge to understand spirit and matter. This is necessary as long as one is not in the association of a pure devotee. Fortunately, if one develops directly a desire to engage in Kṛṣṇa consciousness in pure devotional service, he does not need to undergo step-by-step improvements in spiritual realization. Devotional service, as described in the middle six chapters of Bhagavad-gītā, is more congenial. One need not bother about materials to keep body and soul together, because by the grace of the Lord everything is carried out automatically. Thus end the Bhaktivedanta Purports to the Twelfth Chapter of the Śrīmad Bhagavad-gītā in the matter of Devotional Service.

    Commentary

    Summarizing the qualities fixed in various types of His devotees which He has just mentioned, the Lord now describes the result of hearing and deliberating on the these qualities by devotees desirous of obtaining all those qualities. Since these qualities arise from sense control (śānti, mentioned in verse 12), which in turn arises from bhakti, they are thus not material qualities. bhaktyā tuṣyati kṛṣṇo ṇa guṇaiḥ: Kṛṣṇa is pleased by bhakti, not by good material qualities. (Padyāvalī 8) Such statements are innumerable in the scriptures. Tu here indicates differentiation. The various devotees previously mentioned were fixed in their individual natures with certain qualities. But those practitioners who desire all of the qualities of all the types of devotees are superior to those just mentioned, who have attained only some of the qualities. He is called the knower of the field, situated in these two conditions. Like one who ploughs the field, he is the knower of the field, and is the enjoyer of the fruit. Gṛdhraḥ means “those who desire.” [Note: This word also means “vulture.”] Those who have desire and who move in society, the conditioned jīvas, eat one fruit of the tree called distress, because the tree has the ability to generate distress, even with its ripened fruits of Svarga. The swans, which live in the forest, the liberated jīvas, eat another fruit called happiness, because the tree has ability to generate happiness in the form of liberation. Thus, the one tree of saṁsāra has many forms since it has the capacity to let one attain hell, Svarga, and liberation. It is called māyā-mayam, made of māyā, because it is generated from the Lord’s māyā-śakti. He who knows this tree with the help of the worshipable gurus (ijyaiḥ) knows the Vedas. Tad-vidaḥ in the Gītā verse refers to persons who know the field and the knower of the field.

    Personal Guidance
    Those who follow this imperishable path of devotional service with full faith, making Me the supreme goal — are extremely dear to Me.
    Practical Applications
    • Treat the chapter's full description as one path; commit to it integrally.
    • Don't pick and choose virtues.
    • Make Kṛṣṇa the explicit supreme goal.
    Reflection Questions
    • Which virtues from this chapter have I been cherry-picking?
    • What integral commitment is being asked?
    Analogy

    A river that has been told the whole shape of its journey — every bend, every drop — and that now flows toward the sea with all its waters faithfully turned that way: such a river the ocean draws in with double love. Kṛṣṇa closes the chapter saying of those who follow this whole imperishable path with faith and Him as supreme: 'atīva me priyāḥ' — they are dear to Me beyond measure.

    Stories
    The gopīs at Kurukṣetra

    When Kṛṣṇa met the gopīs again at the solar eclipse pilgrimage at Kurukṣetra, they had long since lost His outward company in Vṛndāvana — but they had followed this whole imperishable path: making Him the supreme goal, completely engaging with faith, dropping every other refuge. They asked for no reunion in Dvārakā's splendor; they wanted Him under the kadamba trees again.

    Kṛṣṇa, in the Bhāgavatam, calls them the topmost of all His devotees. The chapter's closing superlative — atīva me priyāḥ — is fulfilled in them. Not 'dear' once, but 'very, very dear,' as the verse intensifies.

    Important Words
    • dharma — of religion
      Paired in the verse with amṛtam. Kṛṣṇa calls this whole chapter dharma — and immediately calls it amṛta. Duty here is itself ambrosial, not a burdensome obligation.
    • amṛtam — nectar, the deathless
      Negation of mṛta (dead). Amṛta is what does not die — the gods' nectar, also a name for liberation. Kṛṣṇa labels the chapter's whole path with this word: it ends in deathlessness.
    • uktam — as said
      Past participle of vac, 'to speak.' The reference is to the entire chapter Kṛṣṇa has just delivered. Not part of it — the whole. The verse demands integral practice.
    • paryupāsate — completely worship
      Same word Arjuna used in v.1 for both classes of worshipers. Kṛṣṇa returns the word at the close, applying it now to those who follow His own prescription — settling the comparison His chapter began.
    • śraddadhānāḥ — with faith
      Present participle of śrad-dhā, 'to place faith.' Echoes parayā śraddhayā of v.2. Faith bookends the chapter: the practice opens and closes in śraddhā.
    • mat - paramāḥ — taking Me as supreme
      Compound: mat + parama (highest). Same construction as v.6-7's mat-parāḥ. Kṛṣṇa is positioned at the apex of all goals. Without this positioning, the practice has no center.
    • atīva — very, very, exceedingly
      Intensifier. Throughout verses 13-19 the bhakta is priyaḥ. Here, at the close, he is atīva priyaḥ — exceedingly dear. The chapter's affection climbs as it ends.
    • priyāḥ — dear
      Final word of the chapter, plural, intensified by atīva. The whole chapter's argument terminates not in metaphysical resolution but in declared love. Kṛṣṇa's last word is His own affection.
