Bhagavad-gītā 1.39

Important Words
  • kula - kṣaye — in destroying the family
    Locative — *in the destruction of the family*. The whole next stretch of verses uses *kula* as its hinge. The *kula* is the Vedic unit of moral transmission — bigger than the household, smaller than the *varṇa*. The Gītā will eventually replace *kula-dharma* with *sanātana-dharma* (eternal dharma directly to the Lord), but here Arjuna mourns the smaller unit appropriately.
  • praṇaśyanti — become vanquished
    *Pra-naś* — to perish utterly. Same root *naś-* as in *vinaśyatv apuruṣaḥ* ("may the man perish"), and as in *naṣṭa* (lost). Arjuna will use this root again in 1.40 (*dharme naṣṭe*). The same root will recur in BG 18.73 (*naṣṭo mohaḥ*) — "my illusion is destroyed." Same verb of destruction; different objects.
  • kula - dharmāḥ — the family traditions
    *Kula* + *dharma* in compound. The *household / clan* dharmas — the daily worship, the seasonal observances, the marriage rules, the offerings to ancestors. The micro-fabric of Vedic life. Arjuna is right that when these collapse, the larger fabric tears.
  • sanātanāḥ — eternal
    *Sanātana* — eternal, ancient-and-ongoing. The same word that names *sanātana-dharma*. Arjuna calls the *kula-dharmas* *sanātana* — they have always been so. But the Gītā will later teach that what is most *sanātana* is not the *kula-dharma* but the soul itself (*sanātana eva ca*, BG 2.24). Arjuna's *sanātana* is the smaller one.
  • adharmaḥ — irreligion
    *A-* (negation) + *dharma*. Not just *absence* of dharma but *active opposition* to it. The Gītā treats *adharma* as a force, almost a personality: when dharma weakens, *adharma* *overcomes* (*abhibhavati*) the family. Vedic moral physics has both forces — they do not coexist neutrally.
With the destruction of the dynasty, the eternal family tradition is vanquished, and thus the rest of the family becomes involved in irreligion.
Purport

In the system of the varṇāśrama institution there are many principles of religious traditions to help members of the family grow properly and attain spiritual values. The elder members are responsible for such purifying processes in the family, beginning from birth to death. But on the death of the elder members, such family traditions of purification may stop, and the remaining younger family members may develop irreligious habits and thereby lose their chance for spiritual salvation. Therefore, for no purpose should the elder members of the family be slain.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
39. With the destruction of the family, the eternal proper conduct of the family is destroyed. When that dharma is destroyed, the family is completely filled with adharma.
Commentary

The right conduct maintained by the family, which has been passed down from many generations (sanātanāḥ kula-dharmāḥ), will be destroyed with the destruction of the family (kula-kṣaye).

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
39. With the destruction of the family, the traditional rules of conduct of the family are destroyed. When that dharma is destroyed, adharma will conquer all the remaining members of the family.
Commentary

In this verse Arjuna expands upon the sin of destroying the family. The religious activities such as fire sacrifice (kula dharmāḥ) which have been obtained through the family traditions (sanatanāḥ) will be destroyed because of destruction of the performers. The word uta here means “even,” and is linked to kṛtsnam. With the destruction of performance of religious activities, irreligion will conquer (abhibhavati) even (uta) all the remaining family members consisting of small children and others.

Translation
1.39 Sri Sankaracharya did not comment on this sloka. The commentary starts from 2.10.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
1.26 - 1.47 Arjuna said - Sanjaya said Sanjaya continued: The high-minded Arjuna, extremely kind, deeply friendly, and supremely righteous, having brothers like himself, though repeatedly deceived by the treacherous attempts of your people like burning in the lac-house etc., and therefore fit to be killed by him with the help of the Supreme Person, nevertheless said, 'I will not fight.' He felt weak, overcome as he was by his love and extreme compassion for his relatives. He was also filled with fear, not knowing what was righteous and what unrighteous. His mind was tortured by grief, because of the thought of future separation from his relations. So he threw away his bow and arrow and sat on the chariot as if to fast to death.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
1.35 1.44 Nihatya etc. upto anususruma. Sin alone is the agent in the act of slaying these desperadoes. Therefore here the idea is this : These ememies of ours have been slain, i.e., have been take possession of, by sin. Sin would come to us also after slaying them. Sin in this context is the disregard, on account of greed etc., to the injurious conseences like the ruination of the family and the like. That is why Arjuna makes a specific mention of the [ruin of the] family etc., and of its duties in the passage 'How by slaying my own kinsmen etc'. The act of slaying, undertaken with an individualizing idea about its result, and with a particularizing idea about the person to be slain, is a great sin. Swami Sivananda Classical
Translation
1.39. Why should not we who clearly see evil in the destruction of families, learn to turn away from this sin, O Janardana (Krishna)?
Commentary

1.39 कथम् why? न not? ज्ञेयम् should be learnt? अस्माभिः by us? पापात् from sin? अस्मात् this? निवर्तितुम् to turn away? कुलक्षयकृतम् in the destruction of families? दोषम् evil? प्रपश्यद्भिः clearly seeing? जनार्दन O Janardana.Commentary Ignorance of law is no excuse but wanton sinful conduct is a grave crime? unworthy of us? who are wiser.

Swami Gambhīrānanda Classical
Translation
1.38 1.39 O Janardana, although these people, whose hearts have become perverted by greed, do not see the evil arising from destroying the family and sin in hostility towards, friends, yet how can we who clearly see the evil arising from destroying the family remain unaware of (the need of) abstaining from this sin?
Shri Purohit Swami Classical
Translation
1.39 Should not we, whose eyes are open, who consider it to be wrong to annihilate our house, turn away from so great a crime?

Personal Guidance
When the structures that hold society together collapse, the soul drifts; protect the structures of dharma in your own life with care.
Practical Applications
  • Identify the small daily structures that hold your spiritual life together (morning sādhana, deity worship, association) and protect them fiercely.
  • Do not casually break the spiritual rhythms of your home — they hold more than you realize.
  • Build at least one 'family tradition' rooted in Kṛṣṇa-bhakti that future generations can inherit.
Reflection Questions
  • Which spiritual structures in my life are quietly eroding?
  • What inheritance of dharma am I leaving — by my actions, not my words?
Analogy

A river loses its banks and floods the village. A family loses its dharma and the family itself dissolves into adharma. The structure does not survive its principle; the form perishes with the spirit. Arjuna is right about the analysis — only the prescription will be corrected.
