Important Words
  • śvetaiḥ — with white
    Vedic colour-coding: *śveta* = pure, sāttvika, brāhmaṇa-like. Arjuna's chariot is yoked to the colour of sattva. Krishna's chariot driving is the yoking of action (kṣatriya) to purity (brāhmaṇa) — the very synthesis the Gītā will later teach.
  • syandane — in a chariot
    *Syandana* — from *syand* (to flow, to run swiftly). The chariot is the *flowing one*, the moving platform. The Upaniṣads use the chariot as the body-image (Kaṭha 1.3.3: *ātmānaṁ rathinaṁ viddhi*). Vyāsa here puts the Lord *as charioteer* on a chariot — anticipating the metaphor that lets the whole Gītā be heard as Kṛṣṇa driving the soul.
  • mādhavaḥ — Kṛṣṇa, husband of the goddess of fortune
    *Mā* (Lakṣmī) + *dhava* (husband). The first of Kṛṣṇa's many names in the Gītā — and it is His relational name, not His personal one. Vyāsa introduces Him as *Lakṣmī's lord*. Fortune itself rides on this chariot.
  • pāṇḍavaḥ — Arjuna, son of Pāṇḍu
    Arjuna is named only as *Pāṇḍu's son* — by lineage, not by personal name. Vyāsa pairs *Mādhava* with *Pāṇḍava* — Lakṣmī's lord beside Pāṇḍu's son. The two relational names sit in dignified balance: Lord and devotee, named not by ego but by belonging.
  • divyau — transcendental
    *Divya* — from *div* (to shine, the heavens). Same root as *deva*, *divine*. The opposing conches were earthly *śaṅkhāḥ*; these are *divyau śaṅkhau*. Two conches, dual number — the Lord and His servant always sound together. The Gītā's whole theology of *nāma-saṅkīrtana* is foreshadowed here: divine sound, sounded in pairs.
On the other side, both Lord Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna, stationed on a great chariot drawn by white horses, sounded their transcendental conchshells.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Sañjaya pans the camera to the Pāṇḍava side. The image is regal: Kṛṣṇa as charioteer and Arjuna as warrior on one great chariot drawn by white horses, blowing 'divya' (celestial) conchs. The dual verbs and dual nouns underline their inseparable partnership.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
The white horses (śveta-haya) are gifts from Citraratha (Mahābhārata Ādi-parvan). The chariot itself is Agni's, given to Arjuna during the Khāṇḍava burning. Note the deliberate use of dual number throughout (sthitau, divyau, śaṅkhau, pradadhmatuḥ) — Sañjaya marks Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna as a pair, Nara and Nārāyaṇa, anticipating the deeper theology of their relationship that the Gītā will unfold.

Purport

In contrast with the conchshell blown by Bhīṣmadeva, the conchshells in the hands of Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are described as transcendental. The sounding of the transcendental conchshells indicated that there was no hope of victory for the other side because Kṛṣṇa was on the side of the Pāṇḍavas. Jayas tu pāṇḍu-putrāṇāṁ yeṣāṁ pakṣe janārdanaḥ. Victory is always with persons like the sons of Pāṇḍu because Lord Kṛṣṇa is associated with them. And whenever and wherever the Lord is present, the goddess of fortune is also there because the goddess of fortune never lives alone without her husband. Therefore, victory and fortune were awaiting Arjuna, as indicated by the transcendental sound produced by the conchshell of Viṣṇu, or Lord Kṛṣṇa. Besides that, the chariot on which both the friends were seated had been donated by Agni (the fire-god) to Arjuna, and this indicated that this chariot was capable of conquering all sides, wherever it was drawn over the three worlds.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Commentary

In this verse, the desire to fight which arose in the troops of the Pāṇḍavas is described. Though the others were also standing in their chariots, Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are particularly mentioned in this regard to indicate the special nature of the chariot, which was given by Agni himself, [Note: The chariot, quiver, horses were given to Arjuna and cakra to Kṛṣṇa by Agni before he devoured the Khāṇḍava forest.] which had great power and could conquer the three worlds.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Here are a few more symbols indicating the Pandavas' victory: Krsna is on their side. Krsna and Arjuna's conchshells are called 'divyau',transcendental. Where Krsna is present there is also Laksmidevi, the goddess of fortune.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa and the pure devotee blow their conches together — the Lord and His servant always work in unison.
Practical Applications
  • When you serve, picture yourself working alongside a great devotee, not alone.
  • Read about Arjuna's friendship with Kṛṣṇa to feel the sweetness of confidential service.
  • Allow Kṛṣṇa to act through you today — set aside the burden of being the doer.
Reflection Questions
  • When I serve, am I aware of Kṛṣṇa as my companion, or only as my employer?
  • What 'conch' has Kṛṣṇa given me to blow — and am I sounding it?
Analogy

When the Lord and His friend stand on one chariot drawn by white horses and blow their conches together, the sound is not louder than the enemy's — it is *different*. A single transcendental note set against a hundred war-drums: small in volume, infinite in source.

Stories
The white horses of Arjuna's chariot

Arjuna's chariot was given by Agni, the fire-god, after Arjuna helped him consume the Khāṇḍava forest. Its four white horses came from the celestial regions, gifted by Citraratha the king of the Gandharvas. The chariot was indestructible and the banner was Hanumān himself, who had agreed to ride along until the war's end.

Now Kṛṣṇa drives this chariot. Vyāsa names the colour of the horses — *śveta* — because whiteness in Vedic poetry is the colour of purity, of brāhmaṇa-knowledge, of Sarasvatī. The Lord and the kṣatriya ride together, drawn by purity itself, and from this chariot the *divyau śaṅkhau* — transcendental conches — are sounded. The opposing side blew earthly conches; this side blows *divine* ones. The verse already hints at the unequal sources of sound.
