Bhagavad-gītā 1.42

दोषैरेतैः कुलघ्न‍ानां वर्णसङ्करकारकैः । उत्साद्यन्ते जातिधर्माः कुलधर्माश्च शाश्वताः ॥ ४२ ॥

Important Words
  • doṣaiḥ — by such faults
    *Doṣa* (v.37–38) returns — now in the instrumental plural, as *the agency* by which destruction occurs. Same root *duṣ-* (to spoil) as *duṣṭa* (corrupt) and *Kāli-yuga*'s *duṣṭam*. The verse is naming *doṣa* as a self-propagating force.
  • kula - ghnānām — of the destroyers of the family
    Same compound as v.41, repeated. Arjuna keeps applying this label to himself by anticipation. The Gītā will eventually relieve him of this anxiety by showing that the *true* preservation of the *kula* requires the discharge of his duty, not its abandonment.
  • kārakaiḥ — which are causes
    *Kāraka* — agent, cause. From *kṛ* — to do, to make. Same root as *karma*. *Kāraka* is also a technical grammatical term: the syntactic relation a word bears to an action (agent, instrument, object, etc.). The unwanted progeny become *kārakas* — *agents* — of further destruction. Adharma propagates by producing new agents of itself.
  • jāti - dharmāḥ — community projects
    *Jāti* (birth, community) + *dharma*. The *community-level* dharmas, broader than the *kula*. The verse hierarchizes: *kula-dharma* is the family unit; *jāti-dharma* is the community unit. Arjuna sees both collapsing. The Gītā will eventually transcend both with *bhakti-dharma* (BG 9.32), open to all *jātis*.
  • śāśvatāḥ — eternal
    *Śāśvata* — perpetual, abiding. A close synonym of *sanātana* (v.39). The Gītā uses *śāśvata* repeatedly for the soul (BG 2.20, 11.18) and for Kṛṣṇa (BG 10.12). Arjuna applies *śāśvata* to *kula-dharma*. The Gītā will gently relocate the adjective onto the soul itself: the only truly *śāśvata* thing is the *ātman*.
By the evil deeds of those who destroy the family tradition and thus give rise to unwanted children, all kinds of community projects and family welfare activities are devastated.
Purport

Community projects for the four orders of human society, combined with family welfare activities, as they are set forth by the institution of sanātana-dharma, or varṇāśrama-dharma, are designed to enable the human being to attain his ultimate salvation. Therefore, the breaking of the sanātana-dharma tradition by irresponsible leaders of society brings about chaos in that society, and consequently people forget the aim of life – Viṣṇu. Such leaders are called blind, and persons who follow such leaders are sure to be led into chaos.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
42. Both caste and family rules are destroyed by this fault of mixed varṇas caused by the killers of family members.
Commentary

By the faults of mixture of castes, both the caste rules and the family rules will be destroyed (utsādyate).

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
42. Both caste and traditional family rules are destroyed by this fault of mixed varṇas caused by the killers of family members.
Commentary

In two verses, Arjuna summarizes the faults. The principles of being a kṣatriya (jāti dharma) and the principles and special rules of the particular family (kula dharma), and āśrāma principles as well (ca), will disappear (utsādyante).

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
1.42 Sri Sankaracharya did not comment on this sloka. The commentary starts from 2.10.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
1.26 - 1.47 Arjuna said - Sanjaya said Sanjaya continued: The high-minded Arjuna, extremely kind, deeply friendly, and supremely righteous, having brothers like himself, though repeatedly deceived by the treacherous attempts of your people like burning in the lac-house etc., and therefore fit to be killed by him with the help of the Supreme Person, nevertheless said, 'I will not fight.' He felt weak, overcome as he was by his love and extreme compassion for his relatives. He was also filled with fear, not knowing what was righteous and what unrighteous. His mind was tortured by grief, because of the thought of future separation from his relations. So he threw away his bow and arrow and sat on the chariot as if to fast to death.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
1.35 1.44 Nihatya etc. upto anususruma. Sin alone is the agent in the act of slaying these desperadoes. Therefore here the idea is this : These ememies of ours have been slain, i.e., have been take possession of, by sin. Sin would come to us also after slaying them. Sin in this context is the disregard, on account of greed etc., to the injurious conseences like the ruination of the family and the like. That is why Arjuna makes a specific mention of the [ruin of the] family etc., and of its duties in the passage 'How by slaying my own kinsmen etc'. The act of slaying, undertaken with an individualizing idea about its result, and with a particularizing idea about the person to be slain, is a great sin. Personal Guidance
Without the offerings and remembrances rooted in dharma, the lineage of souls — past, present, and future — loses its anchor.
Practical Applications
  • Maintain regular remembrance of departed family members through Vaiṣṇava means (offering prasāda, chanting, śrāddha).
  • Treat your gṛhastha responsibilities as worship, not as competing with worship.
  • Pass on at least one spiritual practice to the next generation — not as duty but as gift.
Reflection Questions
  • How am I caring for the lineage of souls that came before me?
  • What spiritual gift am I leaving for those who come after?
Analogy

Arjuna is now naming the chain of consequence in full: family-destroyers → mixed offspring → loss of *jāti-dharma* → loss of *kula-dharma*. The logic is air-tight as dharma-sociology. What is missing is only the higher principle that the Gītā will reveal: *Kṛṣṇa Himself is the maintainer of dharma; refusing one's duty does not preserve dharma — it abandons it to Him to repair alone*.
