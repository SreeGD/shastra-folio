Important Words
  • utsanna — spoiled
    *Ut-sad* — to be uprooted, to be spoiled. Same root *sad-* (to sink, to settle) that produced *viṣīda* (v.27) and *sīdanti* (v.28). Vyāsa keeps weaving this root through the chapter. *Sad-* is the underlying physics of the whole *viṣāda-yoga* — things keep *settling, sinking, being uprooted*.
  • manuṣyāṇām — of such men
    *Manuṣya* — human being. From *manu* (the first man) — same root as *manas* (mind). Sanskrit's word for human is *the one with mind*. Arjuna names humans through the mind-faculty; the Gītā will spend chapters disciplining precisely the *manas* of *manuṣya*.
  • narake — in hell
    *Naraka* — the Vedic underworlds, repeated from v.41. Arjuna doubles down: *eternally* in *naraka*. The Gītā does not contradict the cosmology; it offers the way out of it (BG 9.30–32: *even the worst sinner, if turning to Me, is to be considered righteous*).
  • anuśuśruma — I have heard by disciplic succession
    First-person plural perfect of *anu-śru* — to hear *along*, to hear *after* a teacher. The technical Vedic word for *received tradition*. Arjuna invokes *paramparā* to justify his refusal. The Gītā's response in chapter 4 will be precisely this: *evaṁ paramparā-prāptam* — this knowledge was lost; I am restoring the paramparā by speaking it to you now. The very tradition Arjuna invokes is being re-spoken by its source.
O Kṛṣṇa, maintainer of the people, I have heard by disciplic succession that those whose family traditions are destroyed dwell always in hell.

Plain meaning (तात्पर्य · Tātparya)
Arjuna closes the chain with scriptural authority: 'we have heard (by paramparā) that those who destroy kula-dharma are damned to hell.' He appeals to anuśuśruma — the inherited tradition — as the seal on his case for not fighting.
Further notes (विवरण · Vivaraṇa)
Anuśuśruma is a striking perfect: 'we have heard from those before us' — structurally indicating a paramparā (chain of transmission). It is the same √śru that gives śruti. Niyataṁ vāsaḥ ('inevitable dwelling') marks the hell-state as karmically fixed. This is Arjuna's appeal to authority, the closing move in his speech. Kṛṣṇa will counter in 2.7 by inviting Arjuna into another, deeper paramparā — beginning the Gītā's actual teaching.

Purport

Arjuna bases his argument not on his own personal experience, but on what he has heard from the authorities. That is the way of receiving real knowledge. One cannot reach the real point of factual knowledge without being helped by the right person who is already established in that knowledge. There is a system in the varṇāśrama institution by which before death one has to undergo the process of atonement for his sinful activities. One who is always engaged in sinful activities must utilize the process of atonement, called prāyaścitta. Without doing so, one surely will be transferred to hellish planets to undergo miserable lives as the result of sinful activities.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
43. O Janārdana, we have heard that those men whose family rules, varṇa and āśrāma rules are destroyed live in hell permamently.
Commentary

In this verse the phrase kula dharma stands for jāti dharma and āśrāma dharma as well. We have heard from the mouth of the guru (anuśuśruma) about this, with such words as the following: prāyaścittam akurvāṇāḥ pāpeṣu niratā narāḥ apaścāt-tāpinaḥ kaṣṭān nirayān yānti dāruṇān Those who do not perform prāyaścittas after engaging in sins, and are not repentant, go to the most terrible hells. [Note: This is quoted by Śrīdhara Svāmī.]

2. Family Traditions Stop: Irreligion increases (1.39)

With the destruction of dynasty, the entire family tradition is vanquished, and thus the rest of the family becomes involved in irreligion

3. Women polluted (1.40)

When irreligion is prominent, the women in the family become polluted
Chanakya Pandit says “Women need protection”
Why women need special protection – Because they are
Less intelligent
Not trustworthy
Prone to degradation
How they are protected – By engaging in religious principles, either under father, husband or son
If they are not protected, it leads to adultery and varna-sankara

4. Varna-sankara (unwanted progeny) (1.40*)

From degradation of woman comes unwanted progeny
Varna-sankara has three effects
Risk of war and pestilence
Hellish life
For the whole family – Ancestors falldown because pinda-dana stops

▪

For those who destroy family

All community projects and family welfare acts stop

5. Hellish life for ‘family’ and ‘destroyers of family’ (1.41*)

6. Culture is destroyed – Community projects and family welfare acts stop (1.42)

Focus of Learning

(c) Correct understanding about forgiveness / saintliness

Saintliness is not justified for a ksatriya
Compared to “Rama-rajya” – Lord Rama gave sufficient lessons to Ravana who was an aggressor because he kidnapped Rama’s wife, Mother Sita

(d) ‘Madhava’ – Arjuna addresses Krishna as ‘Madhava’ to indicate:

Krishna is the husband of the Goddess of ‘fortune’
Krishna should not cause an action which brings ‘misfortune’

Obligation (To not refuse when challenged to battle or gamble) is binding when the effect is good, not otherwise

Personal Guidance
The destruction of caste-dharma — the systematic structures that allow each soul to do its proper work — leaves society leaderless and disordered.
Practical Applications
  • Honor the natural varṇāśrama tendencies in yourself and others rather than forcing everyone into the same role.
  • Recognize the contribution of each devotee's nature; do not demand they all become like you.
  • Build community structures that allow brāhmaṇical, kṣatriya, vaiśya, and śūdra natures to all serve Kṛṣṇa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I trying to force everyone into one mold rather than honoring different natures?
  • How might my community better serve the diversity of Vaiṣṇava temperaments?
Analogy

Arjuna invokes tradition (*anuśuśruma* — "we have heard by disciplic succession") to support his refusal. But to claim *paramparā* against the personal instruction of Kṛṣṇa Himself, who *is* the source of the *paramparā* (BG 4.1–3), is to use the river's water to argue against the river's spring. The Gītā will not deny tradition; it will reseat tradition in its true source.
