Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu .3.53

nitya-priyāḥ sura-carāḥ sādhakāś ceti te tridhā |
[nitya-priyaah sura-charaah saadhakaash cheti te tridhaa |]
kecid eṣu sthirā jātyā mantrivat tam upāsate ||53||
[kechid eshu sthiraa jaatyaa mantrivat tam upaasate ||53||]
taṁ hāsayanti cāpalāḥ kecid vaihāsikopamāḥ |
[tam haasayanti chaapalaah kechid vaihaasikopamaah |]
kecid ārjava-sāreṇa saralāḥ śīlayanti tam ||54||
[kechid aarjava-saarena saralaah sheelayanti tam ||54||]
vāmā vakrima-cakreṇa kecid vismāyayanty amum |
[vaamaa vakrima-chakrena kechid vismaayayanty amum |]
kecit pragalbhāḥ kurvanti vitaṇḍām amunā |
[kechit pragalbhaah kurvanti vitandaam amunaa |]
saumyāḥ sūnṛtayā vācā dhanyā dhinvanti taṁ pare ||55||
[saumyaah soonritayaa vaachaa dhanyaa dhinvanti tam pare ||55||]
Blended reading (Sanskrit + English inline)
nitya-priyāḥ sura-carāḥ sādhakāś ceti te tridhā |
kecid eṣu sthirā jātyā mantrivat tam upāsate ||53||
taṁ hāsayanti cāpalāḥ kecid vaihāsikopamāḥ |
kecid ārjava-sāreṇa saralāḥ śīlayanti tam ||54||
vāmā vakrima-cakreṇa kecid vismāyayanty amum |
kecit pragalbhāḥ kurvanti vitaṇḍām amunā |
saumyāḥ sūnṛtayā vācā dhanyā dhinvanti taṁ pare ||55||
There are three types of friends: nitya-priya (nitya-siddha), devatā (previously) and sādhana-siddha. Some, steady and acting like advisors, worship Kṛṣṇa. Others have a fickle nature and make Kṛṣṇa laugh in the manner of jesters. Others, with meek nature, make Him happy by their serious behavior. Others astonish Him by crooked behavior. Other confident friends argue with Him. Some peaceful, fortunate boys please Him with truthful, pleasing words.
Purport

There are three divisions among Kṛṣṇa’s friends: some are eternally in friendship with Kṛṣṇa, some are elevated demigods, and some are perfected devotees. In all of these groups there are some who by nature are fixed in Kṛṣṇa’s service and are always engaged in giving counsel; some of them are very fond of joking and naturally cause Kṛṣṇa to smile by their words; some of them are by nature very simple, and by their simplicity they please Lord Kṛṣṇa; some of them create wonderful situations by their activities, apparently against Kṛṣṇa; some of them are very talkative, always arguing with Kṛṣṇa and creating a debating atmosphere; and some of them are very gentle and give pleasure to Kṛṣṇa by their sweet words.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Sādhaka here means sādhana-siddha (since those performing sādhana could not directly associate with the Lord.) The suracaras (those who were previously devatās) are also sādhana-siddha, but they have been considered separately to show their different origin. Vismāyayanti is the original version. Because the agent (subject) is not the direct cause of astonishment the form vismāpayanti is not used. They cause astonishment by crooked behavior. (Pāṇini 7.3.40 in relation to bhaya) The form vismerayanti from the noun vismera could also be used, though usually in making causative verbs there is reversion to the root of the verb. Thus, karoti (he does) becomes the causative kārayati (cause him to do) by adding the suffix ya to the root. Some forms of the verbs drop the causative suffix ya however. The causative form vāditavān (he causes someone to speak) formed from the past passive participle from the root vad, drops the ya, as does the causative form of aorist tense avīvadat (he caused someone to speak). Of course, there are examples like the noun ūḍhi (fame), derived from the verb root vah (to carry), which could become the verb ūḍhayati (he causes fame or he becomes famous), which could form the aorist aujiḍhat (he became famous). If such forms are not seen however, what is the loss?
