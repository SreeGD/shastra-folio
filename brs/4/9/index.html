    rasā eva rasābhāsā rasa-jṣair anukīrtitāḥ ||1||
    [rasaa eva rasaabhaasaa rasa-jshair anukeertitaah ||1||]
    Blended reading (Sanskrit + English inline)
    pūrvam evānuśiṣṭena vikalā rasa-lakṣaṇā |
    rasā eva rasābhāsā rasa-jṣair anukīrtitāḥ ||1||
    What appears to be rasa but is without the characteristics previously mentioned is called rasābhāsa by the knowers of rasa.
    Purport

    NoD 51: Perverted Expression of Mellows

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    The word rasa here means “that which suddenly appears to be rasa.” Vikalā means “deficient,” and vikalā rasa-lakṣaṇā means “with deficiency in the characteristics of rasa such as vibhāva, anubhāva, sthāyi-bhāva, vyabhicāri-bhāva and sāttvika-bhāvas.”

    ↗
    tatra śāntoparasāḥ –
    [tatra shaantoparasaah –]
    brahma-bhāvāt para-brahmaṇy advaitādhikya-yogataḥ |
    [brahma-bhaavaat para-brahmany advaitaadhikya-yogatah |]
    tathā bībhatsa-bhūmādeḥ śānto hy uparaso bhavet ||4||
    [tathaa beebhatsa-bhoomaadeh shaanto hy uparaso bhavet ||4||]
    Blended reading (Sanskrit + English inline)
    tatra śāntoparasāḥ –
    brahma-bhāvāt para-brahmaṇy advaitādhikya-yogataḥ |
    tathā bībhatsa-bhūmādeḥ śānto hy uparaso bhavet ||4||
    Śāntoparasa arises from accepting the impersonal brahman feature of the Lord, from contemplating oneness of everything with the Lord, from continual disdain for the body, and from discrimination of cit and acit.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    The para-brahma mentioned is Kṛṣṇa, who is the basis of the impersonal aspect (brahma-bhāva). This is explained in the Gītā verse brahmaṇo hi pratiṣṭḥāham: I am the basis of brahman. Thus, śāntoparasa can arise from appreciating exclusively the impersonal aspect (brahma-bhāvāt) of the Lord (para-brahmaṇi). Advaitādhikya-yogataḥ means “from considering the absolute non-difference of everything from the Lord since He is the cause of everything.” Bībhatsa-bhūmādeḥ means “from continual disgust with the body and other material factors.” Ādi indicates that śāntoparasa can also arise from absorption in discriminating cit from acit (matter and spirit). [Note: This is one of the activities of the jṣānī.] From this point onwards, examples will be raised showing only some of these factors.

    atha śṛṅgāroparasaḥ | tatra sthāyi-vairūpyam –

    yānekatra tathaikasya sthāyinaḥ sā virūpatā |
    vibhāvasyaiva vairūpyaṁ sthāyiny atropacaryate ||13||
    madhuroparasa: The sthāyi-bhāva of madhura-rasa is distorted where the love between two persons is one-sided or where one of the persons has many lovers. The distortion of the vibhāva brings about a distortion in the sthāyi-bhāva.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Vibhāvasyaiva vairūpyam means irregularity in the ālambana (viṣaya and āśraya). There may be some distortion of either the body or the antaḥkaraṇa (internal organ). That is because there can be no real distortion of the sthāyi-bhāva by its very nature. In the example of one-sided rati, there is irregularity in the bodies of the brāhmaṇas’ wives, since their bodies are from brāhmaṇa families. This causes irregularity in the rati (being one-sided): since it is improper, it does not invoke a similar rati in Kṛṣṇa. Thus it is said that one fault brings about another fault elsewhere, by transference. One party having more than one lover indicates irregularity in the antaḥkaraṇa, since there is no dedication to one object of love. This fault would be in reference to the women who love Kṛṣṇa. Another irregularity in female lovers arises when they do not distinguish between superior and inferior male lovers.

    Purport

    When Nārada Muni was passing through Vṛndāvana, he came to the Bhāṇḍīravana forest and saw in one of the trees the famous parrot couple that always accompanies Lord Kṛṣṇa.     Purport

    When Kṛṣṇa was fleeing from the battlefield, from a distant place Jarāsandha was watching Him with restless eyes and was feeling very proud. Being thus puffed up with his conquest, he was repeatedly laughing. This is an example of aparasa.

    The laughter of Jarāsandha is certainly aparasa. If one of Jarāsandha’s followers, with demonic nature laughs at Kṛṣṇa or the event, it is also aparasa. If one of the devotees laughs at the event, it is pure hāsya-rasa.
