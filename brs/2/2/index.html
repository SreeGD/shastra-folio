    Blended reading (Sanskrit + English inline)
    anubhāvās tu citta-stha-bhāvānām avabodhakāḥ |
    te bahir vikriyā prāyāḥ proktā udbhāsvarākhyayā ||1||
    Anubhāvas refers to those things which express the bhāva situated within the heart. They are predominantly external transformations, and are thus called udbhāsvara (shining in the body).
    Purport

    NoD 27: Symptoms of Ecstatic Love The bodily symptoms manifested by a devotee in expressing ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa are called anubhāva.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

    huṅkāro jṛmbhaṇaṁ śvāsa-bhūmā lokānapekṣitā |
    lālā-sravo ’ṭṭahāsaś ca ghūrṇā-hikkādayo ’pi ca ||2||
    External transformations known as anubhāvas are actions, such as dancing, rolling on the ground, singing, shouting, stretching the body, bellowing, yawning, breathing heavily, disregarding others, drooling, laughing loudly, whirling around, and hiccups.
    Purport

    Practical examples of anubhāva are as follows: dancing, rolling on the ground, singing very loudly, stretching the body, crying loudly, yawning, breathing very heavily, neglecting the presence of others, drooling, laughing like a madman, wheeling the head and belching. When there is an extraordinary excess of ecstatic love, with all of these bodily symptoms manifested, one feels relieved transcendentally.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Verse 2.1.12 has already described anubhāvas with the phrase smitādi: smiling and other items. Thus smiling should be understood to be included in the list in this verse, which is elaborating on the word ādi of that previous verse.

    Anubhāvas are of two types with the suitable names of śīta (meaning “cool,” with lack of bodily movement) and kṣepaṇa (meaning “throwing about,” involving distinct bodily movements). Śīta includes singing, yawning, breathing heavily, disregarding others, drooling and smiling. Kṣepaṇa includes dancing, rolling on the ground, shouting, stretching the body, bellowing, laughing loudly, [Note: The definition of aṭṭa-hāsa (laughing loudly) is given in the commentary on verse 18.     Purport

    These symptoms are divided into two parts: one is called śīta, and the other is called kṣepaṇa. When there is yawning, the symptoms are called śīta, and when there is dancing they are called kṣepaṇa.

    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya

    The phrase gīta-jṛmbhādyā means singing, yawning and the items immediately following, namely breathing heavily, disregard, and drooling. Smiling from verse 2.1.12 is also included in the śīta anubhāvas.

    Singing: Moon of Gokula! Today, Rādhā, whose mind is possessed by the beauty of the śrīrāga (or the zeal of love), is singing a new song about you. Her friends have become like stones, and the stones have melted.
    Purport

    Singing Loudly One gopī informed Kṛṣṇa that when Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī was singing about His glories, She enchanted all of Her friends in such a way that they became stonelike and dull. At the same time, the nearby stones began to melt away in ecstatic love.

    Heavy breathing: When the monsoon arrived with colorful clothing, the Cātakī bird named Lalitā became increasingly thirsty. Seeing the form of that black cloud called Kṛṣṇa driven away by the strong breathing of the monsoon wind mixed with rain, she became disturbed.
    Purport

    Breathing Heavily As far as breathing heavily is concerned, it is stated, “Lalitā [one of the gopīs] is just like a cātakī bird, which only takes water falling directly from the rain cloud and not from any other source.” In this statement Kṛṣṇa is compared to the dark cloud, and Lalitā is compared to the cātakī bird seeking only Kṛṣṇa’s company. The metaphor continues to say, “As a heavy wind sometimes disperses a mighty cloud, so the heavy breath from Lalitā’s nostrils caused her to miss Kṛṣṇa, who had disappeared by the time she recovered herself.”

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Ambudāgame refers to the monsoon rains. Trikāṇḍa-śesa says: vātulo vātagulmah syāt caravāyur nidāghajaḥ, jaṣjānilaḥ prāvṛṣijo vāsanto malayānilaḥ: in the hot season there will be strong winds and gales, in the monsoon season there will be rain mixed with wind, and during the spring there will be a fragrant breeze. Because of the strength of her breathing and because of the mixture with tears (wind and rain), Kṛṣṇa disappeared from her sight, because of the disturbance to His cloth.

    An example: I think that the creeper of devotion in the heart of the servant of Kṛṣṇa has been blossoming for a long time, because a multitude of flowers in the form of his loud laughing is shaking his trembling lips.
    Purport

    When a devotee becomes affected with this mental condition, his love is expressed through the lips. The laughing sounds, coming one after another, are compared to flowers falling from the creeper of devotion which grows within the heart of the devotee. In the Caitanya-caritāmṛta devotional service to the Lord is also compared to a creeper which rises up to the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa in Goloka Vṛndāvana.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    The characteristics of aṭṭa-hāsa are as follows. Utphulla-nāsikā-randhram āloḍita-mukhekṣaṇam, uddhataṁ vikṛtākāraṁ nāṭye ’ṭṭa-hasitaṁ viduḥ: aṭṭa-hāsya consists of rude, loud laughing with flared nostrils and agitated glances while dancing. Though the loud laugh presents itself as ferocious to an opposing party when accompanied by abusive language, it presents itself as pleasing to one’s own party. Thus, it can be depicted as somewhat soft. In this verse, someone of the same party describes the devotee’s loud laughing, which indicates scorn for those who criticize bhakti.
