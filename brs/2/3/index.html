    Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu .3.2

    ↗
    sattvād asmāt samutpannā ye ye bhāvās te tu sāttvikāḥ |
    [sattvaad asmaat samutpannaa ye ye bhaavaas te tu saattvikaah |]
    snigdhā digdhās tathā rukṣā ity amī trividhā matāḥ ||2||
    [snigdhaa digdhaas tathaa rukshaa ity amee trividhaa mataah ||2||]
    Blended reading (Sanskrit + English inline)
    sattvād asmāt samutpannā ye ye bhāvās te tu sāttvikāḥ |
    snigdhā digdhās tathā rukṣā ity amī trividhā matāḥ ||2||
    The transformations which arise solely from this sattva are called sāttvika-bhāvas. There are three types of sāttvi­ka-bhāvas: snigdha (affectionate, arising from genuine rati), digdha (tainted, arising from other emotions) and rukṣa (contami­nated, arising in a person without rati).
    Purport

    The symptoms originating from such existential ecstatic love are divided into three headings—namely moist, burnt and dried-up.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    In this definition, it should be understood that transformations arising from sattva (sattvād asmāt samutpannā) should really mean transformations arising solely from sattva. That is because the anubhāvas such as dancing also arise from sattva (heart overwhelmed by rati), but in combination with the intelligence. The sāttvika-bhāvas such as being stunned (stambha) arise from sattva alone, with no help from the intelligence. Thus there is no overlapping of items between anubhāva and sāttvika-bhāva. [Note: Sāttvika-bhāvas can be defined as completely spontaneous transformations of body, without any use of intellect. Another meaning of sāttvika is nature, unaffected by other elements.]

Thus the shaking of her body is classed as digdha-sāttvika-bhāva.] In verse 8 Yaśodā’s anger (secondary rati) due to separation from Kṛṣṇa caused change of her color and choking of her voice.

This niḥsattva, or external manifestation of symptoms, arises by practice in persons who have hardness (no emotions) internally. Because of being manifesting only through this special concentration, they do not occur distinctly. Thus only one example will be given. The wise who consider niḥsattva among the types of sāttvikābhāsa do so only because they appear similar to the sāttvika-bhāvas.
