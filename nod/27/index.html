Chapter 27

Symptoms of Ecstatic Love

Follows Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 2.2.1–2.2.21 · 21 sūtras shown in place, with Jīva Gosvāmī's and Viśvanātha Cakravartī's commentaries

The bodily symptoms manifested by a devotee in expressing ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa are called anubhāva. Practical examples of anubhāva are as follows: dancing, rolling on the ground, singing very loudly, stretching the body, crying loudly, yawning, breathing very heavily, neglecting the presence of others, drooling, laughing like a madman, wheeling the head and belching. When there is an extraordinary excess of ecstatic love, with all of these bodily symptoms manifested, one feels relieved transcendentally.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.1 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
anubhāvās tu citta-stha-bhāvānām avabodhakāḥ | te bahir vikriyā prāyāḥ proktā udbhāsvarākhyayā ||1||
Anubhāvas refers to those things which express the bhāva situated within the heart. They are predominantly external transformations, and are thus called udbhāsvara (shining in the body).
Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.2 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
nṛtyaṁ viluṭhitaṁ gītaṁ krośanaṁ tanu-moṭanam | huṅkāro jṛmbhaṇaṁ śvāsa-bhūmā lokānapekṣitā | lālā-sravo ’ṭṭahāsaś ca ghūrṇā-hikkādayo ’pi ca ||2||
External transformations known as anubhāvas are actions, such as dancing, rolling on the ground, singing, shouting, stretching the body, bellowing, yawning, breathing heavily, disregarding others, drooling, laughing loudly, whirling around, and hiccups.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Verse 2.1.12 has already described anubhāvas with the phrase smitādi: smiling and other items. Thus smiling should be understood to be included in the list in this verse, which is elaborating on the word ādi of that previous verse.

These symptoms are divided into two parts: one is called śīta, and the other is called kṣepaṇa. When there is yawning, the symptoms are called śīta, and when there is dancing they are called kṣepaṇa.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.3 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
te śītāḥ kṣepaṇāś ceti yathārthākhyā dvidhoditāḥ | śītāḥ syur gīta-jṛmbhādyā nṛtyādyāḥ kṣepaṇābhidhāḥ ||3||
Anubhāvas are of two types with the suitable names of śīta (meaning “cool,” with lack of bodily movement) and kṣepaṇa (meaning “throwing about,” involving distinct bodily movements). Śīta includes singing, yawning, breathing heavily, disregarding others, drooling and smiling. Kṣepaṇa includes dancing, rolling on the ground, shouting, stretching the body, bellowing, laughing loudly, [Note: The definition of aṭṭa-hāsa (laughing loudly) is given in the commentary on verse 18. It involves bodily movement as well as laughing. It would seem that shouting, bellowing and hiccups would also involve more bodily movement than usual as kṣepaṇa anubhāvas.] whirling around and having hiccups.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
The phrase gīta-jṛmbhādyā means singing, yawning and the items immediately following, namely breathing heavily, disregard, and drooling. Smiling from verse 2.1.12 is also included in the śīta anubhāvas.

DANCING

While watching the rāsa dance performed by Lord Kṛṣṇa and the gopīs, Lord Śiva beheld the beautiful face of Kṛṣṇa and immediately began to dance and beat upon his small diṇḍima drum. While Lord Śiva was dancing in ecstasy, his eldest son, Gaṇeśa, joined him.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.4 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
tatra nṛtyaṁ, yathā – muralī-khuralī-sudhā-kiraṁ hari-vaktrendum avekṣya kampitaḥ | gaṇane sagaṇeśa-ḍiṇḍima- dhvanibhis tāṇḍavam āśrito haraḥ ||4||
Dancing: Seeing the moon-like face of Hari, radiating sweetness as He practiced His flute playing, Lord Śiva began to tremble and then began to dance in the sky along with Ganeśa to the rhythm of his drum.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
The word muralī means the sound of the flute. Khuralī means practice. Trikhāṇḍa-śeṣa (Amara-kośa) says abhyāsaḥ khuralī yogya: khuralī means abhyāsaḥ or practice.

ROLLING ON THE GROUND

In the Third Canto, first chapter, verse 32 of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Vidura inquires from Uddhava, “My dear friend, is Akrūra in an auspicious condition? Not only is he a learned scholar and sinless, but he is also a devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa. He has such ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa that I have seen him rolling upon Kṛṣṇa’s footprints in the dust as if bereft of all sense.” Similarly, one gopī gave a message to Kṛṣṇa that Rādhārāṇī, because of Her separation from Him and because of Her enchantment with the aroma of His flower garlands, was rolling on the ground, thereby bruising Her soft body.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.5 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
viluṭhitaṁ, yathā tṛtīye – kaccid budhaḥ svasty-anamīva āste śvaphalka-putro bhagavat-prapannaḥ | yaḥ kṛṣṇa-pādāṅkita-mārga-pāṁsuṣv aceṣṭata prema-vibhinna-dhairyaḥ ||5||
Falling on the ground, from the Third Canto: Please tell me whether Akrūra, the son of Śvaphalka, is doing well. He is a faultless soul surrendered unto the Personality of Godhead. He once lost his mental equilibrium due to his ecstasy of transcendental love and fell down on the dust of a road which was marked with the footprints of Lord Kṛṣṇa. SB 3.1.32
Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.6 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
yathā vā – navānurāgeṇa tavāvaśāṅgī vana-srag-āmodam avāpya mattā | vrajāṅgane sā kaṭhine luṭhantī gātraṁ sugātrī vraṇayāñcakāra ||6||
Another example: O Kṛṣṇa! Because of her new attraction for You, Rādhā is not in control of her limbs. On smelling the fragrance of Your forest garland, becoming intoxicated, she has fallen on the hard ground of Vraja and injured her beautiful body.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Vraṇayāṣ cakāra is equivalent to vraṇavac cakāra. This follows from the rule vinmator luk: words ending in vin, mat or vat form comparative and superlative degrees by adding iyas and iṣṭa and dropping the vin, mat or vat. (Pāṇini 5.3.65)

SINGING LOUDLY

One gopī informed Kṛṣṇa that when Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī was singing about His glories, She enchanted all of Her friends in such a way that they became stonelike and dull. At the same time, the nearby stones began to melt away in ecstatic love.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.7 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
gītaṁ, yathā – rāga-ḍambara-karambita-cetāḥ kurvatī tava navaṁ guṇa-gānam | gokulendra kurute jalatāṁ sā rādhikādya dṛṣadāṁ suhṛdāṁ ca ||7||
Singing: Moon of Gokula! Today, Rādhā, whose mind is possessed by the beauty of the śrīrāga (or the zeal of love), is singing a new song about you. Her friends have become like stones, and the stones have melted.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Rāga here means either the melodic rāgas such as śrī-rāga or simply anurāga, meaning love. Suhṛdām means her friends. Those friends become paralyzed (jaḍatām), and the stones (dṛṣadām) become liquid like water (jalatām). The letters ḍ and l can be used interchangeably. Thus one word can have two meanings.

When Nārada Muni was chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, he chanted so loudly that it was apprehended that Lord Nṛsiṁha had appeared. Thus all the demons began to flee in different directions.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.8 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
krośanaṁ, yathā – hari-kīrtana-jāta-vikriyaḥ sa vicukrośa tathādya nāradaḥ | acirān nara-siṁha-śaṅkayā danujā yena dhṛtā vililyire ||8||
Shouting: Because of the transformations brought about by the chanting of the name of Hari, Nārada began shouting in such a manner that the demons immediately hid themselves, trembling in fear that Narasiṁha had made His appearance.

STRETCHING THE BODY

It is said that sometimes when Nārada, the carrier of the vīṇā, remembers his Lord Kṛṣṇa in great ecstasy, he begins to stretch his body so vigorously that his sacred thread gives way.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.10 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
tanu-mocanaṁ, yathā – kṛṣṇa-nāmani mudopavīṇite prīṇite manasi vaiṇiko muniḥ | udbhaṭaṁ kim api moṭayan vapus troṭayaty akhila-yajña-sūtrakam ||10||
Stretching the body: Nārada, blissfully singing the names of Kṛṣṇa on his vīnā with a happy mind, stretched his body in a most astonishing way and completely broke his sacred thread.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
While singing with bliss (mudā) accompanied by the vīṇā (upavīṇite), Nārada stretched his body violently (udbhaṭam) in an indescribably way (kim api), so that his sacred thread broke completely.

CRYING LOUDLY

A gopī once said to Kṛṣṇa, “My dear son of Nanda Mahārāja, by the sound of Your flute Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī has become full of lamentation and fear, and thus, with a faltering voice, She is crying like a kurarī bird.”

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.9 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
yathā vā – urarīkṛta-kākur ākulā kurarīva vraja-rāja-nanda | muralī-taralī-kṛtāntarā muhur ākrośad ihādya sundarī ||9||
Another example: O son of Vraja’s king! Today in Vṛndāvana the beautiful Rādhā, her heart melted by sound of Your flute, was constantly shouting in agitation like a female osprey with a changed voice.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Taralī-kṛtāntarā is formed from tarala and the verb kṛ. This particular ending called cvi indicates that the subject becomes transformed. In this case the heart turns liquid by the sound of the flute.

It is described that by hearing the vibration of Kṛṣṇa’s flute, Lord Śiva becomes very puzzled and begins to cry so loudly into space that the demons become vanquished and the devotees become overwhelmed with joy.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.11 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
huṅkāro, yathā – vaiṇava-dhvanibhir udbhramad-dhiyaḥ śaṅkarasya divi huṅkṛti-svanaḥ | dhvaṁsayann api muhuḥ sa-dānavaṁ sādhu-vṛndam akarot sadā navam ||11||
Bellowing: Hearing the sound of the vīṇā, Śiva, mind excited, bellowed in such a way that it destroyed the demons and gave the highest bliss to the devotees at every moment.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
He roared in a suitable manner continuously, so that it made the devotees at every moment experience the highest bliss (sadā navam akarot) and it destroyed the associates of the demons (sa-dānavam). Virodhālaṅkāra is used. Sādhu-vṛṇdam akarot sadānavam could also mean “The sound made the devotees become demons.” However, that is a contradiction. By dividing the syllables differently into sadā navam the real meaning appears.

YAWNING

It is said that when the full moon rises, the lotus petals become expanded. Similarly, when Kṛṣṇa used to appear before Rādhārāṇī, Her face, which is compared to the lotus flower, would expand by Her yawning.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.12 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
jṛmbhaṇam, yathā – vistṛta-kumuda-vane ’sminn udayati pūrṇe kalānidhau purataḥ | tava padmini mukha-padmaṁ bhajate jṛmbhām aho citram ||12||
Yawning: O lotus! [Note: The kumuda blooms at night and the lotus blooms during the day.] How astonishing that in the grove of blossoming water lilies, you have blossomed at the rising of the full moon on the eastern horizon.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
O Rādhā! When the ocean of all arts, Kṛṣṇa, who has appeared to give full bliss to the earth, arrives before you, it is astonishing that your lotus face shows lethargy by yawning. Vistṛta-kumuda-vana (broad lotus forest) can be analyzed as vistṛta-ku-mud-avanam (extensive preservation of the bliss of the earth). Thus the sentence can mean “When Kṛṣṇa, who preserves the bliss of the earth, appears in front of you, it is surprising that you yawn in lethargy.”

BREATHING HEAVILY

As far as breathing heavily is concerned, it is stated, “Lalitā [one of the gopīs] is just like a cātakī bird, which only takes water falling directly from the rain cloud and not from any other source.” In this statement Kṛṣṇa is compared to the dark cloud, and Lalitā is compared to the cātakī bird seeking only Kṛṣṇa’s company. The metaphor continues to say, “As a heavy wind sometimes disperses a mighty cloud, so the heavy breath from Lalitā’s nostrils caused her to miss Kṛṣṇa, who had disappeared by the time she recovered herself.”

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.13 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
śvāsa-bhūmā, yathā – upasthite citra-paṭāmbudāgame vivṛddha-tṛṣṇā lalitākhya-cātakī | niḥśvāsa-jhañjhā-marutāpavāhitaṁ kṛṣṇāmbudākāram avekṣya cukṣubhe ||13||
Heavy breathing: When the monsoon arrived with colorful clothing, the Cātakī bird named Lalitā became increasingly thirsty. Seeing the form of that black cloud called Kṛṣṇa driven away by the strong breathing of the monsoon wind mixed with rain, she became disturbed.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Ambudāgame refers to the monsoon rains. Trikāṇḍa-śesa says: vātulo vātagulmah syāt caravāyur nidāghajaḥ, jañjānilaḥ prāvṛṣijo vāsanto malayānilaḥ: in the hot season there will be strong winds and gales, in the monsoon season there will be rain mixed with wind, and during the spring there will be a fragrant breeze. Because of the strength of her breathing and because of the mixture with tears (wind and rain), Kṛṣṇa disappeared from her sight, because of the disturbance to His cloth.

NEGLECTING THE PRESENCE OF OTHERS

As far as neglecting the presence of others is concerned, the wives of the brāhmaṇas who were performing sacrifices at Vṛndāvana left home as soon as they heard that Kṛṣṇa was nearby. They left their homes without caring for their learned husbands. The husbands began to discuss this among themselves: “How wonderful is the attraction for Kṛṣṇa that it has made these women leave us without any care!” This is the influence of Kṛṣṇa. Anyone who becomes attracted to Kṛṣṇa can be relieved from the bondage of birth and death, which can be compared to the locked-up homes that were neglected by the wives of the brāhmaṇas.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.14 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
lokānapekṣitā, yathā śrī-daśame – aho paśyata nārīṇām api kṛṣṇe jagad-gurau | duranta-bhāvaṁ yo ’vidhyan mṛtyupāśān gṛhābhidhān ||14||
Disregard for all others, from the Tenth Canto: Just see the unlimited love these women have developed for Lord Kṛṣṇa, the spiritual master of the entire universe! This love has broken their very bonds of death—their attachment to family life. SB 10.23.41
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
This is a statement by the brāhmaṇas performing sacrifice.

In the Padyāvalī there is a statement by some devotees: “We shall not care for any outsiders. If they should deride us, we shall still not care for them. We shall simply enjoy the transcendental mellow of chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, and thus we shall roll on the ground and dance ecstatically. In this way we shall eternally enjoy transcendental bliss.”

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.15 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
yathā vā padyāvalyām (73) – parivadatu jano yathā tathā vā nanu mukharo na vayaṁ vicārayāmaḥ | hari-rasa-madirā madātimattā bhuvi viluṭhāma naṭāma nirviśāma ||15||
Another example, from Padyāvalī: Let the big mouths gossip! We will not consider it at all! By tasting the sweet liquor of Hari, we have become joyful and intoxicated. Let us fall on the ground, dance and enjoy.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Nirviśāma means “let us enjoy.” An alternative reading which is also suitable is paryaṭāma: let us wander about. Putting all three verbs in the imperative first person is most suitable. The adjective atimattā also agrees with the subject vayam (we).

DROOLING

As an example of the running down of saliva from the mouth, it is stated that sometimes when Nārada Muni was chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, he remained stunned for a while, and saliva oozed from his mouth.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.16 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
lālā-sravo, yathā – śaṅke prema-bhujaṅgena daṣṭaḥ kaṣṭaṁ gato muniḥ | niścalasya yad etasya lālā sravati vaktrataḥ ||16||
Drooling: I think that Nārada, bitten by the scorpion of prema, is suffering in pain, because he has fallen into a motionless state, and saliva is dripping from his mouth.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Because he is Nārada, his state must be caused by prema, but lying motionless and drooling are the symptoms of scorpion bite.

LAUGHING LIKE A MADMAN

When a devotee laughs very loudly like a madman, it is done out of an extraordinary agitation of ecstatic love within the heart. Such mad laughing is an expression of the condition of the heart that is technically called aṭṭa-hāsa. When a devotee becomes affected with this mental condition, his love is expressed through the lips. The laughing sounds, coming one after another, are compared to flowers falling from the creeper of devotion that grows within the heart of the devotee. In the Caitanya-caritāmṛta devotional service to the Lord is also compared to a creeper that rises up to the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa in Goloka Vṛndāvana.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.17 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
aṭṭahāsaḥ –hāsād bhinno ’ṭṭahāso ’yaṁ citta-vikṣepa-sambhavaḥ ||17||
Laughing loudly: That laughing which arises from disturbance of the heart is called laughing loudly or aṭṭahāsa. This is distinguished from the normal laugh.
Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.18 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
yathā – śaṅke ciraṁ keśava-kiṅkarasya cetas taṭe bhakti-latā praphullā | yenādhi-tuṇḍa-sthalam aṭṭahāsa- prasūna-puñjāś caṭulaṁ skhalanti ||18||
An example: I think that the creeper of devotion in the heart of the servant of Kṛṣṇa has been blossoming for a long time, because a multitude of flowers in the form of his loud laughing is shaking his trembling lips.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
The characteristics of aṭṭa-hāsa are as follows. Utphulla-nāsikā-randhram āloḍita-mukhekṣaṇam, uddhataṁ vikṛtākāraṁ nāṭye ’ṭṭa-hasitaṁ viduḥ: aṭṭa-hāsya consists of rude, loud laughing with flared nostrils and agitated glances while dancing. Though the loud laugh presents itself as ferocious to an opposing party when accompanied by abusive language, it presents itself as pleasing to one’s own party. Thus, it can be depicted as somewhat soft. In this verse, someone of the same party describes the devotee’s loud laughing, which indicates scorn for those who criticize bhakti.

WHEELING OF THE HEAD

One gopī told her friend, “It appears that Lord Kṛṣṇa, the enemy of the demon Agha, has released from His mouth a whirlwind which is acting on your head and is gradually proceeding to do the same to the other lotus-eyed gopīs.”

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.19 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
ghūrṇā, yathā – dhruvam agharipur ādadhāti vātyāṁ nanu murali tvayi phutkṛti-cchalena | kim ayam itarathā dhvanir vighūrṇanaṁ sakhi tava ghūrṇayati vrajāmbujākṣīḥ ||19||
Whirling around: O my friend, dear flute! Kṛṣṇa, on the pretext of blowing you, is certainly producing a gale. Otherwise, how does your sound alone cause the beautiful women of Vraja to whirl around?

BELCHING

Sometimes belching also becomes a symptom of ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa. There is evidence of this in Paurṇamāsī’s address to one crying associate of Rādhārāṇī: “My dear daughter, don’t be worried because Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī is belching. I am about to offer a remedial measure for this symptom. Do not cry so loudly. This belching is not due to indigestion; it is a sign of ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa. I shall arrange to cure this belching symptom immediately. Don’t be worried.” This statement by Paurṇamāsī is evidence that ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa is sometimes manifested through belching.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.20 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
hikkā, yathā – na putri racayauṣadhaṁ visṛja rodam atyuddhataṁ mudhā priya-sakhīṁ prati tvam aśivaṁ kim āśaṅkase | hari-praṇaya-vikriyākulatayā bruvāṇā muhur varākṣi harir ity asau vitanute ’dya hikkā-bharam ||20||
Hiccups: O daughter! Are you worrying uselessly that something has happened to your dear friend Rādhā? Do not apply any medicine! Give up this loud weeping! O beautiful eyed girl! Today, Rādhā, afflicted by the transformations brought on by love for Kṛṣṇa, is afflicted with hiccups while chanting His name incessantly.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
This is a statement of Paurṇamāsī to Lalitā. In the Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi she is described as having a sympathetic mood of love. Thus, there is the suggestion that she will find a remedy for Rādhā’s hiccups. Rodam atyuddhatam is the acceptable version.

Sometimes trembling of the whole body and hemorrhaging from some part of the body are also manifested in response to ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa, but such symptoms are very rare, and therefore Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī does not discuss any further on this point.

Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu · BRS 2.2.21 — Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the sūtra behind this passage
vapur utphullatāraktodgamādyāḥ syuḥ pare ’pi ye | atīva-viralatvāt te naivātra parikīrtitāḥ ||21||
Other symptoms such as extreme goose bumps and sweating blood are not described here since they are very rare.
Jīva Gosvāmī · Durgama-saṅgamanī
Vapur utphullatā refers to extreme goose bumps on the body. Raktodgama refers to blood oozing with the perspiration.
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura · Bhakti-sāra-pradarśinī
