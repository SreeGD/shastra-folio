Asteya (Non-stealing / Honesty)

अस्तेयasteya[ah-STAY-ya]
Dharma (Right Conduct)Root Value (depth 0) non-stealinghonestyintegritynon-appropriation

Asteya is the divine quality of complete honesty and integrity, encompassing not only refraining from taking what belongs to others but also avoiding the misuse of time, resources, or trust. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, asteya reflects the soul's natural inclination toward dharmic conduct when consciousness is purified of material desires. This virtue manifests as contentment with what Krishna provides and the understanding that all belongs ultimately to the Supreme Lord.

Definition — Layers
Physical non-stealing - not taking material objects that belong to others
Subtle non-stealing - not misusing time, attention, or emotional energy
Spiritual non-stealing - recognizing Krishna as the ultimate proprietor of all
Devotional non-stealing - offering everything to Krishna rather than claiming ownership

Anti-patterns — What this value is NOT
Tamasic Appropriation
Source: Bhagavad Gita modes of nature
Taking what belongs to others through ignorance, laziness, or entitlement
Key distinguisher: Lacks awareness of wrongdoing, driven by inertia and delusion
Rajasic Acquisition
Source: Bhagavad Gita modes of nature
Aggressive pursuit of others' possessions driven by passion and desire
Key distinguisher: Actively schemes and competes to obtain what others have
False Proprietorship
Source: Gaudiya Vaishnava philosophy
Claiming ownership over what ultimately belongs to Krishna
Key distinguisher: Spiritual blindness to the true proprietor of all existence
Time Theft
Source: Contemporary application
Wasting or misusing time that could be used for service or growth
Key distinguisher: Subtle stealing from one's own spiritual development
