Dana (Charity / Generosity)

दानdāna[DAH-na]
Dharma (Right Conduct)Core Value (depth 1) charitygivingdonationgenerosity

Dana is the sacred act of giving with a pure heart, free from attachment to results or recognition. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, true dana flows from understanding that all wealth belongs to Krishna and we are merely instruments of His compassion. It purifies both giver and receiver, creating spiritual merit that transcends material exchange.

Definition — Layers
Material giving without expectation
Spiritual offering of one's resources
Recognition of divine ownership
Instrument of Krishna's compassion
Purification through selfless service

Anti-patterns — What this value is NOT
Rajasika Dana
Source: BG 17.21
Giving with expectation of return, recognition, or reluctantly
Key distinguisher: Motivated by ego and desire for reciprocation rather than pure compassion
Tamasika Dana
Source: BG 17.22
Giving at wrong time, place, to unworthy recipients with contempt
Key distinguisher: Lacks discrimination and proper understanding of when and how to give
Miserly Hoarding
Source: Traditional teaching
Excessive attachment to wealth and refusal to share with those in need
Key distinguisher: Driven by fear and possessiveness rather than trust in divine providence
