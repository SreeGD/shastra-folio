Bhagavad-gītā 3.7

यस्त्विन्द्रियाणि मनसा नियम्यारभतेऽर्जुन । कर्मेन्द्रियैः कर्मयोगमसक्तः स विशिष्यते ॥ ७ ॥

On the other hand, if a sincere person tries to control the active senses by the mind and begins karma-yoga [in Kṛṣṇa consciousness] without attachment, he is by far superior.
Purport

Instead of becoming a pseudo transcendentalist for the sake of wanton living and sense enjoyment, it is far better to remain in one’s own business and execute the purpose of life, which is to get free from material bondage and enter into the kingdom of God. The prime svārtha-gati, or goal of self-interest, is to reach Viṣṇu. The whole institution of varṇa and āśrama is designed to help us reach this goal of life. A householder can also reach this destination by regulated service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. For self-realization, one can live a controlled life, as prescribed in the śāstras, and continue carrying out his business without attachment, and in that way make progress. A sincere person who follows this method is far better situated than the false pretender who adopts show-bottle spiritualism to cheat the innocent public. A sincere sweeper in the street is far better than the charlatan meditator who meditates only for the sake of making a living.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
7. He who, free from attachment, controlling the senses by the mind, begins karma-yoga with his active senses, is superior.
Commentary

In contrast to this, the householder (not the sannyāsī), who performs scriptural actions (karma-yogam) without longing for fruits of his actions (asaktaḥ) is superior. Rāmānujācārya says in this regard that he is superior to the person practicing jṣāna because of the impossibility of going astray by this method.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
7. He who, free from attachment, controlling the senses by the mind, performs karma yoga with his active senses, is superior to a person acting prematurely from the position of jṣāna .
Commentary

On the other hand, the householder who performs his prescribed activities is better. That person is superior who, devoid of desire for the results (aśaktaḥ), performs the method (yoga) in the form of actions (karma) by the action senses, with regulation of those senses by the mind (manasā) which has the intention of realizing ātmā. He is better than a person acting on the platform of jṣāna which is only to be achieved in the future.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Translation
Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work.
Commentary

Gradually we will fill all the stages on this yoga ladder. There is karma by which one is attached ‑ this is called "sakama" where in the beginning one's adhikary is that one has material attachments (this comes from the mode of passion). And then, when one is in the mode of passion, by working according to the authority of the scriptures then gradually one detachment and that practically means that he changes from passion to goodness,because detachment is the quality of goodness. Then one comes to the platform of jnana which is detachment or renunciation. But what we have heard befor that in this jnana platform one should still work ‑ this wh going to be explained in this Chapter. That work on the jnana platform is called Buddhi‑yoga or "Niskama‑karma". That is karma but on the jnana platform. This yoga ladder is very, very important because one has to know where to place himself and where to place others.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% niyatam kuru karma tvam karma jyayo hy akarmanah sarira‑yatrapi ca te na prasiddhyed akarmanah

niyatam‑‑prescribed; kuru‑‑do; karma‑‑duties; tvam‑‑you; karma‑‑work; jyayah‑‑better; hi‑‑certainly; akarmanah‑‑than no work; sarira‑‑bodily; yatra‑‑maintenance; api‑‑even; ca‑‑also; te‑‑your; na‑‑never; prasiddhyet‑‑is effected; akarmanah‑‑without work.

TRANSLATION

Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work.

"Karma for you whose adhikary is to work, is better than to renounce work, because you are not on the jnana platform to renounce work. And now to bring that niskama‑karma, or jnana and karma combined platform, to Krsna consciousness platform, (because one can perform one's prescribed duties in a detached way, but that is not Krsna consciousness, because Krsna consciousness means materially detached but spiritually attached to Krsna) explains that which what makes it Krsna consciousness.

Translation

This being so, therefore,
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
3.7 Conseently, he who, with aspiration to have the vision of the self, directs his senses to action according to the scriptures, such action being of the same class as those which he practised earlier, and who then begins to practise Karma Yoga, after renouncing attachment, with the senses which are naturally inclined to action - he, by reason of there being no chance of errors, excels a man following Jnana Yoga, because there is no fear of a fall in his case.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
3.7 Yas tu etc. When actions are being performed [by him], there is no loss of his knowledge. For, when the mind does not function, he does his work like a machine-man. Therefore -

3.7 But, O Arjuna, one who engages in Karma-yoga with the organs of action, controlling the organs with the mind and becoming unattached-that one excels.

3.7 But, O Arjuna! All honour to him whose mind controls his senses, for he is thereby beginning to practise Karma-Yoga, the Path of Right Action, keeping himself always unattached.

Personal Guidance
Sincere action — even imperfect — with the mind regulated is far superior to performed restraint with the mind wandering.
Practical Applications
  • Choose sincere imperfect action over polished hypocrisy.
  • Engage your senses in service rather than fight to silence them.
  • Use your work itself as the field for taming the mind.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I being too pristine to be genuinely useful?
  • What ordinary work could become sādhana if I brought my mind fully into it?
Analogy

A gardener with calloused hands and a steady aim is more sincere than a perfumed visitor who fears to touch the soil. Real work, offered without attachment, is far closer to bhakti than ornamental purity.

Stories
Arjuna the Gṛhastha-warrior

Arjuna is not a sage in robes; he is a kṣatriya with a bow. Kṛṣṇa, having just named the hypocrite in verse 6, immediately names Arjuna's own honest path in verse 7 — engage the senses in karma-yoga, without attachment, and be far superior.

The whole Gītā is delivered to a man holding a weapon, not to one sitting under a tree. Verse 7 is the verse that lets the householder, the soldier, the manager, the farmer hear the teaching as addressed to *them* — sincere active engagement, mind regulated, attachment dropped — and not feel that scripture is only for the orange-clothed.

Important Words
  • niyamya — regulating
    From ni-√yam, to bind down — same root as niyama and yama. The senses are not killed; they are *bound to their proper post*. The verse is about discipline, not destruction.
  • karma - indriyaiḥ — by the active sense organs
    Instrumental. The same action-senses condemned as hypocritical in verse 6 are here praised — what changed is not the senses but who is regulating them. Same instrument; different conductor.
  • karma - yogam — devotion (action-yoga)
    The crucial term of the chapter. Action is not abandoned; it is yoked. Prabhupāda's gloss 'devotion' is not a softening — karma-yoga, fully understood, *is* devotional service in action.
  • asaktaḥ — without attachment
    From a + √sañj, to cling. The same root as saṅga in 2.47. The whole karma-yoga teaching turns on this one switch: act, but without clinging.
  • viśiṣyate — is by far the better
    From vi-√śiṣ, to remain after distinguishing — i.e. to stand out. The verb is comparative-superlative; the sincere active person *outshines* the hypocrite-sitter, not merely equals him.
