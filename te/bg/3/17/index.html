Bhagavad-gītā 3.17

యస్త్వాత్మరతిరేవ స్యాదాత్మతృప్త‍శ్చ మానవః । ఆత్మన్యేవ చ సన్తుష్టస్తస్య కార్యం న విద్యతే ॥ ౧౭ ॥

Important Words
  • ātma - ratiḥ — taking pleasure in the Self
    Compound: ātman + rati (pleasure, delight). The very faculty that ordinarily flows outward to objects now rests in the self. Rati is also the technical word for love in bhakti; here it has turned inward.
  • ātma - tṛptaḥ — self-illuminated (self-satisfied)
    Compound. From √tṛp, to be satiated. Satiated by oneself, not by any external supply. The verse defines a person whose hunger has met its real food.
  • santuṣṭaḥ — perfectly satiated
    Past participle of sam-√tuṣ, completely pleased. The triple emphasis (ātma-rati, ātma-tṛpta, santuṣṭa) is deliberate — Kṛṣṇa is closing every door through which more 'need' could enter.
  • kāryam — duty
    Gerundive of √kṛ — *what is to be done*. For the realized one, no kāryam remains. But Prabhupāda is firm: this category is rare, and the next verses keep us honest about who actually inhabits it.
But for one who takes pleasure in the Self, whose human life is one of self-realization, and who is satisfied in the Self only, fully satiated – for him there is no duty.
Purport

A person who is fully Kṛṣṇa conscious, and is fully satisfied by his acts in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, no longer has any duty to perform. Due to his being Kṛṣṇa conscious, all impiety within is instantly cleansed, an effect of many, many thousands of yajña performances. By such clearing of consciousness, one becomes fully confident of his eternal position in relationship with the Supreme. His duty thus becomes self-illuminated by the grace of the Lord, and therefore he no longer has any obligations to the Vedic injunctions. Such a Kṛṣṇa conscious person is no longer interested in material activities and no longer takes pleasure in material arrangements like wine, women and similar infatuations.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
17. The human who is attracted to the ātmā, satisfied in the ātmā, and completely satisfied with ātmā alone, has nothing to do in regard to fulfilling desires.
Commentary

It has now been stated that if you cannot be without desires, you can perform actions with desires. But he who reaches the stage of jṣāna because of purity of heart never performs actions out of desire. That is expressed in two verses. Because he is enjoying in the self, is blissful by realization of the bliss of the ātmā, he does not engage at all in the pleasure of material objects. Being engaged only in the ātmā (ātmāny eva), he does not perform any actions related to the external objects (tasya kāryam na vidyate).

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
17. The human who is attracted to the ātmā, satisfied in the ātmā, completely pleased with ātmā alone, has nothing to do in regard to seeing the ātmā.
Commentary

That person who has seen the ātmā by ātmā’s inherent knowledge, [Note: Baladeva uses the phrase dharma bhūta jṣāna. Rāmanuja would use the phrase svarūpa bhūta jṣana or dharma bhūta jṣāna for self awareness, and dharma bhūta jṣāna for awareness of other things.] which arises from cleansing the mirror of the heart by niṣkāma karma and by worship of Me, as recommended previously by Me, is not obliged to do any actions. This is explained in two verses. He who has a taste (ātma rati) for ātmā, who sees his very self, endowed with eight qualities such as sinlessness; who is satisfied (ātma tṛpta) by the ātmā with its self-manifesting bliss, rather than by food and drink; who is completely pleased only in that ātmā with those qualities— not in singing and dancing—need not do any prescribed activity as duty in order to perceive the ātmā, since he is always seeing the ātmā as his very nature.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

So question is: why should one act and follow prescribed duties, which are basically karma‑kanda, if he has no desire to gain anything material? The answer is: to act dutifully (even though he has no reason to perform this duties for his selfish interest).

The idea it that, for a man who is such a knower of the Self, there is no duty to undertake.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
3.17 But for him, who is not in need of the means of Jnana Yoga and Karma Yoga, who finds delight in the self on his own, i.e., who is established in the self, who is satisfied by the self alone and not by food, drink and other things which are other than the self, who rejoices in the self alone and not in pleasure gardens, garlands, sandalpaste, vocal and instrumental music etc., and for whom everything, his subsistence, nourishment and enjoyment, is the self alone - for him nothing remains to be performed for the vision of the self, because the essential nature of the self is perpetually in his unaided vision.

Personal Guidance
The realized soul who is satisfied in the self alone has no duty to perform — duty arises only when one is not yet realized.
Practical Applications
  • Don't claim the privileges of the realized while still in the position of the seeker.
  • Treat your prescribed duties as the very means of arriving at self-satisfaction.
  • Do not romanticize 'beyond all duties' as a current state.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I claiming a freedom that I have not yet earned?
  • Which duty is my actual training-ground right now?
Analogy

A ray of the sun, when traced fully home, does not annihilate itself in the disc — it rests in its source while remaining a ray. The ātma-tṛpta soul is that ray: no longer chasing external warmth, satisfied at the feet of Kṛṣṇa as Paramātmā within. Even the ātmārāma, says Sūta in SB 1.7.10, is drawn to render ahaitukī-bhakti to Hari — the self that has come home does not vanish; it serves.

Stories
Sage Śuka's Bodiless Roaming

Śukadeva Gosvāmī, the son of Vyāsa, attained self-realization in the womb and emerged already free. He had no āśrama, no duty, no fixed work — he wandered naked, sometimes following his weeping father only out of compassion. He spoke the Bhāgavatam to Parīkṣit not because he was obliged to but because the līlā arranged it.

Verse 3.17 names Śuka's exact category — one who is ātma-tṛpta, self-satisfied, for whom no prescribed kāryam remains. The next verses will warn the rest of us not to *claim* this category prematurely.
