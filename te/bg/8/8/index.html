Bhagavad-gītā 8.8

Important Words
  • abhyāsa - yoga — in the practice of meditation
    Abhyāsa (repeated practice) + yoga (yoking). The technical compound for spiritual training as deliberate repetition. The Gītā treats abhyāsa as one of the two main methods (along with vairāgya, 6.35) for steadying the restless mind.
  • cetasā — by the mind and intelligence
    Instrumental — 'with the consciousness'. Cetas is the unified faculty of awareness, broader than manas alone. The verse asks the entire awareness to be yoked, not just one psychological function.
  • na anya - gāminā — without their being deviated
    Na (not) + anya (other) + gāmin (going). 'Not going elsewhere.' A precise negation: the test of abhyāsa-yoga is that the mind has stopped wandering off to other objects. Not 'tries not to' — actually does not.
  • paramam — the Supreme
    Para in superlative — 'highest'. Often paired with puruṣa to name the Supreme Person specifically. The combination paramam puruṣam in this verse is the explicit naming of bhakti's object: not Brahman impersonally but the Highest Person.
  • puruṣam — Personality of Godhead
    Personhood asserted as the Supreme's essential category. Against any reading that the goal of yoga is impersonal merging, the Gītā here keeps the puruṣa-word: He is a Person, and what is attained is a Person.
  • divyam — transcendental
    From div — to shine, to play, the same root as deva. Divya means 'of the divine play', luminous, beyond the dull material register. The Person attained is not a magnified human but transcendentally other.
  • anucintayan — constantly thinking of
    Anu (along, after) + cint (to think) + present participle. Continuous thinking-after. Different from anusmara in 8.7 — cintana is meditative pondering, smaraṇa is recollective holding-in-mind. The chapter uses both, marking complementary aspects of the same practice.
He who meditates on Me as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, his mind constantly engaged in remembering Me, undeviated from the path, he, O Pārtha, is sure to reach Me.
Purport

In this verse Lord Kṛṣṇa stresses the importance of remembering Him. One’s memory of Kṛṣṇa is revived by chanting the mahā-mantra, Hare Kṛṣṇa. By this practice of chanting and hearing the sound vibration of the Supreme Lord, one’s ear, tongue and mind are engaged. This mystic meditation is very easy to practice, and it helps one attain the Supreme Lord. Puruṣam means enjoyer. Although living entities belong to the marginal energy of the Supreme Lord, they are in material contamination. They think themselves enjoyers, but they are not the supreme enjoyer. Here it is clearly stated that the supreme enjoyer is the Supreme Personality of Godhead in His different manifestations and plenary expansions as Nārāyaṇa, Vāsudeva, etc. The devotee can constantly think of the object of worship, the Supreme Lord, in any of His features – Nārāyaṇa, Kṛṣṇa, Rāma, etc. – by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa. This practice will purify him, and at the end of his life, due to his constant chanting, he will be transferred to the kingdom of God. Yoga practice is meditation on the Supersoul within; similarly, by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa one fixes his mind always on the Supreme Lord. The mind is fickle, and therefore it is necessary to engage the mind by force to think of Kṛṣṇa. One example often given is that of the caterpillar that thinks of becoming a butterfly and so is transformed into a butterfly in the same life. Similarly, if we constantly think of Kṛṣṇa, it is certain that at the end of our lives we shall have the same bodily constitution as Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
8. Continuously contemplating Me with mind engaged in the practice of repeated remembrance, without deviation, one attains the transcendental supreme person.
Commentary

Therefore, that person who practices remembrance of Me during his life will naturally remember Me at the time of death, and by that, he will attain Me. Therefore, remembering Me by the mind is the highest yoga. That is expressed in this verse. Abhyāsa means to repeatedly remember Me. The mind should be engaged in this method (yoga) of repetition of remembering Me. It should not wander to other objects of remembrance. By this practice one will conquer of the nature of the mind.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
8. Continuously contemplating Me, with mind engaged in the practice of repeated remembrance without deviation, one attains the transcendental supreme person.
Commentary

In this verse the Lord emphasizes that constant remembrance leads to remembrance at death. With consciousness unswerving, fixed on one point (cetasā nānya gāminā), engaged in the function (yoga) of remembrance by repetition (abhyāsa), contemplating the supreme person, Vāsudeva, Nārāyaṇa, endowed with great opulence (divyam), a person, becoming like the Lord, [Note: This indicates he can attain sārūpya, or other types of superior liberation, but not necessarily the various rasas like dāsya and sakhya.] just as an insect remembering a bee becomes a bee, attains Him alone. [Note: kīṭaḥ peśaskṛtā ruddhaḥ kuḍyāyāṁ tam anusmaran saṁrambha-bhaya-yogena vindate tat-svarūpatām A grassworm confined in a hole of a wall by a bee always thinks of the bee in fear and enmity and later becomes a bee simply because of such remembrance. Further stresses the importance of remembering Him: Assurance that such a person who meditates

on the Supreme Personality of Godhead (cf. Text 8.9 also discusses the aspect of meditation); and who

Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
8.8 Contemplating on Me, the Supreme Divine Person, in the way to be specified further, at the last moment, with a mind trained by constant practice and Yoga, and not moving towards anything else, one reaches Me alone, i.e., attains a form similar to that of Mine, by virtue of the attributes of enjoyment and the prosperity contemplated upon, like the royal sage Bharata who acired the form of a deer on account of contemplating on it at the last moment. Abhyasa is the training of the mind to be often in touch with the object of meditation at all times without obstruction to the performance of the prescribed periodical and occasional rituals. Yoga is the meditation practised day by day at the time of Yoga practice in the manner prescribed.

Personal Guidance
By practicing constant remembrance with mind never deviating — engaged in mantra meditation — one attains the Supreme Personality without fail.
Practical Applications
  • Take up regular japa as the central daily practice.
  • Don't deviate the mind into other targets during chant.
  • Trust the 'without fail' guarantee.
Reflection Questions
  • What is my actual mantra-meditation discipline?
  • Where does the mind most often deviate?
Analogy

Constant remembrance is like a river worn into rock: at first the water finds many crooked channels and loses itself, but after long flowing the same path is cut so deep that the water has nowhere else to go. The mind grooved by abhyāsa-yoga falls toward Kṛṣṇa the way the river finally falls toward the sea.

Stories
Haridāsa Ṭhākura's three lakhs

Haridāsa Ṭhākura, the nāmācārya of the Gauḍīya line, vowed to chant three lakhs of Kṛṣṇa's names daily and kept the vow for the rest of his life. No persecution, no illness, no temptation broke the count. Even on his last day, weakened beyond rising, he finished his rounds before laying his head at Mahāprabhu's feet.

Kṛṣṇa's promise in 8.8 — that one whose mind is undeviated by the practice of yoga attains the Supreme — is what Haridāsa's life concretely demonstrates. Abhyāsa-yoga is not a mood; it is a count, day after day, until the count and the heart fuse.

Dhruva's tapasya

The boy Dhruva, stung by a stepmother's words, went into the forest and was given the seed mantra by Nārada. He sat fixed in meditation, the Bhāgavatam records, without food or motion, his mind locked on the form of Viṣṇu Nārada had described.

The Lord appeared, and Dhruva, given the gift of fixed remembrance, was granted the polar star itself — a place of eternal, undeviating position. Anucintayan — 'constantly thinking of' — is what Dhruva did in his tapasya, and what Kṛṣṇa here asks of every serious practitioner.
