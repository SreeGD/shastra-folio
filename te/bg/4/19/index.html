Bhagavad-gītā 4.19

Important Words
  • samārambhāḥ — endeavors, undertakings
    Sam + ā + √rabh — 'beginnings undertaken together.' Plural — every endeavor, the whole portfolio of initiatives. The test is not the avoidance of action but the cleanness of every initiation.
  • kāma — desire
    From √kam — 'to desire.' Specifically sense-desire here. The first thread that must be absent. Kāma is the seed of every grasping action.
  • saṅkalpa — selfish intention / mental determination
    Sam + √klṛp — 'the mental fixing of a plan.' Saṅkalpa is the inner formulation: 'I will do X so that Y happens for me.' Even without kāma's heat, saṅkalpa's quiet plan can carry self-purpose. The verse demands both be absent.
  • varjitāḥ — devoid of, free from
    Past passive participle of √vṛj — 'avoided, excluded.' Strong word: not merely 'reduced' but excluded. The paṇḍita's endeavors do not contain even traces of kāma-saṅkalpa.
  • dagdha — burned
    Past passive participle of √dah — 'burned, incinerated.' Paired with jñāna-agni (the fire of knowledge), the verse names karma as that which has been *burned through*. Knowledge is grammatically the fire; the residue of action is incinerated at the moment of doing.
  • paṇḍitam — a learned man
    Accusative. Not just 'knowledgeable' — the Sanskrit paṇḍita signifies one whose knowledge is integrated into life. The budhāḥ (wise ones) call such a person paṇḍita. Recognition by the wise is the criterion.
One is understood to be in full knowledge whose every endeavor is devoid of desire for sense gratification. He is said by sages to be a worker for whom the reactions of work have been burned up by the fire of perfect knowledge.
Purport

Only a person in full knowledge can understand the activities of a person in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Because the person in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is devoid of all kinds of sense-gratificatory propensities, it is to be understood that he has burned up the reactions of his work by perfect knowledge of his constitutional position as the eternal servitor of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. He is actually learned who has attained to such perfection of knowledge. Development of this knowledge of eternal servitorship to the Lord is compared to fire. Such a fire, once kindled, can burn up all kinds of reactions to work.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
19. The wise say that he is learned who, engaging in all activities, but being devoid of desire for enjoyment, has burned up all actions by the fire of knowledge.
Commentary

This concept is further explained in five verses. One who completely engages in activity (samārambhāḥ) devoid of desires to attain objects of enjoyment performs actions consumed by the fire of knowledge, whether the actions are prescribed (karma) or forbidden (vikarma). In this way the nature of vikarma should be understood. [Note: Vikarma is not directly discussed in the text, though it was mentioned in verse 17.] Just as the qualified person sees that karma does not lead to bondage, he also sees that vikarma or forbidden action do not accrue bondage. This verse is in agreement with the previous verse (seeing inaction in action). Later it will be said: api ced asi pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ pāpa-kṛt-tamaḥ sarvaṁ jṣāna-plavenaiva vṛjinaṁ santariṣyasi yathaidhāṁsi samiddho ’gnir bhasma-sāt kurute ’rjuna jṣānāgniḥ sarva-karmāṇi bhasma-sāt kurute tathā Even if you are considered to be the most sinful of all sinners, when you are situated in the boat of transcendental knowledge you will be able to cross over the ocean of miseries. As a blazing fire turns firewood to ashes, O Arjuna, so does the fire of knowledge burn to ashes all reactions to material activities. BG 4.36-37

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
19. The wise say that he is learned who engages in all activities, devoid of expectation of desired results, because he burns up all actions by the fire of his eventual realization of ātmā.
Commentary

In five verses the Lord speaks karma as a form of jṣāna. He whose performance of all actions (samārambhāh) is devoid of expectation of desired results (kāma saṅkalpa varijitāḥ), who aims at realizing ātmā through his actions, is called a learned man, one who knows ātmā (paṇḍitam), by the wise (budhāḥ). Why is he called so? When his heart becomes pure by execution of desireless duties, then all his accumulated karmas will have been burned up by the fire of ātmā jṣāna which has made its appearance.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

What makes a person akarma? When he has no desires for sense gratification. That is niskama karma, but what makes it bhakti is in addition to not desire sense gratification for himself,is his desire to please Krsna. Texts 20 to 24 describe this akarma work.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

A devotee in transcendental knowledge never desires sense enjoyment because He knows Kṛṣṇa as

his Lord and master and himself as Kṛṣṇa’s servant. He is thus freed from material desires, and his

actions (akarma) produce no reactions.

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
4.19 Budhah, the wise, the knowers of Brahman; ahuh, call; tam, him; panditam, learned, in the real sense; yasya, whose, of the one who perceives as stated above; samarambhah, actions-whatever are undertaken; are sarve, all; kama-sankalpa-varjitah, devoid of desires and the thoughts which are their (desires') causes (see 2.62)-i.e., (those actions) are performed as mere movements, without any selfish purpose: if they are performed by one (already) engaged in actions, then they are for preventing people from going astray, and if they are done by one who has withdrawn from actions, then they are merely for the maintenance of the body-; and jnanagni-dagdha-karmanam, whose actions have been burnt away by the fire of wisdom. Finding inaction etc. in action etc. is jnana, wisdom; that itself is agnih, fire. He whose actions, karma, described as good and bad, have been dagdhani, burnt away by that fire of wisdom, is jnana-agni-dagdha-karma. However, one who is a perceiver of 'inaction' etc. [Perceiver of inaction etc.: He who knows the truth about action and inaction as explained before.-Tr.] is free from actions owing to the very fact of his seeing 'inaction' etc. He is a monk, who acts merely for the purpose of maintaining the body. Being so, he does not engage in actions although he might have done so before the dawn of discrimination. He again who, having been engaged in actions under the influence of past tendencies, later on becomes endowed with the fullest Self-knowledge, he surely renounces (all) [Ast. adds this word sarva, all.-Tr.] actions along with their accessories as he does nnot find any purpose in activity. For some reason, if it becomes impossible to renounce actions and he, for the sake of preventing people from going astray, even remains engaged as before in actions-without attachment to those actions and their results because of the absence of any selfish purpose-, still he surely does nothing at all! His actions verily become 'inaction' because of having been burnt away by the fire of wisdom. By way of pointing out this idea, the Lord says:
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
4.19 In the case of an aspirant for release, all undertakings of actions in the form of obligatory, occasional and desiderative acts accomplished through the acisition of materials for their performance as also other works, are free from desire, i.e., are devoid of attachment to fruits. They are devoid of delusive identification. If the mind identifies the self with Prakrti and its Gunas, it is Sankalpa, i.e., 'delusive identification.' Genuine Karma Yoga is free from such identification. Such identification is overcome through contemplation on the real nature of the self as different from Prakrti. Those who know the truth call him a sage, who acts in this way and whose previous Karmas are thery burnt up by the fire of knowledge of the real nature of the self generated along with his actions. He is a true Karma Yogin. Thus that knowledge is involved in true Karma Yoga, is established. Sri Krsna elaborates this point again:

Personal Guidance
He whose every undertaking is free from desire and selfish purpose, his work burned by the fire of perfect knowledge — him the wise call learned.
Practical Applications
  • Aspire to actions whose backward-trace shows no selfish thread.
  • Examine your intentions before, during, and after — not just before.
  • Allow knowledge to burn the karmic residue of past selfish acts.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I still working from a hidden selfish thread?
  • What knowledge would burn it cleanly?
Analogy

A blacksmith's bellows-fire reduces iron-ore impurities to slag in minutes; the same metal would corrode for years without it. Knowledge is the bellows-fire of karma. Actions undertaken without selfish thread are burned clean before they cool; the verse calls this the paṇḍita's signature.

Stories
Bharata Mahārāja's restitution

When Emperor Bharata, after his fall as the merchant Jaḍa-Bharata, finally re-emerged as a self-realized brāhmaṇa, every action he performed was free of saṅkalpa for sense pleasure. He carried palanquins when ordered, walked through forests when set free, accepted abuse silently — every endeavor cleansed of personal motive.

Bhāgavatam holds him up as one whose work the budhāḥ call paṇḍita — not because he was clever but because his karma was already dagdha by jñāna at the point of doing.
