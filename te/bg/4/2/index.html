Bhagavad-gītā 4.2

ఏవం పరమ్పరాప్రాప్తమిమం రాజర్షయో విదుః । స కాలేనేహ మహతా యోగే నష్టః పరన్తప ॥ ౨ ॥

Important Words
  • paramparā — by disciplic succession
    Param-paraḥ — 'one after another,' a chain of links each connected to the previous. The instrumental case here means knowledge is *received through* the chain, not in spite of it. There is no private channel.
  • rāja - ṛṣayaḥ — the saintly kings
    A compound naming the original carriers — kings who were also seers. The Gītā's yoga was first held by ruling sages who applied it to governance, not by world-renunciates. This positions the entire Gītā as a king's manual.
  • kālena — in the course of time
    Instrumental case — time itself acts as the corrupting agent. Not human malice in the first instance; not invasion; just kāla. The chain dies of attrition unless someone keeps re-founding it.
  • naṣṭaḥ — scattered, lost
    Past passive participle of √naś, 'to perish.' The chain is not weakened; it is broken. Kṛṣṇa is signalling that the very act of speaking the Gītā now is itself a re-foundation.
  • param - tapa — O subduer of the enemies
    Arjuna's epithet — 'scorcher of foes.' Kṛṣṇa addresses him as a warrior at the precise moment of giving him a contemplative science. The reception of paramparā is itself a kind of warfare against the decay of kāla.
This supreme science was thus received through the chain of disciplic succession, and the saintly kings understood it in that way. But in course of time the succession was broken, and therefore the science as it is appears to be lost.
Purport

It is clearly stated that the Gītā was especially meant for the saintly kings because they were to execute its purpose in ruling over the citizens. Certainly Bhagavad-gītā was never meant for the demonic persons, who would dissipate its value for no one’s benefit and would devise all types of interpretations according to personal whims. As soon as the original purpose was scattered by the motives of the unscrupulous commentators, there arose the need to reestablish the disciplic succession. Five thousand years ago it was detected by the Lord Himself that the disciplic succession was broken, and therefore He declared that the purpose of the Gītā appeared to be lost. In the same way, at the present moment also there are so many editions of the Gītā (especially in English), but almost all of them are not according to authorized disciplic succession. There are innumerable interpretations rendered by different mundane scholars, but almost all of them do not accept the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, although they make a good business on the words of Śrī Kṛṣṇa. This spirit is demonic, because demons do not believe in God but simply enjoy the property of the Supreme. Since there is a great need of an edition of the Gītā in English, as it is received by the paramparā (disciplic succession) system, an attempt is made herewith to fulfill this great want. Bhagavad-gītā – accepted as it is – is a great boon to humanity; but if it is accepted as a treatise of philosophical speculations, it is simply a waste of time.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

2. The saintly kings understood this knowledge received in this disciplic succession. By the powerful influence of time, this jṣāna-yoga was lost, O afflicter of enemies.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
2. The saintly kings understood this knowledge received in this disciplic succession. By the influence of powerful time, this disciplic succession for yoga was broken, O afflicter of enemies.
Commentary

Thus the great saintly kings know this yoga which was received through the line of disciplic succession starting from Vivasvān, and which was taught by their forefathers such as Ikṣvāku. The sampradāya of this yoga in this world (iha) was broken by the influence of powerful time.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

COMMON THEME: Ancient origins of Bhagavad-gita and qualifications to receive

The Lord explains that science of work, explained in Chapters 2 and 3, is very old and authorized; and He gave this knowledge to Vivasvan and then the Supreme science was received through ‘disciplic succession’ (Answer to how it was received) but in the course of time the succession was broken.
Who is qualified to receive the transcendental mystery of this science?
Devotee
Friend (Devotee can be in one of the five primary rasas,e.g. Arjuna is a friend)
Other references on Qualifications Ref. 4.34, 4.39, 9.1, 18.67-68
Focus of Learning

(a) Importance of Bhagavad-gita for Royal order. Why? (4.1)

To rule the citizens and protect them from material bondage to lust
To impart “Krishna consciousness” and “value of human life” to citizens by education, culture and devotion
Thus helps them pursue a successful path of human life

(b) Importance of Bhagavad-gita being given to the sun-god as the first disciple (4.1)

This indicates that Bhagavad-gita is not a speculative treatise for insignificant mundane scholars but a standard book of knowledge from time immemorial
Krishna did not give this knowledge to any ordinary person
He selected sun god because the sun is the king of all planets and controls all planets and is rotating under Krishna’s order (Ref. Brahma Samhita 5.52)

(c) History of Bhagavad-Gita (4.1)

Exists in human society since Maharaj Iksvaku (2,005,000 years)
Given to Vivasvan at least 120,400,000 years ago
Again spoken to Arjuna 5000 years ago. Why? – Because original purpose was scattered by motives of unscrupulous commentators (4.2 purport)

PRACTICAL APPLICATION OF TEXTS 4.1 – 4.3

The presentation of Bhagavad-gita’s history is a precursor to Krishna’s revelation of His

transcendental nature

Learning the tradition of this knowledge will give Arjuna faith in what he is hearing
Additionally, Krishna’s claim to be the original proponent of knowledge that is more than 2 million years old sparks a conversation that allows Krishna to reveal His supreme transcendental position

PRACTICAL APPLICATION OF SECTION I

Krishna is Absolute Truth, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who comes

to the world to protect eternal dharma. One who knows this is factually liberated

Personal Guidance
By the long passage of time the disciplic succession was broken; therefore the science is now received as it was originally given.
Practical Applications
  • Accept that traditions can be corrupted and need re-establishment.
  • Trust authentic re-transmissions (like Prabhupāda's restoration of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism).
  • Study the original texts under guidance, not modern reinterpretations.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I accepting a corrupted version of a teaching?
  • Whose careful restoration am I drawing from?
Analogy

Imagine an oral lineage of singers, each one teaching the next the precise melody. After several generations of careful repetition, a war scatters the singers; the descendants remember the song approximately but no longer cleanly. The notes are not lost in principle — the melody still exists — but the line has to be re-founded by someone who actually heard it sung pure.

Stories
Vyāsa compiling the Vedas

Tradition records that toward the close of Dvāpara-yuga, Vyāsa saw that human memory and lifespan were declining. The single Veda which earlier sages had carried whole could no longer be held by ordinary minds.

So he divided it into four — Ṛk, Yajus, Sāma, Atharva — and entrusted each to a chief disciple to safeguard for a smaller capacity in a darker age. Then he composed the Mahābhārata and the Bhāgavatam as further protections against the same decay. The verse's diagnosis — kālena... naṣṭaḥ — is the diagnosis Vyāsa himself acted on.
