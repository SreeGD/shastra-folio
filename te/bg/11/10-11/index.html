Bhagavad-gītā 11.10-11

Important Words
  • aneka — many
    Repeated four times in two verses. Sañjaya's vocabulary breaks down under the pressure of plurality — he can only keep saying aneka, aneka, aneka.
  • vaktra — mouths
    Mouths in particular, because the mouths will become terrible in verses 25–30 — the same vaktras that now hold garlands will later devour kings. The seed of the awful is in the wonderful.
  • divya — divine, celestial
    Used four times — for ornaments, weapons, garments, garments-and-fragrances. Everything Arjuna sees is divya; the material register has been suspended.
  • udyata — upraised
    Past participle of ud-yam, 'to lift.' The weapons are not at rest — they are raised, in active readiness. Even at this descriptive moment, the form is implicitly about to act.
  • anantam — unlimited
    Neuter singular, agreeing with devam. Sañjaya's last word in the description: after every count and catalogue, the only honest adjective is 'limitless.'
  • viśvataḥ - mukham — facing all directions
    Compound: 'with mouth(s) on every side.' The Lord has no front and no back; every approach meets a face. This will become Arjuna's terror in verse 24.
Arjuna saw in that universal form unlimited mouths, unlimited eyes, unlimited wonderful visions. The form was decorated with many celestial ornaments and bore many divine upraised weapons. He wore celestial garlands and garments, and many divine scents were smeared over His body. All was wondrous, brilliant, unlimited, all-expanding.
Purport

In these two verses the repeated use of the word many indicates that there was no limit to the number of hands, mouths, legs and other manifestations Arjuna was seeing. These manifestations were distributed throughout the universe, but by the grace of the Lord, Arjuna could see them while sitting in one place. That was due to the inconceivable potency of Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
10. It had many mouths and eyes, many astonishing aspects, many wonderful ornaments, uplifting many wonderful weapons. 11. That form wore astonishing garlands and dress, astonishing perfumes and ointments. It was shining, greatly amazing, infinite, with faces in every direction.
Commentary

Viśvato mukham means “whose faces are everywhere.”

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
10. It had many mouths and eyes, many astonishing aspects, many wonderful ornaments, uplifting many wonderful weapons. 11. That form wore astonishing garlands and dress, astonishing ointments and fragrances. It was shining, greatly amazing, beyond compare, with faces in every direction.
Commentary

That form had thousands (aneka) of mouths and eyes, since later there is a description of the form with thousands of arms: sahasra-bāho bhava viśva-mūrte (BG 11.46) The use of the word aneka here indicates that the arms and eyes were so numerous that they could not be counted (instead of just several, which is the other meaning of aneka.) This is understood from the śruti statement viśvataś cakṣur uta viśvatomukhaḥ: His eyes are everywhere, His faces are everywhere. (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.3) In that form he saw many astonishing things (anekādbhuta darśanam).

That form had celestial fragrances and ointments. It was shining (devam), unprecedented (anantam), and had faces in all directions (viśvato mukham).

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

The word divya is used four times in these two verses to describe the extraordinary ornaments,

weapons, garlands and fragrances of the universal form. Śrīla Prabhupāda describes the word aneka

(“many”) in the purport, showing that Arjuna saw unlimited manifestations distributed throughout

the universe.

Personal Guidance
Arjuna saw unlimited mouths, unlimited eyes, unlimited wonderful visions, weapons, garlands, anointed with celestial perfumes — all wonderful, brilliant, unlimited.
Practical Applications
  • Don't reduce the universal form to symbol; the verse says actual unlimited features.
  • Honor the multisensory richness of the vision.
  • Cultivate the imagination to receive scriptural description.
Reflection Questions
  • Where have I reduced spiritual reality to mere symbol?
  • What imaginative receptivity is being asked?
Analogy

Imagine a king's coronation portrait: every ornament catalogued, every weapon held aloft, every fragrance noted. The catalogue itself is the portrait. So Sañjaya, unable to paint, lists — and the list becomes the form.

Stories
Markaṇḍeya in the Belly of Viṣṇu

When the sage Markaṇḍeya was swept into the cosmic waters of dissolution (Mahābhārata, Vana-parva 188–189; Bhāgavatam 12.9), he wandered inside the body of the Supreme Lord and saw the same kinds of marvels Arjuna now sees — unlimited mouths, eyes, ornaments, weapons, garlands, all radiant beyond measure.

The Gītā's description in 11.10–11 echoes Markaṇḍeya's seeing. The viśva-rūpa is not a one-off display for a battlefield; it is the standing truth, occasionally unveiled to a few prepared souls.
