Bhagavad-gītā 2.33

If, however, you do not perform your religious duty of fighting, then you will certainly incur sins for neglecting your duties and thus lose your reputation as a fighter.
Purport

Arjuna was a famous fighter, and he attained fame by fighting many great demigods, including even Lord Śiva. After fighting and defeating Lord Śiva in the dress of a hunter, Arjuna pleased the lord and received as a reward a weapon called pāśupata-astra. Everyone knew that he was a great warrior. Even Droṇācārya gave him benedictions and awarded him the special weapon by which he could kill even his teacher. So he was credited with so many military certificates from many authorities, including his adoptive father Indra, the heavenly king. But if he abandoned the battle, not only would he neglect his specific duty as a kṣatriya, but he would lose all his fame and good name and thus prepare his royal road to hell. In other words, he would go to hell not by fighting but by withdrawing from battle.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
33. If you do not take part in this righteous war, giving up your duty and fame, you will instead reap sin.
Commentary

In four verses he describes the fault in doing the opposite.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
33. If you do not take part in this righteous war, giving up your duty and fame, you will instead reap sin.
Commentary

In this and the following verses the Lord shows the faults in not fighting. Giving up your duty of fighting (dharmyam saṁgrāmam) and also the glory attained by pleasing Śiva and killing the Nivāta kavacas and others, [Note: Arjuna went to Mount Kailāsa and pleased Śiva. Śiva bestowed on him his Pāśupata weapon. When Indra was attacked by the Nivātakavacas, he called Arjuna, who killed them all.] you will reap only sin, by neglect of your duty, as such conduct is forbidden by the smṛtis. na nivarteta saṅgrāmād: one should not turn away from battle. (Manu Smṛti 7.88)

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Text 32 spoke of the gains Arjuna could obtain by fighting, and Text 33 describes the losses that he is goig to obtain if he doesn't fight. And the next four verses in addition tell Arjuna's losses if he doesn't do his duties and fight. Because Arjuna here is thinking: "O it is more noble, it is more big hearted to leave the battlefield." But Krsna says:

Translation
2.33 Atha, on the other hand; cet, if; tvam, you; na karisyasi, will not fight; even imam, this; dharmyam, righteous; samgramam, battle, which has presented itself as a duty, which is not opposed to righteousness, and which is enjoined (by the scriptures); tatah, then, because of not undertaking that; hitva, forsaking; sva-dharmam, your own duty; ca, and; kritim, fame, earned from encountering Mahadeva (Lord Siva) and others; avapsyasi, you will incur; only papam, sin.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
2.33 If in delusion, you do not wage this war, which has started and which is the duty of a Ksatriya, then, owing to the non-performance of your immediate and incumbent duty, you will lose the immeasurable bliss which is the fruit of discharging your duty and the immeasurable fame which is the fruit of victory. In addition, you will incur extreme sin.

Personal Guidance
If you abandon dharma out of fear of social judgement, you will incur sin and lose honor — there is no neutral retreat from duty.
Practical Applications
  • Refuse to act out of fear of public opinion when dharma is clear.
  • Distinguish between healthy reputation and idolatry of reputation.
  • Notice when 'maintaining peace' is actually moral cowardice.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I about to abandon a dharma because of how it will look?
  • Whose opinion of me am I unduly weighing against Krishna's instruction?
Analogy

A soldier who refuses to defend his city when it is attacked loses both his honor and his standing — and his refusal itself constitutes a transgression. Inaction here is not zero; it is negative. Skipping the verse you were called to recite is its own kind of broken vow.

Important Words
  • dharmyam — dharmic
    The battle is again qualified — Kṛṣṇa keeps marking that this is no ordinary war. If it were not dharmya, the argument would not hold. The instruction is specific to the case, not a blanket endorsement of violence.
  • saṅgrāmam — battle
    From sam + √gram — coming together (in combat). The word is military and specific; Kṛṣṇa is not speaking metaphorically. The battle in front of Arjuna is the referent.
  • kīrtim — reputation
    Renown — for the kṣatriya, kīrti is dharmic capital. Losing kīrti is not vanity but the collapse of one's social form. Cf. verse 2's akīrti.
  • hitvā — having abandoned
    Gerund of √hā — to abandon. The verb suggests *active letting-go*, not mere lapse. Arjuna's refusal will be read by the world as an *abandonment*, not an oversight.
  • pāpam — sin
    Refusal of dharma incurs pāpa. The verse closes the moral arithmetic: action — heaven; inaction — sin. There is no neutral middle in a situation that demands engagement.
