Bhagavad-gītā 2.32

यदृच्छया चोपपन्नं स्वर्गद्वारमपावृतम् । सुखिनः क्षत्रियाः पार्थ लभन्ते युद्धमीदृशम् ॥ ३२ ॥

O Pārtha, happy are the kṣatriyas to whom such fighting opportunities come unsought, opening for them the doors of the heavenly planets.
Purport

As supreme teacher of the world, Lord Kṛṣṇa condemns the attitude of Arjuna, who said, “I do not find any good in this fighting. It will cause perpetual habitation in hell.” Such statements by Arjuna were due to ignorance only. He wanted to become nonviolent in the discharge of his specific duty. For a kṣatriya to be on the battlefield and to become nonviolent is the philosophy of fools. In the Parāśara-smṛti, or religious codes made by Parāśara, the great sage and father of Vyāsadeva, it is stated: kṣatriyo hi prajā rakṣan śastra-pāṇiḥ pradaṇḍayan nirjitya para-sainyādi kṣitiṁ dharmeṇa pālayet “The kṣatriya’s duty is to protect the citizens from all kinds of difficulties, and for that reason he has to apply violence in suitable cases for law and order. Therefore he has to conquer the soldiers of inimical kings, and thus, with religious principles, he should rule over the world.” Considering all aspects, Arjuna had no reason to refrain from fighting. If he should conquer his enemies, he would enjoy the kingdom; and if he should die in the battle, he would be elevated to the heavenly planets, whose doors were wide open to him. Fighting would be for his benefit in either case.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
32. Warriors are the happy who obtain such a battle which comes by chance and which opens the doors to heaven.
Commentary

Moreover, more than even the victors, those who die in a just battle attain happiness. By killing Bhīṣma and others, you make them happier. Even without doing karma-yoga, one can attain Svarga through the battle, without any obstructions (apāvṛtam).

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
32. Warriors are the happy who obtain such a battle which comes just without effort and which opens the doors to heaven.
Commentary

Moreover, since without your effort this war will bring great benefit, your fear is not justified. Ca is used here for emphasis only (yadṛcchayā ca—just without effort). Fortunate (sukhinaḥ) kṣatriyas obtain such wars— with great warriors such as Bhīṣma, which come to them without effort—because if they are victorious they attain fame and kingdom by honest effort, and if they die, they very quickly attain svarga. The Lord indicates this by saying that there is no obstacles to attain svarga (svarga dvāram apāvṛtam), whereas by performance of the jyotiṣṭoma sacrifice one attains svarga only after a long time. Thus the Lord indicates the superiority of fighting to sacrifices.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

This is the goal of Karma‑kanda.

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
2.32 Why, again, does that battle become a duty? This is being answered (as follows) [A specific rule is more authoritative than a general rule. Non-violence is a general rule enjoined by the scriptures, but the duty of fighting is a specific rule for a Ksatriya.]: Partha, O son of Partha; are not those Ksatiryas sukhinah, happy [Happy in this world as also in the other.] who labhante, come across; a yuddham, battle; idrsam, of this kind; upapannam, which presents itself; yadrcchaya, unsought for; and which is an apavrtam, open; svarga-dvaram, gate to heaven? [Rites and duties like sacrifices etc. yield their results after the lapse of some time. But the Ksatriyas go to heaven immediatley after dying in battle, because, unlike the minds of others, their minds remaind fully engaged in their immediate duty.]
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
2.32 Only the fortunate Ksatriyas, i.e., the meritorious ones, gian such a war as this, which has come unsought, which is the means for the attainment of immeasurable bliss, and which gives an unobstructed pathway to heaven.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
2.32 Yadrcchaya etc. A war of this nature, because it is conducive to the heaven, should not be avoided even by other such Ksatriyas who are full of desires How much less [it is to be avoided] in the case of one to whom the science of knowledge of this nature has been taught ? This is what is intended to be conveyed [here] And the verse does not at all end with [determining how to attain] the heaven. The very thing (i.e. sin), fearing which you withdraw from the battle, will befall you branching off hundredfold. This [the Lord] says-
Swami Sivananda Classical
Translation
2.32 Happy are the Kshatriyas, O Arjuna! who are called upon to fight in such a battle that comes of itself as an open door to heaven.

Swami Gambhīrānanda Classical
Translation
2.32 O son of Partha, happy are the Ksatriyas who come across this kind of a battle, which presents itself unsought for and which is an open gate to heaven.
Shri Purohit Swami Classical
Translation
2.32 Blessed are the soldiers who find their opportunity. This opportunity has opened for thee the gates of heaven.

Personal Guidance
Fortunate are those to whom such an opportunity for dharma comes unsought — recognize providence when it knocks.
Practical Applications
  • When an unsought opportunity for service arrives, treat it as Krishna's hand and respond.
  • Stop strategizing for the perfect moment; perfect moments come unscheduled.
  • Cultivate the habit of saying yes to dharma when it presents itself.
Reflection Questions
  • What unsought opportunity is currently in front of me that I am hesitating over?
  • How often do I miss providence because I was looking elsewhere?
Analogy

A door once opened to a banquet hall closes if not entered. The dharmic war has opened such a door — yadṛcchayā, by good fortune, without contrivance. To turn away from an unsought duty that leads to the higher worlds is to refuse a garland Kṛṣṇa Himself has placed in one's hand.

Stories
Hanumān's leap, given to him unsought

The need to leap the ocean came to Hanumān not because he sought it — he had even forgotten his own power. The opportunity arrived; Jāmbavān recognized it; Hanumān rose to it.

When great service comes yadṛcchayā — by fortunate accident, by the Lord's quiet arrangement — refusing it is its own kind of fall. The verse names exactly this: the door has opened by no effort of yours; happy is the one who walks through.

Important Words
  • yadṛcchayā — of itself, by chance
    Instrumental of yadṛcchā — *as it comes*, spontaneously, without contrivance. The dharmic war was not Arjuna's project; it arrived. Duties that arrive of themselves carry a special grace.
  • svarga - dvāram — door of heaven
    The traditional kṣatriya promise: dying in dharmic battle opens svarga. Kṛṣṇa is not yet teaching liberation here — He is teaching at the level of dharma the warrior already accepts, on its own terms.
  • apāvṛtam — wide open
    From apa-√vṛ — uncovered, unbarred. The door is not merely available; it is *swung open*. The metaphor is vivid: turning away from a flung-open door is an act of conscious refusal, not oversight.
  • sukhinaḥ — fortunate, happy
    Plural of sukhin. Those for whom such an opportunity arrives are *blessed*. The verse reframes the obligation as a privilege.
  • īdṛśam — of this sort
    Demonstrative — *like this*. Modifying yuddham. The pronoun carries the whole weight of qualification: this *kind* of battle — dharmic, unsought, heaven-opening. Not every battle qualifies.
