Important Words
  • sat - bhāve — in the sense of the nature of the Supreme
    Locative compound: sat (the real) + bhāva (nature, mood). The verse opens by declaring that the syllable sat is *first* applied to the Lord's own nature. Bhāva here is technical: not generic mood but ontological character. Sat-bhāva is what the Supreme intrinsically *is*.
  • sādhu - bhāve — in the sense of the nature of the devotee
    The twin compound: sādhu (the saintly person) + bhāva. Paired with sat-bhāve. The grammar refuses to separate the two — the devotee participates in sat by being yoked to it. Sādhu-bhāva is the participatory mode of sat-bhāva.
  • sat - śabdaḥ — the sound sat
    Compound: sat + śabda (sound, word). The śabda-brahman dimension again. The audible utterance of sat is itself a participation in the reality sat names. Sound and substance fused — the perennial Vedic claim that the word, rightly uttered, carries the thing.
  • karmaṇi — activities
    Locative of karman — action. Used in the verse with praśaste (praised, approved). Sat consecrates the praiseworthy act — and conversely, the use of sat marks the act *as* praiseworthy. The same word karman that runs through the whole Gītā here gets its highest qualifying epithet: sat-praśaste-karmaṇi.
  • sthitiḥ — the situation, the standing
    From √sthā — to stand. The same root that gave niṣṭhā in 17.1. The chapter ends as it began: by asking where one stands. The answer: in sat — the only standing that endures.
  • arthīyam — meant for
    From artha (purpose) + suffix -īya. 'For-the-purpose-of.' In the verse it modifies karma in conjunction with tat: 'action meant for *that* (the Supreme).' The whole chapter culminates in this prepositional structure of devotion: every act *meant for* the Lord, not for the doer.
The Absolute Truth is the objective of devotional sacrifice, and it is indicated by the word sat. The performer of such sacrifice is also called sat, as are all works of sacrifice, penance and charity which, true to the absolute nature, are performed to please the Supreme Person, O son of Pṛthā.
Purport

The words praśaste karmaṇi, or “prescribed duties,” indicate that there are many activities prescribed in the Vedic literature which are purificatory processes, beginning from the time of conception up to the end of one’s life. Such purificatory processes are adopted for the ultimate liberation of the living entity. In all such activities it is recommended that one vibrate oṁ tat sat. The words sad-bhāve and sādhu-bhāve indicate the transcendental situation. Acting in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is called sattva, and one who is fully conscious of the activities of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is called a sādhu. In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (3.25.25) it is said that the transcendental subject matter becomes clear in the association of the devotees. The words used are satāṁ prasaṅgāt. Without good association, one cannot achieve transcendental knowledge. When initiating a person or offering the sacred thread, one vibrates the words oṁ tat sat. Similarly, in all kinds of performance of yajña the object is the Supreme, oṁ tat sat. The word tad-arthīyam further means offering service to anything which represents the Supreme, including such service as cooking and helping in the Lord’s temple, or any other kind of work for broadcasting the glories of the Lord. These supreme words oṁ tat sat are thus used in many ways to perfect all activities and make everything complete.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

Here, the direct indications of devotional service, are given ‑ the work should be done in the mode of goodness with the purpose the Supreme.

[2. Sacrifice, penance, and austerity done without faith in the Supreme ‑ without a transcendental objective ‑ are useless both in this life and the next. (28) ]

Personal Guidance
Sat indicates the absolute truth, the goal of devotional sacrifice — sacrifice, austerity, and charity in pursuit of the Absolute are also called Sat.
Practical Applications
  • Use Sat to mark acts oriented toward the Supreme.
  • Recognize that Sat is both reality and the practice oriented toward it.
  • Cultivate Sat-oriented activity.
Reflection Questions
  • Which of my activities qualify as Sat?
  • Which need redirection?
Analogy

Sat is like the sun, and sacrifice, charity, austerity, the sādhu, and the act itself are its rays — one in substance with their source yet never collapsed into it. Each ray shines only because it is held by the sun; each is still a ray and not the sun. The devotee remains the devotee and the act remains the act, but each becomes sat by being yoked to Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Person whom sat names — simultaneously one with Him and distinct from Him.
