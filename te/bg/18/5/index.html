Bhagavad-gītā 18.5

యజ్ఞదానతప:కర్మ న త్యాజ్యం కార్యమేవ తత్ । యజ్ఞో దానం తపశ్చైవ పావనాని మనీషిణామ్ ॥ ౫ ॥

Important Words
  • yajñaḥ — sacrifice
    The first of the three irreducibles. Not optional even for the manīṣī. In Kali-yuga, harināma-saṅkīrtana is yajña — the same purifying act in its current authorized form.
  • dānam — charity
    The breaking of clutching. Giving keeps a soul from calcifying into proprietorship. The renouncer who still hoards has renounced nothing.
  • tapaḥ — penance, austerity
    Voluntary heat. Fasting, vows, silence, ekādaśī — the friction that burns out the subtle film of sense-attachment.
  • pāvanāni — purifying agents
    Plural participle. These three are *agents of purification* — they act on the practitioner. They are not symbolic gestures; they actually clean the heart.
  • manīṣiṇām — even of the great thinkers
    The very class invoked in verse 3 to argue for abandoning karma. Kṛṣṇa turns the term back: even those manīṣīs themselves are still being purified by these acts. No one outgrows the bath.
Acts of sacrifice, charity and penance are not to be given up; they must be performed. Indeed, sacrifice, charity and penance purify even the great souls.
Purport

The yogīs should perform acts for the advancement of human society. There are many purificatory processes for advancing a human being to spiritual life. The marriage ceremony, for example, is considered to be one of these sacrifices. It is called vivāha-yajña. Should a sannyāsī, who is in the renounced order of life and who has given up his family relations, encourage the marriage ceremony? The Lord says here that any sacrifice which is meant for human welfare should never be given up. Vivāha-yajña, the marriage ceremony, is meant to regulate the human mind so that it may become peaceful for spiritual advancement. For most men, this vivāha-yajña should be encouraged even by persons in the renounced order of life. Sannyāsīs should never associate with women, but that does not mean that one who is in the lower stages of life, a young man, should not accept a wife in the marriage ceremony. All prescribed sacrifices are meant for achieving the Supreme Lord. Therefore, in the lower stages, they should not be given up. Similarly, charity is for the purification of the heart. If charity is given to suitable persons, as described previously, it leads one to advanced spiritual life.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
5. Sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be given up but should be performed, since they purify even the wise.
Commentary

According to the Lord, even among kāmya-karmas (optional), those sacrifices, charities and austerities which are sattvic in nature, , should be performed without desire for results. [Note: In the second verse, sannyāsa was defined as giving up kāmya-karmas.] This is explained in this verse. Sacrifice, austerity and charity must be done, because they cause purification of the consciousness.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
5. Sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be given up but should be performed, since they purify the wise.
Commentary

In two verses, the Lord first states His own conviction in the matter. For the wise, the obligatory activities such as sacrifices, charity and austerity should never be given up, because they purify (pāvaṇāni)—they destroy the faults accumulated by repeated births, through the knowledge arising within them like a lotus blossoming from a root.

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Commentary

The acaryas mention in their explanation that: 'as a lotus stem has fibers inside, these three activities have the fiber of jnana, or knowledge, inside. Do these activities as Krsna's prescriptions and become purified in the heart and thereby you will attain knowledge.'

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
18.5 Yajna-dana-tapah-karma, the practice of sacrifice, charity and austerity-this threefold practice; na tyajyam, is not to be abandoned; tat, it; is eva, surely; karyam, to be undertaken. Why? Yajnah, sacrifice; danam, charity; and tapah, austerity; are eva, verily; pavanani, the purifiers, the causes of sanctification; manisinam, of the wise, i.e. of those who do not seek results for themselves.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
18.5 Acts such as sacrifices, gifts, austerities etc., enjoined in the Vedas should not be relinished by the aspirant for release, but should be performed day after day until his death. Why? Acts like sacrifices, gifts and austerities associated with the different stations of life, are the means of purification for the wise., i.e., for those given to contemplation. Contemplation is worship. For the aspirants who perform such worship (Upasana) throughout their lives, they (sacrifices etc.) are a help to erase the previous Karmas which stand in the way of the fulfilment of such worship.

Personal Guidance
Sacrifice, charity, austerity should not be given up — they are to be performed; they purify even the great souls.
Practical Applications
  • Do not abandon sacrifice, charity, or austerity in the name of renunciation.
  • Honor them as purifiers.
  • Continue them through your life-stages.
Reflection Questions
  • Where have I been tempted to abandon the three?
  • What sustained practice is calling?
Analogy

Even the seasoned monk does not stop bathing. Yajña, dāna, and tapas are the soul's daily washing — to skip them is not advancement but neglect. The fire of purification belongs to every stage of the path, not just the beginner's.

Stories
King Yudhiṣṭhira's Rājasūya

Even after the Pāṇḍavas had received Kṛṣṇa's direct association and the highest spiritual instruction, Yudhiṣṭhira did not consider himself above sacrifice. He performed the great Rājasūya yajña with full splendor — and Kṛṣṇa Himself attended and washed the feet of the guests.

The greatest of dharmic kings did not skip yajña, dāna, or tapas. These three remain pāvanāni — purifying — even for those whom one would assume to need no further purification.
