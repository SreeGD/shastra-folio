Bhagavad-gītā 14.14

Important Words
  • sattve pravṛddhe — when goodness has developed
    Locative absolute. Not just dying-while-sattvic, but dying when sattva is in mature, developed condition. The chapter has been careful to distinguish bare sattva from vivṛddha sattva (v. 11). It is the developed form that the verse rewards.
  • pralayam yāti — goes to dissolution
    Pralaya here means the individual's death, not cosmic dissolution — though the word's larger sense lingers. Death is a small pralaya. The same logic that runs sarga (v. 2) runs every individual exit.
  • deha-bhṛt — the embodied one
    Deha-bhṛt — bearer-of-body. Kṛṣṇa keeps the soul and the body terminologically distinct. The soul bears the body; it is not the body. This word at the moment of death is doing precise work.
  • uttama-vidām lokān amalān — the pure planets of those who know the highest
    Three loaded words. Uttama-vid: knowers of the supreme. Amala: stainless. Loka: world. Sattvic death lands the soul among the company of the highest knowers, in stainless realms. Company at death is a function of mode at death.
When one dies in the mode of goodness, he attains to the pure higher planets of the great sages.
Purport

One in goodness attains higher planetary systems, like Brahmaloka or Janaloka, and there enjoys godly happiness. The word amalān is significant; it means “free from the modes of passion and ignorance.” There are impurities in the material world, but the mode of goodness is the purest form of existence in the material world. There are different kinds of planets for different kinds of living entities. Those who die in the mode of goodness are elevated to the planets where great sages and great devotees live.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
14. When a person with predominance of sattva dies, he attains the pure planets of the worshippers of Brahmā.
Commentary

When a person with a predominance of sattva dies (pralayaṁ yati), he attains the pure worlds of the worshipers of Hiraṇyagarbha (uttamā-vidām) and others, worlds which give happiness. The word uttama vidam means “of those who attain (vindanti) the supreme (uttama).”

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
14. When a person with predominance of sattva dies, he attains the pure planets of the worshippers of Brahmā.
Commentary

Two verses describe the particular results of the dominant guṇa at the time of death. With dominance of sattva when the jīva (deha bhṛt) dies, he attains the places of heavenly enjoyment of the worshippers of Hiraṇyagarbha and others (uttama vidām), with absence of contamination (amalān) of rajas and tamas.

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
14.14 Yada, when; deha-bhrt, an embodied one, the soul; yati, undergoes; pralayam, death; sattve pravrddhe, while sattva is predominant; tu, exclusively; [Tu is used to exclude rajas and tamas.-S.] tada, then; pratipadyate, he attains, i.e. gains; the amalan, tainless, stainless; lokan, worlds; [The worlds of Brahma, etc., which are free from the impurity of predominance either of rajas or tamas.] uttamavidam, of those who know the highest, i.e. of those who have known the principles-mahat and the rest.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
14.14 When the 'Sattva prevails' i.e., while the Sattva continues to be prevalent, if the embodies self meets with death, It reaches the pure worlds, i.e., regions conducive to the knowledge of the self. The purport is this: If Satva preponderates in a person at the time of death, he will be rorn in the families of those who have the knowledge of the self, and thus be alified to perform auspicious acts which are the means of attaining the true knowledge of the self.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
14.14 See Comment under 14.15
Swami Sivananda Classical
Translation
14.14 If the embodied one meets with death when Sattva is predominant, then he attains to the spotless worlds of the knowers of the Highest.
Commentary

Swami Gambhīrānanda Classical
Translation
14.14 When an embodied one undergoes death while sattva is exclusively prodominant, then he attains the taintless worlds of those who know the highest (entities).
Shri Purohit Swami Classical

14.14 When Purity prevails, the soul on quitting the body passes on to the pure regions where live those who know the Highest.

Personal Guidance
If one dies in sattva, he attains the pure higher planets of the wise.
Practical Applications
  • Cultivate sattva approaching the time of death.
  • Treat each evening's lying-down as practice.
  • Don't let tamas dominate the late hours.
Reflection Questions
  • What is my evening mode usually?
  • What would steady sattva at evening change?
Analogy

The last note of a song decides which key the next song will be in. Death is that last note. Whichever mode is sounding at the end determines the next composition entirely.

Stories
Bhīṣma on the bed of arrows

Lying pierced through on Kurukṣetra, awaiting the uttarāyaṇa, Bhīṣma did not lapse into pain-tamas or into grief-rajas. He spent his final weeks in unbroken sattva: instructing Yudhiṣṭhira on dharma, glorifying Kṛṣṇa, mind fixed on the Lord.

When the moment came he gave up his body deliberately, in the mode of goodness, and the tradition records his attainment of the higher realms. The verse made flesh: deha-bhṛt in sattve pravṛddha, uttama-vidām lokān prāpta.
