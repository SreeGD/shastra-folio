Important Words
  • su-hṛt — to well-wishers by nature
    Su-hṛt — 'good-hearted one.' The verse lists six categories and concludes with a seventh attitude (sama-buddhi). The sheer length of the list is the verse's argument: the equality must hold across all categories, no exceptions.
  • udāsīna — neutrals
    Ud-ā-√sad, 'one who sits apart.' The neutral observer — often the easiest category to be indifferent to. The verse names them precisely so they are not forgotten.
  • dveṣya — the envious
    From √dviṣ, 'to hate.' The hardest test. The hostile and the envious are placed mid-list, not buried at the end — Kṛṣṇa wants the reader to register them clearly.
  • pāpeṣu — unto the sinners
    Locative plural. Even the sinful are within the sama-buddhi. The yogī's equality does not bless sin; it sees the soul beneath the sin. The error is registered; the soul is not condemned.
  • sama-buddhiḥ — having equal intelligence
    The verse's hinge term. Not sama-bhāva (equal feeling) — buddhiḥ, intelligence. The equality is reached by understanding, not by sentiment. The discriminating mind itself sees equally.
  • viśiṣyate — is far advanced
    Vi-√śiṣ, 'to distinguish, to stand out.' Beautiful paradox: the one who treats all equally is the one who stands distinguished. Sameness of vision is the rarest excellence.
A person is considered still further advanced when he regards honest well-wishers, affectionate benefactors, the neutral, mediators, the envious, friends and enemies, the pious and the sinners all with an equal mind.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
9. He sees the natural well-wisher, the friend, the neutral person, the mediator, the enemy, and the relative, the sinner and pious person as the same.
Commentary

The yogī sees equally the person who wishes one well (suhṛt) by his nature, one who actually does good out of affection (mitra), the killer (ari), one who is neutral in times of conflict or honor (udāsīna), the mediator who tries to resolve conflict between the two sides (madhyastha), the person who deserves hatred because he harms one (dveṣya), the relative (bandhu), the follower of dharma and the follower of adharma. This position is superior to that of seeing dirt and gold as the same.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
9. He is better who sees the natural well wisher, the friend, the neutral person, the mediator, the enemy, and the relative, the sinner and pious person as the same.
Commentary

He who sees friends and enemies as equal is superior (viśiṣyate) to the yogī who sees stone and gold as one. A suhrt is one who by his very nature desires another person’s benefit. Mitra is one who actually does beneficial acts for another person out of affection. Ari is one who desires another’s misfortune, due to not having affection. Udāsin is one who is neutral to two arguing parties. Madhya stha is one who attempts to stop the dispute. Dveṣya is one who deserves to be hated because of his desire to injure another person. Bandhu is a person who desires ones benefit because of a family relationship. The sadhu is one who follows dharma. The pāpa is one who does not follow dharma.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

Symptoms of yoga-rudha stage

COMMON THEME: Complete indifference to material existence by realized knowledge of Supersoul



Focus of Learning

(a) Intended goal for every living entity – to abide by the dictation of the Supreme Personality of

Godhead who is seated in everyone’s heart as Paramatma

(b) Deviation from the goal because of misled mind – When the mind is misled by the external

illusory energy, one becomes entangled in material activities

(c) Solution to avoid deviation

Mind must admit some superior dictation and follow it
When the mind is fixed on the superior nature, he has no alternative but to follow the dictations of the Supreme
Effect of controlling the mind – One automatically follows the dictation of Paramatma

(d) Krishna consciousness offers this very practical solution

A devotee automatically follows the Lord’s dictation and therefore he is unaffected by dualities, being fully absorbed in the Supreme
This state is called ‘practical-samadhi’

SUMMARISED THEME 6A

SYMPTOMS OF YOGARUDHA STAGE (6.7 – 6.9)

A) If the mind is made a friend as per Text 6.6, the following symptoms develop, as the realizations

deepen and grow, in the following sequence:

1. Completely neutral to all material existence and dualities (6.7)

2. Sees all object equally – Pebbles, gold, stone (6.8)

3. Fully satisfied by jnana and vijnana (6.8)

4. Sees all people equally – “most advanced stage” (6.9)

(d) Best way to disentangle mind – Always engage mind in Krishna consciousness

(e) Real yoga practice entails – Meeting Paramatma in the heart and following His dictation

cf. Unconquered mind has to serve the dictations of lust, anger, avarice, illusion etc.

(f) How Krishna consciousness fulfils this – Perfect surrender to Lord’s dictations follows

automatically in Krishna Consciousness

Therefore, to acire this excellent result-
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
6.9 'Well-wishers' (Suhrt) are those who wish one well, regardless of differences in age. 'Friends' (Mitra) are well-wisheres of eal age. 'Foes' (Ari) are those who wish ill to one because of some particular cause. 'The indifferent' (Udasina) are those devoid of both friendship and hostility because of the absence of causes for both; 'neutrals' (Madhyastha) are those who are by their very nature incapable of both friendship and hostility. 'The hateful' are those who wish ill to one even from birth. 'Relations' are those who bear goodwill from birth. 'The good' are those devoted to virtue. 'The sinful' are those given to sin. Because of the self being the only end of Yoga, and because of there being no gain and no opposition from well-wishers, friends, etc., he who could regard them all with an eal eye as selves, excels in respect of fitness for the practice of Yoga.
Śrī Abhinavagupta Classical
Translation
6.9 Suhrt etc. Friend is one whose heart remains good and auspicious without cause (on its own accord). Companionship is [that which is felt] mutually. Enmity is [also that which is felt] between one another. The indifferent one : the one, who is free from both these. One who remains in the middle : he who is partly a friend and partly an enemy. Foe : he who deserves to be hated, [but] cannot be hated. Relative : the one [connected] by marital bondage. Whosoever is with his mind eal to all these as well as to the righteous and the sinful; he excells [all] i.e., stage after stage he goes out of the cycle of birth-and-death. By the worshipful-footed persons of this sort -

Personal Guidance
Equal toward well-wishers, friends, enemies, the envious, the pious, the sinner — that one is advanced.
Practical Applications
  • Notice differential treatment based on like/dislike — and gradually equalize.
  • Practice sending good wishes even toward the envious or hostile.
  • Treat the test as the practice.
Reflection Questions
  • Whom am I treating differentially that I shouldn't?
  • What would equality of vision allow?
Analogy

The sun shines equally on a hostile city wall and a friendly garden gate — not because the sun cannot distinguish, but because its nature is to give light, not to weigh recipients. The advanced yogī's regard works the same way: not blindness to character, but a sameness in well-wishing that flows from a source that does not depend on the receiver.

Stories
Yudhiṣṭhira and Duryodhana After Kurukṣetra

When Yudhiṣṭhira finally entered Hastināpura as emperor, he insisted on offering the proper funeral rites for Duryodhana, Karṇa, and the other Kaurava warriors — the very men who had stolen his kingdom and humiliated his wife. He grieved for them as for his own brothers.

The Mahābhārata records this not as weakness but as the dharma-rāja's natural equality of vision: su-hṛn-mitra-ari-udāsīna-madhya-stha — well-wisher, friend, enemy, neutral — held with one steady heart now that the war was done. Sama-buddhiḥ in a king.
