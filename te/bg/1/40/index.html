Bhagavad-gītā 1.40

అధర్మాభిభవాత్కృష్ణ ప్రదుష్యన్తి కులస్త్రియః । స్త్రీషు దుష్టాసు వార్ష్ణేయ జాయతే వర్ణసఙ్కరః ॥ ౪౦ ॥

Important Words
  • adharma — irreligion
    Same compound as v.39, now in a longer form: *adharma-abhibhavāt*. Arjuna is tracking the cause-and-effect. *Adharma overpowers — therefore* women are polluted — *therefore* mixed populations — and so on. The Gītā does not contest this dharma-physics.
  • kula - striyaḥ — family ladies
    *Kula* + *strī* (woman). The wives, daughters, sisters who carry the family's continuity. The same word *strī* whose root the Vedic tradition connects to *expansion* (the womb expands the lineage). The corruption of women is the corruption of futurity itself.
  • vārṣṇeya — O descendant of Vṛṣṇi
    Arjuna addresses Kṛṣṇa by His clan name — *descendant of Vṛṣṇi*. The Yādava clan to which Kṛṣṇa belongs. By naming Kṛṣṇa's *own kula*, Arjuna implicitly says: *you too have a family — you understand what its destruction would mean*. Family-feeling is invoked to amplify the appeal.
  • varṇa - saṅkaraḥ — unwanted progeny / mixing of varṇas
    *Varṇa* (colour, class) + *saṅkara* (mixing, confusion). A precise sociological term: the breakdown of the *varṇa* order, the loss of the principle by which each class fulfils its proper function. The Gītā in BG 3.24 will revisit this — Kṛṣṇa Himself acknowledges *saṅkara* as a real risk, but treats *His own dereliction* of cosmic duty as its true source — not Arjuna's discharge of his.
When irreligion is prominent in the family, O Kṛṣṇa, the women of the family become polluted, and from the degradation of womanhood, O descendant of Vṛṣṇi, comes unwanted progeny.
Purport

Good population in human society is the basic principle for peace, prosperity and spiritual progress in life. The varṇāśrama religion’s principles were so designed that the good population would prevail in society for the general spiritual progress of state and community. Such population depends on the chastity and faithfulness of its womanhood. As children are very prone to be misled, women are similarly very prone to degradation. Therefore, both children and women require protection by the elder members of the family. By being engaged in various religious practices, women will not be misled into adultery. According to Cāṇakya Paṇḍita, women are generally not very intelligent and therefore not trustworthy. So the different family traditions of religious activities should always engage them, and thus their chastity and devotion will give birth to a good population eligible for participating in the varṇāśrama system. On the failure of such varṇāśrama-dharma, naturally the women become free to act and mix with men, and thus adultery is indulged in at the risk of unwanted population. Irresponsible men also provoke adultery in society, and thus unwanted children flood the human race at the risk of war and pestilence.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
40. From prevalence of adharma, the women of the family become contaminated. O Kṛṣṇa, when the women are spoiled, mixed varṇas arise.
Commentary

When adharma is prevalent, the women of the family will become spoiled (praduṣyanti), by becoming adulterous.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
40. From prevalence of adharma, the women of the family will become contaminated. O Kṛṣṇa, when the women are spoiled, mixed varṇas arise.
Commentary

Just as we, having been made to trespass dharma by our brothers, and bring about sin in the form of destruction of the family (adharmābhibhavāt), so the women, afflicted with ignorance, thinking that they may engage in sin, being made to defy vows of chastity by us, will become corrupted.

How they are protected – By engaging in religious principles, either under father, husband or son
If they are not protected, it leads to adultery and varna-sankara

From degradation of woman comes unwanted progeny
Varna-sankara has three effects
Risk of war and pestilence
Hellish life
For the whole family – Ancestors falldown because pinda-dana stops

▪

For those who destroy family

All community projects and family welfare acts stop

5. Hellish life for ‘family’ and ‘destroyers of family’ (1.41*)

6. Culture is destroyed – Community projects and family welfare acts stop (1.42)

Saintliness is not justified for a ksatriya

(a) Varnashrama Institution (1.39 purport) – Principles of religious traditions to help members of

(b) Role of elders (1.39 purport)

Based on rules and regulations of fruitive acts
What it is – To offer periodical food and water to forefathers
Right process – worship of Vishnu done / remnants offered to forefathers
Principle: To release them from many sinful reactions / ghostly existence etc.
Who is not obliged for this – One in devotional life
Why devotees are not obliged – Because devotional service relieves hundreds and thousands of forefathers from all misery (Ref. SB. 11.5.41 “devarsi bhutapta…”)

Sets duties for four orders of society
Purpose – To help attain ultimate salvation
‘Result of breaking’ such projects
Chaos in society
People forget ultimate aim of life – Vishnu

Arjuna based his arguments not on personal experience, but on what he has heard from authorities

(h) Principle of ‘Prayascitta’ (1.43 purport) – A system in the varnashrama system by which before

death one has to undergo the process of atonement for his sinful activities

Without doing so, one surely will be transferred to the hellish planets to undergo miserable lives

Personal Guidance
The destruction of dharma in a family is the destruction of the family itself; the surface form survives, but the soul of the lineage is gone.
Practical Applications
  • Notice the difference between maintaining a family's outer customs and maintaining its dharma.
  • Have one explicitly spiritual conversation with a family member this month.
  • Do not let economic concerns silently displace the religious heart of your home.
Reflection Questions
  • Where have we kept the form of family but lost its dharma?
  • What am I willing to do to restore the spiritual current of my lineage?
Analogy

When the household lamp is extinguished, the moths fly elsewhere. Without the protections that *dharma* gives the home, the women — who carry the future of the lineage in their bodies — are the first to be exposed to corruption. The verse names the social physics: the dignity of women rises and falls with the dharma of the men.

Stories
Draupadī in the dice-hall

Years before Kurukṣetra, in this very Kuru court, Yudhiṣṭhira had gambled away his kingdom, his brothers, himself, and finally Draupadī. Duḥśāsana dragged her by the hair into the assembly, attempted to disrobe her, and the elders of the family — Bhīṣma included — sat silent. Kṛṣṇa alone, unseen, extended her sari endlessly.

Arjuna saw this. He had been present, bound by his elder's pledge, watching his wife humiliated by his cousins. *Strīṣu duṣṭāsu vārṣṇeya jāyate varṇa-saṅkaraḥ* — "O descendant of Vṛṣṇi, from women being so afflicted, mixed-up society is born." When he says these words on the chariot, he is remembering the dice-hall. The verse is not theory for him — it is biography. The *kula-dharma* of the Kurus had already failed once, in his own life. The war is the consequence of that earlier failure.
