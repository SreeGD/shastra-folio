Bhagavad-gītā 1.27

Important Words
  • kṛpayā — by compassion
    Instrumental of *kṛpā* — compassion, pity. A divine quality in its purified form (Kṛṣṇa's own *kṛpā* on the devotee). But here Arjuna's *kṛpā* is *bodily compassion* — pity rooted in identification with bodies, not souls. The Gītā distinguishes the two; this verse marks the lower one.
  • parayā — of a high grade
    *Paraḥ* — supreme, highest. Arjuna's compassion is *supreme*-grade — but supreme in *intensity*, not in correctness. The Gītā uses *parā* both for the Lord (*parā prakṛti*, BG 7.5) and, here, for an intensity of misplaced feeling. Intensity alone does not equal truth.
  • āviṣṭaḥ — overwhelmed
    Past participle of *ā-viś* — to enter, to be entered into. Arjuna has been *entered* by compassion as though by a possessing force. Same root as *āveśa* (possession). The Gītā recognizes that even noble emotions can *take possession* of the soul; the discipline is to be moved by them, not occupied.
  • viṣīdan — while lamenting
    Present participle of *vi-sad* — to sink, to despond. Same root as *Viṣāda-yoga*, the title of this very chapter. *Viṣāda* is the lowest psychological state in the Gītā's atlas — the necessary collapse that precedes surrender. The chapter is named for what is happening to Arjuna in this very word.
When the son of Kuntī, Arjuna, saw all these different grades of friends and relatives, he became overwhelmed with compassion and spoke thus.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
27. The son of Kuntī, seeing all his relatives situated there, overcome with compassion and depressed, spoke as follows.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
27. The son of Kuntī, seeing all his relatives situated there, overcome with compassion and depressed, spoke as follows.
Commentary

The merciful Lord of all beings, desiring to deliver the universe through instructions to His dear associate, put Arjuna under illusion, making it appear that his duty to fight was against religious principles, through a distortion of the philosophy of non-violence mentioned in the śruti with mā hiṁsyāt sarva-bhūtāni: do not commit violence against any living entity. [Note: Ahiṁsan sarva bhūtāni is found in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad, 8.15. Na hiṁsyāt sarva bhūtāni is quoted by Rāmānuja in 2.19 and Srīdhara Svāmī in 18.3. na hiṁsyāt sarva bhūtāni maitrayāṇa gataḥ caret is found in Mahābharata, 12.316.18.] By addressing Arjuna as the son of Kuntī (kaunteya), as the son of his father’s sister, thereby suggesting Arjuna’s kṣatriya background, lamentation and bewilderment concering his kṣatriya dharma are here suggested. Because of his extremely compassionate nature (parayā kṛpayā), he showed remorse (viṣīdan). Because of the word krpayā in the instrumental case, there is a suggestion that this mercy is an inherent and perefect quality, emphasized by the word para, superior. Through analyzing the saṅdhi differently, the word aparayā can also be formed. In that case the meaning is “Though he had previously shown compassion for those in his own army, now he also showed different compassion for the members of the opposing army (aparayā kṛpāyā).” In stating that Arjuna talked while lamenting at the same time, it is suggested that while talking he showed the effects of remorse—tears, quivering body and contracted throat.

Study Notes — Gauranga Priya Dāsa
Gauranga Priya Dāsa Study
Bhakti-śāstrī Notes, ISKCON Chowpatty (2009)
Theme

COMMON THEME: Arjuna sees all the relatives assembled on both the sides and thus overwhelmed with

compassion, he starts speaking

Focus of Learning

(a) Different grades of relatives mentioned

Grandfathers Bhisma and Somadatta; Father’s contemporaries like Bhurisrava
Teachers like Dronacharya and Krpacharya; Maternal uncles like Salva and Sakuni
Brothers like Duryodhana; Sons like Laksma
Friends like Asvatthama; Well-wishers like Krtavarma etc.
The armies containing many of his friends

(a) Lord as Hrsikesa – Lord knows Arjuna’s mind and thus knows everything being the Supersoul of all

living entities

(b) Arjuna referred to as ‘Partha’ – Indicates son of aunt ‘Prtha'. Thus Krishna wants to point out:

He became charioteer because Arjuna is son of aunt (Queen Kunti)
Krishna said to Arjuna: “Behold the Kurus” – This phrase indicates Arjuna’s mind “Do you want to stop and not fight?”
Krishna does not expect such cowardly action from the son of his aunt
Mood of friendly joking expressed here.

(a) Arjuna as Gudakesh – one who conquers sleep

Gudaka means sleep; sleep also means ignorance
Arjuna conquered sleep & ignorance because of friendship with Krishna
Nature of the devotee
Cannot forget Krishna even for a moment (Samadhi); even while walking or asleep condition
Devotee conquers sleep and ignorance; because of constant Krishna consciousness. (This is called Samadhi)

(b) Krishna as Hrsikesa – As director of mind and senses, He could understand Arjuna’s purpose in

placing the chariot in the midst of the armies – “hestitation to fight”

Classical Ācāryas
Śrī Śaṅkarācārya Classical
Translation
1.27 Sri Sankaracharya did not comment on this sloka. The commentary starts from 2.10.
Śrī Rāmānujācārya Classical
Translation
1.26 - 1.47 Arjuna said - Sanjaya said Sanjaya continued: The high-minded Arjuna, extremely kind, deeply friendly, and supremely righteous, having brothers like himself, though repeatedly deceived by the treacherous attempts of your people like burning in the lac-house etc., and therefore fit to be killed by him with the help of the Supreme Person, nevertheless said, 'I will not fight.' He felt weak, overcome as he was by his love and extreme compassion for his relatives. He was also filled with fear, not knowing what was righteous and what unrighteous. His mind was tortured by grief, because of the thought of future separation from his relations. So he threw away his bow and arrow and sat on the chariot as if to fast to death.

Personal Guidance
Material compassion — born from bodily identification — is not real compassion; true compassion is rooted in the eternal soul.
Practical Applications
  • Distinguish in your own reactions between sentimental compassion (avoiding others' discomfort) and real compassion (serving their eternal good).
  • Ask: would Prabhupāda call this compassion, or attachment?
  • When you feel pulled to 'protect' someone, check if you are protecting their soul or their body.
Reflection Questions
  • Where is my 'compassion' actually a refusal to do what dharma requires?
  • Whom am I 'sparing' from a truth they need to hear?
Analogy

Compassion (*kṛpā*) is a fine thing — but compassion mixed with confusion is a paralysis disguised as virtue. Arjuna is *overwhelmed* (*āviṣṭa*) by compassion the way a swimmer is overwhelmed by a wave: it does not save him; it takes him under.
