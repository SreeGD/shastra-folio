Bhagavad-gītā 1.45

Important Words
  • apratīkāram — without being resistant
    *A-* (negation) + *prati-kṛ* (to act against, to oppose). "Non-resisting." The same syntactic logic as *ahiṁsā* (non-injury). Arjuna proposes *apratīkāra* as his stance — but the Gītā will distinguish *non-resistance under provocation* (a virtue in śānti circumstances) from *non-resistance in defence of dharma* (a vice, here).
  • aśastram — without being fully equipped
    *A-* + *śastra* (weapon). "Weaponless." Arjuna is proposing the classical ascetic / Jain image: to stand unarmed before the armed. A noble image when the path is *sannyāsa*; an evasion when the path is *kṣatriya-dharma*. The Gītā will later teach that one's *svadharma*, even if bloody, is superior to another's *paradharma*, even if peaceful (BG 3.35).
  • śastra - pāṇayaḥ — those with weapons in hand
    *Śastra* + *pāṇi* (hand) — "weapon-handed." Bahuvrīhi compound. Arjuna stages a tableau: he, weaponless; they, weapon-handed. The image is morally tilted by its very framing. The Gītā will reframe: he is not weaponless either — he has Gāṇḍīva *and* Kṛṣṇa as charioteer. Refusing the weapon is its own kind of pride.
  • kṣema - taram — better
    *Kṣema* — welfare, peace, security. Same root as in *yoga-kṣema* (BG 9.22: "I provide and preserve"). *Kṣema-tara* — comparative — *more peaceful*. Arjuna says his death would be *more conducive to welfare*. The Gītā will eventually take this very word and place it inside Kṛṣṇa's promise — *I* carry the *yoga-kṣema* of the devotee. Self-sacrificed *kṣema* is a poor substitute for surrendered *kṣema*.
Better for me if the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, weapons in hand, were to kill me unarmed and unresisting on the battlefield.
Purport

It is the custom – according to kṣatriya fighting principles – that an unarmed and unwilling foe should not be attacked. Arjuna, however, decided that even if attacked by the enemy in such an awkward position, he would not fight. He did not consider how much the other party was bent upon fighting. All these symptoms are due to soft-heartedness resulting from his being a great devotee of the Lord.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
45. It would be better for me if the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, weapons in hand, were to kill me without weapons and without opposition.
Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa Gauḍīya
Translation
45. It would be better for me if the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, weapons in hand, were to kill me without weapons and without opposition.
Commentary

“Even if you are disinclined to fight, Bhīṣma and others are inclined to fight and will kill you. So what is to be done?” “Not opposing them will be my prāyaścitta for the sin of thinking of killing relatives. This is most beneficial (kṣemataram), removing sin just by this prāyaścitta at the end of life (when they kill me.) And Bhīsma and others will also not incur sin by killing me in this manner.”

Bhūrijana Dāsa Gauḍīya
Translation
Better for me if the sons of Dhrtarastra, weapons in hand, were to kill me unarmed and unresisting on the battlefield. (cn/DS) To beg, for a ksatriya, is not part of his culture. And Arjuna is saying that is better for him to beg and suffer complete humiliation in this life then to have to kill all those kinsmen and then suffer all the reactions for all the sins that will make him suffer for thousands and thousands of years.

SUMMARISED THEME 1A
ARJUNA’S SAINTLINESS REVEALED (1.28 — 1.46)

Arjuna’s saintliness is revealed by the following points:

(a) Softheartedness (1.28)

(b) Does not want to kill even for exchange of three worlds (1.35)

(c) Ready to forgive any aggressors (1.36)

(d) Considers evil consequences even at the risk of refusing obligation to fight (1.37 – 1.38)

(e) Conscious of obligations to protect family traditions and morals (1.39 – 1.44)

He does not think that: “I am transcendental and I have no obligations”
Arjuna being saintly is conscious of moral principles – How he can kill one’s relatives based on selfish motives (1.44)

(f) Ready to be killed unarmed, but not retaliate (1.45)

Custom according to Ksatriya fighting principles – unarmed and unwilling foe should not be attacked
These symptoms are due to softheartedness resulting from being a great devotee

(g) Kept his bow down (even broke his vow of not keeping the bow once lifted) (1.46)

Conclusion: Such a kind and softhearted person is fit to receive self-knowledge (1.46 purport)

Personal Guidance
Arjuna would rather die than violate his sentiment — but dying for sentiment is not the same as dying for dharma.
Practical Applications
  • Do not glorify renunciation that masks an unwillingness to act.
  • Test your own 'I would rather die than do that' statements — what is being protected, sentiment or dharma?
  • Ask Kṛṣṇa to remove the romance from your renunciations and leave only the substance.
Reflection Questions
  • Which of my hard lines are dharmic, and which are merely dramatic?
  • Where am I willing to be 'killed' (criticized, lose status) just to avoid doing what is right?
Analogy

"Better for me to be killed unarmed" is the rhetoric of romance, not of dharma. There is a kind of moral grandstanding in offering one's life on terms one chooses; it can look like saintly non-resistance and yet be a refusal to enter the situation one's birth has placed one in. The Gītā will treat such offers with great gentleness — and with no acceptance.
