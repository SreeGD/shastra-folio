Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.18.6

అగాధతోయహ్రదినీతటోర్మిభి- ర్ద్రవత్పురీష్యా: పులినై: సమన్తత: । న యత్ర చణ్డాంశుకరా విషోల్బణా భువో రసం శాద్వలితం చ గృహ్ణతే ॥ ౬ ॥

Important Words
  • agādha-toya — of unfathomable water
    A+gādha (not-fathomable) + toya (water). Vraja's rivers are not measured. Gauḍīya commentators note that even physical descriptions of Vraja resist measurement — the dhāma overflows its category.
  • caṇḍa-aṁśu-karāḥ — the rays (of the sun whose) rays are fierce
    Caṇḍa (fierce) + aṁśu (ray) + kara (maker/ray). The sun is named only by its fierceness — and even so, named only to be defeated by Vraja's soaked earth.
  • viṣa-ulbaṇāḥ — fierce like poison
    Viṣa (poison) + ulbaṇa (intense). The sun's rays elsewhere are poisonous to the green of the world; in Vraja the green is unkillable. A quiet rejoinder to the Kāliya-viṣa motif that runs through this canto.
  • bhuvaḥ-rasam — the sap of the earth
    Bhū (earth) + rasa (juice, essence). The word rasa is doing double duty — earthly sap and devotional taste. In Vraja the rasa of the earth is itself a participant in the līlā.
  • śādvalitam — the greenness
    From śādvala — greensward. The grass that survives the poison-sun is the same grass on which Kṛṣṇa later sits to take His midday meal with the sakhās. The chapter is laying the picnic-cloth.
With their flowing waves the deep rivers drenched their banks, making them damp and muddy. Thus the rays of the sun, which were as fierce as poison, could not evaporate the earth’s sap or parch its green grass.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
With their flowing waves the deep rivers drenched their banks, making them damp and muddy. Thus the rays of the sun, which were as fierce as poison, could not evaporate the earth's sap or parch its green grass.
Commentary

One reason for absence of the heat of the sun was mentioned previously: the tall trees giving shade. This verse mentions another reason. The sun could not dry up the mud (purisyah) on the bank of the deep flowing rivers mixed with the green grass.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Why is the grass green? Since the water is deep, the river always creates waves and tends to be broad. Purīṣyāḥ is a feminine form of purīṣa (mud) following Pāṇini 4.1.42. Because the many banks, and because the waves touched the shores of the deep lakes, the fierce rays of the sun could remove the muddy earth’s moisture, which produced grass. Or, because the banks are made muddy by the waves touching the shores of the deep lakes, the fierce rays of the sun could remove the earth’s moisture, with grass. The word samantataḥ (everywhere) should be understood for second part of the verse as well (the banks were everywhere and the damp earth was everywhere). This happens throughout Vṛndāvana because the lakes are plentiful.

Analogy

The rivers wet the banks deeply enough that the sun's poison-rays cannot drink the green dry — bhakti soaks the heart deeply enough that the heat of saṁsāra cannot evaporate one's taste. The defence is not a shield against the sun; it is saturation from below.
