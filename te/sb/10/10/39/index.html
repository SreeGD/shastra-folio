Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.10.39

Important Words
  • gokula-īśvaraḥ — the master of Gokula
    Compound: Gokula + īśvara. Kṛṣṇa is Lord even in His most intimate Vraja-līlā. Gauḍīya: aiśvarya and mādhurya coexist; the gopīs feel mādhurya predominantly, but the title īśvara is never relinquished.
  • dāmnā — by the rope
    Instrumental of dāman, 'rope.' The source of the name Dāmodara. The rope is a piece of household twine; the bound one is the cause of all causes. Gauḍīya: this is the supreme image of Kṛṣṇa being controlled by His devotee's love.
  • baddhaḥ — bound
    Past participle from √bandh. The same word used for the conditioned soul's bondage — turned upside down. The Lord of bondage is the bound One; the rope-binder is the devotee. Mādhurya inverts the cosmology.
  • prahasan — smiling
    Present participle from pra-√has, 'to smile widely.' Kṛṣṇa smiles because the binding is His own play. Gauḍīya: every līlā has hāsya as undertone in mādhurya — the Lord's delight in being constrained by His devotee's affection.
Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: The two young demigods thus offered prayers to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Although Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Godhead, is the master of all and was certainly Gokuleśvara, the master of Gokula, He was bound to the wooden mortar by the ropes of the gopīs, and therefore, smiling widely, He spoke to the sons of Kuvera the following words.
Purport

Kṛṣṇa was smiling because He was thinking to Himself, “These two young demigods fell from the higher planetary system to this planet, and I have delivered them from the bondage of standing for a long time as trees, but as for Me, I am bound by the ropes of the gopīs and am subject to their chastisements.” In other words, Kṛṣṇa submits to being chastised and bound by the gopīs because of pure love and affection worthy of being praised by a devotee in so many ways.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: The two young demigods thus offered prayers to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Although Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Godhead, is the master of all and was certainly Gokuleśvara, the master of Gokula, He was bound to the wooden mortar by the ropes of the gopīs, and therefore, smiling widely, He spoke to the sons of Kuvera the following words.
Commentary

Sankirtitah means completely glorified. Damna ca hints at being bound not only by ropes, but by prema also. Prahasan means that he smiled very sweetly, thinking these two minor devatas have been bound up by his maya. "They are praising me for freeing them from this bondage. But mother Yasoda binds me up and scolds me instead of praising me. Still out of love I stay in Gokula. The love I have for the scolding by the inhabitants of Vraja is much more than the praises I receive from these sons of Kuvera."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Laughing, the lord of Gokula spoke to the two sons of Kuvera. Referring to their previous state, the Lord showed their great fortune. He smiled because, though he was glorified by the two as the Lord, he was still bound to the mortar by a rope. First of all he was bound. But he was bound by a mere rope, and to a mortar among all things! The two in fear did not laugh. Seeing them in that condition he laughed. He rules over Gokula and as the cause of our good fortune (bhagavān) is eternally performing pastimes in Gokula because of being controlled by the prema of his devotees. Gokula should be understood to have the highest qualities.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Though he was lord of Gokula, and was bound to the mortar, because he was affectionate to the devotees, he spoke to the two. Or though he was absorbed in playing in Gokula (ca), he spoke to them. Or though giving mercy solely to the people of Gokula, and though bound by the mortar, he spoke to them, because he appeared to give benefit to all people (bhagavān). Or as lord of Gokula, taking birth there, it was proper to speak even to the two, because he was controlled by the people of Gokula, even (ca) being tied up by them. He did not even free himself, because he was having fun playing in Gokula (gokuleśvaraḥ). He was moreover the essence of all unlimited powers. By the mercy of Nārada the two were most fortunate. His laugh was natural. Or he laughed because of the mercy of Nārada on their previous condition. Or he laughed in joy on hearing their praises. Or he laughed in amusement, on showing himself bound up. He was not being deceptive in laughing. Another meaning is he is lord of all the senses (gokuleśvaraḥ). By his power, he can give bhakti to all the senses. Thus the prayer of the two would be fulfilled.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa, master of Gokula, lets Himself be bound by the gopīs' ropes — pure love conquers the Lord whom no power can bind.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where you try to 'control' Kṛṣṇa through bargaining or demand, and instead offer one act of simple, ropelike affection — a chant, a prayer, a service done without leverage.
  • Speak to someone you love with the bhāva of a gopī — small, tender, unselfconscious — rather than with the calculation of a worshipper seeking returns.
  • Refuse to reduce Kṛṣṇa to an impersonal Absolute today; address Him by name in your sādhana as Gokuleśvara, the personal Lord who submits to His devotees' love.
Analogy

A young king ties up his own father's hand in a game of dice and the father lets him — the strength of the bound man is far greater than the rope, but the rope is held by his beloved, and so it holds. Kṛṣṇa is Gokuleśvara, Lord of all Gokula — and He is bound to a mortar by Yaśodā's rope. Love's geometry: the supreme is constrained by the small, because He chooses to be.

Stories
Yaśodā's rope and the two-finger excess

Yaśodā tried to bind the naughty Kṛṣṇa to the mortar; her rope fell two fingers short. She added another, and another — every rope was always two fingers short. SB 10.9 records this exquisitely.

Finally, seeing her exhaustion, Kṛṣṇa allowed Himself to be bound. The 'two fingers' Gauḍīya ācāryas read as vidhi and bhāva — both are needed; rules alone never reach Him, but rules paired with the gopī's love do. SB 10.10.39 closes the curtain on this scene: the Lord of Gokula speaks while bound, smiling — because the bondage is His own choice and His own pleasure.
