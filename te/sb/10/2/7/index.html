Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.7

Important Words
  • devi — O worshipable one
    The Lord addresses His own energy as 'Devī' — worshipable. The Gauḍīya reading: śakti is to be honored, not bypassed. Even those who go directly to Kṛṣṇa must reverence Yoga-māyā, His arranger.
  • vrajam — to Vraja
    Vraja is named here for the first time in Canto 10 — the holy land of mādhurya-līlā. The Six Gosvāmīs spent their lives recovering this place. The verse's geography is the geography of the highest rasa.
  • bhadre — O auspicious one
    Bhadrā — auspicious, bringing welfare. Yoga-māyā is by nature maṅgala; she does not bewilder devotees as mahā-māyā does — she arranges their union with the Lord.
  • gopa - gobhiḥ — with cowherds and cows
    The compound names Vraja's two essential ornaments: gopas and gos. Prabhupāda often notes that Kṛṣṇa's descent is incomplete without cows — they are not livestock but eternal participants in His pastime.
  • nanda - gokule — in Nanda's Gokula
    Gokula — 'the abode of cows.' The Caitanya tradition holds Goloka-Vṛndāvana as the eternal spiritual residence of Kṛṣṇa; Gokula is its earthly manifestation. The Lord is sending His potency to His own home.
The Lord ordered Yoga-māyā: O My potency, who are worshipable for the entire world and whose nature is to bestow good fortune upon all living entities, go to Vraja, where there live many cowherd men and their wives. In that very beautiful land, where many cows reside, Rohiṇī, the wife of Vasudeva, is living at the home of Nanda Mahārāja. Other wives of Vasudeva are also living there incognito because of fear of Kaṁsa. Please go there.
Purport

Nanda-gokula, the residence of King Nanda, was itself very beautiful, and when Yoga-māyā was ordered to go there and encourage the devotees with fearlessness, it became even more beautiful and safe. Because Yoga-māyā had the ability to create such an atmosphere, the Lord ordered her to go to Nanda-gokula.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The Lord ordered Yogamāyā: O My potency, who are worshipable for the entire world and whose nature is to bestow good fortune upon all living entities, go to Vraja, where there live many cowherd men and their wives. In that very beautiful land, where many cows reside, Rohiṇī, the wife of Vasudeva, is living at the home of Nanda Mahārāja. Other wives of Vasudeva are also living there incognito because of fear of Kaṁsa. Please go there.
Commentary

After Devaki’s six children were killed, Vasudeva, seeing Rohini pregnant, sent her secretly to Gokula. Others also, being afraid of Kamsa (kamsa samvigna), lived in secret places (vivaresu—literally holes).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

O Devī, worshipped by the world, full of all auspiciousness! He addresses her in this way to inspire her. Or the words show her qualification for going to Vraja. The other words also are meant to inspire her. The cowherds and cows are most astonishing, unimaginable by her. Other wives of Vasudeva were also living there.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa Himself dispatches Yoga-māyā to Vraja so His devotees may live in fearless beauty — His protection precedes our awareness of it.
Practical Applications
  • Notice one place in your day where fear has quietly settled in, and consciously remember that Kṛṣṇa has already arranged shelter for those who take refuge in Him.
  • Speak to one devotee today as Vasudeva's wives lived in Nanda-gokula — incognito, sheltered, sustained by association rather than by external safety.
  • Refuse to treat the world's threats as the final word; before sleep tonight, name one 'Kaṁsa' in your life and entrust it to the Lord's potency rather than your own strategy.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like Vasudeva's other wives, hiding in 'caves' out of fear of some Kaṁsa — a person, an outcome, a worldly threat — instead of trusting Kṛṣṇa's arrangement to place me where I truly need to be?
  • If Yoga-māyā is the one who makes a place safe and beautiful for devotional service, what 'Nanda-gokula' am I being asked to relocate to today — what setting, association, or sādhana — that I keep postponing because I'd rather engineer my own security?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your friend group the way Krishna audited Vraja: who are the 'gopas and cows' — the unglamorous, steady people who make your life spiritually safe — and who are the Kaṁsa-figures whose presence keeps you living incognito, hiding the parts of you that actually want to chant, study, or behave well? This week, spend one more hour with the first group and one less with the second.
  • Notice where you are 'living in vivareṣu' — hiding your sādhana inside your own life out of fear of being mocked on the group chat or in the dorm. Pick one small practice (a tulasī mālā, a verse on your lock screen, declining a party for an ārati) that you stop hiding this week. Watch what fear surfaces; that fear is your Kaṁsa.
  • Before your next exam or big submission, do what Krishna did: send Yoga-māyā ahead. That means preparing the environment (clean desk, phone in another room, one round of japa) before you sit down — not relying on willpower in a hostile setting. Real vairāgya at your age is environmental, not heroic.
householder
  • Treat your home as Nanda-gokula in miniature: ask whether it is currently 'gopa-gobhir alaṅkṛtam' — adorned by simple, devotional things (kitchen prasāda, a clean altar, regular guests who chant) — or adorned by screens, clutter, and ambient anxiety. Pick one room this month and re-decorate it according to the verse's standard, not the catalog's.
  • Identify the Kaṁsa in your family's life — the pressure (career ladder, school rankings, in-laws' expectations, mortgage fear) that has your spouse and children quietly 'living incognito,' hiding their devotional inclinations to keep peace. Have one direct conversation this week naming it, and decide together which fear you will stop organizing your household around.
  • Before launching the next big family decision (move, school change, job switch), explicitly invite Yoga-māyā first: a week of extra japa, consultation with a senior devotee, and an honest check on whether the move serves the soul or only the body. Do not confuse providing for your children's bodies with protecting their souls — the Gītā's whole pivot applies inside your own house.
senior
  • Krishna chose to descend in a cowherd village, not a capital — and you are now in the season of life where you can actually choose the same. Begin loosening one attachment to status, property, or position this quarter, not as renunciation theater but as a real handover, so that what remains of your life is increasingly 'gopa-gobhir alaṅkṛtam' rather than résumé-shaped.
  • Become Yoga-māyā for someone younger: go ahead of them and prepare the ground. That means a phone call to a struggling grandchild, a letter to a former colleague, an introduction between two devotees who should know each other. At your stage, the most important work is no longer your own arrival but arranging the safe village others will arrive into.
  • Sit with the fact that Vasudeva's wives lived in Vraja in fear of Kaṁsa — and that Kaṁsa, in your life, is now mortality itself. Where are you still 'living incognito,' postponing the hard conversations, the unsaid forgiveness, the explicit instruction to your children about what to do with your body and your dharma when you go? Name one such postponement and address it this month, under Yoga-māyā's shelter, with dhairya rather than denial.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading Yoga-māyā as a vague impersonal 'positive energy' or 'universe vibe,' rather than as Kṛṣṇa's personal potency acting under His direct order — collapsing the personalist line into generic spirituality.
  • Romanticizing Vraja as a faraway mythic landscape and missing the practical point: that the Lord himself arranges shelter and association for surrendered devotees here and now, in concrete circumstances.
  • Mistaking the wives' 'hiding incognito' for mere fearfulness and dismissing it, instead of recognizing strategic vairāgya — withdrawing from a Kaṁsa-dominated environment until Kṛṣṇa's arrangement matures — and so refusing to step back from toxic settings in one's own life when prudence calls for it.
Discussion Prompts
  • Krishna sends Yoga-māyā ahead to prepare Nanda-gokula as a place of safety and beauty before the Lord's own descent. Where in your life have you tried to do Krishna's work without first letting His potency prepare the ground — and what broke as a result? Push back on each other if someone's answer sounds like spiritualized procrastination.
  • Vasudeva's other wives are living incognito in Vraja out of fear of Kaṁsa — yet that hiding is precisely what places them under Yoga-māyā's shelter. Is there a difference between cowardly hiding and protective concealment in spiritual life? Try to disagree before you agree.
  • The verse calls Vraja 'beautiful' specifically because of cowherds and cows, not because of palaces or wealth. If you had to name the equivalent 'gopa-gobhir alaṅkṛtam' of your own daily environment — the simple, unglamorous thing that actually makes a place spiritually safe — what would it be, and what does your answer reveal about what you currently value?
Role Play

A devotee couple has just received a job offer that would move them from a small Vaiṣṇava community (think: temple within walking distance, prasāda four nights a week, children running between four families' homes) to a much higher-paying role in a city where the nearest sādhu-saṅga is a two-hour drive. They sit down with a senior gṛhastha for advice. One person plays the husband (drawn to the salary, framing it as 'serving Krishna better with more resources'); one plays the wife (uneasy, citing this verse — that Krishna Himself chose Nanda-gokula for its cowherds and cows, not for opulence); one plays the senior devotee. After ten minutes, swap roles so the 'husband' must now defend the wife's position. The teacher then asks: where did each of you confuse material compassion (income, schools, safety) with real shelter under Yoga-māyā?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just mythology dressed up as instruction. Krishna 'sending' a goddess to 'prepare' a village reads like a fairy tale — what does it actually have to do with my life?
    Treat the verse as a description of how reality is arranged, not a bedtime story. Krishna acts through His potencies before He acts directly — the safe place exists before the Lord arrives in it. In your own life, notice that the conditions for serious sādhana are usually arranged for you long before you recognize them: the friend who first mentioned a verse, the illness that finally slowed you down, the disappointment that emptied a room in your heart. That is Yoga-māyā at work. You can either cooperate with that arrangement or insist on building your own Vraja out of ahaṅkāra and wonder why it never feels safe.
  • If Krishna is all-powerful, why does He need Yoga-māyā at all? Why the elaborate machinery — sending a potency, hiding wives, arranging incognito living — instead of just protecting devotees directly?
    The 'machinery' is the point, not a workaround. Krishna's relationships are personal, and personal relationships require distinct persons — a potency who acts, devotees who fear and are reassured, a Vraja that can be made beautiful. An impersonal Absolute would simply zap the problem; Krishna instead orchestrates a līlā in which Yoga-māyā, Rohiṇī, Nanda, and the cows each have a real role. This is the difference the Gauḍīya line insists on: surrender is not absorption into a featureless light, it is entering a living arrangement. Your sādhana, too, is meant to take place inside that arrangement — not in some private direct line that bypasses guru, devotees, and the protective veil His potency draws around the practitioner.
Analogy

Before the king's coronation, a herald is sent ahead to Vraja to prepare the cowherd village. Yoga-māyā is the herald; Rohiṇī's bed in Nanda's house is the prepared throne; the cows and the cowherds are the courtiers already gathered. The whole stage is set before the king moves.
