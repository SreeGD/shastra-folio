Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.14

Thus instructed by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Yoga-māyā immediately agreed. With the Vedic mantra om, she confirmed that she would do what He asked. Thus having accepted the order of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, she circumambulated Him and started for the place on earth known as Nanda-gokula. There she did everything just as she had been told.
Purport

After receiving the orders of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Yoga-māyā twice confirmed her acceptance by saying, “Yes, sir, I shall do as You order,” and then saying om. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura comments that om signifies Vedic confirmation. Thus Yoga-māyā very faithfully received the Lord’s order as a Vedic injunction. It is a fact that whatever is spoken by the Supreme Personality of Godhead is a Vedic injunction that no one should neglect. In Vedic injunctions there are no mistakes, illusions, cheating or imperfection. Unless one understands the authority of the Vedic version, there is no purpose in quoting śāstra. No one should violate the Vedic injunctions. Rather, one should strictly execute the orders given in the Vedas. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (16.24) : tasmāc chāstraṁ pramāṇaṁ te kāryākārya-vyavasthitau jñātvā śāstra-vidhānoktaṁ karma kartum ihārhasi “One should understand what is duty and what is not duty by the regulations of the scriptures. Knowing such rules and regulations, one should act so that one may gradually be elevated.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Thus instructed by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Yogamāyā immediately agreed. With the Vedic mantra oṁ, she confirmed that she would do what He asked. Thus having accepted the order of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, she circumambulated Him and started for the place on earth known as Nanda-gokula. There she did everything just as she had been told.
Commentary

Saying "tatha iti" and then "om iti" is a sign of respect for the Lord. First she accepted the words, then she did parikrama , and then went to the earth (gam), one act following the other.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

She acted according to what the Lord said (tathā), not deviating at all.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

She obeyed because he was the supreme Lord (bhagavatā). She circumambulated him to fulfill his order. She acted according to what the Lord said (tathā), not deviating at all.

Concepts
Mantra
Personal Guidance
Receive Kṛṣṇa's word as Yoga-māyā did — with om and immediate execution; His instruction is itself the Vedic injunction, free of flaw.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one direct instruction from śāstra or guru you have been softening, and execute it today exactly as given — no edits, no delay.
  • Before acting on any opinion or trend, test it against śāstra-pramāṇa; refuse to treat your preference as equal in authority to Kṛṣṇa's word.
  • When you receive an order in your sva-dharma today (from guru, scripture, or duty), answer inwardly with 'tathā' and then circumambulate it with full attention before beginning.
Reflection Questions
  • When the Lord — through guru, śāstra, or sādhu — gives me a clear instruction, where do I delay, negotiate, or 'think it over' instead of responding with Yoga-māyā's immediate 'tathā' and 'om'?
  • What scriptural injunction do I currently treat as optional or open to interpretation, and what would change today if I received it as a Vedic order containing no mistake, illusion, or imperfection?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a teacher, parent, or senior devotee gives you a clear instruction this week — about study, screen time, or company — practice Yoga-māyā's two-stage acceptance: first say it back ('yes, I will do X by Y'), then actually start within the hour. Notice how often you skip stage two.
  • Pick one śāstric instruction you've been quietly editing to fit your social life — maybe avoiding intoxication at a party, not gossiping about a classmate, or finishing your japa before opening Instagram. For seven days, treat it as a non-negotiable Vedic injunction, not a suggestion. Journal the resistance.
  • Yoga-māyā circumambulated Kṛṣṇa before acting. Before your next exam or important task, take 60 seconds to formally offer it — not as a vague 'good vibes' gesture, but as a deliberate act of placing the work under His order. See if the quality of your effort changes.
householder
  • Identify one family decision currently in limbo — a move, a school choice, a financial commitment, a difficult conversation with a parent. Bring it to śāstra and to a senior devotee, get a clear direction, and then execute it with Yoga-māyā's tathā-oṁ within a defined timeframe. Stop polling for a fifth opinion.
  • Audit your weekly schedule against the orders you've actually received — from guru, from scripture, from the duties of gṛhastha-āśrama. Where are you treating Kṛṣṇa's instructions as flexible while treating your work calendar as fixed? Reverse one such inversion this month.
  • When you give an instruction to your child or a junior at work, model the acceptance you want from them: visibly accept a small instruction from your own spouse or senior with the same fullness you expect. Children learn the verse by watching you live it, not by hearing you quote it.
senior
  • At this stage the orders coming to you are increasingly subtle — a doctor's restriction, a body's limitation, a scripture's call to vairāgya and Kṛṣṇa-smaraṇa. Pick the one you have been most resisting and accept it this week with the formality of Yoga-māyā: spoken acceptance, then immediate action.
  • Mentor one younger devotee specifically on the discipline of receiving instruction. Share a story (truthfully) of an order from your guru or śāstra that you initially negotiated with, and what the cost of the delay was. Your honest history is more useful to them than your polished advice.
  • Use the loosening of worldly responsibility to deepen Vedic obedience, not to relax it. Set a daily sādhana — japa rounds, Bhāgavatam reading, a fixed hour of seva — and treat it as a non-negotiable injunction from Kṛṣṇa Himself. The body's decline is the Lord's instruction to redirect; receive it with oṁ, not with grief.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Yoga-māyā's instant acceptance as blind obedience and using that as an excuse to suspend discrimination toward unauthorized 'orders' — forgetting that her surrender was specifically to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, not to any voice claiming authority.
  • Quoting śāstra selectively to win arguments while privately ignoring the injunctions that inconvenience us — the very 'no purpose in quoting śāstra' Prabhupāda warns against.
  • Mistaking circumambulation, chanting om, and ritual confirmation for actual execution — Yoga-māyā went to Gokula and 'did everything just as she had been told,' but we often stop at the 'yes, sir' and never reach the doing.
Discussion Prompts
  • Yoga-māyā says 'tathā' and then 'oṁ' — a double confirmation before she even moves. Share with your partner one instruction from your guru, parent, or scripture that you 'agreed' to but never actually executed. What was the gap between your tathā and your oṁ?
  • Prabhupāda says 'whatever is spoken by the Supreme Personality of Godhead is a Vedic injunction that no one should neglect.' Where in your life do you quietly negotiate with śāstra — keeping the parts that fit your lifestyle and softening the parts that don't? Be specific; don't let your group settle for a generic answer.
  • Yoga-māyā circumambulates Kṛṣṇa before leaving — she does not just nod and walk off. Discuss: what would it look like to add a parikramā-like ritual of acceptance to the orders you receive (from guru, śāstra, even your boss)? Does anyone in the group think this is performative rather than substantive — and why?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a senior devotee who has just been asked by their temple president to relocate to another city to help open a new preaching center — a clear, direct instruction with scriptural and guru sanction. Student A has career plans, a comfortable apartment, and a circle of friends here. Student B is Student A's close godbrother/godsister, sitting with them the evening after the request was made. Student B's job is NOT to convince or comfort, but to surface — using this verse — the difference between Yoga-māyā's tathā-oṁ acceptance and Student A's actual internal response. Run for 7 minutes, then switch. Debrief: where did the conversation drift toward 'finding a compromise' and away from the verse's standard?

Objections & Responses
  • Yoga-māyā is a divine personality with no material conditioning. It's unrealistic — even unfair — to hold ordinary conditioned souls to her standard of instant, unquestioning acceptance. Doesn't this verse just describe a transcendental transaction that has nothing to do with us?
    It is precisely because she is liberated that she is shown to us — she is the model, not the exception. The Bhāgavatam does not record her acceptance to entertain us; it records it so that we may calibrate our own response. Yes, our acceptance will be halting, partial, mixed with doubt — but the *direction* must be hers. The Gauḍīya path does not ask us to already be Yoga-māyā; it asks us to stop pretending that our negotiation with the Lord's order is somehow more sophisticated than her simple oṁ. Begin by noticing the gap, not by excusing it.
  • 'No one should violate the Vedic injunctions' sounds like religious authoritarianism. In a world where scriptures have been used to justify caste oppression, gender hierarchy, and worse, isn't blind acceptance of śāstra dangerous? Shouldn't we test every injunction against reason and conscience?
    The verse Prabhupāda quotes — tasmāc chāstraṁ pramāṇaṁ te — does not abolish discrimination; it locates it. Reason is the instrument by which we understand śāstra, not the tribunal that overrules it. The Gauḍīya teacher would point out two things. First, what gets called 'śāstra' in the abuses you mention is usually smṛti misapplied, cultural accretion, or self-interest dressed in Sanskrit — not the direct words of Kṛṣṇa, which are what this purport is about. Second, the modern alternative — every individual conscience as final authority — has produced its own catastrophes. The question is not whether to accept an authority above your own preference, but which one. Yoga-māyā chose Kṛṣṇa. The verse invites you to examine what you have chosen.
