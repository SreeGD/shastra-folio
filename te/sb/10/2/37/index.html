Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.37

Important Words
  • śṛṇvan — hearing
    Present participle of √śru. Śravaṇa is the first of the nine processes of bhakti (SB 7.5.23). The Gauḍīyas place hearing first because the holy name and the Bhāgavatam enter the heart through the ear.
  • gṛṇan — chanting
    From √gṝ — 'to praise, to chant.' Kīrtana, the second of the nine processes. The verse pairs the two ears-and-mouth processes that Caitanya elevated to yuga-dharma.
  • saṁsmarayan — causing-remembrance
    Causative present participle — 'making others remember.' Not just smaraṇa (one's own remembrance) but saṁsmaraṇa (causing remembrance). The verse honors the preacher-mood, which Caitanya took as the highest service.
  • nāmāni — the holy names
    Plural — many names of the Lord. Caitanya's Śikṣāṣṭaka opens with this plurality: nāmnām akāri bahudhā. The verse silently encodes the future yuga-dharma of nāma-saṅkīrtana.
  • kalpate — is fit (not, here)
    From √kḷp — 'to be arranged for.' Negated: na bhavāya kalpate — 'is not fit for material existence.' The devotee absorbed in nāma-rūpa is technically no longer eligible for saṁsāra. Eligibility-theology stated in passive voice.
Even while engaged in various activities, devotees whose minds are completely absorbed at Your lotus feet, and who constantly hear, chant, contemplate and cause others to remember Your transcendental names and forms, are always on the transcendental platform, and thus they can understand the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
Purport

How bhakti-yoga can be practiced is explained in this verse. Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has said that anyone who has dedicated his life to the service of the Lord ( īhā yasya harer dāsye ) by his activities, his mind and his words ( karmaṇā manasā girā ) may stay in any condition of life ( nikhilāsv apy avasthāsu ) and yet is no longer actually conditioned but is liberated ( jīvan-muktaḥ sa ucyate ). Even though such a devotee is in a material body, he has nothing to do with this body, for he is transcendentally situated. Nārāyaṇa-parāḥ sarve na kutaścana bibhyati: because a devotee is engaged in transcendental activities, he is not afraid of being materially embodied. ( Bhāg. 6.17.28 ) Illustrating this liberated position, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu prayed, mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi: “All I want is Your causeless devotional service in My life, birth after birth.” Even if a devotee, by the supreme will of the Lord, takes birth in this material world, he continues his devotional service. When King Bharata made a mistake and in his next life became a deer, his devotional service did not stop, although some slight chastisement was given to him because of his negligence. Nārada Muni says that even if one falls from the platform of devotional service, he is not lost, whereas nondevotees are lost entirely because they are not engaged in service. Bhagavad-gītā (9.14) therefore recommends that one always engage at least in chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra: satataṁ kīrtayanto māṁ yatantaś ca dṛḍha-vratāḥ namasyantaś ca māṁ bhaktyā nitya-yuktā upāsate “Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination, bowing down before Me, the great souls perpetually worship Me with devotion.” One should not give up the process of devotional service, which is performed in nine different ways ( śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṁ pāda-sevanam, etc.). The most important process is hearing ( śravaṇam ) from the guru, sādhu and śāstra — the spiritual master, the saintly ācāryas and the Vedic literature. Sādhu-śāstra-guru-vākya, cittete kariyā aikya . We should not hear the commentaries and explanations of nondevotees, for this is strictly forbidden by Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī, who quotes from the Padma Purāṇa: avaiṣṇava-mukhodgīrṇaṁ pūtaṁ hari-kathāmṛtam śravaṇaṁ naiva kartavyaṁ sarpocchiṣṭaṁ yathā payaḥ We should strictly follow this injunction and never try to hear from Māyāvādīs, impersonalists, voidists, politicians or so-called scholars. Strictly avoiding such inauspicious association, we should simply hear from pure devotees. Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī therefore recommends, śrī-guru-padāśrayaḥ: one must seek shelter at the lotus feet of a pure devotee who can be one’s guru. Caitanya Mahāprabhu advises that a guru is one who strictly follows the instructions of Bhagavad-gītā: yare dekha tare kaha ‘kṛṣṇa’-upadeśa (Cc. Madhya 7.128). A juggler, a magician or one who speaks nonsense as an academic career is not a guru. Rather, a guru is one who presents Bhagavad-gītā, Kṛṣṇa’s instructions, as it is. Śravaṇa is very important; one must hear from the Vaiṣṇava sādhu, guru and śāstra. The word kriyāsu, meaning “by manual labor” or “by work,” is important in this verse. One should engage in practical service to the Lord. In our Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement, all our activities are concentrated upon distributing Kṛṣṇa literature. This is very important. One may approach any person and induce him to read Kṛṣṇa literature so that in the future he also may become a devotee. Such activities are recommended in this verse. Kriyāsu yas tvac-caraṇāravindayoḥ . Such activities will always remind the devotees of the Lord’s lotus feet. By fully concentrating on distributing books for Kṛṣṇa, one is fully absorbed in Kṛṣṇa. This is samādhi.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Even while engaged in various activities, devotees whose minds are completely absorbed at Your lotus feet, and who constantly hear, chant, contemplate and cause others to remember Your transcendental names and forms, are always on the transcendental platform, and thus they can understand the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
Commentary

This verse points out that practice of hearing, glorifying and remembering the name and form of the Lord is the means for realization of the Lord. The word kriyasu indicates that even in the midst of matters relating to ones material body, the devotee is not affected by the material world, but remains deeply absorbed in the happiness of his realization of the Lord.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“If these actions are the cause of realization, why do they not produce realization from the beginning?” There are impressions of material addiction, nāma aparādhas, which act as obstacles. But these disappear by constant practice of the actions. That is explained in this verse. Varieties in each of the processes should be understood. The names and forms are considered auspicious (maṅgalāni) because they destroy all suffering and produce the highest bliss. Thus the processes are by their nature the highest goal. One who is situated in his unlimited duties, by becoming absorbed in your lotus feet, goes beyond samṣāra. Or, the person who hears, chants and remembers your auspicious names and forms, becoming absorbed in these devotional acts at your lotus feet, or becomes absorbed in your pastimes, is beyond saṁsāra. Instead of yas tvac sometimes yuṣmad (your) is seen. This person is beyond saṁsāra. Though saṁsāra exists, he is not affected by it. He has no desire for it.

Personal Guidance
Hear, chant, remember, and help others remember Kṛṣṇa's names and forms — even mid-task, a mind fixed at His lotus feet stays transcendental.
Practical Applications
  • Begin one ordinary task today (cooking, commuting, emailing) by audibly chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, and keep returning the mind to His lotus feet whenever it drifts.
  • Hear hari-kathā only from a bona fide Vaiṣṇava sādhu, guru, or śāstra today — refuse podcasts, commentaries, or videos by Māyāvādīs, impersonalists, or so-called scholars.
  • Engage in kriyā — concrete service — by handing one piece of Kṛṣṇa literature to someone, or reading aloud from Bhāgavatam to a family member, so others too remember His names.
Reflection Questions
  • Across the activities that fill my day — work, errands, meals, conversations — where does my consciousness actually rest, and what would it take to relocate it at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet without abandoning the activity itself?
  • Which sources am I currently allowing to shape my hearing about Kṛṣṇa — guru, sādhu, śāstra, or the commentaries of non-devotees, impersonalists, and so-called scholars — and what specifically must I stop ingesting to protect my śravaṇa?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your feed for one week. Every account, podcast, and 'spiritual influencer' you consume — ask: would Sanātana Gosvāmī call this milk, or milk touched by the serpent? Unfollow at least three sources that fail the test, even ones your friends quote.
  • During exam prep or a long study session, set a quiet rule: every time you switch tabs or take a break, chant one round of Hare Kṛṣṇa before scrolling. Make the kriyā of studying itself the place where smaraṇam lives, instead of waiting for a 'free' moment that never comes.
  • Instead of debating Kṛṣṇa consciousness with skeptical friends, give one of them a book — physically, in their hand. Prabhupāda calls book distribution samādhi. Your hostel room or college campus is the field; one book given with sincerity outranks an hour of clever argument.
householder
  • Pick one weekly family activity — cooking, the school commute, paying bills — and convert it explicitly into a kriyā at His lotus feet: chant aloud during it, or discuss one line of śāstra with your spouse or child while doing it. Stop pretending sādhana lives only on the cushion at 5 a.m.
  • Examine where your family's money and time go for 'spiritual content' — courses, retreats, books, streaming teachers. Apply the avaiṣṇava-mukhodgīrṇa test rigorously and redirect that budget toward guru, sādhu, and śāstra in the paramparā. Your children are watching what you actually fund.
  • Take on one concrete book-distribution commitment as a household — even one book a month, given to a neighbor, a colleague, a relative. Let your children see you do it. This is the kriyā Prabhupāda places at the center; do not outsource it to the temple.
senior
  • Reorganize your daily routine so that the activities still in your hands — walking, cooking for grandchildren, phone calls, doctor visits — become explicit kriyās of smaraṇam and saṅkīrtanam. The body is narrowing its options; let bhakti expand into every remaining one.
  • Mentor a younger devotee in śravaṇam from guru, sādhu, śāstra. Your legacy is not your savings or your stories; it is whether you transmitted the discipline of hearing only from pure sources. Sit with one person weekly and read Prabhupāda's purports aloud together.
  • Meditate daily on Caitanya Mahāprabhu's prayer quoted in the purport — mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi. As mortality comes closer, loosen the grip on 'finishing well' in worldly terms and ask only for uninterrupted devotional service, birth after birth.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating 'absorption at the lotus feet' as an internal mood that excuses withdrawal from kriyā — practical service, manual labor, book distribution — when the verse explicitly unites the two.
  • Assuming that any 'spiritual' input qualifies as śravaṇa, and casually consuming Māyāvādī, impersonalist, or academic commentaries on the same texts, ignoring Sanātana Gosvāmī's warning that this is milk touched by a serpent's lips.
  • Reducing the four practices — śṛṇvan, gṛṇan, saṁsmarayan, cintayan — to private hearing and chanting alone, while neglecting saṁsmarayan (causing others to remember) by failing to share Kṛṣṇa-kathā or Kṛṣṇa literature with the people one meets.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse promises that even in the middle of ordinary kriyās, a devotee absorbed at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet remains transcendental. Share with your partner one specific activity from your week where you tried this — and one where it felt impossible or even fake. Where is the line between genuine absorption and self-deception?
  • Prabhupāda is uncompromising: do not hear hari-kathā from Māyāvādīs, impersonalists, voidists, politicians, or so-called scholars. In an age of podcasts, YouTube ācāryas, and 'spiritual but not religious' commentators, name one source you currently consume that would fail this test — and argue, honestly, whether you are willing to drop it.
  • Prabhupāda equates book distribution with samādhi and calls it the central kriyā of the movement. Do you actually believe distributing Kṛṣṇa literature is equivalent to deep meditation, or does that sound like sectarian inflation? Push back, and let your partner defend the verse.
Role Play

Two students, A and B. A is a serious devotee working a demanding corporate job. B is A's close friend and colleague — sympathetic, intellectually curious, but firmly secular, and a fan of a popular non-devotee 'Gītā teacher' on YouTube whose talks frame Kṛṣṇa as a metaphor for inner consciousness. Over lunch, B says: 'I started listening to him because of you. You should hear his episode on SB 10.2.37 — he says the whole verse is about mindfulness at work.' A must respond in real time, honoring the friendship, without flinching from Sanātana Gosvāmī's injunction (sarpocchiṣṭaṁ yathā payaḥ) and without becoming preachy or dismissive. After 4–5 minutes, swap roles. Then debrief as a group: where did A soften the teaching to keep the friendship? Where did A become harsh and lose the friend? What would actually be śravaṇam from a sādhu in that moment?

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like spiritual bypassing. 'Stay absorbed in Kṛṣṇa while you do anything' becomes a license to keep living a materialistic life and just slap a mental label of 'devotion' on top. How is this different from telling someone to think about God while they keep chasing money, status, and sense gratification?
    The verse is precise about what the absorption is in — tvac-caraṇāravindayoḥ, Your lotus feet — and Prabhupāda is precise about what the kriyās are: śravaṇam, kīrtanam, smaraṇam, and concretely, distributing Kṛṣṇa literature. He does not say 'do anything you like and think of Kṛṣṇa.' He says engage your karmaṇā manasā girā in His service. Bypassing happens when we keep viṣaya untouched and add a Kṛṣṇa-flavored mood; bhakti happens when the activity itself is reorganized around Him. The test is simple: is the activity something you could honestly offer? If not, it is not what the verse is describing.
  • Prabhupāda's hard line — never hear from Māyāvādīs, impersonalists, scholars, politicians — feels like intellectual quarantine. In a pluralist world, refusing to listen to anyone outside the lineage looks like fundamentalism, not devotion.
    Sanātana Gosvāmī's image is not fundamentalism, it is hygiene: milk touched by serpent's lips is still milk, but it now carries poison. The objection assumes the content is neutral and only the source varies; the Gauḍīya position is that hari-kathā transmits the bhāva of the speaker, not just information. A scholar who treats Kṛṣṇa as mythology cannot transmit śraddhā in Him, however eloquent. This is not a refusal to think; it is a refusal to let the most precious organ — śravaṇa — be shaped by those who do not accept what is being heard. We can read widely for many purposes; for the food of the soul, we choose the cook carefully.
Analogy

An apprentice who all day repeats the master's gestures — turning the loom, sweeping the floor, fetching the cloth — at evening discovers he can weave without thinking. The verse names exactly such an apprentice: ears full of the Lord's name, lips remembering His pastimes, hands at their daily work. The transcendental platform arrives without his looking up.

Stories
Mīrā at her loom and her lord

Mīrābāī, Rāṇā's princess of Mewar, did not retreat from her palace duties to attain her Giridhāra Gopāla. She sang to Him while spinning, danced before His Deity between court appearances, and absorbed her mind in Him through poisonings, persecution, and a husband's wrath.

Without naming her, the verse describes her exactly: śṛṇvan, gṛṇan, saṁsmarayan, cintayan — hearing, chanting, causing-remembrance, contemplating — while engaged in kriyās. Her bhakti was inserted into ordinary activity, not subtracted from it. Gauḍīya hagiography honors her as one of the most luminous instances of this verse's promise.
