Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.29

బిభర్షి రూపాణ్యవబోధ ఆత్మాక్షేమాయ లోకస్య చరాచరస్య । సత్త్వోపపన్నాని సుఖావహానిసతామభద్రాణి ముహు: ఖలానామ్ ॥ ౨౯ ॥

Important Words
  • rūpāṇi — forms (plural)
    The plural is doctrinal: the Lord has many forms (Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Nṛsiṁha, Vāmana, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa…), but all are aṁśa or avatāra of svayaṁ-bhagavān Kṛṣṇa. SB 1.3.28 — kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam — controls the reading.
  • avabodhaḥ ātmā — of self-knowing nature
    Avabodha — 'awake, knowing.' Even in His many forms, He remains the same fully-conscious Self. Gauḍīya theology refutes the misreading that avatāras 'forget' their divinity; the verse explicitly preserves their svarūpa-jñāna.
  • sattva - upapannāni — endowed with pure existence
    Sattva here is śuddha-sattva, transcendental existence — not the material guṇa of goodness. Gauḍīyas distinguish carefully: avatāras are not made of even sāttvika-prakṛti; they are made of viśuddha-sattva, beyond all guṇas.
  • satām — of the saintly
    Sat — the truthful, the devotee. The verse classifies humans by their orientation toward Him: those for whom His appearance is sukha (sat) and those for whom it is annihilation (khala). Same event, two opposite mokṣas.
  • abhadrāṇi — inauspicious / annihilations
    Abhadra — non-auspicious, harmful. The verse does not euphemize: for the wicked, the Lord's coming is annihilation. Gauḍīyas read this as mercy too — vināśa as the form of grace appropriate to the asura.
O Lord, You are always in full knowledge, and to bring all good fortune to all living entities, You appear in different incarnations, all of them transcendental to the material creation. When You appear in these incarnations, You are pleasing to the pious and religious devotees, but for nondevotees You are the annihilator.
Purport

This verse explains why the Supreme Personality of Godhead appears as an incarnation again and again. The incarnations of the Supreme Personality of Godhead all function differently, but their main purpose is paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām — to protect the devotees and annihilate the miscreants. Yet even though the duṣkṛtīs, or miscreants, are annihilated, this is ultimately good for them.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Lord, You are always in full knowledge, and to bring all good fortune to all living entities, You appear in different incarnations, all of them transcendental to the material creation. When You appear in these incarnations, You are pleasing to the pious and religious devotees, but for nondevotees You are the annihilator.
Commentary

In this verse they explain that the Lord has many avataras such as Matsya and Kurma. He has forms of pure knowledge (avabodhah). These forms are all pure sattva (sattvopapannani). They destroy the wicked (khalanam) and protect the devoted (satam).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

You, the basic form (avabodha ātmā), the son of Devakī, manifest many forms such as the puruṣas authorized by the Vedas through śuddha-sattva (sattvena) endowed with sac-cid-ānanda, and you also manifest the material universe. This means that because you appear through your own śakti in the form of Devakī, even though you are her son, you are not impeded by matter. You do this for the benefit of the people by protecting the pious and punishing the demons. You stop the demons from performing sinful actions. The pious are those who follow dharma and the wicked are those who do not follow. Or, though you are the son of Devakī, the purpose of the forms mentioned in the previous verse is explained in this verse. If all these other forms carry out perfect action, what can be said of your form as Svayam Bhagavān?

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa descends in sattva-laden incarnations to gladden devotees and annihilate the wicked — and even that annihilation is His mercy.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where Kṛṣṇa's apparent 'cutting down' of your false plans is actually His protection — and thank Him for the loss.
  • Read aloud one pastime of an avatāra (Nṛsiṁha, Rāma, or Kṛṣṇa) and identify the sādhu being protected and the duṣkṛta being removed within your own heart.
  • Refuse the impersonalist reflex that treats God as formless force — speak to Kṛṣṇa today as the personal Lord who takes form for your welfare.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I quietly hoping Kṛṣṇa will arrange the 'sukha-āvaha' outcome for me, while resisting the abhadra He may need to send to annihilate the duṣkṛti tendencies I am still feeding?
  • When I read that the same descent is sweet to the sādhus and annihilating to the khalas, which side of that response do I actually exhibit when His instructions cut against my plans — and what does that reveal about my current standing?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick the one social-media account, group chat, or 'friend' whose presence is quietly khala in your life — leaving you more anxious, more comparing, more cynical. Treat unfollowing or muting as a small sattvopapanna act: letting Kṛṣṇa's protection work through your thumb.
  • Before your next exam or interview, spend two minutes acknowledging that the outcome is in the hands of the avabodha ātmā — the Lord in full knowledge — and that your job is only sincere preparation. Then study harder, not less, because you are no longer studying alone.
  • When a peer is 'annihilated' in front of the group — publicly humiliated, dropped from the circle, called out — practice real compassion: reach out privately, see the soul, not the social verdict. This is the daily training ground for distinguishing sādhu-vision from herd-vision.
householder
  • When a career setback, financial loss, or family conflict 'annihilates' a plan you were attached to, sit with your spouse for ten minutes and ask together: what was Kṛṣṇa removing, and what is He protecting? Make this a recurring practice, not a one-time consolation.
  • Raise your children to know that Kṛṣṇa is a Person, not a feeling — tell them stories from the source texts (only what you have actually read), and resist the cultural pressure to flatten Him into 'the universe' or 'positive energy.' This is your most consequential parenting decision.
  • In your work life, distinguish daksya (real skill, fully engaged) from frantic control. Be the most competent person in the room precisely because you have surrendered the fruit — let your detachment show up as crisper decisions, not as withdrawal.
senior
  • Mortality is the body's own annihilation, and the verse promises this is kṣema for the surrendered soul. Each evening, practice releasing one attachment — a possession, a grievance, an identity — as rehearsal for the final release.
  • Mentor at least one younger person in your circle through the lens of svādhyāya: read one verse together weekly, not to teach them, but to let them watch an elder still studying. Legacy is not what you leave; it is what they see you still doing.
  • When you witness the 'annihilation' of peers — illness, decline, death — refuse both denial and despair. Speak of Kṛṣṇa plainly with your spouse, your children, your old friends. At this stage, your unembarrassed personalism is the single greatest gift you can give the next generation.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's incarnations as mythological symbols of an impersonal principle, rather than as the personal descents of the supreme avabodha-ātmā who knowingly acts for the welfare of cara and acara alike.
  • Assuming that because the Lord's appearance is 'for the good of all,' there will be no painful annihilation in one's own life — and then blaming Him or losing faith when sattva-upapanna circumstances arrive disguised as loss.
  • Reading 'abhadrāṇi muhuḥ khalānām' as license to label other people as khalas deserving annihilation, instead of using the verse as a mirror to identify and surrender the khala-vṛttis still operating within oneself.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse says Kṛṣṇa's annihilation of the khalas is 'good for them.' Share with your partner: where in your own life have you experienced an 'annihilation' — a job loss, a relationship ending, a humiliation — that you can now see as Kṛṣṇa's mercy? Then push each other: are you sure that's not just retrospective sweetening?
  • We say the incarnations are sattvopapannāni — fully in śuddha-sattva, transcendental. Yet most of us picture Rāma, Nṛsiṁha, and Kṛṣṇa as mythological characters or cultural symbols. Where in your sādhana does this gap actually live, and what would it cost you to close it?
  • If 'pleasing to the sādhus, terrifying to the khalas' is one and the same Lord acting, then the difference is in us, not Him. Disagree productively: is there a part of you that still wants Kṛṣṇa to be pleasant on your terms — and what does that say about which side of the verse you currently stand on?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a devotee whose elderly father — a lifelong skeptic and harsh critic of religion — has just died after a sudden, painful illness. Student B is a well-meaning friend from class who tries to comfort A by saying, 'Kṛṣṇa annihilated him because he was a duṣkṛti, but it's actually good for him.' Student A reacts. Run the scene for five minutes. Then swap: B must now offer the same truth without weaponizing it — speaking from real compassion (serving the soul) rather than material compassion or doctrinal point-scoring. Class debriefs: what did the second version sound like, and what did it cost the speaker to find it?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling God an 'annihilator of the wicked' is just primitive tribal religion dressed up — it's the same vengeful-deity thinking we've outgrown. A loving God wouldn't need to destroy anyone.
    Notice the verse does not say Kṛṣṇa annihilates out of wrath; it says His appearance is sattvopapannāni — in pure goodness — and that even abhadra to the khalas is ultimately their kṣema, their welfare. A surgeon cutting out a tumor is not vengeful. The 'loving God who never opposes anything' of modern sentiment is actually a smaller god — one who cannot be trusted to act when action is what love requires. Gauḍīya theology refuses that smallness: Kṛṣṇa's love is strong enough to liberate even those who hate Him, and the form that liberation takes is sometimes the dissolution of the false self that was clinging to harm.
  • If Kṛṣṇa is in full knowledge (avabodha ātmā) and arranges everything for the welfare of all, then human effort is pointless — I might as well sit back and let Him handle it.
    This is the very fatalism the Gītā spends eighteen chapters dismantling. Kṛṣṇa's avabodha and His descent for lokasya kṣema do not replace your sva-dharma; they ground it. Because He is in full knowledge, your sincere effort is never wasted (BG 6.35 — abhyāsena tu, kaunteya). Because He acts for the welfare of all, your action under His shelter participates in that welfare. Sitting back is not surrender; it is tamas wearing surrender's clothes. Real surrender looks like doing today's duty with everything you have, and then offering the result.
Analogy

A great king grants safe-conduct to all of his subjects, but raises his sword against the bandit. He is one and the same king in both gestures; what differs is the relationship of the recipient. So the avatāras — sweet to the devotee, fierce to the demon — are not split-personality but undivided love seen from two sides.

Stories
Nṛsiṁha — terror and tenderness in one body

When Nṛsiṁhadeva burst from the pillar, His roar split the sky and His claws disemboweled Hiraṇyakaśipu — but the same Lord, moments later, gathered the boy Prahlāda onto His lap and licked the dust from his hair. His ferocity and His tenderness were not two natures; they were one love, addressed to two relationships.

This verse names exactly that double aspect: 'pleasing to the pious, annihilator for the non-devotee.' The Gauḍīyas treasure Nṛsiṁha as the iconographic proof that the avatāra's anger and the avatāra's affection share one body. SB 7.8-9.
