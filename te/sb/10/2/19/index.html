Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.19

సా దేవకీ సర్వజగన్నివాస-నివాసభూతా నితరాం న రేజే । భోజేన్ద్రగేహేఽగ్నిశిఖేవ రుద్ధాసరస్వతీ జ్ఞానఖలే యథా సతీ ॥ ౧౯ ॥

Devakī then kept within herself the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the cause of all causes, the foundation of the entire cosmos, but because she was under arrest in the house of Kaṁsa, she was like the flames of a fire covered by the walls of a pot, or like a person who has knowledge but cannot distribute it to the world for the benefit of human society.
Purport

In this verse the word jñāna-khala is most significant. Knowledge is meant for distribution. Although there is already much scientific knowledge, whenever scientists or philosophers awaken to a particular type of knowledge, they try to distribute it throughout the world, for otherwise the knowledge gradually dries up and no one benefits from it. India has the knowledge of Bhagavad-gītā, but unfortunately, for some reason or other, this sublime knowledge of the science of God was not distributed throughout the world, although it is meant for all of human society. Therefore Kṛṣṇa Himself appeared as Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and ordered all Indians to take up the cause of distributing the knowledge of Bhagavad-gītā throughout the entire world. yāre dekha, tāre kaha ‘kṛṣṇa’-upadeśa āmāra ājñāya guru hañā tāra’ ei deśa “Instruct everyone to follow the orders of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa as they are given in Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. In this way become a spiritual master and try to liberate everyone in this land.” (Cc. Madhya 7.128) Although India has the sublime knowledge of Bhagavad-gītā, Indians have not done their proper duty of distributing it. Now, therefore, the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement has been set up to distribute this knowledge as it is, without distortion. Although previously there were attempts to distribute the knowledge of Bhagavad-gītā, these attempts involved distortion and compromise with mundane knowledge. But now the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement, without mundane compromises, is distributing Bhagavad-gītā as it is, and people are deriving the benefits of awakening to Kṛṣṇa consciousness and becoming devotees of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Therefore the proper distribution of knowledge has begun by which not only will the whole world benefit, but India’s glory will be magnified in human society. Kaṁsa tried to arrest Kṛṣṇa consciousness within his house ( bhojendra-gehe ), with the result that Kaṁsa, with all his opulences, was later vanquished. Similarly, the real knowledge of Bhagavad-gītā was being choked by unscrupulous Indian leaders, with the result that India’s culture, and knowledge of the Supreme were being lost. Now, however, because Kṛṣṇa consciousness is spreading, the proper use of Bhagavad-gītā is being attempted.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Devakī then kept within herself the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the cause of all causes, the foundation of the entire cosmos, but because she was under arrest in the house of Kaṁsa, she was like the flames of a fire covered by the walls of a pot, or like a person who has knowledge but cannot distribute it to the world for the benefit of human society.
Commentary

Devaki became the shelter of the shelter of all material and non-material worlds, but all people could not take advantage of that bliss-giving form. Only a few close persons could appreciate her condition. In the prison house of Kamsa (bhojendra) she was like a covered fire. When a fire is burning in a house, it does not illuminate the town, but only the objects or persons near it in the house. However when the fire becomes strong it can burn down the restricting house. In this way, Devaki also would in due time destroy Kamsa. Knowledge (sarasvati) trapped in the hearts of men who do not distribute it (literally, cheaters of knowledge) does not flourish and benefit others. That knowledge will eventually destroy its restrictor, who is very sinful. In the same way Devaki would destroy offensive Kamsa.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The word jagat means “that which moves.” Thus it means temporary. Thus sarva-jagat means everything temporary. However by taking sarva as a separate indicator, sarva (all) can also indicate entities which are eternal. Thus sarva-jagan-nivāsa means the Lord who is the shelter of eternal and temporary beings. As the śruti says yasya bhāsā sarvam idam; vibhāti: by his light everything illuminates. (Śvetaśvatāra Upaniṣad) This indicates Kṛṣṇa. Devakī acted as his shelter (nivāsa-bhūtā). But though she was thus qualified to be the giver of all bliss and auspiciousness, she did not shine externally with those qualities but shone internally with her own bliss which excelled that of Vasudeva and others. A covered flame and a person with suppressed knowledge do not shine with all brilliance but shine with brightness internally. These two by their light give internal light because of their personal splendor. For the person in knowledge, the internal aspect is the mind. Even if a flame is covered it can burn down a house. Covered knowledge destroys its shelter (the subtle body) along with sin.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

She had become the supreme shelter of Kṛṣna who is the shelter of the whole universe. She appeared in this world as the mother of svayam bhagavān. She was most fortunate and most beautiful because of this. Still, she did not shine or appear beautiful (na reje). She was like a covered fire since she was not happy. An example is given. She was like person who is the personification of the Vedas (sarasvatī), who does not reveal the knowledge to others (jṣāna-khale). Though being happy in himself with that knowledge, a person deprives another person of knowledge. Hiding knowledge however does not depend on being in Kaṁsa’s prison. (Thus it is a general example.) Or knowledge attained by a person whose intention is to harm others (jṣāna-khale) does not shine. The defect is in the receptacle of knowledge (being within the prison). Bhojendra-gehe can also mean “in the kitchen.” Just as a flame, covered by smoke in the kitchen, does not shine, so she did not shine because of Kaṁsa’s evil actions. Or did she not shine constantly, being imprisoned by Kaṁsa? Yes, she shone because she contained the lord. Just as a flame shines even though repressed, she shone. Similarly a person with great knowledge shines constantly, by special cleverness through suggestion and other devices of speech (jṣāna-khale).

Concepts
JivaGuru
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa-knowledge withheld is like fire trapped in a pot — receive it from your guru and pass it on, or it dies in you.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one person in your life who could benefit from something you have learned about Kṛṣṇa, and share it plainly with them today.
  • Refuse the impulse to hoard sādhana as a private comfort — speak one verse or teaching aloud to someone before the day ends.
  • Notice where you have been a jñāna-khala — withholding what you know out of shyness or self-importance — and break the pattern in one concrete conversation.
Reflection Questions
  • What kṛṣṇa-conscious knowledge has Kṛṣṇa already placed within me — through hearing, reading, or association — that I am keeping 'arrested in the Bhojendra-gehe' of my private life instead of distributing to those around me?
  • Where am I behaving like the jñāna-khala — guarding what I have received as personal capital — and who in my immediate circle is being deprived because of that withholding?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick one thing you've actually understood from Gītā or Bhāgavatam study and say it out loud this week to one non-devotee friend — not as a lecture, just one sentence in a real conversation. Notice the fear that rises (looking weird, losing status in the friend group); that fear is the wall of Kaṁsa's house.
  • Audit your social media for one week: how much of what you post is curated self-image (Kaṁsa's opulence) versus anything that points to Kṛṣṇa or to the soul? You don't have to start posting verses — but notice how much energy goes into the prison and how little into the flame.
  • When a classmate is breaking down before exams or after a breakup, resist two reflexes — (a) the secular reflex of 'you do you, follow your truth,' and (b) the preachy reflex of quoting verses at them. Instead, sit with them, then offer one concrete thing rooted in sva-dharma: a walk, a meal, a question about what actually matters. That is karuṇā in your stage of life.
householder
  • Your children are watching what you protect and what you transmit. This week, name one piece of Kṛṣṇa-conscious understanding you have been keeping behind the walls of 'they're too young' or 'my spouse won't like it' — and find one age-appropriate way to share it (a story at dinner, a verse on the fridge, a question in the car). Jñāna-khala begins at home.
  • In your professional life, identify the one place where you compromise the truth you know — softening, omitting, going along — to keep the income or the relationship intact. Don't quit the job. But this week, in one meeting or one family decision, speak the thing you would have swallowed. Test whether the walls were as load-bearing as you thought.
  • Look at how you spend money on the family. How much goes to maintaining the bhojendra-gehe (the comfortable house, the optics, the schools that signal status), and how much actually flows toward distributing Kṛṣṇa — books, prasāda for guests, supporting a temple, hosting a kīrtana? Re-balance one line item this month.
senior
  • You have decades of realization that younger devotees and family members need. Identify one specific person — a grandchild, a younger colleague at the temple, a neighbor — who would receive what you have to give, and schedule a recurring time with them. Do not wait to be asked; Devakī was not asked either, and the cost of her silence was the world's loss.
  • Begin to dismantle the walls of your own bhojendra-gehe before death does it for you. Choose one possession, one grudge, or one identity (former title, former role, former grievance) and actively release it this month. Notice that as the walls come down, the flame Devakī carried — the same Kṛṣṇa within you — has more room to be seen by others.
  • Write down, in your own hand, the three things about Kṛṣṇa and this path you most do not want to die without having said clearly to your children and grandchildren. Don't aestheticize it, don't make it a memoir — just the three things. Then find the occasion, however ordinary, to say them. Legacy in this line is not what you owned; it is what of Kṛṣṇa got out of the house through you.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating the verse as a license for aggressive proselytizing or correcting strangers, instead of the humble, relational distribution Caitanya Mahāprabhu modeled with 'yāre dekha, tāre kaha kṛṣṇa-upadeśa.'
  • Equating 'distribution' with social-media broadcasting or content production, while neglecting the harder work of speaking Kṛṣṇa-kathā to one's own family, classmates, and colleagues.
  • Distorting or diluting the message to make it palatable — the very compromise Prabhupāda warns against — and calling that 'distribution,' when in fact it choke s the knowledge just as surely as Kaṁsa's walls did.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī held Kṛṣṇa within her but could not share Him because she was imprisoned by Kaṁsa. Where in your own life are you 'imprisoned' — by job, family expectation, fear of judgment, social media optics — such that the kṛṣṇa-jñāna you've received cannot get out? Be specific about the walls, not just the regret.
  • Prabhupāda calls the one who hoards knowledge a jñāna-khala — a miser of knowledge — and says such knowledge 'gradually dries up.' Disagree if you can: is there ever a legitimate case for keeping spiritual realization private (humility, lack of qualification, fear of misrepresenting), or is that always Kaṁsa's prison dressed up as virtue?
  • Prabhupāda says previous attempts to distribute the Gītā 'involved distortion and compromise with mundane knowledge.' In your own speaking about Kṛṣṇa — to coworkers, in-laws, skeptical friends — where do you compromise to make it palatable, and where is the line between skillful framing (upāya) and dilution (apa-siddhānta)?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a software engineer who has been chanting japa and reading Bhāgavatam for two years and has just been promoted to team lead at a secular tech company. A junior teammate confides she is suicidal after a breakup and asks, 'How do you stay so steady?' Student A has thirty seconds to respond. Student B plays the teammate. Run the scene three times: (1) A says nothing about Kṛṣṇa, only generic empathy — afterward, debrief whether that was karuṇā or cowardice; (2) A delivers a mini-lecture about the eternal soul — debrief whether that was distribution or jñāna-khala-style showing-off; (3) A speaks one sentence that points to Kṛṣṇa without using the word, and offers to talk more after work. Class then names which version was the bhojendra-gehe (Kaṁsa's house) and which was the open flame.

Objections & Responses
  • Isn't 'distributing knowledge to the whole world' just religious imperialism in spiritual clothing? People have their own paths — who are we to evangelize the Gītā at them?
    Notice the verse's image: Devakī suffers because the knowledge cannot get out, and Prabhupāda compares it to a scientist who refuses to publish. We don't accuse the cancer researcher of imperialism for sharing her findings — we'd accuse her of cruelty if she didn't. The Gauḍīya position is that Kṛṣṇa is the actual proprietor of every living being (BG 5.29), so introducing someone to Him is restoring a relationship, not imposing a foreign one. The distortion to avoid is forcing or manipulating; the distortion equally to avoid is silence dressed up as tolerance. Real karuṇā speaks; false karuṇā stays comfortable.
  • Devakī's 'imprisonment' is just a poetic frame — the verse is mythology. The practical lesson about distributing knowledge feels grafted on by Prabhupāda to promote his own movement.
    Two things. First, the Bhāgavatam itself supplies the simile (sarasvatī jñāna-khale yathā satī) — Vyāsa, not Prabhupāda, equates Devakī's confinement with knowledge trapped in a miser. The application is in the verse, not added to it. Second, even read as story, the structure is exact: a tyrant (Kaṁsa / our own ahaṅkāra and social fear) tries to seal Kṛṣṇa inside four walls, and the result is the tyrant's destruction, not Kṛṣṇa's. Prabhupāda is doing what every ācārya in this line does — showing the verse is about you, today, not about a long-ago royal family. If it were only mythology, you would not be uncomfortable with the question.
