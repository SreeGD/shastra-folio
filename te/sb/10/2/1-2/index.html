Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Under the protection of Magadharāja, Jarāsandha, the powerful Kaṁsa began persecuting the kings of the Yadu dynasty. In this he had the cooperation of demons like Pralamba, Baka, Cāṇūra, Tṛṇāvarta, Aghāsura, Muṣṭika, Ariṣṭa, Dvivida, Pūtanā, Keśī, Dhenuka, Bāṇāsura, Narakāsura and many other demoniac kings on the surface of the earth.
Purport

This verse supports the following statement given by the Lord in Bhagavad-gītā (4.7-8): yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge “Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion — at that time I descend Myself. To deliver the pious and to annihilate the miscreants, as well as to reestablish the principles of religion, I Myself appear millennium after millennium.” The Lord’s purpose in maintaining this material world is to give everyone a chance to go back home, back to Godhead, but kings and political leaders unfortunately try to hinder the purpose of the Lord, and therefore the Lord appears, either personally or with His plenary portions, to set things right. It is therefore said: garbhaṁ sañcārya rohiṇyāṁ devakyā yogamāyayā tasyāḥ kukṣiṁ gataḥ kṛṣṇo dvitīyo vibudhaiḥ stutaḥ “Kṛṣṇa appeared in the womb of Devakī after transferring Baladeva to the womb of Rohiṇī by the power of Yoga-māyā.” Yadubhiḥ sa vyarudhyata. The kings of the Yadu dynasty were all devotees, but there were many powerful demons, such as Śālva, who began to persecute them. At that time, Jarāsandha, who was Kaṁsa’s father-in-law, was extremely powerful, and therefore Kaṁsa took advantage of his protection and the help of the demons in persecuting the kings of the Yadu dynasty. The demons naturally appeared more powerful than the demigods, but ultimately, because of help received from the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the demons were defeated and the demigods triumphant.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Under the protection of Magadharāja, Jarāsandha, the powerful Kaṁsa began persecuting the kings of the Yadu dynasty. In this he had the cooperation of demons like Pralamba, Baka, Cāṇūra, Tṛṇāvarta, Aghāsura, Muṣṭika, Ariṣṭa, Dvivida, Pūtanā, Keśī, Dhenuka, Bāṇāsura, Narakāsura and many other demoniac kings on the surface of the earth.
Commentary

In the second chapter the child of Devaki is transferred to Rohini by Yogamaya, Krsna appears in Devaki’s womb and the devatas praise the Lord. Kamsa’s persecution of the Yadus, which was mentioned in the first chapter, is now described in detail. In this list of the asuras, Mahasana (big eater) means Aghasura.

Personal Guidance
When demoniac power overwhelms the world, Kṛṣṇa Himself descends to protect His devotees and restore dharma — never doubt His personal intervention.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where adharma is pressing on you or those you serve, and respond by taking explicit shelter of Kṛṣṇa rather than relying on your own strength.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's name aloud at least once when you feel surrounded by 'demons' — distractions, hostile people, or inner asuric tendencies — and trust His paritrāṇāya promise.
  • Refuse the cynical view that the world is hopeless; remember that the Yadus, though persecuted, were never abandoned, and serve someone today as if protecting a devotee under siege.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like Kaṁsa, taking shelter of a 'Jarāsandha' — some powerful worldly alliance, ideology, or institution — to legitimize a course of action I already sense is opposed to Kṛṣṇa's purpose?
  • When I see the demonic forces of this age seeming to overpower the devotees and the dharmic, do I quietly conclude that adharma is winning — and what does that quiet conclusion reveal about how much I actually trust paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Identify one 'Kaṁsa' in your social media feed — an account or trend that quietly persecutes your sādhana (your study, your sleep, your self-respect). This week, name its 'Jarāsandha' (the algorithm, the friend group, the FOMO) and cut one specific tie, not vaguely 'use less phone.'
  • In your friend group there is almost always a person who plays Kaṁsa's role — mocking sincerity, pressuring everyone toward viṣaya. Without becoming self-righteous, decide today what you will not laugh along with anymore. Write the line in your notes app.
  • Before your next exam or deadline, do the Yadu-practice: act with full effort while internally taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa. Notice the difference between anxious striving (you alone against Kaṁsa) and śaraṇāgata striving (you working, He arranging).
householder
  • Audit one area of family life — schooling, finances, in-laws, screen time — where an adharmic pattern persists because a 'Jarāsandha' protects it (a relative's pressure, a job's demands, a school's culture). Name it aloud with your spouse this week and decide together on one concrete withdrawal.
  • When you face a workplace 'Kaṁsa' — a boss or client who persecutes the honest path — practice the Yadu posture: do not flatter, do not flee, keep doing your duty, and inwardly hand the outcome to Kṛṣṇa. Each evening, formally offer the day's frictions to Him.
  • Teach your children the difference between real karuṇā (serving the soul) and material compassion (only easing the body). Do this not by lecture but by one visible decision this month — saying no to something they want because it harms their long-term dharma.
senior
  • You have lived through several cycles of 'Kaṁsas' — political, professional, familial — and seen the Lord's rearrangements unfold. Tell one such story this week to a younger person, not as nostalgia but as evidence: glānir bhavati, then sambhavāmi. Make the Gītā 4.7-8 pattern concrete for them.
  • Examine the attachments you are still defending as 'protecting the family' — properties, control of decisions, opinions about how things should go. Ask honestly: am I a Yadu taking shelter, or have I quietly become a small Jarāsandha sheltering someone else's Kaṁsa? Loosen one grip this month.
  • As mortality nears, the demons of fear, regret, and bodily decline grow louder. Do not fight them on their terms. Instead, deepen one act of śaraṇāgati daily — japa, hearing SB, remembering Kṛṣṇa's names — trusting that He who appears 'yuge yuge' also appears at the hour of a devotee's passage.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the long list of demons as mere mythology or distant history, rather than recognizing the same Pralamba-Baka-Pūtanā impulses (illusion, deceit, false nourishment, distraction) operating inside one's own ahaṅkāra today.
  • Mistaking the demons' apparent supremacy for actual strength — concluding that aligning with worldly power (the 'Jarāsandha shelter') is pragmatic, when the verse's whole arc shows such shelter guarantees eventual destruction by the Lord Himself.
  • Twisting 'vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām' into a license for personal vindictiveness or political crusading against people one labels 'demonic,' rather than seeing it as Kṛṣṇa's prerogative and turning the same scrutiny inward on one's own duṣkṛta tendencies.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kaṁsa did not act alone — Jarāsandha sheltered him, and a roster of demons cooperated. In your own life, name one adharmic pattern you participate in that depends on a 'Jarāsandha' (an institution, friend, or system that protects it). Would you actually withdraw that protection? Disagree honestly with each other about whether withdrawal is realistic.
  • Prabhupāda says political leaders 'try to hinder the purpose of the Lord.' Pair up and argue both sides: is it ever genuine devotional service to engage with politics and worldly power for the sake of dharma, or does that engagement always corrupt? Bring concrete present-day examples.
  • The verse names many demons by name — Pūtanā, Tṛṇāvarta, Aghāsura, and so on. Why does Śukadeva bother with the catalogue rather than saying 'and many demons'? In your group, propose competing answers (memorial, literary, devotional, strategic) and see if you can agree which is primary.
Role Play

A teacher divides the class into three roles. One student plays a devoted Yadu citizen whose family business has just been seized by Kaṁsa's tax-collectors. A second plays their cousin, who counsels patience — 'Kṛṣṇa will appear in His own time; do not provoke Kaṁsa.' A third plays a hot-headed neighbor urging armed resistance now. Run the scene for ten minutes. Then debrief: where in the conversation did real karuṇā speak, and where did rajas masquerade as it? What would saraṇāgati look like for each character today, before Kṛṣṇa's appearance is visible?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling political opponents 'demons' is exactly the kind of dehumanizing rhetoric that has fueled real-world violence. Isn't this verse just sanctifying us-versus-them thinking?
    A fair worry, and worth taking seriously. But notice what the text actually does: it names asuras by their conduct — persecuting devotees, obstructing the Lord's purpose — not by birth, ethnicity, or party. Kaṁsa himself was a Yadu by blood; Jarāsandha was a respected king. The category is moral and behavioral, not tribal. Further, the Gauḍīya teacher never authorizes us to play Kṛṣṇa's role of 'vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām.' Our work is sādhana and bhakti; annihilating miscreants is His prerogative, not ours. The verse trains us to see clearly, not to swing swords.
  • If Kṛṣṇa is supreme and protects His devotees, why were the Yadus — His own family — being persecuted in the first place? Either He isn't fully in control, or He doesn't really protect His own.
    Both horns of the dilemma assume that protection means absence of difficulty. But Prabhupāda's purport quotes BG 4.7-8 to frame the persecution as the very stage on which the Lord descends. The Yadus' suffering is not a failure of Kṛṣṇa's protection; it is the invitation for Kṛṣṇa's appearance. A devotee learns to read distress as the front edge of the Lord's intervention rather than as His absence. This is also why the Gauḍīyas treasure verses like BG 12.5 — the personal Lord meets His devotees inside their actual conditions, He does not airlift them out of history.
