Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.28

The efficient cause of this material world, manifested with its many varieties as the original tree, is You, O Lord. You are also the maintainer of this material world, and after annihilation You are the one in whom everything is conserved. Those who are covered by Your external energy cannot see You behind this manifestation, but theirs is not the vision of learned devotees.
Purport

Various demigods, beginning from Lord Brahmā, Lord Śiva and even Viṣṇu, are supposed to be the creator, maintainer and annihilator of this material world, but actually they are not. The fact is that everything is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, manifested in varieties of energy. Ekam evādvitīyaṁ brahma. There is no second existence. Those who are truly vipaścit, learned, are those who have reached the platform of understanding and observing the Supreme Personality of Godhead in any condition of life. Premāñjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena santaḥ sadaiva hṛdayeṣu vilokayanti ( Brahma-saṁhitā 5.38 ). Learned devotees accept even conditions of distress as representing the presence of the Supreme Lord. When a devotee is in distress, he sees that the Lord has appeared as distress just to relieve or purify the devotee from the contamination of the material world. While one is within this material world, one is in various conditions, and therefore a devotee sees a condition of distress as but another feature of the Lord. Tat te ’nukampāṁ susamīkṣamāṇaḥ ( Bhāg. 10.14.8 ). A devotee, therefore, regards distress as a great favor of the Lord because he understands that he is being cleansed of contamination. Teṣām ahaṁ samuddhartā mṛtyu-saṁsāra-sāgarāt (Bg. 12.7). The appearance of distress is a negative process intended to give the devotee relief from this material world, which is called mṛtyu-saṁsāra, or the constant repetition of birth and death. To save a surrendered soul from repeated birth and death, the Lord purifies him of contamination by offering him a little distress. This cannot be understood by a nondevotee, but a devotee can see this because he is vipaścit, or learned. A nondevotee, therefore, is perturbed in distress, but a devotee welcomes distress as another feature of the Lord. Sarvaṁ khalv idaṁ brahma. A devotee can actually see that there is only the Supreme Personality of Godhead and no second entity. Ekam evādvitīyam . There is only the Lord, who presents Himself in different energies. Persons who are not in real knowledge think that Brahmā is the creator, Viṣṇu the maintainer and Śiva the annihilator and that the different demigods are intended to fulfill diverse purposes. Thus they create diverse purposes and worship various demigods to have these purposes fulfilled ( kāmais tais tair hṛta jñānāḥ prapadyante ’nya-devatāḥ ). A devotee, however, knows that these various demigods are but different parts of the Supreme Personality of Godhead and that these parts need not be worshiped. As the Lord says in Bhagavad-gītā (9.23) : ye ’py anya-devatā bhaktā yajante śraddhayānvitāḥ te ’pi mām eva kaunteya yajanty avidhi-pūrvakam “Whatever a man may sacrifice to other gods, O son of Kuntī, is really meant for Me alone, but it is offered without true understanding.” There is no need to worship the demigods, for this is avidhi, not in order. Simply by surrendering oneself at the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, one can completely discharge one’s duties; there is no need to worship various deities or demigods. These various divinities are observed by the mūḍhas, fools, who are bewildered by the three modes of material nature ( tribhir guṇamayair bhāvair ebhiḥ sarvam idaṁ jagat ). Such fools cannot understand that the real source of everything is the Supreme Personality of Godhead ( mohitaṁ nābhijānāti mām ebhyaḥ param avyayam ). Not being disturbed by the Lord’s various features, one should concentrate upon and worship the Supreme Lord ( mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ). This should be the guiding principle of one’s life.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The efficient cause of this material world, manifested with its many varieties as the original tree, is You, O Lord. You are also the maintainer of this material world, and after annihilation You are the one in whom everything is conserved. Those who are covered by Your external energy cannot see You behind this manifestation, but theirs is not the vision of learned devotees.
Commentary

"This tree arises from your energies." With this intention the verse is spoken. "You are the only (eka) creator of this material tree which is real (sat). You are the destruction (sannidhanam) and the maintainer ( anugraha)." "Oh devatas it is said that Brahma, Visnu and Siva are the masters of this world, so why do you say that I am the creator." The next statement answers this. "Those who minds are covered by your maya see Brahma and others as independent, but those whose minds are not covered see only you. They see Brahma and others as your expansions only."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Other meanings of the word tvam (you) are here rejected. The meaning may be taken according to Śrīdhara Svāmī. Or another meaning is as follows. “How can I be the creator, maintainer and destroyer all at once?” This verse answers. The wise with minds uncovered by māyā (tvan-māyayā asaṁvṛta-cetasaḥ) do not see varieties and they see the non-difference of all the Lord’s forms since the one form of the Lord by his innate inconceivable śakti takes on various forms according to the type of worship and way of seeing. maṇir yathā vibhāgena nīla-pītādibhir yutaḥ rūpa-bhedam avāpnoti dhyāna-bhedāt tathācyutaḥ Just as a gem shows blue, yellow and other colors at different times, the Lord takes different forms because of different types of meditation. Nārada-paṣcarātra The gem refers to the vaidurya which possesses various colors. Or persons covered by your māyā see you in various forms as described. You attain such variety by māyā. People then think you are these various forms. But the wise do not think like this because of the Lord’s innate śakti. All the Lord’s forms are without māyā, full of knowledge and bliss.

Concepts
Jiva
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa alone is the source, sustainer, and shelter of all — the vipaścit devotee sees Him in every condition, even distress, as His mercy.
Practical Applications
  • When distress arrives today, name it aloud as Kṛṣṇa's purifying touch rather than as misfortune to be escaped.
  • Refuse to petition any 'demigod' of modern life — money, status, algorithms — and direct one prayer today to Kṛṣṇa alone.
  • Notice one moment when you credit a secondary cause (boss, market, weather) for an outcome, and inwardly trace it back to Kṛṣṇa as the one source.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still attributing credit, blame, or causation to 'demigods' — boss, market, body, government — instead of seeing Kṛṣṇa as the single source, maintainer, and reservoir behind all of it?
  • When the latest distress arrived, did I read it as random misfortune to be escaped, or as Kṛṣṇa's purifying touch — and what would change in my response if I actually saw it as His feature, not His absence?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When the next exam result, rejection, breakup, or social-media humiliation hits, before you call your friends or scroll, sit for five minutes and ask: 'If Kṛṣṇa is the prasūti, sannidhāna, and anugraha of even this, what is He cleaning out of me right now?' Write the answer in your notes app. Don't share it.
  • Make a list of every 'demigod' your friend group quietly worships for separate purposes — the influencer for confidence, the brand for belonging, the GPA for self-worth, the relationship for meaning. Pick the one you yourself bow to most. For one week, when you feel the pull to that source, redirect it: chant one round of japa or read one verse before you give in. Track how often you actually do it.
  • Stop posting your wins as if you caused them and your losses as if life is unfair. For one month, before any post, ask: does this presentation of myself acknowledge that the Supreme Person is behind the whole picture, or am I quietly playing creator-maintainer-annihilator of my own image?
householder
  • When a financial setback, a child's failure, a marital fight, or a career disappointment lands this month, name it out loud at the family altar as anukampā — not to bypass solving it, but to refuse the inner narrative that the universe is against your family. Then take the practical action dharma requires.
  • Audit which 'demigods' your household runs to: the financial advisor as omniscient, the school as the soul-shaper of your child, the doctor as final authority over life and death, the employer as the source of security. Honor each in their proper function — but consciously place Kṛṣṇa above each in your evening offering, and let your spouse and children hear you do it.
  • When raising your children, refuse to teach them the modern myth that they are the cause of their successes and the victims of their failures. The next time your child wins or loses something significant, sit with them and walk through this verse in their language — He is the source, the support, and the one who takes back. Make it a family literacy, not an occasional sermon.
senior
  • Catalog the distresses of the last decade — the surgeries, the losses, the betrayals, the diminishments — and write next to each one what it cleaned out of you. Where you cannot yet see the cleansing, mark it honestly and bring it into japa daily until you can. This is the vipaścit's homework for old age.
  • You are now close enough to mṛtyu-saṁsāra's exit that the verse's promise — teṣām ahaṁ samuddhartā — is no longer abstract. Identify the three attachments (a property, a grudge, a role, a reputation, a relationship in which you still want to be central) that are still anchoring you to this round, and begin the deliberate work of offering each one back to Him as His, not yours.
  • When you mentor younger devotees or family members, refuse to package your life as a story you authored. Tell it as the verse asks — He was the prasūti, the sannidhāna, the anugraha; the credit and the blame both rest with Him. Let your legacy be that you pointed past yourself to the Person behind the manifestation.
Common Pitfalls
  • Sliding into impersonalism: hearing 'everything is the Supreme' and concluding 'all is one consciousness' or 'the divine is within me,' rather than holding the personalist truth that Kṛṣṇa Himself is the cause, maintainer, and shelter — distinct from us even while pervading all.
  • Weaponizing 'distress is His mercy' to spiritually bypass real responsibility — refusing medical care, ignoring family duties, or romanticizing suffering — when the verse asks for inner vision (vipaścit), not outer passivity or neglect of sva-dharma.
  • Hedging surrender by continuing to court demigods or worldly powers 'just in case' — worshipping Kṛṣṇa on the altar while privately propitiating career, reputation, or other deities for results — which Prabhupāda flatly calls avidhi, out of order.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a concrete distress from the past year that, looking back, you can now read as Kṛṣṇa's anukampā purifying you. Then share one you still cannot read that way — and let your partner push back: is your inability to see it as His mercy a failure of vision, or is it a real evil you are being asked to spiritualize away? Don't settle the disagreement too quickly.
  • The verse says non-devotees see 'nānā' — many — while the vipaścit sees one Lord behind everything. In your circle, name a specific demigod-equivalent (a guru, a method, a system, a self-help framework, an institution) that people you respect run to for fulfillment of separate purposes. Would you call that worship avidhi-pūrvakam? Be honest about where you yourself still hedge your bets.
  • Prabhupāda says the Lord 'appears as distress' to relieve the devotee. A skeptic in the room argues this is a coping mechanism that lets devotees rationalize suffering and stay passive in the face of injustice. Take the skeptic's side for two minutes against your partner — argue it as forcefully as you can — then switch. What in the verse actually answers the charge, and what doesn't?
Role Play

Two students sit facing each other. Student A has just received bad news: a parent has been diagnosed with a serious illness, a job has been lost, or a long-planned project has collapsed. Student A speaks for two minutes from genuine distress — not theologically, just as a hurting person. Student B is a fellow devotee who must respond. The trap: B must NOT offer the cheap consolation 'everything is Kṛṣṇa's mercy, just chant.' B must speak as a vipaścit who actually sees the Lord behind this condition, while honoring A's pain as real. After five minutes, the class debriefs: where did B slip into platitude? Where did B substitute 'the universe' or 'the Self' for Kṛṣṇa? Where did B successfully name distress as anukampā without bypassing A's grief? Then swap roles.

Objections & Responses
  • Saying that distress is the Lord's mercy is just a sophisticated form of denial. It teaches devotees to accept abuse, illness, and injustice instead of acting against them. A modern, ethical person should call evil evil — not relabel it as Kṛṣṇa's favor.
    The verse is not asking you to deny what is happening — it is asking you to see who is behind it. A devotee still treats the doctor, still leaves the abuser, still reforms the unjust system; sva-dharma and right action remain (BG 3.10). What changes is the inner reading. A nondevotee in distress concludes the universe is hostile or random and either despairs or rages; a vipaścit concludes that the same Lord who is the prasūti, sannidhāna, and anugraha of all things is using this very condition to loosen his grip on the material world (mṛtyu-saṁsāra). That is not denial — denial would be pretending the distress isn't there. This is the opposite: receiving it consciously, from His hand, while still doing what dharma demands. The sign you have crossed into denial is when you stop acting; the sign you have understood the verse is that you act without the inner crisis of meaning.
  • If everything is the Supreme Personality of Godhead manifested in varieties of energy, why bother with the personalist distinction at all? Whether I call it Brahman, the Self, the universe, or Kṛṣṇa, I'm pointing at the same reality. The verse itself says ekam evādvitīyaṁ brahma.
    Notice what the verse actually says: tvam eka eva — YOU alone, addressed in the second person, with personal pronouns throughout. The oneness here is not the impersonalist erasure of distinctions; it is the oneness of a Person who holds all energies. Prabhupāda is explicit: Brahmā, Śiva, even Viṣṇu in their administrative roles are not the ultimate source — the Supreme Personality of Godhead is. The moment you collapse Kṛṣṇa into 'the Self' or 'consciousness,' you lose the relationship — and bhakti is fundamentally relational. You cannot surrender to an abstraction; you cannot be loved by a principle. The Brahma-saṁhitā verse Prabhupāda cites — premāñjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena santaḥ sadaiva hṛdayeṣu vilokayanti — describes saints who SEE Him in the heart with eyes anointed by love. That seeing is of a Person, not a field. So the question isn't whether you can use abstract language; it's whether your practice still has Someone on the other end of it.
