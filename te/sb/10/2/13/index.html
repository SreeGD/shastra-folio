Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.13

గర్భసఙ్కర్షణాత్ తం వై ప్రాహు: సఙ్కర్షణం భువి । రామేతి లోకరమణాద్ బలభద్రం బలోచ్ఛ్రయాత్ ॥ ౧౩ ॥

Important Words
  • garbha - saṅkarṣaṇāt — because of being drawn from the womb
    Saṅkarṣaṇa from sam-√kṛṣ, 'to drag together, to attract.' His very name records the event of His transfer between wombs. Pastime and theology are inseparable in the Bhāgavatam: the līlā names the form.
  • rāma iti — the name Rāma
    Rāma — from √ram, 'to delight.' Three Rāmas are honored in this tradition: Rāmacandra of Ayodhyā, Paraśurāma, and Balarāma. The verse explicitly grounds Balarāma's Rāma-name in His function of delighting Vraja.
  • loka - ramaṇāt — because He pleases the people
    Lokān ramayati iti rāmaḥ — 'He who delights people is Rāma.' The Gauḍīyas treasure this etymology: bhakti's first criterion is whether the Lord's presence delights — sukha-svarūpa, not severe absolute.
  • balabhadram — He of auspicious strength
    Bala-bhadra — bala (strength) + bhadra (auspicious). His strength is not raw power but auspicious power, dharma-yoked might. He is the elder brother who carries the plough — sustenance, not destruction.
  • bala - ucchrayāt — because of His extensive strength
    Ucchraya — towering, lofty. Balarāma's strength is foundational; He is the ādi-guru-tattva, the original spiritual master, on whose shoulders rest both the cosmic order and Kṛṣṇa Himself in lighter moods.
The son of Rohiṇī will also be celebrated as Saṅkarṣaṇa because of being sent from the womb of Devakī to the womb of Rohiṇī. He will be called Rāma because of His ability to please all the inhabitants of Gokula, and He will be known as Balabhadra because of His extensive physical strength.
Purport

These are some of the reasons why Balarāma is known as Saṅkarṣaṇa, Balarāma or sometimes Rāma. In the mahā-mantra — Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare — people sometimes object when Rāma is accepted as Balarāma. But although devotees of Lord Rāma may object, they should know that there is no difference between Balarāma and Lord Rāma. Here Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam clearly states that Balarāma is also known as Rāma ( rāmeti ). Therefore, it is not artificial for us to speak of Lord Balarāma as Lord Rāma. Jayadeva Gosvāmī also speaks of three Rāmas: Paraśurāma, Raghupati Rāma and Balarāma. All of them are Rāmas.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The son of Rohiṇī will also be celebrated as Saṅkarṣaṇa because of being sent from the womb of Devakī to the womb of Rohiṇī. He will be called Rāma because of His ability to please all the inhabitants of Gokula, and He will be known as Balabhadra because of His extensive physical strength.
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

He glories māyā by her relation to Gokula filled with the highest prema by speaking of another goal of Saṅkarṣaṇa’s appearance. Because of being pulled from the womb (vai is used for emphasis), and not because of withdrawing the universe during destruction in the form of Śeṣa, he will be known as Saṅkarṣaṇa. Being taken from the womb he will then give pleasure to all the people when he comes to Gokula. Thus he will be called Rāma. Because of his state of intense prema, because of his great strength of prema, he will be called Balabhadra. Instead of balabhadraṁ balocchrayāt sometimes balaṁ balavad uccrayāt is seen. This means “because he is stronger than the strong is called Bala. Rāmeti is a compound explained in the following sūtra: saha supā (Pāṇini 1.1.4)

Personal Guidance
Balarāma is Saṅkarṣaṇa, Rāma, and Balabhadra — the original shelter whose strength and sweetness draw every devotee toward Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Chant the mahā-mantra today and consciously include Balarāma when you say 'Hare Rāma,' refusing the mistake of separating Him from Lord Rāma.
  • Before any difficult task, take shelter of Balabhadra's bala — pray for the strength to serve Kṛṣṇa rather than for strength to push your own agenda.
  • Notice one person in your day you have failed to please through pettiness or neglect, and act as Rāma — the one who delights all — by giving them sincere attention.
Reflection Questions
  • Balarāma is called Rāma because He pleases all the inhabitants of Gokula (loka-ramaṇāt) — whose joy am I actually organizing my day around, and whose joy do I quietly resent having to consider?
  • Balarāma's transfer from Devakī's womb to Rohiṇī's is the original saṅkarṣaṇa — where in my own life has Kṛṣṇa already 'transferred' me from one situation to another for protection I did not see at the time, and am I willing to read my present circumstance the same way?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Take the 'Rāma — loka-ramaṇāt' test on your group chat for one week: are you the friend whose presence pleases people, or the one who drains the room with sarcasm, complaints, and one-upmanship? Pick one chat and watch your own messages with this question in mind.
  • Online, devotees of one form of the Lord (Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, Śiva, Devī) often pile onto each other in comment sections. Decide today that you will not participate in that — and if you see a Vaiṣṇava friend doing it, send them this verse privately. Defending Kṛṣṇa by attacking Rāma-bhaktas is not bhakti; it is ahaṅkāra wearing a tilaka.
  • Balabhadra means strength used auspiciously. In the gym, in exams, in competitions — where are you cultivating bala (strength, capability) without the bhadra (the auspiciousness, the orientation toward serving others)? Name one skill you are sharpening purely to outrank a peer, and reframe how you will use it this semester.
householder
  • Saṅkarṣaṇa was 'drawn across' from Devakī's womb to Rohiṇī's — the Lord Himself accepted being moved between mothers for a higher purpose. When your career, your spouse's needs, or your children's schooling demand that you be 'drawn across' to a city, role, or sacrifice you did not choose, remember: even the Lord submits to such transitions. Stop framing relocation, demotion, or caregiving duty as personal loss.
  • Loka-ramaṇāt at home: this week, audit whether your family experiences your arrival home as a source of ramaṇa or as the moment tension enters the house. Ask your spouse directly — and accept the answer without defending. Then, for one evening, walk in with no complaint and no agenda; just be the Rāma of that household.
  • Teach your children that the same Person bears many names. When they meet a friend whose family worships a different deity or who chants a different mantra, do not let them inherit your sectarian preferences as exclusions. Show them Jayadeva's verse: there are three Rāmas, and the Lord is not threatened by His own multiplicity.
senior
  • Balabhadra — bala that is bhadra, strength that is auspicious. In this season, your physical bala is receding; your auspicious bala (your example, your blessings, your steadiness in chanting) is what remains and what matters. Stop mourning the first; start consciously deploying the second on the people around you.
  • You have spent decades worshipping in a particular line, with particular names. The verse asks: when you meet a devotee of another form of the Lord at this stage of life, can you greet them as a brother without the old reflex of correction? Mortality narrows the time available for sectarian distance. Drop one such grudge this month.
  • Saṅkarṣaṇa is the one who is drawn from one womb into another — a direct image of the soul's passage. As you sit with elders, with the dying, or with your own anticipation of leaving this body, meditate on this name. The Lord Himself has accepted such passages; you are not abandoned in yours. Mentor a younger devotee through their own first encounter with grief using this name as a touchstone.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating the Hare Rāma portion of the mahā-mantra as referring only to Lord Rāmacandra and silently excluding Balarāma — Prabhupāda's purport directly corrects this; the verse itself (rāmeti) establishes Balarāma as Rāma.
  • Reducing Balarāma to a 'lesser' or merely supporting figure beside Kṛṣṇa, slipping into demigod-confusion — He is the original Saṅkarṣaṇa, the source of strength (balocchrayāt), not a secondary deity.
  • Romanticizing 'pleasing everyone' (loka-ramaṇa) as a license for people-pleasing or sentimental niceness, when the verse points to a Personality whose very presence delights devotees — not to our managing of others' moods.
Discussion Prompts
  • Balarāma is celebrated under three names — Saṅkarṣaṇa (the one drawn across), Rāma (the one who pleases all of Gokula), and Balabhadra (the auspicious strength). Which of these three qualities is most missing in the way you currently serve your community, and which comes most naturally? Be honest with your partner about the gap.
  • Prabhupāda insists that 'devotees of Lord Rāma may object, but there is no difference between Balarāma and Lord Rāma.' Where in your own bhakti life do you cling to a preferred form, name, or lineage in a way that quietly excludes other Vaiṣṇavas? What would it cost your ego to drop the objection?
  • Loka-ramaṇāt — He is called Rāma because He pleases all the inhabitants. In your household, workplace, or saṅga, are you a source of ramaṇa (genuine pleasure) or of friction? Disagree with your partner if their self-assessment sounds too generous — push them to name one person they consistently fail to please and why.
Role Play

Two students are paired. Student A is a long-time worshipper of Lord Rāmacandra who has just walked into a kīrtana hall and heard the mahā-mantra being sung with 'Hare Rāma' interpreted as Balarāma. They are visibly upset and say, 'You are diluting my Lord — Rāma means Raghupati, not the cowherd's brother.' Student B is a Gauḍīya devotee who must respond — not to win the argument, but to honor the other's bhāva while gently pointing to SB 10.2.13 (rāmeti) and Jayadeva Gosvāmī's recognition of three Rāmas. After five minutes, swap roles. Debrief: where did defensiveness creep in? Where did you confuse loyalty to a name with loyalty to the Person?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole verse is just etymological trivia — explaining nicknames of a mythological character. Why should a serious modern reader care that 'Saṅkarṣaṇa' means 'drawn across'?
    The names of the Lord are not labels we attach from outside; they are revelations of activity. Saṅkarṣaṇa names the act of being drawn from one womb to another — meaning the Lord submits Himself to the same passage we fear most, the transition between bodies and wombs, and does so willingly to protect His devotees. Rāma names the fact that His presence produces ramaṇa, real pleasure, in everyone around Him — not the manufactured pleasure of viṣaya. Balabhadra names auspicious strength, not raw power. Each name is a meditation. To dismiss this as trivia is to assume names are arbitrary; in the Vaiṣṇava view, the name is non-different from the named, and studying why He bears each name is itself sādhana.
  • Prabhupāda is just defending the ISKCON chant against Rāma-bhaktas who disagree with him. This is sectarian apologetics, not scripture.
    Notice that Prabhupāda does not appeal to his own authority — he appeals to Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam itself (rāmeti) and to Jayadeva Gosvāmī, a pre-Caitanya ācārya revered across Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas, who openly speaks of three Rāmas: Paraśurāma, Raghupati, and Balarāma. The point is not to win a turf war but to dissolve a false objection that would set one form of the Lord against another. A genuine Rāma-bhakta, on hearing Jayadeva, has no ground left to stand on. What looks like sectarian defense is actually the opposite — an insistence that the Personality of Godhead is one, even when His names and forms are many. Demigod-confusion runs in the other direction; here, the verse protects us from the equally serious error of fragmenting the one Lord into rival deities.
Analogy

A prince given three names at his investiture — one for his lineage, one for his charm, one for his strength — answers to all three because each names a real facet. Balarāma is Saṅkarṣaṇa to the cosmologists, Rāma to the lovers of Vraja, Balabhadra to the wrestlers — and one boy to His mother.
