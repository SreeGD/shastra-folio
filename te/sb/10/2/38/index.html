Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.38

Important Words
  • diṣṭyā — by fortune
    Diṣṭyā — 'by fate, by luck.' The demigods open and close with this word (the verse uses it twice). Even the demigods, who arrange so much, attribute their good fortune to providence. Theological humility from the top of the cosmos.
  • bhāraḥ — the burden
    Bhāra — burden. The whole opening of Canto 10 (the bhūmi-bhāra-haraṇa motif) is anchored in this single word. The Lord's descent is technically classified as bhāra-haraṇa, burden-removal.
  • apanītaḥ — removed
    Past passive participle of apa-√nī — 'led away.' The burden is led away, not destroyed by force. Gauḍīyas read this: the Lord's removal of evil is gentle, surgical, never apocalyptic without need.
  • tvat - padakaiḥ — by Your footprints
    Tvat-padaka — the marks of Your foot. The Lord's feet bear lotus, conch, disc, mace, fish, flag — sixteen auspicious marks. The Vraja-bhaktas read His footprints in the dust as scripture; this verse celebrates the same reading in advance.
  • tava anukampitām — favored by Your mercy
    Anukampita — 'moved-with-compassion-upon.' Anu-kampā literally is 'trembling-after,' the Lord's heart trembling in response to the devotee's plight. The earth itself is named here as anukampitā by His tava-mercy — a planet beloved.
O Lord, we are fortunate because the heavy burden of the demons upon this earth is immediately removed by Your appearance. Indeed, we are certainly fortunate, for we shall be able to see upon this earth and in the heavenly planets the marks of lotus, conchshell, club and disc that adorn Your lotus feet.
Purport

The soles of the Lord’s lotus feet are marked with śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma — conchshell, disc, club and lotus — and also by a flag and a thunderbolt. When Kṛṣṇa walks on this earth or in the heavenly planets, these marks are visible wherever He goes. Vṛndāvana-dhāma is a transcendental place because of Kṛṣṇa’s walking on this land frequently. The inhabitants of Vṛndāvana were fortunate to see these marks here and there. When Akrūra went to Vṛndāvana to take Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma away to the festival arranged by Kaṁsa, upon seeing the marks of the Lord’s lotus feet on the ground of Vṛndāvana, he fell down and began to groan. These marks are visible to devotees who receive the causeless mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead ( tavānukampitām ). The demigods were jubilant not only because the appearance of the Supreme Lord would do away with the burdensome demons, but also because they would be able to see upon the ground the transcendental marks from the soles of the Lord’s lotus feet. The gopīs always thought of the Lord’s lotus feet when He was walking in the pasturing grounds, and, as described in the previous verse, simply by thinking of the Lord’s lotus feet, the gopīs were fully absorbed in transcendence ( āviṣṭa-cetā na bhavāya kalpate ). Like the gopīs, one who is always absorbed in thought of the Lord is beyond the material platform and will not remain in this material world. It is our duty, therefore, always to hear, chant and think about the Lord’s lotus feet, as actually done by Vaiṣṇavas who have decided to live in Vṛndāvana always and think of the Lord’s lotus feet twenty-four hours a day.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Lord, we are fortunate because the heavy burden of the demons upon this earth is immediately removed by Your appearance. Indeed, we are certainly fortunate, for we shall be able to see upon this earth and in the heavenly planets the marks of lotus, conchshell, club and disc that adorn Your lotus feet.
Commentary

In this verse the devatas hint at the Lord’s purpose of relieving the earth’s burden. Pada here means "coming from the feet," as the sruti says, "Padbhyam bhumi" the earth arose from the Lord’s feet. The burden of the earth, which arose from your feet, has been removed. We have understood that Kamsa, Jarsandha and others have been killed just by your appearance. We are fortunate to see the earth (gam) and heaven (dyam) mercifully marked with your young feet (padakaih), marked with flag and thunderbolt (susobhanaih).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

It is our good fortune that you have relieved the earth (which are your feet) of suffering by your appearance. Or by attaining the highest fortune of your feet when you appear, you have relieved the earth of suffering. Actually the meaning should be “You have relieved the earth which arises from your feet.” But because cause and effect are non-different, the expression padaḥ bhuvaḥ (the burden of the earth which is your foot) is used. Śruti says padyām bhūmiḥ: earth arises from your feet. Akrūra later says agnir mukhaṁ te ’vanir aṅghriḥ: your face is fire and your feet are the earth. (SB 10.40.13) You have removed the suffering caused by Kaṁsa (bhāraḥ) by your appearance in Devakī (janmanā). Because of this, they say “O Lord who removes suffering (hare)!” One goal has been accomplished by benefiting the earth and devotees like us by removing the suffering of the earth which becomes the receptacle of the fragrance of your feet. But the main goal which gives success to all people will be accomplished by your astounding pastimes which give the earth and us the highest benefit. By good fortune we will see the earth marked with your little feet (padakaiḥ). The Lord should actually appear at the end of Dvārakā with a huge body like the devatās. But by mercy he appears with a small, soft body. The earth is ornamented with his foot prints and the heavens receive his mercy by his killing demons. Or the earth get s his foot prints and also his mercy by his foot prints or by spreading of bhakti, and the heavens get his mercy and his footprints when he takes the pārijāta tree since he left footprints in the pollen which fell from the pārijāta.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, marked with śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma, sanctify every place He walks — devotees who receive His mercy see and live in those marks.
Practical Applications
  • Begin your morning by mentally tracing the śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma marks of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, then carry that image into your first task.
  • Refuse to treat your workplace or home as ordinary ground — speak and act as if Kṛṣṇa's footprints could appear there, because for a devotee they do.
  • Each evening, name one 'burden of demons' (anger, envy, lust) Kṛṣṇa removed from you today, and thank Him aloud before sleep.
Reflection Questions
  • The demigods rejoiced not only that the burden of demons would be lifted, but that they would now see Kṛṣṇa's footprints on the earth — where in my own life am I more eager for relief from problems than for the actual darśana of the Lord's presence?
  • Akrūra fell down and groaned upon seeing the marks of the lotus feet on Vṛndāvana's soil — what would it take for me to develop that quality of vision, where ordinary ground becomes sacred because He has walked there?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When you scroll your feed today, notice that you are training your eyes to see brand logos, follower counts, and aesthetic 'marks' everywhere — practice instead remembering, even for thirty seconds before you open the app, that Kṛṣṇa's śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma are the marks worth being trained to see.
  • Before your next exam or audition, instead of the usual 'please let me get the result I want,' offer the effort at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet the way the demigods offered their gratitude — concretely, out loud, in your own words — and notice how much of your anxiety was actually about controlling the fruit.
  • Pick one friend group or online space where you have been quietly tolerating an asuric tone (cruelty dressed as humor, contempt dressed as honesty) — and recognize that your private relief when an adult or moderator finally intervenes is a small echo of what the demigods feel in this verse; then ask whether your own sva-dharma in that space is to keep waiting, or to speak.
householder
  • In family decisions this week — a school for the kids, a job change, a money move — explicitly distinguish the 'burden' you can act on through karma-yoga from the deeper burden of forgetfulness in the home, and address the second by establishing or strengthening one daily moment (a verse, a prasāda offering, a kīrtana) that puts Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet at the center of the household.
  • Teach your children, by your own example and not by lecture, the difference between material compassion (rescuing them from every discomfort) and real karuṇā (giving them a life oriented toward Kṛṣṇa) — pick one situation this week where you have been over-cushioning them out of bodily affection, and choose the harder, soul-serving response.
  • When you next walk into your office or workplace, train yourself to look for the marks of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet there — in an honest colleague, in an unexpected mercy, in a problem solved beyond your effort — and at the end of the day, name them in svādhyāya before bed; this slowly converts a 'workplace' into a place where you remember the Lord.
senior
  • You have already 'transitioned through' more bodies than the youth in your family can imagine — use this season to speak less about your past achievements and more, when asked, about the lotus feet that have actually carried you; mentoring in the Gauḍīya line is not transferring your story, it is pointing past yourself to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Loosen one specific attachment this month — a possession, a role, a grievance you have been polishing for decades — and offer the loosening at His lotus feet, not as renunciation for its own sake, but to make room in your consciousness for the kind of absorption Prabhupāda describes the gopīs and Vṛndāvana Vaiṣṇavas as having twenty-four hours a day.
  • When mortality crosses your mind — a friend's diagnosis, your own body's signals — do not swerve away into distraction or into impersonal consolations about 'energy' or 'the universe'; instead, return deliberately to thinking of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, knowing from this verse that one who is so absorbed na bhavāya kalpate — is not made for further material existence.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating tavānukampitām (His causeless mercy) as something to be earned by sādhana, rather than received humbly — turning devotional practice into a transaction and then resenting Kṛṣṇa when the 'vision' does not arrive on schedule.
  • Romanticizing Vṛndāvana-dhāma as a tourist destination — believing that physically traveling there substitutes for the inner cultivation of always hearing, chanting, and thinking of His lotus feet wherever one currently lives.
  • Conflating relief from material burdens (the demons being killed) with spiritual advancement — celebrating when life's difficulties ease and assuming that ease itself is Kṛṣṇa's mercy, rather than recognizing that real mercy is the awakened capacity to see His marks.
Discussion Prompts
  • The demigods rejoice that the earth's burden is being removed by Kṛṣṇa's appearance. Where in your own life do you secretly hope someone (or something) else removes a burden you've been quietly enabling — and what would it mean to recognize Kṛṣṇa, not circumstance, as the one who actually relieves it?
  • Akrūra fell down groaning when he saw the marks of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet on Vṛndāvana's soil. Most of us would walk over the same ground and notice nothing. Share with your partner one concrete habit of perception you would need to change to even begin to see śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma in the world around you — and where you suspect you are unwilling to make that change.
  • Prabhupāda says the gopīs were 'beyond the material platform' simply by thinking of the Lord's lotus feet. A skeptic in the room would say this is escapism dressed up as spirituality. How would you distinguish absorption in Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet from a sophisticated form of avoidance — and have you ever blurred the two in your own sādhana?
Role Play

A small group is leading a Sunday class for newcomers. One newcomer raises her hand: 'I love the idea that Kṛṣṇa walked the earth and left footprints, but honestly, when I went to Vṛndāvana last year I just saw dust, monkeys, and souvenir shops. I felt nothing. Am I doing something wrong, or is this whole tavānukampitām thing reserved for people the Lord has already favored — which feels like a closed loop?' Two students play the facilitators. One must respond from the verse and purport without retreating into platitudes ('keep chanting, it will come'); the other must press back as the newcomer until a real answer about causeless mercy, qualification, and the role of hearing/chanting/remembering the lotus feet is articulated. The teacher pauses the role-play whenever either side drifts into impersonalism or modern self-help.

Objections & Responses
  • Talking about 'the burden of demons being removed by the Lord's appearance' sounds like mythological wish-fulfillment. In the actual world, evil people are removed by courts, armies, and political work — not by waiting for an avatāra. Isn't this verse just a pre-modern way of outsourcing responsibility?
    The demigods in this verse are not sitting back waiting — they are the very administrators of the universe, fully engaged in their duties. Their joy is not that they no longer have to act, but that the deepest burden — one that no court, army, or policy can finally lift — is being addressed at its root by Kṛṣṇa Himself. Material arrangements relieve symptoms; the burden the demigods name is the burden of asuric consciousness, of forgetfulness of Kṛṣṇa, which reproduces itself faster than any institution can contain it. A Gauḍīya practitioner does both: discharges sva-dharma in the world with full effort, and simultaneously recognizes that lasting relief comes only when Kṛṣṇa's presence is admitted into the field. That is not outsourcing; it is correctly locating the cause.
  • The purport says these footprint-marks are 'visible to devotees who receive the causeless mercy.' That is a perfectly unfalsifiable claim — the people who see the marks are the ones the Lord has favored, and the ones who do not are simply not favored yet. How is this different from any other in-group epistemology that protects itself from challenge?
    The criticism would land if Gauḍīya tradition told the unconvinced to wait passively for mercy to descend. It does not. Prabhupāda ends the purport with a precise, testable instruction: hear, chant, and think about the Lord's lotus feet, as the Vaiṣṇavas of Vṛndāvana do. The 'causeless' in causeless mercy means it is not earned as wages — but it is invited, and the invitation has a concrete form: śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ smaraṇam. Anyone, of any background, can take up that practice today and check, over months and years, whether their perception of the world begins to change — whether dust starts to look like dust marked by Kṛṣṇa's feet. That is not a closed loop; it is an open door with a threshold that requires you to actually walk through it.
Analogy

When the king's marching standard appears on the horizon, the people in the fields know the war is over even before they hear the news; the standard's pattern — lotus, conch, club, disc — is itself the proclamation. The verse celebrates exactly such a sighting: the lotus feet are coming, and their imprint is already legible on the earth.
