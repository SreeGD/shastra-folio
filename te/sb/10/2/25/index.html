Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.25

బ్రహ్మా భవశ్చ తత్రైత్య మునిభిర్నారదాదిభి: । దేవై: సానుచరై: సాకం గీర్భిర్వృషణమైడయన్ ॥ ౨౫ ॥

Important Words
  • brahmā — the four-headed creator
    Brahmā stands at the head of every Vaiṣṇava deputation in Canto 10 — the same Brahmā who at 10.14 will confess his bewilderment before Kṛṣṇa. His role here as petitioner is the first beat of an arc that ends in his great prayer.
  • bhavaḥ ca — and Śiva
    Bhava — 'being,' an epithet of Śiva. The verse explicitly subordinates Śiva to Kṛṣṇa: Mahādeva, the lord of the universe, comes to pray to Devakī's child. Gauḍīya theology: Śiva is the topmost Vaiṣṇava (vaiṣṇavānāṁ yathā śambhuḥ).
  • munibhiḥ — with great sages
    Munis — silent ones, contemplatives. Plural and instrumental: Brahmā does not arrive alone. The Bhāgavatam consistently depicts dharmic missions as collegial, never solitary. Sādhu-saṅga is structural even in the heavens.
  • gīrbhiḥ — by transcendental prayers
    Gīr — words, speech, hymns. The instrumental case is precise: the demigods please the Lord by their words. The next many verses are these gīrs — the chapter is, formally, a hymnal.
  • vṛṣaṇam — the bestower of blessings
    Vṛṣa — from √vṛṣ, 'to rain, to shower.' The Lord is the rain-cloud of blessings; the bull (vṛṣabha) of dharma. Both senses live in the word. The demigods are positioning themselves as parched earth awaiting His shower.
Lord Brahmā and Lord Śiva, accompanied by great sages like Nārada, Devala and Vyāsa and by other demigods like Indra, Candra and Varuṇa, invisibly approached the room of Devakī, where they all joined in offering their respectful obeisances and prayers to please the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who can bestow blessings upon everyone.
Purport

Dvau bhūta-sargau loke ’smin daiva āsura eva ca ( Padma Purāṇa ). There are two classes of men — the daivas and the asuras — and there is a great difference between them. Kaṁsa, being an asura, was always planning how to kill the Supreme Personality of Godhead or His mother, Devakī. Thus he was also Kṛṣṇa conscious. But devotees are Kṛṣṇa conscious favorably ( viṣṇu-bhaktaḥ smṛto daivaḥ ). Brahmā is so powerful that he is in charge of creating an entire universe, yet he personally came to receive the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Bhava, Lord Śiva, is always jubilant in chanting the holy name of the Lord. And what to speak of Nārada? Nārada-muni, bājāya vīṇā, rādhikā-ramaṇa-nāme. Nārada Muni is always chanting the glories of the Lord, and his engagement is to travel all over the universe and find a devotee or make someone a devotee. Even a hunter was made a devotee by the grace of Nārada. Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī, in his Toṣaṇī, says that the word nārada-ādibhiḥ means that Nārada and the demigods were accompanied by other saintly persons, like Sanaka and Sanātana, all of whom came to congratulate or welcome the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Even though Kaṁsa was planning to kill Devakī, he too awaited the arrival of the Supreme Personality of Godhead ( pratīkṣaṁs taj janma ).

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Lord Brahmā and Lord Śiva, accompanied by great sages like Nārada, Devala and Vyāsa and by other demigods like Indra, Candra and Varuṇa, invisibly approached the room of Devakī, where they all joined in offering their respectful obeisances and prayers to please the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who can bestow blessings upon everyone.
Commentary

Vrsanam refers to the Lord who like a cloud rains the nectar of his pastimes. Brahma acts as the chief cultivator of the field of the fourteen worlds. Bhava or Siva is like the chief peacock with his tail feathers spread, fond of dancing before the rains. Narada and others are like cataka birds, expressing great longing for the rains of Krsna’s pastimes. The devatas are like elephants surrounded by the forest fire of Kamsa, Jarasandha and others. They all came to the prison house of Kamsa and praised Hari.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

In the prison Brahmā, Śiva, Nārada and sages like the Kumāras praised the Lord. Nārada is mentioned because he is the chief devotee with the bliss of pure devotion. Hari-vaṁśa mentions that he came first before all of them in joy to make successful his personal attempts to bring about the appearance of the Lord. The others came with followers like Gandharvas. The verb should be in the dual form but is in the plural by poetic license. Because the sages came as assistants after Brahmā and Śiva, they are mentioned in the instrumental case. Though they praised simultaneously, the main persons are mentioned as the subject. Brahmā and Śiva came first and others followed, but all of them praised together. Because the others are secondary and came late, the first verb participle may be used to indicate Brahmā and Śiva and the second verb to indicate all who came, though the normal rule is that the participle and main verb should have the same subject.

Personal Guidance
Even Brahmā, Śiva, and the great sages came humbly to welcome Kṛṣṇa — if they bow, your day's first act should be to bow too.
Practical Applications
  • Begin today by offering praṇāma before any work, joining the line of Brahmā, Śiva, and Nārada in welcoming Kṛṣṇa into your day.
  • Notice when you are positioning yourself as 'in charge' of your domain, and consciously step down — even Brahmā, who creates universes, comes as a servant.
  • Chant the holy name aloud at least once today in the mood of Lord Śiva — jubilantly, not as a duty but as a welcome.
Reflection Questions
  • Brahmā, Śiva, and Nārada — beings of vast cosmic stature — set aside their posts to invisibly approach Devakī's room and offer prayers. Where in my life do I quietly assume I am 'too senior,' too capable, or too busy to take the lower seat before Kṛṣṇa and His devotees?
  • Prabhupāda contrasts the daiva mentality (Kṛṣṇa-conscious favorably) with the āsura mentality (Kṛṣṇa-conscious as enemy, like Kaṁsa). In what specific area of my day am I tracking Kṛṣṇa the way Kaṁsa did — anxiously, as an obstacle to my plans — rather than welcoming Him the way the demigods did?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick the one classmate, teammate, or group-chat member you secretly look down on — the 'cringe' one, the try-hard, the one who doesn't fit your aesthetic. This week, greet them first, by name, in person. Brahmā goes to the prison cell; you can cross the cafeteria.
  • Before posting or commenting online today, ask: am I about to speak like Kaṁsa (Kṛṣṇa-conscious unfavorably — sharp, dismissive, scoring a point) or like Nārada (looking for one more soul to encourage)? If it's the first, don't post.
  • When the group ranks people — hottest, smartest, richest, most followed — physically step out of the conversation once. You don't need to lecture anyone. Just notice that the highest beings in this verse don't rank; they bow. Practice the muscle now, before college and career make ranking a reflex.
householder
  • This week, do one piece of household seva — taking out trash, packing a child's lunch, serving your spouse tea — that you usually consider 'beneath' your professional standing. Do it as Brahmā arriving at the prison: the senior comes in person, with prayers, not with delegation.
  • Identify the in-law, neighbor, or colleague you have quietly written off as 'an asura' in your private narration. Without changing your boundaries, change your prayer: ask Kṛṣṇa to bless them, by name, every morning for one week. Watch what loosens in your own ahaṅkāra.
  • Talk to your children about someone in the family they dismiss — the boring uncle, the loud aunt. Tell them the story of Brahmā and Śiva crowding into a cell to greet a baby, and ask them where in their own week they could 'show up invisibly' for someone overlooked. Make the verse a family conversation, not a parental lecture.
senior
  • Make a list of three people junior to you — in age, position, or spiritual seniority — and this month write each of them a short letter or call them to express genuine appreciation. Brahmā outranks Devakī cosmically and still arrives in person; rank is no excuse for distance.
  • Identify one possession, title, or role you are still privately holding as 'mine' — the seat in the temple, the deciding voice in family matters, the property deed. Begin the practice of tyāga not by giving it up dramatically, but by naming it daily before Kṛṣṇa as His, not yours. Loosen the grip while you still can choose to.
  • Find one person — a grandchild, a younger devotee, a neighbor's child — who is not yet a devotee, and become Nārada for them: not preaching, just steady, affectionate presence and the holy name audibly in your own life. Nārada made a hunter into a devotee; your remaining years are long enough for one soul.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the demigods' obeisance as mere mythic decoration and missing the point: even the universe's chief administrators model the anahaṅkāra of bowing — so the student who feels above āratī, kīrtana, or maṅgala-prayers has misunderstood his actual position.
  • Flattening 'everyone is Kṛṣṇa conscious in some way' (Kaṁsa included) into a vague inclusivism, forgetting Prabhupāda's sharp distinction — favorable remembrance is bhakti, hostile remembrance is asuric, and the two do not lead to the same destination.
  • Treating this verse's atmosphere of pre-arrival prayer as something only 'great souls' do, and skipping the daily, personal sādhana of inviting Kṛṣṇa into one's own room — one's heart, work, relationships — through gīrbhiḥ (words of prayer) before the day begins.
Discussion Prompts
  • Brahmā and Śiva — the universe's chief administrator and its great destroyer — personally show up at Devakī's door to offer obeisances. Where in your own life do you treat 'high status' or 'spiritual seniority' as a reason NOT to bow? Share a specific example with your partner where rank quietly blocked your humility.
  • The purport draws the line: Kaṁsa was Kṛṣṇa conscious too — obsessively, but as an asura. What's the honest test that distinguishes your own bhakti from Kaṁsa-style preoccupation? Disagree with each other if your tests differ — don't settle for 'we both love Kṛṣṇa.'
  • Nārada's whole engagement is to wander the universe finding or making devotees — even a hunter became one by his grace. Who in your life have you written off as 'not the type'? Name them out loud, and let the group push back on whether that judgment is yours to make.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a longtime devotee who has just been asked by their temple president to personally go and welcome a newcomer at the train station — a person from a background A privately considers 'beneath' their service (a drunk neighbor, a politically opposite relative, a former bully, etc.). Student B plays A's inner objection: 'You're senior now, send someone junior.' A must answer from the position of Brahmā and Śiva in this verse — the most powerful beings in the universe arriving invisibly, in person, with prayers in hand. Run it for five minutes, then swap. Debrief: what did your voice change when you spoke as one who bows, not as one who delegates bowing?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just mythology — Brahmā, Śiva, Indra, Candra, Varuṇa all crowding into a prison cell? It reads like a devotional fantasy, not something a modern person can take literally.
    The Gauḍīya line does take it literally — but notice what's actually being claimed. Brahmā has charge of universal creation; Śiva is the great destroyer; these are not mascots, they are the highest functionaries within material existence. The verse says the highest beings of this cosmos arrive invisibly to greet a newborn child in a prison. If even the literal frame strains you, sit first with what the picture is doing: it dismantles every hierarchy you would use to refuse to bow. The devotee does not begin by demanding the modern reader believe in Candra and Varuṇa; the devotee begins by asking — if the chief of the universe bows, on what grounds do I refuse?
  • If Kaṁsa was also 'Kṛṣṇa conscious' — just unfavorably — then consciousness of Kṛṣṇa isn't the criterion. So what's the real difference, and isn't the whole 'just remember Kṛṣṇa' teaching collapsing here?
    Sanātana Gosvāmī's line in the Padma Purāṇa cited in the purport is exact: viṣṇu-bhaktaḥ smṛto daivaḥ — the daiva is defined by bhakti, devotion, not by mere mental occupation. Kaṁsa thought of Kṛṣṇa constantly, but with the ahaṅkāra of the killer, calculating an enemy. Brahmā and Śiva also thought of Kṛṣṇa constantly, but with prayers — gīrbhir aiḍayan — the orientation of the servant. The teaching is not 'remember Kṛṣṇa anyhow'; it is remember Him as His servant. Obsession plus enmity is asura. Remembrance plus surrender (Īśvara-praṇidhāna) is bhakti. The verse is in fact tightening the criterion, not loosening it.
Analogy

When the king is about to enter the disputed city, ambassadors from every allied court gather invisibly at the gate to offer welcome and ensure safe passage. Brahmā, Śiva, the demigods and the sages are exactly such ambassadors — and Devakī's prison, by their arrival, is silently re-zoned as Vaikuṇṭha for the night.
