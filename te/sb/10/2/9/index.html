Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.9

అథాహమంశభాగేన దేవక్యా: పుత్రతాం శుభే । ప్రాప్స్యామి త్వం యశోదాయాం నన్దపత్న్‍యాం భవిష్యసి ॥ ౯ ॥

Important Words
  • aham — I
    The Lord uses the first-person singular — He is not delegating His own descent, He is taking it personally. Svayaṁ-bhagavān theology (SB 1.3.28) rests on verses like this: Kṛṣṇa does not send an avatāra in His place; He comes.
  • aṁśa - bhāgena — with My plenary expansion
    Aṁśa-bhāga is technical — the 'portion-share.' Gauḍīyas insist this does not mean a partial Kṛṣṇa appears; it means He appears with all His aṁśas (Baladeva, the catur-vyūha) accompanying Him. Pūrṇa-avatāra, not partial.
  • devakyāḥ — of Devakī
    Genitive — He will be Devakī's son. The Gauḍīyas hold that Kṛṣṇa's eternal relationship is with Yaśodā in Vraja; Devakī is honored as His Mathurā-mother whose vātsalya is a reflection of the eternal Vraja vātsalya.
  • śubhe — O all-auspicious one
    Yoga-māyā addressed again, with a different epithet — śubhā. Each time the Lord speaks to her, He names her potency. To name a śakti is to invoke her; the Lord is teaching by example.
  • yaśodāyām — in the womb of Yaśodā
    Yaśodā — 'she who gives fame.' Her name is etymological prophecy: through her vātsalya, Kṛṣṇa's fame will be sung in every yuga. The Gauḍīyas place her vātsalya-rasa just below the gopīs' mādhurya.
O all-auspicious Yoga-māyā, I shall then appear with My full six opulences as the son of Devakī, and you will appear as the daughter of mother Yaśodā, the queen of Mahārāja Nanda.
Purport

The word aṁśa-bhāgena is important in this verse. In Bhagavad-gītā (10.42) the Lord says: athavā bahunaitena kiṁ jñātena tavārjuna viṣṭabhyāham idaṁ kṛtsnam ekāṁśena sthito jagat “But what need is there, Arjuna, for all this detailed knowledge? With a single fragment of Myself I pervade and support this entire universe.” Everything is situated as a part of the Supreme Lord’s potency. In regard to Lord Kṛṣṇa’s appearance in the womb of Devakī, Brahmā played a part also because on the bank of the Milk Ocean he requested the Supreme Personality of Godhead to appear. A part was also played by Baladeva, the first expansion of Godhead. Similarly, Yoga-māyā, who appeared as the daughter of mother Yaśodā, also played a part. Thus jīva-tattva, viṣṇu-tattva and śakti-tattva are all integrated with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and when Kṛṣṇa appears, He appears with all His integrated parts. As explained in previous verses, Yoga-māyā was requested to attract Saṅkarṣaṇa, Baladeva, from the womb of Devakī to the womb of Rohiṇī, and this was a very heavy task for her. Yoga-māyā naturally could not see how it was possible for her to attract Saṅkarṣaṇa. Therefore Kṛṣṇa addressed her as śubhe, auspicious, and said, “Be blessed. Take power from Me, and you will be able to do it.” By the grace of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, anyone can do anything, for the Lord is present in everything, all things being His parts and parcels ( aṁśa-bhāgena ) and increasing or decreasing by His supreme will. Balarāma was only fifteen days older than Kṛṣṇa. By the blessings of Kṛṣṇa, Yoga-māyā became the daughter of mother Yaśodā, but by the supreme will she was not able to enjoy the parental love of her father and mother. Kṛṣṇa, however, although not actually born from the womb of mother Yaśodā, enjoyed the parental love of mother Yaśodā and Nanda. By the blessings of Kṛṣṇa, Yoga-māyā was able to achieve the reputation of being the daughter of mother Yaśodā, who also became famous by the blessings of Kṛṣṇa. Yaśodā means “one who gives fame.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O all-auspicious Yogamāyā, I shall then appear with My full six opulences as the son of Devakī, and you will appear as the daughter of mother Yaśodā, the queen of Mahārāja Nanda.
Commentary

Amsa bhaga means that knowledge, strength and other secondary qualities (amsa) accompany (bhaga=bhajana) the Lord so that he manifests completely. "I will appear as the son of Devaki with all qualities." This is Sridhara Swami’s explanation of the words in Bhavartha Dipika. According to Jiva Goswami in Vaisnava tosani there are two meanings. He is perfect in whom all the parts (amsa) enter (bhajana= pravesa or entrance). "I will appear as Devaki’s son perfect with all parts." Another meaning of the words is that amsa refers to Brahma and all other servants and bhaga refers to auspiciousness. The Lord appears for the auspiciousness of Brahma and others. " I will appear as the son of Devaki for the welfare of Brahma and others." Or another meaning is as follows. Bhaga means amsa "I will appear in the mood of a son of Devaki as a part of a part, or partially manifesting myself, not completely. Devaki will love me only with aisvarya bhava. Yasoda will manifest complete parental love for me, devoid of the restriction of aisvarya. But you, Yogamaya will simply appear from Yasoda (bhavisyasi)." It is not mentioned that she will become the daughter of Yasoda, though it is mentioned that Krsna will become the son (putratam) of Devaki. "She will not have affection for you as her daughter. Your presence in Vraja will not be noticed by anyone."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Then, very soon after, the Lord says he will appear as the son of Devakī. Later it will be explained that Baladeva was only slightly older in birth. Śrīdhara Svāmī explained the word aṁśa as follows. Bhāgena may be formed from bhaj, to possess. Thus the meaning is “existing with all his śaktis.” Or it can mean “endowed with all his qualities like knowledge and power.” Or it can mean “with the form who glances at māyā as the puruṣa.” Or it can mean “with a form which enters into all his expansions.” Or it can mean “with a form bestowing auspiciousness to jīvas like Brahmā.” Dūrgā may ask, “What is my qualification for being born to Yaśodā?” Kṛṣṇa says “O auspicious one (śubhe)!” By my order, you attain auspiciousness and thus have attained qualification. This is a benediction. This means she will be able to bewilder Nanda and others. Of the word suggests “Because you will appear in Yaśodā you will have fame (yaśaḥ) and bliss (nanda).” The confidential meaning is this. With different (bhāgena) forms (aṁśa) I will appear as the son of Devakī and also as the son of Yaśodā. Śrīdhara Svāmī explains that the word svāṁśena in the verse avatīrṇau jagaty-arthe svāṁśena bala-keśavau (SB 10.38.32) to mean “with their distinct forms.” And you will be present in Yaśodā (bhaviṣyati) but not has her daughter. This meaning can be understood because of the use of different verbs (prāpysāmi and bhaviṣyati). And if the same verb were used for both statements then it would be understood that Durgā would be born as the son of Yaśodā. But calling a girl a son must be rejected by resorting to normal usage as śruti statements like pumāṁsām putram ādhehi: place a son with Viṣṇu’ṣ form in the womb. Just as Devakī carried the Lord in her heart (SB 10.2.18) so Yaśodā also carried the Lord. Just as I take Devakī as a mother, I also take Yaśodā as my mother. Śukadeva will describe this later “Kṛṣṇa appeared as Nanda’s son.” (SB 10.5.1) Thus you will just make a show of your potency since you are māyā.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa descends with His full six opulences as Devakī's son, while Yoga-māyā appears as Yaśodā's daughter — every part of His pastime is empowered by Him alone.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where you assume credit for outcomes — quietly remember that whatever capacity you have is aṁśa-bhāgena, a fragment of His potency loaned to you.
  • When asked to do something heavier than you feel able to bear, pray as Yoga-māyā did and proceed, trusting His śubhe blessing rather than your own measured strength.
  • Refuse the impersonalist habit of crediting 'the universe' — speak Kṛṣṇa's name explicitly when offering one task back to Him this evening.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I trying to act as the sole 'doer' — when in truth, like Yoga-māyā needing Kṛṣṇa's śubhe blessing to attract Saṅkarṣaṇa, I am only a part being moved by His potency?
  • What 'heavy task' is currently before me that feels impossible by my own strength, and how would my approach change today if I treated it as something I can only accomplish aṁśa-bhāgena — by taking power from Him rather than from myself?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick one group project, club role, or study-group task this week where you have been quietly coasting because 'someone else will carry it' — own your aṁśa, your part, fully, even if it is small and unseen.
  • Yoga-māyā told Kṛṣṇa the task looked impossible; He said śubhe, take power from Me. Before your next exam or hard conversation, spend two minutes asking Kṛṣṇa for the strength specifically — not for the outcome — and notice how that changes your preparation.
  • Yaśodā got the love Devakī gave birth to. On social media you will constantly see peers receiving recognition you feel you earned. Today, when that sting hits, name it out loud as Devakī-feeling and refuse to post the subtle comparison — practice early vairāgya from the metric.
householder
  • In one family or work decision this week, deliberately act as aṁśa-bhāgena — a contributing part, not the controlling whole. Make the decision, then explicitly hand the result to Kṛṣṇa instead of micromanaging the outcome with your spouse, child, or team.
  • Identify a 'heavy task' in your household or career you have been calling impossible — a hard conversation with a teenager, a financial restructuring, a career pivot. Say to Kṛṣṇa what Yoga-māyā heard: 'I cannot see how,' and ask for the śubhe-blessing. Then take the next concrete step today, not after more planning.
  • Yoga-māyā became the daughter of Yaśodā but could not enjoy parental love as a daughter. Notice today the roles in your household where you do the work without receiving the warmth — cooking unthanked, earning unappreciated, parenting unseen — and offer that specific unreciprocated portion to Kṛṣṇa as your real vairāgya, not a vacation from family life.
senior
  • Yaśodā means 'one who gives fame,' and her fame came by Kṛṣṇa's blessing, not her biology. Pick one younger person — a grandchild, a junior colleague, a new devotee — and this month deliberately give them the fame: credit them publicly for something you could have claimed. Practice being the source, not the recipient, of yaśas.
  • Reflect that Kṛṣṇa Himself appears aṁśa-bhāgena, integrated with His parts. Make a written list of the 'parts' of your life-work — children, students, projects, possessions — and consciously demote each from 'mine' to 'a part He arranged.' Read the list at your evening sandhyā for one week and notice which item your heart refuses to release.
  • Balarāma was only fifteen days older than Kṛṣṇa, yet stepped aside from Devakī's womb so the līlā could unfold. As a senior, identify one role — in the temple, family, or community — where your continued presence is more habit than service, and take the Balarāma-step: arrange your own transfer to Rohiṇī while you still can, training a successor before mortality forces the transition.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading aṁśa-bhāgena as if Kṛṣṇa Himself is only a part or fragment of some larger Absolute — inverting the verse, which actually says the entire cosmos is sustained by a single fragment of Him; this slides into impersonalism and demotes Kṛṣṇa from supreme Personality of Godhead to one expression among many.
  • Using 'everything is His potency' as a quietist excuse to abdicate responsibility — sitting back and saying 'Kṛṣṇa will do it through me' without actually doing the heavy task in front of you; Yoga-māyā still had to act, she simply acted on borrowed power.
  • Romanticizing Yaśodā's parental love while overlooking the verse's harder teaching — that Yoga-māyā, by supreme will, was denied the parental affection her 'sister' Kṛṣṇa enjoyed; mistaking devotional service for a guarantee of the sweet outcomes we want, rather than a placement assigned by His will.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kṛṣṇa says He will appear with His full six opulences (aṁśa-bhāgena) while Yoga-māyā plays an integrated 'part.' Where in your own service do you confuse playing your part with claiming the whole credit — and where do you refuse a part because it isn't the whole? Disagree with each other honestly.
  • Yoga-māyā 'naturally could not see how it was possible' to do what Kṛṣṇa asked, and Kṛṣṇa answered 'śubhe — take power from Me.' Share a current task you have privately written off as impossible. Push back on each other: is this real impossibility, or refusal disguised as humility?
  • Yaśodā received the parental love that Devakī biologically earned; Yoga-māyā took the form of a daughter she could not enjoy as a daughter. Where in your own life have you been given Devakī's role and where Yaśodā's — and which one secretly stings? Do not let each other off with pious answers.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a senior devotee asked by the temple president to step down from a visible preaching role and instead train a younger devotee behind the scenes — 'aṁśa-bhāgena, just a part.' Student B is the younger devotee being elevated, who privately suspects A is being shelved unfairly and feels guilty taking the spot. Play out a fifteen-minute conversation where A must speak from the position of Yoga-māyā being told 'take power from Me and do this heavy task,' and B must respond as someone receiving Yaśodā's unearned good fortune. The teacher pauses the scene at any point either student slips into vague spirituality or false humility, and asks: 'What would real vairāgya sound like here, right now?'

Objections & Responses
  • This whole verse is mythological stage-management — Kṛṣṇa assigning roles to Yoga-māyā, choosing wombs, arranging swaps. It reads like a script, not a revelation. Why should a modern reader take it as anything more than ancient narrative theater?
    The 'stage-management' is precisely the point: the verse insists that nothing in Kṛṣṇa's appearance is accidental, including the apparently small parts. Modern readers are trained to dismiss what feels arranged because we worship spontaneity. But the Bhāgavatam is showing the opposite — that jīva-tattva, viṣṇu-tattva and śakti-tattva all cooperate by the Lord's will, and that even Brahmā on the bank of the Milk Ocean played a part. If you take it as theater, you reduce yourself to an audience. If you take it as the structure of reality, you become a participant who can also be assigned a part — and that is what sādhana is. The choice the verse forces is not believe-or-disbelieve; it is spectator-or-servant.
  • Telling Yoga-māyā 'take power from Me' and then telling us the same is just a religious version of 'manifest your dreams.' It is empowerment language dressed in Sanskrit. Where is the actual difference?
    The difference is the direction of dependence. 'Manifest your dreams' locates the power in you and makes Kṛṣṇa, if mentioned at all, an accessory to your agenda. Kṛṣṇa's word to Yoga-māyā is the inverse: the task is His, the power is His, and she is asked to surrender her own assessment ('I cannot see how this is possible') and become an instrument. Notice that Yoga-māyā does not get to enjoy being Yaśodā's daughter — empowerment without personal payoff is not what self-help sells. This is bhakti: you accept a heavy assignment, you draw strength from Him, and you do not collect the fruit. Test it by the fruits clause — anything that ends in your enlargement is the modern myth; anything that ends in His service is the verse.
Analogy

A king and his queen-consort enter the city through different gates so that the celebration may unfold on both sides at once. Kṛṣṇa enters through Devakī's womb in Mathurā; Yoga-māyā enters through Yaśodā's womb in Vraja. The exchange at midnight will fold the two processions into one.
