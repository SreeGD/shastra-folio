    Important Words
    • krīḍā — for playing
      Krīḍā signals līlā — sport, not necessity. The Lord's killing of Śālva is not the labour of a finite hero but the play of Bhagavān; this single word frames the entire chapter as transcendental pastime, not mundane war.
    • nara-śarīrasya — of Him whose body is humanlike
      Contiguous join (nara + śarīrasya). Gauḍīyas read this through Brahma-saṁhitā 5.39: Kṛṣṇa's form is sac-cid-ānanda even when it appears nara-like. The 'humanlikeness' is the very door by which rasa with devotees becomes possible.
    • karma adbhutam — wonderful deed
      Contiguous join. The Bhāgavatam repeatedly catalogues Kṛṣṇa's karmāṇy adbhutāni (cf. SB 10.1, 10.14). Adbhuta-rasa — wonder — is the secondary rasa that fringes every Vraja and Dvārakā pastime; Śuka pauses to mark this episode as belonging to that catalogue.
    • saubha-patiḥ — the lord of Saubha (Śālva)
      Compound from synonyms (saubha + patiḥ). Śālva is named not by his own name but by his sky-city — a deliberate Bhāgavata signal that he is property of his vehicle, not its master. The asuric pattern: identification with one's hardware.
    • śṛṇu — please hear
      Imperative of √śru — the very root that names śravaṇam, the first of the nine limbs of bhakti (SB 7.5.23). Śuka begins not with 'see' or 'understand' but with 'hear': Bhāgavata-rasa enters through the ear.
    Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Now please hear, O King, another wondrous deed performed by Lord Kṛṣṇa, who appeared in His humanlike body to enjoy transcendental pastimes. Hear how He killed the master of Saubha.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Now please hear, O King, another wondrous deed performed by Lord Kṛṣṇa, who appeared in His humanlike body to enjoy transcendental pastimes. Hear how He killed the master of Saubha.
    Commentary

    In the seventy sixth chapter, Salva, obtaining a boon from Siva, enters battle. Dyuman strikes Pradyumna so that he must leave the battle field. Hear the exploits of Krsna, who took a human form and performed playful pastimes (krida nara sarirasya). It is comparable to the compound saka parthivah, vegetable king, a king who enjoys vegetables.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    After this (atha), when Kṛṣṇa returned to Dvārakā, he killed Sālva. That is explained later. This act was astonishing because Kṛṣṇa had a human like body for pastimes. He distributed various sweet pastimes in this form. What was the action? His manner of killing Sālva was amazing.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    After this (atha), when Kṛṣṇa returned to Dvārakā, he killed Sālva. That is explained now. This act was astonishing because Kṛṣṇa had a human like body for pastimes. He distributed various sweet pastimes in this form, because he attracted everyone’s heart (kṛṣṇasya). O king! You are suitable for protecting the people by hearing this. What was the action? Killing Sālva was amazing. Hear how he killed Sālva.

    Important Words
    • śiśupāla-sakhaḥ — a friend of Śiśupāla's
      Compound from synonyms. In Gauḍīya hermeneutics sakhya defines a person more than birth does: by being Śiśupāla's friend, Śālva inherits Śiśupāla's offense. The verse is silently teaching the doctrine of sat-saṅga and asat-saṅga.
    • rukmiṇī-udvāhe — at Rukmiṇī's wedding
      Compound from synonyms. Rukmiṇī is Lakṣmī herself (SB 10.52.39 commentary, Prabhupāda); the wedding from which Śālva was driven was not a mundane elopement but Lakṣmī returning to Nārāyaṇa. To stand against it is to stand against Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa.
    • yadubhiḥ — by the Yadus
      Instrumental plural of Yadu. The Yadu dynasty is Kṛṣṇa's own clan — Bhagavān has personally chosen them as His parikara. Defeat by the Yadus is defeat by an extension of the Lord; Śālva fails to read it that way.
    • nirjitaḥ — defeated
      Past participle of ni + √ji. The same verbal stem that gives jaya — victory. Śālva has tasted parājaya at the hands of Kṛṣṇa's men and chooses revenge instead of śaraṇa; the chapter will trace that fork to its end.
    • jarāsandha-ādayaḥ — Jarāsandha and others
      Compound from synonyms. Jarāsandha is the archetypal Kṛṣṇa-vairī of Canto 10 — killed by Bhīma at Kṛṣṇa's direction (SB 10.72). Naming his company here marks Śālva as belonging to the line of asura-allies who must eventually be cleared from the earth.
    Śālva was a friend of Śiśupāla’s. When he attended the wedding of Rukmiṇī, the Yadu warriors defeated him in battle, along with Jarāsandha and the other kings.

    Analogy

    Like a moth whose first scorching at the lamp's edge only sharpens its appetite for the flame, Śālva — once defeated at Rukmiṇī's wedding — returns now with greater force. Saṅga with Śiśupāla has made his envy structural.

    Stories
    Rukmiṇī's wedding and the kings who left humiliated

    In SB 10.52–54, Kṛṣṇa carries off Rukmiṇī from the Vidarbha wedding pavilion as Śiśupāla stands waiting at the altar. Jarāsandha, Śālva, Dantavakra, Pauṇḍraka and the other kings pursue and are routed by the Yadu warriors led by Balarāma.

    That humiliation is the seed of this chapter. Where a Yudhiṣṭhira would have taken defeat as instruction, Śālva — bound by sakhya to Śiśupāla — takes it as fuel. Saṅga with a Kṛṣṇa-dveṣī decides his trajectory more than his own native disposition.

    Important Words
    • pratijñām — a promise
      Accusative of pratijñā — formal vow before witnesses. In dharma-śāstra a pratijñā binds the kṣatriya; here it binds Śālva only to his death. Vows untethered from dharma are ropes that hang the swearer.
    • ayādavām — devoid of Yādavas
      Negative compound (a + yādava). Since Kṛṣṇa Himself is a Yādava (yadu-pati), to make the earth ayādava is to make it akṛṣṇa — an explicit declaration of war against Bhagavān, however ignorantly framed.
    • kṣmām — the earth
      Accusative of kṣmā — Bhū-devī, who in SB 10.1.17 had prayed to Brahmā precisely for relief from such kings. Śālva imagines himself the earth's saviour while being one of the burdens the earth begged to be freed from.
    • pauruṣam — prowess
      From puruṣa. Pauruṣa is manly valor; the Bhāgavatam consistently contrasts asuric pauruṣa (self-derived, soon-broken) with the daiva pauruṣa of devotees who fight knowing Bhagavān is the doer (cf. Arjuna at Kurukṣetra).
    • paśyata — just see
      Imperative plural of √paś. The asura always demands an audience: see my power. The devotee instead asks the Lord to see him (paśya mām, Gītā 11.7). The grammar of the eye reverses across the asura/devotee line.
    Śālva swore in the presence of all the kings: “I will rid the earth of Yādavas. Just see my prowess!”
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Śālva swore in the presence of all the kings: "I will rid the earth of Yādavas. Just see my prowess!"
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    He made a promise to kill all the Yādavas while the kings listened. The possessive case is used to suggest that they listened, but did not respect his oath, since it was impossible to fulfill. Or the possessive case can stand for the locative. Then the meaning is that they supported his promise.

    Analogy

    Like a man who, stung by a wasp, vows aloud to empty the air of insects — the vow announces only the depth of his sting. Śālva's promise to make the earth ayādava is the cry of a wound, not the speech of a hero.

    Stories
    Hiraṇyakaśipu's vow against Viṣṇu

    In SB 7.3, Hiraṇyakaśipu, brother of Hiraṇyākṣa, vows in the same way before his court to make the world devoid of Viṣṇu-devotees, and goes off to perform austerities for a boon. Śālva's pattern is identical: public oath, then tapasya aimed at a weapon.

    The Bhāgavatam repeats the structure to teach that vows born from envy of Bhagavān curve back on the vow-maker. Hiraṇyakaśipu's own son Prahlāda becomes the instrument; Śālva's chosen vehicle Saubha will likewise prove no shelter.

    Important Words
    • mūḍhaḥ — the fool
      From √muh — to be deluded. Śuka's editorial label. The Bhāgavatam calls anyone mūḍha who undergoes immense effort without first asking who Bhagavān is (cf. Gītā 7.15, na māṁ duṣkṛtino mūḍhāḥ).
    • paśu-patim — Śiva, the protector of animallike men
      Compound from synonyms. Paśu literally means animal — by extension the bound jīva. Prabhupāda's gloss reads paśu-pati as guardian of those still in animal consciousness: the right deity for one who wants only a weapon, not Bhagavān Himself.
    • āradhayām āsa — worshiped
      Periphrastic perfect of √rādh. Same verbal root from which ārādhana and rādhā arise: the action of pleasing. Here the action is correctly performed but pointed at the wrong end — kāmya-rādhana, worship for a private wish, not nirupādhi-bhakti.
    • pāṁśu — of dust
      A handful of dust per day. The Bhāgavatam's austerity-asuras choose extreme physical reduction, but the Gauḍīya reading is sharp: tapas without bhakti, however terrifying, is bhukti or mukti aimed asuric — not the road to Bhagavān.
    • muṣṭim — a handful
      Accusative of muṣṭi. The same word that names the boxing-match grip of Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika in SB 10.43–44 — the asuras Kṛṣṇa killed. Even the dust Śālva clenches foreshadows the asura's clenched, fruitless effort.
    Having thus made his vow, the foolish King proceeded to worship Lord Paśupati [Śiva] as his deity by eating a handful of dust each day, and nothing more.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Having thus made his vow, the foolish King proceeded to worship Lord Paśupati [Śiva] as his deity by eating a handful of dust each day, and nothing more.
    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    He made this promise because he was foolish, with no intelligence, since it was impossible to kill them. He then worshipped Śiva because he was his family deity (devam), the master of people who were like animals (such as Sālva), and who could fulfill his desires (prabhum). He should enjoy happiness (nṛpaḥ). His vow was quite suitable: eating dust. It was ridiculous.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    He then worshipped Śiva because Śiva was his family deity or deity of the demons (devam), the protector of animals or people who were like animals (such as Sālva), indicating his very merciful nature, and who could quickly fulfill his desires (prabhum). The king (nṛpaḥ), though qualified for enjoying happiness, ate dust.

    Analogy

    Like a man who plants a tree in poisoned soil and waters it daily expecting sweet fruit, Śālva subjects himself to year-long austerity, but the goal — destruction of Kṛṣṇa's people — is the poison; no length of tapas can purify a fruit aimed at Bhagavān's devotees.

    Stories
    Vṛkāsura's handful of ash

    In SB 10.88 Vṛkāsura performs severe tapas to Śiva, eating only what he can pick up from sacrificial ash, and obtains the boon that anyone whose head he touches will die. He immediately attempts to use it on Śiva himself.

    The pattern is the Bhāgavata's standard diagnosis: severe austerity, plus envy of Bhagavān, plus the easy-to-please Śiva, yields a boon that destroys the asker. Śālva walks this same road one canto earlier.

    Important Words
    • āśu-toṣaḥ — he who is quickly pleased
      Compound from synonyms (āśu + toṣaḥ). A standard epithet of Śiva — the easy-to-please. The Bhāgavatam uses it ironically here: Śiva's very generosity becomes the asura's noose. Prabhupāda repeatedly warns devotees not to approach āśu-toṣa devas for material boons.
    • umā-patiḥ — the master of Umā
      Compound from synonyms. Naming Śiva by his relation to Umā (Pārvatī) gentles the verse — even when about to grant a destructive boon, Śiva is a husband, a householder. The Bhāgavatam never reduces him to mere boon-machine.
    • saṁvatsara — of a year
      A full solar year of tapas. In dharma-śāstra a saṁvatsara is the ritually complete cycle. Śālva pays the full price; even so, what he buys is his own death — proof that ritual completeness without right object is empty.
    • śaraṇam — for shelter
      Same word that anchors the great śaraṇāgati verses — Gītā 18.66 (mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja) and SB 11.5.41. Śālva goes to śaraṇa, but to the wrong feet and for the wrong reason. The grammar of surrender is empty when the heart is full of vengeance.

    Important Words
    • nirudhya — besieging
      Gerund of ni + √rudh (to obstruct). The same root behind nirodha — yogic restraint. The asura's nirodha is a siege; the yogī's is of the senses. Same grammar, opposite directions.
    • vimāna-agryāt — from the best of airships
      Contiguous join (vimāna + agryāt). Vimāna is the Vedic flying chariot — devas have them, asuras imitate. Agrya means foremost; even the foremost asuric craft is, in Bhāgavata measure, less than a devotee's bare feet on Vraja's dust.
    • śastra-vṛṣṭayaḥ — torrents of weapons
      Contiguous join (śastra + vṛṣṭayaḥ). Vṛṣṭi literally means rain — the asura's offering to Dvārakā is a perverse rain, mirroring the prema-vṛṣṭi that devotees pray Kṛṣṇa will rain upon them (cf. Caitanya-caritāmṛta's prema-megha imagery).
    • cakravātaḥ — a whirlwind
      From cakra + vāta. A cyclone. Prabhupāda's commentary reads Śālva's whirlwind as māyic show-business — calculated terror against a city protected by the Lord's own sudarśana-cakra. Cakravāta against cakra-pāṇi: the wind cannot win.
    • purīm — the city
      Accusative of purī. The same word used for Vaikuṇṭha and for Kṛṣṇa's heart. The Bhāgavatam often reads attack on the Lord's city as the asura's confession that the Lord has a city — that He is personal, located, available.
    • rajasā — with dust
      Instrumental of rajas. Pun: rajas the substance (dust) and rajas the guṇa (passion). The whole asuric assault is rajas-blanketed — covering the directions, the way passion covers the cit-svarūpa of the jīva.

    Tripurāri Śiva and the three cities

    Verse 12 itself draws the simile: as the earth was tormented by the three aerial cities of the demons. In Purāṇic tradition the three cities (Tripura) of Maya Dānava — gold, silver, iron — afflicted the earth until Śiva, with the earth as chariot and Viṣṇu as arrow, burned them down.

    Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī explains that actually Lord Pradyumna has a sac-cid-ānanda body, an eternal, spiritual form that can never be wounded by mundane weapons. Dāruka’s son, however, was a great devotee of the Lord, and out of intense love he feared for the safety of his master and thus removed Him from the battlefield. He was very powerful, and although bitten by twenty-five of Pradyumna’s arrows, he suddenly attacked Pradyumna with his fierce club and struck Him so strongly that Pradyumna became unconscious.     Important Words
    • pitarau — with My fathers
      Dual of pitṛ — 'the two fathers.' Rāma (Balarāma) is Pradyumna's uncle but elder enough to be addressed as a father — and in Gauḍīya theology Balarāma is the ādi-guru, while Kṛṣṇa is the iṣṭa. Both fathers must be faced.

      Compound from synonyms. Rāma — Balarāma; Keśava — Kṛṣṇa, He of beautiful hair (also slayer of the demon Keśī). The dual form binds the two brothers as one ālambana for Pradyumna's bhāva.

      Gerund of abhi + sam + √gam. Coming-toward-and-together-with. Used in Sanskrit for both formal audiences and intimate meetings; here both senses operate — formal Yadu-court report and intimate father-son encounter.

      Past participle of apa + √kram. Stepping-away. Same prefix as apasarpaṇam (verse 28) — Pradyumna lingers on the cluster of 'away-ness' words; each is a different way of naming the same scratch on his honor.

As Pradyumna fought with Dyumān, other Yadu heroes like Gada, Sātyaki, and Sāmba began to create havoc among Śālva's army. The battle continued in this way for twenty-seven days and nights. When Lord Kṛṣṇa returned to Dvārakā, He found it under siege. At once He ordered Dāruka to drive Him onto the battlefield. Suddenly Śālva noticed the Lord and threw his spear at Kṛṣṇa's charioteer, but the Lord shattered the weapon into hundreds of pieces and pierced Śālva and his Saubha vehicle with numerous arrows. Śālva responded by shooting an arrow that struck Kṛṣṇa's left arm. Amazingly, the Lord dropped the Śārṅga bow He was holding in His left hand. The demigods watching the battle cried out in alarm upon seeing the bow fall, while Śālva took the opportunity to insult Kṛṣṇa. Lord Kṛṣṇa then struck Śālva with His club, but the demon, vomiting blood, disappeared. A moment later a man came before Lord Kṛṣṇa and, after offering Him obeisances, introduced himself as a messenger from mother Devakī. The man informed the Lord that His father, Vasudeva, had been kidnapped by Śālva. Upon hearing this, Lord Kṛṣṇa seemed to lament like an ordinary man. Śālva then led forward someone who looked just like Vasudeva, decapitated him and took the head with him into his Saubha airship. Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, however, could understand the magic tricks of Śālva. Thus He pierced Śālva with a shower of arrows and struck the Saubha vehicle with His club, demolishing it. Śālva descended from his airplane and rushed toward Lord Kṛṣṇa to attack Him, but the Lord took up His Sudarśana disc and severed Śālva's head from his body. With the killing of Śālva, the demigods in the sky played kettledrums in jubilation. The demon Dantavakra then took a vow to avenge his friend Śālva's death.
