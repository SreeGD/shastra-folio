Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.76.18-19

Important Words
  • vivyādha — He shot
    Perfect of vi + √vyadh (to pierce). The same root as ā-vedha — initiation by piercing. Gauḍīya guru-paramparā speaks of mantra-vedha; Pradyumna's arrows have, narratively, a piercing-to-awaken function.
  • svarṇa-puṅkhaiḥ — with gold-shafted
    Contiguous join (svarṇa + puṅkhaiḥ). Gold is Lakṣmī's metal; Kṛṣṇa's son's arrows carry Lakṣmī on their tails. The weapon's metallurgy itself testifies to the warrior's lineage.
  • ayaḥ-mukhaiḥ — with iron heads
    Contiguous join (ayaḥ + mukhaiḥ). Iron at the head, gold on the shaft — the arrow itself is a model of the kṣatriya: hard at the point of contact, lustrous at the source of release.
  • sannata-parvabhiḥ — with smooth joints
    Contiguous join (sannata + parvabhiḥ). The joints of an arrow's reed-stem must be polished or it flies untrue. Bhāgavata craftsmen of war honor detail; the verse pauses to admire the workmanship.
  • dhvajinī-pālam — the commander-in-chief
    Contiguous join (dhvajinī + pālam) from synonyms. Dhvajinī — 'she who has a banner,' the army. Pradyumna strikes the banner-keeper with twenty-five, the king with a hundred — śāstric proportionality of arrows to rank.
  • vāhanāni — the carriers
    Neuter plural of vāhana — mounts. Even the horses and elephants receive only three arrows each. The Bhāgavatam in passing teaches the kṣatriya-dharma of mercy toward non-combatant animals.
Lord Pradyumna’s arrows all had gold shafts, iron heads and perfectly smooth joints. With twenty-five of them He struck down Śālva’s commander-in-chief [Dyumān], and with one hundred He struck Śālva himself. Then He pierced Śālva’s officers with one arrow each, his chariot drivers with ten arrows each, and his horses and other carriers with three arrows each.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Lord Pradyumna's arrows all had gold shafts, iron heads and perfectly smooth joints. With twenty-five of them He struck down Śālva's commander-in-chief [Dyumān], and with one hundred He struck Śālva himself. Then He pierced Śālva's officers with one arrow each, his chariot drivers with ten arrows each, and his horses and other carriers with three arrows each.
Commentary

He pierced the leaders of the armies (dhvajinipalan) with twenty five arrows with smooth joints. With ten arrows each he pierced the charioteers (netrn).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

With his arrows having iron heads and gold shafts he was able to deeply pierce the enemies’ bodies. He pierced each of the officers with one arrow each, each driver with ten arrows each and each carrier with three arrows each.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

Like an expert physician whose every dose is calibrated — twenty-five drops here, a hundred there, three for the carriers — Pradyumna's arrows are not a flood but a precise prescription. The asura's army is treated, not slaughtered.
