Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.6.41

Important Words
  • saurabhyam — the fragrance
    Accusative of saurabhya (fragrance). The same root as aguru-saurabha in verse 34 — Bhāgavata is building a fragrance-thread across verses. Gauḍīya note: saurabhya is one of the standard signs of divine presence (śrī-vigraha emits fragrance), and here the by-product of His act emits it.
  • vraja - okasaḥ — the inhabitants of Vrajabhūmi
    Compound — vraja + okas. The same Vrajavāsīs across verses, now in distant villages. The verse widens its geography: Vraja is not only Gokula proper but its extended pastoral countryside, all of which receives the news of Pūtanā-mokṣa through her own scent.
  • avaghrāya — having smelled (through the nostrils)
    Gerund of ava-ghrā (to smell out). Bhāgavata's choice of olfactory verb — not śravaṇa (hearing), the usual entry-point, but ghrāṇa (smell). Even the most peripheral sense, when met by Kṛṣṇa's act, becomes a vehicle of recognition.
  • vrajam — to Vrajabhūmi
    Accusative — toward Vraja proper, Nanda's village. The villagers move inward toward the source. Bhakti's geography is centripetal — every clue draws the searcher closer to the center where the Lord is.
  • kaṭa - dhūmasya — of the smoke from the pyre
    Compound — kaṭa (mat, here pyre-pile) + dhūma (smoke) + genitive. Even the noun for the smoke is precise — funerary smoke, not kitchen smoke. The verse refuses to soften the source; the fragrance comes from a real cremation.
Upon smelling the fragrance of the smoke emanating from Pūtanā’s burning body, many inhabitants of Vrajabhūmi in distant places were astonished. “Where is this fragrance coming from?” they asked. Thus they went to the spot where Pūtanā’s body was being burnt.
Purport

The aroma of the smoke emanating from a burning fire is not always very favorable. Therefore upon smelling such a wonderful fragrance, the inhabitants of Vraja were astonished.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Upon smelling the fragrance of the smoke emanating from Pūtanā's burning body, many inhabitants of Vrajabhūmi in distant places were astonished. "Where is this fragrance coming from?" they asked. Thus they went to the spot where Pūtanā's body was being burnt.
Commentary

After settling the philosophical issues, Sukadeva returns to the topic at hand. Kata means burning of the dead body. Kutah kim idam. Where does this smell come from? Is this aguru incense coming from Indrapuri down through earth and trying to enter Sutala? Or is it ascending from Bali’s abode on Sutala through the earth up to Svarga? Or is it coming from Kuvera’s city in the north or from Varuna’s city in the west? In this way the cowherd men entertained many questions.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Kaṭa means a crematorium according to Kṣīrasvāmī. Inhabitants who had gone elsewhere before these events spoke in this way.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Kaṭa means a crematorium. Vrajauksasaḥ refers to Nanda and others.

Personal Guidance
When a soul aggressor is consumed in Kṛṣṇa's service, even her remains exude divine fragrance — proximity to Him purifies what would otherwise reek.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today one association or input you assume is 'toxic smoke' and ask whether contact with Kṛṣṇa's service could transmute it into something fragrant.
  • Offer one ordinary task — cooking, cleaning, a work email — explicitly to Kṛṣṇa and watch how its 'aroma' changes for those around you.
  • Refuse to judge a person solely by their outward role; investigate, like the Vrajavāsīs tracking the scent, what unseen contact with the Lord may be at work.
Analogy

Vrajavāsīs in distant villages catching the aguru-fragrance of Pūtanā's burning body and walking toward it puzzled is the neighborhood drawn by the smell of incense to a temple they did not know had been built overnight. Where Kṛṣṇa has acted, even the smoke advertises Him; bhakti is the nose that learns to follow that scent past the obvious source.

Stories
The fragrance of Caitanya's chanting

Caitanya-bhāgavata records that as Mahāprabhu walked the streets of Nadia in saṅkīrtana, the air around Him became unaccountably fragrant — passersby came out of houses asking where the scent was coming from, and were drawn into the kīrtana itself.

SB 10.6.41 is the seed of this signature: contact with the Lord's body transmutes even smoke into aguru, and that fragrance is recruiting. Vraja's distant inhabitants are being pulled in by what is, on the surface, the fumes of a corpse — but is, in truth, an invitation.
