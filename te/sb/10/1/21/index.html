Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.21

గిరం సమాధౌ గగనే సమీరితాంనిశమ్య వేధాస్త్రిదశానువాచ హ । గాం పౌరుషీం మే శ‍ృణుతామరా: పున-ర్విధీయతామాశు తథైవ మా చిరమ్ ॥ ౨౧ ॥

While in trance, Lord Brahmā heard the words of Lord Viṣṇu vibrating in the sky. Thus he told the demigods: O demigods, hear from me the order of Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, the Supreme Person, and execute it attentively without delay.
Purport

It appears that the words of the Supreme Personality of Godhead can be heard in trance by competent persons. Modern science gives us telephones, by which one can hear sound vibrations from a distant place. Similarly, although other persons cannot hear the words of Lord Viṣṇu, Lord Brahmā is able to hear the Lord’s words within himself. This is confirmed in the beginning of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.1.1) : tene brahma hṛdā ya ādi-kavaye. Ādi-kavi is Lord Brahmā. In the beginning of the creation, Lord Brahmā received the instructions of Vedic knowledge from Lord Viṣṇu through the medium of the heart ( hṛdā ). The same principle is confirmed herewith. While Brahmā was in trance, he was able to hear the words of Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, and he carried the Lord’s message to the demigods. Similarly, in the beginning, Brahmā first received the Vedic knowledge from the Supreme Personality of Godhead through the core of the heart. In both instances the same process was used in transmitting the message to Lord Brahmā. In other words, although Lord Viṣṇu was invisible even to Lord Brahmā, Lord Brahmā could hear Lord Viṣṇu’s words through the heart. The Supreme Personality of Godhead is invisible even to Lord Brahmā, yet He descends to this earth and becomes visible to people in general. This is certainly an act of His causeless mercy, but fools and nondevotees think that Kṛṣṇa is an ordinary historical person. Because they think that the Lord is an ordinary person like them, they are described as mūḍha ( avajānanti māṁ mūḍhāḥ ). The causeless mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead is neglected by such demoniac persons, who cannot understand the instructions of Bhagavad-gītā and who therefore misinterpret them.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
While in trance, Lord Brahmā heard the words of Lord Viṣṇu vibrating in the sky. Thus he told the demigods: O demigods, hear from me the order of Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, the Supreme Person, and execute it attentively without delay.
Commentary

Brahma did not directly see or meet Visnu. In a state of meditation he heard a voice in the sky. From this one can understand that to see Visnu directly is difficult even for Brahma. Thus when the origin ofVisnu, Lord Krsna, can be seen directly by all men when he appears on the earth, it is a display of extraordinary mercy, that is not available at any other time. Paurusim gam means the purusa’s or Ksirodakasayi Visnu’s words.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

On the bank of the Milk Ocean, while in samādhi he heard a message spoken by the Lord, without seeing any person, which came from the sky. This indicates that even the form of Viṣṇu was difficult to see. This shows that people who saw the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, Svayam Bhagavān, the source of this Viṣṇu in Vṛndāvana later, had special good fortune. The word ha means “clearly” or “in joy.” Pauruṣīm means “by the puruṣa.” Brahmā understood who was speaking: sṛjāmi tan-niyukto ’haṁ haro harati tad-vaśaḥ | viśvaṁ puruṣa-rūpeṇa paripāti tri-śakti-dhṛk || I create under his order, and Śiva destroys under his order. Holding his three energies, he protects the universe as the Paramātmā. SB 2.6.32 “Hear the words that the puruṣa known as Viṣṇu spoke.” He said this to give the others faith in those words. Hear now (āśu) without commotion. O devatās (amarāḥ)! This means “You will become immortal by following the instructions.”

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa's voice reaches the surrendered heart in samādhi — Brahmā hears, then transmits without delay; our work is to listen inwardly and obey at once.
Practical Applications
  • Sit in silence before your morning duties and listen for Kṛṣṇa's guidance through the heart (caitya-guru) rather than scrolling for outside opinions.
  • Notice the next clear instruction you receive from guru, śāstra, or conscience today, and execute it āśu — immediately, without the usual delay of debate.
  • Refuse the mūḍha view that Kṛṣṇa is an ordinary historical figure; speak of Him today as the living Supreme Person who still instructs His devotees from within.
Reflection Questions
  • When the Lord's instructions arrive through the heart in trance, am I sitting still enough — through japa, śravaṇa, and svādhyāya — to actually hear, or is my inner space so crowded with my own commentary that even Brahmā's reception couldn't penetrate?
  • Which order from guru, śāstra, or sādhus have I already 'heard' but quietly delayed — and what does Brahmā's āśu tathaiva mā ciram (do it now, without delay) expose about my preferred mode of obedience?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before opening any feed today, sit for five minutes in deliberate silence — your version of samādhi as a beginner — and notice what 'voice' actually has access to your heart when the noise stops.
  • Pick one instruction you have already received from a trustworthy elder (parent, teacher, devotee mentor) and execute it tathaiva mā ciram — exactly as told, without delay — instead of negotiating it down to something easier.
  • Watch how your friend group handles a directive from a coach, professor, or senior — who delays, who edits the message, who acts cleanly? Locate yourself honestly in that pattern and adjust this week.
householder
  • Treat your morning sādhana as the channel — if you skip it, do not be surprised when family decisions later in the day feel reactive rather than guided; protect that time the way Brahmā protects his samādhi.
  • When you receive a clear instruction from śāstra or guru about money, children, or work, do not let it sit in 'I'll think about it' — translate it into one concrete action this week, the way the demigods translated Viṣṇu's order into immediate execution.
  • In your role as the 'Brahmā' of your household — the one who passes guidance to children and dependents — examine whether you are softening Kṛṣṇa's message to make it palatable, or transmitting it cleanly with the diacritics intact.
senior
  • Use the diminishing demands of this stage to deepen the listening — what younger life would not give you the silence for, age is now offering; do not waste it on television and grievance.
  • Identify one mentee — a grandchild, a younger devotee, a colleague — and accept the Brahmā-role consciously: you are the link in the chain now, and what you transmit (or fail to transmit) will outlive you.
  • When the body sends its own 'instructions' — pain, fatigue, the nearness of death — practice hearing through them rather than being deafened by them, so that Kṛṣṇa's voice in the heart remains the loudest signal in the room.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating 'hearing in the heart' as license for personal intuition or feelings — confusing the mind's whispers with Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu's actual instruction, which arrives only through the bona fide paramparā and the Bhāgavatam, not through one's own ahaṅkāra.
  • Drifting into impersonalism by equating Brahmā's inward reception with a generic 'inner voice' or 'higher Self,' when the verse insists on a specific personal Lord (Viṣṇu) speaking and a specific person (Brahmā) hearing and faithfully relaying.
  • Hearing the message but stalling on transmission and execution — savoring the contemplative 'samādhi' phase while neglecting the punar vidhīyatām āśu duty to immediately carry the order to those one is responsible for (family, students, juniors).
Discussion Prompts
  • Brahmā hears Viṣṇu only because he is in samādhi — not because Viṣṇu spoke louder. Where in your own life are you waiting for Kṛṣṇa to 'speak louder' instead of doing the work to quiet the receiver? Push back honestly on each other if someone offers a tidy answer.
  • Prabhupāda compares hearing Viṣṇu in trance to a telephone — modern science only catches up to what yogīs already did. Is this comparison persuasive to you, or does it feel apologetic? Defend your actual position to your partner, not the 'devotee' position.
  • The verse turns on a chain of command: Viṣṇu instructs Brahmā, Brahmā instructs the demigods, the demigods act 'without delay.' Where in your life are you the demigod who delays? Where are you the Brahmā who hears but softens the message before passing it on?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a senior devotee who has just come out of japa and feels strongly that the group should change a service arrangement — they say it 'came in meditation.' Student B is a thoughtful but skeptical peer who wants evidence, not impressions. Role-play a 5-minute conversation where A must convey what they heard with the gravity of Brahmā addressing the demigods (śṛṇuta — hear me, and act without delay), while B presses on the difference between Brahmā's qualified hearing and ordinary mental impressions. The teacher then asks: how would each of you actually verify the instruction before acting tathaiva mā ciram?

Objections & Responses
  • Hearing Viṣṇu 'in the sky' while in trance sounds like auditory hallucination dressed up in Sanskrit. Why should a modern person take this literally?
    We are not asked to take Brahmā's hearing as a private psychological event — it is a transmission within a lineage that produced the Vedas, the Bhāgavatam, and a continuous tradition of realized teachers whose instructions, when followed, yield observable change in the practitioner. Prabhupāda himself uses the telephone analogy not to flatter science but to point out that subtle transmission of sound at a distance is not, in principle, absurd to the modern mind. The real question is not whether the channel exists but whether you have purified the receiver — hṛdā, the heart — enough to trust what you hear. Until then, you do not rely on private audition; you rely on guru, sādhu, and śāstra. That is the safeguard against hallucination.
  • If Viṣṇu can speak only to Brahmā and Brahmā alone passes it down, this is just spiritual hierarchy protecting itself. Why can't Kṛṣṇa speak directly to me?
    He does — through the caitya-guru in the heart, through the dīkṣā- and śikṣā-guru, through śāstra, and ultimately through the holy name, which is non-different from Him. The verse is not gate-keeping; it is describing how revelation is structured so that it remains undistorted. Brahmā receives, the demigods execute — and notice that even Brahmā does not freelance; he says 'hear from me the order of Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu,' attributing it carefully. The hierarchy is not a wall between you and Kṛṣṇa; it is the very means by which His words reach you uncorrupted. Bypass it and you will hear plenty of voices, but they will mostly be your own ahaṅkāra speaking in a Sanskrit accent.
