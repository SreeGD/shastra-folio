Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.56

Important Words
  • kāle upāvṛtte — in due course of time, when it was ripe
    Kāle (locative of kāla, time) + upāvṛtte (locative of past participle of upa-ā-vṛt, 'having approached'). 'When the time had ripened.' The same kāla that is Bhagavān's own śakti (verse 22) operates here through Devakī's pregnancies.
  • devakī — Devakī, the wife of Vasudeva
    From devaka (her father, named in 31-32). The verse uses her name for the first time in narrative position rather than dialogic position. She is now the chapter's pivotal womb — Bhāgavata's narrative honors her by name placement.
  • sarva-devatā — Devakī, to whom all the demigods and God Himself appeared
    Sarva (all) + devatā (deity). The epithet is staggering: she from whom all the devatās appeared. Gauḍīya reading: Devakī is the human form of the cosmic feminine through whom Bhagavān enters human history. The honor is doctrinal.
  • anuvatsaram — year after year
    Anu (after) + vatsara (year). Year-by-year, the eight sons came. The Bhāgavata's narrative rhythm: the cosmic plan unfolds in ordinary human time. Devakī's calendar is also Bhagavān's calendar.
Each year thereafter, in due course of time, Devakī, the mother of God and all the demigods, gave birth to a child. Thus she bore eight sons, one after another, and a daughter named Subhadrā.
Purport

The spiritual master is sometimes glorified as sarva-devamayo guruḥ ( Bhāg. 11.17.27 ). By the grace of the guru, the spiritual master, one can understand the different kinds of devas. The word deva refers to God, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is the original source of all the demigods, who are also called devas. In Bhagavad-gītā (10.2) the Lord says, aham ādir hi devānām: “I am the source of all the devas. ” The Supreme Lord, Viṣṇu, the Original Person, expands in different forms. Tad aikṣata bahu syām ( Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.2.3). He alone has expanded into many. Advaitam acyutam anādim ananta-rūpam ( Brahma-saṁhitā 5.33 ). There are different grades of forms, known as svāṁśa and vibhinnāṁśa. The svāṁśa expansions, or viṣṇu-tattva, are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, whereas the vibhinnāṁśa are jīva-tattva, who are part and parcel of the Lord ( mamaivāṁśo jīva-loke jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ ). If we accept Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead and worship Him, all the parts and expansions of the Lord are automatically worshiped. Sarvārhaṇam acyutejyā ( Bhāg. 4.31.14 ). Kṛṣṇa is known as Acyuta ( senayor ubhayor madhye rathaṁ sthāpaya me ’cyuta ). By worshiping Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa, one automatically worships all the demigods. There is no need of separately worshiping either the viṣṇu-tattva or jīva-tattva. If one concentrates upon Kṛṣṇa, one worships everyone. Therefore, because mother Devakī gave birth to Kṛṣṇa, she is described here as sarva-devatā.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Each year thereafter, in due course of time, Devakī, the mother of God and all the demigods, gave birth to a child. Thus she bore eight sons, one after another, and a daughter named Subhadrā.
Commentary

Devaki is described as sarva devata, the devata of all the devas, because she is worshipable as the mother of the Lord. The daughter mentioned is Subhadra. Every year for eight years a son was born. The reason is given inverse 58.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The word atha indicates auspiciousness. Viṣṇu Purāṇa says yuvaiva vasudevo ‘bhut vihāyābhyāgatāṁ jarām: Vasudeva was young, having given up old age. In Hari-vaṁśa Akrūra says to Kṛṣṇa, vṛddhau tavādya pitarau: your parents were elderly. This means that Kṛṣṇa was born to them when they were quite old. Thus after some time Devakī gave birth to her sons. She is called the devatā among all devatās because she possessed the power of the Lord. She is praised by the devatās in Viṣṇu Purāṇa as the supreme, subtle prakṛti. The meaning is this. Vasudeva was the cause of the Lord’s appearance because he is śuddha sattva. That is the meaning of the word vasudeva. Śiva says sattvaṁ viśuddhaṁ vasudeva-śabditaṁ: vasudeva means śuddha-sattva. (SB 4.3.23) Vasudevaṁ hareḥ sthānaṁ vadanty ānakadundubhim: Vasudeva, who provided the proper place for the appearance of the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa, was also known as Ānakadundubhi. (SB 9.24.30) Devakī has great qualities similar to her husband’s. The daughter was called Subhadrā. They were born one year after the previous child, in order that Kṛṣṇa would appear quickly.

Personal Guidance
Devakī is sarva-devatā because she bore Kṛṣṇa — worship the source, and every deva, every part, is automatically honored.
Practical Applications
  • Center today's worship on Kṛṣṇa alone, and refuse the urge to scatter your devotion across competing demigods or generic 'higher powers'.
  • When you serve one person well today — a parent, child, colleague — consciously offer that service to Kṛṣṇa, trusting that the whole is served through Him.
  • Open your sādhana with the prayer sarva-devamayo guruḥ, asking the spiritual master to reveal Kṛṣṇa as the source of all you have been chasing piecemeal.
Reflection Questions
  • When I worship or seek favor from various devas in my life — whether literal demigods or modern equivalents like wealth, health, status — am I bypassing the source (Kṛṣṇa) for the branches, and what does that bypass cost me in real devotional depth?
  • If sarva-devamayo guruḥ means honoring Kṛṣṇa automatically honors all, where in my practice am I still hedging — splitting my śraddhā across many objects instead of concentrating it on Him?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • You are pulled in twenty directions — grades, club leadership, the friend group, the feed, the side project, the parent-pleasing, the crush. Pick the one 'demigod' you have been worshiping hardest this week (the algorithm? the GPA? the approval of one specific person?) and consciously redirect that energy this evening into one round of Hare Kṛṣṇa japa or one honest reading of a Gītā verse. Notice whether the smaller worships actually suffered.
  • Devakī kept producing children year after year inside a prison. Your 'prison' might be a semester, a toxic group chat, or a job you cannot quit yet. Stop waiting for the conditions to be clean before you do your sādhana. Today, do one thing for Kṛṣṇa inside the bad conditions — even five minutes — without first fixing the conditions.
  • When a friend says 'why are you only into Kṛṣṇa now, what about [other deity / other path / no path]?' — practice the sarva-devatā answer without arrogance. Try the sentence out loud with a roommate: 'I'm not against anyone — I'm trying to water the root.' If it comes out as superiority, you are not yet ready to say it in public.
householder
  • Your household has many 'devas' to please — your spouse, your children, your parents, your boss, your in-laws, the school, the bank. List them honestly. Then ask: which decisions this week can I make by serving Kṛṣṇa first, trusting that the legitimate claims of the others are served at the root? Pick one decision and act on it before Sunday.
  • Devakī's eight sons were taken from her, one after another. As a householder you will be asked, again and again, to release things you produced — children growing up and leaving, money going out, projects you built being changed by others. Today, formally offer one such 'child' (a real child's choice you disagree with, a project at work, a financial loss) to Kṛṣṇa, and stop trying to recover it.
  • If your family worships a kula-devatā, a regional deity, or follows mixed practices, do not make this verse a hammer. Keep the family observances with vinaya, and quietly let your own concentration deepen on Kṛṣṇa. Real consolidation in the heart will look like more affection for the family, not less. If it is producing contempt, the consolidation has not happened — the ego has just changed costumes.
senior
  • At 60+ you have already 'birthed' many things — children, a career, a reputation, perhaps a community. Devakī is honored not for the eight sons she lost but for the one Son she bore who is the source of all. Ask yourself: of everything I have produced, what was actually for Kṛṣṇa? Spend an hour writing the honest answer; then re-direct what time remains.
  • Loosen one specific attachment this month that you have been quietly clutching — a role in the family you will not relinquish, a grievance against a sibling, a piece of property, a title at the temple. Hand it over to Kṛṣṇa in writing or in front of a witness. Test the sarva-devatā principle: when the root is held, the branches do not need your grip.
  • Mentor one younger person — a grandchild, a junior at the temple, a neighbor — not by lecturing them about demigod worship versus Kṛṣṇa worship, but by showing them what concentrated devotion in old age looks like. Let them see your japa, your reading, your steadiness as kāla closes in. That is the inheritance that matters; the rest will be distributed by lawyers.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'Devakī as sarva-devatā' as a poetic compliment to motherhood in general, instead of recognizing the specific theological claim that she bore the source of all devas — and thereby flattening Kṛṣṇa into a sentimental figure rather than the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
  • Using the line 'worship Kṛṣṇa and all are worshiped' as license for spiritual laziness — skipping prescribed duties, respect for guru, or care for jīvas — rather than as the principle that concentrates and clarifies all worship.
  • Drifting into impersonalism by treating 'the source of all devas' as an abstract Absolute or 'divine essence,' missing that the verse and purport insist on Kṛṣṇa as a specific Person — Acyuta — whom Devakī personally bore.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī is called sarva-devatā because she gave birth to Kṛṣṇa, the source of all devas. Share with your partner: where in your own worship do you still divide your loyalty among many forms or causes, and what would it cost you to consolidate that devotion at the root? Push each other past the polite answer.
  • Prabhupāda says that by worshiping Acyuta, one automatically worships everyone — there is no need to separately worship the demigods or even the jīva-tattva. Discuss honestly: does this strike you as liberating or as exclusionary? Where does your discomfort actually come from?
  • Devakī bore eight sons 'in due course of time' (kāla upāvṛtte) — a quiet line covering years of waiting under Kaṁsa's threat. Talk about a long stretch in your own life where you had to keep producing under hostile conditions. Did you experience the time as Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, or as your own endurance? Be willing to disagree on the answer.
Role Play

Two students. Student A has just started attending a Gauḍīya temple and tells Student B (a longtime family friend, raised in a household that worships a particular family deity and several demigods on fixed days) that they have stopped the family pūjās in favor of chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa exclusively, citing this verse and the principle that worshiping Acyuta means worshiping all. Student B is hurt and accuses Student A of arrogance and of insulting the family elders. Run the conversation for five minutes. Then swap roles. The teacher debriefs: where did Student A fall into impersonalist or sectarian language, where could the sarva-devatā principle have been presented with humility (vinaya) rather than as a trump card, and how would Prabhupāda have framed the same shift without breaking śraddhā in the family?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling Devakī sarva-devatā — 'mother of all the demigods' — sounds like mythological inflation. She was a human mother who had nine children over nine years under terrible circumstances. Why dress that up in cosmic titles?
    The title is not inflation; it is precision. Devakī is called sarva-devatā for one specific reason — she gave birth to Kṛṣṇa, who is Himself the source of all devas (aham ādir hi devānām, BG 10.2). The honor flows from the child, not from a sentimental upgrade of the mother. A Gauḍīya teacher would say: this is exactly how reality works for any of us. We become 'related to everything' only to the degree we are related to the source. Devakī's nine years of bearing children under Kaṁsa's sword are not erased by the title — they are revealed by it. She suffered as a mother; she is glorified as the mother of the source of the devas. Both are true, and the second does not flatten the first.
  • The claim that worshiping Kṛṣṇa alone is sufficient — that one need not worship the demigods or even honor the jīva separately — sounds like the same kind of 'one true God' exclusivism that has caused so much harm in other traditions. Why is this not just sectarianism?
    It would be sectarianism if Kṛṣṇa were one deity competing with others for territory. The Gauḍīya position is structurally different: Kṛṣṇa is the source from whom the viṣṇu-tattva (svāṁśa) and the jīva-tattva (vibhinnāṁśa) both proceed. Watering the root feeds every leaf — sarvārhaṇam acyutejyā (Bhāg. 4.31.14). This is not the rejection of others; it is the recognition of where they all stand. A Gauḍīya teacher would add: notice that the verse does not tell you to break anyone else's worship or insult anyone else's iṣṭa-devatā. It tells you where your own concentration belongs. The exclusivism you fear is interpersonal; what is being taught here is ontological. Practiced with vinaya (humility), it produces friendship (maitri) with all beings, not contempt for them — because you finally see the one Lord behind every form.
Analogy

Devakī, year after year, gives birth — eight sons and a daughter — like a great river that does not know it is being dammed downstream. The Bhāgavata names her sarva-devatā — she from whom all the demigods and God Himself will appear. The womb that Kaṁsa will plunder is the same womb the cosmos is queuing into. The verse's serenity is its sting: anuvatsaram, year after year, the catastrophe unfolds on schedule.
