Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.15

Śrīla Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O Your Majesty, best of all saintly kings, because you are greatly attracted to topics of Vāsudeva, it is certain that your intelligence is firmly fixed in spiritual understanding, which is the only true goal for humanity. Because that attraction is unceasing, it is certainly sublime.
Purport

Kṛṣṇa-kathā is compulsory for the rājarṣi, or executive head of government. This is also mentioned in Bhagavad-gītā ( imaṁ rājarṣayo viduḥ ). Unfortunately, however, in this age the governmental power is gradually being captured by third-class and fourth-class men who have no spiritual understanding, and society is therefore very quickly becoming degraded. Kṛṣṇa-kathā must be understood by the executive heads of government, for otherwise how will people be happy and gain relief from the pangs of materialistic life? One who has fixed his mind in Kṛṣṇa consciousness should be understood to have very sharp intelligence in regard to the value of life. Mahārāja Parīkṣit was rājarṣi-sattama, the best of all saintly kings, and Śukadeva Gosvāmī was muni-sattama, the best of munis. Both of them were elevated because of their common interest in kṛṣṇa-kathā. The exalted position of the speaker and the audience will be explained very nicely in the next verse. Kṛṣṇa-kathā is so enlivening that Mahārāja Parīkṣit forgot everything material, even his personal comfort in relation to food and drink. This is an example of how the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement should spread all over the world to bring both the speaker and the audience to the transcendental platform and back home, back to Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Śrīla Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O Your Majesty, best of all saintly kings, because you are greatly attracted to topics of Vāsudeva, it is certain that your intelligence is firmly fixed in spiritual understanding, which is the only true goal for humanity. Because that attraction is unceasing, it is certainly sublime.
Commentary

Because (yat) your intelligence is fixed (samyag vyavasita) you have developed fixed attachment to the Lord. "Previously you have addressed me as muni sattama, best among the munis and devotees, so this is my response, rajarsi satttama, o king among the best of rajarsis and devotees. You are called the king of the best of rsis and the devotees, because of your superior devotion to the Lord, through whose mercy you were able to withstand the power of brahma teja (astra) at the time of birth and death."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The last verse mentioned that Śukadeva began speaking. Now this is repeated to make it clearly understood by all listeners. Most were enthusiastic to hear, but a few were inattentive. This should be understood in other verses as well. He is addressed as Śrī-śuka because of his sweet voice caused by his profound prema. This should be understood in other texts as well. Rasa appears in the conversation by mutual affection between the speaker and listener. Śuka was overjoyed at the King’s questions. This is expressed in two verses. Your intelligence is completely fixed in topics of Vāsudeva, the Lord who appeared from Vasudeva to show all his powers. That intelligence has reached the highest level of affection (naiṣṭhikī ratiḥ). He thus addresses him as the best among all the saintly kings. Though sometimes he addresses the King to get his attention since Parīkṣit is overcome with prema on hearing the topics, sometimes he addresses him in a special manner according to the situation and topic as in this verse.

Personal Guidance
Steady, unceasing attraction to vāsudeva-kathā is the sign that intelligence is firmly fixed in the only true goal of human life — devotion to Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Begin today by hearing or reading even one verse of vāsudeva-kathā before touching your phone or work.
  • Notice when your attention drifts from kṛṣṇa-kathā to material chatter, and gently return — track this once at midday and once before sleep.
  • Speak a single point from today's hearing to one family member or friend, so that the rati you receive becomes rati you give.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my day am I starving my intelligence of vāsudeva-kathā and feeding it instead on news, gossip, or strategy — and what does that diet reveal about where my buddhi is actually 'firmly fixed'?
  • If naiṣṭhikī rati means an unceasing attraction, what is the honest test: when kṛṣṇa-kathā is removed for a day, do I feel the absence the way Parīkṣit forgot food and drink, or do I barely notice?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick one ongoing 'feed' you cannot stop scrolling — Instagram, a group chat, a sports thread — and for one week replace ten minutes of it with ten minutes of audio Bhāgavatam on the way to class. Notice whether your buddhi feels more samyag vyavasitā or more scattered by the end of the week.
  • Parīkṣit forgot food and drink in kathā; you forget meals during exam cramming or a Netflix binge. Same mechanism, different viṣaya. This week, before each study session, read the translation of one SB verse aloud — train the same absorption capacity toward Vāsudeva.
  • In your friend group, you are already a 'leader' of taste — what everyone watches, listens to, jokes about. Practice being a small-scale rājarṣi: introduce one piece of kṛṣṇa-kathā (a verse, a kīrtana, a Prabhupāda lecture clip) into the group's shared attention this month, without preaching, without apology.
householder
  • You are the 'executive head' of your home — a household-scale rājarṣi. This week, hold one family decision (a purchase, a school choice, a conflict) and before deciding, sit ten minutes with a Bhāgavatam verse. Watch whether the decision shifts when buddhi is fixed in Vāsudeva first.
  • Children imitate what parents are absorbed in, not what parents lecture about. If your unceasing rati is visibly toward markets, careers, or screens, that is the dharma you are transmitting. Choose one daily slot — morning chai, evening meal, bedtime — where vāsudeva-kathā is audibly part of household life, not a private retreat.
  • Audit your household budget and calendar the way a rājarṣi audits a kingdom: how much time, money, and attention is allocated to kṛṣṇa-kathā versus to viṣaya? Make one concrete reallocation this month — a sādhu-saṅga subscription, a weekly satsaṅga, a tīrtha trip — that materially shifts the ratio.
senior
  • At this stage, food, sleep, and bodily comfort can quietly become the center of the day. Parīkṣit forgot all of it in kathā. Choose one fixed hour daily — ideally early morning — that belongs to vāsudeva-kathā before the body's demands set the agenda.
  • The legacy a senior leaves is not property but the kathā their grandchildren overheard. Begin recording — voice notes, written, or in person — your own reflections on specific Bhāgavatam verses for the next generation. Make naiṣṭhikī rati visible to them.
  • Loosen one attachment this season by replacing it, not erasing it. Pick a relationship, possession, or role you have been gripping, and each time the grip tightens, turn deliberately to one verse of kṛṣṇa-kathā. Let unceasing rati for Vāsudeva be what gradually does the work of vairāgya, rather than willpower.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating kṛṣṇa-kathā as an intellectual hobby — collecting verses and purports without the rati, the actual taste, that Śukadeva is praising here.
  • Assuming this verse is only for renunciants and not for the rājarṣi-in-training — the householder, manager, or executive who thinks spiritual fixity is incompatible with worldly responsibility.
  • Mistaking emotional excitement during a class or kīrtana for naiṣṭhikī rati — confusing a passing wave of sattva with the steady, unceasing attraction that survives boredom, distraction, and difficulty.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says kṛṣṇa-kathā is compulsory for the rājarṣi, and that society degrades when third- and fourth-class men capture power. Pair up: is this an indictment of secular leadership as such, or a call for spiritually trained leaders within any system? Where do you actually disagree?
  • Parīkṣit forgot food and drink while hearing kṛṣṇa-kathā. Share with your partner one 'kathā' (podcast, news feed, gossip thread) you currently can't stop consuming — and honestly assess whether vāsudeva-kathā has any chance of competing for that same hunger in your life right now.
  • Śukadeva calls Parīkṣit's intelligence samyag vyavasitā — firmly fixed — because his rati for Vāsudeva is naiṣṭhikī, unceasing. In small groups, debate: can a person have 'fixed intelligence' in Kṛṣṇa while their daily attention is scattered across a hundred material topics? What would have to change first — the attention or the intelligence?
Role Play

Two students role-play. Student A is a newly elected member of a city council who has just begun hearing Bhāgavatam daily and feels their decisions shifting; their party leadership has told them to drop the 'religious stuff' before the next public appearance because it will cost votes. Student B is the senior party advisor making the case. Student A must defend, in plain language a non-devotee can hear, why kṛṣṇa-kathā is not a private hobby but the very thing qualifying them as a rājarṣi-style leader — without quoting scripture at the advisor. Run it for five minutes, then swap.

Objections & Responses
  • This verse just sounds like a guru flattering his royal patron — 'best of saintly kings, your intelligence is firmly fixed.' Isn't this religious sycophancy, not real teaching?
    Śukadeva is not a court priest dependent on Parīkṣit; he is a paramahaṁsa who left home naked at birth and owes nothing to any king. He is naming a diagnostic fact: when rati for vāsudeva-kathā becomes naiṣṭhikī — unceasing — that itself is the evidence that buddhi has become samyag vyavasitā. He is reading the symptom, not paying a compliment. A Gauḍīya teacher does the same with us: not 'you are wonderful,' but 'this taste you are showing for hari-kathā is itself the proof that something real is happening — do not waste it.'
  • Saying governmental power has fallen to 'third-class and fourth-class men' is elitist and casteist. Modern democracy is precisely the rejection of that hierarchy.
    Varṇa in Prabhupāda's usage is not birth-caste; it is guṇa and karma — qualification by quality and work, as Kṛṣṇa Himself defines it. The point is not that some humans are inherently lower, but that running a society requires people trained in sattva, in self-restraint, in answerability to something higher than the next election. When we hand executive power to people whose entire formation is in viṣaya — appetite, image, ambition — we should not then be surprised that policy follows appetite. Prabhupāda is not nostalgic for hereditary kings; he is saying: a leader who has never sat with kṛṣṇa-kathā has no inner ballast, and the citizens pay the price. That critique cuts across every political system, including ours.
