Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.11

Important Words
  • deham mānuṣam — body exactly like a man
    Kṛṣṇa's mānuṣa form is not a material body — it is His sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha appearing in human dimensions by yoga-māyā. The Gauḍīya line is strict: Kṛṣṇa does not 'have' a body; He is the body. Avajānanti māṁ mūḍhāḥ (BG 9.11) — fools mistake His mānuṣa form for material flesh.
  • āśritya — accepting
    Gerund of ā-śri, 'to take refuge in, to assume.' The same root that gives śaraṇa. Kṛṣṇa āśritya the human form — He takes shelter in it as a form to be served, which is itself a doorway: He hides His full opulence so that intimate rasa becomes possible.
  • vṛṣṇibhiḥ — in the company of the Vṛṣṇis
    Instrumental plural. The Vṛṣṇi clan is the political body of the Yadu dynasty in Dvārakā. Parīkṣit is asking: how long did Kṛṣṇa stay in the aiśvarya-līlā? The Vraja-Vṛṣṇi pairing in this very Canto is the spinal column of Bhāgavata-10.
  • patnyaḥ — wives
    Plural. Kṛṣṇa's 16,108 queens in Dvārakā are not concession to lust — they are 16,108 expansions of His svarūpa-śakti, each a manifestation of His acceptance of mādhurya in vaikuṇṭha-mood. The number is precise; the relationships are tattva, not biography.
Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, has no material body, yet He appears as a human being. For how many years did He live with the descendants of Vṛṣṇi? How many wives did He marry, and for how many years did He live in Dvārakā?
Purport

In many places the Supreme Personality of Godhead is described as sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha, possessing a spiritual, blissful body. His bodily feature is narākṛti, that is, exactly like that of a human being. Here the same idea is repeated in the words mānuṣam āśritya, which indicate that He accepts a body exactly like that of a man. Everywhere it is confirmed that Kṛṣṇa is never nirākāra, or formless. He has His form, exactly like that of a human being. There is no doubt about this.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, has no material body, yet He appears as a human being. For how many years did He live with the descendants of Vṛṣṇi? How many wives did He marry, and for how many years did He live in Dvārakā?
Commentary

How many years did he live with the Yadus? By using the phrase "deham manusam asrita" he indicates that the years should be in human calculation, not that of the devas. In other places the Lord is referred to as " paramatma narakrti", "narakrti parabrahma" and "budha parabrahma manusya cihnadhari". These statements show that the human-like form of Krsna is his svarupa or spiritual form. Thus "deham manusam " cannot mean that Krsna has a material body.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Dehaṁ mānuṣam āśritya: he took shelter of this material world (deham) filled with humans (mānuṣam). The world is like his body, since the universal form is his expansion. For the ignorant he was the universal form (virāḍ aviduṣām). Since this form is merely a portion of his real body, he cannot have a material human body. Thus, the yogīs saw his body as the supreme truth (tattvaṁ paraṁ yoginām). (SB 10.43.17) Another meaning of mānuṣam is “coming in Manu’s line” just as a Yādava comes in Yadu’s line. The word āśritya means “taking shelter of the eye with a particular form” or in other words “manifesting.” The Lord is his own shelter and never takes shelter of a material body. Thus we find verse such as sa ātmā svāśrayāśrayaḥ: the shelter of the ātmā is the Paramātmā, who takes shelter of himself. (SB 2.10.9) Yoga-māyām upāśritaḥ: he took shelter of his own yoga-māyā potency. (SB 10.29.1) thus meaning of dehaṁ mānuṣam āsritya is “revealing his most beautiful body with a human form” since his body is eternal, being completely sac-cid-ānanda. He lived with the Vṛṣṇis. They also must have similar bodies. For how many years did he manifest this spiritual form in Dvārakā? Some versions have the word madhupuryām instead of yadupuryām. How many were the wives that he had? The meaning is “For how many women was there so much good fortune?”

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha — never formless; His human-like form in Dvārakā is the supreme Person to whom we surrender, not an abstract Absolute.
Practical Applications
  • Refuse, in your japa and prayer today, to drift into 'formless light' or 'inner Self' — address Kṛṣṇa as the personal Lord with a spiritual form like your own.
  • When you next see a Deity or picture of Kṛṣṇa, pause and affirm aloud that this narākṛti form is sac-cid-ānanda, not an idol or symbol — let that conviction settle into your bowing.
  • Notice one moment today when you reduce Kṛṣṇa to a 'concept' or 'energy' in conversation, and correct yourself by naming Him as the Personality of Godhead who lived, married, and ruled in Dvārakā.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I quietly treating Kṛṣṇa as merely a great human, an inspiring teacher, or a formless 'consciousness' — and what specifically would change in my sādhana today if I held Him as sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha, narākṛti, personally present?
  • The verse asks how many years He lived in Dvārakā and how many wives He accepted — Kṛṣṇa fully entered household life without being touched by it; where in my own gṛhastha or pre-gṛhastha life am I using 'spirituality' to avoid responsibility, and where am I using responsibility to avoid Him?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a friend posts a glossy photo of their 'best life,' notice the pull. Now, for one week, replace one scroll-session a day with reading even ten lines of Kṛṣṇa's Dvārakā pastimes — let the mind feed on the Lord's narākṛti form instead of curated faces.
  • In your friend group there is probably one person who jokes that 'religion is just mythology.' Without arguing, be ready to say one specific, unshakeable thing: 'Kṛṣṇa is not formless, and He is not a tribal hero — He has a spiritual form exactly like a person, and He lived in Dvārakā for real years.' Practice saying it without flinching, without apologizing.
  • Pick one possession you cling to (phone, sneakers, a post's like-count). For seven days, before touching it, mentally offer it to Kṛṣṇa — the same Kṛṣṇa who lived among the Vṛṣṇis with real wealth and yet was perfectly aparigraha. Watch what loosens.
householder
  • Your house, spouse, kids, salary — Kṛṣṇa Himself accepted a household life in Dvārakā, with patnīs and a city. So your gṛhastha-āśrama is not a spiritual demotion. This week, conduct one family decision (a purchase, a school choice, a money question) by explicitly asking: 'How would I decide this if our home were Dvārakā and the Lord were the unseen head of the household?'
  • Practice real dāna, not material compassion: instead of only solving your children's bodily comfort, today tell them one concrete pastime of Kṛṣṇa — even thirty seconds at the dinner table. Feed the soul, not just the body. Do this daily for a month and watch what shifts in the home's atmosphere.
  • Make a written list of what you 'own.' Beside each item write either 'tool for Kṛṣṇa's service' or 'private kingdom.' Do not throw anything away — re-classify. Aparigraha for a householder is not renunciation of objects but renunciation of the false claim of ownership, which is exactly what Kṛṣṇa modeled in Dvārakā.
senior
  • You have already 'transitioned through' more bodies than the youth in your life can imagine — child, spouse, parent, professional, retiree. Now sit with this verse: Kṛṣṇa Himself accepted a mānuṣa body for a finite span of years. Spend ten minutes a day this week meditating not on your remaining years, but on His Dvārakā years — let your timeline dissolve into His.
  • Identify one possession, one role, or one resentment you are still quietly clutching as you near the end. Today, hand it over — to a child, a disciple, a younger devotee, or directly to Kṛṣṇa in your morning offering. This is the senior's specific tapas: pre-emptive aparigraha, before death does it for you.
  • Find one younger person — grandchild, neighbor, junior colleague — and tell them, in your own words, that Kṛṣṇa is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha, has a form like a human being, and is not the same as a vague 'universal energy.' Do not preach; just plant the personalist seed. This is the mentoring dāna only an elder can give.
Common Pitfalls
  • Sliding into impersonalism — reading mānuṣam āśritya as if Kṛṣṇa is 'really' formless and the human form is a concession or illusion, rather than recognizing that His form is eternal, spiritual, and exactly as described.
  • Reducing Kṛṣṇa's Dvārakā life — the wives, the kingdom, the years among the Vṛṣṇis — to allegory or symbol, and thereby losing the personalist, historically present Lord whom Parīkṣit Mahārāja is actually asking about.
  • Inverting the lesson into self-flattery — 'if Kṛṣṇa looks human, then my human form is divine too' — collapsing the distinction between the Supreme Personality and the conditioned jīva, and quietly excusing viṣaya-attachment as 'līlā.'
Discussion Prompts
  • Kṛṣṇa accepts a body 'exactly like that of a man' (mānuṣam āśritya) and yet remains the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Share with your partner: where in your own thinking do you slide toward treating Kṛṣṇa as either (a) merely a great historical hero or (b) a formless light wearing a costume? Be specific — what triggers the drift?
  • Parīkṣit asks a very 'material' question: how many years, how many wives, how long in Dvārakā. Some students will say this is small-minded; others will say it is the right way to honor Kṛṣṇa's narākṛti form. Take opposite sides for five minutes and argue it out — then notice which side felt more natural to you, and what that reveals.
  • The verse pairs Kṛṣṇa's mānuṣa-līlā with the Vṛṣṇis, His patnīs, and Dvārakā — a domestic, householder setting, not a renounced one. Discuss with your group: what does it mean that the Lord chose a gṛhastha-like external life with many relationships and possessions, while the verse list still names Aparigraha and Vairāgya as related values? How do you reconcile this for your own life?
Role Play

A skeptical college classmate sees a picture of Kṛṣṇa with His queens in Dvārakā on your phone and laughs: 'So your God had 16,108 wives and a gold city? That's not a God, that's a Bronze Age king with good PR.' One student plays the classmate, pressing hard for three minutes — mocking the wives, the wealth, the 'human' body. The other student responds as a Gauḍīya practitioner, holding two truths at once: Kṛṣṇa is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha with a narākṛti form (not nirākāra, not a tribal chief), AND His Dvārakā pastimes are not material indulgence but the Lord's own dāna, daya, and prema enacted for our benefit. After three minutes, swap roles. Debrief: where did you lose your footing? Where did you accidentally concede that Kṛṣṇa is 'just' a man, or accidentally deny that He really lived as a man?

Objections & Responses
  • If Kṛṣṇa has 'no material body' but appears 'exactly like a human being,' isn't this just theological hand-waving? You can't have it both ways — either His body is matter or it isn't, and if it isn't, the question 'how many years did He live' is meaningless.
    The question is meaningful precisely because Kṛṣṇa's form is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha — eternal, fully cognizant, fully blissful — and yet truly enters time and space for His devotees. The 'either/or' is a materialist's trap: matter and spirit are the only two categories the conditioned mind knows, so it forces Kṛṣṇa into one or the other. Prabhupāda is explicit here — Kṛṣṇa is never nirākāra, He has a form, and that form is narākṛti. The years in Dvārakā are real years; the wives are real wives; and none of it is composed of the five gross elements. This is not contradiction; it is the specific claim of the personalist line, and surrendering to it is the beginning of bhakti. Refuse it and you collapse either into Bronze-Age literalism or into impersonalist fog.
  • Even granting Kṛṣṇa is divine, why dwell on the wives, the city, the years? Isn't this exactly the gossipy, possessive consciousness (the opposite of Aparigraha) that bhakti is supposed to cure? Parīkṣit sounds like he is reading a celebrity tabloid.
    Parīkṣit is on the verge of death and has seven days to live — he is not gossiping. He is doing the most aparigraha act possible: stripping away every other interest and asking only about Kṛṣṇa, including the most concrete, embodied details of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. Hearing of Kṛṣṇa's queens, His city, His years among the Vṛṣṇis is not an inventory of possessions; it is kṛṣṇa-kathā, which itself is the highest dāna and the doorway to prema. The cure for possessive consciousness is not to refuse all detail and retreat into a featureless Absolute — that is impersonalism — but to let your appetite for detail be re-tuned toward Kṛṣṇa's līlā. A devotee who can recount Kṛṣṇa's Dvārakā-līlā for an hour and forget what is in his own bank account has practiced more vairāgya than a meditator straining to think of 'nothing.'
Analogy

Asking how many wives Kṛṣṇa married and how many years He lived is like asking how many strings are on the vīṇā Sarasvatī plays — every detail of the instrument is the instrument's glory. Parīkṣit is not gathering trivia; he is collecting the very specifics through which the Person makes Himself recognizable to the next generation of hearers.
