Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.9

Important Words
  • mukundaḥ — Kṛṣṇa, who can award liberation
    Mukti + da, 'giver of liberation.' But for the Gauḍīya line, Kṛṣṇa is mukunda primarily because He gives mukti as a side-effect while offering the higher gift — prema. Mukti is a free sample for those who came for the medicine; prema is for those who came for Him.
  • vrajam — to Vrajadhāma
    Accusative. Vraja = the cow-pasturing land, but in Gauḍīya theology Vraja-dhāma is the topmost manifestation of Kṛṣṇa's own abode — the seat of mādhurya-rasa, where the Six Gosvāmīs later recovered the lost līlā-sthalas. To be 'transferred to Vraja' is the line of every aspiring rāgānuga sādhaka.
  • jñātibhiḥ — His relatives
    Instrumental plural of jñāti. In Vraja Kṛṣṇa lives in jñāti-sambandha — with Nanda as father, Yaśodā as mother, Balarāma as brother, the gopas as cousins. The verse foregrounds that Bhagavān's līlā in Vraja is conducted in family relations, not in court or temple.
  • sātvatām patiḥ — the master of all Vaiṣṇava devotees
    Sātvata = the Vaiṣṇava community, devotees in sattva. Patiḥ = lord, husband, master. Kṛṣṇa is not merely a deity to be worshiped; He is patiḥ of the sātvatas — the One who maintains and is maintained by His own devotees in mutual relation.
Why did Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, leave the house of His father, Vasudeva, and transfer Himself to the house of Nanda in Vṛndāvana? Where did the Lord, the master of the Yadu dynasty, live with His relatives in Vṛndāvana?
Purport

These are inquiries about the itinerary of Kṛṣṇa. Just after His birth in the house of Vasudeva in Mathurā, Kṛṣṇa transferred Himself to Gokula, on the other side of the Yamunā, and after some days He moved with His father, mother and other relatives to Nanda-grāma, Vṛndāvana. Mahārāja Parīkṣit was very much eager to hear about Kṛṣṇa’s activities in Vṛndāvana. This entire canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is full of activities performed in Vṛndāvana and Dvārakā. The first forty chapters describe Kṛṣṇa’s Vṛndāvana affairs, and the next fifty describe Kṛṣṇa’s activities in Dvārakā. Mahārāja Parīkṣit, to fulfill his desire to hear about Kṛṣṇa, requested Śukadeva Gosvāmī to describe these activities in full detail.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Why did Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, leave the house of His father, Vasudeva, and transfer Himself to the house of Nanda in Vṛndāvana? Where did the Lord, the master of the Yadu dynasty, live with His relatives in Vṛndāvana?
Commentary

He went from Vasudeva’s house to Vraja. Here Vraja means Mahavana. After going to Vraja he left the house of Nanda. Where did he go and establish his house along with the cowherd men?

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Mukunda means giver of liberation. Kṛṣṇa should have stayed in Mathurā to give liberation to Kaṁsa instead of going to Vraja. This question will be answered in the eighth and ninth chapters. Or since Mukunda means the happiness of liberation, as derived from mukti with dā (to give) with elision of syllables as in words like pṛṣodara from pṛṣat and udara and since that happiness is useless, the real meaning of Mukunda must be “the giver of the bliss of prema.” Therefore it is suitable that he went to Vraja to give prema, but he still must have produced a pretext for going. Later it will be understood that the Lord made Kaṁsa fear his father and mother by his going to Vraja. Parīkṣit uses the word “father” to indicate Vasudeva because of the apparent birth from Vasudeva, and out of respect because of his relationship with the Yadus. Śukadeva however later identifies Kṛṣṇa’s actual father with the words nandaḥ sva-putram ādāya pretyāgatam udāra-dhīḥ: generous Nanda immediately took his son Kṛṣṇa on his lap as if Kṛṣṇa had returned from death. (SB 10.6.43) Kva means “what particular place.” Jṣātibhiḥ means the cowherds and the Yādavas. Giving up Mahāvana where did he live with the cowherds? Giving up Mathurā, where did he live with the Yādavas? Thus the word has a different meaning in reference to Vṛndāvana and Dvārakā. However Kṛṣṇa considered the cowherds to be his main relatives. yāta yūyaṁ vrajaṁn tāta vayaṁ ca sneha-duḥkhitān jṣātīn vo draṣṭum eṣyāmo vidhāya suhṛdāṁ sukham Now you should all return to Vraja, dear father. We shall come to see you, our dear relatives who suffer in separation from us, as soon as we have given some happiness to your well-wishing friends. SB 10.45.23 It is understood that Śukadeva would explain the significance of leaving Mahāvana and living in Vṛndāvana. Kṛṣṇa is described as the protector of the devotees (sātvatāṁ patiḥ). He lived with his devotees to protect them with suitable pastimes. The answers to this question will be found later in the words of Upananda and the Lord.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa's move from Vasudeva's prison-house to Nanda's Vraja shows that the Lord seeks the soil of pure vātsalya-bhakti — go where Kṛṣṇa actually plays, not where He is merely born.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today which 'Mathurā' (a place of obligation and formality) you remain in out of habit, and identify one 'Vraja' (a setting of warm, personal devotion) you can step toward this week.
  • Sit for ten minutes and hear or read about Kṛṣṇa's Vṛndāvana līlā with the same eagerness as Mahārāja Parīkṣit — ask Śuka-like sources, not your own speculation, for the next detail.
  • Refuse to treat Kṛṣṇa as a distant majestic figure today; address Him by a vātsalya name (Mukunda, Gopāla) in your japa and seva, inviting the intimacy that Vraja embodies.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my own life have I been clinging to the 'house of Vasudeva' — a setting of formal reverence and security — when Kṛṣṇa is actually inviting me into the more intimate, demanding sevā of Vraja?
  • When I hear of Kṛṣṇa moving from Mathurā to Gokula to Nanda-grāma, what does it expose about my own resistance to letting the Lord rearrange my itinerary, relationships, and residence for His pleasure rather than mine?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the 'Mathurā' you were born into — your school's prestige ladder, your friend group's Instagram aesthetic, the career your family assumes you will pursue. Name it specifically this week, in writing.
  • Kṛṣṇa moved to where He could be loved freely, not where He was expected. Identify one 'Vraja' relationship in your life — a sādhu-saṅga, a devotee friend, a temple, a family elder who actually hears you — and visit it on purpose this week, not by accident.
  • Before you post, text, or scroll today, ask: am I performing for Kaṁsa's Mathurā (the algorithm, the peer audience), or am I living for Vraja (the small circle where Kṛṣṇa is actually present)? Cut one performance today.
householder
  • Parīkṣit asked, 'where did the Lord live with His relatives?' Audit your home this week: is it a place where Kṛṣṇa could plausibly 'live with His relatives,' or only a place where the family's material ambitions live? Make one concrete change — a tulasī, a morning kīrtana, a meal offered before eaten.
  • Distinguish, in one current family decision (school, job, move, money), between material compassion (keeping everyone comfortable) and real compassion (serving the soul). Write both columns. Decide from the second.
  • Kṛṣṇa's move to Vraja was protective — Vasudeva carried Him to safety. As a parent or spouse, what are you carrying your dependents away from this season — what Kaṁsa-influence (a toxic environment, a screen habit, a social pressure) needs the Yamunā crossed today?
senior
  • At Parīkṣit's stage of life — final chapter, eager to hear Kṛṣṇa's itinerary — what 'Vṛndāvana' do you still need to hear about? Choose one section of the Tenth Canto and commit to hearing it daily, in order, before you go.
  • Kṛṣṇa loosened His grip on Mathurā's royal identity to live as a cowherd boy. Name one prestige-attachment (title, expertise, your role as the family's decision-maker) you can quietly hand over this year, and to whom.
  • Mentor one younger person not by giving them advice, but by telling them about Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Vraja — the way Śukadeva told Parīkṣit. Legacy in this line is not what you built; it is what kṛṣṇa-kathā you transmitted.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's transfer to Vṛndāvana as mere biography to be memorized, rather than as the Lord's deliberate choice of an intimate setting — and so missing the personalist invitation entirely.
  • Romanticizing Vraja as a mood or aesthetic ('simple village life,' 'be more present in nature') and quietly substituting that sentiment for actual sevā to Kṛṣṇa and His devotees in one's current āśrama.
  • Imitating Parīkṣit Mahārāja's eagerness to hear about Vṛndāvana while skipping his prerequisite — the disciplined inquiry of a sātvata under a bona fide guru — and thereby drifting into sahajiyā-style familiarity instead of guided śravaṇa.
Discussion Prompts
  • Parīkṣit Mahārāja asks where Kṛṣṇa lived 'with His relatives' — yet the Lord left His biological father Vasudeva for Nanda Mahārāja. Where in your own life have you confused biological obligation with sva-dharma, and what would it cost you to reorder them the way Kṛṣṇa did?
  • Kṛṣṇa moves from the prison-palace of Mathurā to the cowherd village of Vraja. Discuss with your partner: which 'Mathurā' (prestigious, secure, but constricted) are you still clinging to, and which 'Vraja' (humbler, freer, more intimate with Kṛṣṇa) are you avoiding? Push back if your partner's Vraja sounds like an escape rather than a surrender.
  • Parīkṣit's question is not abstract theology — he is dying in seven days and wants to hear about Kṛṣṇa's itinerary. Is curiosity about Kṛṣṇa's pastimes (līlā) actually bhakti, or can it become a refined form of viṣaya? Disagree concretely: when does hearing kṛṣṇa-kathā nourish the soul, and when does it become spiritual entertainment?
Role Play

Two students sit across from each other. Student A is a 28-year-old who has just been offered a high-paying job in their hometown, near aging parents who expect them to stay and 'fulfill their duty.' Student B is the same person's older sibling, who took a similar path twenty years ago and is now quietly bitter. A devotee-friend (a third student, observing) must counsel A — drawing from SB 10.1.9 — on the difference between Vasudeva's house (the place of biological birth and worldly expectation) and Nanda's Vraja (the place where one is actually nourished in bhakti). The friend must NOT tell A what to decide, but must help A distinguish material compassion (keeping parents comfortable) from real compassion (serving the soul — A's own and the parents'). Run it for ten minutes, then swap roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This whole question is mythological geography — Mathurā, Gokula, Vṛndāvana. Why should a modern person care which village a deity supposedly lived in?
    Parīkṣit Mahārāja did not ask out of tourist interest; he asked because he had seven days to live and wanted to hear Kṛṣṇa's actual movements, in actual places, with actual people. Bhakti in the Gauḍīya line is personalist — Kṛṣṇa is not a symbol or a 'divine spark within,' He is the Supreme Person who took birth, crossed the Yamunā, and lived in Nanda's house. The geography matters precisely because it refuses to dissolve Him into an abstract Absolute. If you let Mathurā and Vṛndāvana become 'just myth,' you are quietly importing impersonalism. Hear them as real places, and Kṛṣṇa stays a real Person you can surrender to today.
  • Kṛṣṇa abandoning Vasudeva's house to live with Nanda sounds like a child being taken from his parents — it seems ethically uncomfortable, even irresponsible, to hold this up as a model.
    Two clarifications. First, this was not abandonment — Vasudeva himself carried the infant Kṛṣṇa across the Yamunā to protect Him from Kaṁsa; the move was Vasudeva's act of love, not Kṛṣṇa's rejection. Second, the verse is not asking you to walk out on your family. It is asking you to notice that Kṛṣṇa's deepest relationships unfold in Vraja, among those who love Him without knowing He is God — pure vātsalya, sakhya, mādhurya. The instruction for the householder is not to flee Vasudeva's house, but to understand that real compassion (serving the soul) sometimes reorders our material expectations of where love is supposed to happen. Sva-dharma is not the same as biological convenience.
Analogy

Kṛṣṇa leaving Vasudeva's house for Nanda's home in Vraja is like a king who, owning a palace, chooses to be raised in a cowherd's hut not from poverty but from love. Where He is born establishes His mission; where He is raised reveals His heart. The question 'why Vraja?' is the question of mādhurya itself.
