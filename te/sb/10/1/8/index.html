Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.8

Important Words
  • rāmaḥ — Balarāma
    From ram, 'to delight.' Balarāma is rāma in the original sense — He in whom the devotees take pleasure. Distinguish from Daśaratha-Rāma; here Bala-rāma is svayaṁ-prakāśa of Saṅkarṣaṇa, the original servitor-elder-brother of Kṛṣṇa.
  • saṅkarṣaṇaḥ — the first Deity in the quadruple group
    From sam + karṣ, 'to draw together.' Saṅkarṣaṇa is so named because at the end of each kalpa He withdraws (draws back together) the manifested creation. The same Person who is the cosmic withdrawer plays as a child's elder brother in Vraja — acintya-bhedābheda in person.
  • garbha-sambandhaḥ — connected with the womb
    Sambandha = connection. The very word names the first of the three Gauḍīya divisions (sambandha-abhidheya-prayojana). Here it is used at the physical level, but the question (how is connection possible without ordinary mechanics?) is exactly the deep question of Bhagavān's relation to embodied jīvas.
  • deha-antaram — transferring bodies
    Deha + antara, 'another body.' The ordinary law for the jīva — karma-driven transmigration (BG 2.13, tathā dehāntara-prāptiḥ). Parīkṣit's question highlights that Balarāma is not under this law: His 'transfer' is not the jīva's saṁsāra.
My dear Śukadeva Gosvāmī, you have already explained that Saṅkarṣaṇa, who belongs to the second quadruple, appeared as the son of Rohiṇī named Balarāma. If Balarāma was not transferred from one body to another, how is it possible that He was first in the womb of Devakī and then in the womb of Rohiṇī? Kindly explain this to me.
Purport

Here is a question particularly directed at understanding Balarāma, who is Saṅkarṣaṇa Himself. Balarāma is well known as the son of Rohiṇī, yet it is also known that He was the son of Devakī. Parīkṣit Mahārāja wanted to understand the mystery of Balarāma’s being the son of both Devakī and Rohiṇī.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
My dear Śukadeva Gosvāmī, you have already explained that Saṅkarṣaṇa, who belongs to the second quadruple, appeared as the son of Rohiṇī named Balarāma. If Balarāma was not transferred from one body to another, how is it possible that He was first in the womb of Devakī and then in the womb of Rohiṇī? Kindly explain this to me.
Commentary

In the next four verses there is inquiry about the particulars of Krsna’s birth. The general description was given in the 9th canto (24. 54) There he is called sankarsana and ahisvara, the seventh child of Devaki and Vasudeva. "Since elsewhere you have refered to Sankarsana as the son of Rohini, how could he come from Devaki’s womb without having to change bodies?" This is the indication.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

In four verses Parīkṣit asks specific questions. You mentioned in SB 9.24.54 that Balarāma or Saṅkarṣaṇa was one among the sons of Devakī. But you also said that he was the son of Rohiṇī (SB 9.24.46). These statements referred to the same person.

Personal Guidance
Balarāma is Saṅkarṣaṇa Himself — His womb-to-womb passage is no ordinary transmigration but Kṛṣṇa's personal yoga-māyā arranging the līlā.
Practical Applications
  • Refuse the impersonalist reflex that flattens Kṛṣṇa's and Balarāma's appearances into 'just another birth' — chant their names today knowing They descend, not transmigrate.
  • When a scriptural detail confuses you, follow Parīkṣit's example: ask your guide directly with vinaya rather than fabricating your own explanation.
  • Offer one act of seva to Balarāma today (a praṇāma, a remembrance before study) acknowledging Him as the source of spiritual strength, not a demigod.
Reflection Questions
  • Where do I quietly assume the body is the self — treating my own changes of body, role, or station as if they were transfers of *me* — and how would I act differently today if I held Balarāma's truth that the soul does not migrate even when the vessel does?
  • In what part of my sādhana am I trying to 'figure out' Kṛṣṇa's and His associates' movements with mundane logic, like Parīkṣit's apparent puzzle, instead of standing in vinaya and asking a guru — and what specific question have I been too proud or too lazy to put before a senior Vaiṣṇava?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When friends or social media flatten Kṛṣṇa into 'one of the Hindu gods' or 'a symbol of love,' practice the small courage of correction — say once today, plainly: 'In my understanding, He is the Supreme Person, not a symbol.' Notice the discomfort; do not smooth it.
  • Before your next exam or interview, consciously distinguish the body that is performing (which will pass through youth, middle age, old age — BG 4.20 territory of acting without attachment) from the soul that is eternal. Write the distinction on the corner of your notes. Act from the second.
  • Pick one peer-pressure situation this week — a party, a group chat, a trend — where you've been quietly going along. Ask: am I serving my friends' souls or just their bodily comfort? Then choose the harder, real-compassion move, even if it costs you cool points.
householder
  • In your next family decision (school for the kids, a job offer, a property purchase), explicitly name which axis you're deciding on — material welfare or spiritual welfare of the souls involved. Both can matter, but refuse to let the first silently swallow the second.
  • Teach your children — even once this week, at the dinner table — that Kṛṣṇa is not one deity among many and not a metaphor. Use simple language, not jargon. The verse's distinction (Balarāma is not a transmigrating jīva) is exactly the distinction you are quietly transmitting or failing to transmit every day.
  • When stress about money or career rises, deliberately invoke vairāgya by remembering: this body and its career are passing through stages; the relationship with Kṛṣṇa is not. Offer the day's earnings, mentally, before you sleep — sevā reframes the same work without changing the work.
senior
  • Use the proximity of your own dehāntara — the body's coming change — to deepen, not avoid, hard questions about Kṛṣṇa's nature. Like Parīkṣit, ask your teacher or sādhu-saṅga the question you have been embarrassed to ask. Seven days, seven months, seven years — the posture is the same.
  • Mentor one younger devotee or family member specifically on the personalist line: that Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Person, that Balarāma is Saṅkarṣaṇa Himself, that surrender is to Him, not to an impersonal 'light.' Do not assume they have absorbed this; most have not.
  • Begin actively loosening one attachment this month — a possession, a role, an opinion you have defended for forty years. Offer it consciously as sevā. The verse trains you to distinguish what merely changes bodies from what is eternal; your remaining years are for living on the right side of that line.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using the 'soul does not change bodies' principle as a shrug — drifting into vairāgya-flavored neglect of one's actual sva-dharma duties (to family, varṇa, āśrama) under the cover of 'I am not this body.'
  • Flattening Balarāma into an impersonal 'energy' or 'principle' to resolve the Devakī-Rohiṇī mystery, instead of honoring Him as Saṅkarṣaṇa, a distinct personal expansion of Kṛṣṇa — and thereby slipping into the impersonalist habit of dissolving persons into concepts.
  • Imitating Parīkṣit Mahārāja's bold inquiry without his qualification — firing speculative 'how can this be?' questions at śāstra in a debating mood, rather than approaching with the humility of a disciple who has already surrendered and is seeking deeper darśana, not ammunition.
Discussion Prompts
  • Parīkṣit asks how Balarāma could move from Devakī's womb to Rohiṇī's without 'transferring bodies' — share with your partner: when have you confused a transcendental act of Kṛṣṇa with an ordinary material event, and what tipped you off that you were thinking materialistically? Push each other past the safe answers.
  • The question is asked by a king who is seven days from death, to a sage who knows everything. Discuss: why does Parīkṣit still ask 'kindly explain this to me' rather than accept it on faith? Where in your own sādhana have you mistaken silent acceptance for vinaya, when in fact honest questioning would have served Kṛṣṇa more?
  • Balarāma is Saṅkarṣaṇa Himself, not a jīva taking another body (dehāntara). Discuss with your group: which of Kṛṣṇa's or Balarāma's activities have you been quietly explaining to skeptical friends in jīva-terms — 'just a symbol,' 'just a teaching story' — and what would it cost you socially to stop? Disagree honestly about whether that cost is worth paying.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a devotee who has just told a college classmate (Student B, a thoughtful skeptic raised on comparative religion) that Balarāma was simultaneously the son of Devakī and Rohiṇī, and that this was not reincarnation or body-transfer (dehāntara) but the Lord's own inconceivable potency. Student B presses: 'So you're saying the rules don't apply to your god? Isn't that exactly what every tradition says about its own miracles?' Student A must respond as Parīkṣit-like inquirer turned Śuka-like teacher — neither retreating into 'it's just faith' nor pretending it's scientifically demonstrable. After 5 minutes, swap roles. Debrief: where did each of you reach for material compassion (smoothing the discomfort) instead of real compassion (serving the soul of your friend)?

Objections & Responses
  • This is a contradiction dressed up as mystery. A baby cannot be in two wombs. Calling it 'inconceivable potency' (acintya-śakti) is just a way of refusing to answer the question — it's a thought-stopper, not theology.
    Parīkṣit Mahārāja raises exactly your objection — 'kuto dehāntaraṁ vinā,' how is this possible without transmigration? The verse honors the question; it does not suppress it. The Gauḍīya answer is not 'stop thinking' but 'think more carefully about categories.' The jīva is bound by the laws of dehāntara (BG's framework of bodies changing); Kṛṣṇa and His direct expansions like Saṅkarṣaṇa are not bound by those laws because They are not products of prakṛti. To insist Balarāma must obey jīva-rules is to have already concluded He is a jīva — which is the very point in dispute. Acintya-śakti is not a curtain pulled over ignorance; it names the precise place where our material logic, calibrated for conditioned souls, stops applying. The honest move is to notice the limit, not to pretend the limit is the whole map.
  • Even granting the theology, why does this matter to me? I have exams, a job, a marriage. A debate about whose womb Balarāma was in 5,000 years ago is the definition of irrelevant.
    It matters because the verse is training your discriminative wisdom (viveka) for the choice you make every hour: do I treat Kṛṣṇa as one more item inside the material system, or as the Person who stands outside it? If Balarāma is just another soul shuffling bodies, then bhakti is one more self-improvement project alongside your exams and your marriage — useful, optional, replaceable. If He is Saṅkarṣaṇa Himself, transcendent to dehāntara, then surrender to Him is categorically different from any other investment of your time. Parīkṣit asks this question seven days before his death. He is not doing trivia. He is sorting, at the end of his life, what is material and what is not — and he is showing you that the difference is not abstract. Today, in one decision — what you watch, what you say to your spouse, how you spend the next hour — locate which side of that line you are standing on.
Analogy

Asking how Balarāma could be in two wombs without 'changing bodies' is like asking how the same flame can light a second lamp without leaving the first. Saṅkarṣaṇa is not a body to be moved; He is the source-flame from which Rohiṇī's lamp was lit while Devakī's lamp continued to burn. Parīkṣit asks the question precisely so Śukadeva can establish: Bhagavān and His expansions are not subject to the ordinary mechanics of embodiment.
