Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.68

ఆత్మానమిహ సఞ్జాతం జానన్ప్రాగ్ విష్ణునా హతమ్ । మహాసురం కాలనేమిం యదుభి: స వ్యరుధ్యత ॥ ౬౮ ॥

In his previous birth, Kaṁsa had been a great demon named Kālanemi and been killed by Viṣṇu. Upon learning this information from Nārada, Kaṁsa became envious of everyone connected with the Yadu dynasty.
Purport

Persons who are demons, enemies of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, are called asuras. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā, the asuras, because of their enmity toward the Supreme Personality of Godhead, take birth after birth in asura families and therefore glide down to the darkest hellish regions.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In his previous birth, Kaṁsa had been a great demon named Kālanemi and been killed by Viṣṇu. Upon learning this information from Nārada, Kaṁsa became envious of everyone connected with the Yadu dynasty.
Commentary

Knowing from Narada that he had previously as Kalanemi been killed by Visnu and had now been born as Kamsa in Mathura, he made the Yadus his enemies. Surasena indicates Mathura, which is within Surasena. Thus the statement "He enjoyed Surasena:" means "He enjoyed Mathura and the surrounding lands."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

He was previously killed by Viṣṇu, the Lord who spreads himself throughout the universe by his powers. This indicates Viṣṇu’ṣ ability to kill him. Kaṁsa was able to remember his previous birth because of Nārada’s statement bhūtapūṛvaṁ ca tam smara: remember that previous birth.

Personal Guidance
Envy of Kṛṣṇa and His devotees is not a passing mood — it is a karmic posture that follows the asura from birth to birth.
Practical Applications
  • Notice the first flicker of envy when a devotee is praised today, and immediately offer a silent praṇāma to Kṛṣṇa instead of feeding the thought.
  • Refuse to align yourself — in speech, humor, or sympathy — with anyone mocking the Yadu-paramparā of devotees Kṛṣṇa claims as His own.
  • Before sleep, name one resentment you carried today and consciously hand it to Kṛṣṇa, so it does not harden into the asura-saṁskāra Kaṁsa carried across lives.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like Kaṁsa, nursing an old grievance — treating someone connected to a past wound as an enemy today, rather than seeing them as a soul before me?
  • When I learn something about my own past (a failure, a karmic debt, a previous defeat), do I respond with vairāgya and surrender to Kṛṣṇa, or do I respond like an asura — by sharpening my enmity and plotting against the very people Kṛṣṇa has placed near me?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the next time you feel a flash of envy when a classmate or someone in your feed gets recognition — name it as matsaratva, not 'I just don't like them.' Naming it honestly is the first cut against the Kaṁsa-pattern.
  • Audit one group you have written off wholesale — a clique at school, a 'type' of person on social media, students from a rival college. Choose one individual from that group this week and have a real conversation; refuse the dynasty-level grudge.
  • When you learn something unflattering about yourself (from a teacher, a parent, a hard piece of feedback), do not weaponize it the way Kaṁsa weaponized Nārada's news. Take it to your japa or to a senior devotee within twenty-four hours, before the ahaṅkāra builds a story around it.
householder
  • Watch for blanket resentments at work — 'that whole department,' 'those vendors,' 'people from that background.' These are the householder's version of resenting the Yadus. Each week, identify one and replace it with a specific, named, person-by-person assessment.
  • In family life, do not pass dynasty-grudges to your children. If there is a cousin's family, an in-law's circle, or a community you carry old enmity toward, decide deliberately what you will and will not say in front of the kids — they inherit the matsaratva otherwise.
  • When a peer is promoted, praised, or prospering — especially someone you quietly compete with — perform one concrete act of honor toward them within the week (a sincere message, a referral, an introduction). Use vairāgya from the fruit of your own work (BG 2.47) as the fuel.
senior
  • Old enmities calcify with age. Make a list of people and groups you have carried lifelong resentment toward; before this body is given up, take one off the list each month through a letter, a call, or sincere internal forgiveness offered before the Deity.
  • Kaṁsa's tragedy was that knowledge of his past did not soften him — it sharpened his enmity. As you reflect on your own life-review, watch for the same temptation: do not let memoir become a ledger of grievances. Let it become an offering.
  • Mentor the young in your family or community away from dynasty-grudges — the 'we don't deal with those people' inheritances. Your final teaching is what you refuse to pass on, as much as what you pass on.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as ancient mythology about one demon king, rather than recognizing the same asuric pattern — birth-after-birth enmity toward Kṛṣṇa and His devotees — as a tendency latent in one's own ahaṅkāra.
  • Mistaking Kaṁsa's daksya and tejas (his sharpness, his vigour, his strategic intelligence) as virtues in themselves; these qualities, divorced from devotion to Kṛṣṇa, only deepen the descent into darker regions, not elevate the practitioner.
  • Using the doctrine of asuric birth-after-birth to write off difficult people in one's life as 'demons,' thereby justifying one's own envy and hostility toward them — the very mood of Kaṁsa — instead of examining where that asuric mood lives in oneself.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kaṁsa learned of his past life as Kālanemi and used that knowledge to deepen his enmity. Share with your partner: when you have learned something true about yourself (a tendency, a past pattern), did it move you toward Kṛṣṇa or did it harden a familiar identity? Disagree if you must — is self-knowledge automatically purifying?
  • Prabhupāda says asuras take birth after birth in asura families and 'glide down to the darkest hellish regions.' Discuss: does this mean a person born into a demonic environment has no agency? Where does the line fall between saṁskāra and choice? Push back on each other.
  • The verse shows envy (matsaratva) directed at an entire dynasty — not one person but everyone connected to the Yadus. In your own life, name a group (a profession, a community, a family) toward whom you carry blanket resentment. Is that envy a 'small' character flaw or, as the Bhāgavatam suggests, the seed of asura-bhāva?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a longtime devotee who has just been passed over for a service role they wanted; the role went to a newer devotee from a well-connected family. Student B is their friend. A vents: 'It's always those people — the whole circle protects each other.' B must respond not with therapy-speak ('your feelings are valid') and not with spiritual bypass ('just chant more'), but as a Gauḍīya friend who has read SB 10.1.68 — naming the Kaṁsa-pattern of resenting an entire group, while still honoring the real hurt. Run the scene for five minutes; then swap and run it again with B as the venter.

Objections & Responses
  • Past-life karma is unfalsifiable. Saying Kaṁsa was Kālanemi in a previous birth, and that this 'explains' his envy, is just a story to make demons seem cosmically demonic. It removes responsibility from upbringing, trauma, social conditions — the things we can actually study and address.
    The Bhāgavatam is not offering a sociological explanation in competition with psychology; it is showing the depth of the disease. Upbringing and trauma are real — Kṛṣṇa Himself accepts that the guṇas act on the embodied soul (BG 13.1, 3.21). What past-life continuity adds is sobering, not exculpating: the ahaṅkāra you nurse today is not new, and it will not end with this body unless you take shelter. Far from removing responsibility, it raises the stakes of this human birth. The point is not 'Kaṁsa couldn't help it' — the point is that envy of the Lord and His devotees, left unchecked across lifetimes, hardens into asura-svabhāva. Look honestly at your own recurring resentments before dismissing the diagnosis.
  • Calling people 'asuras' and consigning them to 'the darkest hellish regions' is exactly the kind of in-group / out-group thinking that makes religion dangerous. It dehumanizes opponents.
    Notice what the verse actually condemns: not a race, not a caste, not a rival sect — it condemns enmity toward the Supreme Personality of Godhead and His devotees. Asura is a diagnosis of a disposition, not a label slapped on people we dislike. Prabhupāda himself, in the line of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, taught that even the lowest can be elevated by the holy name; the Yadus did not exterminate Kaṁsa's family, Kṛṣṇa took that act on Himself for a precise purpose. The Gauḍīya teacher's job is to recognize asura-bhāva first within one's own heart — the matsaratva that flares when a 'rival' is praised — and to treat it with sādhana, not to go hunting outward for asuras to denounce. Used that way, the category protects against in-group cruelty rather than authorizing it.
