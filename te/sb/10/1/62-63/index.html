Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.62-63

The inhabitants of Vṛndāvana, headed by Nanda Mahārāja and including his associate cowherd men and their wives, were none but denizens of the heavenly planets, O Mahārāja Parīkṣit, best of the descendants of Bharata, and so too were the descendants of the Vṛṣṇi dynasty, headed by Vasudeva and Devakī and the other women of the dynasty of Yadu. The friends, relatives and well-wishers of both Nanda Mahārāja and Vasudeva and even those who externally appeared to be followers of Kaṁsa were all demigods.
Purport

As previously discussed, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, informed Lord Brahmā that Lord Kṛṣṇa would personally descend to mitigate the suffering on the earth. The Lord ordered all the denizens of the heavenly planets to take birth in different families of the Yadu and Vṛṣṇi dynasties and in Vṛndāvana. Now this verse informs us that all the family and friends of the Yadu dynasty, the Vṛṣṇi dynasty, Nanda Mahārāja and the gopas descended from the heavenly planets to see the pastimes of the Lord. As confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā (4.8) , the Lord’s pastimes consist of paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām — saving the devotees and killing the demons. To demonstrate these activities, the Lord called for devotees from different parts of the universe. There are many devotees who are elevated to the higher planetary systems. prāpya puṇya-kṛtāṁ lokān uṣitvā śāśvatīḥ samāḥ śucīnāṁ śrīmatāṁ gehe yoga-bhraṣṭo ’bhijāyate “The unsuccessful yogī, after many, many years of enjoyment on the planets of the pious living entities, is born into a family of righteous people, or into a family of rich aristocracy.” ( Bg. 6.41 ) Some devotees, having failed to complete the process of devotional service, are promoted to the heavenly planets, to which the pious are elevated, and after enjoying there they may be directly promoted to the place where the Lord’s pastimes are going on. When Lord Kṛṣṇa was to appear, the denizens of the heavenly planets were invited to see the pastimes of the Lord, and thus it is stated here that the members of the Yadu and Vṛṣṇi dynasties and the inhabitants of Vṛndāvana were demigods or almost as good as demigods. Even those who externally helped the activities of Kaṁsa belonged to the higher planetary systems. The imprisonment and release of Vasudeva and the killing of various demons were all manifestations of the pastimes of the Lord, and because the devotees would be pleased to see these activities personally, they were all invited to take birth as friends and relatives of these families. As confirmed in the prayers of Kuntī ( Bhāg. 1.8.19 ), nato nāṭya-dharo yathā. The Lord was to play the part of a demon-killer, and a friend, son and brother to His devotees, and thus these devotees were all summoned.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The inhabitants of Vṛndāvana, headed by Nanda Mahārāja and including his associate cowherd men and their wives, were none but denizens of the heavenly planets, O Mahārāja Parīkṣit, best of the descendants of Bharata, and so too were the descendants of the Vṛṣṇi dynasty, headed by Vasudeva, and Devakī and the other women of the dynasty of Yadu. The friends, relatives and well-wishers of both Nanda Mahārāja and Vasudeva and even those who externally appeared to be followers of Kaṁsa were all demigods.
Commentary

These verses are spoken by Sukadeva, or they can be taken as the words of Narada to Kamsa. When Kamsa gave back the child to Devaki, Narada could see that the appearance of the Lord would be blocked. He therefore appeared at this time and spoke these words. In order to derive personal joy by seeing his Lord, in order to give joy to the devatas by provoking Kamsa to violence against the devotees to cause the Lord to appear, in order accomplish the slaying of Kamsa for his violence to the devotees, in order to shower himself in the intense devotion of Vasudeva and Devaki and get their blessings by delivering to them a confirmation of the Lord's appearance, in order to increase their eagerness to see the Lord, and drown them in an ocean of bliss by cutting down their doubts of whether the Lord would really appear, in order to please Devaki and Vasudeva by making them blissful even in those circumstances of suffering, and in order to make Kamsa favorable to himself by presenting false friendship, Narada Muni came and spoke confidential words to Kamsa in these two verses. Devata praya (most of the devatas) means that though the devatas were born in the Yadu family, some demons were also born among them. Kamsa is addressed as bharata because bha means desire in the mode of ignorance and rata means absorption. He was absorbed in enjoying in ignorance. (If it is Sukadeva speaking, bharata refers to Pariksit)

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

All (ca) the cowherds living in Mahāvana and what to speak of (ca) their wives and even the servants as well as the Vṛṣṇis or Yādavas and others also were the Lord’s associates (devatā). Almost all of the Yadus were associates of the Lord, but not all, because some were demons. O descendant of Bharata, Parīkṣit! This is also true of your grandfather. The members of the Bhoja dynasty also, devoted to Kaṁsa, were associates of the Lord. Direct relatives (jṣātayaḥ), those more indirectly related (bandhu) and friends (suhṛdaḥ) were also associates of the Lord. Thus, one should not hate Nārada for informing Kaṁsa.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa personally summons His devotees from the higher planets to play in His pastimes — even those who appear to oppose Him are part of His līlā.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today the people around you who feel like 'opponents' and consider that they too may be playing a role in Kṛṣṇa's larger plan for your bhakti.
  • Read aloud one verse from the Tenth Canto and pray for the privilege of witnessing the Lord's pastimes through hearing.
  • Refuse to see your family and friends as accidents — treat each relationship as a stage Kṛṣṇa has set, and serve them as fellow participants in His līlā.
Reflection Questions
  • This verse reveals that even those who 'externally appeared to be followers of Kaṁsa' were demigods playing a part in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — where in my own life have I judged someone as an obstacle when they may actually be an instrument in a larger arrangement I cannot see?
  • If Kṛṣṇa personally summons devotees from the heavenly planets to witness His pastimes, what does it mean that I have been given this human birth with access to this very narrative — and what am I doing with that summons today?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • That classmate who beat you out for the internship, the friend who ghosted you, the professor who graded you harshly — pick one this week and write down: what role might they be playing in your formation that you cannot yet see? Do not post it; just sit with it.
  • Your social feed is a gallery of people who look more 'chosen' than you — better grades, better partners, better aesthetics. The verse reframes this: every soul is placed exactly where Kṛṣṇa needs them. Spend one full day this week without checking who else got what. Notice what your mind does in the silence.
  • Find one older devotee on campus or in your sangha — not the most charismatic, just one who is steady — and ask them how they ended up in bhakti. The verse's point is that the right people are placed around you; your job is to recognize them past the externals.
householder
  • The difficult in-law, the frustrating boss, the child who tests you the most — treat one of them this month as a soul Kṛṣṇa has placed in your household for a reason you may not understand. Do not change your boundaries; change the silent commentary you run about them.
  • When making your next significant family decision (school for the kids, a job change, a move), explicitly include this question in your deliberation: 'Whose spiritual life depends on the answer I give here?' The Yadus and gopas understood their domestic arrangements were part of a larger arrangement; ours are too, even when invisible.
  • Identify one relationship in your extended family that you have written off as 'just toxic.' Before the next gathering, do one small act of service for that person — a phone call, a remembered birthday, a meal sent over — without expectation of change. Practice seeing past the kaṁsa-vesha (the Kaṁsa-costume) to the soul underneath.
senior
  • Look back over the people who shaped your life — including the ones who hurt you most. Write a short list of those whose role you can now see was, in retrospect, providential. Share it with one younger person this month; this is the kind of testimony only your stage of life can give.
  • Mortality concentrates the question of who and what we have been clinging to. Practice loosening your grip on one possession, one role, or one expectation each month — not by giving it away dramatically, but by mentally offering it to Kṛṣṇa and noticing what relaxes in the body. The Yadus knew they were temporary; so are you.
  • Mentor someone half your age into bhakti this year — not by lecturing, but by inviting them into your sādhana (japa, reading, prasāda). The verse shows that sādhu-saṅga is how Kṛṣṇa moves souls into position. Your last decades are for being one of those positioning hands.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as a charter for spiritual elitism — concluding 'the devotees around Kṛṣṇa were special demigods, so I cannot aspire to that intimacy' — when the purport actually describes yoga-bhraṣṭas (fallen yogīs) being given a second chance, which is an invitation, not a closed door.
  • Using the 'they were all demigods in disguise' framing to romanticize one's own circle, casting family and friends as cosmic players in a personal drama — a subtle ahaṅkāra that puts oneself at the center of a Kṛṣṇa-līlā that isn't happening here.
  • Treating the demigod identity of Kaṁsa's apparent followers as license for moral relativism — 'if even the villains are demigods, then good and evil don't matter' — missing that the Lord still came vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām, and the play of demon-killing was real within the līlā.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse says even those who externally appeared to follow Kaṁsa were demigods sent to participate in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. Share a time when someone you judged as an obstacle later turned out to be playing a necessary role in your spiritual life. Where did your judgment fail you, and where might it still be failing you today?
  • Prabhupāda quotes BG 6.41 — the yoga-bhraṣṭa is born into a pious or wealthy family and may even be promoted to where the Lord's pastimes are happening. Discuss with your partner: does this make our present circumstances feel like a gift, or like a debt? Is there a difference between the two, and which one motivates you more honestly?
  • The Yadus, Vṛṣṇis, and gopas were all 'almost as good as demigods' — yet they appeared as ordinary cowherds, mothers, and townsfolk. Debate: when we look for sādhu-saṅga today, are we looking for the right markers, or are we still drawn to externals (charisma, position, public reputation)? Give a concrete example from your own life.
Role Play

Two students sit across from each other. Student A is a serious sādhaka who has just learned that a colleague at work — someone who openly mocks their bhakti practice and has been undermining their projects — was actually instrumental in a promotion they just received. Student B plays a fellow devotee that A is venting to. A must wrestle aloud with whether to keep treating this colleague as an adversary or to recognize, in light of SB 10.1.62-63, that even those who 'externally appeared to be followers of Kaṁsa' may be playing a role in the Lord's larger arrangement. B should not let A off easy with cheap spiritualization — push A to articulate what concretely changes in tomorrow's interaction, without becoming naive or sentimental. After 7 minutes, swap roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like elitism dressed in Sanskrit. Saying that Kṛṣṇa's family and friends were 'demigods' from higher planets makes ordinary devotees feel like they could never have that kind of intimacy with the Lord. Isn't this just spiritual aristocracy?
    The verse is not drawing a line that excludes us — it is showing why those particular souls were placed in those particular roles for that particular līlā. The same Lord who summoned demigods to play His parents and cowherd friends also says in the Gītā that He is bhakti-priya — He is conquered by devotion, not by pedigree. Prabhupāda emphasizes the yoga-bhraṣṭa point precisely so we do not despair: pious birth is itself a sign of incomplete devotional service from a previous life being carried forward. The intimacy of Vṛndāvana is not closed; it is the destination of sincere bhakti, not an inherited privilege. The aristocracy reading is the ahaṅkāra reading — it makes the verse about status. The bhakti reading makes it about Kṛṣṇa's careful arrangement of every soul, including yours.
  • If even Kaṁsa's followers were secretly demigods playing a role, doesn't this collapse moral seriousness? Then nobody is really an enemy, nothing is really wrong, everyone is just acting out a script. Why bother with vairāgya or tapas if the whole thing is choreographed?
    The verse does not say that ordinary human evil is choreographed — it says that for this specific descent of the Lord, devotees were summoned to fill every role so that the pastimes could unfold. That is not a metaphysics of moral relativism; it is a statement about the unique circumstances of Kṛṣṇa-līlā. In our own lives, we are not in Vṛndāvana 5,000 years ago — we are in the field of karma, where wrong is wrong and our choices have real reactions. What the verse offers us is not an excuse to drop sādhana but a corrective to our small-mindedness about who Kṛṣṇa is using and how. Tapas and vairāgya remain non-negotiable precisely because we are not yet the kind of soul who can be trusted with a part in the līlā. The verse should sober us, not relax us.
