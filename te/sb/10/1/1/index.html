Important Words
  • kathitaḥ — has already been described
    Past passive participle of kath, 'to tell.' Parīkṣit acknowledges receipt before asking for more — the disciplic posture: I have heard, and because I have heard, I am qualified to ask the next question. Śravaṇam earns the right to praśna.
  • vaṁśa-vistāraḥ — broad description of the dynasties
    Vaṁśa = lineage, succession; vistāra = elaboration, spread. The Bhāgavatam does not abridge — it spreads out the family tree of dharma because Kṛṣṇa enters human history through a specific vaṁśa, not as an abstract principle.
  • soma-sūryayoḥ — of the moon-god and the sun-god
    Genitive dual. The two great royal lines (Candra-vaṁśa, Sūrya-vaṁśa) into which Bhagavān's avatāras appear — Rāma in the Sūrya line, Kṛṣṇa in the Candra (Yadu) line. The verse situates Kṛṣṇa's appearance in real, transmissible history, not myth.
  • caritam — the character, conduct
    From car, 'to move, act.' Carita is what someone did — biography rather than philosophy. The Bhāgavatam's evidence for Kṛṣṇa is not argument but caritam: His own and His devotees' deeds, witnessed and transmitted.
  • parama-adbhutam — supremely wonderful
    Parama (highest) + adbhuta (astonishing) — beyond the ordinarily astonishing. Parīkṣit applies this to the kings already heard, signaling that what is coming (Kṛṣṇa-līlā) must be more wonderful still. Adbhuta-rasa here functions as a doorway to the higher rasas the Tenth Canto will open.
King Parīkṣit said: My dear lord, you have elaborately described the dynasties of both the moon-god and the sun-god, with the exalted and wonderful character of their kings.
Purport

At the end of the Ninth Canto, Twenty-fourth Chapter, Śukadeva Gosvāmī summarized the activities of Kṛṣṇa. He spoke of how Kṛṣṇa had personally appeared to reduce the burden on the earth, how He had manifested His pastimes as a householder, and how, soon after His birth, He had transferred Himself to His Vrajabhūmi-līlā. Parīkṣit Mahārāja, being naturally a devotee of Kṛṣṇa, wanted to hear more about Lord Kṛṣṇa. Therefore, to encourage Śukadeva Gosvāmī to continue speaking about Kṛṣṇa and give further details, he thanked Śukadeva Gosvāmī for having described the activities of Kṛṣṇa in brief. Śukadeva Gosvāmī had said: jāto gataḥ pitṛ-gṛhād vrajam edhitārtho hatvā ripūn suta-śatāni kṛtorudāraḥ utpādya teṣu puruṣaḥ kratubhiḥ samīje ātmānam ātma-nigamaṁ prathayañ janeṣu “The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, known as līlā-puruṣottama, appeared as the son of Vasudeva but immediately left His father’s home and went to Vṛndāvana to expand His loving relationships with His confidential devotees. In Vṛndāvana the Lord killed many demons, and afterward He returned to Dvārakā, where according to Vedic principles He married many wives who were the best of women, begot through them hundreds of sons, and performed sacrifices for His own worship to establish the principles of householder life.” ( Bhāg. 9.24.66 ) The Yadu dynasty belonged to the family descending from Soma, the moon-god. Although the planetary systems are so arranged that the sun comes first, before the moon, Parīkṣit Mahārāja gave more respect to the dynasty of the moon-god, the soma-vaṁśa, because in the Yādava dynasty, descending from the moon, Kṛṣṇa had appeared. There are two different kṣatriya families of the royal order, one descending from the king of the moon planet and the other descending from the king of the sun. When the Supreme Personality of Godhead appears, He generally appears in a kṣatriya family because He comes to establish religious principles and the life of righteousness. According to the Vedic system, the kṣatriya family is the protector of the human race. When the Supreme Personality of Godhead appeared as Lord Rāmacandra, He appeared in the sūrya-vaṁśa, the family descending from the sun-god, and when He appeared as Lord Kṛṣṇa, He did so in the Yadu dynasty, or yadu-vaṁśa, whose descent was from the moon-god. In the Ninth Canto, Twenty-fourth Chapter, of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, there is a long list of the kings of the yadu-vaṁśa. All the kings in both the soma-vaṁśa and sūrya-vaṁśa were great and powerful, and Mahārāja Parīkṣit praised them very highly ( rājñāṁ cobhaya-vaṁśyānāṁ caritaṁ paramādbhutam ). Nonetheless, he wanted to hear more about the soma-vaṁśa because that was the dynasty in which Kṛṣṇa had appeared. The supreme abode of the Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is described in Brahma-saṁhitā as the abode of cintāmaṇi: cintāmaṇi-prakara-sadmasu kalpa-vṛkṣa-lakṣāvṛteṣu surabhīr abhipālayantam. The Vṛndāvana-dhāma on this earth is a replica of that same abode. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (8.20) , in the spiritual sky there is another, eternal nature, transcendental to manifested and unmanifested matter. The manifested world can be seen in the form of many stars and planets such as the sun and moon, but beyond this is the unmanifested, which is imperceptible to those who are embodied. And beyond this unmanifested matter is the spiritual kingdom, which is described in Bhagavad-gītā as supreme and eternal. That kingdom is never annihilated. Although material nature is subject to repeated creation and annihilation, that spiritual nature remains as it is eternally. In the Tenth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, that spiritual nature, the spiritual world, is described as Vṛndāvana, Goloka Vṛndāvana or Vraja-dhāma. The elaborate description of the above-mentioned śloka from the Ninth Canto — jāto gataḥ pitṛ-gṛhād ( Bhāg. 9.24.66 ) — will be found here, in the Tenth Canto.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
King Parīkṣit said: My dear lord, you have elaborately described the dynasties of both the moon-god and the sun-god, with the exalted and wonderful character of their kings.
Commentary

In order to increase the speaker’s enthusiasm to speak about the most crucial topics, Pariksit praises the previous descriptions in the Bhagavatam. Because candra is the grandson of Brahma, because candra is his amsa, because he is the deity of the mind, and because Krsna appeared in that dynasty, candra is mentioned before surya, though the usual order would place surya first. Though the wonderful activities of Svayambhuva and his dynasty were related in the fourth canto, they did not culminate in sweetness. With Krsna of the moon dynasty and Rama of the sun dynasty, sweetness was finally attained. Thus, discussions of these dynasties became very prominent.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

I surrender to Madana-gopāla, Govinda, king of Vṛndāvana, who is anxious to give mercy to the wretched. I offer respects to the Supreme Lord, Kṛṣṇa-caitanya, the ocean of mercy, who appeared in Bengal to spread prema-bhakti. I offer respects to Mādhava-purī along with his disciples. By him the desire tree of Kṛṣṇa bhakti was sown among the people. I offer respects to Śrīdhara Svāmī, the sole protector of bhakti, who gave a systematic commentary for the purpose of perfecting bhakti. I offer respects to Sārvabhauma Vidyāvacaspati Bhaṭṭācārya, ornamented with all knowledge and who was the ornament of Bengal. The word śrī is added to rāja to indicate the great prema of Parīkṣit at the beginning of recitation of Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes. It is conjugated as śrī-rājaḥ rather than śrī-rājā (as above) optionally. The King makes this request to inspire Śukadeva and give him joy by showing eagerness to hear in detail the stories about Kṛṣṇa, the root of all stories. You have related the dynasties, in detail, listing the sons and grandsons, not with the sole intention of telling about the births and lives of those individuals. He uses bhavatā (by you) to show respect. He places the word “moon” before “sun” to indicate the importance of Kṛṣṇa’s dynasty. You have also related the heroic deeds of the members of both dynasties. There were most astonishing deeds: for instance the marriage of Purūravā to Urvaśī and Puraṣjaya with his Indravāha pastimes. Though you related the exploits of the dynasty of Svāyambhūva Manu and others starting in the Third Canto, the exploits of the sun and moon dynasties are most astonishing, for in those dynasties have appeared Rāmacandra and Kṛṣṇa.

Personal Guidance
Hearing about Kṛṣṇa's appearance and activities is not optional storytelling — it is the doorway out of repeated birth and death.
Practical Applications
  • Open the Tenth Canto today and read even one paragraph, treating the act of hearing as direct service to Kṛṣṇa.
  • When tempted to scroll news or entertainment, substitute fifteen minutes of Bhāgavatam hearing in its place.
  • Ask one specific question about Kṛṣṇa's appearance to a senior devotee or sādhu this week, modeling Parīkṣit's eager inquiry.
Reflection Questions
  • When I hear about Kṛṣṇa's appearance in the Yadu dynasty, do I treat it as ancient history or as a confidential subject I am personally meant to penetrate — and what specifically have I done this week to penetrate it?
  • Parīkṣit calls the kings' character paramādbhutam — supremely wonderful — and then asks for more about Kṛṣṇa Himself. Where in my own hearing am I content to stop at 'wonderful stories' instead of pressing forward into the Personality whom those stories exist to reveal?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Parīkṣit was 60 and had seven days; you may have 60 years and an open calendar. That is not an advantage if those years go to scrolling, optimizing your résumé, and waiting for life to 'really start.' Pick one hour this week — same hour, same place — and read SB 10.1 aloud. Treat it as the meeting you would not cancel for an internship interview.
  • In your friend group, Kṛṣṇa's 'appearance in the Yadu dynasty' will sound either embarrassingly literal or quaintly cultural. You will be tempted to translate Him into a metaphor to make Him survive at the dining hall table. Don't. Learn to say 'I take this as historically real and I'm still working out what that means' — that sentence is more honest than either belief-signaling or apologetic hedging.
  • When choosing a partner, a major, a city — notice that Parīkṣit, facing death, asked about a vaṁśa, a lineage. You are also choosing a lineage: who you marry, where you train, whose authority you accept. Make those choices in front of someone who has actually heard Kṛṣṇa-kathā for forty years, not only in front of peers your own age.
householder
  • The Yadu dynasty was a working royal family — politics, marriages, succession, wealth — and Kṛṣṇa entered exactly there. Your spreadsheet, your kids' school admissions, your aging parents' medical decisions are not obstacles to bhakti; they are the field where Kṛṣṇa's divyam character meets your sādhana. Stop waiting for a quieter season.
  • Parīkṣit asked Śukadeva about lineage because lineage transmits dharma. Audit, this month, what your children are actually receiving from you: which stories they hear at dinner, which adults you let shape them, what they see you do at 5 a.m. and at 10 p.m. A vaṁśa is built by repetition, not by occasional festivals.
  • Prabhupāda warns that without grasping Kṛṣṇa's appearance one stays in material bondage. For the householder, 'material bondage' is not abstract — it is the specific compulsion to take one more promotion, one more renovation, one more upgrade, postponing the question of what you are actually living for. Sit with your spouse this week and name one bondage you will both stop feeding.
senior
  • You are now closer to Parīkṣit's situation than to the youth in the room — fewer years ahead than behind, and the question 'what do I want to hear before I go' is no longer theoretical. Choose your Śukadeva: which voice, which text, which association will you actually sit with daily? Stop sampling and commit.
  • The dynasties Parīkṣit asked about were the ones his ancestors and descendants belonged to. Your equivalent task is to leave a transmittable lineage — not your portfolio, not your professional reputation, but the specific bhakti-saṁskāras your grandchildren will remember. Write down three pastimes of Kṛṣṇa you want them to hear from your mouth, and start telling them.
  • Loosening attachment at this stage does not mean withdrawal; Parīkṣit became more eager, not less, in his final week. Look honestly at what you are still gripping — a house, a grievance, an adult child's choices, a designation at the temple — and offer one of them this month. The test of understanding janma karma ca me divyam is whether you can leave the body without that grip still tightening.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating the Tenth Canto as the 'fun part' to skip ahead to, bypassing the foundation of the first nine cantos that Parīkṣit deliberately heard first.
  • Hearing Kṛṣṇa's janma and karma as mythology or symbolism rather than as divya — transcendental events whose factual acceptance is the very condition Bhagavad-gītā 4.9 sets for liberation.
  • Approaching the descriptions of Kṛṣṇa with the same appetite one brings to the dynastic narratives — as entertainment or cultural heritage — instead of with Parīkṣit's mood of a man preparing to die in seven days.
Discussion Prompts
  • Parīkṣit, hours from death, asks not for comfort but for more detail about Kṛṣṇa's family line. Share with your partner: when have you avoided going deeper into Kṛṣṇa-kathā because you felt you 'already knew enough'? Be honest — was it humility, or was it actually fatigue or distraction dressed up as understanding?
  • Prabhupāda says without grasping janma karma ca me divyam 'one cannot be liberated from material bondage.' That is a strong claim. Discuss: do you actually believe a sincere, ethical person who never hears about Kṛṣṇa's appearance is bound by karma? Where does this leave your non-devotee parents, friends, or colleagues — and how do you carry that without becoming sectarian or sentimentally universalist?
  • Śukadeva opens the Tenth Canto only after nine cantos of cosmology, dynasties, and devotee biographies. Many of us want to skip to Kṛṣṇa's pastimes immediately. Argue both sides with your partner: is the traditional sequence still necessary today, or is it acceptable for a modern seeker to begin with Daśama-skandha and work backward?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a long-time devotee visiting Student B, a college roommate who was raised culturally Hindu but is now agnostic. Student B asks, sincerely and without hostility: 'You keep saying Kṛṣṇa appeared in the Yadu dynasty. Why does it matter to you whether He literally took birth in a specific family 5,000 years ago? Isn't the teaching what counts?' Student A must respond using only what Parīkṣit's question and Prabhupāda's purport on SB 10.1.1 actually establish — without bluffing, without quoting verses they don't understand, and without dismissing the friend. After five minutes, switch roles. Then debrief: where did you reach for slogans? Where did you actually feel the divyam (transcendental nature) of Kṛṣṇa's janma and karma in your own conviction?

Objections & Responses
  • Genealogies of solar and lunar dynasties read like ancient mythology. Why should a modern, scientifically literate person treat the Yadu-vaṁśa as historical and not as religious folklore that frames a moral teaching?
    The objection assumes 'history' means what archaeology can verify. Parīkṣit himself calls these dynasties paramādbhutam — supremely wonderful — precisely because they are not ordinary history. The Bhāgavata is a śabda-pramāṇa: knowledge received through a disciplic line, not reconstructed from artifacts. We don't ask the Bhāgavata to compete with carbon dating, and we don't ask geology to disclose Kṛṣṇa's birth. The relevant test Prabhupāda gives is the one from Bhagavad-gītā 4.9 — janma karma ca me divyam — does understanding Kṛṣṇa's appearance as transcendental actually free a person from repeated birth and death? That is verifiable in a practitioner's life, and that is the proof the tradition stakes itself on.
  • Saying 'without understanding Kṛṣṇa's appearance one cannot be liberated' sounds like every other religion claiming exclusive truth. How is this not just Hindu sectarianism with better Sanskrit?
    Fair challenge, and it deserves a careful answer rather than a defensive one. Prabhupāda is not saying 'only those who use the name Kṛṣṇa are saved.' He is pointing to a specific mechanism: liberation from the cycle of janma and mṛtyu requires knowing the Lord tattvataḥ — in truth, as a person, with His own divyam activities — not as an abstraction. Anyone, anywhere, who comes to that realization through whatever causeless mercy reaches them, attains the same destination — mām eti so 'rjuna. The exclusivity is not about a religion's brand; it is about the difference between knowing a person and theorizing about an idea. A Gauḍīya teacher should hold this firmly without weaponizing it — Caitanya Mahāprabhu Himself wept for the conditioned souls.
Analogy

Parīkṣit is like a man on the last day of a long voyage who, instead of asking what awaits the ship, turns to the captain and says, 'Tell me again, from the beginning, who the King is who sent us.' The Yadu and Soma-Sūrya dynasties are the route map; Kṛṣṇa is the King. The dying king hears the route to confirm whose service he was on the whole time.

Stories
Parīkṣit cursed at the river

The young king, exhausted from hunting, had silently entered the meditation hut of the sage Śamīka and, in a flicker of dishonor, draped a dead snake on his neck. The sage's son Śṛṅgi cursed him — a serpent-bird would kill him in seven days.

Parīkṣit could have raged or denied; instead he laid down his sword, retired to the bank of the Gaṅgā, and called the sages together. When Śukadeva arrived, he asked the question every dying person should ask: what should a man on the threshold of death hear, do, and remember? Out of that question the Bhāgavatam was spoken.

This verse opens the Tenth Canto of that very conversation — Parīkṣit, having heard nine cantos, asks now for Kṛṣṇa's own appearance. The pious dynasties are recounted; he wants the King they were preparing for.
