Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.5.7

Important Words
  • gāvaḥ — the cows
    Plural nominative of go. The cow is the foundation of Vraja's economy and theology — Kṛṣṇa is go-pāla, go-vinda. To honor Him is first to honor the cows; the verse's ornamentation of cows precedes the dressing of the humans (verse 8).
  • vṛṣāḥ — the bulls
    The bull is Dharma personified (SB 1.16–17). Decorating bulls at Kṛṣṇa's birth means dharma itself is being celebrated — the Lord's appearance restores the bull on all four legs.
  • vatsatarāḥ — the calves
    Specifically the young calves. Kṛṣṇa's earliest pastime in Vraja is as a vatsa-pāla (calf-keeper). The calves are not background; they are the future companions of His sakhya-līlā.
  • haridrā — turmeric
    Haridrā is auspicious and antiseptic — the same powder smeared at weddings and births. The cows are being marked with the same maṅgalya the householders use, signaling that they are full members of the festive community.
  • barha-srak — peacock-feather garlands
    Barha: peacock feather, the signature ornament Kṛṣṇa Himself will wear in His crown. The cows are pre-figured in the Lord's own emblem — their decoration is a quiet prophecy of who has just arrived.
The cows, the bulls and the calves were thoroughly smeared with a mixture of turmeric and oil, mixed with varieties of minerals. Their heads were bedecked with peacock feathers, and they were garlanded and covered with cloth and golden ornaments.
Purport

The Supreme Personality of Godhead has instructed in Bhagavad-gītā (18.44) , kṛṣi-go-rakṣya-vāṇijyaṁ vaiśya-karma-svabhāvajam: “Farming, cow protection and trade are the qualities of work for the vaiśyas. ” Nanda Mahārāja belonged to the vaiśya community, the agriculturalist community. How to protect the cows and how rich this community was are explained in these verses. We can hardly imagine that cows, bulls and calves could be cared for so nicely and decorated so well with cloths and valuable golden ornaments. How happy they were. As described elsewhere in the Bhāgavatam, during Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira’s time the cows were so happy that they used to muddy the pasturing ground with milk. This is Indian civilization. Yet in the same place, India, Bhārata-varṣa, how much people are suffering by giving up the Vedic way of life and not understanding the teachings of Bhagavad-gītā.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The cows, the bulls and the calves were thoroughly smeared with a mixture of turmeric and oil, mixed with varieties of minerals. Their heads were bedecked with peacock feathers, and they were garlanded and covered with cloth and golden ornaments.
Commentary

Rusita means anointed with.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Sometimes the word ca is added to the verse. This indicates that not only were the cows ornamented but the calves and bulls as well. And even the buffalos were ornamented.

Personal Guidance
Cow protection and reverence for the vaiśya's wealth-in-life is real dharma — honor what sustains you as Kṛṣṇa's gift, not as raw material.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one source of daily sustenance (food, livelihood, household) and treat it today with the care Nanda's herdsmen gave their cows — clean it, decorate it, honor it.
  • Refuse to participate, even passively, in the modern reduction of animals to commodities; choose one meal today aligned with go-rakṣya and offer it to Kṛṣṇa first.
  • Notice when you treat your work or possessions as mere utility, and re-frame one task this evening as service to Kṛṣṇa's creation rather than extraction from it.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I extracting from creatures, people, or resources I am meant to protect — and how would my day look if I treated them as Nanda Mahārāja treated his cows?
  • What concrete wealth in my care (animals, dependents, tools, money) is being neglected because I treat it as 'just utility' rather than as something to honor and decorate?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit where your discretionary money actually goes this month — coffee, subscriptions, clothes, food delivery. Pick one recurring expense, cut it, and redirect that exact amount to gośālā seva or feeding a stray animal on campus. Track it for 30 days; do not tell anyone on social media.
  • Nanda Mahārāja honored his vaiśya sva-dharma without apology. If you are in commerce, engineering, or a 'non-spiritual' major, stop treating it as second-tier. Write down, in one paragraph, how your chosen field can be offered as karma-yoga — and read it before each exam this term.
  • Among your friend group, identify the one being treated as the 'cow' — the dependable, generous one whom everyone uses but no one celebrates. This week, garland them in the modern sense: a sincere message naming what they actually give, with no ask attached.
householder
  • Reframe the family budget as yajña, not consumption. Before approving the next big purchase — car upgrade, vacation, renovation — first commit a fixed percentage to cow protection, gośālā, or a dependent (aged parent, household help, animal). Make this the first line item, not the leftover.
  • Teach your children, by example this week, that wealth is for protecting dependents — not for display. Take them physically to a gośālā, animal shelter, or vṛddha-āśrama and let them see your money, not just your words, going there. Decorate the cows if you can; let the kids tie the cloth.
  • Examine your career through the BG 18.44 lens — kṛṣi-go-rakṣya-vāṇijya. If you are a vaiśya by nature, stop quietly disrespecting your own work as 'just business.' Identify one concrete way your trade or profession this quarter can directly fund or perform go-rakṣya, and execute it before the quarter ends.
senior
  • Your decorations are coming off, not going on — this is the season of loosening, not acquiring. Identify the most ornamented attachment you still wear (a property, a title, an investment, a social standing) and begin its formal handover this year, redirecting at least part of it toward go-rakṣya or the deity.
  • Mentor a younger householder specifically on the dignity of vaiśya sva-dharma — that earning, farming, trading, and cow protection offered to Kṛṣṇa are not a lesser path. Many in the next generation have absorbed contempt for commerce; your testimony from a long life is the antidote.
  • Plan your legacy around what you protect, not what you leave. Restructure your will so that a meaningful portion endows ongoing cow protection, deity service, or care of dependents in perpetuity — and tell your children, while you are still here, why.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reducing this verse to sentimental cow-imagery while ignoring its hard claim that go-rakṣya is sva-dharma for the vaiśya — letting it stay aesthetic instead of vocational.
  • Reading the ornaments and turmeric as opulent excess, missing that the affection itself was the wealth — and then justifying one's own stinginess toward dependents as 'simplicity'.
  • Romanticizing a lost Vedic India in the abstract while making no concrete change today in how one spends on, cares for, or even notices the living beings under one's protection.
Discussion Prompts
  • Nanda Mahārāja's wealth was poured into decorating cows, bulls, and calves — not into personal luxury. Where in our own communities do we genuinely route surplus toward protected dependents (animals, elders, the unwell), and where do we just claim to? Be specific — name something you have actually done in the last 30 days, and something you have rationalized away.
  • Prabhupāda says the cows of Yudhiṣṭhira's time muddied the pasture with milk, and that India's suffering today follows from abandoning this Vedic way of life. Do you actually accept that material prosperity follows from go-rakṣya and yajña, or do you secretly think it is a charming metaphor? Disagree with each other openly — don't just nod.
  • The vaiśya in this verse is celebrated, not minimized — kṛṣi-go-rakṣya-vāṇijya is glorious sva-dharma when offered to Kṛṣṇa. Many of us in modern professions quietly think 'real' spiritual life is sannyāsa or temple service. Where has this snobbery shaped your career choices, and what would change if you took your varṇa-karma as worship seriously?
Role Play

Two students play family members at a dinner table. Student A has just inherited a small piece of ancestral farmland with a few cows; the easy move is to sell it to a developer and invest the proceeds. Student B is a sibling who quotes this verse and BG 18.44 and insists the family has a duty of go-rakṣya. Student A must press hard on the practical objections — it doesn't pencil out, no one in the family knows how to care for cows, the kids live in the city. Student B must respond as a Gauḍīya practitioner, not a sentimentalist: what does Nanda Mahārāja's example actually obligate, and what does it not? Swap roles after five minutes. Debrief: where did the pro-surrender argument get weakest, and what would Prabhupāda have said there?

Objections & Responses
  • Decorating cows with turmeric, peacock feathers, cloth, and gold ornaments is sentimental cow-worship. Animals don't care about ornaments — this is just human projection dressed up as dharma.
    The decoration is not for the cow's aesthetic pleasure; it is the vaiśya household offering its wealth back through the cow, who is the visible recipient of go-rakṣya as sva-dharma. The same logic governs deity worship and yajña — Kṛṣṇa does not need a garland either, but the offering trains the heart of the offerer and consecrates the wealth. Strip the ornaments away and you don't get a more rational dharma; you get a household that has quietly redirected its surplus to its own comforts. The cow here is the test of where the family's prosperity actually flows.
  • Prabhupāda blames India's suffering on abandoning the Vedic way of life, but India's poverty has obvious modern economic causes — colonialism, policy, climate. Tying it to go-rakṣya is romantic nostalgia.
    The Gauḍīya teacher does not deny proximate causes; varṇāśrama-dharma operates alongside, not instead of, ordinary causation. The point is deeper — a civilization that severs the link between sva-dharma, yajña, and the protection of cows and brāhmaṇas loses the subtle basis on which its material prosperity rested. Yudhiṣṭhira's pastures muddy with milk are not a fable about bovine biology; they are a description of what happens when the whole society's daily action is offered to Kṛṣṇa through one's varṇa-karma. Remove that orientation and even abundant policy will not produce contented cows or contented people. The objection assumes the material is a closed system; the verse assumes it is not.
Analogy

When Bhagavān is born, even the animals are dressed for darśana. The cow standing decorated in turmeric and peacock-feather is not a costume — she is the visible form of the cowherds' love spilling outward, sanctifying every creature in the household. Vātsalya does not stop at the human gate.

Stories
Surabhi's worship of Kṛṣṇa

Bhāgavatam 10.27 records that after Govardhana-līlā, the cows of Vraja — led by the celestial cow Surabhi — bathed Kṛṣṇa in their milk in the abhiṣeka known as Govinda-paṭṭābhiṣeka. The cow is not a peripheral participant in Vraja's love; she is a central rasika.

The ornamentation in verse 7 is the cowherds' return-gesture: just as Surabhi will one day anoint Kṛṣṇa, the cowherds now anoint the cows in honor of Kṛṣṇa's birth. The circle of go-bhakti closes.
