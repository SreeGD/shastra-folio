Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.11.20

Important Words
  • tam aśeṣa - śekharam — Him, the crown of all
    Aśeṣa-śekhara — crest of all that is. The verse insists on Kṛṣṇa's supreme position precisely in the moment of His being bathed and dressed by His mother. Gauḍīya theology demands this co-presence: were He less than aśeṣa-śekhara, the rasa would collapse into mere sentiment.
  • matvā — considering
    Past active participle of man (to think) — 'having considered.' Yaśodā 'considers' Kṛṣṇa her son. The Gauḍīya gloss: this consideration is not a cognitive error; it is yoga-māyā-supported truth at the rasa-level. He genuinely is her son in Vraja, while remaining svayaṁ-bhagavān at the tattva-level.
  • sneha - nibaddha - dhīḥ — with intelligence bound by affection
    A precise rasa-compound: dhī (intelligence) ni-baddha (bound) by sneha (affection). The Gauḍīya teaching: prema does not bypass the intellect — it binds it, redirects it. Yaśodā's intellect, fully functional, is in service of her sneha rather than against it. This is the integration prema produces.
  • acyutam — Kṛṣṇa, the infallible
    Acyuta — never-falling. The verse twice marks His Godhood (aśeṣa-śekharam, acyutam) inside the act of being led home by the hand. The grammatical insistence is the doctrinal point: He is led, He is bathed, He is fed — and He remains acyuta throughout.
  • kṛtavatī — performed
    Past active participle, feminine — 'she having performed.' The active voice is hers; she is the doer of His bath, dress, feeding. Gauḍīya theology: Yaśodā's kartṛtva (doership) here is real seva, not a stage-show. The Lord receives doership from His devotees by allowing them this role.
My dear Mahārāja Parīkṣit, because of intense love and affection, mother Yaśodā, Kṛṣṇa’s mother, considered Kṛṣṇa, who was at the peak of all opulences, to be her own son. Thus she took Kṛṣṇa by the hand, along with Balarāma, and brought Them home, where she performed her duties by fully bathing Them, dressing Them and feeding Them.
Purport

Kṛṣṇa is always neat, clean and opulent and does not need to be washed, bathed or dressed, yet mother Yaśodā, because of affection, considered Him her ordinary child and did her duties to keep her son brilliant.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
My dear Mahārāja Parīkṣit, because of intense love and affection, mother Yaśodā, Kṛṣṇa's mother, considered Kṛṣṇa, who was at the peak of all opulences, to be her own son. Thus she took Kṛṣṇa by the hand, along with Balarāma, and brought Them home, where she performed her duties by fully bathing Them, dressing Them and feeding Them.
Commentary

She thought of the crest jewel of Ananta, the source of all avataras (asesa sekharam), as her son. Another meaning is: she considered him not as bhagavan (a- sesasekharam). Actually he was both. Another meaning could be: she thought of her son as the crest jewel of the entirety (asesa) of their family. Taking him to his house (sva batam) she made him bathe, eat and then decorated him (udayan).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

This verse summarizes her actions. Approaching slowly while uttering affectionate words, thinking of him as her son though he was to the head ornament of unlimited avatāras, her intelligence controlled intensely by affection, she took him by the hand though he was playing. She brought him back to her house (svabāṭam) in a way that he could not escape (acyutam) and performed auspicious acts (udayam) such as bathing, feeding and ornamenting him. Or she performed the festive rites suitable to his birth star, for which he was eager, as she had mentioned previously. She did this immediately (atha) or completely. Śukadeva addresses Parīkṣit with “O King!” Nrpa means the protector of the people. He addresses Parīkṣit with affection, on seeing Parīkṣit’s manifestation of affection for Kṛṣṇa. śrī-śuka uvāca gopa-vṛddhā mahotpātān anubhūya bṛhadvane nandādayaḥ samāgamya vraja-kāryam amantrayan śrī-śukaḥ uvāca—Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said; gopa-vṛddhāḥ—the elderly persons among the cowherd men; mahā-utpātān—very great disturbances; anubhūya—after experiencing; bṛhadvane—in the place known as Bṛhadvana; nanda-ādayaḥ—the cowherd men, headed by Nanda Mahārāja; samāgamya—assembled, came together; vraja-kāryam—the business of Vrajabhūmi; amantrayan—deliberated on how to stop the continuous disturbances in Mahāvana.

Personal Guidance
Even Kṛṣṇa, the peak of all opulences, accepts a devotee's loving sevā as her son — He is conquered by vātsalya-bhakti, not by power.
Practical Applications
  • Choose one small act of sevā today (cooking, cleaning, dressing the Deity or serving a guest as Kṛṣṇa) and perform it with the conviction that He personally accepts it.
  • Refuse the modern cynicism that sevā is merely 'symbolic' — bathe, dress, and feed Kṛṣṇa (or those entrusted to your care) as Yaśodā did, with shaucha and tenderness, not as a chore.
  • Notice when you withhold affection because someone 'doesn't need it' — give it anyway, the way Yaśodā bathed the already-pure Lord, and let love, not utility, govern your duty.
Analogy

An empress washing the feet of her own son — even though those feet are worshipped by all the gods — would not feel her affection diminished by His glory; if anything it would deepen, because she alone holds Him by the right of love. Yaśodā considers aśeṣa-śekhara (the crown of everything) her own son, and serves Him at full domestic detail: hand-led home, bathed, dressed, fed.

Stories
Yaśodā binding Kṛṣṇa (Dāmodara)

The most famous icon of this verse's principle is the Dāmodara-līlā: Yaśodā tries to tie the toddler Kṛṣṇa to the mortar, and the rope keeps falling exactly two fingers short — until her love exhausts her and Kṛṣṇa allows the binding. Sanātana Gosvāmī's Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta and the Caitanya-caritāmṛta both treat this scene as the highest aiśvarya of Bhagavān: that He, who is uncontainable, makes Himself containable by a devotee's prema.

The present verse's 'aśeṣa-śekharam … sneha-nibaddha-dhīḥ' is the same revelation in domestic form: He is the crown of all opulence; her dhī (intellect) is bound by sneha; she serves Him as her son. Both halves are simultaneously true — acintya-bhedābheda in its rasa-form.
