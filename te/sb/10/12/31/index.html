Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.12.31

తతోఽతికాయస్య నిరుద్ధమార్గిణో హ్యుద్గీర్ణద‍ృష్టేర్భ్రమతస్త్వితస్తత: । పూర్ణోఽన్తరఙ్గే పవనో నిరుద్ధో మూర్ధన్ వినిర్భిద్య వినిర్గతో బహి: ॥ ౩౧ ॥
tato ’tikāyasya niruddha-mārgiṇo

Important Words
  • ati-kāyasya — of him with an excessively large body
    Compound: ati (excessive) + kāya (body). The demon's body becomes hyperbolic — even bigger than at verse 16. Yet still finite. Gauḍīyas: no quantity of māyic expansion can match Bhagavān's qualitative infinity.
  • niruddha-mārgiṇaḥ — whose passages were blocked
    Compound: niruddha (blocked) + mārga (path). All openings sealed. The same niroddhum used in verse 25 (Kṛṣṇa wishing to restrain His friends) returns as niruddha — Kṛṣṇa restrains the demon instead. Verbal echo across the chapter.
  • mūrdhan vinirbhidya — breaking out through the head
    Compound: mūrdhan (crown of head) + vinirbhidya (breaking through). The prāṇa exits through the brahma-randhra — the same opening through which yogīs voluntarily leave the body at death (BG 8.10–13). Gauḍīyas read this as the gift of liberation given to Aghāsura through Kṛṣṇa's body.
  • pavanaḥ — life-air
    Wind, breath — but specifically the prāṇa. The demon's vital air, having nowhere else to go, departs as a liberated soul departs. The mechanism of his death is also the mechanism of his mukti.
Then, because Kṛṣṇa had increased the size of His body, the demon extended his own body to a very large size. Nonetheless, his breathing stopped, he suffocated, and his eyes rolled here and there and popped out. The demon’s life air, however, could not pass through any outlet, and therefore it finally burst out through a hole in the top of the demon’s head.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Then, because Kṛṣṇa had increased the size of His body, the demon extended his own body to a very large size. Nonetheless, his breathing stopped, he suffocated, and his eyes rolled here and there and popped out. The demon's life air, however, could not pass through any outlet, and therefore it finally burst out through a hole in the top of the demon's head.
Commentary

His throat blocked up (niruddha marginoh), his eyes falling out, the air, trapped in his body with no way of getting out, went out through the brahma randhra at the top of the head, and attained perfection (purnah).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Because Kṛṣṇa had expanded (tataḥ), Agha suffocated certainly (hi) and (tu) he became bewildered (bhramataḥ). He became like an insane person, thinking in pain “Where am I? Where will I go?” He became completely full, because his prāṇa was blocked. Thus his body swelled bigger and because of that, the prāṇa burst out of his head from a special spot (vi), the brahma-rundhra.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

Picture a balloon being inflated from the inside until it bursts — except the balloon is a demon's head, and the air is Kṛṣṇa Himself, growing without limit. The demon also tries to grow, but a finite creature cannot keep pace with the Infinite. His own life-air, with no other exit, breaks through the crown of his skull.
