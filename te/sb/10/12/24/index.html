Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.12.24

అస్మాన్ కిమత్ర గ్రసితా నివిష్టా- నయం తథా చేద్ బకవద్ వినఙ్‌క్ష్యతి । క్షణాదనేనేతి బకార్యుశన్ముఖం వీక్ష్యోద్ధసన్త: కరతాడనైర్యయు: ॥ ౨౪ ॥

Important Words
  • baka-vat — like Baka
    baka (heron-demon) + -vat (like). The boys explicitly compare Aghāsura to Bakāsura of chapter 11. Gauḍīyas: the cowherd boys have already metabolized Kṛṣṇa's previous līlās — Vraja's children are also Bhāgavatam-students.
  • baka-ari-uśat-mukham — the beautiful face of the enemy of Baka
    Compound: baka (heron-demon) + ari (enemy) + uśat (beautiful) + mukha (face). Kṛṣṇa is named here by His relation to the previous demon — 'Baka-enemy.' Each pastime adds a name; Kṛṣṇa's name-store grows by demon-defeat.
  • uddhasantaḥ — laughing loudly
    ud + has- (to laugh out). The boys laugh as they enter the demon's mouth. Gauḍīyas read this as the perfect sakhya-fearlessness: the joke is on the demon, not on them.
  • kara-tāḍanaiḥ — by clapping hands
    Compound: kara (hand) + tāḍana (striking). Hand-clapping — the universal child's signal for game-on. The cowherd boys are playing kabaddi with a python; the rules are their own.
Then the boys said, “Has this living creature come to swallow us? If he does so, he will immediately be killed like Bakāsura, without delay.” Thus they looked at the beautiful face of Kṛṣṇa, the enemy of Bakāsura, and, laughing loudly and clapping their hands, they entered the mouth of the python.
Purport

After talking about the terrible animal this way and that way, they decided to enter the demon’s mouth. They had full faith in Kṛṣṇa because they had experienced how Kṛṣṇa had saved them from the mouth of Bakāsura. Now, here was another asura, Aghāsura. Therefore, they wanted to enjoy the sport of entering the demon’s mouth and being saved by Kṛṣṇa, the enemy of Bakāsura.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Then the boys said, "Has this living creature come to swallow us? If he does so, he will immediately be killed like Bakāsura, without delay." Thus they looked at the beautiful face of Kṛṣṇa, the enemy of Bakāsura, and, laughing loudly and clapping their hands, they entered the mouth of the python.
Commentary

Together they spoke with a little fear. "This thing, if this is really a snake, and we enter his mouth, will he swallow us up?" Someone then answered to calm them down. "If it is so, then in an instant (ksanena) he will be killed as Baka was killed by Krsna (anena)." Saying this he smiled while looking at Krsna, who was standing some distance away. "Krsna is within our glance, so what have we to worry?" Getting this answer, they all began to laugh loudly and said, "Friends, we must find out what is in that mouth." Then they entered the mouth out of boyish curiosity. They ran in, clapping their hands, to show their fearlessness and courage, or to dismiss the snake. The calves also raising their tails ran after them inside the snake’s mouth.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Saying this, they laughed loudly on making analogies with a snake. Or they looked at Kṛṣṇa’s face and laughed loudly. Or they laughed to show that what they said was a joke, saying “You should be careful here.” To show courage and pride along with fearlessness, as if driving away a snake, they ran forward.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

Picture children walking into a tiger's open mouth while pointing at a friend and saying, 'If it tries anything, he'll handle it.' That friend is Kṛṣṇa. Their entire calculation rests on Him; they do not even need to articulate it. Sakhya-rasa is the rasa in which faith does not look like faith — it looks like assumption.

Stories
Bakāsura swallowed and split

Just one chapter earlier (SB 10.11), Bakāsura — Aghāsura's elder brother — appeared as a giant heron and swallowed Kṛṣṇa whole. Kṛṣṇa heated Himself inside the heron's throat until the demon released Him; then He grabbed the beak and tore the demon in two.

The boys remember this. When verse 24 says 'he will be killed like Bakāsura,' they are not speculating — they are citing yesterday's precedent. Sakhya-rasa has memory; Kṛṣṇa's exploits are oral tradition among His friends.
