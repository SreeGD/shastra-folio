Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.37-38

Important Words
  • parituṣṭaḥ aham — I was fully satisfied
    Pari (around, fully) + tuṣṭa (satisfied) + aham (I). Bhagavān's complete satisfaction with their tapas. Reading: His satisfaction (parituṣṭi) is what produces His appearance — bhakti pleases the Lord, and His pleasure is then visibly given as Himself.
  • śraddhayā — with faith
    Instrumental of śraddhā. Without śraddhā tapas is mere athletics. Caitanya-caritāmṛta defines śraddhā as the confidence that by serving Kṛṣṇa, all other duties are automatically performed (śraddhā śabde — viśvāsa kahe sudṛḍha niścaya). Foundation of bhakti.
  • bhaktyā — by devotional service
    Instrumental of bhakti. Crucial: their tapas was bhakti-yukta, not jñāna-mārga or yoga-mārga. The same actions performed with bhakti produce different fruit than performed for liberation. The verb's instrumental marks the means by which Bhagavān is reached — bhakti and bhakti alone.
  • vara-da-rāṭ — the king among benediction-givers
    Vara (boon) + da (giver) + rāṭ (king). The supreme bestower of boons. Bhagavān names Himself: not 'a' giver of benedictions but the best of them. Sambandha: knowing who gives is half of asking rightly.
  • mādṛśaḥ — (a son) like Me
    Mā (Me) + dṛśa (likeness). The famous request — 'a son like You'. Bhagavān's compassionate correction (verse 41): finding no one truly equal to Himself, He gave Himself instead. The bhakta's request reaches further than the bhakta knew.
  • vṛtaḥ — was asked for
    Past participle of √vṛ — 'to choose, ask for'. Same root as vara (boon). The shape of their request: they CHOSE Him. Bhakti is a chosen relation, not a metaphysical necessity. The Bhāgavata stresses this — surrender is voluntary, and Bhagavān reciprocates accordingly.
O sinless mother Devakī, after the expiry of twelve thousand celestial years, in which you constantly contemplated Me within the core of your heart with great faith, devotion and austerity, I was very much satisfied with you. Since I am the best of all bestowers of benediction, I appeared in this same form as Kṛṣṇa to ask you to take from Me the benediction you desired. You then expressed your desire to have a son exactly like Me.
Purport

Twelve thousand years on the celestial planets is not a very long time for those who live in the upper planetary system, although it may be very long for those who live on this planet. Sutapā was the son of Brahmā, and as we have already understood from Bhagavad-gītā (8.17) , one day of Brahmā equals many millions of years according to our calculation ( sahasra-yuga-paryantam ahar yad brahmaṇo viduḥ ). We should be careful to understand that to get Kṛṣṇa as one’s son, one must undergo such great austerities. If we want to get the Supreme Personality of Godhead to become one of us in this material world, this requires great penance, but if we want to go back to Kṛṣṇa ( tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti so ’rjuna ), we need only understand Him and love Him. Through love only, we can very easily go back home, back to Godhead. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu therefore declared, premā pum-artho mahān: love of Godhead is the highest achievement for anyone. As we have explained, in worship of the Lord there are three stages — jñāna, jñānamayī and rati, or love. Sutapā and his wife, Pṛśni, inaugurated their devotional activities on the basis of full knowledge. Gradually they developed love for the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and when this love was mature, the Lord appeared as Viṣṇu, although Devakī then requested Him to assume the form of Kṛṣṇa. To love the Supreme Personality of Godhead more, we want a form of the Lord like Kṛṣṇa or Rāma. We can engage in loving transactions with Kṛṣṇa especially. In this age, we are all fallen, but the Supreme Personality of Godhead has appeared as Caitanya Mahāprabhu to bestow upon us love of Godhead directly. This was appreciated by the associates of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu. Rūpa Gosvāmī said: namo mahā-vadānyāya kṛṣṇa-prema-pradāya te kṛṣṇāya kṛṣṇa-caitanya- nāmne gaura-tviṣe namaḥ In this verse, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu is described as mahā-vadānya, the most munificent of charitable persons, because He gives Kṛṣṇa so easily that one can attain Kṛṣṇa simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra. We should therefore take advantage of the benediction given by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, and when by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are cleansed of all dirty things ( ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam ), we shall be able to understand very easily that Kṛṣṇa is the only object of love ( kīrtanād eva kṛṣṇasya mukta-saṅgaḥ paraṁ vrajet ). Therefore, one need not undergo severe penances for many thousands of years; one need only learn how to love Kṛṣṇa and be always engaged in His service ( sevonmukhe hi jihvādau svayam eva sphuraty adaḥ ). Then one can very easily go back home, back to Godhead. Instead of bringing the Lord here for some material purpose, to have a son or whatever else, if we go back home, back to Godhead, our real relationship with the Lord is revealed, and we eternally engage in our eternal relationship. By chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, we gradually develop our eternal relationship with the Supreme Person and thus attain the perfection called svarūpa-siddhi. We should take advantage of this benediction and go back home, back to Godhead. Śrīla Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura has therefore sung, patita-pāvana-hetu tava avatāra: Caitanya Mahāprabhu appeared as an incarnation to deliver all fallen souls like us and directly bestow upon us love of Godhead. We must take advantage of this great benediction of the great Personality of Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O sinless mother Devakī, after the expiry of twelve thousand celestial years, in which you constantly contemplated Me within the core of your heart with great faith, devotion and austerity, I was very much satisfied with you. Since I am the best of all bestowers of benediction, I appeared in this same form as Kṛṣṇa to ask you to take from Me the benediction you desired. You then expressed your desire to have a son exactly like Me.
Commentary

"Because of your meditation on me, I am very pleased. I appeared in that form (four handed)." The austerity performed was constant meditation on the Lord with devotion and faith. The use of the three words, bhakti, sraddha and nitya indicate that the devotion was not merely an anga of yoga practice. Rather it was absorption in the Lord. That by itself leads to perfection; it is a cause of prema. Their bhakti was mixed however with jnana and aisvarya because of practice of yoga and austerity. Some also reason that since nitya siddha Vasudeva and Devaki had nitya siddha aisvarya-jnana mixed bhakti, their amsas Prsni and Sutapa also had mixed bhakti. The tapa and yoga practice were therefore incidental. In this verse the word bhavita is used instead of cintata. Their meditation was filled with bhava. In other words they had genuine bhakti for the Lord.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

O Devakī, devoid of aparādhas (anaghe)! This indicates her faultless austerity. Seeing you, I was satisfied. The affix pari denotes repeated action. I was satisfied with you, birth after birth. “But the Lord is satisfied with bhakti, not austerity.” That is true. Successive causes are shown. From austerity meant to please me faith in nine types of bhakti was produced. From faith came bhakti. By that bhakti, I was attained constantly in your hearts, with effort (bhāvitaḥ). The word ca indicates that then, by prema I was attained without effort. Their prema was filled with loving the Lord as their son. I was satisfied completely and have been obtained in your hearts. I, Kṛṣṇa (amunā vapuṣā), was the cause of this. Instrumental case expresses cause. Varada-raṭ is explained by Śrīdhara Svāmī. I give great results for those who do austerities. I give greater results for those having faith. I give greatest results for those having bhakti and prema. I said you should ask for a benediction. You asked for a son like me. They did not ask for the Lord directly, out of shyness.

Personal Guidance
Devakī's twelve thousand years of tapas brought Kṛṣṇa as her son — but Caitanya Mahāprabhu now gives that same Kṛṣṇa freely through the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra.
Practical Applications
  • Chant a fixed number of Hare Kṛṣṇa rounds today as your sādhana — receive directly what Devakī earned through twelve thousand years of austerity.
  • Refuse the impulse to bargain with Kṛṣṇa for some material 'son' — a result, a promotion, a relationship — and instead pray only to go back home to Him.
  • Speak Caitanya Mahāprabhu's name once today with gratitude as the mahā-vadānya who delivered kṛṣṇa-prema to fallen souls like you.
Reflection Questions
  • Sutapā and Pṛśni performed twelve thousand celestial years of tapas just to have the Lord come down as their son — what am I attempting to drag Kṛṣṇa into my world to obtain, instead of letting Him draw me back to His?
  • When I sit to chant or contemplate, am I cultivating love for Kṛṣṇa Himself, or am I quietly bargaining for a specific 'benediction' I would name if He asked vriyatāṁ varam?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit one prayer you have actually prayed this semester — for a grade, an admit, a crush, a follower count. Ask honestly: was I asking Kṛṣṇa to come into my plan, or asking Him to take me into His? Write the answer down, do not just feel it.
  • Pick one tapas-sized commitment for the next thirty days that costs you something real on campus — chanting one round before opening your phone in the morning, or skipping one weekend party to read Kṛṣṇa book. Twelve thousand celestial years are not on offer; thirty days are.
  • Notice the next time you treat sādhana like a transaction with God ('I chanted, so the exam should go well'). Catch it, name it, and replace it with one line — 'Kṛṣṇa, I want You, not Your arrangements' — before you go to sleep.
householder
  • Sutapā and Pṛśni did tapas together as a couple before Kṛṣṇa came as their child. This week, sit with your spouse for fifteen minutes a day in shared sādhana — japa, reading, or kīrtana. Treat the household itself as the tapovana.
  • Examine the boons you are currently asking from Kṛṣṇa through your work and parenting — promotion, the right school for the kids, a bigger home. For each one, ask: am I asking Him to bless my material project, or am I using this project to take my family back to Him? Re-aim one decision this week accordingly.
  • Each evening, before sleep, formally offer the day's actions — the meetings, the school run, the difficult conversation with your child — to Kṛṣṇa. Do not bargain for outcomes; just hand the day over. This is the householder's version of twelve thousand years.
senior
  • You have already lived through several 'bodies' — student, professional, parent. Sit quietly today and trace the continuity of the same self through them. Then ask: what tapas is appropriate for this final āśrama, and am I doing it, or am I still performing the householder's tapas out of habit?
  • Identify one attachment — a possession, a role, an opinion you are known for — that you are still asking Kṛṣṇa to preserve for you. Begin the practice of offering it back. Sutapā's prayer was 'come to me'; the senior's prayer ripens into 'take me to You.'
  • Mentor one younger devotee or family member this month not by giving advice but by quietly modeling chanting and remembrance. Prabhupāda's whole point in this purport is that Caitanya Mahāprabhu's gift travels through living examples, not lectures. Be one.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading Devakī's request as the ideal — modeling one's sādhana on 'getting Kṛṣṇa as my son/asset/answer' rather than on going back to Him, when Prabhupāda explicitly contrasts the two and recommends the latter.
  • Concluding that because twelve thousand celestial years of tapas were required, sincere bhakti now is futile — and missing Caitanya Mahāprabhu's mahā-vadānya gift of kṛṣṇa-prema through the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra in this age.
  • Treating chanting as a shortcut technique to skip discipline, while neglecting the śraddhā, nityaṁ, and bhaktyā that the verse names as the very texture of Sutapā's contemplation hṛdi bhāvitaḥ — chanting without that inner orientation becomes mechanical, not ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam.
Discussion Prompts
  • Sutapā and Pṛśni performed twelve thousand celestial years of tapas just to have Kṛṣṇa as their son — yet Prabhupāda says we today need only chant and love. Is this a real shortcut, or are we minimizing what bhakti actually costs? Where does each of you land, and why?
  • Devakī asked the Lord to appear as her son — a personal, almost 'material' request. Prabhupāda gently reframes: better to go back to Him than bring Him here. Share a benediction you have actually asked Kṛṣṇa for. Was it a 'bring Him here' prayer or a 'take me there' prayer? Be honest with each other.
  • The verse holds together two things modern devotees often split: severe tapas and intimate love. In your sādhana right now, which one are you neglecting — the austerity or the affection? Push back on each other's diagnosis.
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a sincere new bhakta who has just decided to chant sixteen rounds and follow the four regulative principles strictly, and is privately hoping this 'austerity' will produce a specific result — a marriage, a child, a job, healing for a parent. Student B is a slightly senior devotee who notices the transactional undertone in A's sādhana. B must, in the spirit of this verse, neither crush A's enthusiasm (Sutapā and Pṛśni's tapas was honored by Kṛṣṇa) nor leave the misunderstanding intact (Prabhupāda warns: don't bring Him here for material purposes; go back to Him). Play it for five minutes, then switch. Afterward the class discusses: where did the conversation get stuck, and what would Prabhupāda have said?

Objections & Responses
  • Twelve thousand celestial years of austerity to earn a son is mythology, not spirituality. This kind of story makes bhakti sound like a cosmic vending machine — insert tapas, receive boon. Why should a thinking modern person take it seriously?
    Read the verse again with Prabhupāda. He is not selling vending-machine spirituality — he is dismantling it. He explicitly says: if you want Kṛṣṇa as a son in this material world, yes, that costs twelve thousand years of tapas. But if you want to go back to Him, you need only love Him, and Caitanya Mahāprabhu has made even that available through the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra. The story is not a prescription for transactional religion; it is a measuring stick that shows how absurdly cheap our path actually is. The 'mythology' is doing the opposite of what the objector thinks — it is exposing how unwilling we are to pay even the small price of chanting attentively.
  • Prabhupāda says we should not bring Kṛṣṇa here 'for some material purpose, to have a son or whatever else.' But Devakī did exactly that and is celebrated for it. Isn't this a contradiction — we're praising her and being warned against being like her?
    It is not a contradiction; it is the Gauḍīya pivot itself. Devakī's desire was material in form but personalist in substance — she wanted Kṛṣṇa Himself, not wealth or power. Even her 'lower' prayer was infinitely closer to bhakti than the impersonal liberation many seekers chase. Prabhupāda is honoring her while also showing us a higher option that her own son later opened up: instead of asking the Lord to come into our temporary family, ask to be brought into His eternal one. Same Person, deeper relationship. That is svarūpa-siddhi, and it is the destination Caitanya Mahāprabhu came to give us directly.
Analogy

A great patron, hearing of a generation of artisans who have labored for years to produce a single masterwork in his honor, comes personally to their workshop and says, 'Ask anything.' Their answer is not 'wealth' or 'fame' but 'a son like yourself, who will live with us.' Bhagavān is the patron; Pṛśni and Sutapā are the artisans; their request — sutaḥ mādṛśaḥ — is the perfect mistake.

Stories
The gopīs' Kātyāyanī-vrata

The Vraja-gopīs (SB 10.22) undertook the Kātyāyanī-vrata to obtain Kṛṣṇa as husband. Bhagavān fulfilled their request by ultimately giving them Himself in mādhurya-rasa. The principle is the same: prolonged worship of Kṛṣṇa with a specific relational request results in His granting exactly that relationship.

Pṛśni and Sutapā asked for vātsalya — a son like Me; the gopīs asked for mādhurya — a beloved who is Me. Each received what they asked. Bhakti's reciprocity is exact.

Yaśodā and Nanda's prior tapas

Caitanya-caritāmṛta and various Purāṇic accounts indicate that Yaśodā and Nanda are eternal vātsalya-rasa associates of Kṛṣṇa, and that Droṇa and Dharā (their pre-incarnations) underwent austerities to receive Him as their son. The Vraja-vātsalya is the more intimate; the Mathurā-vātsalya here is its preface.

Bhagavān has two sets of parents in Canto 10 — Devakī-Vasudeva (aiśvarya-mixed vātsalya) and Yaśodā-Nanda (pure vātsalya). Both pairs earned their place. The Bhāgavatam is naming Devakī-Vasudeva's earning here.
