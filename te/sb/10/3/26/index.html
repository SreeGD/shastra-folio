Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.26

యోఽయం కాలస్తస్య తేఽవ్యక్తబన్ధో చేష్టామాహుశ్చేష్టతే యేన విశ్వమ్ । నిమేషాదిర్వత్సరాన్తో మహీయాం- స్తం త్వేశానం క్షేమధామ ప్రపద్యే ॥ ౨౬ ॥

O inaugurator of the material energy, this wonderful creation works under the control of powerful time, which is divided into seconds, minutes, hours and years. This element of time, which extends for many millions of years, is but another form of Lord Viṣṇu. For Your pastimes, You act as the controller of time, but You are the reservoir of all good fortune. Let me offer my full surrender unto Your Lordship.
Purport

As stated in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.52) : yac-cakṣur eṣa savitā sakala-grahāṇāṁ rājā samasta-sura-mūrtir aśeṣa-tejāḥ yasyājñayā bhramati saṁbhṛta-kāla-cakro govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi “The sun is the king of all planetary systems and has unlimited potency in heat and light. I worship Govinda, the primeval Lord, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, under whose control even the sun, which is considered to be the eye of the Lord, rotates within the fixed orbit of eternal time.” Although we see the cosmic manifestation as gigantic and wonderful, it is within the limitations of kāla, the time factor. This time factor is also controlled by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, as confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā ( mayādhyakṣeṇa prakṛtiḥ sūyate sa-carācaram ). Prakṛti, the cosmic manifestation, is under the control of time. Indeed, everything is under the control of time, and time is controlled by the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore the Supreme Lord has no fear of the onslaughts of time. Time is estimated according to the movements of the sun ( savitā ). Every minute, every second, every day, every night, every month and every year of time can be calculated according to the sun’s movements. But the sun is not independent, for it is under time’s control. Bhramati saṁbhṛta-kāla-cakraḥ: the sun moves within the kāla-cakra, the orbit of time. The sun is under the control of time, and time is controlled by the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore the Lord has no fear of time. The Lord is addressed here as avyakta-bandhu, or the inaugurator of the movements of the entire cosmic manifestation. Sometimes the cosmic manifestation is compared to a potter’s wheel. When a potter’s wheel is spinning, who has set it in motion? It is the potter, of course, although sometimes we can see only the motion of the wheel and cannot see the potter himself. Therefore the Lord, who is behind the motion of the cosmos, is called avyakta-bandhu. Everything is within the limits of time, but time moves under the direction of the Lord, who is therefore not within time’s limit.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O inaugurator of the material energy, this wonderful creation works under the control of powerful time, which is divided into seconds, minutes, hours and years. This element of time, which extends for many millions of years, is but another form of Lord Viṣṇu. For Your pastimes, You act as the controller of time, but You are the reservoir of all good fortune. Let me offer my full surrender unto Your Lordship.
Commentary

In the previous verse it appears that time is independent. Thus this verse speaks of time’s origin. It speaks of the cause of time-- who does not fear fearful time? That time is your kriya sakti or cesta. Therefore the Lord is addressed as the instigator of prakriti (avyakta (prakrti) bandho). By the influence of time, the universe runs. What is time? Time is nimesa or moments amounting to years, which again multiply into a day of brahma (dvi prarardha). I surrender to you (tva). Since you are fearless, you can make me fearless.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

After the influence of time was mentioned, the independence of time is refuted by Devakī. She rejects fear of time and surrenders to the Lord’s lotus feet out of fear of Kaṁsa. Time, which starts with second and ends with years, which rotate to make the life time of Brahmā (mahīyān), and by which the universe moves, is said to be the movement of you (te) who are of that fearless nature (tasya). Therefore I surrender to you, the Supreme Lord who can destroy the fear of the surrendered, who are the place of fearlessness, and bestower of happiness (kṣema-dhāma). Or, you are the shelter of all auspicious objects.

Personal Guidance
Time devours every cosmos but bows to Kṛṣṇa — take shelter of the kṣema-dhāma, the reservoir of all good fortune, today.
Practical Applications
  • Notice each time anxiety about the future grips you, and inwardly surrender that moment to Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of kāla.
  • Spend the first five minutes of your day acknowledging that this day's hours are not yours to command, and offer them to Him.
  • Refuse to treat any deadline, deterioration, or delay as ultimate — trace it back to time, and trace time back to its Controller.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still pretending to be the controller of time — scheduling, planning, gripping outcomes — when this verse names Kṛṣṇa Himself as the One who moves the cosmic wheel?
  • When the kāla-cakra grinds against me through aging, missed chances, or sudden loss, do I run toward Him as kṣema-dhāma, the reservoir of all good fortune, or do I run toward distractions that promise to stop the wheel?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam or deadline, write at the top of the page: 'I am not the potter, I am the wheel — Kṛṣṇa is avyakta-bandhu.' Then study with full effort (udyama), but watch how the panic about results loosens.
  • Audit one week of your social media use against kāla. Every minute is literally a form of Viṣṇu passing through your hand; would you scroll for two hours straight if you could see the Lord in those minutes? Cut one specific app or one specific hour and offer the recovered time as sādhana.
  • When a friend group, a relationship, or a peer ranking makes you feel like your worth depends on it lasting forever, recall that nimeṣa to vatsara — second to year — all dissolve under kāla. Practice one act of vairāgya today: do the right thing in that group even if it costs you status, because status is on a clock anyway.
householder
  • In your next major family or career decision — a job change, a school for the kids, a financial commitment — name out loud, before deciding, that you are not the controller of the outcome. Do the planning fully (dakṣya), then formally offer the decision and its fruits to Kṛṣṇa before acting.
  • Set up a household practice that honours kāla as the Lord's form: a fixed morning sādhana time and a fixed evening offering of the day, both unmovable for work. The point is not the duration; it is treating time itself as belonging to Him, not to your calendar.
  • When a child, spouse, or parent disappoints you and you feel the urge to tighten control, remember Devakī in the prison cell — she did not control Kaṁsa, she surrendered to kṣema-dhāma. Practice kṣamā in one specific recurring family conflict this week: hold your ground on dharma, release your grip on outcome.
senior
  • Take stock of what you are still gripping — a property, a title, a grudge, a child's life choice — and name it as a thing you have been pretending to be the avyakta-bandhu of. Choose one and consciously hand it over to Kṛṣṇa this week, in word and in deed.
  • Mortality is not an interruption of your life; it is kāla revealing its identity as Viṣṇu. Spend ten minutes daily reading or chanting with the explicit awareness that the time remaining is a personal gift from Him, not a dwindling resource. Notice how this changes what you choose to do with the afternoon.
  • Mentor one younger person — a grandchild, a junior colleague, a neighbour — not by giving them your accumulated opinions, but by pointing them past you to Kṛṣṇa as kṣema-dhāma. Adambhitva matters here: the goal is that they remember Him, not that they remember how wise you were.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating time-consciousness as mere productivity discipline — squeezing more output from each minute — instead of letting kāla humble the false sense of doership and drive surrender to the avyakta-bandhu behind the wheel.
  • Slipping into impersonal awe of 'cosmic time' or 'the universe' as if time itself were ultimate, forgetting that kāla is only another form of Lord Viṣṇu and that surrender is to Him personally, not to an abstraction.
  • Using the inevitability of time as an excuse for fatalism or inaction — 'everything is in His hands anyway' — rather than performing one's sva-dharma with vairāgya, knowing the Lord directs time but still calls us to right effort (udyama).
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī addresses Kṛṣṇa as avyakta-bandhu — the unseen one behind the wheel of time. Share with your partner one area of your life where you have been operating as if you were the potter, not the wheel. Where does the other person see this self-deception more clearly than you do? Push back on each other; do not let either of you off easy.
  • The verse says time is 'another form of Lord Viṣṇu.' For some, this is comforting; for others, it is terrifying — time is not a neutral resource but a personal force already accounting for me. Which response is yours, honestly, and what does the other response in the room see that yours misses?
  • Devakī surrenders to Kṛṣṇa as 'kṣema-dhāma' — the reservoir of all good fortune — at the very moment her seven previous children have been killed by Kaṁsa and this eighth one is in mortal danger. Is calling Him 'kṣema-dhāma' there an act of denial, a forced piety, or the deepest realism? Defend your view against someone who reads it the opposite way.
Role Play

A senior software engineer, Ravi, has just been passed over for promotion for the third year running, while a younger colleague was promoted past him. He sits down with a Gauḍīya friend the same evening, furious — 'I have given fifteen years to this company. Time is running out. I am being erased.' One student plays Ravi; another plays the friend. The friend must use the substance of SB 10.3.26 — that kāla is not Ravi's enemy but a form of the Lord, that Ravi is not the avyakta-bandhu behind his career, that surrender to Kṛṣṇa as kṣema-dhāma is the actual response — without quoting the verse cleanly, without sounding pious, and without dismissing Ravi's hurt. After five minutes, swap roles. The class then debates: where did the friend slide into platitude, and where did real consolation land?

Objections & Responses
  • Saying 'time is controlled by God' is just a coping mechanism for people who feel powerless. In the real world, time management, planning, and seizing opportunity are what produce results — not surrender.
    The verse does not abolish effort; udyama and dakṣya are listed among its values. Devakī is not a passive woman — she is a mother fighting for her child's life inside a tyrant's prison. What she rejects is the illusion that her planning is the cause. Kṛṣṇa Himself in the Gītā says mayādhyakṣeṇa prakṛtiḥ sūyate — prakṛti works under His supervision. A Gauḍīya does not stop planning; he stops imagining the plan is the potter. The result is not less competence but less anxiety, because the outcome was never his to grip. This is the opposite of coping; coping pretends control still exists. Surrender drops the pretence.
  • If kāla is 'another form of Viṣṇu' and everything is under His control, then Kaṁsa's murders of the first six babies are also under His control. That makes God complicit in evil, and the 'reservoir of all good fortune' line sounds obscene right after six infanticides.
    This is the correct place for the objection — Devakī herself is the one speaking, over the bodies of her own children. Notice she does not say Kṛṣṇa caused Kaṁsa's cruelty; she says Kṛṣṇa is the controller of time, and that time has now brought Him into her womb. Kaṁsa acted by his own free will under tamas; the Lord did not script the asura's hatred, but neither is the asura outside His ultimate sovereignty. The personalist line is precise here: Kṛṣṇa is not an impersonal mechanism that 'allows' evil, He is a person who enters the prison Himself. Kṣema-dhāma is not the claim that nothing bad happens; it is the claim that the final shelter is real and personal, and the proof is that He has just arrived in the cell. The objection assumes a deistic god who should have prevented the suffering from a distance. Devakī is worshipping a God who is right there, in the cell, in time, with her.
