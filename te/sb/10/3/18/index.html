Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.18

య ఆత్మనో ద‍ృశ్యగుణేషు సన్నితి వ్యవస్యతే స్వవ్యతిరేకతోఽబుధ: । వినానువాదం న చ తన్మనీషితం సమ్యగ్ యతస్త్యక్తముపాదదత్ పుమాన్ ॥ ౧౮ ॥

Important Words
  • ātmanaḥ — of the self
    Genitive of ātman. Gauḍīya distinction: ātman here means jīvātmā — the individual soul — to be distinguished from Paramātmā and from Brahman. The fool conflates the body with the jīvātmā; the wise see the jīvātmā as distinct from the body AND as eternally subordinate to Paramātmā.
  • dṛśya-guṇeṣu — in the visible (bodily) qualities
    Dṛśya (visible) + guṇa (quality, attribute). The visible attributes of the body — color, age, strength, beauty. The locative says: he is 'situated AMONG' these features and mistakes his being for them. Sharp diagnostic — modern identity-formation lives entirely in dṛśya-guṇa.
  • abudhaḥ — a fool, rascal
    A- (privative) + budha (one who has buddhi, intelligence). Without intelligence. Strong word; Prabhupāda translates 'rascal' to keep the bite. Bhakti-śāstra does not soften the diagnosis: bodily identification is not a mild error but the foundational stupidity from which all suffering flows.
  • vinā anuvādam — without analytical consideration
    Vinā (without) + anuvāda (reflection, repeated discussion). The fault is not just ignorance but unwillingness to think it through. Modern variant: refusing the inquiry 'what am I, really?' because the answer would unsettle the comfortable identification.
  • tyaktam — rejected
    Past participle of tyaj — 'to abandon, give up'. The śāstra-vādins have already rejected the body-as-self conclusion, yet the fool keeps clinging. The word marks the gap between śāstric availability and personal acceptance — a perennial failure.
  • upādadat — accepts (anyway)
    Present participle of upā-√dā — 'to take up, accept, appropriate'. The fool actively reaffirms what has been refuted. Bondage is not passive ignorance; it is active mis-identification, renewed every moment.
One who considers his visible body, which is a product of the three modes of nature, to be independent of the soul is unaware of the basis of existence, and therefore he is a rascal. Those who are learned have rejected this conclusion because one can understand through full discussion that with no basis in soul, the visible body and senses would be insubstantial. Nonetheless, although his conclusion has been rejected, a foolish person considers it a reality.
Purport

Without the basic principle of soul, the body cannot be produced. So-called scientists have tried in many ways to produce a living body in their chemical laboratories, but no one has been able to do it because unless the spirit soul is present, a body cannot be prepared from material elements. Since scientists are now enamored of theories about the chemical composition of the body, we have challenged many scientists to make even a small egg. The chemicals in eggs can be found very easily. There is a white substance and a yellow substance, covered by a shell, and modern scientists should very easily be able to duplicate all this. But even if they were to prepare such an egg and put it in an incubator, this man-made chemical egg would not produce a chicken. The soul must be added because there is no question of a chemical combination for life. Those who think that life can exist without the soul have therefore been described here as abudhaḥ, foolish rascals. Again, there are those who reject the body, regarding it as insubstantial. They are of the same category of fools. One can neither reject the body nor accept it as substantial. The substance is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and both the body and the soul are energies of the Supreme Godhead, as described by the Lord Himself in Bhagavad-gītā (7.4-5): bhūmir āpo ’nalo vāyuḥ khaṁ mano buddhir eva ca ahaṅkāra itīyaṁ me bhinnā prakṛtir aṣṭadhā apareyam itas tv anyāṁ prakṛtiṁ viddhi me parām jīva-bhūtāṁ mahā-bāho yayedaṁ dhāryate jagat “Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and false ego — all together these eight comprise My separated material energies. But besides this inferior nature, O mighty-armed Arjuna, there is a superior energy of Mine, which consists of all living entities who are struggling with material nature and are sustaining the universe.” The body, therefore, has a relationship with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, just as the soul does. Since both of them are energies of the Lord, neither of them is false, because they come from the reality. One who does not know this secret of life is described as abudhaḥ. According to the Vedic injunctions, aitadātmyam idaṁ sarvam, sarvaṁ khalv idaṁ brahma: everything is the Supreme Brahman. Therefore, both the body and the soul are Brahman, since matter and spirit emanate from Brahman. Not knowing the conclusions of the Vedas, some people accept the material nature as substance, and others accept the spirit soul as substance, but actually Brahman is the substance. Brahman is the cause of all causes. The ingredients and the immediate cause of this manifested material world are Brahman, and we cannot make the ingredients of this world independent of Brahman. Furthermore, since the ingredients and the immediate cause of this material manifestation are Brahman, both of them are truth, satya; there is no validity to the expression brahma satyaṁ jagan mithyā. The world is not false. Jñānīs reject this world, and foolish persons accept this world as reality, and in this way they are both misguided. Although the body is not as important as the soul, we cannot say that it is false. Yet the body is temporary, and only foolish, materialistic persons, who do not have full knowledge of the soul, regard the temporary body as reality and engage in decorating this body. Both of these pitfalls — rejection of the body as false and acceptance of the body as all in all — can be avoided when one is fully situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If we regard this world as false, we fall into the category of asuras, who say that this world is unreal, with no foundation and no God in control ( asatyam apratiṣṭhaṁ te jagad āhur anīśvaram ). As described in the Sixteenth Chapter of Bhagavad-gītā, this is the conclusion of demons.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
One who considers his visible body, which is a product of the three modes of nature, to be independent of the soul is unaware of the basis of existence, and therefore he is a rascal. Those who are learned have rejected his conclusion because one can understand through full discussion that with no basis in soul, the visible body and senses would be insubstantial. Nonetheless, although his conclusion has been rejected, a foolish person considers it a reality.
Commentary

But can the material gunas bestow some sort of happiness? This verse answers. He is unintelligent who thinks that the objects seen such as a woman, garland etc. are enjoyable, because they give misery, lamentation and illusion, since they can only be possessed temporarily. They induce one to repeated birth. The mimasakas conclude that wealth and possessions are the most valuable things and think themselves intelligent. But their arguments are not without rebuttal. They don’t have true wisdom. What the devotee rejects with scorn they accept with eagerness.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“The devatās sometimes appear as a son to some people. Why are you attached to me being your son?” Two verses answer. Other than you, the supreme creator and cause, others cannot be the goal since they are not independent. Or the person who discerns a devatā as supreme, since among them that devatā has attractive qualities directly perceivable (ātmanaḥ dṛṣyaḥ guṇeṣu), without giving regard to you, the root of all forms, (sva-vyatirekataḥ) is a fool since the devatā subject to perception (tad-manīṣitam) is not supreme (samyak), since one cannot discern the truth without mutual discussions (anuvādam), without devotional association. That person accepts what is rejected by the devotee.

Personal Guidance
Body and soul are both real energies of Kṛṣṇa — neither reject the body as illusion nor cling to it as the self; honor both in His service.
Practical Applications
  • Today, when grooming or feeding the body, do it as upkeep of Kṛṣṇa's property — not as servicing 'me,' and not as something to despise.
  • Notice when you slip into 'I am this body' or its mirror 'the body is nothing' — and correct yourself to: both are His energies.
  • Refuse the asura conclusion that the world is unreal or godless; speak and act today as if every atom rests on Kṛṣṇa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I quietly behaving as if my body is independent of the soul — decorating it, fearing for it, identifying with it — even though I would never say so aloud?
  • Which side of the abudhaḥ ditch do I tend to fall into more often: treating the body as all-in-all, or dismissing it as unreal — and what does that pattern reveal about my unwillingness to see Kṛṣṇa as the substance behind both?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the two camps in your feed this week: the gym/skincare/optimization camp that worships the body, and the 'nothing matters, it's all a simulation, vibes only' camp that dismisses it. Name one friend in each camp — and remember the verse calls both abudhaḥ. Don't pick a side; serve the soul in each.
  • Before your next exam, do not pray 'help me get the marks' (body-as-everything) and do not say 'marks don't matter, I'm a soul' (body-as-illusion). Instead: study with full effort because this body and intelligence are Kṛṣṇa's energy, and offer the result to Him. Watch how that changes your anxiety.
  • On social media you curate a 'self' — angles, filters, captions. Sit for ten minutes tonight and ask: which version of me am I defending when someone unfollows? The decorated body, or the soul? If the answer is the body, you have just met the abudhaḥ this verse describes — and you can choose differently tomorrow morning.
householder
  • In family decisions this week — schools, medical care, finances — refuse both extremes: the materialist 'only the body and its security matter' and the bypasser 'it's all māyā, why bother planning.' Plan rigorously, provide thoroughly, and offer the whole effort to Kṛṣṇa as His energy serving His energy.
  • When raising your children, teach the body's care (sleep, food, exercise, study) as sādhana, not as ultimate goal. Tell them once this week, in plain words: 'Your body is real and your soul is real — both belong to Kṛṣṇa, and that is why we take care of both.' Watch what they do with that.
  • In your career, name the one decision you have been postponing by telling yourself 'it's all temporary anyway.' That is jñānī-style escapism wearing spiritual clothes. Make the decision this week with full karma-yoga seriousness — the work is Kṛṣṇa's energy; do it well and offer the fruit.
senior
  • As the body's strength wanes, watch carefully for the tempting drift into 'the body was never real anyway' — the verse warns this is the same foolishness as the young man who thought it was everything. Care for the body as Kṛṣṇa's energy until the last day; do not abandon it philosophically before He withdraws you from it.
  • In mentoring younger family members, do not let your hard-won detachment curdle into dismissiveness about their worldly concerns — their marriages, mortgages, careers. Their world is not false. Counsel them as one who has seen both the body's limits and its sacred function as a vehicle for service.
  • Spend one session this month writing down what you are leaving behind — property, relationships, reputation — and beside each item write: 'energy of Kṛṣṇa, returning to Kṛṣṇa.' Not 'illusion I am escaping' and not 'treasure I am losing.' This is the dhīra's vyatireka, performed in real time as life closes.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking 'the body is not the self' for 'the body is false' — sliding into the māyāvāda position the verse explicitly rejects, and then neglecting bodily duties under a spiritual pretext.
  • Swinging to the opposite extreme: treating the body as substantial in its own right — pouring time, money, and anxiety into its decoration as if it had any standing apart from the soul and Kṛṣṇa.
  • Using 'everything is Brahman' as a flat slogan that erases personal surrender to Kṛṣṇa, instead of seeing that body and soul are both His energies and therefore both call for engagement in His service.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse calls a person abudhaḥ (foolish) for treating the body as independent of the soul. In your circle of family and friends, which side does the culture lean toward more aggressively today: 'the body is everything' (materialist decoration) or 'the body is nothing real' (spiritual bypassing)? Share a concrete example of each from the past month.
  • Prabhupāda insists 'the world is not false' and rejects the slogan brahma satyaṁ jagan mithyā. Where in your own sādhana have you been quietly using the world's unreality as a reason to avoid responsibility — work, family, money, the body's care? Push back honestly on each other: where is renunciation real, and where is it cosplay?
  • The verse hinges on the word vyatireka — distinguishing what is dependent on what. Practice it out loud: pick one thing you treasure (a relationship, a skill, this body) and walk your partner through whether it has independent existence or is an energy of Kṛṣṇa. Where does the argument get hard? Where do you catch yourself talking like an impersonalist?
Role Play

A teacher splits the class into trios. One student plays a college freshman whose roommate has just discovered a popular wellness influencer who teaches that 'the body is just a vessel, an illusion — only awareness is real,' and is now neglecting sleep, nutrition, and class attendance to 'transcend.' A second student plays the freshman's mother, a Sunday-school materialist who insists 'the body is the only thing that's real — get a job, eat protein, forget this nonsense.' The third student plays a Gauḍīya elder visiting campus, who must — in five minutes, without lecturing — show both that they are abudhaḥ in the same way: one rejecting the body as false, the other accepting it as all in all. The elder must land the point that both body and soul are energies of Kṛṣṇa, and offer one concrete next step to each. Rotate roles so every student plays the elder.

Objections & Responses
  • Modern biology has explained life entirely through chemistry and DNA. The 'soul' is an unfalsifiable holdover. Prabhupāda's egg-in-the-incubator challenge is just an argument from ignorance — give science another fifty years.
    Notice what the verse actually does: it does not stop at 'the body needs a soul' — it goes further and says rejecting the body as insubstantial is equally foolish. So the Gauḍīya position is not anti-science; it is anti-incompleteness. Chemistry is real, DNA is real — these are Kṛṣṇa's bhinnā prakṛti, His separated material energy, described in BG 7.4. What chemistry cannot account for is the parā prakṛti, the conscious knower who experiences the chemistry. The challenge is not 'wait and see if you can build life'; it is: in your own first-person experience right now, can you reduce the one who is reading these words to the words themselves? If not, you have already met the jīva. Science describes the inferior energy beautifully. It is silent, by method, about the superior one.
  • But many respected Vedāntists — Śaṅkara's followers — teach exactly brahma satyaṁ jagan mithyā. Who is Prabhupāda to call that conclusion foolish? Isn't this just sectarian Vaiṣṇava polemics?
    It is not sectarian; it is textual. The verse itself, spoken before any Gauḍīya commentary, says samyag yatas tyaktam upādadat pumān — the foolish person picks up again what has already been thoroughly rejected by careful analysis. Kṛṣṇa Himself in BG 7.4–5, which Prabhupāda quotes in the purport, calls both matter and the living entities His prakṛti — His energies. An energy of the Real cannot be unreal; it can only be temporary, dependent, and misunderstood. The Gauḍīya line, following Śrī Caitanya, holds acintya-bhedābheda: the world is simultaneously one with and different from Kṛṣṇa, inconceivably. That preserves the personal Lord, the reality of His energies, and the seriousness of our service in this world. To dismiss the jagat as mithyā is, as the purport warns, to slip toward the asura's view in BG 16.8 — asatyam apratiṣṭhaṁ te jagad āhur anīśvaram. The disagreement is not about respect; it is about what the verse will and will not allow us to say.
Analogy

A man stands before a mirror and shouts at the reflection for being late to dinner — never noticing that the reflection cannot eat. The materialist who identifies with his body is shouting at the reflection: scolding the image, dressing it, polishing it, mourning when it cracks — and never feeding the one who actually stands there. Vasudeva names this folly with exact charge: abudhaḥ, rascal.

Stories
Bharata Mahārāja reborn as a deer

Bharata Mahārāja, in SB 5.7–9, became so attached to a baby deer that at the moment of death his consciousness was on the deer; he took a deer's body in his next birth. The point of the story is that bodily identification — even at one remove, even with a beloved animal — sets the next body.

The folly Vasudeva names here is the same: treating dṛśya-guṇeṣu, the visible bodily features, as one's real identity. Bharata's deer-life is the parable of the principle this verse states philosophically.

Maitreya questioning the four Kumāras

When the four Kumāras — eternal child-sages — appear in SB 3.15, they are not identified by their bodies (which appear as five-year-olds) but by their fixed consciousness in Bhagavān. The Bhāgavata is contrasting them with the materialists Vasudeva indicts: the Kumāras KNOW the body is not the self, and so their bodies remain forever young.

Bodily identification ages the body; ātma-jñāna leaves it untouched. The fool grasps what cannot be held; the wise see through what cannot be the self.
