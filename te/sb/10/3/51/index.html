Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.51

Important Words
  • nanda-vrajam — Nanda's village
    Nanda + vraja (cowherd settlement, encampment). The first naming of Vraja in the canto. Vraja is the holy land — Kṛṣṇa's eternal dhāma. Gauḍīya theology: Vraja is identical with Goloka Vṛndāvana; Kṛṣṇa never leaves it in svarūpa-vichara. The 'transfer' here is to His own true home.
  • śauriḥ — the descendant of Śūra (Vasudeva)
    Vasudeva, son of Śūrasena. The patronymic is used to mark his Yādava lineage. Vasudeva is no anonymous prisoner; he is a Yādava chief, eternal associate, śauri-kula. Sambandha-tattva: kula and parivāra matter.
  • yaśodā-śayane — on the bed of Yaśodā
    Yaśodā (literally 'giver of fame') + śayana (bed) + locative. The site of mādhurya-vātsalya begins here. Yaśodā's name itself — 'she who gives fame' — names her function: she will give Kṛṣṇa the unparalleled fame of being her son.
  • tat-sutām — her (Yaśodā's) daughter
    Tat (her) + sutā (daughter) + accusative. Yoga-māyā in her newborn form. The exchange is a swap, not a theft — Vasudeva places his son, takes the daughter back to Mathurā in his arms.
When Vasudeva reached the house of Nanda Mahārāja, he saw that all the cowherd men were fast asleep. Thus he placed his own son on the bed of Yaśodā, picked up her daughter, an expansion of Yoga-māyā, and then returned to his residence, the prison house of Kaṁsa.
Purport

Vasudeva knew very well that as soon as the daughter was in the prison house of Kaṁsa, Kaṁsa would immediately kill her; but to protect his own child, he had to kill the child of his friend. Nanda Mahārāja was his friend, but out of deep affection and attachment for his own son, he knowingly did this. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura says that one cannot be blamed for protecting one’s own child at the sacrifice of another’s. Furthermore, Vasudeva cannot be accused of callousness, since his actions were impelled by the force of Yoga-māyā.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
When Vasudeva reached the house of Nanda Mahārāja, he saw that all the cowherd men were fast asleep. Thus he placed his own son on the bed of Yaśodā, picked up her daughter, an expansion of Yogamāyā, and then returned to his residence, the prison house of Kaṁsa.
Commentary

Taking Yasoda’s daughter, Vasudeva returned to the prison. From the ordinary point of view it appears unjustified that Vasudeva, who was supposed to be following the highest principles, accepted the murder of Yasoda’s daughter to save the life of his son. But his normal sense of discrimination was covered by the paternal prema for the Lord who had appeared as his son.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Getting close to Nanda’s house, he did not even know the path. This means that the Lord made arrangements. The doors opened by themselves as in the prison. As he approached Yaśodā, he became attracted to the sweetness of the child. Thus the child is called śiśum (young child). Another version has the word sutam. He placed the child in Yaśodā’s bed like a secret treasure (nidhāya-placed, nidhi-treasure). He took the daughter who was māyā, taken in order to fool Kaṁsa. He did this out of affection for the Lord. For his purpose, he accepted the daughter known as māyā. Though he was aware that this was the daughter of his friend, he did not give regard for the fact she would be killed by Kaṁsa. He then returned to his house where Devakī was anxiously waiting. Gṛhān is in the plural because he was repeatedly thinking of that house.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Nanda’s place was generally a place for the cows to live. The doors were not locked. Vasudeva came (upetya) to the house. He arrived there quickily. Or he came carefully by some means (upetya) since Nanda had ordered the place to be surrounded by thorns and trenches so it was hard to enter. He saw that Nanda and others were sleeping naturally. Or though they were accustomed to stay awake, he saw they were sleeping without awareness (prasuptān) because of yogamāyā. Śiśum (instead of sutam) inciates that he had great affection for the humanlike child. He thought of the child as his. Or though Kṛṣṇa was not a baby he had become a baby (śiṣum). This is Śukadeva’s utterance out of devotion. Vasudeva took the daughter carefully or for his advantage (upādāya). Vasudeva had great affection for the Lord and thus this act was performed by māyā. Thus he disregarded the fact that it was wrong that Kaṁsa should kill the daughter of his friend. He then returned to the prison.

Concepts
Maya
Personal Guidance
Vasudeva, moved by Yoga-māyā, places Kṛṣṇa in safety and carries the daughter back to Kaṁsa — surrendered duty under Kṛṣṇa's arrangement is never callousness.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where you hesitate to act because the duty looks harsh — and ask whether Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, not your comfort, is the real measure.
  • Carry out one difficult, unglamorous responsibility before sunrise tomorrow with the steadiness of Vasudeva walking through sleeping Vraja.
  • Refuse the modern impulse to second-guess every protective act for your family as 'selfish' — discharge your sva-dharma as offering to Kṛṣṇa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like Vasudeva, quietly carrying out a duty that looks harsh on the surface but is actually being moved by a deeper providence — and have I had the honesty to recognize Yoga-māyā's hand in it rather than crediting my own cleverness?
  • Whose child (whose trust, whose welfare) am I implicitly placing at risk by protecting what is 'mine,' and what does sva-dharma — not sentimentality — actually ask of me in that situation?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a friend group pressures you to lie for one person at the cost of another (cover stories, group-chat screenshots, exam help), notice that Vasudeva's situation is the opposite — he acts under divine arrangement, not peer arrangement. Ask which 'Kaṁsa' (fear, image, FOMO) is actually directing your hand.
  • Vasudeva walks through guards made unconscious by Yoga-māyā. This week, identify one 'guard' in your own life — a notification, a comparison habit, an opinion you fear — and deliberately walk past it once to do something śraddhā-driven (japa, a call to a parent, an honest apology).
  • Before posting or sharing anything tonight, pause and ask: am I doing this from sva-dharma at my stage (student, learner, friend), or am I performing for an audience? Delete the post if it is the second.
householder
  • Vasudeva acted to protect his child while inside a prison he could not yet leave. Name the 'prison' you are currently inside (mortgage, job, in-law dynamic) and make one decision this week that protects your dependents without pretending the prison is not there — daksya, not denial.
  • Have an honest conversation with your spouse about which family decision you have been postponing because you cannot guarantee the outcome — schooling, an aging parent's care, a career move. Take one concrete step, offering the result to Kṛṣṇa rather than to your own anxiety.
  • Notice where you are doing the right thing publicly while privately attached to looking like a good parent or provider — adambhitva means dropping that second layer. Pick one area this week where you act for the family's actual welfare even if no one will praise you for it.
senior
  • Vasudeva had to hand over what looked like his own child into a larger arrangement he did not control. Identify one attachment — a possession, a role in the family, a lingering resentment — and consciously hand it over this month, in writing if needed.
  • Mentor one younger person (child, grandchild, neighbor) not by giving advice but by telling them, plainly, one situation in which you saw Yoga-māyā at work in your life — where your plans failed and Kṛṣṇa's arrangement appeared. Sthitaprajna is transmitted, not lectured.
  • Begin or deepen a daily practice of remembering that this body, like the bodies before it, is a passage. Use the dhīra-bhāva of BG 2.13 alongside this verse — Vasudeva's steadiness in Kaṁsa's prison is the same steadiness asked of you now toward mortality.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using 'it was Yoga-māyā' or 'it was Kṛṣṇa's arrangement' as a retroactive excuse for ordinary self-interested choices, rather than recognizing it only where the devotee is genuinely a passive instrument as Vasudeva was.
  • Reading this verse as license for situational ethics — concluding that any harm done to another can be justified if it protects one's own — and missing that Vasudeva acted under direct divine orchestration, not personal calculation.
  • Sliding into material compassion for Nanda's daughter (sentimentalizing the bodily exchange) and missing the real teaching: that the actors here are souls under Kṛṣṇa's direction, and the 'loss' is only at the level of the body.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva knowingly delivers Yoga-māyā's form to be killed in his own child's place. Was this a moral act, an act of pure surrender, or both? Where does each person in the group draw the line — and what does that line reveal about their own attachments?
  • Prabhupāda quotes Viśvanātha Cakravartī: one cannot be blamed for protecting one's own child at the sacrifice of another's. Do you actually accept this as nyāya, or does it scrape against modern moral instincts? Defend your position to a partner who disagrees.
  • The verse says Vasudeva acted 'impelled by the force of Yoga-māyā.' Where in your own life have you done something that, in retrospect, you can see was being arranged through you rather than by you — and how do you tell that apart from rationalization after the fact?
Role Play

Two students play Vasudeva and a modern ethicist meeting on the road back to the prison. The ethicist has just learned what Vasudeva is carrying and what he has left in Yaśodā's bed. The ethicist presses: 'You traded one infant's life for another. You deceived your friend Nanda. How is this not the worst kind of partiality dressed up as devotion?' Vasudeva must answer not from cleverness but from his actual situation — fear for his son, friendship with Nanda, the silent operation of Yoga-māyā, and his own helplessness inside Kaṁsa's prison. After three minutes, swap roles. Then the class discusses: what could only be said from inside the situation, not from outside it?

Objections & Responses
  • This story endorses favoring your own family at any cost. It is exactly the tribalism that causes most of the world's suffering — 'protect mine, sacrifice yours.'
    Read the verse carefully — Vasudeva is not acting from ordinary tribal preference. He is inside Kaṁsa's prison, his previous children already murdered, and the child he carries away is Yoga-māyā's expansion, not an ordinary infant who will be 'lost.' Prabhupāda is precise: the action is impelled by Yoga-māyā, not by Vasudeva's calculation. The Gauḍīya teacher does not generalize this into a rule that you may sacrifice anyone for your own. The lesson runs the other way — even sva-dharma toward one's own child is finally surrendered into Kṛṣṇa's larger arrangement. If you want a rule for daily life, take BG 18.66, not this verse stripped from its context.
  • Why does an all-powerful Krishna need this elaborate baby-swap at all? It reads like a folktale, not theology.
    Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are not problem-solving by an engineer; they are līlā, in which the Lord plays inside the constraints He Himself accepts so that His devotees can act, love, and be glorified. Vasudeva and Devakī are not props — their śraddhā, daksya, and steadiness under Kaṁsa's terror are part of what is being revealed. Yoga-māyā arranges the swap so that Nanda and Yaśodā receive Kṛṣṇa as their own son in the mood of pure parental love, untainted by knowledge of His Godhood. Strip out the 'folktale' texture and you lose the very rasa the Bhāgavatam exists to deliver. The personalist tradition does not apologize for the specificity — it insists on it.
Analogy

A father, having walked through monsoon flood with his infant, arrives at the house of a friend and silently lays the child on the friend's wife's bed — and silently takes another child away. The exchange is done in love, not concealment-only; it is an act of saving by entrusting. Bhagavān is given to the family that will love Him most freely, and Yaśodā wakes to a son she did not know she had carried.

Stories
Moses in the bulrushes (parallel motif)

Across traditions, the motif of a child placed by a hidden river with a foster-family awaiting recurs. In our śāstra the motif is concrete: Vasudeva placing Kṛṣṇa on Yaśodā's bed parallels none other narrative more closely than this very moment — the original.

Gauḍīya tradition treats this transfer as the constitutive act of Vraja-līlā: from this moment Yaśodā is functionally Kṛṣṇa's mother. The 'biological' parents (Devakī-Vasudeva) are aiśvarya-mixed vātsalya associates; the 'foster' parents (Yaśodā-Nanda) are mādhurya-leaning vātsalya. The exchange does the work.
