    Important Words
    • vijñāta — aware (of what had actually happened)
      Past participle of vi-√jñā. The first word of the chapter installs Kṛṣṇa's sarvajñatā as the frame for everything that follows. Whatever He does next — grieving, pursuing, conciliating — is done in full knowledge, not as discovery.
    • govindaḥ — Lord Kṛṣṇa, who gives pleasure to the cows and the senses
      Go (cows / senses / earth) + vinda (one who finds / pleases). Gauḍīyas insist on the Vraja-flavored name even in political contexts: the Lord who is dealing with Kuru affairs is the same boy who tends Nanda's cows.
    • kulya - karaṇe — for the performance of family obligations
      Compound: kulya (pertaining to the family) + karaṇa (carrying out). Bhagavān honors kinship duties though He stands above all duty. Gauḍīyas read this as Kṛṣṇa's līlā of consenting to be related — the same consent that makes Yaśodā His mother.
    • saha - rāmaḥ — together with Lord Balarāma
      Compound: saha (with) + rāmaḥ (Balarāma). Throughout this chapter the two brothers move as a pair — Gauḍīya theology reads Balarāma as Kṛṣṇa's first expansion (mūla-saṅkarṣaṇa); their inseparability is doctrinal, not merely affectionate.
    Śrī Bādarāyaṇi said: Although Lord Govinda was fully aware of what had actually occurred, when He heard reports that the Pāṇḍavas and Queen Kuntī had burned to death, He went with Lord Balarāma to the kingdom of the Kurus to fulfill the family obligations expected of Him.
    Purport

    The Lord was quite aware that the Pāṇḍavas had escaped the assassination plot of Duryodhana, although the world heard the false news that the Pāṇḍavas and their mother had perished in the fire.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Śrī Bādarāyaṇi said: Although Lord Govinda was fully aware of what had actually occurred, when He heard reports that the Pāṇḍavas and Queen Kuntī had burned to death, He went with Lord Balarāma to the kingdom of the Kurus to fulfill the family obligations expected of Him.
    Commentary

    The fifth seventh chapter describes the killing of Satrajit, Krsna killng Satadhanva, and receiving the jewel from Akrura. As the jewel was the cause of the death of Satrajit, the lesson taught is that the object which is ordinarily ones protector can also kill one, if one offends Krsna.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Śrīdhara Svāmī summarizes the chapter. After clearing his defamation caused by the death of Śatadhanvā and Akrūra taking the jewel, Kṛṣṇa accepted Akrūra in his mind. Ascertaining the news of Hastināpura by private consultation, Kṛṣṇa with Balarāma went to Hastināpura. Of course, not just clearing his bad name, Kṛṣṇa made himself famous by his actions in this pastime. Kṛṣṇa understood the facts since he is the controller of the senses (govindaḥ). He went because of family obligations, as part of his pastimes. The word rāmaḥ suggests he actually went to please his devotees.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Kṛṣṇa understood the facts since he is the controller of the senses (govindaḥ). He is the antaryāmī of all beings. He went because of family obligations. The word rāmaḥ suggests they actually went to please the devotees. Govinda also means that he protects the cows. This suggests he is affectionate to his devotees. Thus he went for protecting the Pāṇḍavas.

    Personal Guidance
    Analogy

    Kṛṣṇa already knows the Pāṇḍavas survived the lac house, yet He travels to Hastināpura and grieves with the elders — the way a father plays along with a child's hide-and-seek though He saw the hiding from the beginning. Bhagavān's omniscience does not cancel His participation; it perfects it.

    Stories
    Vidura's warning at the lac house

    In the Mahābhārata's Ādi-parva, Vidura sent a tunneller and a coded message to the Pāṇḍavas before Duryodhana's wax palace was set ablaze. Kuntī and her five sons escaped through the earth while another family's bodies were mistaken for theirs.

    Gauḍīyas read Kṛṣṇa's visit in this verse against that backdrop: He knew the rescue, He knew Vidura's hand in it, and still He came to weep with Bhīṣma and Gāndhārī. Lokavat — He behaves like a kinsman because kinship is the rasa He is cultivating.

    Important Words
    • bhīṣmam — Bhīṣma
      Grandsire of the Kurus, one of the twelve mahājanas (SB 6.3.20). Gauḍīyas hold him as a paramount śānta- and dāsya-bhakta whose śara-śayyā discourse at SB 1.9 is the canonical death-meditation on Kṛṣṇa.
    • tulya - duḥkhau — both equally sorrowful
      Compound: tulya (equal) + duḥkhau (sorrowful, dual). Bhagavān matches the grief of His devotees down to the measure. Gauḍīyas read this as the precise mechanism of Kṛṣṇa's bhakta-vātsalya — He does not stand outside their pain, He stands level with it.
    • hā kaṣṭam — alas, how painful
      The lament idiom of human grief, placed in Kṛṣṇa's mouth. Acintya-bhedābheda at the level of language: the Supreme says exactly what a stricken nephew would say, without thereby ceasing to be the Supreme.
    The two Lords met with Bhīṣma, Kṛpa, Vidura, Gāndhārī and Droṇa. Showing sorrow equal to theirs, They cried out, “Alas, how painful this is!”
    Purport

    Śrīla Śrīdhara Svāmī points out that those who were involved in the assassination attempt were of course not at all sorry to hear of the Pāṇḍavas’ death. The persons specifically mentioned here, however — Bhīṣma, Kṛpa, Vidura, Gāndhārī and Droṇa — were actually unhappy to hear of the supposed tragedy.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    The two Lords met with Bhīṣma, Kṛpa, Vidura, Gāndhārī and Droṇa. Showing sorrow equal to theirs, They cried out, "Alas, how painful this is!"
    Commentary

    Though knowing that the Pandavas had escaped through a tunnel, hearing that the Pandavas and Kunti were burned, he went to the Kurus to perform the necessary rites.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Bhīṣma is described with Vidura (sa-viduram) since they had similar desires or were both devotees of Kṛṣṇa. Or that day they by chance were together. Bhīṣma is mentioned first because they met Bhīṣma first, since he was most respected. The others are in the order that they met them on the path. The two, as well as Vidura (who also knew the truth), imitated grief like the others. Gathering with them, they all cried loudly (ha).

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    By showing conviction that the Pāṇḍavas were burned, evil Duryodhana hoped that attempts to find news of the Pāṇḍavas living would cease. The word ca indicates all the persons mentioned and Dhṛtarāṣṭra and others. Even Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma (ca) lamented. Vidura is mentioned with Bhīṣma and Kṛpa because he wanted to comfort them. Or he is mentioned as a secondary figure because he was a devotee in that group. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma are mentioned at the end to show that they showed equal sorrow with Bhīṣma and others. Gathering with them they cried out loudly (hā). The elders such as Bhīṣma are mentioned first. Gāndhārī was superior to Droṇa. Because he came later, Kṛpa is considered inferior.

    Important Words
    • antaram — opportunity, gap, opening
      Literally an interval or breach. Bhāgavata diagnostics: envy waits for the Lord's absence to act. Gauḍīyas read antara-vāda as the structural condition of all conspiracies in Kṛṣṇa-līlā — Kaṁsa, Duryodhana, Śatadhanvā all act 'in the gap.'
    • akrūra - kṛtavarmāṇau — Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā
      Dual compound. Striking pairing: Akrūra is the great bhakta of chapters 38-40, Kṛtavarmā a Yādava warrior. The verse shows even senior devotees can be drawn into anartha when envy over the jewel is unaddressed. Sober warning to sādhakas.
    • śatadhanvānam — to Śatadhanvā
      Literally 'he of a hundred bows.' Brother of Satrājit on some accounts; here the instrument of others' envy. The Bhāgavatam frames him as a tool, not the prime mover — his bows do not save him from Kṛṣṇa's disc.
    • maniḥ — the jewel (Syamantaka)
      From √maṇ. The Syamantaka has been the engine of the previous chapter's saga; Gauḍīyas read it as a token of mundane opulence that, when grasped outside of Kṛṣṇa's sanction, drags every holder into ruin — Prasena, Satrājit, now Śatadhanvā.
    Taking advantage of this opportunity, O King, Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā went to Śatadhanvā and said, “Why not take the Syamantaka jewel?
    Purport

    Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā reasoned that since Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma were absent from Dvārakā, Satrājit could be killed and the jewel stolen. Śrīla Śrīdhara Svāmī mentions that these two must have flattered Śatadhanvā telling him, “You are much braver than we are; so you kill him.”

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Taking advantage of this opportunity, O King, Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā went to Śatadhanvā and said, "Why not take the Syamantaka jewel?
    Commentary

    Taking the opportunity when Krsna and Balarama were not in Dvaraka, Krtavarma and Akrura went to Satadhanva and said, "You can kill Satrajit and take the jewel. Since you are more courageous than us you should kill him."

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    They took advantage of Kṛṣṇa’s absence (etat). A person with bad character cannot take the jewel. Therefore you should take it.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    They took advantage of Kṛṣṇa’s absence (etat). They went to Śatadhanvā because of his wicked nature or to arouse hatred for Satrājit because he gave his daughter who had been promised to him. A person with bad character cannot take the jewel. Therefore you should not take it.

    Important Words
    • sampratiśrutya — having promised
      Gerund of sam-prati-√śru. The technical word for a binding word given. The conspirators frame Satrājit's breaking of his promise as the entire moral case — Gauḍīyas note that they conveniently forget Kṛṣṇa Himself returned the jewel and accepted Satyabhāmā by Satrājit's free choice.
    • vigarhya — contemptuously rejecting
      Gerund of vi-√garh. They claim Satrājit despised them; the Bhāgavata's syntax lets the reader hear it the other way too — that they despised Satrājit for choosing Kṛṣṇa over them. Gauḍīya diagnostic: those who feel slighted by a devotee's preference for Kṛṣṇa often project the slight outward.
    • kanyā - ratnam — jewel-like daughter
      Compound: kanyā (daughter) + ratnam (jewel). Satyabhāmā is herself a ratna; the conspirators reduce her, like the gem, to a piece of property withheld. Reading women and bhaktas as possessions is the underlying anartha the chapter exposes.
    • bhrātaram anviyāt — should follow his brother (in death)
      The chilling euphemism. Anu-√i (to follow after). Gauḍīyas hear in this phrase the structure of all asuric counsel: a violent end framed as natural sequence, killing dressed up as kinship.
    “Satrājit promised his jewellike daughter to us but then gave her to Kṛṣṇa instead, contemptuously neglecting us. So why should Satrājit not follow his brother’s path?”
    Purport

    Since Satrājit’s brother, Prasena, had been violently killed, the implication of “following his brother’s path” is obvious. What we have here is an assassination plot. It is well known that both Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā are exalted, pure devotees of the Supreme Lord, so their unusual behavior requires some explanation. The ācāryas provide it as follows: Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī states that Akrūra, although a first-class, pure devotee of the Lord, was a victim of the anger directed against him by the residents of Gokula because he took Lord Kṛṣṇa away from Vṛndāvana. The gosvāmī further states that Kṛtavarmā had associated with Kaṁsa — both of them being members of the Bhoja dynasty — and thus Kṛtavarmā was now suffering from this undesirable association. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī offers an alternative explanation: Both Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā were furious with Satrājit because he had insulted Lord Kṛṣṇa and spread false rumors about Him in Dvārakā. Under normal circumstances Akrūra and Kṛtavarmā would have been most pleased that Lord Kṛṣṇa married the beautiful Satyabhāmā. Being pure devotees, they could not actually be unhappy about this match, nor could they become jealous rivals of the Lord. Therefore they had an ulterior motive in behaving like His rivals.

    • gaja - sāhvayam — to Hastināpura (named after the elephant)
      Compound: gaja (elephant) + āhvaya (name). Hastina-pura, 'elephant city,' the Kuru capital where Kṛṣṇa was performing kinship rites. Satyabhāmā crosses kingdoms with her father's corpse riding behind her — bhakti's geography in motion.

      Compound: vidita (known) + artha (matter). The verse repeats verse 1's frame: Kṛṣṇa already knows. Yet she tells Him, because telling Bhagavān what He already knows is itself one of the limbs of bhakti — āvedana, surrendering one's affairs by speaking them.

      Past participle of √tap (to heat, burn). The same root as tapas — austerity. Gauḍīyas hear a deep tone: she is undergoing an involuntary tapas of grief that Kṛṣṇa will redirect. Bhakti often begins in heat the devotee did not choose.

    Analogy

    Kṛtavarmā recites the obituary of every previous opponent of Kṛṣṇa — Kaṁsa, his ministers, Jarāsandha — like a man pulling up a long ledger of corpses before declining to add his own name. Even an erring devotee can refuse the next step when he remembers Bhagavān's track record.

    Stories
    Jarāsandha's seventeen failed campaigns

    After Kṛṣṇa killed Kaṁsa, Jarāsandha — Kaṁsa's father-in-law and king of Magadha — attacked Mathurā seventeen times in sequence and was repulsed each time.     Kṛtavarmā cites this history not as theology but as policy — and yet the citation is doctrinally correct. Sometimes a wavering devotee is saved by remembering the wreckage of Kṛṣṇa's enemies before remembering His sweetness.

    Kaṁsa's followers in chapter 44

    When Kṛṣṇa killed Kaṁsa in the wrestling arena (SB 10.44), He simultaneously broke the entire administrative apparatus — Kaṁsa's eight brothers fell to Balarāma in the same hour. The Bhāgavatam's pattern: an offense against Kṛṣṇa propagates through every collaborator.

    Kṛtavarmā has read this lesson; tragically, he reads it well enough to refuse Śatadhanvā but not well enough to repent of his own role in the conspiracy. Gauḍīyas read this as a warning to half-bhaktas: the right citation is not yet surrender.

I will accept no one else as my husband, and until He marries me I will remain in the Yamunā, living in a house my father built for me here." After Arjuna reported all this to Kṛṣṇa, the omniscient Lord took Kālindī onto His chariot, and then the three of them returned to Yudhiṣṭhira's residence. Later the Pāṇḍavas requested Kṛṣṇa to build them a city, and He did so by having Viśvakarmā, the architect of the demigods, construct one that was extremely attractive. The Lord satisfied His beloved devotees by remaining with them there for some time. Then, to please Agni, the fire-god, Kṛṣṇa arranged to offer him the Khāṇḍava forest. The Lord asked Arjuna to burn down the forest and accompanied him as his charioteer. Agni was so satisfied with the offering that he presented Arjuna with the Gāṇḍīva bow, a team of horses, a chariot, two inexhaustible quivers, and armor. While the Khāṇḍava forest burned, Arjuna saved a demon named Maya from the blaze. Maya Dānava reciprocated by building Arjuna a splendid palace. In this building Duryodhana would later get a good drenching after mistaking the surface of a pond for a solid floor, thus embarrassing himself. Next Lord Kṛṣṇa took permission from Arjuna and His other relatives and went back to Dvārakā with His entourage. There He married Kālindī. Some time later He went to Avantīpura, where, in the presence of manv kings, He abducted the King of Avantī's sister, Mitravindā, who was very much attracted to Him. In the kingdom of Ayodhyā lived a devout king named Nagnajit. He had an extraordinarily beautiful, marriageable daughter named Satyā, or Nāgnajitī. The girl's relatives had laid down the stipulation that any man who could subdue a certain group of seven ferocious bulls would win her hand. When Kṛṣṇa heard about this princess, He went to Ayodhyā with a large contingent of soldiers. King Nagnajit greeted Him hospitably and joyfully worshiped Him with various offerings. When Satyā saw Kṛṣṇa, she immediately desired Him as her husband, and King Nagnajit, understanding his daughter's intentions, informed Lord Kṛṣṇa of his own wish that the Lord and his daughter be married. The King affectionately told the Lord, "You alone would be a suitable husband for my daughter, and if You subdue the seven bulls You may certainly marry her." Lord Kṛṣṇa then manifested Himself in seven separate forms and subdued the seven bulls. King Nagnajit duly presented his daughter to the Lord, together with a dowry of abundant gifts, and the Lord took Satyā onto His chariot for the journey back to Dvārakā. Just then the rival kings who had been defeated by the bulls tried to attack Lord Kṛṣṇa. But Arjuna easily beat them back, and Kṛṣṇa proceeded with Nāgnajitī to Dvārakā. Subsequently Śrī Kṛṣṇa married Bhadrā after abducting her from her svayaṁ-vara ceremony, and He also married Lakṣmaṇā, the royal daughter of the King of Madra.

    The chapter ends with phala-śruti — whoever recites, hears, or remembers this narration of Bhagavān's vīrya has his infamy and sins driven away. Gauḍīyas treat phala-śruti not as bribe but as the Bhāgavata's truth-claim: katha about Kṛṣṇa is itself purifying contact, the same as darśana of His form.
