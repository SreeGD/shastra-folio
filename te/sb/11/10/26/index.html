Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.10.26

తావత్ స మోదతే స్వర్గే యావత్ పుణ్యం సమాప్యతే । క్షీణపుణ్య: పతత్యర్వాగనిచ్ఛన్ కాలచాలిత: ॥ ౨౬ ॥
tāvat sa modate svarge
[taavat sa modate svarge]
yāvat puṇyaṁ samāpyate
[yaavat punyam samaapyate]
kṣīṇa-puṇyaḥ pataty arvāg
[ksheena-punyah pataty arvaag]

Until his pious results are used up, the performer of sacrifice enjoys life in the heavenly planets. When the pious results are exhausted, however, he falls down from the pleasure gardens of heaven, being moved against his desire by the force of eternal time.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Until his pious results are used up, he enjoys life in Svarga. When the pious results are exhausted, however, he falls from Svarga against his will, by the force of time. He is made to fall (cālitaḥ) by time.
Personal Guidance
Heavenly pleasures last only as long as their puṇya — when the credit is spent, kāla itself drags you back down against your will.
Practical Applications
  • Audit one pleasure you are currently "spending puṇya" to enjoy, and ask whether you would still chase it if the bill came due tomorrow.
  • Redirect one piece of devotional effort today from a heavenly-style reward (comfort, prestige, ease) toward direct service of Kṛṣṇa, which yields no fall-down.
  • When you feel entitled to enjoy a result, remember the falling sacrificer and offer the fruit upward instead of consuming it.
Reflection Questions
  • Which "heaven" am I quietly saving up puṇya to enter, and what will my fall from it look like when the balance runs out?
  • Where in my life am I being moved anicchan — against my own desire — by kāla, and what does that reveal about the shelter I have actually chosen?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice that the "high" of exam success, a relationship, or a viral post behaves exactly like svarga — intense, time-bound, and followed by a drop; plan for the drop now.
  • Refuse the story that your current youth, energy, and freedom are permanent capital; treat them as puṇya that is actively being spent and invest some of it in sādhana while it lasts.
  • When peers chase a lifestyle that clearly runs on borrowed merit (money, looks, popularity), speak honestly with at least one friend about what happens when that merit runs out.
householder
  • Look at your career trajectory and family comforts and ask which are sustained by ongoing dharma and which are coasting on past puṇya about to expire.
  • Build one family practice — a meal offered, an evening kīrtana, a verse read with the children — that does not depend on the household's current prosperity continuing.
  • When making a major financial or career decision, refuse to evaluate it only by the "heaven" it buys; weigh whether it strengthens or weakens your shelter in Kṛṣṇa.
senior
  • Take inventory of the comforts of this stage — respect, savings, settled home — and consciously hold them as kṣīṇa-puṇya in progress, not as a final achievement.
  • Mentor someone younger by being candid about a "heaven" you once entered and fell from, so they can recognize the pattern earlier than you did.
  • Loosen grip on one attachment this month — a possession, a role, an expectation of being served — as rehearsal for the kāla-cālita departure that is certainly coming.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as a warning about a far-off afterlife and missing that every temporary high in this life follows the same exhaust-and-fall pattern.
  • Concluding that puṇya is worthless, and so neglecting duty and dharma, rather than seeing that puṇya is real but insufficient.
  • Imagining that careful management of one's pious credit can postpone the fall indefinitely, instead of taking shelter beyond the karma economy altogether.
Discussion Prompts
  • Name a "heavenly garden" in modern life — a status, lifestyle, or relationship — that people enter on accumulated merit and fall from when it runs out. Do you agree it follows this verse's pattern?
  • If the sacrificer falls anicchan, against his desire, how much real freedom did he ever have in heaven? Discuss where your own freedom is actually located.
  • Is it dishonest to do good works while knowing puṇya alone will eventually exhaust? How do you keep performing duty without secretly banking on its fruits?
Role Play

One student plays a hard-working professional who has just retired into a long-dreamed-of comfortable life — house, savings, respect — and is beginning to sense the "garden" thinning. The other plays a fellow devotee visiting for tea. Without lecturing, the visitor must help the retiree see the kṣīṇa-puṇya pattern in their situation and point toward a shelter that does not exhaust.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like a cosmic accounting system — do good, earn points, spend them in heaven, fall. Isn't that a crude, transactional view of spiritual life?
    The verse is precisely exposing that the karma-puṇya system is transactional, and that is its limit. Kṛṣṇa is not denying the accounting; He is showing that anything bought with puṇya must also be spent, and inviting us beyond the ledger into bhakti, where the relationship is personal, not transactional.
  • I don't believe in heavenly planets, so this verse has nothing to say to me.
    Set the cosmology aside for a moment and look at the structure: any enjoyment funded by accumulated credit — savings, youth, reputation, health — lasts only until that credit is used up, and then kāla moves you whether you consent or not. The verse is describing a law you are already living under; the heavenly planets are simply its clearest case.
