Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.13

యద్ ఘ్రాణభక్షో విహిత: సురాయా- స్తథా పశోరాలభనం న హింసా । ఏవం వ్యవాయ: ప్రజయా న రత్యా ఇమం విశుద్ధం న విదు: స్వధర్మమ్ ॥ ౧౩ ॥

According to the Vedic injunctions, when wine is offered in sacrificial ceremonies it is later to be consumed by smelling, and not by drinking. Similarly, the sacrificial offering of animals is permitted, but there is no provision for wide-scale animal slaughter. Religious sex life is also permitted, but only in marriage for begetting children, and not for sensuous exploitation of the body. Unfortunately, however, the less intelligent materialists cannot understand that their duties in life should be performed purely on the spiritual platform.
Purport

Madhvācārya has given the following statement in regard to animal sacrifice:

yajṣeṣv ālabhanaṁ proktaṁ devatoddeśataḥ paśoḥ himsā nāma tad-anyatra tasmāt tāṁ nācared budhaḥ

yato yajṣe mṛtā ūrdhvaṁ yānti deve ca paitṛke ato lābhād ālabhanaṁ svargasya na tu māraṇam

According to this statement, the Vedas sometimes prescribe animal sacrifice in ritual performances for the satisfaction of the Supreme Lord or a particular demigod. If, however, one whimsically slaughters animals without rigidly following the Vedic prescriptions, such killing is actual violence and should not be accepted by any intelligent person. If the animal sacrifice is perfectly performed, the sacrificed animal immediately goes to the heavenly planets of the demigods and the forefathers. Therefore such a sacrifice is not for killing animals but for demonstrating the potency of Vedic mantras, by the power of which the sacrificed creature is immediately promoted to a higher situation.

Caitanya Mahāprabhu, however, has forbidden such animal sacrifice in this age because there are no qualified brāhmaṇas to chant the mantras, and the so-called sacrificial arena becomes an ordinary butcher shop. And in an earlier era, when unscrupulous persons tried to establish that animal killing and meat-eating are acceptable by misinterpreting the Vedic sacrifices, Lord Buddha personally appeared and rejected their heinous proposition. This is described by Jayadeva Gosvāmī:

nindasi yajṣa-vidher ahaha śruti-jātaṁ sadaya-hṛdaya darśita-paśu-ghātam keśava dhṛta-buddha-śarīra jaya jagad-īśa hare

Unfortunately, the conditioned souls are afflicted by four imperfections, one of which is the cheating propensity, and thus they tend to exploit the concessions that the Lord mercifully gives to them in religious scriptures for their gradual purification. Rather than follow the Vedic injunctions for simultaneously satisfying their senses and gradually elevating themselves, the conditioned souls reject the actual purpose of such apparently materialistic ceremonies and simply become more and more degraded in the ignorance of the bodily concept of life. Thus they fall down altogether from the varṇāśrama system and, taking birth in violent non-Vedic societies, foolishly presume the small fragments of universal religious principles prevalent there to be the exclusive religion of the soul. As a result, they fall into fanaticism, embracing merely sectarian, dogmatic views of religion. Such unfortunate persons are completely out of touch with their own eternal function in life and consider things to be vastly different than they are in reality.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Liquor is to be consumed by smelling, and animals’ limbs are cut during sacrifice but they are not killed. Sex is permitted for producing children, not for enjoyment. The people do not know this purification of their duties. Permission for sex, meat and liquor is not for full indulgence, but rather the opposite. Smelling liquor is considered drinking it. One does not drink as much as one can. Sacrifice of an animal means to cut a limb, not to kill it. Sex is for producing children, not for enjoyment.
Personal Guidance
Vedic concessions for wine, meat, and sex are not licenses for enjoyment but disciplined ramps toward purification — performed on the spiritual platform, not as cover for sense gratification.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one scriptural concession you have quietly stretched into a license, and reset it today to its original, restrictive intent.
  • Before any act of eating, intimacy, or consumption, ask whether it is being performed as sva-dharma on the spiritual platform or as exploitation of the body.
  • Refuse to use the language of religion to dress up sense gratification — name plainly what is sādhana and what is viṣaya.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life have I treated a Vedic or guru-given concession as permission for indulgence rather than as a leash gradually pulling me toward purity?
  • What would it look like for my eating, sexuality, and consumption to be performed viśuddham — purely on the spiritual platform — beginning this week?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice where you use "everyone does it" or "it's technically allowed" — in drinking, dating apps, late-night scrolling — as a concession you have quietly upgraded into a lifestyle.
  • Before the next party, date, or binge, ask honestly whether this act is forming you into a dhīra or training your senses to demand more next time.
  • Refuse to launder peer-group indulgence in spiritual language; if you would not describe it plainly to your guru or parents, do not describe it spiritually to yourself.
householder
  • Sit with your spouse and re-examine one area — food, intimacy, spending, entertainment — where household "permission" has slowly drifted from regulated sva-dharma into routine sense exploitation, and rewrite the rule together.
  • Model for your children that married life, eating, and earning are performed as offerings on the spiritual platform, not as adult license to enjoy what youth must postpone.
  • When tempted to justify a household indulgence by citing scripture or tradition, test it against the verse's standard: is the act actually conditioned by the limits śāstra attaches, or only by your preference?
senior
  • Use this stage to gradually withdraw from concessions that were once appropriate — rich food, accumulated comforts, social drinking — recognizing that what was a leash earlier is now an unnecessary weight.
  • Mentor younger devotees by openly naming, from your own life, where you mistook a concession for an endorsement, and what it cost in time and clarity.
  • Let your remaining years be visibly viśuddham: let family and community see eating, speaking, and resting performed as sva-dharma on the spiritual platform, not as the entitlements of age.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as endorsement of regulated sense enjoyment, when its thrust is that even the regulations point beyond enjoyment.
  • Using "it's permitted in the śāstra" as a rhetorical shield while ignoring the strict conditions the śāstra attaches.
  • Swinging to fanatical condemnation of householders and ritual life instead of recognizing the graded, purifying logic of varṇāśrama concessions.
Discussion Prompts
  • Where does our community most often confuse a concession with an endorsement, and what does that cost us?
  • How do we distinguish a genuine, purifying regulation of the senses from a sophisticated rationalization of indulgence in our own lives?
  • Caitanya Mahāprabhu forbids animal sacrifice in this age because qualified brāhmaṇas are absent — what comparable concessions today are similarly unsafe to enact, and who decides?
Role Play

A devotee couple tells you they have decided that since śāstra permits sex within marriage, they need not restrict it to begetting children; another devotee, overhearing, declares all household sex life to be animalistic. Stand between them and speak as a Gauḍīya teacher: clarify what this verse actually permits, what it restricts, and how each is missing the viśuddham sva-dharma at its center.

Objections & Responses
  • This verse sounds like outdated puritanism — restricting sex to procreation and reducing wine and meat to ritual symbolism ignores adult freedom and modern science.
    The verse is not puritanical but diagnostic: it shows that even the Vedas' apparent permissions are tightly hedged, because the conditioned soul will twist any concession into bondage. Freedom, in Gauḍīya understanding, is not the absence of restraint but the soul's release from compulsive sense demand; the regulations exist to escort us toward that freedom, not to deny adulthood.
  • If the Vedas themselves prescribe animal sacrifice and ritual wine, isn't it inconsistent for Vaiṣṇavas to now forbid them?
    Caitanya Mahāprabhu's prohibition is not a contradiction of the Vedas but their faithful application in Kali-yuga: the prescriptions assumed qualified brāhmaṇas and mantras whose potency could elevate the sacrificed animal, conditions no longer present. As Madhvācārya notes, killing outside that exact frame is plain hiṁsā, and Lord Buddha himself appeared to stop its misuse — the same internal logic the verse upholds.
