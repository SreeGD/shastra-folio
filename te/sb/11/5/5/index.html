Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.5

విప్రో రాజన్యవైశ్యౌ వా హరే: ప్రాప్తా: పదాన్తికమ్ । శ్రౌతేన జన్మనాథాపి ముహ్యన్త్యామ్నాయవాదిన: ॥ ౫ ॥

On the other hand, brāhmaṇas, members of the royal order and vaiśyas, even after being allowed to approach the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord, Hari, by receiving the second birth of Vedic initiation, can become bewildered and adopt various materialistic philosophies.
Purport

It is said that a little knowledge can be very dangerous. Those who are falsely proud of material social status and who thus neglect to perfect their worship of the Personality of Godhead are condemned in this verse. Muhyanty āmnāya-vādinaḥ: becoming attracted by the sense gratification of high status within the varṇāśrama social system, such persons become more attracted to illusory material philosophy than to the Absolute Truth, which is not material. Within the Vedic system, brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas and vaiśyas, as mentioned in this verse, are all offered initiation into the Gāyatrī mantra and are considered twice-born, or highly civilized men. By studying Vedic literature, chanting Vedic mantras, executing ritualistic ceremonies and worshiping the spiritual master and the Personality of Godhead such persons gradually come near to the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord. If one in such an exalted position becomes proud of his status or fascinated by the heavenly material enjoyment that is undoubtedly available to the followers of the varṇāśrama system, then one so bewildered returns to the illusory material platform of birth and death. Even the highly posted demigods become victims of māyā’s enticement, as described in the first verse of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam: muhyanti yat sūrayaḥ.

In order to justify their illusory desire to enjoy dead matter, such foolish persons condemn themselves by minimizing the necessity of worshiping the Personality of Godhead ( avajānanti ) and falsely try to give equal importance to the karma-kāṇḍa section of the Vedas, which awards heavenly sense gratification in exchange for the performance of prescribed ritualistic ceremonies. Such bogus rationalizers are described in Bhagavad-gītā (2.42):

yām imāṁ puṣpitāṁ vācaṁ pravadanty avipaścitaḥ veda-vāda-ratāḥ pārtha nānyad astīti vādinaḥ

“Men of small knowledge are very much attached to the flowery words of the Vedas, which recommend various fruitive activities for elevation to the heavenly planets, resultant good birth, power and so on. Being desirous of sense gratification and opulent life, they say that there is nothing more than this.”

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s purport to this verse of Bhagavad-gītā gives a clear picture of the offensive class of men described therein. “People in general are not very intelligent, and due to their ignorance they are most attached to the fruitive activities recommended in the karma-kāṇḍa portions of the Vedas. They do not want anything more than sense gratificatory proposals for enjoying life in heaven, where wine and women are available and material opulence is very common. In the Vedas many sacrifices are recommended for elevation to the heavenly planets, especially the jyotiṣṭoma sacrifices. In fact, it is stated that anyone desiring elevation to heavenly planets must perform these sacrifices, and men with a poor fund of knowledge think that this is the whole purpose of Vedic wisdom. It is very difficult for such inexperienced persons to be situated in the determined action of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. As fools are attached to the flowers of poisonous trees without knowing the results of such attractions, similarly, unenlightened men are attracted by such heavenly opulence and the sense enjoyment thereof.

“In the karma-kāṇḍa sections of the Vedas it is said that those who perform the four monthly penances become eligible to drink the soma-rasa beverage to become immortal and happy forever. Even on this earth some are very eager to have soma-rasa to become strong and fit to enjoy sense gratification. Such persons have no faith in liberation from material bondage, and they are very much attached to the pompous ceremonies of Vedic sacrifices. They are generally sensual, and they do not want anything other than the heavenly pleasures of life. It is understood that there are gardens called Nandana-kānana, in which there is good opportunity for association with angelic, beautiful women and having a profuse supply of soma-rasa wine. Such bodily happiness is certainly sensual; therefore there are those who are purely attached to material, temporary happiness, as lords of the material world.”

The significant point in this verse is that such bewildered materialists who have become attracted to the materialistic portions of the Vedas ( muhyanty āmnāya-vādinaḥ ) desire to ignore the supreme proprietorship of the Personality of Godhead, who is the supreme enjoyer ( bhoktāraṁ yajṣa-tapasām ). And at the same time they wish to preserve their exalted status as followers of the Vedic principles. Such duplicitous persons take shelter of materialistic philosophers, such as Jaimini, who deny the existence of God as a tangible principle ( īśvarāsiddheḥ ) and therefore recommend material fruitive activities as the highest knowable truth. Such so-called Vedic philosophers amount to little more than polished atheists and are therefore understood to be anīśvara-vādinaḥ, or those who preach against the supremacy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Although foolish materialistic followers of the varṇāśrama system desire to maintain their exalted status as Āryans, or twice-born men, and at the same time neglect the Personality of Godhead, it is clearly stated in the Bhāgavatam (11.5.3) , sthānād bhraṣṭāḥ patanty adhaḥ: such persons inevitably fall from their position into a degraded condition of life. As confirmed in this verse by the word muhyanti, they fall into the darkness of ignorance. Sometimes such pompous persons even present themselves as gurus. However, Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura has pointed out that they are actually laghu, or flimsy, rather than guru, or heavy with Vedic knowledge. It is one’s ultimate duty to oneself ( svārtha-gati ) and to the Personality of Godhead to give up all materialistic activities characterized by karma and jṣāna and surrender fully at the lotus feet of the Lord. Only the most unfortunate person will consider that there is a higher pleasure than ecstatic surrender at the lotus feet of Gokulānanda, the Personality of Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
On the other hand, brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas and vaiśyas, even after being allowed to study the Vedas by receiving Vedic initiation, become bewildered since they interpret the meaning of the Vedas. This verse describes those who become indifferent to bhakti by obtaining a small amount of jṣāna. Brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas and vaiśyas, having attained qualification to worship the Lord’s feet by study of the Vedas, through receiving the sacred thread (śrautena), become bewildered by the results of karma, since they interpret the meaning of the Vedas, which causes the bewilderment. It is said in the Gītā: yām imāṁ puṣpitāṁ vācaṁ pravadanty avipaścitaḥ | veda-vāda-ratāḥ pārtha nānyad astīti vādinaḥ || The unintelligent people, absorbed in discussing the Vedas, who propound the theory that matter is everything, attractively speak flowery words. BG 2.42

Personal Guidance
Even twice-born initiates fall when pride in varṇa-status or attraction to karma-kāṇḍa fruits eclipses direct worship of Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where you trade on a religious or social credential (initiation, lineage, title) instead of doing the actual sādhana behind it.
  • Audit one ritual or duty you performed this week and ask whether it was offered to Kṛṣṇa personally, or quietly aimed at heavenly comfort, reputation, or self-image.
  • Refuse the inner argument that Vedic study and pious activity can substitute for surrender at the lotus feet of Hari — chant your rounds before any other 'Vedic' achievement today.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my practice have I started preserving status as a 'serious devotee' while quietly drifting into āmnāya-vādī rationalizations that downgrade Kṛṣṇa's supremacy?
  • Which heavenly outcome — health, family success, respect, subtle 'spiritual progress' — am I actually working for when I tell myself I am worshipping Kṛṣṇa?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice when you use your school, college, or family background as a stand-in for actual character or sādhana — and do one concrete thing today (a round of japa, an honest study session) that only counts if no one sees it.
  • Audit your feed: which 'spiritual' or self-improvement content is actually selling you heavenly outcomes (success, looks, status) dressed in Sanskrit or wellness vocabulary?
  • Refuse the trap of becoming the 'religious one' in your friend group as an identity; instead, privately commit to one practice that points to Kṛṣṇa personally, not to your reputation.
householder
  • Notice where decisions about career, money, schooling, or marriage are being optimized for a refined heavenly life — comfort, respectability, good rebirth for the kids — rather than as offerings to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Audit your family's rituals (festivals, pūjās, donations): are they centered on direct worship of Kṛṣṇa, or have they slid into custom and social performance, the exact muhyanty āmnāya-vādinaḥ pattern?
  • Refuse to outsource your bhakti to qualifications — your initiation, your guru's name, your temple attendance — and ensure that today, inside the household, you personally chant, hear, and serve before invoking any of those credentials.
senior
  • Notice which 'twice-born' credentials of a lifetime — degrees, titles, devotional seniority, lineage — you are tempted to lean on now, and consciously set them aside in your daily remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Audit the legacy you are shaping for children and disciples: are you handing on direct attachment to Kṛṣṇa, or merely a refined varṇāśrama respectability they can wear without surrender?
  • Refuse the comfort of being treated as a guru figure if you are not, in your own heart, surrendered; speak openly with younger devotees about your own struggle against pride and fruitive thinking, modelling the very humility this verse demands.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Vedic study, mantra-knowledge, or initiation as the endpoint, when the verse says even these brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas and vaiśyas can still become bewildered.
  • Reading the verse as being about 'other people' — fallen brāhmaṇas elsewhere — instead of recognizing the same pride and fruitive drift in oneself.
  • Concluding that varṇāśrama duties are therefore worthless, when the actual instruction is to keep them subordinate to direct, personal worship of Kṛṣṇa.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one 'twice-born' credential you rely on — academic, professional, devotional, family — and discuss how it could quietly become a substitute for surrender rather than a support for it.
  • Where do you see modern 'āmnāya-vādī' thinking inside devotional communities themselves — language that sounds Vedic but smuggles in materialism or impersonalism?
  • Disagree honestly: is karma-kāṇḍa-style motivation (health, prosperity, good rebirth) a useful on-ramp to bhakti, or is it the very trap this verse is warning about?
Role Play

A senior, well-read disciple has just given a class quoting many Vedic mantras and emphasizing the dignity of brahminical culture, but never mentioned Kṛṣṇa personally or the necessity of surrender. A younger student approaches afterwards, uneasy but unsure why. Two participants play the younger student and the senior speaker; the senior must defend the class, the younger must articulate, from SB 11.5.5, exactly what was missing and why it matters.

Objections & Responses
  • Isn't it elitist and outdated to single out brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas and vaiśyas? Surely sincere worship is what matters, regardless of varṇa.
    The verse is not elevating those classes — it is warning the very people who looked most qualified that their qualification did not save them. Far from elitist, it democratizes the danger: if even initiated twice-born men get bewildered through pride and fruitive thinking, no social or devotional status exempts us. The point is the universal need for direct, personal surrender to Kṛṣṇa.
  • The karma-kāṇḍa is also Veda. Calling its followers 'polished atheists' sounds sectarian — aren't all paths leading to the same truth?
    Prabhupāda's purport, following Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, is precise: thinkers like Jaimini who deny īśvara as a tangible principle while using Vedic vocabulary are anīśvara-vādinaḥ — they retain the form of Veda while removing its Person. A Gauḍīya is not condemning ritual; he is refusing to let ritual be used to deny the supreme enjoyer. 'All paths same' only sounds generous; in practice it lets us keep our preferences and quietly demote Kṛṣṇa.
