Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.6

కర్మాణి కర్మభి: కుర్వన్సనిమిత్తాని దేహభృత్ । తత్తత్కర్మఫలం గృహ్ణన్భ్రమతీహ సుఖేతరమ్ ॥ ౬ ॥

Impelled by deep-rooted material desires, the embodied living entity engages his active sense organs in fruitive activities. He then experiences the results of his material actions by wandering throughout this world in so-called happiness and distress.
Purport

The argument may be given that if a living entity were subject to the results of his previous activities there would be no scope for free will; once having committed a sinful action, the living entity would be bound in an endless chain of suffering, being perpetually subject to previous reactions. According to this speculation there cannot be a just and omniscient God, since the living entity is forced to commit sinful activities by the reactions of his previous activities, which were reactions to still previous activities. Since even an ordinary gentleman will not unfairly punish an innocent person, how could there be a God witnessing the helpless suffering of the conditioned souls within this world?

This foolish argument can easily be refuted by a practical example. If I purchase a ticket for an airline flight, board the plane and commence the flight, once the plane has taken off my decision to board the plane forces me to continue flying until the plane lands. But although I am forced to accept the reaction of this decision, on board the plane I have many new decisions I can make. I may accept the food and drink from the stewardesses or reject it, I may read a magazine or newspaper, I may sleep, walk up and down the aisle, converse with other passengers, and so on. In other words, although the general context — flying to a particular city — is forcibly imposed upon me as a reaction to my previous decision to board the plane, even within that situation I am constantly making new decisions and creating new reactions. For example, if I cause a disturbance on the airplane I may be arrested when the plane lands. On the other hand, if I make friends with a businessman sitting next to me on the plane, such a contact may lead to a favorable business transaction in the future.

Similarly, although the living entity is forced to accept a particular body by the laws of karma, within the human form of life there is always scope for free will and decision-making. Therefore the Supreme Personality of Godhead cannot be considered unjust for holding the living entity in human life responsible for his present activities despite the living entity’s undergoing the reactions of his previous work.

According to Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura the influence of māyā is so strong that even in a hellish condition the proud conditioned soul thinks that he is enjoying life.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The jīva engages his active sense organs in activities accompanied by impressions, and, accepting the karma in the form of enjoyment and suffering, wanders in this world. Then the living entity continues in saṁsāra. Performing actions accompanied by vāsanās or impressions (sa-nimttāni), using the action senses (karmabhiḥ), the jīva attains the results of action in the form of happiness and distress and enjoys, since it is seen that one even enjoys being born in hellish situations.
Personal Guidance
Driven by old desires, the embodied soul keeps acting through the senses and wandering through karma's so-called happiness and distress.
Practical Applications
  • Name one recurring 'happiness' you chase today and notice how it always arrives twinned with its opposite.
  • Before reacting to a craving, pause and ask which previous decision is now flying you toward this destination.
  • Inside today's fixed circumstances, choose one fresh, Kṛṣṇa-conscious decision instead of acting on autopilot.
Reflection Questions
  • Which karmic 'flight' am I already on, and where exactly am I still free to choose differently before it lands?
  • Where am I mistaking a hellish condition for enjoyment because māyā has flattered my pride?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice how the 'happiness' of a streak, a like, or a grade always shows up paired with anxiety about losing it.
  • Inside a friend group or course you cannot easily exit, identify one fresh decision you can still make today as a devotee.
  • Refuse the story that you are simply 'wired this way' — in this human form, free will is the whole point.
householder
  • When a family or career situation feels 'forcibly imposed,' list the decisions still genuinely yours inside it before complaining.
  • Track one week of household 'wins' and notice how each so-called happiness arrived with its shadow of distress.
  • Make at least one decision this week — about money, children, or work — explicitly as an offering to Kṛṣṇa rather than to karma.
senior
  • Review the major 'flights' your earlier choices put you on, and forgive yourself and others for the seats you all ended up in.
  • Loosen your grip on a long-cherished comfort by naming the distress that has always sat quietly beside it.
  • Use the remaining onboard decisions of this human form to mentor someone younger toward Kṛṣṇa rather than toward more wandering.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using karma as fatalism — 'it was destined' — to excuse passivity in the human form, where free will still operates.
  • Imagining that only 'distress' is karma while treating 'happiness' as something one has earned and owns.
  • Blaming Kṛṣṇa or God as unjust for present suffering instead of seeing the structure of one's own prior choices.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a current life situation that feels 'forcibly imposed' — where is the genuine scope for new decisions inside it?
  • Where do you and a fellow student honestly disagree about what counts as karmic 'happiness' versus actual distress?
  • Discuss a time you mistook a hellish condition for enjoyment — what finally broke the spell?
Role Play

One student plays a friend complaining bitterly that life is unfair because 'I was born into this situation, I didn't choose it.' The other student, drawing on the airplane example from the purport, must acknowledge the fixed context while pointing out the real decisions still available right now — without becoming preachy or dismissive.

Objections & Responses
  • If my present body and circumstances are reactions to past lives I cannot remember, how is it fair for Kṛṣṇa to hold me responsible now?
    The verse and purport answer this directly: the past reaction fixes the general flight, not every onboard decision. In the human form, fresh choices are constantly available, and Kṛṣṇa holds you responsible only for these present choices, not for the seat you were assigned.
  • This sounds like a grim worldview — wandering in 'so-called happiness and distress' forever. Isn't life meant to be enjoyed?
    The verse is not denying enjoyment; it is naming what material enjoyment actually is — a wandering paired with its opposite. Real ānanda is not on this circuit at all; it begins when one uses the human form's free will to turn toward Kṛṣṇa instead of toward another lap of sukha-itara.
