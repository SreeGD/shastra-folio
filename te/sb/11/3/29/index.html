Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.29

ఏవం కృష్ణాత్మనాథేషు మనుష్యేషు చ సౌహృదమ్ । పరిచర్యాం చోభయత్ర మహత్సు నృషు సాధుషు ॥ ౨౯ ॥

One who desires his ultimate self-interest should cultivate friendship with those persons who have accepted Kṛṣṇa as the Lord of their life. One should further develop an attitude of service toward all living beings. One should especially try to help those in the human form of life and, among them, especially those who accept the principles of religious behavior. Among religious persons, one should especially render service to the pure devotees of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
Purport

According to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, the highest duty for devotees of the Supreme Lord is to establish friendship with those who have completely surrendered to Kṛṣṇa and who have thus achieved śaraṇāgati, shelter at the Lord’s lotus feet. One should render service to both the Lord and His devotees, since the Lord is more pleased by sincere service rendered to His pure devotees. One should not only officially offer respect to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, His devotees and His worshipful paraphernalia, but should actually render service to the representatives of the Lord, who are known as mahā-bhāgavatas.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
One should learn to have friendship with people who have devoted their lives to Kṛṣṇa and to serve Kṛṣṇa, the devotees, those of higher status and those of equal status in the proper manner. One should learn to have affection for humans whose very life is Kṛṣṇa. One should learn to serve Kṛṣṇa and his devotees. One should learn to serve persons worthy of respect and persons who are equal according to their status.

Personal Guidance
Your true self-interest: befriend those who have taken Kṛṣṇa as their life's Lord, serve all beings, and above all serve the mahā-bhāgavatas.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one person in your sphere who has actually accepted Kṛṣṇa as the Lord of their life, and deliberately deepen friendship with them this week.
  • Render one concrete act of service today to a pure devotee — clean their room, cook for them, help with their seva — not just offer formal respect.
  • Widen your circle of sauhṛdam: treat every living being you meet today, human and otherwise, as a candidate for some small service rather than as a means to your own use.
Reflection Questions
  • Whose friendships am I actually investing in — and do those people draw me closer to Kṛṣṇa or further from Him?
  • Where am I substituting outward respect to devotees for the actual paricaryā this verse asks of me?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your closest five friends and your social media follows — which of them treat Kṛṣṇa as the Lord of their life, and which are quietly pulling you elsewhere?
  • Pick one older devotee in your community this term — a senior at college, a temple devotee, a teacher — and offer concrete paricaryā: rides, errands, help with their projects, not just polite namaskāras.
  • In your friend group, refuse the modern script of using people; practice small acts of service — carrying a bag, covering a shift, helping with notes — as your default mode of relating.
householder
  • Decide as a family who the mahā-bhāgavatas in your life are, and budget time, money, and hospitality for their paricaryā the way you budget for vacations or upgrades.
  • Teach your children sauhṛdam by example: when a devotee visits, involve the kids in cooking, cleaning their room, and serving them — not as a chore but as the household's highest privilege.
  • Examine your professional and social circles: which relationships are you cultivating for advancement, and where could you instead invest in friendships with surrendered souls who will help your family's spiritual life?
senior
  • Use this stage to render the kind of hands-on paricaryā you were too busy for earlier — sit with elderly Vaiṣṇavas, cook for them, hear from them, write down what they say.
  • Reorganize your legacy giving and your time so that service to pure devotees and devotee institutions, not bodily concerns of relatives, becomes the visible center of your final decades.
  • Mentor younger devotees specifically into sauhṛdam with surrendered souls — introduce them, take them to satsaṅga, model what paricaryā looks like before you can no longer demonstrate it.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating 'service to all living beings' as a vague universal goodwill while quietly avoiding the harder, specific duty of serving Kṛṣṇa's pure devotees.
  • Reducing service to mahā-bhāgavatas to ritual gestures — pranāms, garlands, donations — instead of personal, hands-on paricaryā.
  • Using the verse's hierarchy as grounds for contempt toward non-devotees, rather than as a structure for graded, genuine sauhṛdam toward everyone.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a friendship in your life that has either pulled you toward Kṛṣṇa or away from Him — what made the difference?
  • How do you decide, in practice, who in your community counts as a mahā-bhāgavata worth rendering paricaryā to, without becoming either naïve or cynical?
  • Where does 'service to all living beings' end and sentimental humanitarianism begin — and how does this verse draw that line?
Role Play

A new member of your saṅga asks you over chai: 'I already serve in a soup kitchen every weekend and I'm kind to everyone at work — isn't that enough? Why do I also need to single out devotees for special service?' Respond from this verse — honoring their compassion, but opening the distinction between general sauhṛdam and paricaryā to mahā-bhāgavatas.

Objections & Responses
  • Singling out devotees for special service sounds sectarian — shouldn't a spiritual person treat everyone exactly the same?
    The verse explicitly graduates our response: sauhṛdam toward all, service toward all living beings, special help to humans, more to the religious, and paricaryā especially to pure devotees. This is not sectarianism but realism — a sick person needs different care from a healthy one, and those carrying Kṛṣṇa as the Lord of their lives are the channels through which mercy reaches everyone else. Serving them is not exclusion of others; it is how the whole world is ultimately served.
  • How can I 'serve pure devotees' when I cannot even reliably recognize who is actually pure? Isn't this a setup for being exploited by frauds?
    The verse trusts that as you cultivate friendship with those who have taken śaraṇāgati, your discrimination matures. Start where the symptoms are visible — those whose lives are visibly oriented around Kṛṣṇa, who deepen your taste for chanting and hearing, who are honored by other sādhus. Render small, concrete service there and watch the fruit. Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura is not asking for blind submission; he is asking for actual paricaryā to actual representatives, tested over time.
