Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.55

Thus the worshiper of the Supreme Lord should recognize that the Personality of Godhead is all-pervading and should worship Him through His presence in fire, the sun, water and other elements, in the heart of the guest one receives in one’s home, and also in one’s own heart. In this way the worshiper will very soon achieve liberation.
Purport

Thus end the purports of the humble servants of His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda to the Eleventh Canto, Third Chapter, of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, entitled “Liberation from the Illusory Energy.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Thus the worshiper of the Supreme Lord should recognize that the Lord is all-pervading and should worship him through his presence in fire, the sun, water and other elements, in the heart of the guest one receives in one's home, and also in one's own heart. In this way the worshiper will very soon achieve liberation. Karma-yoga which includes of bhakti and ahaṅgrahopāsanā is better than the previously described karma-yoga since one more quickly attains liberation. However one should understand that pure bhakti is referred to with the words bhāgavatān dharmān in verses such as SB 11.2.34 and SB 11.3.22 Thus ends the commentary on Third Chapter of the Eleventh Canto of the Bhāgavatam for the pleasure of the devotees, in accordance with the previous ācāryas. Chapter Four Avatāras of the Lord 11.4: Drumila Explains the Incarnations of Godhead to King Nimi 4. Drumila Explains the Incarnations of Godhead to King Nimi 11.4 Summary The various forms of the past, present and future incarnations of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Hari, and the various transcendental characteristics of each of these avatāras constitute the subject matter of this chapter. Even if it were ever possible to count all the particles of dust on the face of the earth, any attempt to count all the innumerable transcendental qualities of the unlimited Lord Hari, the foundation of all potencies, would be simply madness. The Personality of Godhead Nārāyaṇa created the universe out of the five elements manufactured by His own māyā, entered into that universe in the form of the Supersoul and became known as the Puruṣa-avatāra. He effects the work of creation in the mode of passion through the personal form of Brahmā, of protection in the mode of goodness through the form of the Lord of sacrifice, Viṣṇu, and of annihilation in the mode of ignorance through the form of Rudra. Incarnating in the form of Nara-Nārāyaṇa, the greatest of sages, from the womb of Mūrti, the daughter of Dakṣa and wife of Dharma, He preached by His own practical demonstration the science of naiṣkarmyam. When Cupid (Kandarpa) and his hosts were sent to Badarikāśrama by King Indra, who had become fearful and envious at seeing the austerities of Lord Nara-Nārāyaṇa, that best of sages welcomed Cupid as an honored guest. The pacified Cupid then offered prayers to the Supreme Personality Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi. By the order of the sage, Cupid returned from there with Urvaśī and upon presenting himself before Indra related to him everything that had taken place. The Personality of Godhead Lord Viṣṇu has appeared in various partial incarnations for the benefit of the entire world and has given instructions in spiritual knowledge in the forms of Haṁsa, Dattātreya, Sanaka and the other Kumāra brothers, and Ṛṣabhadeva. In the form of Hayagrīva He killed the demon Madhu and saved all the Vedas. In the avatāra of Matsya, the fish, He protected both the earth and Satyavrata Manu. In the incarnation of Varāha, the boar, He delivered the earth and destroyed Hiraṇyākṣa; in that of Kūrma, the tortoise, He carried Mandara Mountain upon His back; and in the form of Śrī Hari He gave liberation to the king of the elephants. The Lord delivered the Vālakhilyas, who had been trapped in the water in a cow's hoofprint, He delivered Indra from the reaction for murdering a brāhmaṇa, and He delivered the wives of the demigods from imprisonment in the palaces of the demoniac asuras. In the avatāra of Nṛsiṁha, He killed Hiraṇyakaśipu. In the reign of each Manu He kills the demons, fulfills the needs of the demigods and protects all the planetary systems. In the form of Vāmana, the dwarf brāhmaṇa boy, He cheated Bali Mahārāja; in the form of Paraśurāma He rid the earth of kṣatriyas twenty-one times; and in the form of Śrī Rāma He brought the ocean under His submission and killed Rāvaṇa. Descending into the Yadu dynasty, He removed the burden of the earth. In the form of Buddha, by His argumentative preaching in defiance of the Vedas He bewildered the demons who were unqualified to perform sacrifices, and at the end of Kali-yuga He will destroy the śūdra kings in His form of Kalki. In this way the innumerable appearances and activities of the Supreme Personality of Godhead Lord Hari are described.

Personal Guidance
Worship Kṛṣṇa as the one Lord present in fire, sun, water, the guest at your door, and your own heart — and liberation comes swiftly.
Practical Applications
  • Before your first meal today, pause and offer it inwardly to Kṛṣṇa present in the digestive fire, recognizing Him as the actual enjoyer.
  • Treat the next unannounced guest, delivery worker, or stranger at your door as the Lord Himself standing there — speak and serve accordingly.
  • Spend five minutes at dawn facing the sun and at night near a flame, addressing Kṛṣṇa as the indweller of both, not the elements themselves.
Reflection Questions
  • Which of the Lord's five seats — fire, sun, water, the guest, my own heart — do I most consistently overlook, and what does that reveal about where my worship has narrowed?
  • When I worship 'in my heart,' am I actually addressing Kṛṣṇa as Īśvara seated there, or merely consulting my own preferences dressed up as devotion?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before opening your laptop for study or an exam, offer the lamp of your screen and your concentration to Kṛṣṇa seated in your heart as the real knower.
  • The next time a classmate or roommate interrupts you uninvited, treat them in that moment as the guest the verse names — adjust your tone before you adjust your schedule.
  • When you scroll past sunrise photos or fire-pit videos online, pause once and address Kṛṣṇa as the one present in that sun and that flame, instead of just liking the image.
householder
  • Cook one meal this week with the conscious awareness that the kitchen fire is a seat of Kṛṣṇa, and serve your family from that mood rather than from duty or fatigue.
  • When a guest, in-law, or your child's friend arrives at an inconvenient hour, decide before opening the door that you are receiving Kṛṣṇa, and let that decision shape what you say next.
  • End each workday by offering the day's earnings and decisions to the Lord in your own heart, asking Him — not your anxieties — whether the day was rightly spent.
senior
  • Make your daily contact with water — bathing, drinking, the morning glass — a deliberate worship of Kṛṣṇa present there, since the body's grip on these acts is loosening anyway.
  • Receive visiting children, grandchildren, and old friends as guests in the verse's sense; speak to them as you would to the Lord arriving, knowing such occasions are now numbered.
  • In nightly meditation, locate Kṛṣṇa specifically as Īśvara in your heart rather than as a vague presence, and rehearse handing over the legacy, the regrets, and the remaining years to Him there.
Common Pitfalls
  • Sliding into pantheism — worshiping fire, sun, and water as themselves divine rather than as places where Kṛṣṇa is honored.
  • Reducing 'the Lord in the heart' to one's own conscience or intuition, so that surrender quietly becomes self-approval.
  • Performing the outer rituals toward elements and guests while neglecting the inner worship of Kṛṣṇa in one's own heart, or vice versa.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share an instance where you treated a guest as an inconvenience; what would have changed in that encounter if you had truly seen Kṛṣṇa standing in their place?
  • The verse names five specific locations of worship — does adding the guest and the heart to the elemental list change what 'worship' even means for you, and how?
  • If liberation comes 'very soon' from this practice, why are most of us not liberated — where is the practice actually breaking down in our day?
Role Play

A tired devotee returns home after a long workday wanting only silence; the doorbell rings and an unexpected relative stands there expecting dinner and a bed for the night. One student plays the householder, another the guest; pause the scene three times and have the householder narrate aloud how seeing Kṛṣṇa in the guest, in the cooking fire, and in their own heart reshapes the next words spoken.

Objections & Responses
  • Seeing the Lord in fire, sun, water and people sounds like the very pantheism Gauḍīyas reject — how is this different from saying everything is God?
    The verse does not say the elements are the Lord; it says the Lord, Īśvara, is present within them as the one to be worshiped. Kṛṣṇa remains the supreme Personality; fire and sun are His seats, not His substance, just as a throne is not the king.
  • Liberation 'very soon' from honoring a guest and lighting a lamp seems too cheap — surely real bhakti demands more than this.
    It is not the outer act that liberates but the recognition of Kṛṣṇa as Īśvara in all five locations, sustained until it becomes one's actual vision. That recognition is rare and costly; what looks cheap is in fact the fruit of steady sādhana, and Kṛṣṇa's promise of swift liberation is His own generosity, not a discount.
