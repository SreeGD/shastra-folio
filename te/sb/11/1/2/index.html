Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.1.2

యే కోపితా: సుబహు పాణ్డుసుతా: సపత్నై- ర్దుర్ద్యూతహేలనకచగ్రహణాదిభిస్తాన్ । కృత్వా నిమిత్తమితరేతరత: సమేతాన్ హత్వా నృపాన్ నిరహరత్ క్షితిభారమీశ: ॥ ౨ ॥

Because the sons of Pāṇḍu were enraged by the numerous offenses of their enemies, such as duplicitous gambling, verbal insults, the seizing of Draupadī’s hair, and many other cruel transgressions, the Supreme Lord engaged those Pāṇḍavas as the immediate cause to execute His will. On the pretext of the Battle of Kurukṣetra, Lord Kṛṣṇa arranged for all the kings who were burdening the earth to assemble with their armies on opposite sides of the battlefield, and when the Lord killed them through the agency of war, the earth was relieved of its burden.
Purport

The Pāṇḍavas were repeatedly harassed by their enemies, such as Duryodhana and Duḥśāsana. As innocent young princes, the Pāṇḍavas had no enemy, but Duryodhana was constantly plotting against his helpless cousins. The Pāṇḍavas were sent to a house of lac, which was later burned to the ground. They were administered poison, and their chaste wife Draupadī was publicly insulted when her hair was pulled and an attempt was made to strip her naked. Throughout these dangers, Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa constantly protected the Pāṇḍavas, who were fully surrendered to Him and who knew no shelter other than Him.

In this verse the word itaretarataḥ is significant. Previous to the Battle of Kurukṣetra, Kṛṣṇa had personally killed many demons, including Pūtanā, Keśī, Aghāsura and Kaṁsa. Now, Kṛṣṇa wanted to complete His mission of removing the earth’s burden by killing the remaining impious persons. But as stated here, kṛtvā nimittam: the Lord did not personally kill, but empowered His devotees Arjuna and the other Pāṇḍavas to remove the impious kings. Thus acting personally and through His immediate expansion Balarāma, as well as by empowering His pure devotees such as the Pāṇḍavas, Kṛṣṇa fully displayed the pastimes of the yugāvatāra by reestablishing religious principles and ridding the world of demons. Although the general purpose of the Kurukṣetra battle was to kill the demons, by Kṛṣṇa’s arrangement some great devotees such as Bhīṣma also appeared to be inimical toward the Lord. But as described in the First Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.9.39) by the words hatā gatāḥ svarūpam, many great devotees played with the Lord as enemies and upon being killed by Kṛṣṇa returned immediately to His abode in the spiritual sky in their original spiritual bodies. Because God is absolute, by His killing He simultaneously removes the demons from the earth and encourages His pure devotees.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The Supreme Lord made the Pāṇḍavas, enraged by the numerous offenses of their enemies, such as duplicitous gambling, verbal insults, the seizing of Draupadī's hair, the immediate cause, and then had all the assembled kings kill each other, thus relieving the earth of its burden. This verse described the quarrel. The Lord made Arjuna and others, enraged greatly (subahu) by the unfair gambling match and other incidents, the cause, and then had the assembled kings kill each other and thus removed the burden of the earth.
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa engages His surrendered devotees as the nimitta to remove the world's burden — our outrage and effort, offered to Him, become His instrument.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one injustice that genuinely angers you and act on it today as Kṛṣṇa's instrument, not as the doer.
  • Before any difficult task, audibly say 'kṛtvā nimittam' — I am only the pretext — and proceed with full effort.
  • When wronged, refuse private revenge; bring the grievance into your sādhana and let Kṛṣṇa arrange the outcome.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like the Pāṇḍavas, being 'enraged' or pressed by circumstances that Kṛṣṇa is actually using as nimitta to move me toward His will — and how am I resisting being used?
  • When I look at the people who have wronged me, can I distinguish the burden Kṛṣṇa wants removed from the personal grudge my ahaṅkāra wants to nurse, and what would change today if I held that distinction honestly?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • You have probably been wronged this semester — a friend who lied, a group chat that turned on you, a teacher who graded unfairly. Before you draft the angry message, sit for two minutes and ask: am I the Pāṇḍava here being moved by Kṛṣṇa, or am I just Duryodhana wearing better clothes? The Pāṇḍavas' kopa was clean because they had no shelter other than Kṛṣṇa — test your anger against that bar.
  • Notice the difference between real compassion and material compassion in your friend group this week. Material compassion is laughing along so no one feels awkward, staying silent while a friend ruins themselves on Instagram or with substances. Real compassion is one honest sentence to one person, even if it costs you the friendship that week.
  • Pick one task you are avoiding — an exam to study for, an apology to make, a course to drop — and do it today as nimitta. Not 'I am crushing this,' not 'I'll do it when I feel ready,' but 'I am the immediate cause; the outcome is Kṛṣṇa's.' Then notice how much of your usual exam anxiety was actually proprietorship over the result.
householder
  • A real grievance against a relative, a colleague, or a business partner is probably sitting in your chest right now — a 'duplicitous gambling match' of your own. Before you act, write down: what would I do if I were certain Kṛṣṇa had already arranged the outcome and was only using me as nimitta? Then do that, not the version driven by needing to win.
  • With your children, distinguish material compassion (shielding them from every discomfort, every failed test, every disappointment) from real compassion (training them in sva-dharma, in honesty, in tolerance, in remembrance of Kṛṣṇa). The Pāṇḍavas were not protected from the house of lac or the dice game — they were trained to take shelter of Him through it. Pick one area this week where you have been over-shielding and step back.
  • Money decisions this month — a job offer, an investment, a purchase — make one of them explicitly as a karma-yogī: full diligence, full skill, and at the moment of signing, offer the outcome to Kṛṣṇa aloud. Then watch which decisions you are still secretly making as the doer, and bring those into your evening japa as a question.
senior
  • The 'kings burdening the earth' inside you at this stage are old resentments, unresolved family feuds, a list of people who wronged you across decades. Kṛṣṇa removed Bhīṣma and returned him to his svarūpa — He can remove these too. Each morning this week, name one such 'king' in your heart and offer it to Him; do not try to forgive by willpower, give it over as kṣiti-bhāra.
  • Your mortality is no longer abstract. Meditate that even Bhīṣma — grandfather, warrior, devotee — lay on a bed of arrows and left when Kṛṣṇa willed it. Arrange one practical thing this month — a will, a letter to a child, a transfer of responsibility — as a conscious laying-down of burden, not as a morbid task but as preparation to leave as a devotee, not as a proprietor.
  • Find one younger person — a grandchild, a junior at the maṭha, a neighbor's child — and tell them, in plain words, one story of when you mistook yourself for the doer and what it cost. Mentoring as nimitta means you are not building your legacy; you are letting Kṛṣṇa use the wreckage and the grace of your life to instruct the next one.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'Kṛṣṇa arranges the killing' as license for one's own anger or vengeance — forgetting that the Pāṇḍavas acted only after exhausting every reconciliation and only under Kṛṣṇa's direct sanction, never on private initiative.
  • Concluding that since Kṛṣṇa orchestrates everything, one's own choices and sva-dharma don't matter — missing that He specifically empowers surrendered devotees as nimitta, which requires active, surrendered participation, not passivity.
  • Sentimentalizing the verse into a general 'God uses bad things for good' platitude, and losing the sharper point: real compassion sometimes looks like opposing adharma directly, not avoiding others' bodily discomfort at the cost of the soul's welfare.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse says Kṛṣṇa made the Pāṇḍavas nimitta — the immediate cause — for killing kings who were burdening the earth. Share a time you were certain you were 'doing what had to be done.' Looking back, were you the doer, or were you being used? How would you tell the difference in the moment, not afterward?
  • Prabhupāda writes that Bhīṣma, a great devotee, appeared on the side of the demons and was killed by Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, yet returned to the spiritual sky. Discuss with a partner: does this comfort you or disturb you? What does it do to your sense of 'good side / bad side' in the conflicts you are currently inside?
  • The Pāṇḍavas were enraged — kopitāḥ — by duplicitous gambling, the insult to Draupadī, the house of lac. Is righteous anger a qualification for being Kṛṣṇa's instrument, or a disqualification? Where in your own life have you confused the two, and how did it land?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a Pāṇḍava on the eve of Kurukṣetra: cousins, teachers, and grandfather Bhīṣma stand on the opposing side. Student A says, 'I would rather live by begging than kill these men — what burden of the earth is worth this?' Student B is a fellow devotee who has heard this verse — that Kṛṣṇa has already arranged the assembly, that the Lord Himself is removing kṣiti-bhāra and is using the Pāṇḍavas only as nimitta. Student B must persuade Student A to fight, without flattening the grief and without sliding into 'it's all just an illusion.' After five minutes, swap roles. Then debrief: where did the argument feel honest, and where did it feel like spiritual bypassing?

Objections & Responses
  • This verse makes Kṛṣṇa sound like a cosmic manipulator — He stages a world war, provokes the Pāṇḍavas through Draupadī's humiliation and the gambling match, and then uses them as nimitta to kill millions. How is that the Supreme Personality of Godhead and not a tyrant?
    The objection assumes Kṛṣṇa is one party among many, optimizing outcomes the way a politician would. He is not. He is the proprietor — the kings He removes are usurpers ruling lands and bodies that were never theirs, oppressing the saintly and burdening the earth. The Pāṇḍavas were not provoked into hatred; they were already, as Prabhupāda says, 'fully surrendered to Him and knew no shelter other than Him.' Their kopa was not personal vendetta but a surrendered devotee's response to adharma touching their guru, their grandfather's honor, and their wife. And consider whom He 'kills': Bhīṣma is delivered back to the spiritual sky — hatā gatāḥ svarūpam. A tyrant takes life. Kṛṣṇa returns devotees to their svarūpa. That is not manipulation; that is the absolute Lord acting simultaneously as judge of the demons and liberator of His devotees, which only He, being absolute, can do.
  • Fine for an epic, but I work in a software company, not Kurukṣetra. The 'Kṛṣṇa is the real doer' framing sounds like a recipe for either passivity ('He'll handle it') or fanaticism ('I'm His instrument, get out of my way'). Which is it?
    Neither, and the verse itself shows why. The Pāṇḍavas were not passive — they drew bows, picked targets, suffered wounds, mourned the dead. Nor were they fanatics — Arjuna's whole crisis in the Gītā is that he refuses to be a fanatic, and Kṛṣṇa does not cure him by inflaming him but by instructing him. 'Kṛṣṇa is the doer' means kṛtvā nimittam — you do the work fully, with skill and responsibility, but you drop the proprietorship of the result. In your office that looks very ordinary: ship the code, make the call, fire if you must hire if you must, and at the end of the day offer the day to Him. Passivity says 'I won't act.' Fanaticism says 'my act is His will, therefore unquestionable.' Sva-dharma done as nimitta says 'I will act carefully, consult sādhus and śāstra, and let Him own the outcome.' That is neither limp nor dangerous — it is simply sober.
