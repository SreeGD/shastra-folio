Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.1.8

శ్రీరాజోవాచ బ్రహ్మణ్యానాం వదాన్యానాం నిత్యం వృద్ధోపసేవినామ్ । విప్రశాప: కథమభూద్ వృష్ణీనాం కృష్ణచేతసామ్ ॥ ౮ ॥

King Parīkṣit inquired: How could the brāhmaṇas curse the Vṛṣṇis, who were always respectful to the brāhmaṇas, charitable, and inclined to serve senior and exalted personalities and whose minds were always fully absorbed in thought of Lord Kṛṣṇa?
Purport

Brāhmaṇas generally become angry at persons who disrespect the brahminical class, who are uncharitable and who decline to serve senior, respectable personalities. The Vṛṣṇis, however, were not like that, and thus they are described here by King Parīkṣit as brahmaṇyānām, or sincere followers of brahminical culture. Further, even if the brāhmaṇas became angry, why would they curse members of Kṛṣṇa’s own family? Since the brāhmaṇas were well learned, they must have known that it is offensive to oppose personal associates of the Supreme Lord. The Yadu dynasty is specifically described here as vṛṣnīnām and kṛṣṇa-cetasām. In other words, they were Lord Kṛṣṇa’s own men, and they were always absorbed in thinking of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore, even if somehow or other the brāhmaṇas had cursed them, how could that curse have had any effect? These are the questions of Parīkṣit Mahārāja.

Although the Vṛṣṇis are described in this verse as kṛṣṇa-cetasām, always absorbed in thinking of Kṛṣṇa, it is clearly indicated that Kṛṣṇa desired that the brāhmaṇas become angry and curse the Yadu dynasty. The Supreme Lord desired to remove His personal dynasty from the earth, and therefore uncustomary offensive behavior was exhibited by the young boys of Kṛṣṇa’s own family.

It is to be understood from this incident that when a man displays envy and ridicule of devotees of Viṣṇu, his brahmaṇyatā, or high spiritual qualifications, along with his reverence for Śrī Kṛṣṇa, are all destroyed. Contempt and ridicule directed toward respectable persons and true brāhmaṇas vanquish all good qualities. If there is a break in etiquette toward His devotees, the Supreme Lord will become ill-disposed even to His own relatives and friends and thus arrange to destroy those who oppose His devotees. If foolish persons in the guise of members of Kṛṣṇa’s personal family express enmity toward Vaiṣṇavas, such offenders cannot properly be called offspring of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s dynasty. That is the supreme equality of the Personality of Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
King Parīkṣit said: How could the brāhmaṇas curse the Vṛṣṇis, who were always respectful to the brāhmaṇas, who were charitable, who were inclined to serve seniors and whose minds were always absorbed in Kṛṣṇa?

Personal Guidance
Even minds absorbed in Kṛṣṇa lose their shelter when they ridicule Vaiṣṇavas — Kṛṣṇa Himself withdraws favor from offenders of His devotees.
Practical Applications
  • Audit your speech today for any ridicule, sarcasm, or dismissiveness toward a Vaiṣṇava or sincere brāhmaṇa, and retract it before sundown.
  • Seek out one senior devotee (vṛddha) and render a small, concrete service — carry their bag, fetch water, listen without interrupting.
  • Refuse the assumption that closeness to Kṛṣṇa's family, institution, or culture exempts you from vaiṣṇava-aparādha — guard your tongue precisely there.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my own life have I, like the young Yadus, treated proximity to Kṛṣṇa or His devotees as a license to mock or test those I should be honoring?
  • Reading that even kṛṣṇa-cetasām status did not protect the Vṛṣṇis from the consequence of vipra-śāpa, what reverence toward brāhmaṇas and senior Vaiṣṇavas am I quietly neglecting today?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your group chats and social feeds from the past week: where did you join in mocking a 'religious' or 'old-fashioned' person — a teacher, a relative, a devotee — for laughs? Screenshot one, delete your contribution, and don't replace it.
  • Pick one vṛddha in your real life (a grandparent, a temple elder, a professor you respect) and serve them concretely this week — fifteen minutes of undistracted listening, a chore done without being asked. Notice what it costs your ego.
  • In your friend group, there is usually one person quietly more sincere about Kṛṣṇa than the rest, and the group knows it. Stop being the one who softens that person with a joke. Defend them once this week, even at social cost.
householder
  • Look at where the family's charity actually goes — not the intention, the bank statements. Redirect a fixed percentage this month specifically to brāhmaṇas and Vaiṣṇavas engaged in Kṛṣṇa's service, before any other discretionary spending clears.
  • Identify one senior in the extended family or community whom you have been quietly avoiding because serving them is inconvenient — an aging parent, an awkward elder devotee. Schedule the visit this week and keep it.
  • Watch how you and your spouse speak about devotees at the dinner table in front of the children. One critical, ridiculing remark — even casual — teaches them that Vaiṣṇavas are fair game. Name this pattern aloud with your spouse and agree on a correction.
senior
  • You are now a vṛddha others are meant to serve. Make yourself genuinely serve-able — be present, be reachable, drop the prickliness that pushes younger devotees away. Their service to you is part of their sādhana; don't deprive them of it by being difficult.
  • Review your will, your estate, your remaining attachments with this verse in mind: even Kṛṣṇa's own dynasty was withdrawn. Loosen one specific grip this month — an heirloom, a property claim, a grudge against a family member — and offer the loosening to Him.
  • If you have been holding contempt for a younger Vaiṣṇava — for being unrefined, unread, too enthusiastic, not your style — go to them this week and ask their blessings. Carry no seniority into that exchange. The verse warns that even kṛṣṇa-cetasām status does not survive Vaiṣṇava-aparādha; your decades of practice will not either.
Common Pitfalls
  • Assuming that being born into, or affiliated with, a devotee family or institution makes one immune to the reactions of offending Vaiṣṇavas and brāhmaṇas — the very point Parīkṣit's question exposes.
  • Reading 'Kṛṣṇa desired it' as cover for one's own offensive behavior toward devotees, instead of recognizing that the Lord still arranged destruction for those who ridiculed His servants.
  • Mistaking outward marks of brahmaṇyatā — charity, respect, ritual service — for safety, while inwardly harboring envy or ridicule toward true Vaiṣṇavas, which the purport says vanquishes all good qualities.
Discussion Prompts
  • The purport says Kṛṣṇa Himself desired that the brāhmaṇas curse the Yadus so the dynasty could be withdrawn. Share with a partner: when has something that looked like a 'curse' in your life — a failure, a rebuke, a loss — turned out to be the Lord's arrangement? Where do you still resist reading your reverses this way?
  • Parīkṣit lists four credentials of the Vṛṣṇis: respectful to brāhmaṇas, charitable, serving vṛddhas, absorbed in Kṛṣṇa. Rank these four in the order you find each personally hardest, and tell your group why your hardest one is hard. Don't agree too quickly — push back where you actually disagree.
  • The purport says contempt and ridicule of devotees vanquishes brahmaṇyatā and even reverence for Kṛṣṇa. Discuss a time you (or your circle) ridiculed a devotee — for being unsophisticated, fanatic, awkward, 'cultish.' What did you lose by it that you didn't notice losing?
Role Play

Three students play a scene in a temple community. Student A is a long-time donor and respected member whose adult son (Student B) has begun openly mocking a senior Vaiṣṇava (Student C) — calling him out of touch, financially dependent, behind the times — and is gathering peers around this view. Student A must decide in this conversation whether to side with blood (the son) or with the offended devotee, knowing both the social cost and the spiritual cost. Run the scene twice: once where A defends the son's 'right to question,' once where A names the aparādha directly. Debrief: which version felt like real compassion, and which felt like material compassion?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just tribalism dressed up as theology — 'don't insult our group's elders or you'll be cursed.' Every religion says this to protect its authority structure.
    A fair challenge, and worth taking seriously. But notice what the verse actually does: it doesn't protect the in-group, it indicts the in-group. The Vṛṣṇis are Kṛṣṇa's own family, and they are the ones destroyed. If this were tribalism, the text would shield them; instead it says blood relation to the Lord Himself gives no immunity once Vaiṣṇava-aparādha enters. The principle isn't 'respect our elders or else' — it's that the soul of another devotee is sacred ground, and the Lord guards His devotees more fiercely than He guards His own kinsmen. That is the opposite of tribal protectionism.
  • This makes brāhmaṇas and senior devotees untouchable to honest critique. Surely some 'senior Vaiṣṇavas' deserve correction.
    Yes — and the tradition itself distinguishes correction from ridicule. Approaching a senior with humility, in private, to raise a concern is not aparādha; treating him as a figure of fun, gossiping about him, or building a peer-group identity around mocking him is. The verse targets the second, not the first. The test is simple and uncomfortable: am I speaking to serve his soul and the mission, or to elevate myself by lowering him? The Vṛṣṇi boys failed that test, and so do we, more often than we admit.
