Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.6.1

శ్రీశుక ఉవాచ అథ బ్రహ్మాత్మజై: దేవై: ప్రజేశైరావృతోఽభ్యగాత్ । భవశ్చ భూతభవ్యేశో యయౌ భూతగణైర్వృత: ॥ ౧ ॥

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Brahmā then set off for Dvārakā, accompanied by his own sons as well as by the demigods and the great Prajāpatis. Lord Śiva, the bestower of auspiciousness to all living beings, also went, surrounded by many ghostly creatures.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Brahmā then set off for Dvārakā, accompanied by his sons as well as by the devatās and the great Prajāpatis. Śiva, the bestower of auspiciousness to all living beings, also went, surrounded by many ghostly creatures. In the Sixth Chapter the Lord is praised by Brahmā and others. Understanding that the Lord desired to disappear with his dynasty, Uddhava, dear to the Lord, made a request to Kṛṣṇa. The sons of Brahmā were the Kumāras and others. Bhūta-bhavyeśaḥ means the bestower of auspiciousness to all beings. They went to Dvārakā. This is understood in verse 4.

Personal Guidance
Even Brahmā and Śiva, with all demigods and Prajāpatis, travel together to wait upon Kṛṣṇa at Dvārakā — the highest powers bow before the Person.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one authority figure or capability you secretly rank above Kṛṣṇa today, and consciously place it under Him in your morning offering.
  • Approach your sādhana the way Brahmā approached Dvārakā — go to Kṛṣṇa with company, not alone; sit with devotees today instead of practicing in isolation.
  • Refuse the modern reflex that says 'I have outgrown needing to bow'; physically offer praṇāma before the Deity or your guru-paramparā today.
Reflection Questions
  • Which demigod-equivalents in my life — bosses, intellects, ideologies, my own intelligence — am I treating as terminal rather than as fellow servants on the way to Kṛṣṇa?
  • If even Brahmā and Śiva are 'surrounded by' their attendants when they approach Him, what does my own insistence on a private, solitary spirituality reveal?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick the one professor, founder, or influencer whose word you treat as final and ask honestly: would they, like Brahmā here, bow before Kṛṣṇa? Adjust how much weight you give them accordingly.
  • Before your next exam or interview, mentally place your intelligence and effort in the role of Brahmā — capable, but going to Kṛṣṇa for shelter — and pray accordingly rather than 'manifesting' on your own.
  • When friends frame surrender as weakness on social media, refuse to argue; instead, point to the fact that the most powerful beings in the cosmos are described here as travelling, accompanied, to bow.
householder
  • Plan the next family decision — school, move, finances — the way Brahmā moves in this verse: gather your dependents, then go together to Kṛṣṇa through prayer and Deity worship before deciding.
  • Identify one professional authority (a senior, a regulator, a market force) you have been quietly worshipping as supreme, and explicitly re-rank it under Kṛṣṇa in how you talk about it at the dinner table this week.
  • Bring your children and spouse into one act of worship today rather than 'doing your sādhana' privately — imitate Brahmā arriving with his sons, not alone.
senior
  • Use this verse to loosen attachment to your own accumulated authority: if Brahmā, the engineer of the universe, still travels to Dvārakā to wait on Kṛṣṇa, you can release the need to be the final voice in your family.
  • Identify the disciples, juniors, or grandchildren you are responsible for and consciously 'bring them with you' toward Kṛṣṇa — through stories, prasāda, kīrtana — rather than retiring inward.
  • As mortality approaches, rehearse Śiva's posture in this verse: powerful, surrounded by his own, yet moving toward the Lord — not clinging to his post but going to Him.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as mere cosmic scenery and missing that even the creator and the destroyer are subordinate worshippers of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Using Brahmā's and Śiva's greatness to flatten them into 'all paths are equal,' ignoring that the verse shows them moving toward Kṛṣṇa, not the reverse.
  • Romanticizing the demigods' entourage as decoration while overlooking the sober point — sva-dharma includes bringing one's dependents and associates along to Kṛṣṇa, not abandoning them.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a moment when you treated a capable person or institution as a final authority. What would it have looked like to relate to them as Brahmā relates to Kṛṣṇa here?
  • Brahmā arrives with sons, demigods, and Prajāpatis; Śiva with bhūta-gaṇas. Whom are you responsible for bringing with you toward Kṛṣṇa — and whom have you been leaving behind?
  • Where does your tradition or sampradāya feel like 'Brahmā's procession' to you, and where does it feel like an obstacle? Disagree honestly.
Role Play

A senior colleague who is brilliant and ethical but openly impersonalist invites you to a study circle that treats Kṛṣṇa as one symbol among many. One student plays the colleague; another plays a Gauḍīya practitioner who must remain respectful — as one would to Brahmā or Śiva — while not conceding that Kṛṣṇa is merely their peer. Run the conversation for five minutes, then switch roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This is just mythology — gods piling onto a chariot to visit another god. Why should a serious modern reader take it as personal guidance?
    Read it not as a tableau but as a hierarchy claim: the very functions you most respect in the universe — creative intelligence (Brahmā), dissolving power (Śiva), administrative order (Prajāpatis) — are depicted as moving toward a Person, not toward an abstraction. The guidance is structural: whatever you treat as ultimate in your life, this verse asks whether it too is on its way to Kṛṣṇa or whether you have stopped it short and crowned it.
  • If Brahmā and Śiva are also great, isn't Gauḍīya insistence on Kṛṣṇa's supremacy just sectarian preference?
    Notice the verb of the verse: Brahmā and Śiva go to Him. Their greatness is not denied — it is honored precisely by showing where it points. Gauḍīyas are not demoting the demigods; we are reading the direction of their own movement. To call that sectarian is to mistake a map's arrow for the mapmaker's bias.
