Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.15

త్యాగోఽయం దుష్కరో భూమన్ కామానాం విషయాత్మభి: । సుతరాం త్వయి సర్వాత్మన్నభక్తైరితి మే మతి: ॥ ౧౫ ॥

My dear Lord, O Supreme Soul, for those whose minds are attached to sense gratification, and especially for those bereft of devotion unto You, such renunciation of material enjoyment is most difficult to perform. That is my opinion.
Purport

Those who are truly devoted to the Supreme Lord do not accept anything for their personal gratification but rather accept those things suitable to be offered in the Lord’s loving service. The word viṣayātmabhiḥ indicates those who desire material objects for their personal gratification instead of for the devotional service of the Lord. The minds of such materialistic persons are suitably disturbed, and it is virtually impossible for such persons to renounce material enjoyment. This is the opinion of Śrī Uddhava.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Lord! O Soul of all beings! For those whose minds are attached to sense enjoyment, and especially for those bereft of devotion unto you, such renunciation of material enjoyment is most difficult to perform. That is my opinion.

Personal Guidance
For minds hooked on sense objects and bereft of devotion to Kṛṣṇa, renouncing material enjoyment is nearly impossible — bhakti is what makes tyāga doable.
Practical Applications
  • Name one viṣaya you have been trying to ‘willpower' away — and today, instead, offer that same energy in some direct service to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Before any indulgence today, pause and ask: ‘Am I taking this for myself, or can I receive it as prasāda in His service?' — and act accordingly.
  • Refuse the modern script that renunciation is achieved through self-discipline alone; add one concrete bhakti act (japa, hearing, offering) to anchor every restraint.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life have I been attempting tyāga as a viṣayātmā — gritting my teeth at the sense object — instead of through devotion to Kṛṣṇa?
  • What specific attachments have I quietly concluded are ‘too hard to give up,' and how would honest surrender to Him reframe their difficulty?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick the one app or feed that owns your attention most, and instead of just deleting it, replace twenty of those minutes daily with hearing or chanting — let bhakti, not willpower, do the heavy lifting.
  • Before the next party, exam-week binge, or relationship drama, notice the viṣayātmā script running in your head and consciously hand that situation to Kṛṣṇa in a short prayer before you act.
  • When friends mock your attempts to cut back on something (drinking, gaming, gossip), do not defend it as self-improvement — name it as a small step in devotion, and let that honesty filter your circle.
householder
  • Identify one family expense or lifestyle upgrade you have been chasing for personal gratification, and reframe the decision by asking how it serves your family's actual sādhana, not just your comfort.
  • Build a non-negotiable household bhakti anchor (morning japa, evening Bhāgavatam reading, Sunday offering) so the renunciations you and your spouse make in money, time, and food are supported by devotion, not white-knuckled.
  • When parenting, stop trying to make your children renounce screens, sweets, or status purely by rules — model and invite them into devotional alternatives that genuinely compete for their hearts.
senior
  • Take one possession, role, or identity you have been gripping (a title, a property, a way of being needed) and, in concrete steps this month, begin offering it into Kṛṣṇa's service rather than waiting for circumstances to pry it loose.
  • Use the natural loosening that age brings — reduced appetite, smaller social world, slowing body — as raw material for genuine vairāgya by tying each diminishment to increased hearing, chanting, and remembrance.
  • Mentor a younger person not by lecturing them on what to give up, but by sharing honestly where your own non-devotional renunciations failed and where devotion finally made tyāga real.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Uddhava's words as license to give up on renunciation — ‘it's impossible without bhakti, so why try' — instead of as a call to deepen devotion.
  • Mistaking dry, willpower-driven suppression of desires for tyāga, and then collapsing into rebound indulgence when the strain breaks.
  • Assuming ‘being a devotee' automatically makes renunciation easy, without doing the actual sādhana that loosens the grip of viṣaya on the mind.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one area where you have tried to renounce something without devotional support — what actually happened, and what does this verse reveal about that experience?
  • Uddhava says this renunciation is ‘most difficult' for the viṣayātmā — do we agree that devotion makes it easier, or does it sometimes make giving things up harder before it gets easier?
  • When a friend who is not a devotee tries to renounce a bad habit, how do we honor their effort while honestly holding the view that bhakti is the real basis of tyāga?
Role Play

Two students sit together: one has just decided to quit social media ‘cold turkey' through pure willpower and is already irritable on day three; the other is a devotee who has been reducing the same habit by replacing scrolling time with hearing from the Bhāgavatam. The devotee must, in this verse's spirit, neither moralize nor flatter — but help the friend see why tyāga without bhakti is so brittle, and offer one practical bhakti-anchored step the friend can actually take.

Objections & Responses
  • Plenty of non-devotees — Stoics, monks of other paths, even disciplined atheists — successfully renounce sense pleasures. So Uddhava's claim that it is ‘virtually impossible' without devotion to Kṛṣṇa is just sectarian overreach.
    Outward restraint is not the same as tyāga of the kāma in the mind. The Gītā (2.59) notes that the rasa, the inner taste, only leaves on seeing the Supreme. Others may discipline the senses heroically, yet the appetite often returns under stress, or migrates to subtler objects — pride, control, reputation. Uddhava is speaking of that deep, rooted renunciation where the mind itself loses its pull toward viṣaya, and in the Gauḍīya line that comes through loving attachment to Kṛṣṇa, not against Him.
  • If renunciation is ‘most difficult' for ordinary people like me, this verse just sets an impossibly high bar and breeds despair — it sounds like the tradition is telling householders to give up before they start.
    Read it the other way: Uddhava is being honest precisely so we stop blaming ourselves for the wrong thing. The verse does not say ‘try harder at renunciation'; it diagnoses why brute-force tyāga keeps failing and points us toward bhakti as the practical solution. For a householder, that means not abandoning duties but converting them — earning, cooking, parenting, working — into offerings to Kṛṣṇa, so detachment grows organically as attachment to Him grows.
