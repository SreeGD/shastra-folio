Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.42

అన్తర్హితశ్చ స్థిరజఙ్గమేషు బ్రహ్మాత్మభావేన సమన్వయేన । వ్యాప్త్యావ్యవచ్ఛేదమసఙ్గమాత్మనో మునిర్నభస్త్వం వితతస్య భావయేత్ ॥ ౪౨ ॥

A thoughtful sage, even while living within a material body, should understand himself to be pure spirit soul. Similarly, one should see that the spirit soul enters within all forms of life, both moving and nonmoving, and that the individual souls are thus all-pervading. The sage should further observe that the Supreme Personality of Godhead, as the Supersoul, is simultaneously present within all things. Both the individual soul and the Supersoul can be understood by comparing them to the nature of the sky: although the sky extends everywhere and everything rests within the sky, the sky does not mix with anything, nor can it be divided by anything.
Purport

Although air exists within the sky, the sky, or space, is different from air. Even in the absence of air, space or sky is present. All material objects are situated within space, or within the vast material sky, but the sky remains undivided and, although accommodating all objects, never actually mixes with anything. In the same way one can understand the situation of both the individual soul and the Supersoul. The individual soul is all-pervading, because there are innumerable jīvātmās, which enter within all things; yet, as confirmed in Vedic literature, each individual ātmā remains infinitesimal. The Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad (5.9) states:

bālāgra-śata-bhāgasya śatadhā kalpitasya ca bhāgo jīvaḥ sa vijṣeyaḥ sa cānantyāya kalpate

“When the upper point of a hair is divided into one hundred parts and again each of such parts is further divided into one hundred parts, each such part is the measurement of the dimension of the spirit soul.” The same is stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam:

keśāgra-śata-bhāgasya śatāṁśaḥ sādṛśātmakaḥ jīvaḥ sūkṣma-svarūpo ’yaṁ saṅkhyātīto hi cit-kaṇaḥ

“There are innumerable particles of spiritual atoms, which are measured as one ten-thousandth of the upper portion of the hair.”

The Supreme Personality of Godhead, however, is all-pervading because He Himself is personally present everywhere. The Lord is known as advaita, or indivisible. Thus the same unique Personality of Godhead exists everywhere, just like the sky, and yet is not attached to anything, although everything is resting within Him. The Lord Himself has confirmed this analysis of His all-pervasiveness in Bhagavad-gītā (9.6) :

yathākāśa-sthito nityaṁ vāyuḥ sarvatra-go mahān tathā sarvāṇi bhūtāni mat-sthānīty upadhāraya

“As the almighty wind, blowing everywhere, always rests in ethereal space, know that in the same manner all beings rest in Me.”

Therefore, although both the jīva soul and the Supersoul are said to be all-pervading, it should be remembered that there are innumerable individual jīva souls, whereas there is only one Supreme Personality of Godhead. The Lord is always supreme, and one who is actually a thoughtful sage never doubts the supreme position of the Lord.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The yogī should meditate on Paramātmā, spreading everywhere and undivided as the medium called Brahman in all moving and non-moving beings, inside and outside, similar to ether. Seeing the ether, one learns that though Paramātmā is inside and outside everything, he is not attached to anything. This is explained in two verses. The yogī should meditate with intelligence on Paramātmā who is like ether, pervading everywhere inside and outside. Paramātmā is undivided, spreading everything as a connecting medium in the form of Brahman in all moving and non-moving beings, just as ether though pervading everywhere, does not mix with the pot or other objects and does not become divided.

Personal Guidance
Like the sky that holds everything yet mixes with nothing, the soul and the Supersoul pervade all bodies while remaining untouched — live as that unmixed witness today.
Practical Applications
  • Each time your body reports a sensation today — hunger, fatigue, pleasure — silently note: "this is happening in me, not to me," and return to the witnessing ātmā.
  • When you meet a person, animal, or even a plant today, pause for one breath and acknowledge the same jīva-ātmā within them, accompanied by Kṛṣṇa as Supersoul.
  • Refuse to let one stinging word or one flattering word "stick" — recall the sky-nature of the soul and let the remark pass through without coloring you.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my day do I most forget the sky-nature of the soul and start identifying as the air, the dust, and the weather passing through it?
  • If Kṛṣṇa as Supersoul is already present within every being I will meet today, what in my behavior toward them would I have to change to honor that fact?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Next time a comment on your post or a grade on an exam tries to define you, name it out loud as "weather in the sky" of the ātmā and refuse to let it set your mood for the day.
  • In your friend group today, pick the person you find most irritating and silently acknowledge Kṛṣṇa as Supersoul seated within them before you speak a single word.
  • When peer pressure pushes you to perform an identity — cool, smart, edgy — pause and ask which "air" you are mistaking for the sky of your real self.
householder
  • In one charged interaction with spouse or child today, consciously hold the sky-vision: their behavior is passing weather, the ātmā within them — and Kṛṣṇa within that — is whom you are actually serving.
  • Before a money or career decision this week, sit for two minutes and locate yourself as the unmixed witness, so the decision is made by the sage in you, not by the anxieties moving through you.
  • When raising your children, speak to the jīva in them at least once a day — a word of respect, a serious question — alongside all the practical instruction you must give the body and mind.
senior
  • As the body's capacities visibly contract, deliberately rehearse the sky-nature of the ātmā each morning, so that aging becomes confirmation of the verse rather than a private grief.
  • Identify one attachment — a possession, a role, a grievance — and consciously let it pass through you this month the way wind passes through sky, offering the release to Kṛṣṇa as Supersoul.
  • When mentoring younger people, address the soul within them, not merely their career or behavior; let them feel that someone finally saw the sky in them, not just the weather.
Common Pitfalls
  • Sliding from "the soul is like the sky" into impersonalism — treating the Supersoul as mere space or consciousness rather than Kṛṣṇa personally present within all.
  • Using "I am untouched spirit soul" as a spiritual alibi for neglecting bodily duties, family, and sevā, when the verse asks for vision, not negligence.
  • Collapsing the distinction between the innumerable infinitesimal jīvas and the one all-pervading Supreme — flattening "all is one" instead of seeing one Lord within many souls.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a recent moment when an insult or a compliment clearly "stuck" to you — what would it have looked like, concretely, to remain sky-like in that moment, and was that even desirable?
  • The verse insists the soul is all-pervading yet infinitesimal, and the Supersoul is all-pervading yet one — where do you find yourself blurring these two, and why does the distinction matter for bhakti?
  • If every moving and non-moving being already has Kṛṣṇa within as Supersoul, how should that change the way we treat the people we find most difficult — and where do you resist that conclusion?
Role Play

Two students are paired. Student A has just been passed over for a promotion at work and vents bitterly about a colleague who got it; Student B, drawing on this verse, must respond — not by dismissing the pain, but by gently helping A see the sky-nature of the ātmā, the Supersoul present even in the colleague, without sliding into platitudes like "let it go" or impersonalist talk of "we are all one."

Objections & Responses
  • If the soul is truly untouched like the sky, then nothing I do to my body or to others really matters — this teaching seems to license indifference.
    The verse describes the soul's ontological nature, not a permission slip for the conditioned self. Precisely because Kṛṣṇa as Supersoul dwells in every being, every action toward every body becomes a transaction with Him; the sky-nature is realized by the sage who serves more carefully, not less.
  • "All-pervading individual souls" and "one all-pervading Supreme" sounds like contradictory metaphysics smuggled in to protect a personal God.
    Gauḍīya ācāryas read this as acintya-bheda-abheda: the jīvas are innumerable and atomic in size yet collectively pervade through their presence in all bodies, while Kṛṣṇa is one and personally all-pervading as Supersoul. The sky analogy is meant to hold both truths together, not to dissolve one into the other.
