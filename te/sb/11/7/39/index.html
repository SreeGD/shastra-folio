Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.39

A learned sage should take his satisfaction in the simple maintenance of his existence and should not seek satisfaction through gratifying the material senses. In other words, one should care for the material body in such a way that one’s higher knowledge is not destroyed and so that one’s speech and mind are not deviated from self-realization.
Purport

A wise man does not absorb his consciousness in the forms, flavors, aromas and sensations of material sense gratification, but rather accepts activities such as eating and sleeping simply to keep body and soul together. One must properly maintain one’s body by regulated activities of eating, sleeping, cleansing, etc., otherwise the mind will become weak, and one’s spiritual knowledge will fade away. If one eats too austerely, or if in the name of selflessness one accepts impure food, surely one loses control of the mind. On the other hand, if one eats food that is excessively fatty or rich there will be an unwanted increase in sleep and semen, and thus the mind and speech will be overwhelmed by the modes of passion and ignorance. Lord Kṛṣṇa has summarized the whole matter in Bhagavad-gītā by His statement yuktāhāra-vihārasya yukta-ceṣṭasya karmasu. One should moderately and intelligently regulate all of one’s bodily activities so that they are favorable for self-realization. This technique is taught by the bona fide spiritual master. If one is too austere or if one engages too much in sense gratification, self-realization is impossible.

It is the duty of a devotee of the Lord to avoid seeing any object as separate from Kṛṣṇa, because that is illusion. A gentleman will never try to enjoy the property of another gentleman. Similarly, if one sees everything in relation to Kṛṣṇa, there is no scope for material sense gratification. But if one sees material objects as separate from Kṛṣṇa, then one’s material enjoying propensity is immediately aroused. A human being must be intelligent enough to distinguish between preyas, or temporary gratification, and śreyas, permanent benefit. One may accept sense activity in a regulated, limited fashion so that one will be strong for serving Kṛṣṇa, but if one excessively indulges the material senses, one will lose one’s gravity and seriousness in spiritual life and act like an ordinary materialist. The ultimate goal, as stated here, is jṣānam, or steady consciousness of the Absolute Truth, Lord Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
A learned sage should take his satisfaction with the actions of his prāṇa and should not seek satisfaction through the sense objects. In other words, one should care for the material body in such a way that one’s higher knowledge is not destroyed and so that one’s speech and mind are not deviated from self-realization. There are two types of air: prāṇa and the external air. This verse speaks of what he learned from prāṇa. Prāṇa operates by eating and other actions, and is not dependent on the objects of the senses like form and taste (indriya-priyaih). By taking satisfaction from the prāṇa he becomes a sage. Jṣāna will be destroyed by disturbance of the mind, caused by disruption of the prāṇa. Therefore one should maintain the body by eating etc., but in such a way that speech and mind will not create disturbance. The mind will become disturbed by eating course or impure food. The mind and speech will be agitated by food which is too oily, because of increase in laziness and semen.

Personal Guidance
Eat, sleep, and care for the body only enough to keep jṣāna intact — never let the senses dictate the terms of your sādhana.
Practical Applications
  • Set today's meals at the threshold of bodily sufficiency — enough to serve Kṛṣṇa clearly, not enough to thicken sleep or stir rajas.
  • Notice one indulgence you justify as 'maintenance' (a richer dish, an extra hour of sleep, a stimulating feed) and cut it back today.
  • Before any sense engagement, ask whether you are seeing the object as separate from Kṛṣṇa or in relation to Him, and refuse the first.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I dressing up indriya-priya as prāṇa-vṛtti — calling sense pleasure mere upkeep — and what knowledge has quietly faded as a result?
  • In the last week, when did my speech or mind get scattered (nāvakīryeta vāṅ-manaḥ), and which 'innocent' bodily indulgence preceded it?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your phone and feed today: cut one stream that is pure indriya-priya (short-form video, gossip threads) and notice how speech and study steady within hours.
  • Eat your hostel or campus meals on a schedule that keeps you alert for class and japa, not heavy and sleepy — refuse the late-night junk run even when friends insist.
  • Before chasing the next 'experience' your peers are pushing (party, binge, hookup, all-nighter), ask whether it will protect or erode your capacity to study śāstra this week.
householder
  • Set the family's table by yuktāhāra — moderate portions, sāttvika food, fixed times — so that children grow up tasting prāṇa-vṛtti rather than indriya-priya as the norm.
  • Review one 'lifestyle upgrade' the family is considering (richer food, bigger home, more entertainment) against the test: will it strengthen or fade our collective remembrance of Kṛṣṇa?
  • Schedule sleep, work, and recreation around morning sādhana and evening hearing, not the other way around — let earning and enjoying serve self-realization, not displace it.
senior
  • Simplify your diet and daily comforts now, deliberately — lighter food, earlier sleep, fewer stimulations — so the mind is clear for hearing, chanting, and remembrance in the years that remain.
  • Mentor a younger devotee specifically on yuktāhāra-vihāra: show, from your own long experience, how over-austerity and over-indulgence both wrecked sādhana, and how regulation protected jṣāna.
  • Loosen one long-held bodily attachment (a favorite rich food, a habitual comfort, a treasured possession) as a concrete rehearsal for the final letting-go, keeping speech and mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa.
Common Pitfalls
  • Swinging into harsh austerity, eating impure or insufficient food in the name of renunciation, and then losing mental control entirely.
  • Swinging the other way — calling rich, heavy, over-stimulating intake 'prasāda' or 'taking care of the body' while sleep and passion swell.
  • Treating yuktāhāra-vihāra as a private formula one can self-calibrate, bypassing the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master.
Discussion Prompts
  • Compare your last three meals: which were truly prāṇa-vṛtti and which were indriya-priya dressed up as maintenance — and how do you tell the difference in your own life?
  • Where is the line between caring properly for the body so jṣāna is not destroyed and pampering the body so jṣāna fades — and who in your life draws that line for you?
  • Share a concrete moment when over-austerity or over-indulgence visibly disturbed your speech or mind, and what you learned about preyas vs. śreyas from it.
Role Play

A new devotee invites a senior student to dinner and serves an elaborate, very rich, very sweet 'offered' feast, insisting that since it is prasāda there can be no excess. The senior student must accept the hospitality with grace, honor the offering, and yet hold the line of yuktāhāra — without shaming the host or pretending the verse does not apply. Two students play the host and guest; a third observes and reports where indriya-priya was disguised as devotion.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like life-denying asceticism dressed in Sanskrit — modern wellness teaches that listening to the body's appetites is health, not bondage.
    The verse explicitly rejects both extremes: too austere and the mind collapses, too indulgent and rajas and tamas overwhelm speech and mind. Kṛṣṇa's own word in the Gītā is yuktāhāra-vihārasya — regulated, not denied. The standard is not suppression of the body but protection of jṣāna; the body is kept fit precisely so that higher knowledge does not fade.
  • If everything can be seen in relation to Kṛṣṇa, then I can enjoy whatever I want and simply 'offer' it — the verse should not restrict me.
    Seeing things in relation to Kṛṣṇa is the opposite of using His name to license enjoyment. The verse warns that the moment you treat the object as separate from Him — as yours to enjoy — material propensity is aroused. The test is honest: after the 'offered' indulgence, are speech and mind steadier in Kṛṣṇa, or scattered? The answer, not the label, decides whether it was prāṇa-vṛtti or indriya-priya.
