Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.4.8

విజ్ఞాయ శక్రకృతమక్రమమాదిదేవ: ప్రాహ ప్రహస్య గతవిస్మయ ఏజమానాన్ । మా భైష్టభో మదన మారుత దేవవధ్వో గృహ్ణీత నో బలిమశూన్యమిమం కురుధ్వమ్ ॥ ౮ ॥

The primeval Lord, understanding the offense committed by Indra, did not become proud. Instead He spoke laughingly as follows to Cupid and his followers, who were trembling before Him: “Do not fear, O mighty Madana, O wind-god and wives of the demigods. Rather, please accept these gifts I am offering you and kindly sanctify My āśrama by your presence.”
Purport

The word gata-vismayaḥ, or “free from false pride,” is very significant. If one becomes proud by performing severe penances, such penances are considered material. One should not think, “I am a great, austere personality.” Śrī Nara-Nārāyaṇa could immediately understand the foolishness of Indra, and thus He was amused by the whole situation. Cupid and the heavenly women, understanding their great offense, were trembling before Nara-Nārāyaṇa in fear of receiving a powerful curse. But the Lord, exhibiting most sublime saintly behavior, reassured them by saying, mā bhaiḥ — “Don’t worry about it” — and actually offered them nice prasādam and articles of worship. “If you do not give Me the opportunity to act as host to the demigods and other respectable personalities,” He said, “what will be the use of My āśrama ? My āśrama will be void without the opportunity to receive respectable personalities such as you.”

Similarly, the International Society for Krishna Consciousness is establishing beautiful centers in all the major cities of the world. In some of these centers, such as those in Los Angeles, Bombay, London, Paris and Melbourne, the Society has established very gorgeous preaching āśramas. But the Vaiṣṇavas who live in these beautiful buildings feel that the buildings are void if guests do not come to hear about Kṛṣṇa and chant His holy name. Thus, one may establish a beautiful āśrama not for one’s personal sense gratification but to execute Kṛṣṇa consciousness peacefully and encourage others to take to Kṛṣṇa consciousness also.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The primeval Lord, understanding the offense committed by Indra and without pride, spoke laughingly as follows to Cupid and his followers, who were trembling before him: “Do not fear, O mighty Cupid, O wind-god and wives of the devatās! Please accept this gift I am offering you, and kindly sanctify my hermitage by your presence.” Akramam means offense. He was without pride, not thinking “Oh! I am fully in control of my senses.” Cupid was trembling in fear of being cursed. “O capable Cupid! O women of the devatās! Do not fear. Please accept as our welcome to guests the articles of worship and vegetable meals made of leaves. “But we have everything we need.” Without your accepting our hospitality, this hermitage is empty.
Personal Guidance
True greatness is gata-vismaya — free of pride; meet provocation with hospitality, not retaliation, and make your space useful to Kṛṣṇa's servants.
Practical Applications
  • When someone tests or insults you today, refuse the satisfaction of a curse-like reply and instead offer them something concrete — tea, food, a kind word.
  • Audit one austerity or discipline you secretly take pride in and offer its fruit aloud to Kṛṣṇa before the day ends.
  • Invite a guest into your home or room this week specifically so the space does not become 'śūnya' — empty of service to devotees and seekers.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I quietly storing pride in my sādhana, waiting for someone like Indra to come along so I can prove my power?
  • Is my home, desk, or schedule currently aśūnya — filled with opportunity to serve Kṛṣṇa's people — or is it a private fortress?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a classmate or online acquaintance tries to provoke or shame you, reply with a genuine compliment or a small favor instead of a clever comeback.
  • After an exam result or a win you are proud of, tell Kṛṣṇa about it before you tell your friends, so the credit lands in the right place.
  • Once this week, invite a lonely or new student to eat with you so your social circle is not śūnya of service.
householder
  • When your spouse or child does something that wounds your pride, lead with a concrete kindness — cook their favorite dish, take a chore off their list — before any correction.
  • Open your home this month to a guest who needs hearing about Kṛṣṇa, treating the cost of food and time as the actual purpose of your earnings.
  • Examine where career success has quietly become a 'penance' you boast about, and re-offer one current project to Kṛṣṇa with no claim to its fruit.
senior
  • When younger family members or juniors challenge or dismiss you, refuse the curse-reflex of cutting them off and instead become a host they want to visit.
  • Use your home and free hours to receive devotees, seekers, and the lonely, so retirement does not become a śūnya āśrama of comfort.
  • Take one austerity you have practiced for decades and stop mentioning it to anyone for a full month, letting Kṛṣṇa alone know it is happening.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking the Lord's mildness for weakness and concluding that a serious sādhaka should never confront offense at all.
  • Performing hospitality outwardly while inwardly cataloguing the offender's debt, so the gesture becomes a subtler form of curse.
  • Treating one's āśrama, home, or practice as a private sanctuary for personal sense gratification rather than a place to receive and serve others.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a time you had every right to retaliate and chose hospitality instead — what did it cost you, and was the cost worth it?
  • Indra sent Cupid to break Nara-Nārāyaṇa's penance because he feared a rival; where in our lives do we send 'Cupids' against people whose sādhana threatens us?
  • What concretely makes a home or center aśūnya versus śūnya in Kṛṣṇa consciousness — and where does your own space currently sit on that line?
Role Play

A senior devotee at the temple has just been publicly insulted by a younger visitor who accuses him of being 'just another proud guru figure.' One student plays the senior devotee, who must respond in the spirit of gata-vismaya — without pride, without curse, and by actually offering the visitor prasādam and a seat. Another student plays the visitor, pushing back skeptically. A third observes and afterwards names the moments pride almost crept back in.

Objections & Responses
  • If the Lord just laughs off Indra's sabotage, isn't this teaching passivity? Real leaders need to set firm boundaries, not hand out gifts to those who attack them.
    Nara-Nārāyaṇa is not passive — He is unshaken. He neither curses Cupid nor abandons His penance; He continues His mission while refusing to let the offender define His mood. That is the highest form of boundary: an inner steadiness that no provocation can disturb, combined with active kindness that converts an adversary into a guest.
  • This sounds like spiritual performance — being conspicuously gracious so everyone notices how humble you are. Isn't that just pride in reverse?
    Prabhupāda flags exactly this danger with the word gata-vismaya: the moment one thinks 'I am a great, austere personality,' the penance becomes material. The cure is not to suppress hospitality but to offer it as Kṛṣṇa's servant, not as its proprietor — the gifts in the verse are the Lord's, the āśrama is the Lord's, and so is the welcome.
