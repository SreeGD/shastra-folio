Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.4.5

ఆదావభూచ్ఛతధృతీ రజసాస్య సర్గే విష్ణు: స్థితౌ క్రతుపతిర్ద్విజధర్మసేతు: । రుద్రోఽప్యయాయ తమసా పురుష: స ఆద్య ఇత్యుద్భ‍వస్థితిలయా: సతతం ప్రజాసు ॥ ౫ ॥

In the beginning, the original Supreme Personality manifested the form of Brahmā through the material mode of passion in order to create this universe. The Lord manifested His form as Viṣṇu, the Lord of sacrifice and protector of the twice-born brāhmaṇas and their religious duties, to maintain the universe. And when the universe is to be annihilated the same Supreme Lord employs the material mode of ignorance and manifests the form of Rudra. The created living beings are thus always subject to the forces of creation, maintenance and destruction.
Purport

In the previous verse the Supreme Personality of Godhead was described as ādi-kartā, the original person responsible for the creation, maintenance and annihilation of the material world. According to Śrīdhara Svāmī, ādi-kartā, or “the original doer,” implies subsequent creators, maintainers and annihilators. Otherwise there would be no meaning to the word ādi, or “original.” Therefore this verse describes that the Absolute Truth expands Himself into the guṇāvatāras, or incarnations who carry out the creation, maintenance and annihilation of the universe through the modes of passion, goodness and ignorance respectively.

It is significant here that although this verse mentions creation through the material mode of passion and annihilation through the material mode of ignorance, it does not mention maintenance by Viṣṇu through the material mode of goodness. That is because Viṣṇu is viśuddha-sattva, or existing on the platform of unlimited transcendental goodness. Although Lord Śiva and Lord Brahmā are somewhat affected by their prescribed duties as superintendents of the modes of nature, Lord Viṣṇu is viśuddha-sattva, completely beyond contamination even by the material mode of goodness. As described in the Vedas, na tasya kāryaṁ karaṇaṁ ca vidyate: the Lord has no occupational duty. Whereas Lord Śiva and Lord Brahmā are understood to be servants of the Lord, Viṣṇu is completely transcendental.

According to Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, Viṣṇu, described in this verse as kratu-patiḥ, the Lord of sacrifice, is understood to have appeared in a previous age as the incarnation Suyajṣa, the son of Prajāpati Ruci. Whereas Brahmā and Śiva faithfully engage in service to the Supreme Lord, Viṣṇu is the Supreme Lord Himself, and therefore His activities of maintaining the brāhmaṇas and religious principles, as mentioned in this verse ( dvija-dharma-setuḥ ), are not occupational duties but līlā. So in addition to being a guṇāvatāra, Viṣṇu is also a līlāvatāra, according to Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī. The Mahābhārata ( Śānti-parva ) describes the birth of Lord Brahmā from the lotus flower emanating from Viṣṇu and the subsequent birth of Lord Śiva from the angry eyes of Lord Brahmā. Viṣṇu, however, is the self-manifested Personality of Godhead who enters the material universe by His own internal potency, as stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (3.8.15) :

tal loka-padmaṁ sa u eva viṣṇuḥ prāvīviśat sarva-guṇāvabhāsam

In conclusion, Lord Viṣṇu, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is the supreme controller whose personal form is full of eternal existence, knowledge and bliss, who is beginningless but is the beginning of everything, who is known as Govinda, and who is the original cause of all causes, as stated in Brahma-saṁhitā. Still, that same eternal Lord manifests Himself as Brahmā and Śiva in the sense that the primeval controllers Brahmā and Śiva manifest the potency and supreme will of the Lord, although they themselves are not supreme.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In the beginning, the puruṣa appeared as Brahmā through rajoguṇa for creation, as Viṣṇu, the lord of sacrifice, and protector of dharma of the brāhmaṇas, for maintenance, and as Śiva through tamo-guṇa for destruction. In this way creation, maintenance and destruction of the living entities takes place at all times. The guṇāvatāras are described. The puruṣa appeared as Brahmā (śata-dhṛtiḥ) for activities of creation by rajoguṇa, as Viṣṇu, the protector of the dharma of the brāhmaṇas, for maintenance and as Śiva for destruction. Iti means “in this way.”

Personal Guidance
Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra preside over creation, maintenance and dissolution — but Viṣṇu alone is viśuddha-sattva, the Lord Himself, the only safe shelter.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today which guṇa — rajas, sattva or tamas — is driving each major decision, and refuse to act from rajas or tamas without first pausing.
  • When you feel proud of building something or devastated when something ends, remind yourself you are watching sṛṣṭi-sthiti-laya pass through you, not happening to you.
  • Direct your worship and shelter-taking specifically to Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa today, not to a generic "higher power" — speak His name aloud at least once.
Reflection Questions
  • Which of the three phases — building, maintaining, or watching something end — am I currently in, and how is the corresponding guṇa quietly shaping my mood?
  • Where in my devotional life have I been treating Brahmā, Śiva and Viṣṇu as interchangeable, and what would change if I honored Viṣṇu as viśuddha-sattva and the others as His servants?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice which guṇa is driving your scrolling, your friend-group dynamics and your exam prep this week — rajas chasing more, tamas avoiding, or something steadier — and name it out loud once a day.
  • When a friendship, a class or a phase of college begins or ends, practice seeing it as sṛṣṭi or laya passing through you, not as your whole identity rising or collapsing.
  • Pick one specific name of Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa and chant it before opening your phone in the morning, so your first shelter of the day is personal, not algorithmic.
householder
  • Before a major family or career decision today, ask which mode you are operating in — ambitious rajas, fearful tamas, or steadying sattva — and delay the decision if it is not the last.
  • When a project at work flourishes or a plan collapses, tell your spouse plainly: "This is sṛṣṭi" or "This is laya," and refuse to let the household mood be hijacked by either.
  • In your family's evening practice, make Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa the explicit center — not a generic "God bless us" — so your children learn that shelter has a name and a face.
senior
  • As bodily strength, friendships and roles enter laya, narrate the dissolution honestly to yourself as the work of tamas under the Lord's sanction, not as personal failure or cosmic injustice.
  • Mentor a younger person this week by helping them recognize which guṇa is running their life, drawing on what you now see clearly in hindsight about your own rajas-driven years.
  • Re-anchor your daily remembrance specifically in Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa as viśuddha-sattva, so that as the body's guṇas thin out, your shelter is in the one who was never inside them.
Common Pitfalls
  • Concluding that since all three deities are "the same Lord," it does not matter whom one worships — flattening the personalist distinction this verse carefully preserves.
  • Romanticizing rajas as healthy ambition or tamas as restful detachment, forgetting that both bewilder the living being while only viśuddha-sattva liberates.
  • Reading the verse as cosmology only and missing that creation, maintenance and destruction are happening in one's own body, relationships and projects right now.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one project, relationship or phase of life you are currently in, and identify which of the three — sṛṣṭi, sthiti or laya — it is in; do others see it the same way you do?
  • Prabhupāda insists Viṣṇu is viśuddha-sattva while Brahmā and Śiva are "somewhat affected" by their duties — does this distinction feel reverent or sectarian to you, and why?
  • If everyone in the room must always be subject to creation, maintenance and destruction, what is the practical difference between a devotee's life and a non-devotee's life?
Role Play

One student plays a sincere seeker who says, "I worship Śiva, my friend worships Brahmā, you worship Kṛṣṇa — we are all reaching the same Absolute, so the names do not matter." The other student, drawing only on this verse and its purport, must respond as a Gauḍīya friend — neither dismissive nor compromising — explaining why Viṣṇu's position as viśuddha-sattva is not just a preference but a structural fact, and inviting the seeker to consider what that means for where one takes shelter.

Objections & Responses
  • This is just primitive cosmology — three gods for three functions. Modern science explains creation, maintenance and destruction without any deities.
    The verse is not competing with physics about mechanism; it is naming the agent and the mode. Science describes how matter behaves under the three guṇas; this verse tells us who is presiding and, more importantly, who stands outside the guṇas. Even if you grant every scientific account, the question "in whose shelter do I live and die?" remains, and this verse answers it.
  • If Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva are all expansions of the one Supreme, isn't it sectarian to single out Viṣṇu as transcendental and call the others "somewhat affected"?
    It would be sectarian if it were a tribal preference, but the verse itself makes the distinction: creation is through rajas, destruction through tamas, while Viṣṇu's maintenance is pointedly not attributed to sattva, because He is viśuddha-sattva, beyond even material goodness. Honoring that asymmetry is not disrespecting Brahmā and Śiva — they themselves serve Viṣṇu — it is reading the verse as it stands.
