Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.8.8

యోషిద్ధిరణ్యాభరణామ్బరాది- ద్రవ్యేషు మాయారచితేషు మూఢ: । ప్రలోభితాత్మా హ్యుపభోగబుద్ధ్యా పతఙ్గవన్నశ్యతి నష్టద‍ృష్టి: ॥ ౮ ॥

A foolish person with no intelligent discrimination is immediately aroused at the sight of a lusty woman beautifully decorated with golden ornaments, fine clothing and other cosmetic features. Being eager for sense gratification, such a fool loses all intelligence and is destroyed just like the moth who rushes into the blazing fire.
Purport

Actually, women have the power to attract men through all of the material senses. Men become lusty by seeing the body of a woman, by smelling her fragrance, by hearing her voice, by tasting her lips and by touching her body. However, the foolish relationship based on material sex attraction begins by seeing, and thus rūpa, or form, is very prominent in the process of ruining one’s intelligence. This fact has been exploited in modern times by huge pornography industries, which prey on unfortunate men and women. The example of the foolish moth rushing into the fire and destroying itself is most appropriate in this regard, for one who becomes addicted to the momentary pleasure of sex indulgence certainly loses his power to understand the spiritual reality behind dull matter.

A lusty person becomes blind and foolish through sex indulgence, and his soul is lost in the fire of sense gratification. This whole disaster can be avoided by taking seriously to the process of chanting the holy names of the Lord: Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. Caitanya Mahāprabhu and His empowered representatives such as Śrīla Prabhupāda have created a movement to save people from the dungeon of material life, and we should all seriously take advantage of this opportunity.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
A foolish person, lured by a woman, gold, ornaments or fine clothing made by māyā, with a desire for enjoyment, loses his vision and is destroyed, just like the moth which rushes into a fire. Though the five sense objects are involved in being attracted to a woman, among all enjoyable objects, first the sense of sight falls victim to women or other desired objects. Thus form is the chief sense object.

Personal Guidance
Like a moth rushing the flame, the lusty fool drawn by form and ornament loses his discrimination and is destroyed — guard the eye, chant the holy names.
Practical Applications
  • Notice the first glance today — the moment rūpa hooks the mind through a body, an image, or a screen — and withdraw the senses before a second look fuses you to it.
  • Replace the pornographic and lust-provoking inputs in your day (feeds, shows, ads) with audible chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa, so the same senses that were burning fuel the soul instead.
  • Refuse the modern myth that sexual indulgence is harmless adult freedom — name it for what the verse calls it: the moth's last flight into the fire.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I the moth — circling something I know will destroy my intelligence, telling myself one more pass is safe?
  • Which sense gate (seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching) most reliably loots my discrimination, and what sādhana have I been avoiding that would close it?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your phone tonight — delete or mute the two accounts you follow mainly because the person is attractive; the moth doesn't have to land in the fire, it dies from circling it.
  • Pick a specific high-temptation slot in your week (a party, a late-night scroll, a particular group chat) and pre-decide a chanting or association alternative before the moment arrives, not during it.
  • Talk honestly with one trusted devotee friend about what your eyes have been doing this week — secrecy is the fuel the verse warns about; daylight starves it.
householder
  • Protect the marriage by treating outside lusty inputs — coworker flirtation, suggestive shows watched 'together,' algorithmic feeds — as the moth's flame, not as harmless background, and remove them from the home today.
  • Model guarded senses in front of your children: what you watch, comment on, and laugh at trains their dṛṣṭi more than any lecture on chastity ever will.
  • Use a portion of household income deliberately for sādhu-saṅga, devotional books, and temple service — redirecting the budget that the world expects you to spend on indulgence into the chanting culture the purport calls the rescue.
senior
  • Recognize that the moth's pull does not retire with the body — subtler forms (nostalgic fantasies, online wandering, prestige-seeking) still scorch the intelligence; chant aloud to keep them named and small.
  • Mentor a younger person honestly about the costs you paid for indulgences your generation called normal, so they hear the verse from a witness rather than a stranger.
  • Loosen your grip on ornaments — possessions, photographs, status objects you have decorated your life with — by giving at least one away each week to a devotee or service, training the dṛṣṭi the verse asks for before the body forces the lesson.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as anti-woman rather than as a diagnosis of one's own pralobhita-ātmā — the mind seduced by māyā-racita forms, of which a decorated body is only the standard example.
  • Treating lust as a private weakness to white-knuckle through, instead of a fire that requires the active medicine of chanting and association the purport prescribes.
  • Congratulating oneself on outward celibacy while feeding the eyes and imagination all day — the moth doesn't have to land in the flame to already be circling it.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one concrete input you consume daily that you suspect is the 'golden ornament' for you — and whether you are willing, in this room, to commit to cutting it.
  • The purport names modern pornography industries explicitly. Where do group members disagree about how strict a Gauḍīya practitioner should be with mainstream films, music, and social media?
  • Prabhupāda says women too are preyed upon by these industries — how does this verse read when the lusty seer and the decorated object are not split along gender lines our culture assumes?
Role Play

A college-age student tells their older friend: 'I scroll reels for an hour every night before bed — it's just relaxation, everyone does it, and I still chant my rounds in the morning.' One student plays the scroller, defending the habit; another plays the friend who has read SB 11.8.8 and must respond without moralizing, using the moth-and-flame image and the chanting remedy from the purport. Switch roles after five minutes.

Objections & Responses
  • This verse is patriarchal — it blames women for men's lust and treats female beauty as a trap.
    The verse is diagnosing the deluded seer, not the seen. The 'fault' named is naṣṭa-dṛṣṭiḥ — lost vision — in the one who looks with upabhoga-buddhi. Prabhupāda himself extends the same warning to women preyed upon by the industries of lust. The teaching is symmetrical: any soul that takes a māyā-decorated form as the object of enjoyment burns.
  • Sexual attraction is biology — calling it the moth's death is religious exaggeration in an adult, consenting world.
    Biology is exactly the point — the moth's pull to the flame is also biology, and it still kills him. The verse is not denying the force; it is naming where the force leads when intelligence (dṛṣṭi) is gone. A Gauḍīya teacher does not ask you to feel nothing, but to keep your discrimination intact and to take shelter of the holy name, which is the only thing strong enough to redirect that fire toward Kṛṣṇa.
