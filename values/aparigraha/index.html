Aparigraha (Non-possessiveness)

अपरिग्रहaparigraha[ah-pah-ree-grah-ha]
Vinaya (Inner Character)Core Value (depth 1)

Aparigraha is the spiritual discipline of accepting only what is necessary for one's maintenance while remaining free from attachment to material possessions and the desire to accumulate beyond need. It represents the understanding that true wealth lies in spiritual realization rather than material accumulation, and that excessive possessions create bondage that hinders devotional service to Krishna.

Also called: non-possessivenessnon-attachment to possessionsfreedom from greedcontentment with necessities

Layers of meaning

  1. Basic: Taking only what you need and not being greedy
  2. Intermediate: Freedom from attachment to material things while using them in Krishna's service
  3. Advanced: Recognition that all possessions belong to Krishna and we are merely trustees

What this value is NOT

Lobha (Greed)
Source: Gita 16.21 - gateway to hell

Insatiable desire to accumulate possessions beyond necessity

Key distinguisher: Driven by tamasika mode - endless wanting versus contentment with what Krishna provides
Parigrahishilata (Possessiveness)
Source: Vedic tradition

Attachment to things as 'mine' and fear of losing possessions

Key distinguisher: False proprietorship versus understanding Krishna as the ultimate owner
Vishaya-trishna (Material Thirst)
Source: Bhagavatam tradition

Constant craving for more material enjoyment and comfort

Key distinguisher: Rajasika restlessness versus sattvika satisfaction in spiritual pursuits
Sangraha-buddhi (Hoarding Mentality)
Source: Dharmic literature

Collecting and storing far beyond present or future needs

Key distinguisher: Anxiety-driven accumulation versus trust in Krishna's provision
