Dhriti (Resilience / Steadfastness / Fortitude)

धृतिःdhrtih[DHRI-ti (the 'dh' is aspirated; 'i' as in 'it')]
Vinaya branch (Inner Character)Root Value (depth 0)

Dhriti is the quality of mind and spirit that allows a person to sustain wholesome action — in thought, word, and deed — through difficulty, opposition, failure, and the passage of time, without losing either purpose or character. In the Gaudiya tradition, dhriti at its highest is grounded not in personal willpower alone but in connection to purpose larger than oneself — whether that be devotion, duty to family and community, or dharma.

Layers of meaning

  1. Dharana — sustaining the right engagement of the senses and vital force
  2. Niscaya — the inner conviction that does not waver under pressure
  3. Seva-bhava — continuing one's duty regardless of whether results are immediately visible

What this value is NOT

Stubbornness (Tamasiki Dhriti)
Source: BG 18.35

Holding fast to wrong understanding, bad habits, or harmful behaviour without discernment.

Key distinguisher: Dhriti is in alignment with viveka (discernment); stubbornness refuses discernment.
Emotional Suppression
Source: General misuse

Pretending not to feel pain, loss, or fear. Dhriti does not require elimination of emotion — Arjuna weeps, Yudhishthira grieves. It requires that emotion does not cause abandonment of duty.

Key distinguisher: Dhriti allows feeling while sustaining action; emotional suppression denies feeling entirely.
Reckless Endurance
Source: General misuse

Continuing despite clear evidence of harm. True dhriti is paired with prajna (wisdom) — one endures what must be endured, not all suffering indiscriminately.

Key distinguisher: True dhriti is paired with prajna (wisdom); reckless endurance lacks discernment about what should be endured.
Anxiety-Driven Striving (Rajasiki Dhriti)
Source: BG 18.34

Holding fast to action driven by attachment to results, wealth, or honour. Sattviki dhriti is steady regardless of outcome; rajasiki dhriti collapses when expected results fail.

Key distinguisher: Sattviki dhriti is steady regardless of outcome; rajasiki dhriti collapses when expected results fail.
Passivity / Resignation
Source: Opposite extreme

Accepting adverse circumstances without effort, justified as 'surrender' or 'detachment.' True surrender (saranagati) includes the effort within one's power.

Key distinguisher: Dhriti involves active, purposeful effort; passivity abandons effort under the guise of acceptance.
