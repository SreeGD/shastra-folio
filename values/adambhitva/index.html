Adambhitva (Non-hypocrisy / Authenticity)

अदम्भित्वadambhitva[a-DAMBH-it-va]
Vinaya (Inner Character)Primary Value (depth 1)

Adambhitva is the refusal to feign virtue one does not actually possess. It demands alignment between one's inner state and outer presentation — being the same person in private as in public. In a tradition that values saintly qualities, the temptation to perform spirituality for social approval is ever-present; adambhitva is the antidote, insisting that authenticity before God matters more than reputation before people. It is the foundation of spiritual integrity without which all other virtues become hollow display.

Layers of meaning

  1. Refusal to present oneself as more spiritually advanced or virtuous than one actually is
  2. Consistency between private conduct and public persona — inner-outer alignment
  3. Valuing genuine progress over social recognition and spiritual reputation
  4. Courage to be honest about one's limitations and struggles on the spiritual path

What this value is NOT

Dambha (Hypocrisy/Pretension)
Source: Bhagavad Gita 16.4

Deliberately projecting an image of virtue, learning, or devotion that one does not genuinely possess

Key distinguisher: Consciously constructs a false spiritual persona for social advantage
Performative Piety
Source: Vedic ethical tradition

Practicing spiritual disciplines primarily to be seen by others rather than for inner transformation

Key distinguisher: External displays of devotion driven by desire for recognition rather than love of God
Spiritual Gaslighting
Source: Psychological analysis

Using spiritual language and posturing to manipulate, control, or shame others while hiding one's own failings

Key distinguisher: Weaponizes the appearance of holiness to maintain power over others
Imposter Complacency
Source: Behavioral analysis

Becoming comfortable in a false persona rather than doing the hard work of genuine transformation

Key distinguisher: Settles for the reputation of virtue rather than pursuing its reality
