Yajña (Sacrifice / Spirit of Offering)

यज्ञyajña[yag-nyah]
KarmaPrimary Value (depth 1)

The spirit of offering one's actions, possessions, and self for a higher purpose. Yajña transforms ordinary action into sacred service by dedicating the fruits to the Divine and to the welfare of all beings. It is the principle that sustains cosmic order (ṛta) through reciprocal giving.

Also called: SacrificeSpirit of OfferingSacred ServiceSelfless Dedication

Layers of meaning

  1. Literal: the fire offering (agnihotra) — dedicating material substance into sacred fire as an act of worship
  2. Ethical: selfless dedication of one's efforts and resources for the welfare of others without expectation of return
  3. Philosophical: cosmic reciprocity — the cycle of mutual sustenance between beings and the Divine (BG 3.14-16)
  4. Transcendental: all action as worship — seeing every act as an offering to Brahman (BG 4.24)

What this value is NOT

Svārtha-karma (Selfish Action)
Source: Bhagavad Gita karma-yoga analysis

Acting purely for personal gain without any spirit of offering or consideration for the welfare of others

Key distinguisher: Motivated entirely by self-interest rather than service or reciprocity
Karma-phala-āsakti (Attachment to Results)
Source: Bhagavad Gita karma-yoga analysis

Performing actions with sole focus on personal rewards, unable to release the fruits of one's work

Key distinguisher: Clings to outcomes rather than offering them to a higher purpose
Yajña-tyāga (Abandoning Sacred Duty)
Source: Bhagavad Gita on tyāga and sannyāsa

Refusing to participate in communal or sacrificial obligations, withdrawing from one's duty to contribute

Key distinguisher: Neglects the reciprocal duty that sustains social and cosmic order
Dambha-yajña (Hypocritical Sacrifice)
Source: BG 16.17

Performing sacrifice for show, social recognition, or pride rather than with genuine devotion and selflessness

Key distinguisher: Outward ritual without inner surrender — driven by ego rather than offering
