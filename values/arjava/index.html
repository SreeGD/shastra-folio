Arjava (Simplicity / Straightforwardness)

आर्जवārjava[AAR-ja-va]
Dharma (Right Conduct)Core Value (depth 1)

Ārjava is the divine quality of straightforwardness in thought, word, and deed, characterized by complete absence of duplicity or hidden agendas. It manifests as natural honesty that flows from inner purity, where one's external expression perfectly aligns with internal reality. This quality enables authentic relationships and spiritual progress by removing the barriers of pretense and deception that cloud consciousness.

Layers of meaning

  1. Behavioral: Consistency between inner thoughts and outer expression
  2. Psychological: Absence of duplicity, pretense, or hidden motives
  3. Spiritual: Transparency that allows divine grace to flow unobstructed
  4. Social: Foundation for trustworthy relationships and community harmony

Developmental specs by audience

Primary Concrete operational - rule-based honesty
Observable behaviors
  • Tells the truth even when it might result in consequences
  • Admits mistakes without being prompted
  • Asks direct questions when confused rather than pretending to understand
  • Shares toys and materials without hiding or hoarding
  • Expresses genuine emotions rather than putting on false faces
Language cues
  • Uses simple, direct statements
  • Says 'I don't know' when uncertain
  • Asks 'What does that mean?' when confused
  • Uses 'I made a mistake' readily
Story hooks
  • Characters who tell the truth despite fear
  • Simple situations where honesty leads to better outcomes
  • Stories contrasting honest and dishonest characters
Anti-pattern

Telling lies to avoid trouble or get what they want

What this value is NOT

Māyāchāra (Deceptive Conduct)
Source: Gita 16.4 (āsurī sampat)

Deliberate deception, manipulation, or presenting false appearances to gain advantage

Key distinguisher: Intentional misrepresentation versus honest transparency
Dambha (Hypocrisy)
Source: Gita 16.4, 18.28 (tamasika nature)

Pretending to possess virtues or knowledge one lacks, especially in spiritual matters

Key distinguisher: False spiritual display versus genuine simplicity
Kūṭa-buddhi (Crooked Intelligence)
Source: Vedic literature

Using intelligence to devise complex schemes for personal gain at others' expense

Key distinguisher: Manipulative cleverness versus straightforward wisdom
Śāṭhya (Cunning)
Source: Classical texts

Strategic dishonesty and calculated deception in dealings with others

Key distinguisher: Calculated manipulation versus natural honesty
