Satya (Truthfulness)

सत्यsatya[SAT-ya]
Dharma (Right Conduct)Root Value (depth 0)

Satya is the divine quality of truthfulness that encompasses honesty in speech, authenticity in action, and alignment with ultimate reality. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, satya represents not merely factual accuracy but the deeper truth of one's relationship with Krishna and the commitment to live according to dharmic principles. It is the foundation upon which all other virtues rest, as Lord Krishna declares it among the divine qualities that lead to liberation.

Layers of meaning

  1. Factual honesty in speech and action
  2. Authenticity of character and intention
  3. Alignment with dharmic principles
  4. Recognition of ultimate spiritual reality
  5. Foundation for all other virtues

What this value is NOT

Anrita (Falsehood)
Source: Vedic literature

Deliberate deception through false speech or misleading actions

Key distinguisher: Intentional distortion of reality for personal gain
Tamasika Speech
Source: Bhagavad Gita mode analysis

Words that are harsh, hurtful, or create confusion and ignorance

Key distinguisher: Speech that increases darkness and delusion rather than clarity
Diplomatic Manipulation
Source: Dharmic analysis

Using partial truths or clever wording to mislead while technically avoiding lies

Key distinguisher: Maintaining technical honesty while violating the spirit of truthfulness
Self-Deception
Source: Spiritual psychology

Refusing to acknowledge one's own faults or spiritual condition

Key distinguisher: Internal dishonesty that prevents genuine spiritual progress
Maya-vada (Illusory thinking)
Source: Gita commentary tradition

Living in denial of spiritual reality, considering material existence as the ultimate truth

Key distinguisher: Material consciousness versus Krishna consciousness as ultimate reality
Dambha (Hypocrisy)
Source: BG 16.4 (asuric qualities)

Pretending to be virtuous while acting otherwise, maintaining false appearances

Key distinguisher: Authentic character alignment versus deceptive presentation
Kaitava (Duplicity)
Source: Srimad Bhagavatam

Double-dealing, saying one thing while meaning another, manipulative communication

Key distinguisher: Straightforward honesty versus calculated manipulation
