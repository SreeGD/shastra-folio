Nyaya (Justice / Righteous Judgment)

न्यायnyāya[NYAA-ya]
Viveka (Wisdom)Primary Value (depth 1)

Nyaya is the active application of dharmic principles to ensure fairness and right order in social relations. It encompasses the wisdom to judge rightly, the courage to uphold dharma in the face of power, and the compassion to ensure that the vulnerable are not trampled by the strong. True nyaya, as exemplified by Rama Rajya, creates conditions where dharma flourishes and every being receives what is due according to their nature and actions.

Layers of meaning

  1. The discernment to evaluate situations according to dharmic principles rather than personal bias
  2. The courage to uphold righteous judgment even against the powerful
  3. The wisdom to balance strict law with compassionate understanding of context
  4. The creation of social conditions where dharma is protected and adharma is checked

What this value is NOT

Mob Justice
Source: Vedic wisdom tradition

Collective punishment driven by anger and herd mentality without proper inquiry or due process

Key distinguisher: Driven by collective rage rather than dharmic deliberation
Partiality and Favoritism
Source: Mahabharata (Dhritarashtra's bias toward Duryodhana)

Rendering judgment based on personal attachment, kinship, or self-interest rather than dharmic merit

Key distinguisher: Attachment and bias corrupt the judgment rather than dharma guiding it
Judgment Without Hearing
Source: Mahabharata (the sabha scene)

Punishing or condemning without allowing the accused to present their side or examining full evidence

Key distinguisher: Lacks the patience and fairness required for righteous judgment
Apathy Toward Injustice
Source: Mahabharata (silence of elders in the sabha)

Witnessing injustice and choosing not to act due to fear, comfort, or indifference

Key distinguisher: Abandons the duty to uphold dharma through passive complicity
