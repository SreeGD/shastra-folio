Ahimsa (Non-violence / Compassion)

अहिंसाahiṃsā[ah-HIM-sa]
Dharma (Right Conduct)Root Value (depth 0)

Ahimsa is the fundamental principle of non-violence that extends beyond physical harm to encompass mental, emotional, and spiritual non-injury to all living beings. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, it represents the natural expression of seeing the divine presence in all creatures, manifesting as compassionate action that protects and nurtures life. This divine quality emerges from understanding our spiritual interconnectedness and recognizing that harming others ultimately harms our own spiritual progress.

Layers of meaning

  1. Physical non-violence - refraining from causing bodily harm
  2. Mental non-violence - avoiding harsh thoughts and judgments
  3. Emotional non-violence - speaking and acting with kindness
  4. Spiritual non-violence - honoring the divine presence in all beings
  5. Environmental non-violence - protecting and nurturing nature
  6. Self non-violence - treating oneself with compassion and care

What this value is NOT

Tamasika Violence
Source: Bhagavad Gita modes of nature

Violence born from ignorance, delusion, and laziness - harming others without purpose or awareness

Key distinguisher: Unconscious, purposeless harm driven by ignorance and mental dullness
Rajasika Aggression
Source: Bhagavad Gita modes of nature

Violence motivated by passion, desire for control, and ego-driven competition

Key distinguisher: Conscious harm driven by selfish desires and the need to dominate
Cruel Indifference
Source: Gaudiya Vaishnava teachings

Callous disregard for the suffering of others, treating living beings as mere objects

Key distinguisher: Emotional disconnection that allows harm through neglect and apathy
Righteous Violence
Source: Misunderstanding of dharmic duty

Using spiritual or moral justifications to cause harm, missing the deeper principle of compassion

Key distinguisher: Intellectual justification that bypasses the heart's natural compassion
