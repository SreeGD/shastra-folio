Daya (Compassion / Mercy)

दयाdayā[dah-YAH]
Prema (Love)Core Value (depth 1)

Daya is the divine quality of compassion that naturally arises when one sees the suffering of others and feels moved to alleviate it. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, true compassion flows from understanding the spiritual nature of all beings as parts of Krishna, making kindness toward others an expression of devotion to the Supreme.

Layers of meaning

  1. Emotional response to others' suffering
  2. Active desire to help and heal
  3. Recognition of spiritual kinship with all beings
  4. Expression of divine love through service

What this value is NOT

Krurya (Cruelty)
Source: Gita 16.4 - asuric qualities

Deliberate infliction of pain or suffering on others

Key distinguisher: Takes pleasure in others' pain vs. feeling moved to help
Udaseenata (Indifference)
Source: Tamasic mode of ignorance

Complete lack of concern for others' wellbeing

Key distinguisher: Emotional numbness vs. active caring response
Kripanata (Miserliness of heart)
Source: Rajasic attachment

Withholding help due to selfishness or fear of loss

Key distinguisher: Self-centered calculation vs. spontaneous giving
Ahimsa-abhava (Absence of non-violence)
Source: Opposite of ahimsa principle

Causing harm through action or negligence

Key distinguisher: Harmful intent vs. protective care
