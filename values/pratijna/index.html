Pratijna (Vow-keeping / Sacred Resolve)

प्रतिज्ञाpratijñā[pra-TIG-nyaa]
Karma (Action)Primary Value (depth 1)

Pratijña is the making and keeping of sacred promises rooted in dharmic conviction. It represents the self-imposed commitment to uphold a righteous vow regardless of personal cost, as exemplified by Bhishma's terrible vow that earned him his very name. True pratijña springs not from impulse but from deep alignment with dharma, transforming spoken words into an unbreakable bond between the soul and its highest purpose.

Layers of meaning

  1. The sacred act of making a dharmic commitment with full awareness of its weight
  2. Unwavering adherence to one's pledged word through all obstacles and temptations
  3. The inner strength that transforms a spoken promise into an unbreakable spiritual bond
  4. Alignment of personal will with dharmic duty, making the vow an expression of one's highest nature

What this value is NOT

Rash Vow-making
Source: Mahabharata tradition

Making vows impulsively without understanding their full consequences or dharmic implications

Key distinguisher: Driven by momentary emotion rather than deliberate dharmic conviction
Convenience-based Promise-breaking
Source: Vedic wisdom tradition

Abandoning commitments when they become personally inconvenient or costly

Key distinguisher: Places personal comfort above the sanctity of one's given word
Ego-driven Vows
Source: Mahabharata (various examples)

Making vows to demonstrate superiority, gain fame, or assert dominance over others

Key distinguisher: Motivated by pride and self-aggrandizement rather than dharmic purpose
Binding Others with Vows
Source: Vedic wisdom tradition

Imposing vows or obligations upon others without their free consent, using one's authority to coerce commitment

Key distinguisher: Violates others' autonomy and corrupts the voluntary nature of sacred resolve
