Ahimsa (Non-violence / Compassion) ahiṃsā

Ahimsa is the fundamental principle of non-violence that extends beyond physical harm to encompass mental, emotional, and spiritual non-injury to all living beings. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, it represents the natural expression of seeing the divine presence in all creatures, manifesting as compassionate action that protects and nurtures life. This divine quality emerges from understanding our spiritual interconnectedness and recognizing that harming others ultimately harms our own spiritual progress.

Senior Formal operational - abstract thinking and philosophical reasoning
You'll notice: Advocates for non-violent solutions in complex situations; Demonstrates emotional regulation under pressure
Watch for: Passive aggression, systemic participation in harmful structures, intellectual violence
College Advanced formal operational - sophisticated abstract reasoning and integration
You'll notice: Integrates ahimsa principles into career and life choices; Demonstrates nuanced understanding of when force may be necessary for protection
Watch for: Intellectual justification of harm, spiritual bypassing of necessary action, privileged non-engagement
Common anti-patterns
  • Tamasika Violence — Unconscious, purposeless harm driven by ignorance and mental dullness
  • Rajasika Aggression — Conscious harm driven by selfish desires and the need to dominate

Asteya (Non-stealing / Honesty) asteya

Asteya is the divine quality of complete honesty and integrity, encompassing not only refraining from taking what belongs to others but also avoiding the misuse of time, resources, or trust. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, asteya reflects the soul's natural inclination toward dharmic conduct when consciousness is purified of material desires. This virtue manifests as contentment with what Krishna provides and the understanding that all belongs ultimately to the Supreme Lord.

Senior Formal operational - abstract moral reasoning
You'll notice: Demonstrates intellectual honesty in academic work; Acknowledges sources and influences transparently
Watch for: Academic dishonesty, materialism, claiming false credentials
College Post-formal - integrated spiritual understanding
You'll notice: Integrates asteya into professional and personal ethics; Recognizes Krishna as the ultimate proprietor in daily life
Watch for: Professional dishonesty, spiritual materialism, false ego in service
Common anti-patterns
  • Tamasic Appropriation — Lacks awareness of wrongdoing, driven by inertia and delusion
  • Rajasic Acquisition — Actively schemes and competes to obtain what others have

Satya (Truthfulness) satya

Satya is the divine quality of truthfulness that encompasses honesty in speech, authenticity in action, and alignment with ultimate reality. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, satya represents not merely factual accuracy but the deeper truth of one's relationship with Krishna and the commitment to live according to dharmic principles. It is the foundation upon which all other virtues rest, as Lord Krishna declares it among the divine qualities that lead to liberation.

Senior Formal operational - abstract moral reasoning
You'll notice: Maintains truthfulness even when facing significant personal cost; Distinguishes between different levels of truth in complex situations
Watch for: Rationalized dishonesty for 'greater good' or ideological purposes
College Post-formal - integration of multiple perspectives
You'll notice: Integrates truthfulness with wisdom and compassion in complex situations; Recognizes and articulates different levels of truth (empirical, moral, spiritual)
Watch for: Academic dishonesty or spiritual bypassing through selective truth-telling
Common anti-patterns
  • Anrita (Falsehood) — Intentional distortion of reality for personal gain
  • Tamasika Speech — Speech that increases darkness and delusion rather than clarity

Bhakti (Devotion) bhakti

Bhakti is the topmost yoga system characterized by pure loving devotional service to Krishna, performed without material desires or expectations. It transforms all activities into spiritual service through surrender and love, providing the most direct path to spiritual realization and eternal happiness.

It transcends material attachment and manifests as spontaneous, causeless affection that seeks only the beloved's happiness without any expectation of return. This pure love represents the soul's natural constitutional position and the ultimate goal of spiritual development.

Tapas (Austerity / Discipline) tapas

Tapas is the disciplined practice of voluntary self-restraint and austerity undertaken to purify consciousness and develop spiritual strength. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, it represents the willing acceptance of difficulty for spiritual advancement, transforming the body, mind, and speech through regulated practices. True tapas is performed without pride or material desire, as an offering to Krishna for the cultivation of devotional service.

Dhriti (Resilience / Steadfastness / Fortitude) dhrtih

Dhriti is the quality of mind and spirit that allows a person to sustain wholesome action — in thought, word, and deed — through difficulty, opposition, failure, and the passage of time, without losing either purpose or character. In the Gaudiya tradition, dhriti at its highest is grounded not in personal willpower alone but in connection to purpose larger than oneself — whether that be devotion, duty to family and community, or dharma.

Senior Formal operations / Conventional to post-conventional morality. Abstract reasoning fully available. Identity formation is the central task.
You'll notice: Maintains character under competitive pressure; Holds to a value decision when it has social or material cost
Watch for: Achievement-driven over-striving leading to burnout (rajasiki dhriti). Rigid adherence to a failing plan. Emotional stoicism disconnecting from peers. Engage with genuine philosophical sophistication.
College Post-formal / Integrative. Systemic thinking, cross-tradition synthesis. Task: identity and purpose integration.
You'll notice: Sustains spiritual practice in unsupportive environments; Maintains ethical commitments in competitive contexts
Watch for: Burnout presented as spiritual discipline. Reckless endurance in harmful relationships. Confusing dhriti with inability to adapt or surrender.
Common anti-patterns
  • Stubbornness (Tamasiki Dhriti) — Dhriti is in alignment with viveka (discernment); stubbornness refuses discernment.
  • Emotional Suppression — Dhriti allows feeling while sustaining action; emotional suppression denies feeling entirely.

Kartavya is the sacred duty or prescribed action that one must perform according to one's position, capacity, and circumstances, as ordained by dharmic principles. It represents the understanding that certain actions are obligatory regardless of personal preference, arising from one's relationship with the Divine, society, and one's own spiritual development. True kartavya is performed with dedication and surrender to Krishna, transforming mundane obligations into acts of devotion.
