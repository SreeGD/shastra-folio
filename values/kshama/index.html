Kshama (Forgiveness / Patience)

क्षमाkṣamā[ksha-MAA]
Vinaya (Inner Character)Core Value (depth 1)

Kshama is the divine quality of forbearance and forgiveness that allows one to remain undisturbed by others' offenses while maintaining compassion for the offender. It represents the spiritual strength to endure difficulties without retaliation, seeing all circumstances as Krishna's mercy for one's purification. This quality transforms potential anger into opportunities for spiritual growth and deeper surrender to the Supreme Lord.

Layers of meaning

  1. Emotional regulation in face of provocation
  2. Spiritual perspective on suffering as purification
  3. Compassionate understanding of others' limitations
  4. Surrender to Krishna's will in all circumstances

What this value is NOT

Krodha (Anger)
Source: Bhagavad Gita 2.62-63

Uncontrolled anger that destroys discrimination and leads to delusion

Key distinguisher: Kshama transforms provocation into spiritual opportunity while krodha destroys wisdom
Pratishodha (Revenge)
Source: Tamasika tendency

The desire to retaliate and cause harm to those who have wronged us

Key distinguisher: Kshama seeks the spiritual welfare of the offender while revenge seeks their harm
Akshama (Impatience)
Source: Rajasika mode

Inability to tolerate delay, difficulty, or opposition

Key distinguisher: Kshama accepts Krishna's timing while impatience demands immediate gratification
Abhimana (False Pride)
Source: Material ego

Taking offense due to wounded ego and sense of superiority

Key distinguisher: Kshama comes from humility while false pride creates the need to defend one's position
