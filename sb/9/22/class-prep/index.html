Chapter 22 · Class prep

BG 22.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O King, the son of Divodāsa was Mitrāyu, and from Mitrāyu came four sons, named Cyavana, Sudāsa, Sahadeva and Somaka. Somaka was the father of Jantu.

Essence. From Mitrāyu come four sons — Cyavana, Sudāsa, Sahadeva, Somaka — and from Somaka, Jantu. Each name in the list once held a destiny we will not learn; the patient counting is a discipline of attention.

BG 22.2

Somaka had one hundred sons, of whom the youngest was Pṛṣata. From Pṛṣata was born King Drupada, who was opulent in all supremacy.

Essence. Somaka has a hundred sons, of whom the youngest Pṛṣata fathers Drupada. The future of a great line can be carried by the most overlooked of its many sons.

BG 22.3

From Mahārāja Drupada, Draupadī was born. Mahārāja Drupada also had many sons, headed by Dhṛṣṭadyumna. From Dhṛṣṭadyumna came a son named Dhṛṣṭaketu. All these personalities are known as descendants of Bharmyāśva or as the dynasty of Pāṣcāla.

Essence. From Drupada come Draupadī and Dhṛṣṭadyumna — characters whose fame would define the Mahābhārata. Bhāgavatam puts famous figures in their dynastic place; their drama is not their identity but a chapter in a longer line.

BG 22.4-5

Another son of Ajamīḍha was known as Ṛkṣa. From Ṛkṣa came a son named Saṁvaraṇa, and from Saṁvaraṇa through the womb of his wife, Tapatī, the daughter of the sun-god, came Kuru, the King of Kurukṣetra. Kuru had four sons — Parīkṣi, Sudhanu, Jahnu and Niṣadha. From Sudhanu, Suhotra was born, and from Suhotra, Cyavana. From Cyavana, Kṛtī was born.

Essence. From Saṁvaraṇa and Tapatī (daughter of the sun) is born Kuru, king of Kurukṣetra. Sacred geography is named after persons; the earth itself carries the memory of those who walked it.

BG 22.6

The son of Kṛtī was Uparicara Vasu, and among his sons, headed by Bṛhadratha, were Kuśāmba, Matsya, Pratyagra and Cedipa. All the sons of Uparicara Vasu became rulers of the Cedi state.

Essence. Uparicara Vasu's sons rule the Cedi state — Bṛhadratha, Kuśāmba, Matsya, Pratyagra, Cedipa. One ancestor can seed an entire ruling family across a region; the multiplication of influence often hides a single source.

BG 22.7

From Bṛhadratha, Kuśāgra was born; from Kuśāgra, Ṛṣabha; and from Ṛṣabha, Satyahita. The son of Satyahita was Puṣpavān, and the son of Puṣpavān was Jahu.

Essence. From Bṛhadratha come Kuśāgra, Ṛṣabha, Satyahita, Puṣpavān, Jahu. The dynasty marches forward through quiet generations until the next eventful figure.

BG 22.8

Through the womb of another wife, Bṛhadratha begot two halves of a son. When the mother saw those two halves she rejected them, but later a she-demon named Jarā playfully joined them and said, “Come to life, come to life!” Thus the son named Jarāsandha was born.

Essence. Bṛhadratha begets a son in two halves; the mother rejects them, but the she-demon Jarā playfully joins them and Jarāsandha is born. Sometimes the most dangerous future figures are reassembled by mercy a mother could not extend.

BG 22.9

From Jarāsandha came a son named Sahadeva; from Sahadeva, Somāpi; and from Somāpi, Śrutaśravā. The son of Kuru called Parīkṣi had no sons, but the son of Kuru called Jahnu had a son named Suratha.

Essence. From Jarāsandha come Sahadeva, Somāpi, Śrutaśravā. Even great enemies of the Lord's plans produce descendants who go on to live ordinary lives — destiny does not necessarily extend through generations.

BG 22.10

From Suratha came a son named Vidūratha, from whom Sārvabhauma was born. From Sārvabhauma came Jayasena; from Jayasena, Rādhika; and from Rādhika, Ayutāyu.

Essence. Suratha, Vidūratha, Sārvabhauma, Jayasena, Rādhika, Ayutāyu — yet another patient stretch. The Bhāgavatam keeps tally; it does not skip a generation just because it lacks drama.

BG 22.11

From Ayutāyu came a son named Akrodhana, and his son was Devātithi. The son of Devātithi was Ṛkṣa, the son of Ṛkṣa was Dilīpa, and the son of Dilīpa was Pratīpa.

Essence. Akrodhana, Devātithi, Ṛkṣa, Dilīpa, Pratīpa — leading up to a familiar name. Sometimes the only purpose of an ancestor is to be the bridge that finally produces the soul scripture is waiting for.

BG 22.12-13

The sons of Pratīpa were Devāpi, Śāntanu and Bāhlīka. Devāpi left the kingdom of his father and went to the forest, and therefore Śāntanu became the king. Śāntanu, who in his previous birth was known as Mahābhiṣa, had the ability to transform anyone from old age to youth simply by touching that person with his hands.

Essence. Pratīpa's sons are Devāpi, Śāntanu, Bāhlīka. Devāpi leaves for the forest; Śāntanu rules and can turn anyone from old age to youth by touch. Even within one family, paths radically diverge — one to forest, one to throne.

BG 22.14-15

Because the King was able to make everyone happy for sense gratification, primarily by the touch of his hand, his name was Śāntanu. Once, when there was no rainfall in the kingdom for twelve years and the King consulted his learned brahminical advisors, they said, “You are faulty for enjoying the property of your elder brother. For the elevation of your kingdom and home, you should return the kingdom to him.”

Essence. Twelve years of drought are traced by brāhmaṇas to Śāntanu's parivettā position — enjoying the kingdom while the elder brother is still alive. Some catastrophes in our lives are quietly traceable to a structural offense we did not name.

BG 22.16-17

When the brāhmaṇas said this, Mahārāja Śāntanu went to the forest and requested his elder brother Devāpi to take charge of the kingdom, for it is the duty of a king to maintain his subjects. Previously, however, Śāntanu’s minister Aśvavāra had instigated some brāhmaṇas to induce Devāpi to transgress the injunctions of the Vedas and thus make himself unfit for the post of ruler. The brāhmaṇas deviated Devāpi from the path of the Vedic principles, and therefore when asked by Śāntanu he did not agree to accept the post of ruler. On the contrary, he blasphemed the Vedic principles and therefore became fallen. Under the circumstances, Śāntanu again became the king, and Indra, being pleased, showered rains. Devāpi later took to the path of mystic yoga to control his mind and senses and went to the village named Kalāpagrāma, where he is still living.

Essence. Śāntanu's minister had earlier instigated brāhmaṇas to deviate Devāpi from Vedic principles, disqualifying him for kingship. Power-loving advisors quietly engineer disqualifications of legitimate claimants — and we should be alert to them in our own decisions.

BG 22.18-19

After the dynasty of the moon-god comes to an end in this Age of Kali, Devāpi, in the beginning of the next Satya-yuga, will reestablish the Soma dynasty in this world. From Bāhlīka [the brother of Śāntanu] came a son named Somadatta, who had three sons, named Bhūri, Bhūriśravā and Śala. From Śāntanu, through the womb of his wife named Gaṅgā, came Bhīṣma, the exalted, self-realized devotee and learned scholar.

Essence. Devāpi still lives in Kalāpagrāma and will revive the moon dynasty in the next Satya-yuga. Bhīṣma is born from Śāntanu through Gaṅgā. Long-range plans of the Lord place both future-restorers and present-heroes within the same family.

BG 22.20

Bhīṣmadeva was the foremost of all warriors. When he defeated Lord Paraśurāma in a fight, Lord Paraśurāma was very satisfied with him. By the semen of Śāntanu in the womb of Satyavatī, the daughter of a fisherman, Citrāṅgada took birth.

Essence. Bhīṣma defeats Paraśurāma and Paraśurāma is pleased. Real warriors honor superior strength even when defeated by it; pride is not the same as warrior dignity.

BG 22.21-24

Citrāṅgada, of whom Vicitravīrya was the younger brother, was killed by a Gandharva who was also named Citrāṅgada. Satyavatī, before her marriage to Śāntanu, gave birth to the master authority of the Vedas, Vyāsadeva, known as Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, who was begotten by Parāśara Muni. From Vyāsadeva, I [Śukadeva Gosvāmī] was born, and from him I studied this great work of literature, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. The incarnation of Godhead Vedavyāsa, rejecting his disciples, headed by Paila, instructed Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam to me because I was free from all material desires. After Ambikā and Ambālikā, the two daughters of Kāśīrāja, were taken away by force, Vicitravīrya married them, but because he was too attached to these two wives, he had a heart attack and died of tuberculosis.

Essence. Citrāṅgada is killed by a Gandharva of the same name; Vyāsa, son of Satyavatī by Parāśara, fathers Śukadeva and teaches him the Bhāgavatam. The transmission of the very text we are reading is traced — we know whom to thank.

BG 22.25

Bādarāyaṇa, Śrī Vyāsadeva, following the order of his mother, Satyavatī, begot three sons, two by the womb of Ambikā and Ambālikā, the two wives of his brother Vicitravīrya, and the third by Vicitravīrya’s maidservant. These sons were Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu and Vidura.

Essence. By Satyavatī's order, Vyāsa begets Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, and Vidura through his brother's wives and maidservant. Even the central families of Mahābhārata are products of niyoga — bypass arrangements undertaken by orders of the mother.

BG 22.26

Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s wife, Gāndhārī, gave birth to one hundred sons and one daughter, O King. The oldest of the sons was Duryodhana, and the daughter’s name was Duḥśalā.

Essence. Gāndhārī gives birth to one hundred sons and one daughter — Duryodhana the eldest, Duḥśalā the daughter. Even cursed lineages can multiply staggeringly; numbers alone are no measure of righteousness.

BG 22.27-28

Pāṇḍu was restrained from sexual life because of having been cursed by a sage, and therefore his three sons Yudhiṣṭhira, Bhīma and Arjuna were begotten through the womb of his wife, Kuntī, by Dharmarāja, by the demigod controlling the wind, and by the demigod controlling the rain. Pāṇḍu’s second wife, Mādrī, gave birth to Nakula and Sahadeva, who were begotten by the two Aśvinī-kumāras. The five brothers, headed by Yudhiṣṭhira, begot five sons through the womb of Draupadī. These five sons were your uncles.

Essence. Cursed against sex, Pāṇḍu's three sons come through Kuntī by Dharma, Vāyu, and Indra; Mādrī bears Nakula and Sahadeva by the Aśvinīs. The Pāṇḍavas are children of demigods received through niyoga — even their bodies are not exactly what they seem.

BG 22.29

From Yudhiṣṭhira came a son named Prativindhya, from Bhīma a son named Śrutasena, from Arjuna a son named Śrutakīrti, and from Nakula a son named Śatānīka.

Essence. Prativindhya from Yudhiṣṭhira, Śrutasena from Bhīma, Śrutakīrti from Arjuna, Śatānīka from Nakula — the Upapāṇḍavas. Even the children of the Pāṇḍavas are listed as background — their dramatic killing in the Mahābhārata is not even noted here.

BG 22.30-31

O King, the son of Sahadeva was Śrutakarmā. Furthermore, Yudhiṣṭhira and his brothers begot other sons in other wives. Yudhiṣṭhira begot a son named Devaka through the womb of Pauravī, and Bhīmasena begot a son named Ghaṭotkaca through his wife Hiḍimbā and a son named Sarvagata through his wife Kālī. Similarly, Sahadeva had a son named Suhotra through his wife named Vijayā, who was the daughter of the king of the mountains.

Essence. Each Pāṇḍava has other wives and other sons — Pauravī, Hiḍimbā, Kālī, Vijayā. Even great devotees had complex marital lives, and Bhāgavatam does not airbrush — it lists.

BG 22.32

Nakula begot a son named Naramitra through his wife named Kareṇumatī. Similarly, Arjuna begot a son named Irāvān through his wife known as Ulupī, the daughter of the Nāgas, and a son named Babhruvāhana by the womb of the princess of Maṇipura. Babhruvāhana became the adopted son of the king of Maṇipura.

Essence. Arjuna's son Babhruvāhana, born of the princess of Maṇipura, becomes the adopted son of the king of Maṇipura. Sons of devotees sometimes get adopted into other thrones; we should not be possessive about lineages the Lord disperses.

BG 22.33

My dear King Parīkṣit, your father, Abhimanyu, was born from the womb of Subhadrā as the son of Arjuna. He was the conqueror of all atirathas [those who could fight with one thousand charioteers]. From him, by the womb of Uttarā, the daughter of Virāḍrāja, you were born.

Essence. Abhimanyu, Arjuna's son by Subhadrā, conquers atirathas; you, Parīkṣit, are born of him and Uttarā. Śukadeva turns the line directly into his listener's own personal history — sacred narration is meant to land in the listener's own body.

BG 22.34

After the Kuru dynasty was annihilated in the Battle of Kurukṣetra, you also were about to be destroyed by the brahmāstra atomic weapon released by the son of Droṇācārya, but by the mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, you were saved from death.

Essence. Parīkṣit was about to be destroyed in the womb by Aśvatthāmā's brahmāstra and was saved by Kṛṣṇa's mercy. Each of us was at some point saved from a destructive impact we were not aware of — and that mercy still claims our gratitude.

BG 22.35

My dear King, your four sons — Janamejaya, Śrutasena, Bhīmasena and Ugrasena — are very powerful. Janamejaya is the eldest.

Essence. Parīkṣit's four sons — Janamejaya, Śrutasena, Bhīmasena, Ugrasena — are named, with Janamejaya the eldest. Even kings who hear the Bhāgavatam at the end of life have sons who will carry on; bhakti at the end of life does not nullify ongoing dynastic responsibility.

BG 22.36

Because of your death by the Takṣaka snake, your son Janamejaya will be very angry and will perform a sacrifice to kill all the snakes in the world.

Essence. Janamejaya will be angry at his father's death by Takṣaka and perform a snake sacrifice. Even after a devoted death, descendants' grief can drive massive retaliatory actions; our exits leave wakes.

BG 22.37

After conquering throughout the world and after accepting Tura, the son of Kalaṣa, as his priest, Janamejaya will perform aśvamedha-yajṣas, for which he will be known as Turaga-medhaṣāṭ.

Essence. Janamejaya will conquer the world, accept Tura as priest, and perform aśvamedhas. Healthy grief eventually transmutes into constructive sacrifice rather than destructive vengeance.

BG 22.38

The son of Janamejaya known as Śatānīka will learn from Yājṣavalkya the three Vedas and the art of performing ritualistic ceremonies. He will also learn the military art from Kṛpācārya and the transcendental science from the sage Śaunaka.

Essence. Śatānīka will learn the Vedas from Yājṣavalkya, military arts from Kṛpa, and transcendental science from Śaunaka. A complete prince is trained in multiple specialties under multiple specialists — generalists are made of specialists.

BG 22.39

The son of Śatānīka will be Sahasrānīka, and from him will come the son named Aśvamedhaja. From Aśvamedhaja will come Asīmakṛṣṇa, and his son will be Nemicakra.

Essence. Sahasrānīka, Aśvamedhaja, Asīmakṛṣṇa, Nemicakra — names that mean 'thousand-army,' 'horse-sacrifice-born,' 'limitless-Kṛṣṇa,' 'wheel-rim.' Names carry the unfolding theology of the line.

BG 22.40

When the town of Hastināpura [New Delhi] is inundated by the river, Nemicakra will live in the place known as Kauśāmbī. His son will be celebrated as Citraratha, and the son of Citraratha will be Śuciratha.

Essence. When Hastināpura is inundated, Nemicakra will move to Kauśāmbī. Capital cities are not permanent; even great seats of governance fall to rivers, and the wise relocate without sentimentality.

BG 22.41

From Śuciratha will come the son named Vṛṣṭimān, and his son, Suṣeṇa, will be the emperor of the entire world. The son of Suṣeṇa will be Sunītha, his son will be Nṛcakṣu, and from Nṛcakṣu will come a son named Sukhīnala.

Essence. Citraratha, Śuciratha, Vṛṣṭimān, Suṣeṇa, Sunītha, Nṛcakṣu, Sukhīnala — kings continue to rule the world. The world has always had emperors, and they have always passed into list-entries.

BG 22.42

The son of Sukhīnala will be Pariplava, and his son will be Sunaya. From Sunaya will come a son named Medhāvī; from Medhāvī, Nṛpaṣjaya; from Nṛpaṣjaya, Dūrva; and from Dūrva, Timi.

Essence. Pariplava, Sunaya, Medhāvī, Nṛpaṣjaya, Dūrva, Timi — yet more names. The Bhāgavatam's patient inventory keeps reminding us that scriptural attention is not commensurate with human drama.

BG 22.43

From Timi will come Bṛhadratha; from Bṛhadratha, Sudāsa; and from Sudāsa, Śatānīka. From Śatānīka will come Durdamana, and from him will come a son named Mahīnara.

Essence. Bṛhadratha, Sudāsa, Śatānīka, Durdamana, Mahīnara — the line keeps coming. Names of the very ordinary continue to appear right next to names of the famous — both go on.

BG 22.44-45

The son of Mahīnara will be Daṇḍapāṇi, and his son will be Nimi, from whom King Kṣemaka will be born. I have now described to you the moon-god’s dynasty, which is the source of brāhmaṇas and kṣatriyas and is worshiped by demigods and great saints. In this Kali-yuga, Kṣemaka will be the last monarch. Now I shall describe to you the future of the Māgadha dynasty. Please listen.

Essence. Kṣemaka will be the last monarch of the moon dynasty in Kali-yuga. Even great lines come to a 'last monarch' — and the Bhāgavatam tells us in advance.

BG 22.46-48

Sahadeva, the son of Jarāsandha, will have a son named Mārjāri. From Mārjāri will come Śrutaśravā; from Śrutaśravā, Yutāyu; and from Yutāyu, Niramitra. The son of Niramitra will be Sunakṣatra, from Sunakṣatra will come Bṛhatsena, and from Bṛhatsena, Karmajit. The son of Karmajit will be Sutaṣjaya, the son of Sutaṣjaya will be Vipra, and his son will be Śuci. The son of Śuci will be Kṣema, the son of Kṣema will be Suvrata, and the son of Suvrata will be Dharmasūtra. From Dharmasūtra will come Sama; from Sama, Dyumatsena; from Dyumatsena, Sumati; and from Sumati, Subala.

Essence. After Jarāsandha's death, his line — Mārjāri, Śrutaśravā, Yutāyu, Niramitra, Sunakṣatra, Bṛhatsena, Karmajit, Sutaṣjaya, Vipra, Śuci, Kṣema, Suvrata, Dharmasūtra, Sama, Dyumatsena, Sumati, Subala — continues for many generations. Even the lines of antagonists are tracked carefully; the Lord's accounting is precise.

BG 22.49

From Subala will come Sunītha; from Sunītha, Satyajit; from Satyajit, Viśvajit; and from Viśvajit, Ripuṣjaya. All of these personalities will belong to the dynasty of Bṛhadratha, which will rule the world for one thousand years.

Essence. The Bṛhadratha dynasty — through Sunītha, Satyajit, Viśvajit, Ripuṣjaya — will rule the world for one thousand years. Even the largest historical structures, scripture says calmly, last one thousand years and end.
