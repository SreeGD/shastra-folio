BG 12.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: The son of Rāmacandra was Kuśa, the son of Kuśa was Atithi, the son of Atithi was Niṣadha, and the son of Niṣadha was Nabha. The son of Nabha was Puṇḍarīka, and from Puṇḍarīka came a son named Kṣemadhanvā.

Essence. After Rāmacandra, Kuśa, Atithi, Niṣadha — the dynasty quietly continues. Even after the Lord's grand pastimes end, history proceeds; the descendants of the divine are also accountable for ordinary stewardship.

BG 12.2

The son of Kṣemadhanvā was Devānīka, Devānīka’s son was Anīha, Anīha’s son was Pāriyātra, and Pāriyātra’s son was Balasthala. The son of Balasthala was Vajranābha, who was said to have been born from the effulgence of the sun-god.

Essence. Vajranābha is said to be born from the sun-god's effulgence. Souls of unusual lineage continue to be sent into the world, but the cosmic backstory does not exempt them from playing their role in the family.

BG 12.3-4

The son of Vajranābha was Sagaṇa, and his son was Vidhṛti. The son of Vidhṛti was Hiraṇyanābha, who became a disciple of Jaimini and became a great ācārya of mystic yoga. It is from Hiraṇyanābha that the great saint Yājṣavalkya learned the highly elevated system of mystic yoga known as ādhyātma-yoga, which can loosen the knots of material attachment in the heart.

Essence. From Hiraṇyanābha, Yājṣavalkya learned ādhyātma-yoga that loosens the heart's knots. Discipleship lines preserve techniques that no amount of self-study can recover — knowledge moves person to person.

BG 12.5

The son of Hiraṇyanābha was Puṣpa, and the son of Puṣpa was Dhruvasandhi. The son of Dhruvasandhi was Sudarśana, whose son was Agnivarṇa. The son of Agnivarṇa was named Śīghra, and his son was Maru.

Essence. Dhruvasandhi, Sudarśana, Agnivarṇa, Śīghra, Maru — the line passes through names that mean very little to us today, yet each life carried the dynasty forward. Anonymity in history does not mean insignificance in providence.

BG 12.6

Having achieved perfection in the power of mystic yoga, Maru still lives in a place known as Kalāpa-grāma. At the end of Kali-yuga, he will revive the lost Sūrya dynasty by begetting a son.

Essence. Maru, having attained a yoga-siddha body, still lives at Kalāpa-grāma and will revive the Sūrya dynasty at the end of Kali-yuga. The Lord keeps reserves we know nothing about, ready for moments thousands of years away.

BG 12.7

From Maru was born a son named Prasuśruta, from Prasuśruta came Sandhi, from Sandhi came Amarṣaṇa, and from Amarṣaṇa a son named Mahasvān. From Mahasvān, Viśvabāhu took his birth.

Essence. Mahasvān, Viśvabāhu — the dynasty proceeds. The Bhāgavatam lingers on names not for our memorization but to humble our sense of being the protagonist of history.

BG 12.8

From Viśvabāhu came a son named Prasenajit, from Prasenajit came Takṣaka, and from Takṣaka came Bṛhadbala, who was killed in a fight by your father.

Essence. Bṛhadbala was killed by Parīkṣit's father Abhimanyu. The history Śukadeva narrates touches Parīkṣit's own family — sacred narrative converges with the listener's own life.

BG 12.9

All these kings in the dynasty of Ikṣvāku have passed away. Now please listen as I describe the kings who will be born in the future. From Bṛhadbala will come Bṛhadraṇa.

Essence. Śukadeva pivots to describe kings yet to be born. The same dynastic logic that records the past projects into the future — providence is not bound by tense.

BG 12.10

The son of Bṛhadraṇa will be Ūrukriya, who will have a son named Vatsavṛddha. Vatsavṛddha will have a son named Prativyoma, and Prativyoma will have a son named Bhānu, from whom Divāka, a great commander of soldiers, will take birth.

Essence. Ūrukriya, Vatsavṛddha, Prativyoma, Bhānu, Divāka — the dynasty leans into another long stretch of names. Reading these is itself a small spiritual practice: humility in scale.

BG 12.11

Thereafter, from Divāka will come a son named Sahadeva, and from Sahadeva a great hero named Bṛhadaśva. From Bṛhadaśva will come Bhānumān, and from Bhānumān will come Pratīkāśva. The son of Pratīkāśva will be Supratīka.

Essence. Sahadeva, Bṛhadaśva, Bhānumān, Pratīkāśva — these names will produce yet more names. The chain is patient, and the Lord watches every link.

BG 12.12

Thereafter, from Supratīka will come Marudeva; from Marudeva, Sunakṣatra; from Sunakṣatra, Puṣkara; and from Puṣkara, Antarikṣa. The son of Antarikṣa will be Sutapā, and his son will be Amitrajit.

Essence. Marudeva, Sunakṣatra, Puṣkara, Antarikṣa — the dynasty keeps producing kings. Each ruled, each is now a name; the role outlasts the actor.

BG 12.13

From Amitrajit will come a son named Bṛhadrāja, from Bṛhadrāja will come Barhi, and from Barhi will come Kṛtaṣjaya. The son of Kṛtaṣjaya will be known as Raṇaṣjaya, and from him will come a son named Saṣjaya.

Essence. Bṛhadrāja, Barhi, Kṛtaṣjaya, Raṇaṣjaya, Saṣjaya — generations of warriors and rulers. Many of these names mean 'conqueror' yet the line itself eventually ends. Even the conquerors are conquered by time.

BG 12.14

From Saṣjaya will come Śākya, from Śākya will come Śuddhoda, and from Śuddhoda will come Lāṅgala. From Lāṅgala will come Prasenajit, and from Prasenajit, Kṣudraka.

Essence. Śākya, Śuddhoda, Lāṅgala, Prasenajit, Kṣudraka — names appear that hint at later traditions (the Buddha's lineage). Spiritual history is more interconnected than the labels we use later suggest.

BG 12.15

From Kṣudraka will come Raṇaka, from Raṇaka will come Suratha, and from Suratha will come Sumitra, ending the dynasty. This is a description of the dynasty of Bṛhadbala.

Essence. Raṇaka, Suratha, Sumitra — the line tapers and ends. Every house has a last bearer of the name, and that is not failure; it is simply the natural end the Lord arranged.

BG 12.16

The last king in the dynasty of Ikṣvāku will be Sumitra; after Sumitra there will be no more sons in the dynasty of the sun-god, and thus the dynasty will end.

Essence. After Sumitra there will be no more sons in the Sūrya dynasty. The chapter ends with a closure stark in its honesty — even the family of Rāma's line has a finite chapter in this world.
