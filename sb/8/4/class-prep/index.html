BG 4.1

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: When the Lord delivered Gajendra, King of the elephants, all the demigods, sages and Gandharvas, headed by Brahmā and Śiva, praised this activity of the Supreme Personality of Godhead and showered flowers upon both the Lord and Gajendra.

Essence. Demigods, sages, and Gandharvas applaud Gajendra's rescue and shower flowers. Heaven celebrates when even one struggling soul is delivered. Your private breakthrough in bhakti is never unnoticed; unseen well-wishers in higher worlds quietly rejoice over each surrender.

BG 4.2

There was a beating of kettledrums in the heavenly planets, the inhabitants of Gandharvaloka began to dance and sing, while great sages and the inhabitants of Cāraṇaloka and Siddhaloka offered prayers to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Puruṣottama.

Essence. Kettledrums sound, Gandharvas dance, Siddhas pray — joy ripples through the cosmos at the Lord's act. The universe is not a flat indifferent machine; it responds with music when bhakti succeeds. Tune yourself to share in that celebration rather than measuring everything by sober utility.

BG 4.3-4

The best of the Gandharvas, King Hūhū, having been cursed by Devala Muni, had become a crocodile. Now, having been delivered by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, he assumed a very beautiful form as a Gandharva. Understanding by whose mercy this had happened, he immediately offered his respectful obeisances with his head and began chanting prayers just suitable for the transcendental Lord, the supreme eternal, who is worshiped by the choicest verses.

Essence. King Hūhū, cursed by Devala into crocodile form, regains his Gandharva body and immediately offers prayers. Apparent curses from saintly persons are blessings in disguise. Treat unwelcome turns of fortune from devoted sources as long-form mercy still in transit.

BG 4.5

Having been favored by the causeless mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead and having regained his original form, King Hūhū circumambulated the Lord and offered his obeisances. Then, in the presence of all the demigods, headed by Brahmā, he returned to Gandharvaloka. He had been freed of all sinful reactions.

Essence. Freed of all sinful reactions, King Hūhū circumambulates the Lord and returns to Gandharvaloka. Even those temporarily fallen into low forms can be fully restored. Karmic dead-ends are not as final as they feel; the Lord's mercy reopens what life seemed to have closed.

BG 4.6

Because Gajendra, King of the elephants, had been touched directly by the hands of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, he was immediately freed of all material ignorance and bondage. Thus he received the salvation of sārūpya-mukti, in which he achieved the same bodily features as the Lord, being dressed in yellow garments and possessing four hands.

Essence. Touched by the Lord's hands, Gajendra receives sārūpya-mukti — the same four-handed form as Nārāyaṇa. Direct contact with the Lord transforms the body itself. Daily worship of the deity is one steady way to invite that transforming touch.

BG 4.7

This Gajendra had formerly been a Vaiṣṇava and the king of the country known as Pāṇḍya, which is in the province of Draviḍa [South India]. In his previous life, he was known as Indradyumna Mahārāja.

Essence. Gajendra was previously Indradyumna, a Vaiṣṇava king of the Pāṇḍya country. Past devotional service quietly secured this rescue across lives. The bhakti you do today builds a safety net you may not recognize until a future crisis.

BG 4.8

Indradyumna Mahārāja retired from family life and went to the Malaya Hills, where he had a small cottage for his āśrama. He wore matted locks on his head and always engaged in austerities. Once, while observing a vow of silence, he was fully engaged in the worship of the Lord and absorbed in the ecstasy of love of Godhead.

Essence. Indradyumna retired to a small forest cottage with matted hair, observing silence and absorbed in love of God. Simplicity, quiet, and ecstatic absorption are the natural climate of advanced bhakti. Voluntary downsizing is often the gateway to expanded inner life.

BG 4.9

While Indradyumna Mahārāja was engaged in ecstatic meditation, worshiping the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the great sage Agastya Muni arrived there, surrounded by his disciples. When the Muni saw that Mahārāja Indradyumna, who was sitting in a secluded place, remained silent and did not follow the etiquette of offering him a reception, he was very angry.

Essence. Agastya Muni arrives, finds the silent king has not risen to greet him, and becomes angry. Even saints can react sharply to perceived disrespect. Devotional absorption does not exempt us from social etiquette toward elders and guests.

BG 4.10

Agastya Muni then spoke this curse against the King: This King Indradyumna is not at all gentle. Being low and uneducated, he has insulted a brāhmaṇa. May he therefore enter the region of darkness and receive the dull, dumb body of an elephant.

Essence. Agastya curses Indradyumna to take an elephant's body — much strength, little intelligence. The curse fits the offense: subdued silence toward a brāhmaṇa becomes life as a powerful but uncomprehending creature. Notice where strength without proper respect is already shaping our own next life.

BG 4.11-12

Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: My dear King, after Agastya Muni had thus cursed King Indradyumna, the Muni left that place along with his disciples. Since the King was a devotee, he accepted Agastya Muni’s curse as welcome because it was the desire of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore, although in his next life he got the body of an elephant, because of devotional service he remembered how to worship and offer prayers to the Lord.

Essence. The king welcomes Agastya's curse as the Lord's will, and devotional memory survives even his elephant birth. A devotee accepts every event as the Lord's arrangement. That acceptance keeps the seed of bhakti viable through any transformation.

BG 4.13

Upon delivering the King of the elephants from the clutches of the crocodile, and from material existence, which resembles a crocodile, the Lord awarded him the status of sārūpya-mukti. In the presence of the Gandharvas, the Siddhas and the other demigods, who were praising the Lord for His wonderful transcendental activities, the Lord, sitting on the back of His carrier, Garuḍa, returned to His all-wonderful abode and took Gajendra with Him.

Essence. The Lord delivers Gajendra from both the crocodile and from material existence itself, then carries him to the spiritual world. For a devotee, mokṣa is not merging but association — direct service near the Lord. Aim your hopes higher than mere escape from pain.

BG 4.14

My dear King Parīkṣit, I have now described the wonderful power of Kṛṣṇa, as displayed when the Lord delivered the King of the elephants. O best of the Kuru dynasty, those who hear this narration become fit to be promoted to the higher planetary systems. Simply because of hearing this narration, they gain a reputation as devotees, they are unaffected by the contamination of Kali-yuga, and they never see bad dreams.

Essence. Those who hear the Gajendra rescue become qualified for higher worlds, are unaffected by Kali, and even stop having bad dreams. Sacred narrative cleans the subconscious. Saturate the mind with such hearing and even sleep itself becomes purer.

BG 4.15

Therefore, after getting up from bed in the morning, those who desire their own welfare — especially the brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and in particular the brāhmaṇa Vaiṣṇavas — should chant this narration as it is, without deviation, to counteract the troubles of bad dreams.

Essence. Recite this narration as it is in the morning to counteract bad dreams. Do not improvise or interpret cleverly; deliver scripture as received. Authentic transmission, not personal flair, is what carries the protective potency forward.

BG 4.16

O best of the Kuru dynasty, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the Supersoul of everyone, being thus pleased, addressed Gajendra in the presence of everyone there. He spoke the following blessings.

Essence. Pleased, the Lord publicly speaks blessings to Gajendra in front of everyone. Heart-to-heart approval from the Lord is sometimes given openly, before witnesses. Live in such a way that any quiet word He might speak about you would be one of approval rather than concern.

BG 4.17-24

The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Freed from all sinful reactions are those who rise from bed at the end of night, early in the morning, and fully concentrate their minds with great attention upon My form; your form; this lake; this mountain; the caves; the gardens; the cane plants; the bamboo plants; the celestial trees; the residential quarters of Me, Lord Brahmā and Lord Śiva; the three peaks of Trikūṭa Mountain, made of gold, silver and iron; My very pleasing abode [the Ocean of Milk]; the white island, Śvetadvīpa, which is always brilliant with spiritual rays; My mark of Śrīvatsa; the Kaustubha gem; My Vaijayantī garland; My club, Kaumodakī; My Sudarśana disc and Pāṣcajanya conchshell; My bearer, Garuḍa, the king of the birds; My bed, Śeṣa Nāga; My expansion of energy the goddess of fortune; Lord Brahmā; Nārada Muni; Lord Śiva; Prahlāda; My incarnations like Matsya, Kūrma and Varāha; My unlimited all-auspicious activities, which yield piety to he who hears them; the sun; the moon; fire; the mantra oṁkāra; the Absolute Truth; the total material energy; the cows and brāhmaṇas; devotional service; the wives of Soma and Kaśyapa, who are all daughters of King Dakṣa; the rivers Ganges, Sarasvatī, Nandā and Yamunā [Kālindī]; the elephant Airāvata; Dhruva Mahārāja; the seven ṛṣis; and the pious human beings.

Essence. The Lord lists what those rising before dawn should meditate on — His form, Gajendra's form, the lake, mountain, peaks, abode, weapons, carrier, expansions, incarnations, sun, moon, fire, oṁkāra, devotional service, cows and brāhmaṇas. Morning is for filling the mind with sacred images before the world fills it.

BG 4.25

My dear devotee, unto those who rise from bed at the end of night and offer Me the prayers offered by you, I give an eternal residence in the spiritual world at the end of their lives.

Essence. Those who rise early and chant Gajendra's prayers receive eternal residence in the spiritual world at life's end. Small, faithful morning practice has cumulative cosmic weight. The destination of the final breath is shaped by the quality of countless first breaths of the day.

BG 4.26

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: After giving this instruction, the Lord, who is known as Hṛṣīkeśa, bugled with His Pāṣcajanya conchshell, in this way pleasing all the demigods, headed by Lord Brahmā. Then He mounted the back of His carrier, Garuḍa.

Essence. Hṛṣīkeśa sounds His Pāñcajanya, delighting the demigods, then mounts Garuḍa. Even the conclusion of a rescue is celebrated. Notice and mark the endings of episodes in your own spiritual life with gratitude rather than rushing into the next concern.
