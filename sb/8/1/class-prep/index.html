Chapter 1 · Class prep

BG 1.1

King Parīkṣit said: O my lord, my spiritual master, now I have fully heard from Your Grace about the dynasty of Svāyambhuva Manu. But there are also other Manus, and I want to hear about their dynasties. Kindly describe them to us.

Essence. Parīkṣit asks his guru about Manus beyond Svāyambhuva, modeling how a sincere student keeps inquiring. Genuine spiritual progress is not satisfied with one round of hearing; it presses deeper, seeking the full story of how the Lord administers the cosmos through His representatives.

BG 1.2

O learned brāhmaṇa, Śukadeva Gosvāmī, the great learned persons who are completely intelligent describe the activities and appearance of the Supreme Personality of Godhead during the various manvantaras. We are very eager to hear about these narrations. Kindly describe them.

Essence. Avatāras are not self-declared; they are recognized through śāstra. A sober devotee learns to test claims of divinity against scripture rather than emotion, and at the same time keeps a hungry ear for the Lord's activities across the ages.

BG 1.3

O learned brāhmaṇa, kindly describe to us whatever activities the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who created this cosmic manifestation, has performed in the past manvantaras, is performing at present, and will perform in the future manvantaras.

Essence. Past, present and future all belong to Kṛṣṇa's ongoing pastimes. The Lord and the living entities are both eternal, but the Lord alone remembers and orchestrates the whole arc. Hearing about His threefold activities anchors us in a story far larger than this lifetime.

BG 1.4

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: In the present kalpa there have already been six Manus. I have described to you Svāyambhuva Manu and the appearance of many demigods. In this kalpa of Brahmā, Svāyambhuva is the first Manu.

Essence. One day of Brahmā contains fourteen Manus, and six have already passed in our current kalpa. Time is vast and orderly under the Lord's arrangement. Realizing this scale humbles the false urgency of our small ambitions and invites trust in cosmic order.

BG 1.5

Svāyambhuva Manu had two daughters, named Ākūti and Devahūti. From their wombs, the Supreme Personality of Godhead appeared as two sons named Yajṣamūrti and Kapila respectively. These sons were entrusted with preaching about religion and knowledge.

Essence. The Lord descends as Kapila and Yajñamūrti to teach religion and self-knowledge through ordinary family relationships. Divinity enters the world through mothers, fathers, and children — reminding us that household life itself can be a stage for transmitting truth.

BG 1.6

O best of the Kurus, I have already described [in the Third Canto] the activities of Kapila, the son of Devahūti. Now I shall describe the activities of Yajṣapati, the son of Ākūti.

Essence. Śukadeva moves systematically through the Lord's incarnations, modeling orderly study. Spiritual life flowers when we follow a structured curriculum under a guide rather than skipping randomly through topics that happen to interest us.

BG 1.7

Svāyambhuva Manu, the husband of Śatarūpā, was by nature not at all attached to enjoyment of the senses. Thus he gave up his kingdom of sense enjoyment and entered the forest with his wife to practice austerities.

Essence. Svāyambhuva Manu was emperor of the whole earth yet had no taste for sense gratification. Real renunciation is not poverty but inner detachment — kings can be saints when they stop measuring success by enjoyment and start measuring it by service.

BG 1.8

O scion of Bharata, after Svāyambhuva Manu had thus entered the forest with his wife, he stood on one leg on the bank of the river Sunandā, and in this way, with only one leg touching the earth, he performed great austerities for one hundred years. While performing these austerities, he spoke as follows.

Essence. Manu stood on one leg for a hundred years, murmuring sacred truths to himself rather than performing for an audience. Deep practice often happens silently and unwitnessed; the most transformative chanting is done not for show but as an inner conversation with the Lord.

BG 1.9

Lord Manu said: The supreme living being has created this material world of animation; it is not that He was created by this material world. When everything is silent, the Supreme Being stays awake as a witness. The living entity does not know Him, but He knows everything.

Essence. When everything else sleeps in oblivion, the Supreme stays awake as witness. The Lord knows us even when we do not know ourselves. Realizing this single fact reorients life: I am never unobserved, never alone, never forgotten.

BG 1.10

Within this universe, the Supreme Personality of Godhead in His Supersoul feature is present everywhere, wherever there are animate or inanimate beings. Therefore, one should accept only that which is allotted to him; one should not desire to infringe upon the property of others.

Essence. Everything in the universe belongs to the Lord; take only what is allotted, do not encroach on others' shares. This is the original ecology and economics — a life of contentment, simple needs, and respect for what is given rather than seized.

BG 1.11

Although the Supreme Personality of Godhead constantly watches the activities of the world, no one sees Him. However, one should not think that because no one sees Him, He does not see, for His power to see is never diminished. Therefore, everyone should worship the Supersoul, who always stays with the individual soul as a friend.

Essence. The Lord watches everyone unseen; His vision is never diminished by our blindness. Worship the Supersoul as the friend who already accompanies you. This shifts spiritual life from search to recognition — He is not far; we are simply not yet looking.

BG 1.12

The Supreme Personality of Godhead has no beginning, no end and no middle. Nor does He belong to a particular person or nation. He has no inside or outside. The dualities found within this material world, such as beginning and end, mine and theirs, are all absent from the personality of the Supreme Lord. The universe, which emanates from Him, is another feature of the Lord. Therefore the Supreme Lord is the ultimate truth, and He is complete in greatness.

Essence. The Lord has no beginning, end, inside, or outside, and the dualities of mine and theirs do not touch Him. He is the complete truth. Meditating on this absoluteness loosens our grip on the divisions we cling to as identity.

BG 1.13

The entire cosmic manifestation is the body of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the Absolute Truth, who has millions of names and unlimited potencies. He is self-effulgent, unborn and changeless. He is the beginning of everything, but He has no beginning. Because He has created this cosmic manifestation by His external energy, the universe appears to be created, maintained and annihilated by Him. Nonetheless, He remains inactive in His spiritual energy and is untouched by the activities of the material energy.

Essence. The cosmic body of the Lord has unlimited names and potencies, and chanting His names directly contacts those potencies. The mahā-mantra is not a symbol pointing to God; it is the Lord Himself appearing in sound, accessible to anyone who calls.

BG 1.14

Therefore, to enable people to reach the stage of activities that are not tinged by fruitive results, great saints first engage people in fruitive activities, for unless one begins by performing activities as recommended in the śāstras, one cannot reach the stage of liberation, or activities that produce no reactions.

Essence. Saints first engage people in regulated work, then gradually lift them to action without reaction. Spiritual life is staged; demanding instant renunciation skips necessary steps. Begin where you actually are, follow the prescribed path, and the path itself will purify you.

BG 1.15

The Supreme Personality of Godhead is full in opulence by His own gain, yet He acts as the creator, maintainer and annihilator of this material world. In spite of acting in that way, He is never entangled. Hence devotees who follow in His footsteps are also never entangled.

Essence. The Lord creates, maintains and destroys yet remains untouched. Devotees who follow His way work hard in this world without becoming entangled by it. The secret is not less activity but a different relationship to the fruits of activity.

BG 1.16

The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, works just like an ordinary human being, yet He does not desire to enjoy the fruits of work. He is full in knowledge, free from material desires and diversions, and completely independent. As the supreme teacher of human society, He teaches His own way of activities, and thus He inaugurates the real path of religion. I request everyone to follow Him.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa works like an ordinary person yet has no personal desires; He sets the standard for all of us. Religion is not a private opinion but the path the Lord Himself walked. To follow Him is to be released from the cocoon of selfish striving.

BG 1.17

Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: Svāyambhuva Manu was thus in a trance, chanting the mantras of Vedic instruction known as the Upaniṣads. Upon seeing him, the Rākṣasas and asuras, being very hungry, wanted to devour him. Therefore they ran after him with great speed.

Essence. Even saintly Manu in trance was hunted by hungry demons. Sincere practice does not exempt us from opposition; in fact, it often draws fresh resistance. The presence of obstacles is not a sign that we are off the path — it can be evidence we are on it.

BG 1.18

The Supreme Lord, Viṣṇu, who sits in everyone’s heart, appearing as Yajṣapati, observed that the Rākṣasas and demons were going to devour Svāyambhuva Manu. Thus the Lord, accompanied by His sons named the Yāmas and by all the other demigods, killed the demons and Rākṣasas. He then took the post of Indra and began to rule the heavenly kingdom.

Essence. The Lord, seated in everyone's heart, personally intervenes when His devotee is in danger. Posts like Indra are not fixed identities but services He can take up Himself when needed. Trust the Lord as your inner companion who notices and acts.

BG 1.19

The son of Agni named Svārociṣa became the second Manu. His several sons were headed by Dyumat, Suṣeṇa and Rociṣmat.

Essence. Manvantaras roll on with their own Manus, sages, and rulers — all partial expansions of the one Lord. Authority structures look different across ages, but their source is constant. Look past the changing faces to the unchanging Person behind them.

BG 1.20

During the reign of Svārociṣa, the post of Indra was assumed by Rocana, the son of Yajṣa. Tuṣita and others became the principal demigods, and Ūrja, Stambha and others became the seven saints. All of them were faithful devotees of the Lord.

Essence. In Svārociṣa's age, leaders were faithful devotees; society was governed by people loyal to the Lord. The quality of an era depends largely on whether its rulers are bhaktas. Pray and act for leadership in your own circles to be God-conscious.

BG 1.21

Vedaśirā was a very celebrated ṛṣi. From the womb of his wife, whose name was Tuṣitā, came the avatāra named Vibhu.

Essence. From the sage Vedaśirā and his wife Tuṣitā came the avatāra Vibhu. The Lord chooses pure households for His appearance. The dignity of a marriage is measured not by wealth but by what kind of being can comfortably appear in it.

BG 1.22

Vibhu remained a brahmacāri and never married throughout his life. From him, eighty-eight thousand other saintly persons took lessons on self-control, austerity and similar behavior.

Essence. Vibhu remained a lifelong brahmacārī, and 88,000 sages took self-control and austerity from him. One genuinely disciplined life can teach thousands. Discipline is not merely personal hygiene of spirit; it is a public service that inspires others.

BG 1.23

O King, the third Manu, Uttama, was the son of King Priyavrata. Among the sons of this Manu were Pavana, Sṛṣjaya and Yajṣahotra.

Essence. Uttama, son of the devoted king Priyavrata, became the third Manu. Spiritual lineage flows through generations when parents take their dharma seriously. The names of our descendants may be remembered if we live so that something worth carrying forward actually forms in us.

BG 1.24

During the reign of the third Manu, Pramada and other sons of Vasiṣṭha became the seven sages. The Satyas, Vedaśrutas and Bhadras became demigods, and Satyajit was selected to be Indra, the King of heaven.

Essence. Each manvantara has its specific sages, demigods, and Indra. The cosmos is not chaotic; positions are filled, duties are assigned. Take your own role seriously — somewhere in the universal arrangement, a small post is currently entrusted to you.

BG 1.25

In this manvantara, the Supreme Personality of Godhead appeared from the womb of Sūnṛtā, who was the wife of Dharma, the demigod in charge of religion. The Lord was celebrated as Satyasena, and He appeared with other demigods, known as the Satyavratas.

Essence. The Lord appears as Satyasena, born of Truth (Dharma) and his wife Sūnṛtā. Truth is the womb from which divinity emerges. A life devoted to honest, kind speech becomes the very environment in which the Lord can make Himself felt.

BG 1.26

Satyasena, along with His friend Satyajit, who was the King of heaven, Indra, killed all the untruthful, impious and misbehaved Yakṣas, Rākṣasas and ghostly living entities, who gave pains to other living beings.

Essence. Satyasena with Indra Satyajit removed untruthful, impious beings who tormented others. The Lord defends those who suffer at the hands of cruelty. Aligning with truth places you under His protection; aligning with falsehood places you among His targets.

BG 1.27

The brother of the third Manu, Uttama, was celebrated by the name Tāmasa, and he became the fourth Manu. Tāmasa had ten sons, headed by Pṛthu, Khyāti, Nara and Ketu.

Essence. Uttama's brother Tāmasa became the next Manu with ten sons of his own. Even within the same family, different souls take very different roles in the divine plan. Honor the unique assignment of every member rather than expecting uniformity.

BG 1.28

During the reign of Tāmasa Manu, among the demigods were the Satyakas, Haris and Vīras. The heavenly King, Indra, was Triśikha. The sages in saptarṣi-dhāma were headed by Jyotirdhāma.

Essence. In Tāmasa's manvantara new groups of demigods and sages took up their duties under Indra Triśikha. The universe runs on rotating teams of servants. No one is indispensable, yet every faithful server matters during their watch.

BG 1.29

O King, in the Tāmasa manvantara the sons of Vidhṛti, who were known as the Vaidhṛtis, also became demigods. Since in course of time the Vedic authority was lost, these demigods, by their own powers, protected the Vedic authority.

Essence. When Vedic authority is lost the universe becomes chaotic, so devoted demigods restore it. Personal lives mirror this: when scripture loses its central place, our days drift into confusion. Re-anchor regularly in śāstra — the Vedas hold the world together, inside and out.

BG 1.30

Also in this manvantara, the Supreme Lord, Viṣṇu, took birth from the womb of Hariṇī, the wife of Harimedhā, and He was known as Hari. Hari saved His devotee Gajendra, the King of the elephants, from the mouth of a crocodile.

Essence. As Hari, the Lord rescued Gajendra from the crocodile's jaws. When devotees call out in crisis with no other shelter, the Lord personally arrives. Helplessness, sincerely expressed, is not weakness in bhakti; it is the doorway through which the Lord enters.

BG 1.31

King Parīkṣit said: My lord, Bādarāyaṇi, we wish to hear from you in detail how the King of the elephants, when attacked by a crocodile, was delivered by Hari.

Essence. Parīkṣit, hours from death, still asks to hear the Gajendra story in detail. The dying king's hunger for kṛṣṇa-kathā shows what real urgency looks like. While we still have voice and breath, we can be turning toward stories of the Lord's rescues.

BG 1.32

Any literature or narration in which the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Uttamaśloka, is described and glorified is certainly great, pure, glorious, auspicious and all good.

Essence. Any literature that describes Kṛṣṇa is great, pure, and auspicious. The hours we spend with such books are not entertainment; they are direct contact with what is supremely good. Choose your reading with this in mind — pages shape lives.

BG 1.33

Śrī Sūta Gosvāmī said: O brāhmaṇas, when Parīkṣit Mahārāja, who was awaiting impending death, thus requested Śukadeva Gosvāmī to speak, Śukadeva Gosvāmī, encouraged by the King’s words, offered respect to the King and spoke with great pleasure in the assembly of sages, who desired to hear him.

Essence. Śukadeva, encouraged by the king's eagerness, begins his discourse with pleasure. Genuine inquiry energizes a teacher; the questions of devoted students unlock deeper speech. Be the kind of listener whose hunger draws the best out of those who can teach you.
