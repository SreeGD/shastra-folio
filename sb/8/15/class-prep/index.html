BG 15.1-2

Mahārāja Parīkṣit inquired: The Supreme Personality of Godhead is the proprietor of everything. Why did He beg three paces of land from Bali Mahārāja like a poor man, and when He got the gift for which He had begged, why did He nonetheless arrest Bali Mahārāja? I am very much anxious to know the mystery of these contradictions.

Essence. Parīkṣit asks: the Lord owns everything — why beg three paces, and why arrest Bali after the gift? The genuine inquirer brings contradictions to the teacher, not just questions. Mystery is the invitation to deeper hearing.

BG 15.3

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O King, when Bali Mahārāja lost all his opulence and died in the fight, Śukrācārya, a descendant of Bhṛgu Muni, brought him back to life. Because of this, the great soul Bali Mahārāja became a disciple of Śukrācārya and began to serve him with great faith, offering everything he had.

Essence. Bali lost everything and died; Śukrācārya revived him by mantra. Bali, in gratitude, became his disciple and offered everything. Receiving life from a teacher creates the deepest discipleship — total offering follows total revival.

BG 15.4

The brāhmaṇa descendants of Bhṛgu Muni were very pleased with Bali Mahārāja, who desired to conquer the kingdom of Indra. Therefore, after purifying him and properly bathing him according to regulative principles, they engaged him in performing the yajṣa known as Viśvajit.

Essence. Bhṛgu's brāhmaṇa descendants, pleased by Bali's desire to conquer Indra, purified him and engaged him in Viśvajit yajña. Even questionable ambitions can be channeled through proper ritual. The wise direct strong desire into sacred form.

BG 15.5

When ghee [clarified butter] was offered in the fire of sacrifice, there appeared from the fire a celestial chariot covered with gold and silk. There also appeared yellow horses like those of Indra, and a flag marked with a lion.

Essence. When ghee was offered in the fire, a gold-and-silk chariot, Indra-like horses, and a lion-marked flag appeared. Properly performed sacrifice produces real equipment. Yajña is not symbolic; it is generative.

BG 15.6

A gilded bow, two quivers of infallible arrows, and celestial armor also appeared. Bali Mahārāja’s grandfather Prahlāda Mahārāja offered Bali a garland of flowers that would never fade, and Śukrācārya gave him a conchshell.

Essence. Gilded bow, infallible quivers, celestial armor — plus Prahlāda's never-fading garland and Śukrācārya's conchshell. Equipment came from many hands: ritual, ancestor, guru. Real preparation gathers from every quarter.

BG 15.7

When Mahārāja Bali had thus performed the special ritualistic ceremony advised by the brāhmaṇas and had received, by their grace, the equipment for fighting, he circumambulated the brāhmaṇas and offered them obeisances. He also saluted Prahlāda Mahārāja and offered obeisances to him.

Essence. Equipped, Bali circumambulated the brāhmaṇas and offered obeisances, also saluting Prahlāda. Even before using the gifts, he honored the givers. Gratitude precedes deployment. Power received without thanks corrupts.

BG 15.8-9

Then, after getting on the chariot given by Śukrācārya, Bali Mahārāja, decorated with a nice garland, put protective armor on his body, equipped himself with a bow, and took up a sword and a quiver of arrows. When he sat down on the seat of the chariot, his arms decorated with golden bangles and his ears with sapphire earrings, he shone like a worshipable fire.

Essence. On the chariot, decorated, armored, with bow, sword, quiver, sapphire earrings — Bali shone like worshipable fire. The Bhāgavatam acknowledges the brilliance of the adversary. Demons can be beautiful, capable, and well-equipped.

BG 15.10-11

When he assembled with his own soldiers and the demon chiefs, who were equal to him in strength, opulence and beauty, they appeared as if they would swallow the sky and burn all directions with their vision. After thus gathering the demoniac soldiers, Bali Mahārāja departed for the opulent capital of Indra. Indeed, he seemed to make the entire surface of the world tremble.

Essence. Assembled with demon chiefs equal in strength, they seemed to swallow the sky and burn directions with their vision. Bali marched on Indra's capital, making the earth tremble. Massed demonic power is genuinely fearful, not cartoonish.

BG 15.12

King Indra’s city was full of pleasing orchards and gardens, such as the Nandana garden. Because of the weight of the flowers, leaves and fruit, the branches of the eternally existing trees were bending down. The gardens were visited by pairs of chirping birds and singing bees. The entire atmosphere was celestial.

Essence. Indra's city was full of pleasing gardens, eternal trees, chirping birds, singing bees, celestial atmosphere. Heavenly opulence is real and detailed. The Bhāgavatam does not minimize the loveliness of what is about to be lost.

BG 15.13

Beautiful women protected by the demigods sported in the gardens, which had lotus ponds full of swans, cranes, cakravākas and ducks.

Essence. Beautiful women protected by the demigods sported in lotus ponds with swans and cakravākas. Even the highest heavens have their distinct sensory pleasures. Pleasure is not anti-spiritual when it exists in dharmic context.

BG 15.14

The city was surrounded by trenches full of Ganges water, known as Ākāśa-gaṅgā, and by a high wall, which was the color of fire. Upon this wall were parapets for fighting.

Essence. Surrounded by Ākāśa-gaṅgā trenches and fire-colored walls with parapets. The most beautiful city is also fortified. Real protection includes barriers; love is not the same as openness to every entry.

BG 15.15

The doors were made of solid gold plates, and the gates were of excellent marble. These were linked by various public roads. The entire city had been constructed by Viśvakarmā.

Essence. Solid gold doors, marble gates, public roads — Viśvakarmā constructed everything. Even paradise is built; not even heaven assembles itself. Order requires specific divine craftsmanship.

BG 15.16

The city was full of courtyards, wide roads, assembly houses, and not less than one hundred million airplanes. The crossroads were made of pearl, and there were sitting places made of diamond and coral.

Essence. Courtyards, wide roads, assembly houses, a hundred million airplanes, pearl crossroads, diamond seats. Cosmic opulence is described in concrete detail. The reader is meant to picture it, not abstract it.

BG 15.17

Everlastingly beautiful and youthful women, who were dressed with clean garments, glittered in the city like fires with flames. They all possessed the quality of śyāmā.

Essence. Everlastingly beautiful and youthful women, dressed cleanly, glittered like fires with flames — śyāmā women, warm in winter and cool in summer. The opulence includes specific qualities of pleasing companionship.

BG 15.18

The breezes blowing in the streets of the city bore the fragrance of the flowers falling from the hair of the women of the demigods.

Essence. The street breezes carried the fragrance of flowers falling from the women's hair. Heaven communicates through breeze and fragrance. The senses, properly elevated, are channels of the divine.

BG 15.19

Apsarās passed on the streets, which were covered with the white, fragrant smoke of aguru incense emanating from windows with golden filigree.

Essence. Apsarās walked on streets covered with the white smoke of aguru incense from golden-filigreed windows. Even atmosphere is curated. The divine world has constant attention to mood-creating detail.

BG 15.20

The city was shaded by canopies decorated with pearls, and the domes of the palaces had flags of pearl and gold. The city always resounded with the vibrations of peacocks, pigeons and bees, and above the city flew airplanes full of beautiful women who constantly chanted auspicious songs that were very pleasing to the ear.

Essence. Pearl-decorated canopies, palace domes with pearl-and-gold flags, peacocks and pigeons, women in airplanes singing auspicious songs. Heavenly aesthetics are layered — color, sound, movement, song.

BG 15.21

The city was filled with the sounds of mṛdaṅgas, conchshells, kettledrums, flutes and well-tuned stringed instruments all playing in concert. There was constant dancing and the Gandharvas sang. The combined beauty of Indrapurī defeated beauty personified.

Essence. Mṛdaṅgas, conches, drums, flutes, strings, dancing, singing Gandharvas — Indrapurī defeated beauty itself. Music and dance are not peripheral; they constitute heavenly life. Sound is intrinsic to higher residence.

BG 15.22

No one who was sinful, envious, violent toward other living entities, cunning, falsely proud, lusty or greedy could enter that city. The people who lived there were all devoid of these faults.

Essence. No sinful, envious, violent, cunning, falsely proud, lusty, or greedy person could enter. The residents were free of these faults. Heaven is not unconditional admission; it is the place such qualities are not.

BG 15.23

Bali Mahārāja, who was the commander of numberless soldiers, gathered his soldiers outside this abode of Indra and attacked it from all directions. He sounded the conchshell given him by his spiritual master, Śukrācārya, thus creating a fearful situation for the women protected by Indra.

Essence. Bali surrounded the city, attacked from all directions, and sounded the guru's conch — creating fear among the protected women. Even fortified heaven can be besieged. Security is never absolute below the spiritual world.

BG 15.24

Seeing Bali Mahārāja’s indefatigable endeavor and understanding his motive, King Indra, along with the other demigods, approached his spiritual master, Bṛhaspati, and spoke as follows.

Essence. Seeing Bali's effort, Indra went to Bṛhaspati, his guru. When power fails, the leader does not just call advisors — he goes to his spiritual master. Authority steps up the chain.

BG 15.25

My lord, our old enemy Bali Mahārāja now has new enthusiasm, and he has obtained such astonishing power that we think that perhaps we cannot resist his prowess.

Essence. Bali has new enthusiasm and astonishing power; we may not be able to resist him. Indra's confession is honest. Real assessment, openly stated to the guru, opens the door for real counsel.

BG 15.26

No one anywhere can counteract this military arrangement of Bali’s. It now appears that Bali is trying to drink up the entire universe with his mouth, lick up the ten directions with his tongue, and raise fire in every direction with his eyes. Indeed, he has arisen like the annihilating fire known as saṁvartaka.

Essence. Bali seems to drink the universe with his mouth, lick directions with his tongue, raise fire with his eyes — like saṁvartaka fire. Indra's vivid metaphor admits something cosmic is happening. Naming threats accurately is the start of dharmic response.

BG 15.27

Kindly inform me. What is the cause for Bali Mahārāja’s strength, endeavor, influence and victory? How has he become so enthusiastic?

Essence. What is the cause of Bali's strength, endeavor, influence, and victory? Indra asks the right diagnostic question. Effects are visible; the question is what generates them.

BG 15.28

Bṛhaspati, the spiritual master of the demigods, said: O Indra, I know the cause for your enemy’s becoming so powerful. The brāhmaṇa descendants of Bhṛgu Muni, being pleased by Bali Mahārāja, their disciple, endowed him with such extraordinary power.

Essence. Bṛhaspati replied: the brāhmaṇa descendants of Bhṛgu, pleased with their disciple, endowed him with extraordinary power. By guru's grace, extraordinary capacity becomes available. The cause is not in Bali; it is in his teacher.

BG 15.29

Neither you nor your men can conquer the most powerful Bali. Indeed, no one but the Supreme Personality of Godhead can conquer him, for he is now equipped with the supreme spiritual power [brahma-tejas]. As no one can stand before Yamarāja, no one can now stand before Bali Mahārāja.

Essence. Neither you nor your men can conquer Bali; only the Supreme Lord can. No one stands before Yamarāja; no one stands before Bali now. Some opponents require divine intervention, not mortal effort.

BG 15.30

Therefore, waiting until the situation of your enemies is reversed, you should all leave this heavenly planet and go elsewhere, where you will not be seen.

Essence. Leave heaven and go elsewhere where you will not be seen, until the situation reverses. Strategic withdrawal under a guru's advice is not cowardice. Sometimes the dharma is to disappear.

BG 15.31

Bali Mahārāja has now become extremely powerful because of the benedictions given him by the brāhmaṇas, but when he later insults the brāhmaṇas, he will be vanquished, along with his friends and assistants.

Essence. Bali will be vanquished when he insults the brāhmaṇas who empowered him. Power received from the wise lasts only while their grace is respected. Future defeat is built into the present insult-readiness.

BG 15.32

Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: The demigods, being thus advised by Bṛhaspati for their benefit, immediately accepted his words. Assuming forms according to their desire, they left the heavenly kingdom and scattered, without being observed by the demons.

Essence. The demigods accepted Bṛhaspati's words, assumed forms at will, and left without being seen. They can take any form they desire. Immediate, full obedience to the guru — even when it means giving up position.

BG 15.33

When the demigods had disappeared, Bali Mahārāja, the son of Virocana, entered the heavenly kingdom, and from there he brought the three worlds under his control.

Essence. With the demigods gone, Bali entered heaven and brought all three worlds under control. When the rightful occupants withdraw, the powerful demonic side temporarily takes over. Heaven was held by inaction, not just power.

BG 15.34

The brāhmaṇa descendants of Bhṛgu, being very pleased with their disciple, who had conquered the entire universe, now engaged him in performing one hundred aśvamedha sacrifices.

Essence. The Bhṛgus engaged him in one hundred aśvamedha sacrifices — the qualifying ritual for staying on Indra's throne. Even illegitimate occupation requires legitimating ritual. Demons borrow dharma's forms when they want to keep what they took.

BG 15.35

When Bali Mahārāja performed these sacrifices, he gained a great reputation in all directions, throughout the three worlds. Thus he shone in his position, like the brilliant moon in the sky.

Essence. After these sacrifices, Bali shone in his position like the brilliant moon. Even demonic conquest, properly ritualized, produces real prestige. Fame is not always a sign of righteousness; it can be a sign of completed yajñas.

BG 15.36

Because of the favor of the brāhmaṇas, the great soul Bali Mahārāja, thinking himself very satisfied, became very opulent and prosperous and began to enjoy the kingdom.

Essence. By the brāhmaṇas' favor, Bali thought himself satisfied, became opulent, and enjoyed the kingdom. Brāhmaṇas guide; kṣatriyas keep law; vaiśyas and śūdras follow. The varṇa-system gradually elevates society to kṛṣṇa consciousness.
