Chapter 52 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 52.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: My dear King, thus graced by Lord Kṛṣṇa, Mucukunda circumambulated Him and bowed down to Him. Then Mucukunda, the beloved descendant of Ikṣvāku, exited through the mouth of the cave.

Essence. Graced by Kṛṣṇa, Mucukunda circumambulates Him, bows, and exits through the cave's mouth. Encounter with the Lord is completed by formal gestures of reverence; the meeting is closed properly.

BG 52.2

Seeing that the size of all the human beings, animals, trees and plants was severely reduced, and thus realizing that the Age of Kali was at hand, Mucukunda left for the north.

Essence. Seeing humans, animals, trees, plants greatly reduced in size, Mucukunda realizes the Age of Kali is at hand and leaves for the north. Scripture-trained perception reads geological-scale shifts as time-period markers; the wise diagnose the age.

BG 52.3

The sober King, beyond material association and free of doubt, was convinced of the value of austerity. Absorbing his mind in Lord Kṛṣṇa, he came to Gandhamādana Mountain.

Essence. Sober, beyond association, free of doubt, convinced of the value of austerity, mind absorbed in Kṛṣṇa, Mucukunda reaches Gandhamādana Mountain. Direction follows internal clarity; the right destination opens to the right inner state.

BG 52.4

There he arrived at Badarikāśrama, the abode of Lord Nara-Nārāyaṇa, where, remaining tolerant of all dualities, he peacefully worshiped the Supreme Lord Hari by performing severe austerities.

Essence. He arrives at Badarikāśrama, abode of Nara-Nārāyaṇa, and remains tolerant of all dualities, peacefully worshiping Hari by severe austerities. The final phase of a long-lived devotee is solitary worship and tolerance of opposites.

BG 52.5

The Lord returned to Mathurā, which was still surrounded by Yavanas. Then He destroyed the army of barbarians and began taking their valuables to Dvārakā.

Essence. The Lord returns to Mathurā, still surrounded by Yavanas, destroys the barbarian army, and takes their valuables to Dvārakā. The Lord returns to finish what He paused for the cave visit; missions interlock.

BG 52.6

As the wealth was being carried by oxen and men under Lord Kṛṣṇa’s direction, Jarāsandha appeared at the head of twenty-three armies.

Essence. As wealth is being transported, Jarāsandha appears with twenty-three armies. The recurring adversary reappears precisely when the work seems wrapped up; spiritual life rarely lacks one more challenge.

BG 52.7

O King, seeing the fierce waves of the enemy’s army, the two Mādhavas, imitating human behavior, ran swiftly away.

Essence. Seeing the enemy's fierce waves, the two Mādhavas — imitating human behavior — run swiftly away. The Lord can choose to flee; running is not always defeat, sometimes it is plan.

BG 52.8

Abandoning the abundant riches, fearless but feigning fear, They went many yojanas on Their lotuslike feet.

Essence. Abandoning abundant riches, fearless but feigning fear, They go many yojanas on lotus feet. Apparent abandonment of valuables can be cover for a deeper move; the Lord doesn't grieve over left-behind wealth.

BG 52.9

When he saw Them fleeing, powerful Jarāsandha laughed loudly and then pursued Them with charioteers and foot soldiers. He could not understand the exalted position of the two Lords.

Essence. Seeing Them flee, Jarāsandha laughs loudly and pursues with charioteers and foot soldiers — not understanding the Lords' exalted position. Misreading retreat as weakness is the enemy's recurring trap.

BG 52.10

Apparently exhausted after fleeing a long distance, the two Lords climbed a high mountain named Pravarṣaṇa, upon which Lord Indra showers incessant rain.

Essence. Apparently exhausted, the two Lords climb Mount Pravarṣaṇa, where Indra showers incessant rain. The pursuit ends with apparent exhaustion at a high place; high ground and rain make a setting for the next move.

BG 52.11

Although he knew They were hiding on the mountain, Jarāsandha could find no trace of Them. Therefore, O King, he placed firewood on all sides and set the mountain ablaze.

Essence. Knowing They are hiding, Jarāsandha places firewood on all sides and sets the mountain ablaze. The enemy responds to apparent victory by overkill; massive destruction is preferred to surgical action.

BG 52.12

The two of Them then suddenly jumped from the burning mountain, which was eleven yojanas high, and fell to the ground.

Essence. The two of Them suddenly jump from the burning eleven-yojana mountain and fall to the ground. The Lord makes impossible jumps; some moves require dropping rather than climbing.

BG 52.13

Unseen by Their opponent or his followers, O King, those two most exalted Yadus returned to Their city of Dvārakā, which had the ocean as a protective moat.

Essence. Unseen by opponent or followers, the two Yadus return to Dvārakā, with ocean as protective moat. Strategic withdrawal lands at a defended home; the work was the journey of preserving forces.

BG 52.14

Jarāsandha, moreover, mistakenly thought that Balarāma and Keśava had burned to death in the fire. Thus he withdrew his vast military force and returned to the Magadha kingdom.

Essence. Jarāsandha mistakenly thinks They burned to death, withdraws, and returns to Magadha. Enemies who think you're dead leave you alone; sometimes appearing destroyed is the cleanest exit.

BG 52.15

As ordered by Lord Brahmā, Raivata, the opulent ruler of Ānarta, gave Lord Balarāma his daughter Raivatī in marriage. This has already been discussed.

Essence. As Brahmā ordered, Raivata gave Lord Balarāma his daughter Raivatī in marriage — already discussed. Balarāma's marriage is mentioned as introduction to Kṛṣṇa's marriage to Rukmiṇī.

BG 52.16-17

O hero among the Kurus, the Supreme Lord Himself, Govinda, married Bhīṣmaka’s daughter, Vaidarbhī, who was a direct expansion of the goddess of fortune. The Lord did this by her desire, and in the process He beat down Śālva and other kings who took Śiśupāla’s side. Indeed, as everyone watched, Śrī Kṛṣṇa took Rukmiṇī just as Garuḍa boldly stole nectar from the demigods.

Essence. Govinda married Bhīṣmaka's daughter Vaidarbhī — a direct expansion of Lakṣmī — by her desire, beating down Śālva and other kings taking Śiśupāla's side. The Lord's marriage is a chosen mutual arrangement with cosmic significance, not an arrangement to be inherited.

BG 52.18

King Parīkṣit said: The Supreme Lord married Rukmiṇī, the beautiful-faced daughter of Bhīṣmaka, in the Rākṣasa style — or so I have heard.

Essence. King Parīkṣit: the Supreme Lord married Rukmiṇī in the Rākṣasa style — bride stolen from her home after killing rivals. Even royal questions name the form precisely; details of marriages matter to scripture-learners.

BG 52.19

My lord, I wish to hear how the immeasurably powerful Lord Kṛṣṇa took away His bride while defeating such kings as Māgadha and Sālva.

Essence. I wish to hear how the immeasurably powerful Kṛṣṇa took away His bride while defeating kings like Māgadha and Sālva. The next question is about the action — devotees want the story details, not just the headline.

BG 52.20

What experienced listener, O brāhmaṇa, could ever grow satiated while listening to the pious, charming and ever-fresh topics of Lord Kṛṣṇa, which cleanse away the world’s contamination?

Essence. What experienced listener could grow satiated hearing the pious, charming, ever-fresh topics of Kṛṣṇa, which cleanse the world's contamination? The Lord's narratives are inexhaustible; saturation never arrives.

BG 52.21

Śrī Bādarāyaṇi said: There was a king named Bhīṣmaka, the powerful ruler of Vidarbha. He had five sons and one daughter of lovely countenance.

Essence. There was a King Bhīṣmaka, powerful ruler of Vidarbha, with five sons and one daughter of lovely countenance. Stories begin with stable family genealogy; characters arrive in context.

BG 52.22

Rukmī was the first-born son, followed by Rukmaratha, Rukmabāhu, Rukmakeśa and Rukmamālī. Their sister was the exalted Rukmiṇī.

Essence. Rukmī was the first-born, then Rukmaratha, Rukmabāhu, Rukmakeśa, Rukmamālī; their sister was exalted Rukmiṇī. Sibling order matters in scripture; the named characters define the constellation.

BG 52.23

Hearing of the beauty, prowess, transcendental character and opulence of Mukunda from visitors to the palace who sang His praises, Rukmiṇī decided that He would be the perfect husband for her.

Essence. Hearing of Mukunda's beauty, prowess, character and opulence from visitors who sang His praises, Rukmiṇī decides He is the perfect husband. Reputation-by-praise can be the doorway to real commitment; hearing precedes choosing.

BG 52.24

Lord Kṛṣṇa knew that Rukmiṇī possessed intelligence, auspicious bodily markings, magnanimity, beauty, proper behavior and all other good qualities. Concluding that she would be an ideal wife for Him, He made up His mind to marry her.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa knew Rukmiṇī had intelligence, auspicious markings, magnanimity, beauty, proper behavior — and decided she would be His ideal wife. The Lord too discerns; reciprocal recognition is part of divine arrangement.

BG 52.25

O King, because Rukmī envied the Lord, he forbade his family members to give his sister to Kṛṣṇa, although they wanted to. Instead, Rukmī decided to give Rukmiṇī to Śiśupāla.

Essence. Rukmī, envious, forbids his family from giving Rukmiṇī to Kṛṣṇa and decides to give her to Śiśupāla. Envious elder brothers misuse position; family dynamics can derail what dharma intends.

BG 52.26

Dark-eyed Vaidarbhī was aware of this plan, and it deeply upset her. Analyzing the situation, she quickly sent a trustworthy brāhmaṇa to Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Dark-eyed Vaidarbhī, aware and upset, quickly sends a trustworthy brāhmaṇa to Kṛṣṇa. Decisive action under threat takes the right messenger to the right Person.

BG 52.27

Upon reaching Dvārakā, the brāhmaṇa was brought inside by the gatekeepers and saw the primeval Personality of Godhead sitting on a golden throne.

Essence. On reaching Dvārakā, the brāhmaṇa is brought inside by gatekeepers and sees the primeval Lord sitting on a golden throne. Even urgent messengers go through gates; access has structure.

BG 52.28

Seeing the brāhmaṇa, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, Lord of the brāhmaṇas, came down from His throne and seated him. Then the Lord worshiped him just as He Himself is worshiped by the demigods.

Essence. Seeing the brāhmaṇa, Kṛṣṇa comes down from His throne, seats him, and worships him as the demigods worship Himself. The Lord ranks the visiting brāhmaṇa above His own throne; brāhmaṇical honor outranks royal one.

BG 52.29

After the brāhmaṇa had eaten and rested, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the goal of saintly devotees, came forward, and while massaging the brāhmaṇa’s feet with His own hands, He patiently questioned him as follows.

Essence. After the brāhmaṇa has eaten and rested, Kṛṣṇa massages his feet with His own hands and patiently questions him. Service before inquiry; rest the messenger before extracting the message.

BG 52.30

[The Supreme Lord said:] O best of exalted brāhmaṇas, are your religious practices, sanctioned by senior authorities, proceeding without great difficulty? Is your mind always fully satisfied?

Essence. O best of brāhmaṇas, are your religious practices, sanctioned by senior authorities, proceeding without great difficulty? Is your mind fully satisfied? The Lord's first question is whether daily practice is sustainable and inner state is content.

BG 52.31

When a brāhmaṇa is satisfied with whatever comes his way and does not fall away from his religious duties, those very religious principles become his desire cow, fulfilling all his wishes.

Essence. When a brāhmaṇa is satisfied with whatever comes and doesn't fall from religious duties, those principles become his desire cow, fulfilling wishes. Contentment plus consistency turns religion into wish-fulfillment.

BG 52.32

An unsatisfied brāhmaṇa wanders restlessly from one planet to another, even if he becomes King of heaven. But a satisfied brāhmaṇa, though he may possess nothing, rests peacefully, all his limbs free of distress.

Essence. Unsatisfied brāhmaṇa wanders restlessly even as King of heaven; satisfied brāhmaṇa rests peacefully though possessing nothing. Position is no insurance against restlessness; possession is no precondition for peace.

BG 52.33

I repeatedly bow My head in respect to those brāhmaṇas who are satisfied with their lot. Saintly, prideless and peaceful, they are the best well-wishers of all living beings.

Essence. I repeatedly bow My head in respect to brāhmaṇas satisfied with their lot — saintly, prideless, peaceful, the best well-wishers of all. The Lord bows; satisfied saints are among the few entities the Lord salutes.

BG 52.34

O brāhmaṇa, is your King attending to your welfare? Indeed, that king in whose country the citizens are happy and protected is very dear to Me.

Essence. Is your King attending to your welfare? A king whose citizens are happy and protected is very dear to Me. Political leaders are evaluated by their care for their people; that care is itself a devotional act.

BG 52.35

Whence have you come, crossing the impassable sea, and for what purpose? Explain all this to Us if it is not a secret, and tell Us what We may do for you.

Essence. Whence have you come crossing the impassable sea, and for what purpose? Tell Us if it is not a secret and what We may do for you. The Lord asks the practical questions; only after care comes business.

BG 52.36

Thus questioned by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who incarnates to perform His pastimes, the brāhmaṇa told Him everything.

Essence. Thus questioned, the brāhmaṇa tells the Lord everything. Comprehensive answer is given to a comprehensive question; not edited summaries, full reports.

BG 52.37

Śrī Rukmiṇī said [in her letter, as read by the brāhmaṇa]: O beauty of the worlds, having heard of Your qualities, which enter the ears of those who hear and remove their bodily distress, and having also heard of Your beauty, which fulfills all the visual desires of those who see, I have fixed my shameless mind upon You, O Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Rukmiṇī's letter: O beauty of the worlds, having heard of Your qualities that remove distress and Your beauty that fulfills visual desire, I have come to seek shelter at Your feet. The letter begins with what she has heard, before what she asks.

BG 52.38

O Mukunda, You are equal only to Yourself in lineage, character, beauty, knowledge, youthfulness, wealth and influence. O lion among men, You delight the minds of all mankind. What aristocratic, sober-minded and marriageable girl of a good family would not choose You as her husband when the proper time has come?

Essence. O Mukunda, You are equal only to Yourself in lineage, character, beauty, knowledge, youth, wealth, and influence — what sober girl of a good family wouldn't choose You? Rukmiṇī's reasoning is plain: nobody better exists.

BG 52.39

Therefore, my dear Lord, I have chosen You as my husband, and I surrender myself to You. Please come swiftly, O almighty one, and make me Your wife. My dear lotus-eyed Lord, let Śiśupāla never touch the hero’s portion like a jackal stealing the property of a lion.

Essence. I have chosen You as my husband and surrender myself; come swiftly and make me Your wife. Let Śiśupāla never touch the hero's portion like a jackal stealing from a lion. Decisive choice and explicit refusal of alternatives both belong in surrender.

BG 52.40

If I have sufficiently worshiped the Supreme Personality of Godhead by pious works, sacrifices, charity, rituals and vows, and also by worshiping the demigods, brāhmaṇas and gurus, then may Gadāgraja come and take my hand, and not Damaghoṣa’s son or anyone else.

Essence. If I have sufficiently worshiped the Lord by pious works, sacrifices, charity, rituals, vows, worship of demigods, brāhmaṇas, and gurus — let Gadāgraja take my hand. Rukmiṇī invokes past merit as basis for present request; karma is the credit.

BG 52.41

O unconquerable one, tomorrow when my marriage ceremony is about to begin, You should arrive unseen in Vidarbha and surround Yourself with the leaders of Your army. Then crush the forces of Caidya and Magadhendra and marry me in the Rākṣasa style, winning me with Your valor.

Essence. Tomorrow, before the marriage ceremony, arrive unseen in Vidarbha with army leaders; crush Caidya and Magadhendra and marry me in Rākṣasa style, winning me with valor. Specific operational plan accompanies the request; courageous prayer can include logistics.

BG 52.42

Since I will be staying within the inner chambers of the palace, You may wonder, “How can I carry you away without killing some of your relatives?” But I shall tell You a way: On the day before the marriage there is a grand procession to honor the royal family’s deity, and in this procession the new bride goes outside the city to visit Goddess Girijā.

Essence. Since I'll be in the palace's inner chambers, how can You carry me away without killing relatives? On the day before, there is a procession to honor the royal family deity. Wisdom anticipates objections and supplies workarounds.

BG 52.43

O lotus-eyed one, great souls like Lord Śiva hanker to bathe in the dust of Your lotus feet and thereby destroy their ignorance. If I cannot obtain Your mercy, I shall simply give up my vital force, which will have become weak from the severe penances I will perform. Then, after hundreds of lifetimes of endeavor, I may obtain Your mercy.

Essence. Even Śiva hankers to bathe in the dust of Your lotus feet to destroy ignorance; if I cannot obtain Your mercy, I shall give up my vital force, weakened by penances I will perform for hundreds of lives. Devotion is willing to die for the chance.

BG 52.44

The brāhmaṇa said: This is the confidential message I have brought with me, O Lord of the Yadus. Please consider what must be done in these circumstances, and do it at once.

Essence. The brāhmaṇa says: this is the confidential message; please consider what must be done and do it at once. Messengers conclude by inviting decisive action, not by lingering on themselves.
