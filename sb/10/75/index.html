    Important Words
    • ajāta-śatroḥ — of Yudhiṣṭhira, whose enemy is never born
      A Vaiṣṇava epithet — the devotee of Hari has no enemy, because he sees no enemy. The irony of this chapter is that the unborn enemy is in fact present in the hall, watching.
    • rājasūya — the Rājasūya sacrifice
      The imperial yajña that establishes a king of kings. For Yudhiṣṭhira, performed as service to Kṛṣṇa, it crowns dharma; for Duryodhana watching, it is the sight that ignites adharma.
    • mumudire — they were delighted
      From √mud (to rejoice). The natural response of every heart not blocked by envy when Kṛṣṇa's devotee is glorified — Gauḍīyas read shared delight (anumodana) as itself a limb of bhakti.
    • duryodhanam — Duryodhana
      Dur-yodhana — 'one who fights wickedly.' Even his name marks him. Parīkṣit names him as the one exception, and Śuka will spend the chapter explaining why envy could not enjoy what love had built.
    • surāḥ — and the demigods
      Even the devas — usually difficult to please — were delighted. The cosmic order rejoices in a devotee-emperor's success; only one envious cousin does not.
    Mahārāja Parīkṣit said: O brāhmaṇa, according to what I have heard from you, all the assembled kings, sages and demigods were delighted to see the wonderful festivities of King Ajātaśatru’s Rājasūya sacrifice, with the sole exception of Duryodhana. Please tell me why this was so, my lord.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Mahārāja Parīkṣit said: O brāhmaṇa, according to what I have heard from you, all the assembled kings, sages and demigods were delighted to see the wonderful festivities of King Ajātaśatru's Rājasūya sacrifice, with the sole exception of Duryodhana. Please tell me why this was so, my lord.
    Commentary

    The seventy fifth chapter describes the details of the sacrifice. After the sacrifice Yudhisthira took avabhrtha bath with Draupadi. Duryodhana, being the object of joking, became offended and angry. Having heard from you that everyone except Duryodhana was pleased, please tell me the reason. Sukadeva already mentioned that Duryodhana was envious (yo na sehe sriyam sphitam 10.74.53). That is one reason he was not pleased. Sukadeva wanted to describe another reason as it arose in his mind which he had not previously described. He describes the supplementary part of the sacrifice now, looking backwards so to speak, since he already described the completion of the sacrifice and departure of the guests.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Though it was already explained that the cause of Duryodhana’s agitation was his sin, he asks again, wanting to know the details while describing the sacrifice. Yudhiṣṭhira did not hate Duryodhana. The cause of Duryodhana’s hatred was that he thought the throne belonged to him. Parīkṣit had heard this from the mouth of the Lord through paramparā.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Though it was already explained that the cause of Duryodhana’s agitation was his sin, he asks again, wanting to know the details while confirming what was said. Yudhiṣṭhira did not hate Duryodhana. Seeing the excellence, wealth or festivity of the sacrifice, which was extraordinary (tam), all the other kings who had gathered were joyful. O Brahman, directly the Lord, you understand this! Duryodhana alone was not happy. This has been heard by us (naḥ). O omniscient Śukadeva (bhagavān)! You know how to explain this.

    Personal Guidance
    Analogy

    Like a single black thread spoiling a bright bridal cloth, one envious heart in a hall full of delighted devotees foreshadows everything that will come. Parīkṣit notices the exception, not the rule — because in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, the seed of the war is hidden inside the festival.

    Stories
    Hiraṇyakaśipu at Prahlāda's class

    In Canto 7, the entire universe was free to drink Prahlāda's kṛṣṇa-kīrtana — only his father burned. The Bhāgavatam pattern is consistent: the festival of bhakti is suffered as fire by the envious.

    Duryodhana at the Rājasūya stands in the same posture as Hiraṇyakaśipu at the gurukula — surrounded by Kṛṣṇa's glories, refusing them, and consequently setting up his own destruction.

    Important Words
    • pitāmahasya — of the grandfather
      Parīkṣit's grandfather is Yudhiṣṭhira. Śuka names the relation to honor Parīkṣit's lineage — the Pāṇḍava blood is itself a bhakta-saṁskāra he carries.
    • rājasūye — at the Rājasūya
      Locative — within the sacrifice itself. Gauḍīyas note that everything from royal kitchen to royal feet-washing happens inside the yajña; the whole household labor becomes oblation.
    • bāndhavāḥ — family members
      From √bandh (to bind). Kin are 'the bound ones' — bound by birth. But in this verse the very next word reframes the binding.
    • paricaryāyām — in humble service
      Paricaryā is one of the classical limbs of devotional life — bodily service. That the imperial brothers do it, not servants, is the Gauḍīya signature: dāsya posture is not beneath nobility, it is its ornament.
    • prema-bandhanāḥ — bound by love
      Compound: prema (love) + bandhana (binding). The Gauḍīya keyword. Bāndhavas are bound by blood; bhaktas are bound by prema. Yudhiṣṭhira's kin happen to be both, and the verse celebrates the second binding, not the first.
    Śrī Bādarāyaṇi said: At the Rājasūya sacrifice of your saintly grandfather, his family members, bound by their love for him, engaged themselves in humble services on his behalf.
    Purport

    King Yudhiṣṭhira did not force his relatives to accept different tasks at the sacrifice. Rather, out of their love for him they volunteered for such duties.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Śrī Bādarāyaṇi said: At the Rājasūya sacrifice of your saintly grandfather, his family members, bound by their love for him, engaged themselves in humble services on his behalf.
    Commentary

    Friends bound by ropes of affection to the king engaged in services at the sacrifice, by their own free will. According to their liking they did activities, not under instruction by Yudhisthira.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Śukadeva calls Yudhiṣṭhira “grandfather” in order to give Parīkṣit joy. Friends who were bound to Yudhiṣṭhira by affection were there to perform various services at the sacrifice of Yudhiṣṭhira, who had an excellent nature (mahātmanaḥ).

    Important Words
    • bhīmaḥ — Bhīma
      Second Pāṇḍava, son of Vāyu. Given the kitchen — the place of feeding. His vātsalya-tinted strength feeds the entire assembly; the man who will later kill Duryodhana now cooks for his guests.
    • suyodhanaḥ — Suyodhana (Duryodhana)
      Śuka uses the polite form 'Su-yodhana' (good fighter) — the family name, before the bitterness shows. He is given charge of the treasury: the very wealth whose abundance will later torment him.
    • kṛṣṇaḥ — Kṛṣṇa
      The Supreme Lord, svayaṁ-bhagavān. Listed in the line of servants — not above them. Gauḍīyas treat this as the master image of the chapter: Bhagavān Himself in dāsya-posture within His own devotee's house.
    • pāda-avanejane — in washing the feet
      Compound: pāda (foot) + avanejana (washing). The lowest sevā in a hierarchical assembly. Kṛṣṇa chooses it. Six Gosvāmīs cite this verse as proof that bhakti inverts pride; the Person who is worshiped chooses to worship.
    • pariveṣaṇe — in distributing food
      Draupadī, the empress, personally serves. The same Draupadī whose attempted disrobing Duryodhana will engineer; the same Draupadī whose laugh in verse 38 will be the spark.
    Bhīma supervised the kitchen, Duryodhana looked after the treasury, while Sahadeva respectfully greeted the arriving guests. Nakula procured needed items, Arjuna attended the respectable elders, and Kṛṣṇa washed everyone’s feet, while Draupadī served food, and generous Karṇa gave out the gifts. Many others, such as Yuyudhāna; Vikarṇa, Hārdikya; Vidura; Bhūriśravā and other sons of Bāhlīka; and Santardana, similarly volunteered for various duties during the elaborate sacrifice. They did so because of their eagerness to please Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira, O best of kings.

    Kṛṣṇa washes the brāhmaṇas' feet at the Rājasūya

Since Draupadī and others were principal to the sacrifice, the word mātuleya (maternal uncle’s son) refers the husband’s cousins, just as “nephew” would refer to nephews on the husband’s side. It would be improper to joke with maternal cousins on the wife’s side. Of Yudhiṣṭhira’s cousins the most famous was Kṛṣṇa. Thus mātuleya here refers to Kṛṣṇa, just as Govardhana is accepted as the most famous among mountains. Hantāyam adrir abalā hari-dāsa-varyah:‘Of all the devotees, this Govardhana Hill is the best! (SB 10.21.18) Kṛṣṇa was the best husband. Others were also husbands such as Bhīma and other friends who were nearby. They were understood to be brothers. The next verse states that the wives of Yudhiṣṭhira squirted water on the brothers of Yudhiṣṭhira (devarān) and their friends. Thus in the eyes of the public it was proper for them to joke with each other. They would not be subject to criticism since their relationship was close.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Draupadī, Rukmiṇī and other queens came to the fun. The word devyaḥ indicates their great beauty. The devatās’ wives came in the sky. The queens were sprinkled by friends of Kṛṣṇa (mātulasya). Some say that the word should be sakhībhiḥ (by wives of Kṛṣṇa). They appeared more beautiful than before.

    Important Words
    • ekadā — one day
      The Bhāgavatam's understated narrative pivot — 'one day' — the way Canto 10 opens nearly every Vraja-līlā. Here it pivots away from celebration, into the war's seed.
    • duryodhanaḥ — Duryodhana
      Now named with his true name (not the polite Suyodhana of verse 4-7). The chapter has crossed the threshold; this is no longer the well-fighter but the dur-yodhana.
    • atapyat — he was pained
      Imperfect of √tap (to heat, to be heated). The same root as tapas (austerity) — but here it is involuntary, ugly heat. Envy is tapas turned inside out: heat without offering, fire without altar.
    • rājasūyasya-mahitvam — the greatness of the Rājasūya
      Phrase: genitive + accusative. The chapter is precise — Duryodhana is not pained by Yudhiṣṭhira's PERSON; he is pained by the YAJÑA'S greatness. He misreads brilliance as a personal affront.
    • acyuta-ātmanaḥ — of him whose very soul was Lord Acyuta
      Compound: acyuta (the infallible — Kṛṣṇa) + ātman (soul). The chapter's diagnostic: Yudhiṣṭhira's soul is Acyuta. Duryodhana sees the Rājasūya's greatness; he does not see that its greatness is Kṛṣṇa. The blindness is the disease.

    Commentary

    Having mentioned that Yudhiṣṭhira’s sacrifice was a cause of Duryodhana’s sorrow, Śukadeva now describes the reason until verse 39. Out of great pride Duryodhana usually stayed in his own camp. But one time, burning with envy on constantly hearing about it he went to the palace and saw the wealth of the kingdom and the sacrifice’s influence and prominence (mahitvam). Previously he did not pay much attention because of the crowds at the time of the sacrifice and occupation with his duties. Now however, with absence of these distraction he saw everything in detail (vi—īkṣya). He showed great pain. But Yudhiṣṭhira’s wealth and the sacrifice’s greatness was proper, without the fault of pride, since his heart was focussed on Kṛṣṇa, who was full of all powers and sweetness (acyutātmanaḥ).

    Commentary

Seeing the wealth in the palace, he was pained by the greatness of the sacrifice and king (mahitvam). Or he was pained by the worship given by all people (mahitvam) to Yudhiṣṭhira, caused by the sacrifice. The wealth and the greatness of the sacrifice were causes. Yudhiṣṭhira’s heart was focused on Kṛṣṇa, who was full of all powers and sweetness (acyutātmanaḥ). This was the particular cause of Duryodhana’s hatred.     Important Words
    • lakṣmīḥ — the opulences
      Plural. Many lakṣmīs — many forms of beauty and wealth. Gauḍīyas hear the deeper resonance: Lakṣmī herself attends the bhakta's house. Duryodhana sees her plural; he does not see Whom she attends.

      Compound: viśva (universe) + sṛj (creator). Maya Dānava is called this by hyperbole. The chapter is playing with creator-language — the real creator is Kṛṣṇa, who sits in the same hall.
    • drupada-rāja-sutā — the daughter of King Drupada (Draupadī)
      Compound: Drupada-rāja + sutā. Draupadī, daughter of the fire — born from yajña, married to dharma. Now she serves; the man watching cannot bear that she serves THEM and not him.
    • upatasthe — she served
      Perfect middle of upa-√sthā — 'attended upon.' Draupadī personally serves her husbands (patīn) in the Maya-Sabhā. The Vaiṣṇava-dharma frame holds the unusual sociology together. Duryodhana cannot hold it; his gaze fractures into possession.

      Past participle of vi-√sañj (to cling, hang on). Duryodhana's hṛdaya is viṣakta — hung up on her. Gauḍīyas read viṣakta as the precise opposite of nyasta (offered, surrendered). Same fluid, opposite direction.

By this he sowed the seed of dissension, desiring to rid the earth of its burden. Of course just by his glance he can bewilder Duryodhana, but he used Mayadanava’s maya as a tool for this.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    With downcast head (arvag-vadanaḥ) Duryodhana left. People cried out because they worried about some mischief since Duryodhana was most evil. Yudhiṣṭhira, being without an enemy, was somewhat (iva) saddened because of his natural compassion. The Lord was silent because his desire was fulfilled. That desire is mentioned: he wanted to remove the burden of the earth. Instead of samujjihīrṣḥr sometimes jihīrṣur ucchaiḥ is seen. But this is not accepted by Śrīdhara Svāmī.

    Personal Guidance
    Analogy

    Bhīma's laugh, the women's laughs, Draupadī's laugh — and Kṛṣṇa's approving silence — are not cruelty. They are the assembly's natural response to pride caught mid-stumble. Gauḍīyas read Kṛṣṇa's anumodana as the moment the war is, in essence, already lit: Bhagavān has let the seed fall.

    Stories
    Draupadī's laugh as karma's hinge

    The Mahābhārata (and folk retellings of the Sabhā-parva) trace the entire later disrobing of Draupadī, the dice game, and the war itself back to this moment — Duryodhana's humiliation in the Maya-Sabhā and the laughter of the assembly, especially Draupadī's.

    Gauḍīyas refuse to moralize the laugh as 'Draupadī's fault.' The chapter says Kṛṣṇa approved (kṛṣṇa-anumoditāḥ). His larger plan — bhū-bhāra-haraṇa (verse 39) — required this seed. The bhakta-laugh is not cruelty; it is the Lord's instrument.

    Important Words
    • vrīḍitaḥ — embarrassed
      Past participle of √vrīḍ. The word also names the chaste shyness of devotees (sa-vrīḍa-hāsa, verse 16). The same emotion in two hearts: bhakta-vrīḍā ornaments; envy's vrīḍā burns. Substance equal, direction opposite.

      Phrase: ruṣā (with anger) + jvalan (burning, pres. part. of √jval). Atapyat in verse 31 was a smolder; here it is open flame. The chapter has tracked his heat from spark to fire.
    • gaja-āhvayam — to Hastināpura
      Compound: gaja (elephant) + āhvaya (named). 'Elephant-named city' — Hastināpura. He returns to his own kingdom carrying the war's seed home.
    • bhagavān — the Supreme Lord
      Named again at the verse's end. Gauḍīyas note: every time Kṛṣṇa is called Bhagavān in this chapter, the chapter is asking us to remember WHO has just allowed what.
    • dṛśā — with His glance
      Instrumental of dṛś (sight, glance). The Lord's glance is the active agent of the bewilderment. Asura-māyā built the hall; Bhagavān's glance let it operate. Gauḍīya reading: Maya's craft is real, but Kṛṣṇa's anumati is the deeper cause.
    • bhuvaḥ-bharam-samujjihīrṣuḥ — desiring to remove the burden of the earth
      Phrase: bhuvaḥ (of the earth, gen.) + bharam (burden) + samujjihīrṣuḥ (desiring to lift up well, desid. of sam-ud-√hṛ). The verse states the cosmic motive openly. The Bhāgavatam refuses to apologize for the Lord's silence: His silence serves a larger lifting.
    • tūṣṇīm — silent
      Adverb. Used twice in this verse — Duryodhana leaves silently; Bhagavān remains silent. Two silences mirror each other. One is humiliation's; the other is providence's.

    Purport

    Śrīla Prabhupāda writes: “When Duryodhana left in such an angry mood, everyone regretted the incident, and King Yudhiṣṭhira also became very sorry. But despite all occurrences, Kṛṣṇa was silent. He did not say anything against or in favor of the incident. It appeared that Duryodhana had been put into illusion by the supreme will of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and this was the beginning of the enmity between the two sects of the Kuru dynasty. This appeared to be a part of Kṛṣṇa’s plan in His mission to decrease the burden of the world.”

    Commentary

    Duryodhana left the assembly in silence because he was ashamed. Because of his shame, he hung his head. He was also angry. He returned to Hastināpura with pomp (pra-yayau), with Yudhiṣṭhira’s servants, or quickly, being consoled by his brothers People cried out because they worried about some mischief, since Duryodhana was most evil. Yudhiṣṭhira, being without an enemy, was somewhat (iva) saddened because of his natural compassion. The Lord who appeared to show unlimited powers to spread bhakti, by destroying the demons and protecting the righteous, directly the supreme (bhagavān), was silent because his desire was fulfilled: removing the burden of the earth. Or the Lord, omniscient (bhagavān), knowing the seed of suffering for the Pāṇḍavas, remained silent, because he desired to remove the burned of the earth. Or the Lord, possessing all powers (bhagavān), able to bewilder Duryodhana by his glance, remained silent. The maker of the hall Maya did not have the power of such bewilderment on his own.

    Analogy

    Duryodhana leaves silent and burning; the saintly cry 'hā hā'; Yudhiṣṭhira is heartsick; Kṛṣṇa says nothing — and Kṛṣṇa's silence is the loudest sound in the chapter. The Lord whose mere glance had bewildered Duryodhana now lets him go, because the earth's burden is going with him.

    Stories
    Bhū-devī's complaint

    The frame of the entire Kṛṣṇa-avatāra (SB 10.1.17–22) is Bhū-devī's complaint: the earth groans under arrogant kṣatriyas. Kṛṣṇa descends bhuvaḥ bharam samujjihīrṣuḥ — desiring to lift the earth's burden.

    Verse 39 names this directly. Duryodhana's silent rage is not a defeat for Kṛṣṇa — it is the agency by which the war that thins the kṣatra burden becomes inevitable. The chapter ends with the Lord's silent gaze; that gaze is the Mahābhārata war already underway.

Śālva was one of the kings who had been defeated at the time of Rukmiṇī-devī's marriage. Having vowed then that he would rid the earth of all the Yādavas, he began worshiping Lord Śiva each day by eating only a palmful of dust. After a year had passed, Śiva appeared before Śālva and asked him to choose a benediction. Śālva begged for a flying machine that could go anywhere and that would strike terror into the hearts of demigods, demons and humans alike. Lord Śiva granted this request and had Maya Dānava construct for Śālva a flying iron city named Saubha. Śālva took this vehicle to Dvārakā, where he and his huge army laid siege to the city. From his airplane Śālva bombarded Dvārakā with tree trunks, boulders and other missiles, and he produced a mighty whirlwind that obscured everything with dust. When Pradyumna, Sātyaki and the other Yadu heroes saw the plight of Dvārakā and her residents, they went out to do battle with Śālva's forces. Pradyumna, the best of warriors, destroyed with His divine weapons all of Śālva's illusory magic, and He also bewildered Śālva himself. Thus Śālva's airplane began wandering aimlessly on the earth, in the sky and on the tops of mountains. But then a follower of Śālva's named Dyumān struck Pradyumna on the chest with his club, whereupon Pradyumna's chariot driver, thinking his master seriously injured, carried Him from the battlefield. But Pradyumna quickly regained consciousness and sharply criticized His driver for doing this.
