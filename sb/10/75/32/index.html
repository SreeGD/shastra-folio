Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.75.32

यस्मिन् नरेन्द्रदितिजेन्द्रसुरेन्द्रलक्ष्मी- र्नाना विभान्ति किल विश्वसृजोपक्लृप्ता: । ताभि: पतीन् द्रुपदराजसुतोपतस्थे यस्यां विषक्तहृदय: कुरुराडतप्यत् ॥ ३२ ॥

Important Words
  • lakṣmīḥ — the opulences
    Plural. Many lakṣmīs — many forms of beauty and wealth. Gauḍīyas hear the deeper resonance: Lakṣmī herself attends the bhakta's house. Duryodhana sees her plural; he does not see Whom she attends.
  • viśva-sṛjā — by the cosmic manufacturer
    Compound: viśva (universe) + sṛj (creator). Maya Dānava is called this by hyperbole. The chapter is playing with creator-language — the real creator is Kṛṣṇa, who sits in the same hall.
  • drupada-rāja-sutā — the daughter of King Drupada (Draupadī)
    Compound: Drupada-rāja + sutā. Draupadī, daughter of the fire — born from yajña, married to dharma. Now she serves; the man watching cannot bear that she serves THEM and not him.
  • upatasthe — she served
    Perfect middle of upa-√sthā — 'attended upon.' Draupadī personally serves her husbands (patīn) in the Maya-Sabhā. The Vaiṣṇava-dharma frame holds the unusual sociology together. Duryodhana cannot hold it; his gaze fractures into possession.
  • viṣakta — attached
    Past participle of vi-√sañj (to cling, hang on). Duryodhana's hṛdaya is viṣakta — hung up on her. Gauḍīyas read viṣakta as the precise opposite of nyasta (offered, surrendered). Same fluid, opposite direction.
  • kuru-rāṭ — the king of the Kurus
    Compound: Kuru + rāj. Duryodhana, the kṣatra heir. The title that should mark service marks here a sickness — kingship without Kṛṣṇa-anchor.
In that palace all the collected opulences of the kings of men, demons and gods were brilliantly manifest, having been brought there by the cosmic inventor, Maya Dānava. With those riches Draupadī served her husbands, and Duryodhana, the prince of the Kurus, lamented because he was very much attracted to her.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In that palace all the collected opulences of the kings of men, demons and gods were brilliantly manifest, having been brought there by the cosmic inventor, Maya Dānava. With those riches Draupadī served her husbands, and Duryodhana, the prince of the Kurus, lamented because he was very much attracted to her.
Commentary

In the chambers were various types of treasures from the kings of humans, demons and devas. It was built by Maya. Draupadi enjoyed those opulences with her husbands in that place. Overcome with envy by those opulences, Durydhona’s heart was pained.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The grandeur of the sacrifice has been described. Now the wealth of the palace is briefly described, as the cause of Duryodhana’s grief. In the palace (yasmin) various types of wealth from kings and devatās shone brilliantly. Sma indicates certainty. This was because the palace was made by Viśvakarmā or because the hall was made by Maya (viśvasṛjā). By the construction of the city, Draupadī served her husband Yudhiṣṭhira. Citsukha’s version has patīn (husbands). She was the daughter of King Drupada. This suggests her extraordinary beauty and qualities, through her remarkable birth. His heart became filled with envy of that wealth (yasyām). Out of envy he thought of himself as the king of the Kurus (kuru-rāṭ).

Personal Guidance
Analogy

Maya Dānava built the assembly hall — and Maya Dānava's gift will mock Duryodhana in a few verses. Wealth crafted by an asura, however dazzling, has hidden trap-doors. The pearl in Yudhiṣṭhira's hand is Kṛṣṇa; the pearl in Duryodhana's eye is the wife he covets.

Stories
Maya Dānava's debt

Maya, an asura architect, was spared from the Khāṇḍava forest's burning by Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa (Mahābhārata Ādi-parva); in gratitude he built the Sabhā for the Pāṇḍavas. The hall is therefore a literal asura-gift — beautiful but with illusion built in.

Gauḍīyas note: bhakta receives even an asura's craft and uses it for dharma; the asura's gift, however, retains its own logic. Duryodhana will meet that logic in verse 37.
