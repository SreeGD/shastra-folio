Important Words
  • didṛkṣayā — with the desire to see
    From √dṛś (to see) — a desiderative. The motive of the ṛṣis is not to instruct or to perform — it is darśana. Gauḍīyas treat darśana of Bhagavān as a prayojana in itself, not a means to something else.
  • munayaḥ — sages
    From √man (to think, contemplate). A muni is one whose thought is silenced into ekāgratā. That such silenced thinkers travel to see Kṛṣṇa argues that Bhagavān is the resting place of contemplation — not its negation.
  • kṛṣṇa-rāma — Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma
    Dvandva compound. Gauḍīyas treat Balarāma as Kṛṣṇa's first expansion (prakāśa) and the original guru-tattva. The sages' single purpose — to see both — registers the inseparability of bhagavat-tattva and its expansion that grants service-eligibility.
  • brahma-putrāḥ — sons of Brahmā (the four Kumāras)
    Sanaka, Sanat-kumāra, Sananda, Sanātana — eternally child-like ṛṣis. Cited in SB 3.15 as the founders of the Kumāra-sampradāya, and revered by Gauḍīyas as living proof that jñāna ripens into bhakti.
  • rāmaḥ — Lord Paraśurāma
    Called bhagavān in the verse (rāmaḥ sa-śiṣyaḥ bhagavān) — an āveśa-avatāra of Viṣṇu. His presence with disciples among ṛṣis registers that even an empowered avatāra approaches Kṛṣṇa as a sage approaching darśana.
As the women thus talked among themselves and the men among themselves, a number of great sages arrived there, all of them eager to see Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Balarāma. They included Dvaipāyana, Nārada, Cyavana, Devala and Asita, Viśvāmitra, Śatānanda, Bharadvāja and Gautama, Lord Paraśurāma and his disciples, Vasiṣṭha, Gālava, Bhṛgu, Pulastya and Kaśyapa, Atri, Mārkaṇḍeya and Bṛhaspati, Dvita, Trita, Ekata and the four Kumāras, and Aṅgirā, Agastya, Yājñavalkya and Vāmadeva.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
As the women thus talked among themselves and the men among themselves, a number of great sages arrived there, all of them eager to see Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Balarāma. They included Dvaipāyana, Nārada, Cyavana, Devala and Asita, Viśvāmitra, Śatānanda, Bharadvāja and Gautama, Lord Paraśurāma and his disciples, Vasiṣṭha, Gālava, Bhṛgu, Pulastya and Kaśyapa, Atri, Mārkaṇḍeya and Bṛhaspati, Dvita, Trita, Ekata and the four Kumāras, and Aṅgirā, Agastya, Yājṣavalkya and Vāmadeva.
Commentary

While the men were talking to the men and the women were talking to the women, the munis came to see Krsna.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

While various interesting events took place, another interesting event occurred. Sages arrived in a spectacular manner (ājamuḥ). Another version has āyayuḥ. The sages are listed in three verses. Dvaipāyaṇa is mentioned first out of great respect as a father among sages. The others are listed in the order that they arrived. Parāśurāma is addresses as bhagavān because of great respect as one of the ten avatāras. Dvita, Trita, Ekata are described as sons of Brahmā (brahma-putrāḥ) but since they were not actually sons of Brahmā, some say the phrase should be brahma-putraḥ (modifying Aṅgirās). Or brahma-putrāḥ refers to the Kumāras. Others who came were Parāśara, Maitreya and Vaiśampāyana.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

There was a great festival of women talking with women and men talking with men. At that times sags arrived with a special goal. They desired to see directly Kṛṣṇa who attracts all hearts and Balarāma, who gives pleasure to all people. Paraśurāma is called Bhagavān because he is an avatāra. Dvita, Trita, Ekata are described as sons of Brahmā (brahma-putrāḥ). Or it refers to the Kumāras. Ādayaḥ refers to Maitryea, Paila, Vaiśampāyana and other sages. They came suddenly or by invitation.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

Picture a great king of the heart being visited at once by all the headwaters of the river-system that feeds the land — Vyāsa, Nārada, Vasiṣṭha, the Kumāras, Paraśurāma. When Kṛṣṇa is present in Kurukṣetra, the rivers come to the ocean; the ṛṣis arrive not to bestow but to be where Bhagavān is, because that is where the Veda has its source.

Stories
Vyāsa at the source of the Bhāgavatam

Vyāsadeva, who heads this list (Dvaipāyana), is the one who, depressed even after compiling the Vedas, met Nārada at the Sarasvatī and was instructed to write the Bhāgavatam centered on Kṛṣṇa's līlā (SB 1.5–6).

Gauḍīyas read his appearance here as poetic: the compiler of Kṛṣṇa-kathā arrives in person to see the subject of his book.

The four Kumāras turning back

The Kumāras, sons of Brahmā, are the ṛṣis who in Third Canto (SB 3.15) reached Vaikuṇṭha, smelled tulasī on Viṣṇu's feet, and were converted on the spot from impersonalist jñāna to bhakti.

Their presence at Vasudeva's gathering shows the Gauḍīya pattern: even seasoned jñānīs cross over to personalism when Kṛṣṇa is the field of view.
