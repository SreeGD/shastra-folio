Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.88.25-26

Important Words
  • prati-vidhim — the counter-measure
    Prati (against) + vidhi (arrangement). The devas — all of them — lack the prati-vidhi. The chapter's quiet thunderbolt: there is exactly one Lord who knows the counter-arrangement.
  • vaikuṇṭham — Vaikuṇṭha, the kingdom of God
    Vai-kuṇṭha = without kuṇṭha (anxiety, hindrance). The realm where no saṅkaṭa exists. Gauḍīyas: not metaphor — a real, eternal, located dhāma, supremely personal. Even Śiva's destination in saṅkaṭa is Hari's home.
  • bhāsvaram tamasaḥ param — luminous, beyond darkness
    Contiguous-join (bhāsvaram + tamasaḥ + param). Beyond all three modes — including the sāttvika luminosity of the heavenly worlds. Hari's realm is paratrayetantum, beyond the threefold ahaṅkāra Śiva embodies (v.3).
  • nārāyaṇaḥ sākṣāt — Nārāyaṇa directly visible
    Contiguous (nārāyaṇaḥ + sākṣāt). Nara-ayana — the resting-place of all jīvas, and the One who rests on the causal waters. Sākṣāt = directly, in person. Gauḍīyas: Vaikuṇṭha is not vague light; it is Bhagavān visible.
  • nyasta-daṇḍānām — of those who have laid down violence
    Contiguous (nyasta + daṇḍānām). Nyāsa = laying down. The destination is for those who have surrendered the rod of violence — and yet Śiva, fleeing one armed with intent to strike, is welcomed. Hari's threshold is generous.
  • na āvartate gataḥ — having gone there, one does not return
    Contiguous (na + āvartate + gataḥ). The same na nivartante that BG 8.21 names. Gauḍīya: this is the technical mark of nitya-dhāma — a place outside the cycle of return.
The great demigods could only remain silent, not knowing how to counteract the benediction. Then Lord Śiva reached the luminous realm of Vaikuṇṭha, beyond all darkness, where the Supreme Lord Nārāyaṇa is manifest. That realm is the destination of renunciants who have attained peace and given up all violence against other creatures. Going there, one never returns.
Purport

According to Śrīla Śrīdhara Svāmī, Lord Śiva entered the planet of Śvetadvīpa, a special outpost of the spiritual world within the confines of the material universe. There, on a beautiful white island surrounded by the celestial Ocean of Milk, Lord Viṣṇu rests on the serpent bed of Ananta Śeṣa, making Himself available to the demigods when they need His help.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The great demigods could only remain silent, not knowing how to counteract the benediction. Then Lord Śiva reached the luminous realm of Vaikuṇṭha, beyond all darkness, where the Supreme Lord Nārāyaṇa is manifest. That realm is the destination of renunciants who have attained peace and given up all violence against other creatures. Going there, one never returns.
Commentary

Brahma and other leaders of the devatas (suresvarah) could not find an remedy so remained silent. He went to Vaikuntha, beyond the material world (tamasah param).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Brahmā and others did not know the remedy. Śiva went to Śvetadvīpa on Bhū-maṇḍala (on the northern side of the milk ocean), which is an avatāra of Mahā-vaikuṇṭha. It is beyond prakṛti (tamasaḥ) and is always self-manifested (bhāsvaram). And like the Lord this place was without changes of the material world (paarm). vikārāvarti ca tathā hi sthitim āha: that abode it is changeless. (Brahma-sūtra 4.4.19) The reason for the purity of the place is described. Nārāyaṇa personally lives there. He is the highest attainment for those who have given up everything for the Lord, or those who are devoid of attraction and repulsion, or have their hearts fixed on the Lord (śāntānām) and who have given up violence. Having gone there one does not return to saṁsāra. Anāvrttīh śabdād: since the scriptures say this, one does not return. (Brahma-sūtra 4.4.22)

Personal Guidance
Analogy

A fugitive king crosses every kingdom of his world looking for shelter and finds only frightened ministers — until he reaches a kingdom that is not on his map: the kingdom where, by definition, return is impossible. Even Mahādeva, when truly in saṅkaṭa, runs not to his own Kailāsa but to Hari's Vaikuṇṭha. The geography of the chapter is doctrinal.

Stories
Gajendra in the lake

When the elephant-king was seized by the crocodile and his strength was exhausted (SB 8.2-3), he cried out beyond all created devas — uttamaśloka — and Viṣṇu came directly to lift him. Even Brahmā and Śiva could not enter that water; only Hari could. The verse here repeats Gajendra's lesson at the cosmic scale: when Śiva himself is the elephant, only Hari is the rescuer.

Gauḍīyas read 25-26 as Mahādeva's Gajendra-moment: the Lord of the great lord runs to the Lord of all lords.
