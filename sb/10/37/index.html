    Important Words
    • keśī — the horse demon, the long-maned one
      From keśa (mane / hair). The name fixes the asura's most recognizable trait — the streaming mane that scatters clouds. Kṛṣṇa is later honored as Keśava (slayer of Keśī) precisely because of this kill; Gauḍīyas note that even the demon's name becomes glorification once Kṛṣṇa touches him.
    • bhagavān — the Supreme Lord
      Śuka pointedly calls the cowherd boy Bhagavān as he walks out to meet a horse-shaped tornado — the technical six-opulence title is reserved for moments when the līlā's stakes need an aiśvarya-frame. Gauḍīyas read it as Śuka's reminder that aiśvarya is always present beneath the mādhurya.
    • sva-gokulam — His own cowherd village
      Sva — His own. Gokula is not a place Kṛṣṇa visits; it is His. The possessive collapses any doubt about whose ground Keśī has just trespassed onto, and frames the killing as a householder defending His yard.
    • mṛgendra-vat — like a lion
      Mṛga-indra — king of beasts. Śuka places the simile on Keśī (he roars like a lion) but the irony lands on Kṛṣṇa: the actual mṛgendra here is the boy who answered the challenge.
    • ājau — for battle, in the arena
      From ājī — combat. The cowherd field has become a kṣatra-bhūmi. Gauḍīyas note that even when Kṛṣṇa fights, Vraja remains Vraja; the battlefield is just one more pasture.
    Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: The demon Keśī, sent by Kaṁsa, appeared in Vraja as a great horse. Running with the speed of the mind, he tore up the earth with his hooves. The hairs of his mane scattered the clouds and the demigods’ airplanes throughout the sky, and he terrified everyone present with his loud neighing.
    Purport

    When the Supreme Personality of Godhead saw how the demon was frightening His village of Gokula by neighing terribly and shaking the clouds with his tail, the Lord came forward to meet him. Keśī was searching for Kṛṣṇa to fight, so when the Lord stood before him and challenged him to approach, the horse responded by roaring like a lion.

    — A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: The demon Keśī, sent by Kaṁsa, appeared in Vraja as a great horse. Running with the speed of the mind, he tore up the earth with his hooves. The hairs of his mane scattered the clouds and the demigods' airplanes throughout the sky, and he terrified everyone present with his loud neighing.
    Commentary

    The thirty seventh chapter describes the killing of Kesi, Narada praising Krsna’s future pastimes, the thieving activities of Vyomasura, and the killing of Vyomasura.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    The sentence in the first verse should be completed with the word “Keśī came to Vraja.” With the hairs (satā) on his mane he scattered the clouds and airplanes. Thus he was called Keśī (possessing hairs).

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    The word tu indicates that Keśī is distinct from Ariṣṭa. He is described. By his nature he was extremely evil. Because of that he was sent by Kaṁsa. Constantly he crushed the earth, and ran as swift as the mind. His name is explained: his mane scattered the clouds.

    Analogy

    Keśī arrives as a hurricane in horse-form — hooves tearing the earth, mane whipping aside the demigods' airplanes, neighing rattling Gokula. Kṛṣṇa, the cowherd boy, steps forward like the calm eye of the storm and calls the storm by name. The world's loudest terror finds itself summoned by a smiling village child.

    Stories
    Why Kaṁsa keeps sending demons

    Ever since Akrūra was dispatched to fetch Kṛṣṇa for the bow-sacrifice (SB 10.36), Kaṁsa has been hedging — sending one more assassin in case the boy somehow eludes the trap in Mathurā. Keśī is that hedge: a demigod-frightener loosed at Vraja so that even if the invitation fails, the threat will end Kṛṣṇa in His own pastures.

    The Bhāgavatam frames the whole horse-līlā as Kaṁsa's last reach into Vraja. After this, Vraja-līlā closes; Mathurā-līlā opens.

    Important Words
    • aravinda-locanam — the lotus-eyed Lord
      Aravinda (lotus) + locana (eye). Mid-combat, Śuka does not call Him 'the warrior' or 'the fighter' — he calls Him lotus-eyed. The epithet keeps mādhurya present inside a fight scene; Gauḍīyas treasure this juxtaposition as Kṛṣṇa's signature.
    • durāsadaḥ — unapproachable, unassailable
      Said of Keśī — no one could come near him. The word is normally reserved for the Lord (cf. Nṛsiṁha's durāsada form); Śuka is letting the demon borrow it for one verse so the contrast in the next verse cuts deeper.
    • abhimukhaḥ — facing toward
      Abhi (toward) + mukha (face). Kṛṣṇa never turns away. The whole pastime is built on this orientation: the demon comes, the Lord faces him.
    • kham — the sky
      Keśī tries to drink up the sky with his open mouth — a gesture of cosmic appetite. Gauḍīyas read the demon's hunger as the inverse of the gopīs' hunger: both reach for Kṛṣṇa, but one wants to devour and one wants to be devoured by His glance.
    Seeing the Lord standing before him, Keśī ran toward Him in extreme rage, his mouth gaping as if to swallow up the sky. Rushing with furious speed, the unconquerable and unapproachable horse demon tried to strike the lotus-eyed Lord with his two front legs.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    Seeing the Lord standing before him, Keśī ran toward Him in extreme rage, his mouth gaping as if to swallow up the sky. Rushing with furious speed, the unconquerable and unapproachable horse demon tried to strike the lotus-eyed Lord with his two front legs.
    Commentary

    Seeing Krsna, Kesi, whom others could not approach (durasadam), what to speak of defeating him (duratyayah), charged at Krsna.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Keśī tried to kill Kṛṣṇa who had lotus eyes. With eyes blossoming like lotuses, Kṛṣṇa glanced at the demon in disrespect. Or Śukadeva criticizes Keśī, since he did not change his evil nature on seeing the young beauty of Kṛṣṇa. The demon, looking at Kṛṣṇa, ran towards him. The horse was difficult to grab (durāsadaḥ) since he moved quickly. Thus he was difficult to defeat.

    Important Words
    • adhokṣajaḥ — the transcendental Lord, beyond sense-perception
      Adhaḥ-kṣaja — He whom the senses cannot reach. Used here precisely when Kṛṣṇa is most visibly using His arms — Śuka's reminder that even when the Lord acts physically, the actor remains beyond what the senses can grasp.
    • sa-avajñam — contemptuously, with disdain
      Sa (with) + avajñā (contempt). Kṛṣṇa fights without effort and without respect for the enemy — Gauḍīyas read this as the asura-saṁhāra mood: the Lord owes the demon no ceremony.
    • tārkṣya-sutaḥ — the son of Tārkṣya, i.e. Garuḍa
      Garuḍa is named only by patronymic — the simile gains weight from lineage. The Lord's hand moves like His own carrier's beak.
    • vyavasthitaḥ — standing, firmly situated
      After flinging Keśī a hundred bow-lengths, Kṛṣṇa simply stands. The participle conveys svastha-bhāva — self-poised, unmoved. The asura travels; the Lord stays.
    But the transcendental Lord dodged Keśī’s blow and then with His arms angrily seized the demon by the legs, whirled him around in the air and contemptuously threw him the distance of one hundred bow-lengths, just as Garuḍa might throw a snake. Lord Kṛṣṇa then stood there.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    But the transcendental Lord dodged Keśī's blow and then with His arms angrily seized the demon by the legs, whirled him around in the air and contemptuously threw him the distance of one hundred bow-lengths, just as Garuḍa might throw a snake. Lord Kṛṣṇa then stood there.
    Commentary

    When Kesi kicked him, Krsna grabbed his extended legs with his hands, twirled him around, and threw him far off. Krsna remained in the same place.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Kṛṣṇa avoided his blow (tat). Since he is beyond the knowledge of the senses (adhokṣajaḥ), how could Keśī strike him with his hooves? How foolish he was! Having avoided the blow, angry because the demon had spread fear in Vraja, Kṛṣṇa grabbed the demon’s legs. Or, in a playful manner (aruṣā) he grabbed the demon’s legs and then threw him at a great distance. Keśī fainted.

    Analogy

    Kṛṣṇa grabs the horse by the legs, whirls him in the air like a sling, and tosses him a hundred bow-lengths — the way Garuḍa flicks a snake aside before resuming flight. The Lord's contempt is not anger; it is the absence of need to take the enemy seriously.

    Stories
    Garuḍa and the snakes

    Garuḍa, son of Kardama-lineage Tārkṣya, is Viṣṇu's eternal carrier and the natural predator of serpents. The comparison here is not casual — Kaliya was crushed under Kṛṣṇa's feet (SB 10.16), and now Keśī is flung aside as if by Garuḍa Himself.

    Gauḍīyas note that Kṛṣṇa borrows His own carrier's gesture: the Lord and His vehicle share the same disdain for venom.

    Translation
    Keśī's teeth immediately fell out when they touched the Supreme Lord's arm, which to the demon felt as hot as molten iron. Within Keśī's body the Supreme Personality's arm then expanded greatly, like a diseased stomach swelling because of neglect.
    Commentary

    "How could the arm of Krsna, tender as the stem of a blue lotus, enter the mouth of Kesi, filled with fierce teeth?" As Pariksit was anxious about this, the verse answers. With the touch of Krsna’s arm, all those teeth fell out, like objects touched by red hot iron. Moreover, though Krnsa’s arm is as soft as the lotus stem, when it touched Kesi, Kesi felt it as hard and hot as a thunderbolt, just as a jaundiced person perceives sugar candy as bitter. Krsna’s arm began to grow within Kesi’s mouth, just as Krsna expanded his body in Aghasura’s mouth.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    His teeth completely fell out at their roots. Hari-vaṁśa says daśanair mūla-nirmuktaiḥ: his teeth came out by their roots. The teeth had touched the arm of Kṛṣṇa endowed with all powers (bhagavat). The arm did not change. Just by its touch, the teeth fell out, though the teeth were hard as thunderbolts and huge. Viṣṇu Purāṇa says sitābhrāvayavā iva: the teeth were like thunderbolts. The possessive case is missing in the phrase taptam ayaḥ sparśaḥ (touch of hot iron). Taptam is a modifier is not combined with the compound. The teeth fell out as if touching hot iron. The visarga is optionally omitted in a compound thus the phrase is aya-sparśaḥ or ayaḥ-sparśaḥ. Both are permitted. The hand of the Lord with unlimited form (mahātmanaḥ) expanded. The arm expanded in his whole mouth. Though it happened quickly it was not astonishing. It expanded completely (sam—vavṛdhe), blocking the path of air. This was not seen by others since it happened within the horse’s body. A partial analogy is given. It was like untreated indigestion. One sees suffering by such diseases. If the stomach suddenly expands within the body, it can cause a person’s death.

    Important Words
    • kṛṣṇa kṛṣṇa — O Kṛṣṇa, O Kṛṣṇa
      The doubled vocative. Gauḍīyas read repetition of the holy name as the signature of bhāva — Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Haridāsa Ṭhākura both repeat 'Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa' the way Nārada does here. The name is sufficient; saying it twice is already a stuti.
    • vāsudeva — son of Vasudeva
      The Bhāgavatam's preferred personalist title — the Lord with parents, a lineage, a body. Nārada places it among cosmic epithets to insist that the universal Lord is also the boy from Mathurā. Gauḍīyas treat Vāsudeva as the catur-vyūha root that contains the universe yet sits on Yaśodā's lap.
    • akhila-āvāsa — shelter of all beings
      Akhila (entire) + āvāsa (residence). Everything resides in Kṛṣṇa — the universe is His housing. Gauḍīyas pair this with the Vraja-āvāsa: He is the shelter of all and yet chooses Gokula as His specific home.
    • guhā-śayaḥ — lying within the cave (of the heart)
      Guhā (cave) + śaya (lier). The antaryāmin doctrine in one compound — the Lord rests in every heart-cave. Gauḍīyas read this as the paramātmā aspect, distinct from but not separate from the Bhagavān Nārada is now facing.
    • mahā-puruṣaḥ — the Supreme Person
      Mahā (great) + puruṣa (person). Nārada refuses any impersonal framing — the witness in the heart is a Person. The compound is a doctrinal flag against Advaitin reading: the antaryāmin has a face.
    • jyotiḥ — fire, light
      Jyotis — flame. The Upaniṣadic image of fire-in-kindling is being used to name Kṛṣṇa as the latent power inside all forms, visible only when struck.

The non-contradiction is explained through an example. You are the one ātmā of all beings. As in dvau suparṇau: two birds in a tree. You are one within all jīvas. You are many but one as Paramātmā, otherwise you could not be the supreme Lord. This is clear in Vedānta. An example is given. Fire appears differently according to different types of wood but no flame becomes visible without one original flame. Actually one fire alone is situated in the various pieces of wood. Paramātmā is like that. Though you are hidden from sight, you happily rest in a cave of Govardhana. Though you are the witness, without association, you are filled with great powers as the form with a thousand heads (mahā-puruṣaḥ). Or you are the supreme person, who gives himself, who is controlled by the devotee. Thus everything is possible. You are the Lord (īśvaraḥ). By this conclusion unlimited contradictions are easily resolved.

    Commentary

    Nārada shows the activity of Mahā-viṣṇu, since he is the chief puruṣa and suitable for the job of creation. You create the guṇas by your māyā.     Important Words
    • cāṇūram — the wrestler Cāṇūra
      Kaṁsa's chief wrestler. Nārada names him first — the wrestler-pair (Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika) is what Kaṁsa is planning right now in Mathurā. The foretelling lands on the most immediate trap.
    • kaṁsam — King Kaṁsa
      The whole Vraja-līlā has been shaped by Kaṁsa's terror. Nārada names his death in the same breath as the wrestlers' — they are one transaction. Gauḍīyas note: Kaṁsa goes from being the cause of fear to being the proof of līlā.
    • śaṅkha-yavana-murāṇām — of Śaṅkha (Pañcajana), Kālayavana and Mura
      Three demons named in one compound — the next wave of asura-saṁhāra after Mathurā. Each will get his own chapter; Nārada compresses them into a single ablative.
    • pārijāta — the heavenly pārijāta flower
      The flower Kṛṣṇa will take from Indra's garden for Satyabhāmā (SB 10.59). Even an item of Vraja-style cowherd thieving — stealing flowers — is on the prophesied list. Gauḍīyas relish that Kṛṣṇa keeps stealing for His lovers in every realm.
    • vīrya-śulka — with valor as the bride-price
      Vīrya (heroism) + śulka (bride-price). Kṛṣṇa marries the daughters of heroic kings by paying for them with His own valor — He fights for each bride. Gauḍīyas read this as the kṣatra-mood of Dvārakā-līlā, distinct from Vraja's mādhurya, but the same Lord.
    • geyāni-kavibhiḥ — to be sung by poets
      Geya (to be sung) + kavi (poet). Nārada is forecasting the kīrtana-tradition itself. Gauḍīyas read this as Kṛṣṇa's own provision for His name to keep traveling: He acts, the poets sing, the singing is the second body of the deed.

    Commentary

    I am speaking about the pastimes that you perform in the future by seeing them in previous yugas. Sankha refers to Pancajanya. An outline of the future events is given, not all the pastimes. I will see you receive the Syamantaka jewel along with your wife Jambatai (bharyaya). I will see you deliver the brahmana’s son from the city of Mahakala.

    Commentary

    The phrase śaṅkha-yavana- murāṇāṁ narakasya ca (of Śaṅkha, Yavana, Mura and Naraka) should be completed with the word vadham (killing). The sentence continues till verse 20 with the statement “I will see all of this.” Udvāhaṁ vīra-kanyānām means “marriage with daughters of the heroes among the kṣatriyas” or it can mean “O brave Kṛṣṇa! I will see your marriage with many daughters” since generally he married them because of his prowess. Therefore it is then said vīrya-śulkādi-lakṣaṇam: those marriages had a dowry of your valor. The word ādi indicates the daughters also had bhakti. This was true in most cases but sometimes only bhakti was present as in the case of Kālindī. You freed Nṛga from the sin (pāpāt) of taking cows from a brāhmaṇa. Sometimes śāpāt is seen but this is not approved by Śukadeva. A curse will be mentioned later. The word dvārakāyām (in Dvārakā) in verse 17 should apply to the previous and later verses also since it is understood he had gone to Dvārakā for those pastimes. O lord of the universe (jagat-pate)! This is said because Kṛṣṇa showed his powers by riding on Garuḍa and Indra and others came to Dvārakā.     Commentary

    The word ca means api (even) and indicates that for certain all of the demons will be killed. I will see them all killed. This indicates his skill in revealing knowledge. Though two other wrestlers, Śala and Toṣaka, were killed, the plural is used (anyān), since killing the chief wrestlers all others were equivalent of being dead. Or because they were the best among the best, they are counted as all of them. “How can all of them be killed at once?” O Lord, you are capable of doing everything (prabho)! Or “Why must this be done so quickly?” O Lord, you are the lord the Yadus! You must do this to help the devotees. The list of demons killed is not given in chronological order. They are listed only to ask for the Lord’s assistance. I will see others like Mura killed also. (verse 16) I will see you marry the daughters of kṣatriyas or the daughters of heroes among kṣatriays (vīra-kanyānām). Or O hero (vīra) you will marry daughters because of your heroism. Thus you pay a dowry of defeating and stealing away with a show of valor (vīya-śukla). Ādi indicates that some such as the wives of Kāliya simply offered themselves with bhakti. You will free Nṛga from his sin, when he unknowingly took cows from a brāhmaṇa. Another version has śapāt (from a curse). However, the word curse is not used in describing the event later, concerning a sage. This happened near Dvārakā, where the lizard had fallen in the well and was saved by Kṛṣṇa, who came out of the city to rescue him. O lord of the universe! You show your powers such as riding on Garuḍā when Indra comes there. (verse 17) Seeing the form of the Lord when he went to Mahākālapura, the brāhmaṇa attained liberation. This is described in Bhāgavatāmṛta. It will also be described in the text later. (verse 18) Later, after the killing of Pauṇḍraka, Śiśupāla would be killed at Yudhiṣṭhira’s sacrifice.     Analogy

    Nārada unrolls a scroll of the future like a herald reading the king's itinerary: in two days, the wrestlers; then Kālayavana, Mura, Naraka; then the pārijāta; then the brides; then Nṛga, Syamantaka, the brāhmaṇa's son; then Pauṇḍraka, Kāśī, Dantavakra, Śiśupāla. The deeds have not happened yet, but for the devotee who sees with Kṛṣṇa's eye they are already sung.

    Stories
    Nārada as forward-narrator of Canto 10

    These six verses are the Bhāgavatam's table of contents for the rest of Canto 10 and into Canto 11. Every pastime Nārada names — Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika (10.43-44), the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa (10.43), Kaṁsa (10.44), Kālayavana (10.51), Mura and Naraka (10.59), Nṛga (10.64), Syamantaka (10.56), the brāhmaṇa's son (10.45 and 10.89), Pauṇḍraka and Kāśī (10.66), Dantavakra (10.78), Śiśupāla at the Rājasūya (10.74) — is fulfilled in chapters to come.

    Gauḍīyas note Nārada is doing exactly what the kavis will later do — geyāni kavibhiḥ bhuvi, sung by poets on earth — but he sings them before they happen. The devotee's vision is not bound by time.

    Kavibhiḥ bhuvi — Vyāsa's own self-reference

    When Nārada says these deeds will be 'sung by poets on earth,' he is, in a self-aware Bhāgavatam moment, gesturing at Vyāsa — his own disciple — who will compose the Bhāgavatam precisely to sing them. Nārada gives Vyāsa his life's assignment inside the very text Vyāsa is writing.

    Gauḍīyas read this recursion as evidence that the Bhāgavatam is bhāgavata-pravara-paramparā: Nārada to Vyāsa to Śuka to Parīkṣit to us.

    Analogy

    Nārada's final preview: Kṛṣṇa will sit on Arjuna's chariot, holding the reins, and from that small seat will unwind whole armies the way a child unwinds a ball of thread. Kāla-rūpa — time-form — is the same Kṛṣṇa, just turned slightly so the burden of the earth slides off.

    Stories
    The chariot-driver who is also Time

    In Bhagavad-gītā 11.32, Arjuna's friend reveals Himself as Time: kālo 'smi loka-kṣaya-kṛt — 'I am Time, the destroyer of worlds, here engaged to destroy.' Nārada is forecasting that very disclosure: the cowherd boy in front of him will, in two generations, drive Arjuna's chariot and reveal that the chariot-driver is also Time.

    Gauḍīyas hold the two images together: the same hands that held the reins for Arjuna held the rope for Yaśodā. Kāla and Vraja are not different Persons.

    Important Words
    • viśuddha-vijñāna-ghanam — full of perfectly pure spiritual awareness
      Viśuddha (pure) + vijñāna (realized knowledge) + ghana (dense, concentrated). Kṛṣṇa is dense pure consciousness. Gauḍīyas read ghana as the personalist refusal to dissolve the Lord into formless ākāśa — He is concentrated, not dispersed.
    • sva-saṁsthayā — situated in His own original form
      Sva (own) + saṁsthā (state, situation). The Lord rests in His own form, not borrowed from prakṛti. Gauḍīyas read this as the doctrinal foundation of sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha: Kṛṣṇa's body is not material at all.
    • amogha-vāñchitam — whose desires are never frustrated
      A-mogha (un-failing) + vāñchita (desired). Kṛṣṇa wants nothing that does not happen. Gauḍīyas couple this with His desire to relish rasa with His devotees — His relishing is also amogha.
    • nivṛtta-māyā-guṇa-pravāham — eternally aloof from the current of māyā's modes
      The Lord stands outside the flow of guṇas even while producing them. This is the bheda half of acintya-bhedābheda — the Lord is one with His energies and yet not in their stream.
    • bhagavantam-īmahi — let us approach the Lord
      Bhagavantam (the six-opulence Lord, accusative) + īmahi (let us approach, Vedic 1st-pers. plural). Nārada speaks in we — the devotee never approaches alone, always with the paramparā. Gauḍīyas read īmahi as the prayerful plural of saraṇāgati: 'we, together, take shelter.'
    Let us approach You, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, for shelter. You are full of perfectly pure spiritual awareness and are always situated in Your original identity. Since Your will is never thwarted, You have already achieved all possible desirable things, and by the power of Your spiritual energy You remain eternally aloof from the flow of the qualities of illusion.

    Commentary

    After describing the future pastimes of Krsna, Narada offers his respects in two verses. I offer my respects to you, the Lord, concentrated Brahman or spiritual essence, the object of pure realization. All the desires of the devotees reside completely in you who exist eternally along with your associates. You fulfill all those desires because of your mercy. By your or your devotees’ energy, all the influence of material gunas is daily destroyed.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    Śrīdhara Svāmī explains that viśuddha-vijṣāna-ghanam means exclusively (kevalam) the one form of knowledge. Śruti says na cakśuṣā gṛhayte rūpam asya: his form cannot be seen by the eye.(Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad) Yam evaiṣa vṛṇute tena labhyas tasyaiṣa ātmā vivṛṇote tanuṁ svām: the Lord can be attained only by his choice and then he reveals his form to that person. (Katha Upaniṣad) Because the form is self-revealing it is called a form of knowledge alone. Thought this form takes many forms Kṛṣṇa is the aṁśī. Akrūra also says bahu-mūrty-eka-mūrtikam: you are one form with many forms. (SB 10.40.7) The prefix vi in viśuddha means “one,” viśiṣṭa, special, or extraordinary. Therefore (ataeva in Śrīdhara’s commentary)-- because you are this form of knowledge, established by realization--by being firmly situated in your svarūpa, the form of the highest bliss, you completely possess all objects (sva-saṁsthayā samāpta-sarvārtham). Ātmā is the svarūpa which is the shelter of all things, of all causes of knowledge. Since you are ātmā, you are the form of highest bliss, the shelter of the highest, unconditional prema. Because of this, by your nature you have attained all objects since by being bliss itself, everything is yours. Etasyaivānandasyānyāni bhūtāni mātrām upajīvanti: all living being live because of his expansion of bliss. (Brhad-āraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.3.32) Ko hy evānyāt kaḥ prāṇyāt yad eṣa ākāśa aṇando na syāt: if the Lord were not blissful, how could any being exist? (Taittirīya Upaniṣad) Thus the meaning of samāpta-sarvārtham is “Because you are bliss, you have many forms by your nature.” Nārada realized that “form of knowledge” in the blissful pastimes of Kṛṣṇa. Though all your desires are already fulfilled, you have desires (vāṣchitam). Though you have attained all desires, you attain them in pastimes—actions to attain the desires. Those desires are always fulfilled (amogha), not being produced at some point in time. Those desires do not arise from material māyā. If they were material they would cause bondage. You are eternally free from the action of the guṇas by your śakti (sva-tejasā). This means that your form, qualities and pastimes are beyond māyā. Therefore I worship Bhagavān, who exists by his own support, not by the agency of Brahman. Or I worship that form which is superior to vijṣānam, famous among the people. It is pure (śuddha). I surrender to Bhagavān, the condensed form of vijṣānam, full of bliss or the condensed form the famous Brahman. “How can vijṣānam be a form with hands and feet? How can Bhagavān have hands and feet?” His outstanding quality proved by scripture is stated: by his svarūpa-śakti his form has all qualities (sva-saṁsthayā samāpta-sarvārtham). Parāsya śaktir vividhaiva śruyate: the śaktis of the Lord are many. (Ṣvetāsvatara Upaniṣad) Na cakṣuṣā gṛhate rūpam asya: his form cannot be seen by the material eye. His desires never go unfulfilled. By his iccha-śakti he can reveal or cause the disappearance of anything. “Well I have a śakti known as material māyā. Māyāṁ tu prakṛtiṁ vidyān māyinam tu maheśvaram (Know the material energy and its Lord, the cause of māyā). I am touched by that fault.” By your svarūpa-śakti (sva-tejasā) you are never influenced by the action of the guṇas.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    You are pure knowledge. What can be offered or communicated to you? Because you, not dependent on anything, show causeless mercy, you will do everything I have stated (sva-saṁsthayā). You never become tired from anything you do (amogha). Or “Why do you ask that I destroy fear of Kaṁsa for the devotees?” You destroy fear of saṁsāra. By your powers (sva-tejasā) you destroy the influence of the guṇas of māyā.

    Important Words
    • sva-āśrayam — self-contained, depending only on Himself
      Sva (own) + āśraya (shelter). The Lord shelters in Himself; no other ground supports Him. Gauḍīyas read this as the asymmetry of bhakti — we take shelter in Him, He takes shelter in nothing.
    • ātma-māyayā — by His own creative potency
      Ātmā (own) + māyā (energy). His own śakti builds the world. Gauḍīyas distinguish ātma-māyā (His personal internal energy) from the externalizing material māyā — both are His, but the first is intimate.
    • manuṣya-vigraham — having taken a human form (for battle)
      Manuṣya (human) + vigraha (form, body, also: combat). Kṛṣṇa has chosen a human body precisely so He can participate in human battle. Gauḍīyas treasure this — the Lord did not merely visit humanity; He took the form to engage as one of us.
    • yadu-vṛṣṇi-sātvatām — of the Yadu, Vṛṣṇi and Sātvata dynasties
      Three lineage-names together. Kṛṣṇa is the dhurya — the chief — of all three. Gauḍīyas note that the Lord's incarnational pedigree is named in detail: His descent has an address.
    • krīḍa-artham — for the sake of play
      Krīḍā (play) + artha (purpose). The whole avatāra is for play. Even the warfare is play. Gauḍīyas keep this in front: nothing the Lord does is grim work; everything is līlā.
    I bow down to You, the supreme controller, who are dependent only on Yourself. By Your potency You have constructed the unlimited particular arrangements of this universe. Now you have appeared as the greatest hero among the Yadus, Vṛṣṇis and Sātvatas and have chosen to participate in human warfare.
    Gauḍīya Ācāryas
    Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
    Translation
    I bow down to You, the supreme controller, who are dependent only on Yourself. By Your potency You have constructed the unlimited particular arrangements of this universe. Now you have appeared as the greatest hero among the Yadus, Vṛṣṇis and Sātvatas and have chosen to participate in human warfare.
    Commentary

    There is no one like you. You have the power to control all others (isvara). You are not under the shelter of anyone and all others must take shelter of you. (svasrayam). (He thus describes the power of the Lord as not being dependent on any other person.) You create this variegated universe by your dependent maya. Your only object is play. But today you will accept combat with the human Kamsa, becoming enemy of Kamsa just as you became enemy of Kesi, Kamsa’s very life. I offer respects to you. All this is your play, in order protect the Yadus,Vrsnis and Satvatas."

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    “Why do you deny that I do not have guṇas?” Or “Why do you deny guṇas in the spiritual Vaikuṇṭha? It is like denying darkness in the sun (an obvious fact).” The answer is given in this verse. By your śakti you create the world. Nārada denies any impurity in the Lord. And in the material world your pastimes are pure. Just as, before the agitation of prakṛti, your pure pastime of glancing accepts prakṛti, so the birth pastimes of you and your associates, pure because of being related to the Lord, accept the Yadu dynasty, which was different by nature previously and afterwards. Just as you are the best within the dynasties, the Yadus are the best among all the intimate dynasties. Vṛṣṇis, Yadus and Sātvatas are mentioned separately to show the superiority of the Yadus. “What is the purpose of my appearing in the material world?” You have brought (atta) a human form, Brahman with human form, for displaying pastimes for the devotees in the material world. Or, you have accepted a battle (vigraha) which is human-like, for performing your pastimes. Abhyātta is another version with the same meaning. You have done this now (adya), at the time of your avatāra. Or this is a summary of events to take place. The word viśuddha should be understood in both verses. The first part of the verse shows the Lord’s expansion as the puruṣa and the second part shows his original form for pastimes.

    Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    You manifest a human form in all ways (abhi or adya). You are the leader (dhūryam) of the Yadus. The word Yadus also included the Bhojas and others, but the Yadus, Vṛṣṇis and Sātvatas are the most prominent so they alone are mentioned. This is more particular than the other general statements of the Lord’s powers. Ātma-māyayā means “by special śakti which is eternity, knowledge and bliss.” Krīḍārtham (for play) should be added to all the statements in the verse. Or though you are the form of pure knowledge, you have appeared for playing, to benefit the world by your great mercy. Displaying your unlimited powers, I praise you and offer respects. Though you are lord of the universe, you are the shelter of the Yadus (svāśrayam). Though you ignore others, you perform pastimes of unlimited variety (vinirmitāśeṣa-viśeṣa-kalpanam). Thus you manifest a human form. Or I offer respects to you. “How can you, a brāhmaṇa, worthy of my respect, offer respects to me, an inferior?” Your manifestation of power overrides normal behavior. You are the supreme Lord, though you have a human form. Though you are the best of the Yadus, you live in Gokula (svāśrayam). To play you, manifest variegated pastimes. Or I offer respects without fear (abhi). I am qualified to offer respects since my father respected you with devotion on seeing your powers. In Dvārakā, restricting his powers, the Lord worshipped Nārada. Or you should forgive my offense. I surrender to you. He then recites this verse. I have done this to benefit the devatās and Yādavas. You know this since you are the Lord. “This means that you have causes suffering for my parents.” You can do everything just by being situated in Gokula (sva-saṁsthayā). Or everything cannot be accomplished by staying in Gokula. You can destroy their suffering by going to Mathurā. “My parents will suffer on seeing me attacked by the wrestlers when I go.” Without effort, by your desire, everything is achieved (amogha). “How can I forgive you for giving information to Kaṁsa so that he tied up my parents? Could you not avoid my going to Mathurā?” You can forgive because you have unlimited compassion. By your powers you constantly manifest your quality of mercy (sva-tejasā nitya-nivṛtta-māyā-guṇa-pravāham). You are bhagavān, who manifests unlimited qualities like mercy. Thus I surrender to you. The plural is used to include his students. “Great offenses should be forgiven by the great devotees, rather than by approaching me.” (verse 23 answers) You are full of all powers (īśvaram). Moreover, you are the shelter of your devotees (svāśrayam). Moreover by your powers, you unlimitedly glorify your devotees. For play you manifest a human form and act as the chief of the Yadus. You appeared in the Yadu dynasty to benefit the world by manifesting unlimited qualities of Bhagavān. Therefore you should also benefit me. You should forgive my offense or ask your parents. That is now your burden. Or as the chief of the Yadus, you should forgive. Then all others such as your father will forgive.

    Important Words
    • praṇipatya — having bowed down respectfully
      Pra-ni-pat — to fall forward in full prostration. The stuti ends in body-language. Gauḍīyas read praṇipāta as the proper close of every prayer: words give way to body, body gives way to silence.
    • abhyanujñātaḥ — having been given leave
      Abhi-anu-jñā — to grant permission. Nārada does not leave on his own; Kṛṣṇa releases him. Even the devotee's exit is at the Lord's pleasure.
    • darśana-utsavaḥ — with the festival of having seen Him
      Darśana (seeing) + utsava (festival). One look at Kṛṣṇa is a festival. Gauḍīyas treasure this compound — it names the central currency of Vaiṣṇavism: not enlightenment, not liberation, but the festival of seeing Him.
    • bhāgavata-pravaraḥ — most eminent of devotees
      Repeated from verse 9 — the Bhāgavatam frames Nārada with the same title at the start and the end of the stuti. The bracket is deliberate: this is what bhāgavata-pravara behavior looks like.

      Vyoman — the sky, empty space. The demon's name is the void itself. Gauḍīyas note: the false friend is named after emptiness — he looks like presence but is absence wearing presence's clothes.
    • gopāla-veṣa-dhṛk — wearing the disguise of a cowherd boy
      Gopāla (cowherd) + veṣa (costume) + dhṛk (wearer). The most painful detail — he wears the dress of Kṛṣṇa's own role. Gauḍīyas read this as the warning about pseudo-devotees: the wolf in vaiṣṇava-cloth is the most dangerous predator in Vraja's woods.
    • corāyitaḥ — pretending to be (playing as) a thief
      Denominative from cora — acting the thief. Within the game he plays a thief; in reality he is a thief. Gauḍīyas relish the layering: the demon's true nature shows through his chosen game-role.

      Guhā (cave) + pidhāna (covering, seal). The same word for 'cave' is used as in verse 10's antaryāmin-image — the heart's cave. Gauḍīyas read the smashing of the cave-seal as the structural echo of the bhakta's prayer: 'remove the stone over my heart.'
    • niḥsārya — having led out
      Niḥ (out) + sṛ (to move). Kṛṣṇa physically conducts the boys out — He does not merely break the seal. Gauḍīyas treasure this — the Lord's rescue is escorted; He walks His devotees out of the dark, He does not leave them to find their own way.
    • stūyamānaḥ-suraiḥ-gopaiḥ — being praised by demigods and cowherd boys
      The two choirs sing at once — aiśvarya-praise from above (suraiḥ) and mādhurya-praise from beside (gopaiḥ). Gauḍīyas note that Kṛṣṇa receives both, and that the gopas' voice is given equal weight in the verse.
    • sva-gokulam — His own Gokula
      Sva (own) + gokula (cow-village). The chapter opened (verse 1-2) with Keśī threatening sva-gokulam; it closes with Kṛṣṇa walking back into sva-gokulam. The chapter is bracketed by this possessive — His cowherd village remains His.

    Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
    Commentary

    He took the boys from the place of suffering (kṛcchrataḥ), the cave. Praised by the devatās and the cowherds following him, he entered Gokula with great joy (pra—viveśa). Gokula is mentioned as Kṛṣṇa’s own (sva-gokulam) because he was worried about the future pastimes in which he would leave. In his heart he concluded that he would not give up Gokula to go far away. This will be revealed later. Instead of gopaiḥ devaiś ca often suraiḥ gopaiḥ is seen. This means that the devatās praised him and he joyfully entered Gokula with the cowherd boys.

Early in the morning on the day after Kaṁsa had ordered him to bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā, Akrūra prepared his chariot and set off for Gokula. As he traveled, he thought as follows: "I am about to attain the great good fortune of seeing Śrī Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, which are worshiped by Brahmā, Rudra and the other demigods. Although Kaṁsa is an enemy of the Supreme Lord and His devotees, still, it is by Kaṁsa's grace that I will get this great boon of seeing the Lord. When I first catch sight of His lotus feet, all my sinful reactions will be destroyed at once. I will descend from my chariot and fall at the feet of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, and even though I have been sent by Kaṁsa, the omniscient Śrī Kṛṣṇa will certainly harbor no animosity toward me." As Akrūra thus thought to himself, he arrived in Gokula at sunset. Alighting from his chariot in the cowherd pasture, he began rolling about in the dust in great ecstasy. Then Akrūra continued on to Vraja. When he saw Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma he fell at Their lotus feet, and both the Lords embraced him. Afterward They brought him to Their residence, inquired from him about the comfort of his trip and honored him in various ways-offering him water for washing his feet, arghya, a seat and so forth. They relieved him of his fatigue by massaging his feet and served him a delicious banquet. Mahārāja Nanda also honored Akrūra with many sweet words.

    Analogy

    Kṛṣṇa breaks the boulder, leads the trapped cowherd boys out of the cave's darkness into daylight, and walks home to Gokula — flanked by demigod-praise from above and friend-praise from beside. The chapter ends as it should: cowherd village restored, friends recovered, day continuing.

    Stories
    The cave that becomes a temple

    In Caitanya-caritāmṛta, the Six Gosvāmīs walking Vraja identified many of the original līlā-spots — including the site associated with this Vyomāsura pastime. The cave where the boys were trapped becomes, in Gauḍīya pilgrimage tradition, a place where devotees remember Kṛṣṇa's rescue and identify their own heart-cave with the boys' cave: the same Lord who broke that boulder is asked to break the stone over our own captivity.

    Gauḍīyas read this last verse as a model for sādhana: the Lord breaks the seal, leads the captives out, and is praised by both the high (devas) and the near (gopas) — both registers of love at once.
