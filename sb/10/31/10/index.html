Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.31.10

Important Words
  • prahasitam — the smiling
    Past participle of pra-√has, 'to smile fully'. The Gopī-gīta returns to Kṛṣṇa's smile four times (vv. 6, 10, 12, 17). Gauḍīya rasa-śāstra treats the smile (mandasmita) as an anubhāva of mādhurya that ignites bhāva in the bhakta — the visible signal of His being moved by them, which moves them in return.
  • prema-vīkṣaṇam — loving glances
    Compound: prema (love) + vīkṣaṇam (glance). Distinct from generic dṛśā — these are the loaded glances of premī to premī. The eye-contact that the gopīs are now starved of is the eye-contact of one who knows He is loved by them and is, in the glance itself, returning that knowledge.
  • rahasi — in solitary places
    Locative of rahas, 'secret place'. The Gauḍīya code-word for nikuñja-līlā — the secluded grove-pastimes that are mādhurya's home. The gopīs are remembering specifically the private register of His love, not its public displays.
  • kuhaka — O cheater
    Vocative — 'magician, deceiver, trickster'. Pointed term: a kuhaka conjures something that is not what it appeared to be. The gopīs accuse Him of arousing a love whose fulfillment He then withdraws — and yet the address is itself a confession that they have been caught, not abandoned.
  • kṣobhayanti — they agitate
    Present indicative third plural of √kṣubh, 'to shake, churn'. Same root from which the Bhāgavatam describes Yamunā churned by Kṛṣṇa's leap into Kāliya's pool. The very memories meant for meditation churn the mind that meditates — viraha is felt as physical agitation, not as quiet sorrow.
Your smiles, Your sweet, loving glances, the intimate pastimes and confidential talks we enjoyed with You — all these are auspicious to meditate upon, and they touch our hearts. But at the same time, O deceiver, they very much agitate our minds.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Your smiles, Your sweet, loving glances, the intimate pastimes and confidential talks we enjoyed with You—all these are auspicious to meditate upon, and they touch our hearts. But at the same time, O deceiver, they very much agitate our minds.
Commentary

"When we do not see you, all objects related to you also become a cause of suffering for us." "Though we try to forget your sweet smile filled with love (prahistam), your intimate exchanges with us (viharam), and all your conversations (samvidah), since they give sorrow, we cannot do so, as they have affected our hearts. (hrdi sprsah). Meditation gives the highest bliss, but thinking of these things (your smile, your glance, your exchanges and your conversations), disturbs our minds. When these things enter the mind, they first give bliss (dhyana mangalam) , but then after a few seconds, they give the most intense sorrow. Therefore, you are the greatest cheater (kuhaka). When the rogue feeds you cakes, at first they seem tasty, but after some time, they give intense pain, and then kill you."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The first meaning of the verse is as follows. “O gopīs with intelligence! Why are you so attracted to me, hard to attain? If you have that attraction, then be content with hearing about me.” Being unsettled by that statement, in three verses overcome with pūrva-rāga, they find fault with his actions. The second meaning is as follows. “We cannot live for a long time on hope alone.” They then recite the verse. The various words express verbal activity (laughing, glancing, playing). Vīkṣaṇam is sometimes seen instead of vīkṣitam. On seeing the gopīs he smiles decisively out of eager emotion. From his natural smile he smiles excellently. And with the smile he glances with love. Then he plays with his friends. That is auspicious for meditation. By thinking of that, desire for meeting becomes strong, since his actions reveal his emotions. Then he goes far off alone and jokes, playing his flute. Then those words touch the heart. Everything has become completely internalized. The word yā indicates astonishment. The phrase yā hṛdi spṛśaḥ applies to all the previous items, though the gender would change. The excellence of the items is from lesser to more. O cheater! They say this out of their suffering. He is the greatest among all deceivers. All of this brings pain to our minds for certain (hi). The plural naḥ (our) is used to show the proof. The reason for the pain is stated: you are enticing us (priya). Our minds are filled with sorrow by being attracted to you. You on the other hand cheat us. With that in mind they say “O cheater!”

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“Unintelligent women! By force, hear my topics, as a medicine.” Or we cannot maintain our lives by a hope alone. This verse describes this. Your (te) pure smiles (prahasitam), your glances with prema, your play, which produce happiness simply by thinking of them, disturb our minds. Or these elements easily produce meditation in prema. dhyānāyanaṁ prahasitaṁ bahulādharoṣṭha- bhāsāruṇāyita-tanu-dvija-kunda-paṅkti One should meditate upon the Lord’s laughter, an easy object of meditation, which shows his teeth like a row of jasmine buds with his shining, full, red lips. SB 3.28.33 These elements please the heart. Or the conversations, just entering the ears touch the heart with disturbance. All these elements agitate us with desire (kṣobhayanti). Sāṁvidaḥ by proximity can modify hṛdi spṛśaḥ. Hrdi spṛśaḥ being at the end, can be connected to the whole list of elements. The smile and glance are mental attractions, arising from his pure bhāva, his mental state in prema. The play is a bodily attraction. The conversations are verbal attractions. The later ones are effective in creating disturbance. Hi indicates certainty. The cause of the disturbance is given. O beloved (priya)! If we did not love you, the disturbance would not take place. Because we love you alone, we are always filled with sorrow and mental disturbance. You cheat us. O cheater (kuhaka)! Or just on seeing the beloved we become joyful (prahasitam). We look at him with smiles of prema. Thinking of him produces auspiciousness. These are cause of our hope. There were conversations. “I will fulfill all promises. Certainly your desires will be fulfilled.” By now ignoring us, doing the opposite of your words, we must conclude that you are a cheater. O cheater (kuhaka)! Or “Women and especially chaste women should have composure.” They speak in misery. Your smile, glance, play and conversations throw us into confusion. They break our composure. That we still live, with this remembrance, is because of our supreme composure and determination.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

A fire and the wind that fans it cannot decide whether they are kin or enemies — they make each other larger. Kṛṣṇa's smiles and glances are like that wind: meditated upon, they are maṅgala; encountered again in memory, they agitate the mind they just consoled. The gopīs name Him kuhaka — cheater — because He is both fire and fan in their single heart.
