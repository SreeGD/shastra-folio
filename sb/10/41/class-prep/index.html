Chapter 41 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 41.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: While Akrūra was still offering prayers, the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa withdrew His form that He had revealed in the water, just as an actor winds up his performance.

Essence. While Akrūra still prays, Kṛṣṇa withdraws the Viṣṇu vision from the water, the way an actor concludes a performance. Visions are granted and then withdrawn at the Lord's discretion — they are not possessions to clutch.

BG 41.2

When Akrūra saw the vision disappear, he came out of the water and quickly finished his various ritual duties. He then returned to the chariot, astonished.

Essence. Seeing the vision disappear, Akrūra finishes his ritual duties and returns to the chariot, astonished. Even astonishing experience returns the devotee to ordinary duty; rituals don't compete with vision, they continue it.

BG 41.3

Lord Kṛṣṇa asked Akrūra: Have you seen something wonderful on the earth, in the sky or in the water? From your appearance, We think you have.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa playfully asks Akrūra: have you seen something wonderful on earth, sky or water? Your appearance suggests so. The Lord notices the marks of darśana on a devotee's face — He doesn't pretend not to know.

BG 41.4

Śrī Akrūra said: Whatever wonderful things the earth, sky or water contain, all exist in You. Since You encompass everything, when I am seeing You, what have I not seen?

Essence. Akrūra replies: whatever wonderful things earth, sky, or water contain all exist in You; seeing You, what have I not seen? After cosmic vision, every separate wonder is folded back into the Person who contains them.

BG 41.5

And now that I am seeing You, O Supreme Absolute Truth, in whom reside all amazing things on the earth, in the sky and in the water, what amazing things could I see in this world?

Essence. Now that I see You — Supreme Absolute Truth, in whom all amazing things reside — what amazing things could I see in this world? Akrūra realizes Kṛṣṇa is not merely his nephew but the totality.

BG 41.6

With these words, Akrūra, the son of Gāndinī, began driving the chariot onward. At the end of the day he arrived in Mathurā with Lord Balarāma and Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. With these words Akrūra drives the chariot onward, arriving in Mathurā at day's end with Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa. Realization is followed by ordinary forward motion; vision becomes driving.

BG 41.7

Wherever they passed along the road, O King, the village people came forward and looked upon the two sons of Vasudeva with great pleasure. In fact, the villagers could not withdraw their eyes from Them.

Essence. Wherever they pass, village people come forward and cannot withdraw their eyes from the two sons of Vasudeva. The Lord's beauty draws ordinary people involuntarily — devotion at first sight is real.

BG 41.8

Nanda Mahārāja and the other residents of Vṛndāvana, having reached Mathurā ahead of the chariot, had stopped at a garden on the outskirts of the city to wait for Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma.

Essence. Nanda and other Vṛndāvana residents reach Mathurā ahead, stop at a garden on the outskirts, and wait. Sometimes parents and well-wishers arrive first and wait — devotional support precedes the protagonist's entry.

BG 41.9

After joining Nanda and the others, the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa, the controller of the universe, took humble Akrūra’s hand in His own and, smiling, spoke as follows.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, controller of the universe, takes humble Akrūra's hand and smiles. The Supreme takes the devotee's hand — initiated greetings of intimacy come from the Lord, not the devotee.

BG 41.10

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] Take the chariot and enter the city ahead of us. Then go home. After resting here a while, we will go to see the city.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa tells Akrūra to take the chariot into the city ahead and go home; They will rest and then come. The Lord doesn't insist on Akrūra accompanying Him to the demon's city — He spares devotees from witnessing certain things.

BG 41.11

Śrī Akrūra said: O master, without the two of You I shall not enter Mathurā. I am Your devotee, O Lord, so it is not fair for You to abandon me, since You are always affectionate to Your devotees.

Essence. Akrūra protests: without You two I will not enter Mathurā; it is not fair to abandon Your devotee, since You are always affectionate to devotees. Real devotion negotiates with the Lord rather than passively accepting separation.

BG 41.12

Come, let us go to my house with Your elder brother, the cowherd men and Your companions. O best of friends, O transcendental Lord, in this way please grace my house with its master.

Essence. Akrūra invites Kṛṣṇa to come to his house with Balarāma, cowherd men and companions, so the master can grace his home. Hospitality is part of devotion — the devotee wants the Lord's company at his actual dwelling.

BG 41.13

I am simply an ordinary householder attached to ritual sacrifices, so please purify my home with the dust of Your lotus feet. By that act of purification, my forefathers, the sacrificial fires and the demigods will all become satisfied.

Essence. Akrūra calls himself a simple householder attached to ritual sacrifices and asks to be purified by the dust of the Lord's feet; this will satisfy forefathers, sacrificial fires, and demigods. One household visit purifies many invisible relationships.

BG 41.14

By bathing Your feet, the exalted Bali Mahārāja attained not only glorious fame and unequaled power but also the final destination of pure devotees.

Essence. By bathing the Lord's feet, exalted Bali Mahārāja attained glorious fame, unequaled power, and the final destination of pure devotees. Foot-bathing is small in form and enormous in effect.

BG 41.15

The water of the river Ganges has purified the three worlds, having become transcendental by bathing Your feet. Lord Śiva accepted that water on his head, and by that water’s grace the sons of King Sagara attained to heaven.

Essence. The Ganges purified three worlds because it bathed Your feet; Lord Śiva accepted that water on his head; Sagara's sons attained heaven by it. The grace touched by His feet ripples through whole cosmoses.

BG 41.16

O Lord of lords, master of the universe, O You whose glories it is most pious to hear and chant! O best of the Yadus, O You whose fame is recounted in excellent poetry! O Supreme Lord Nārāyaṇa, I offer You my obeisances.

Essence. O Lord of lords, whose glories are most pious to hear and chant, best of the Yadus, fame recounted in excellent poetry — Supreme Nārāyaṇa, obeisances. The devotee summarizes praise in stacked titles; layered address itself is worship.

BG 41.17

The Supreme Lord said: I will come to Your house with My elder brother, but first I must satisfy My friends and well-wishers by killing the enemy of the Yadu clan.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa says: I will come to your house with My elder brother, but first I must satisfy My friends by killing the enemy of the Yadu clan. The Lord schedules hospitality after the work; visits to devotees follow the elimination of demons.

BG 41.18

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus addressed by the Lord, Akrūra entered the city with a heavy heart. He informed King Kaṁsa of the success of his mission and then went home.

Essence. Akrūra enters the city with a heavy heart, informs Kaṁsa of the mission's success, and goes home. Devotional service sometimes involves reporting back to enemies; even painful errands are completed honestly.

BG 41.19

Lord Kṛṣṇa desired to see Mathurā, so toward evening He took Lord Balarāma and the cowherd boys with Him and entered the city.

Essence. Toward evening Kṛṣṇa, wanting to see Mathurā, takes Balarāma and the cowherd boys and enters the city. The Lord moves through cities at twilight; entering the demon's capital is His own initiative, not forced.

BG 41.20-23

The Lord saw Mathurā, with its tall gates and household entrances made of crystal, its immense archways and main doors of gold, its granaries and other storehouses of copper and brass, and its impregnable moats. Beautifying the city were pleasant gardens and parks. The main intersections were fashioned of gold, and there were mansions with private pleasure gardens, along with guildhalls and many other buildings. Mathurā resounded with the calls of peacocks and pet turtledoves, who sat in the small openings of the lattice windows and on the gem-studded floors, and also on the columned balconies and on the ornate rafters in front of the houses. These balconies and rafters were adorned with vaidūrya stones, diamonds, crystal quartz, sapphires, coral, pearls and emeralds. All the royal avenues and commercial streets were sprinkled with water, as were the side roads and courtyards, and flower garlands, newly grown sprouts, parched grains and rice had been scattered about everywhere. Gracing the houses’ doorways were elaborately decorated pots filled with water, which were bedecked with mango leaves, smeared with yogurt and sandalwood paste, and encircled by flower petals and ribbons. Near the pots were flags, rows of lamps, bunches of flowers and the trunks of banana and betel-nut trees.

Essence. Mathurā has crystal gates, gold archways, copper-brass storehouses, impregnable moats, pleasant gardens, gold intersections, mansions with pleasure gardens, decorated pots and rice at every doorway. The demonic capital is no less beautifully built than a devotee's home.

BG 41.24

The women of Mathurā hurriedly assembled and went forth to see the two sons of Vasudeva as They entered the city on the King’s road, surrounded by Their cowherd boyfriends. Some of the women, my dear King, eagerly climbed to the roofs of their houses to see Them.

Essence. Mathurā's women hurriedly gather to see the two sons of Vasudeva; some climb their roofs in eagerness. Even in a city of fear, the news of Kṛṣṇa's arrival mobilizes ordinary women to undignified urgency.

BG 41.25

Some of the ladies put their clothes and ornaments on backwards, others forgot one of their earrings or ankle bells, and others applied makeup to one eye but not the other.

Essence. Some put their clothes and ornaments on backwards; others forget earrings or ankle bells; others apply makeup to only one eye. Eagerness to see the Lord undoes self-presentation — devotion looks slightly chaotic at the right moments.

BG 41.26

Those who were taking their meals abandoned them, others went out without finishing their baths or massages, women who were sleeping at once rose when they heard the commotion, and mothers breast-feeding their infants simply put them aside.

Essence. Those eating abandon meals; bathers and massagers stop mid-service; sleepers wake at the commotion; mothers set down breastfeeding infants. Every ordinary activity is interruptable by Kṛṣṇa's arrival; nothing is so important it doesn't pause.

BG 41.27

The lotus-eyed Lord, smiling as He recalled His bold pastimes, captivated those ladies’ minds with His glances. He walked with the gait of a lordly elephant in rut, creating a festival for their eyes with His transcendental body, which is the source of pleasure for the divine goddess of fortune.

Essence. Smiling as He recalls His bold pastimes, the lotus-eyed Lord captivates the ladies' minds with His glances, walking like an elephant in rut, a festival for their eyes. He doesn't suppress His attraction-creating capacity in public.

BG 41.28

The ladies of Mathurā had repeatedly heard about Kṛṣṇa, and thus as soon as they saw Him their hearts melted. They felt honored that He was sprinkling upon them the nectar of His glances and broad smiles. Taking Him into their hearts through their eyes, they embraced Him, the embodiment of all ecstasy, and as their bodily hairs stood on end, O subduer of enemies, they forgot the unlimited distress caused by His absence.

Essence. The Mathurā ladies, having repeatedly heard about Kṛṣṇa, find their hearts melting; they feel honored by His glances, embrace Him through their eyes, hairs on end, forgetting bodily distress. Listening over time prepares the heart to melt at first sight.

BG 41.29

Their lotus faces blooming with affection, the ladies who had climbed to the roofs of the mansions rained down showers of flowers upon Lord Balarāma and Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. From the rooftops, ladies with lotus faces blooming with affection shower flowers on Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma. Even ordinary household women become flower-throwing devotees when the Lord passes; participation is spontaneous.

BG 41.30

Brāhmaṇas standing along the way honored the two Lords with presentations of yogurt, unbroken barleycorns, pots full of water, garlands, fragrant substances such as sandalwood paste, and other items of worship.

Essence. Brāhmaṇas along the way honor the two Lords with yogurt, unbroken barleycorns, water pots, garlands, sandalwood paste and other items. The brāhmaṇical class brings traditional ritual items — devotion expresses itself through inherited forms too.

BG 41.31

The women of Mathurā exclaimed: Oh, what severe austerities the gopīs must have performed to be able to regularly see Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, who are the greatest source of pleasure for all mankind!

Essence. Mathurā's women exclaim: what severe austerities the gopīs must have performed to see Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma regularly! Even strangers recognize that intimate access to the Lord rests on hidden austerity.

BG 41.32

Seeing a washerman approaching who had been dyeing some clothes, Kṛṣṇa asked him for the finest laundered garments he had.

Essence. Seeing a washerman who dyed clothes for the king, Kṛṣṇa asks him for the finest laundered garments. The Lord casually asks ordinary people for what He needs; opportunities for service walk straight up.

BG 41.33

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] Please give suitable garments to the two of Us, who certainly deserve them. If you grant this charity, you will undoubtedly receive the greatest benefit.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa says: please give Us suitable garments; if you grant this charity, you will undoubtedly receive the greatest benefit. The Lord offers explicit upside to anyone who serves Him — service is its own reward and yet still rewarded.

BG 41.34

Thus requested by the Supreme Lord, who is perfectly complete in all respects, that arrogant servant of the King became angry and replied insultingly.

Essence. The arrogant servant of the King becomes angry and replies insultingly. Even directly asked by the Lord, the materially-conditioned can mistake Him for an annoying beggar — proximity to the king clouds the mind.

BG 41.35

[The washerman said:] You impudent boys! You’re accustomed to roaming the mountains and forests, and yet You would dare put on such clothes as these! These are the King’s possessions You’re asking for!

Essence. The washerman says: 'You impudent boys roam mountains and forests — yet would dare put on the King's clothes!' Worldly authority can make its servants speak with derision toward what they don't recognize as divine.

BG 41.36

Fools, get out of here quickly! Don’t beg like this if You want to stay alive. When someone is too bold, the King’s men arrest him and kill him and take all his property.

Essence. Fools, get out before you're killed — the King's men arrest, kill, and take property of bold beggars. The world threatens those who appear to overreach; sometimes the Lord Himself receives such threats.

BG 41.37

As the washerman thus spoke brazenly, the son of Devakī became angry, and then merely with His fingertips He separated the man’s head from his body.

Essence. As the washerman speaks brazenly, Kṛṣṇa angrily separates his head from his body with mere fingertips. Verbal insolence toward the Lord receives swift, definitive answer; the same hand that asks politely also corrects decisively.

BG 41.38

The washerman’s assistants all dropped their bundles of clothes and fled down the road, scattering in all directions. Lord Kṛṣṇa then took the clothes.

Essence. The washerman's assistants drop the bundles and scatter; Kṛṣṇa takes the clothes. When the master falls, dependents flee; the Lord then claims what He had originally asked for politely.

BG 41.39

Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma put on pairs of garments that especially pleased Them, and then Kṛṣṇa distributed the remaining clothes among the cowherd boys, leaving some scattered on the ground.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma put on pleasing garments; Kṛṣṇa distributes the rest among the cowherd boys, leaving some scattered. The Lord shares the spoils with His companions; abundance gets redistributed casually.

BG 41.40

Thereupon a weaver came forward and, feeling affection for the Lords, nicely adorned Their attire with cloth ornaments of various colors.

Essence. A weaver comes forward in affection and adorns the Lords with colored cloth ornaments resembling jewels. Devotees voluntarily upgrade what is already beautiful; love finds details to add.

BG 41.41

Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma looked resplendent, each in His own unique, wonderfully ornamented outfit. They resembled a pair of young elephants, one white and the other black, decorated for a festive occasion.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma look resplendent — one white, one dark — like a pair of young elephants decorated for festival. The Lord welcomes adornment; finely dressed devotion is part of the festival, not a vanity.

BG 41.42

Pleased with the weaver, the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa blessed him that after death he would achieve the liberation of attaining a form like the Lord’s, and that while in this world he would enjoy supreme opulence, physical strength, influence, memory and sensory vigor.

Essence. Pleased, Kṛṣṇa blesses the weaver with future liberation in a form like His own and present supreme opulence, strength, influence, memory and sensory vigor. The Lord gives both future and present in one stroke for sincere service.

BG 41.43

The two Lords then went to the house of the garland-maker Sudāmā. When Sudāmā saw Them he at once stood up and then bowed down, placing his head on the ground.

Essence. The Lords go to the garland-maker Sudāmā's house; he immediately stands, bows, head on the ground. Real devotees recognize Him instantly and the body responds before the mind explains.

BG 41.44

After offering Them seats and bathing Their feet, Sudāmā worshiped Them and Their companions with arghya, garlands, pān, sandalwood paste and other presentations.

Essence. Sudāmā offers seats, bathes Their feet, worships them with arghya, garlands, pān, sandalwood paste and other presentations. Welcome of the Lord is multi-sensory: seat, water, garland, fragrance, refreshment, paste.

BG 41.45

[Sudāmā said:] O Lord, my birth is now sanctified and my family free of contamination. Now that You both have come here, my forefathers, the demigods and the great sages are certainly all satisfied with me.

Essence. Sudāmā says: my birth is sanctified, family freed of contamination; forefathers, demigods, sages are now satisfied by Your coming. A single devotional event purifies multiple generations and unseen relationships.

BG 41.46

You two Lords are the ultimate cause of this entire universe. To bestow sustenance and prosperity upon this realm, You have descended with Your plenary expansions.

Essence. You two are the ultimate cause of this universe; to bestow sustenance and prosperity You have descended with Your plenary expansions. Even private welcome rests on cosmic theology — the Lord at my door is the Lord behind everything.

BG 41.47

Because You are the well-wishing friends and Supreme Soul of the whole universe, You regard all with unbiased vision. Therefore, although You reciprocate Your devotees’ loving worship, You always remain equally disposed toward all living beings.

Essence. As well-wishing friend and Supreme Soul, You see all with unbiased vision; while reciprocating devotees' worship, You remain equally disposed to all beings. Particular reciprocation and universal equality coexist in Him.

BG 41.48

Please order me, Your servant, to do whatever You wish. To be engaged by You in some service is certainly a great blessing for anyone.

Essence. Order me, Your servant, to do whatever You wish; engagement by You is a great blessing for anyone. Sudāmā's prayer is not for benediction but for assignment.

BG 41.49

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued:] O best of kings, having spoken these words, Sudāmā could understand what Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma wanted. Thus with great pleasure he presented Them with garlands of fresh, fragrant flowers.

Essence. Understanding what Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma want, Sudāmā gladly presents garlands of fresh, fragrant flowers. The discerning devotee figures out what the Lord wants without being told and supplies it.

BG 41.50

Beautifully adorned with these garlands, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma were delighted, and so were Their companions. The two Lords then offered the surrendered Sudāmā, who was bowing down before Them, whatever benedictions he desired.

Essence. Beautifully garlanded, the Lords are delighted; the Lords offer surrendered Sudāmā whatever benedictions he desires. When the devotee pleases Kṛṣṇa, the Lord asks the devotee to name the gift.

BG 41.51

Sudāmā chose unshakable devotion for Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Soul of all existence; friendship with His devotees; and transcendental compassion for all living beings.

Essence. Sudāmā chooses unshakable devotion to Kṛṣṇa, friendship with His devotees, and transcendental compassion for all beings. The ideal devotee asks for inner orientation, not external prosperity.

BG 41.52

Not only did Lord Kṛṣṇa grant Sudāmā these benedictions, but He also awarded him strength, long life, fame, beauty and ever-increasing prosperity for his family. Then Kṛṣṇa and His elder brother took Their leave.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa grants the three benedictions and also strength, long life, fame, beauty, and ever-increasing prosperity for the family. The Lord is hard as a thunderbolt for those who defy Him and soft as a rose for those who love.
