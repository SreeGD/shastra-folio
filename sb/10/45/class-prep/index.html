Chapter 45 · Class prep

BG 45.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Understanding that His parents were becoming aware of His transcendental opulences, the Supreme Personality of Godhead thought that this should not be allowed to happen. Thus He expanded His Yoga-māyā, which bewilders His devotees.

Essence. Seeing His parents becoming aware of His transcendental opulences, Kṛṣṇa expands Yoga-māyā to bewilder them — so their love as parents won't be spoiled. The Lord preserves intimate relationships by veiling His full identity from those who love Him as family.

BG 45.2

Lord Kṛṣṇa, the greatest of the Sātvatas, approached His parents with His elder brother. Humbly bowing His head and gratifying them by respectfully addressing them as “My dear mother” and “My dear father,” Kṛṣṇa spoke as follows.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa approaches His parents with Balarāma, humbly bowing His head, addressing them as 'My dear mother' and 'My dear father'. The Lord uses titles of family even though He is the universe; humility chooses to speak from inside the role.

BG 45.3

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] Dear Father, because of Us, your two sons, you and mother Devakī always remained in anxiety and could never enjoy Our childhood, boyhood or youth.

Essence. Dear Father, because of Us your two sons, you and mother always lived in anxiety and never enjoyed Our childhood, boyhood, or youth. The Lord names the cost His parents paid; acknowledging the price of devotion to one's children is part of mature gratitude.

BG 45.4

Deprived by fate, We could not live with you and enjoy the pampered happiness most children enjoy in their parents’ home.

Essence. Deprived by fate, We could not live with you and enjoy pampered happiness in your home. The Lord admits His own loss too; separation was mutual, not just borne by parents.

BG 45.5

With one’s body one can acquire all goals of life, and it is one’s parents who give the body birth and sustenance. Therefore no mortal man can repay his debt to his parents, even if he serves them for a full lifetime of a hundred years.

Essence. With the body one acquires all goals; parents give the body birth and sustenance, so no mortal can repay them even in a hundred years. Filial debt is structurally unrepayable; the only response is ongoing reverence.

BG 45.6

A son who, though able to do so, fails to provide for his parents with his physical resources and wealth is forced after his death to eat his own flesh.

Essence. A son who, though able, fails to provide for his parents is forced after death to eat his own flesh. Scripture pronounces severe consequences for neglect of parents — the threat is graphic, not gentle.

BG 45.7

A man who, though able to do so, fails to support his elderly parents, chaste wife, young child or spiritual master, or who neglects a brāhmaṇa or anyone who comes to him for shelter, is considered dead, though breathing.

Essence. A man who fails to support elderly parents, chaste wife, young child, spiritual master, brāhmaṇa or anyone seeking shelter, is considered dead while breathing. The list is wide; failure to protect dependents is a kind of death.

BG 45.8

Thus We have wasted all these days, unable as We were to properly honor you because Our minds were always disturbed by fear of Kaṁsa.

Essence. Thus We have wasted all these days, unable to honor you because Our minds were always disturbed by fear of Kaṁsa. Even great souls can frame past constraints honestly; you don't have to defend every chapter of your life.

BG 45.9

Dear Father and Mother, please forgive Us for not serving you. We are not independent and have been greatly frustrated by cruel Kaṁsa.

Essence. Dear Father and Mother, please forgive Us — We are not independent and were frustrated by cruel Kaṁsa. The Lord asks forgiveness from His own parents; if He does, who is too high to seek it?

BG 45.10

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus beguiled by the words of Lord Hari, the Supreme Soul of the universe, who by His internal illusory potency appeared to be a human, His parents joyfully raised Him up on their laps and embraced Him.

Essence. Beguiled by His words, His parents joyfully raise Him on their laps and embrace Him. Kṛṣṇa's careful speech restores the family dynamic; words can rebuild what theology threatened to interrupt.

BG 45.11

Pouring out a shower of tears upon the Lord, His parents, who were bound up by the rope of affection, could not speak. They were overwhelmed, O King, and their throats choked up with tears.

Essence. Tears pouring on Him, His parents, bound by the rope of affection, cannot speak — overwhelmed, choked with tears. Real intimate moments don't require articulation; tears say what words can't.

BG 45.12

Thus having comforted His mother and father, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, appearing as the son of Devakī, installed His maternal grandfather, Ugrasena, as King of the Yadus.

Essence. After comforting His parents, Kṛṣṇa installs His maternal grandfather Ugrasena as King of the Yadus. Comfort precedes governance; emotional repair comes before political restructuring.

BG 45.13

The Lord told him: O mighty King, We are your subjects, so please command Us. Indeed, because of the curse of Yayāti, no Yadu may sit on the royal throne.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa tells Ugrasena: We are your subjects — command Us; no Yadu may sit on the throne due to Yayāti's curse. The Lord chooses subordinate position; titles He could have held, He passes to honor lineage.

BG 45.14

Since I am present in your entourage as your personal attendant, all the demigods and other exalted personalities will come with heads bowed to offer you tribute. What, then, to speak of the rulers of men?

Essence. Since I am present as your personal attendant, demigods will come with bowed heads to offer tribute — let alone earthly rulers. Real position rests on whose attendant you are; if Kṛṣṇa attends you, all bow.

BG 45.15-16

The Lord then brought all His close family members and other relatives back from the various places to which they had fled in fear of Kaṁsa. He received the Yadus, Vṛṣṇis, Andhakas, Madhus, Dāśārhas, Kukuras and other clans with due honor, and He also consoled them, for they were weary of living in foreign lands. Then Lord Kṛṣṇa, the creator of the universe, resettled them in their homes and gratified them with valuable gifts.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa brings back family members who had fled in fear of Kaṁsa — Yadus, Vṛṣṇis, Andhakas, Madhus, Dāśārhas, Kukuras — honoring and consoling all. After tyranny falls, the work of reunion is detailed and patient.

BG 45.17-18

The members of these clans, protected by the arms of Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Saṅkarṣaṇa, felt that all their desires were fulfilled. Thus they enjoyed perfect happiness while living at home with their families. Because of the presence of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, they no longer suffered from the fever of material existence. Every day these loving devotees could see Mukunda’s ever-cheerful lotus face, which was decorated with beautiful, merciful smiling glances.

Essence. Protected by Kṛṣṇa's and Saṅkarṣaṇa's arms, the clan members feel all desires fulfilled, enjoy perfect happiness at home with families, no longer suffering. Real peace looks like ordinary household life under the Lord's protection.

BG 45.19

Even the most elderly inhabitants of the city appeared youthful, full of strength and vitality, for with their eyes they constantly drank the elixir of Lord Mukunda’s lotus face.

Essence. Even the most elderly inhabitants look youthful, full of strength and vitality, constantly drinking the elixir of Mukunda's lotus face. The Lord's presence reverses biological aging in those who gaze on Him.

BG 45.20

Then, O exalted Parīkṣit, the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa, the son of Devakī, along with Lord Balarāma, approached Nanda Mahārāja. The two Lords embraced him and then addressed him as follows.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma approach Nanda Mahārāja, embrace him, and address him. Even after returning to biological parents, the Lord seeks out the foster father who raised Him; both fatherhoods are honored.

BG 45.21

[Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma said:] O Father, you and mother Yaśodā have affectionately maintained Us and cared for Us so much! Indeed, parents love their children more than their own lives.

Essence. O Father, you and mother Yaśodā affectionately maintained and cared for Us; parents love their children more than their own lives. Real parenthood is named directly to its bearers, not assumed.

BG 45.22

They are the real father and mother who care for, as they would their own sons, children abandoned by relatives unable to maintain and protect them.

Essence. They are the real father and mother who care for abandoned children as their own. Functional parenthood — care, not biology — defines reality; foster parents are 'real' parents.

BG 45.23

Now you should all return to Vraja, dear Father. We shall come to see you, Our dear relatives who suffer in separation from Us, as soon as We have given some happiness to your well-wishing friends.

Essence. Return to Vraja, dear Father — We will come to see you, Our dear relatives who suffer in separation, as soon as We have given some happiness to your friends. Promises of return are not betrayals; the Lord schedules visits, prioritizing one set without abandoning the other.

BG 45.24

Thus consoling Nanda Mahārāja and the other men of Vraja, the infallible Supreme Lord respectfully honored them with gifts of clothing, jewelry, household utensils and so on.

Essence. The infallible Lord consoles Nanda and the men of Vraja and honors them with gifts of clothing, jewelry, and household utensils. Material gifts accompany words of farewell; tangible objects carry intangible affection.

BG 45.25

Nanda Mahārāja was overwhelmed with affection upon hearing Kṛṣṇa’s words, and his eyes brimmed with tears as he embraced the two Lords. Then he went back to Vraja with the cowherd men.

Essence. Overwhelmed, Nanda embraces the Lords with brimming tears and returns to Vraja with the cowherd men. The deepest goodbye is brief — repeated embraces, then the road home.

BG 45.26

My dear King, then Vasudeva, the son of Śūrasena, arranged for a priest and other brāhmaṇas to perform his two sons’ second-birth initiation.

Essence. Vasudeva arranges priests for his sons' second-birth initiation. After reunion comes formal ritual; family love is followed by sacramental commitment.

BG 45.27

Vasudeva honored these brāhmaṇas by worshiping them and giving them fine ornaments and well-ornamented cows with their calves. All these cows wore gold necklaces and linen wreaths.

Essence. Vasudeva honors the brāhmaṇas with worship, fine ornaments, and ornamented cows with calves, all in gold necklaces and linen wreaths. Sacraments are surrounded by generous, beautifully detailed gifts to the officiants.

BG 45.28

The magnanimous Vasudeva then remembered the cows he had mentally given away on the occasion of Kṛṣṇa’s and Balarāma’s birth. Kaṁsa had stolen those cows, and Vasudeva now recovered them and gave them away in charity also.

Essence. Vasudeva remembers the cows mentally given at Kṛṣṇa's and Balarāma's birth — stolen by Kaṁsa — and now recovers and gives them away. Mental vows made under duress are completed in freedom; freedom honors what bondage promised.

BG 45.29

After attaining twice-born status through initiation, the Lords, sincere in Their vows, took the further vow of celibacy from Garga Muni, the spiritual master of the Yadus.

Essence. After initiation, the Lords take the further vow of celibacy (brahmacarya) from Garga Muni, the Yadus' spiritual master. Even the Lord submits to student life and the vow appropriate to it.

BG 45.30-31

Concealing Their innately perfect knowledge by Their humanlike activities, those two omniscient Lords of the universe, Themselves the origin of all branches of knowledge, next desired to reside at the school of a spiritual master. Thus They approached Sāndīpani Muni, a native of Kāsī living in the city of Avantī.

Essence. Concealing innate perfect knowledge, the omniscient Lords approach Sāndīpani Muni, a native of Kāśī residing at Avantīpura, to reside as students. The origin of all knowledge consents to be a student; learning is honored as a stage even when unnecessary.

BG 45.32

Sāndīpani thought very highly of these two self-controlled disciples, whom he had obtained so fortuitously. By serving him as devotedly as one would serve the Supreme Lord Himself, They showed others an irreproachable example of how to worship the spiritual master.

Essence. Sāndīpani treasures these self-controlled disciples; They serve him as one would serve the Supreme Lord, showing an irreproachable example of guru-worship. The Lord models how to treat a guru — even when the disciple is greater than the teacher.

BG 45.33

That best of brāhmaṇas, the spiritual master Sāndīpani, was satisfied with Their submissive behavior, and thus he taught Them the entire Vedas, together with their six corollaries and the Upaniṣads.

Essence. Satisfied with Their submissive behavior, Sāndīpani teaches them the entire Vedas with six corollaries and Upaniṣads. Submission to a teacher opens floodgates of knowledge that resistance never could.

BG 45.34

He also taught Them the Dhanur-veda, with its most confidential secrets; the standard books of law; the methods of logical reasoning and philosophical debate; and the sixfold science of politics.

Essence. He also teaches Dhanur-veda with confidential secrets, standard law books, logical reasoning, philosophical debate, and the sixfold science of politics. Curriculum is broad: warfare, law, logic, politics — devotional life doesn't preclude any.

BG 45.35-36

O King, those best of persons, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, being Themselves the original promulgators of all varieties of knowledge, could immediately assimilate each and every subject after hearing it explained just once. Thus with fixed concentration They learned the sixty-four arts and skills in as many days and nights. Thereafter, O King, They satisfied Their spiritual master by offering him guru-dakṣiṇā.

Essence. Being themselves originators of all knowledge, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma assimilate each subject after a single explanation, mastering sixty-four arts in sixty-four days. Even instant capacity is exercised respectfully through the formal process.

BG 45.37

O King, the learned brāhmaṇa Sāndīpani carefully considered the two Lords’ glorious and amazing qualities and Their superhuman intelligence. Then, after consulting with his wife, he chose as his remuneration the return of his young son, who had died in the ocean at Prabhāsa.

Essence. Sāndīpani, after consulting his wife, asks the return of his young son who died in the ocean at Prabhāsa — taken by the conchshell demon. Even spiritual masters have specific personal griefs; gurus aren't immune from family loss.

BG 45.38

“So be it,” replied those two great chariot warriors of limitless might, and They at once mounted Their chariot and set off for Prabhāsa. When They reached that place, They walked up to the shore and sat down. In a moment the deity of the ocean, recognizing Them to be the Supreme Lords, approached Them with offerings of tribute.

Essence. 'So be it,' the two great chariot warriors say, and they go to Prabhāsa; the ocean deity recognizes them and brings tribute. Ancient texts' references to deities of ocean and sun are not myth but real personalities responding to the Lord.

BG 45.39

The Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa addressed the lord of the ocean: Let the son of My guru be presented at once — the one you seized here with your mighty waves.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa addresses the ocean: present the son of My guru — the one you seized with your mighty waves. The Lord asks directly; even the ocean is held accountable for what it has taken.

BG 45.40

The ocean replied: O Lord Kṛṣṇa, it was not I who abducted him, but a demonic descendant of Diti named Pañcajana, who travels in the water in the form of a conch.

Essence. The ocean replies: it was Pañcajana, a demon of Diti's line in conch form, not me. Even powerful agents can be overpowered; the ocean honestly identifies the actual culprit.

BG 45.41

“Indeed,” the ocean said, “that demon has taken him away.” Hearing this, Lord Kṛṣṇa entered the ocean, found Pañcajana and killed him. But the Lord did not find the boy within the demon’s belly.

Essence. Lord Kṛṣṇa enters the ocean, finds Pañcajana, kills him — but doesn't find the boy in his belly. Sometimes the immediate enemy doesn't have what you came for; victory and reunion can be two separate steps.

BG 45.42-44

Lord Janārdana took the conchshell that had grown around the demon’s body and went back to the chariot. Then He proceeded to Saṁyamanī, the beloved capital of Yamarāja, the lord of death. Upon arriving there with Lord Balarāma, He loudly blew His conchshell, and Yamarāja, who keeps the conditioned souls in check, came as soon as he heard the resounding vibration. Yamarāja elaborately worshiped the two Lords with great devotion, and then he addressed Lord Kṛṣṇa, who lives in everyone’s heart: “O Supreme Lord Viṣṇu, what shall I do for You and Lord Balarāma, who are playing the part of ordinary humans?”

Essence. Janārdana takes the conch from the demon, goes to Yamarāja's capital Saṁyamanī, blows the conch loudly; Yama, knowing all souls' karma, comes out to receive them. The Pāñcajanya — the same conch sounded at the start of the Gītā — is sourced from this demon.

BG 45.45

The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Suffering the bondage of his past activity, My spiritual master’s son was brought here to you. O great King, obey My command and bring this boy to Me without delay.

Essence. Suffering past karma, My spiritual master's son was brought here; obey My command and bring him to Me without delay. Karma has placed the boy with Yama; the Lord overrules karma's keepers for the sake of His guru.

BG 45.46

Yamarāja said, “So be it,” and brought forth the guru’s son. Then those two most exalted Yadus presented the boy to Their spiritual master and said to him, “Please select another boon.”

Essence. Yama says 'so be it,' brings the boy, and the Yadus present him to their guru, asking him to select another boon. Beyond fulfilling the assigned debt, devoted students keep offering more — generosity isn't bounded by minimum requirements.

BG 45.47

The spiritual master said: My dear boys, You two have completely fulfilled the disciple’s obligation to reward his spiritual master. Indeed, with disciples like You, what further desires could a guru have?

Essence. The spiritual master replies: My dear boys, You have fulfilled the disciple's obligation completely; with disciples like You, what further desires could a guru have? Some gifts are so complete that the giver cannot think of asking for more.

BG 45.48

O heroes, now please return home. May Your fame sanctify the world, and may the Vedic hymns be ever fresh in Your minds, both in this life and the next.

Essence. O heroes, return home; may Your fame sanctify the world and may the Vedic hymns be ever fresh in Your minds, both this life and the next. The guru's blessing is for sustained spiritual memory across lifetimes.

BG 45.49

Thus receiving Their guru’s permission to leave, the two Lords returned to Their city on Their chariot, which moved as swiftly as the wind and resounded like a cloud.

Essence. Receiving permission, the Lords return to their city on a wind-swift, cloud-resounding chariot. Permission to leave from a teacher is part of the journey home; departure with blessing differs from departure without.

BG 45.50

All the citizens rejoiced upon seeing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, whom they had not seen for many days. The people felt just like those who have lost their wealth and then regained it.

Essence. All citizens rejoice on seeing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma after many days, like those who lost wealth and regained it. Reunion of the Lord with His people brings a joy specifically shaped like recovered loss.
