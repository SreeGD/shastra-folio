Chapter 18 · Class prep

BG 18.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Surrounded by His blissful companions, who constantly chanted His glories, Śrī Kṛṣṇa then entered the village of Vraja, which was decorated with herds of cows.

Essence. Surrounded by blissful friends chanting His glories, Kṛṣṇa enters Vraja decorated with herds of cows. The Lord's homecoming is always a procession of song — His arrival is announced not by trumpets but by friends in joy.

BG 18.2

While Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma were thus enjoying life in Vṛndāvana in the guise of ordinary cowherd boys, the summer season gradually appeared. This season is not very pleasing to embodied souls.

Essence. Summer arrives in Vṛndāvana — not welcome to ordinary bodies, but transformed by Kṛṣṇa's presence. Even harsh seasons of life become bearable, even sweet, when His presence is the climate of the place.

BG 18.3

Nevertheless, because the Supreme Personality of Godhead was personally staying in Vṛndāvana along with Balarāma, summer manifested the qualities of spring. Such are the features of the land of Vṛndāvana.

Essence. Because Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma personally stay there, summer in Vṛndāvana wears the qualities of spring. The Lord's residence rewrites the rules of the season — His residence in the heart can do the same to ours.

BG 18.4

In Vṛndāvana, the loud sound of waterfalls covered the crickets’ noise, and clusters of trees constantly moistened by spray from those waterfalls beautified the entire area.

Essence. Waterfalls drown out the crickets; spray-moistened trees beautify Vṛndāvana. The Lord's environment is loud with abundance, not silent austerity — beauty replaces background noise when grace is flowing.

BG 18.5

The wind wafting over the waves of the lakes and flowing rivers carried away the pollen of many varieties of lotuses and water lilies and then cooled the entire Vṛndāvana area. Thus the residents there did not suffer from the heat generated by the blazing summer sun and seasonal forest fires. Indeed, Vṛndāvana was abundant with fresh green grass.

Essence. Wind over lakes carries lotus-pollen, cooling all of Vṛndāvana so the residents do not suffer from sun or seasonal fires. The Lord arranges micro-climates for those who live near Him; what kills others doesn't reach them.

BG 18.6

With their flowing waves the deep rivers drenched their banks, making them damp and muddy. Thus the rays of the sun, which were as fierce as poison, could not evaporate the earth’s sap or parch its green grass.

Essence. Deep rivers drench their banks, keeping the earth damp so even poison-fierce sun cannot evaporate the green grass. Strong inner sources keep the surface alive when external heat tries to burn it.

BG 18.7

Flowers beautifully decorated the forest of Vṛndāvana, and many varieties of animals and birds filled it with sound. The peacocks and bees sang, and the cuckoos and cranes cooed.

Essence. Flowers decorate the forest; peacocks, bees, cuckoos, and cranes fill it with sound. Vṛndāvana is multisensory celebration — beauty for eyes, music for ears, fragrance for nose, all in one place.

BG 18.8

Intending to engage in pastimes, Lord Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, accompanied by Lord Balarāma and surrounded by the cowherd boys and the cows, entered the forest of Vṛndāvana as He played His flute.

Essence. Playing His flute, Kṛṣṇa enters the forest with Balarāma, the boys, and the cows, intending pastimes. Entry into beauty is itself an act — the Lord chooses to step in, not to admire from a distance.

BG 18.9

Decorating themselves with newly grown leaves, along with peacock feathers, garlands, clusters of flower buds, and colored minerals, Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa and Their cowherd friends danced, wrestled and sang.

Essence. Adorned with leaves, peacock feathers, garlands, flower buds, and colored minerals, the boys dance, wrestle, and sing. Devotional play uses the body fully — costume, motion, sound, contact. Holiness includes wrestling.

BG 18.10

As Kṛṣṇa danced, some of the boys accompanied Him by singing, and others by playing flutes, hand cymbals and buffalo horns, while still others praised His dancing.

Essence. As Kṛṣṇa dances, some boys sing, some play flutes and cymbals and horns, some praise. Each contributes what they can; the dance is collective, not solo. The Lord enjoys being praised by His friends without false modesty.

BG 18.11

O King, demigods disguised themselves as members of the cowherd community and, just as dramatic dancers praise another dancer, worshiped Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, who were also appearing as cowherd boys.

Essence. Demigods disguise themselves as cowherds and worship Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma as fellow dancers do another dancer. Higher beings condescend to ground-level mood when the Lord is dancing — they don't watch from above.

BG 18.12

Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma played with their cowherd boyfriends by whirling about, leaping, hurling, slapping and fighting. Sometimes Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma would pull the hair on the boys’ heads.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma whirl, leap, hurl, slap, fight, and pull hair with the boys. The Lord is physically rough in His play — devotional intimacy includes contact that would seem disrespectful from a distance.

BG 18.13

While the other boys were dancing, O King, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma would sometimes accompany them with song and instrumental music, and sometimes the two Lords would praise the boys, saying, “Very good! Very good!”

Essence. When others dance, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma sometimes accompany them with song and music and say 'Very good! Very good!' The Lord cheers for His friends — He is not above being audience to those who are also performers.

BG 18.14

Sometimes the cowherd boys would play with bilva or kumbha fruits, and sometimes with handfuls of āmalaka fruits. At other times they would play the games of trying to touch one another or of trying to identify somebody while one is blindfolded, and sometimes they would imitate animals and birds.

Essence. The boys play with fruits, blind-man's bluff, imitating animals — all the games of childhood. Spiritual life includes ordinary play; Vṛndāvana does not require unusual hobbies to be holy.

BG 18.15

They would sometimes jump around like frogs, sometimes play various jokes, sometimes ride in swings and sometimes imitate monarchs.

Essence. They jump like frogs, play jokes, swing, and pretend to be kings collecting tolls from the gopīs at the river. Even mock-authority becomes play in Vṛndāvana — power itself is comic when held by lovers.

BG 18.16

In this way Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma played all sorts of well-known games as They wandered among the rivers, hills, valleys, bushes, trees and lakes of Vṛndāvana.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma roam the rivers, hills, valleys, bushes, trees, and lakes of Vṛndāvana playing well-known games. The Lord uses the whole landscape — every feature is a venue. Holiness is not confined to one location.

BG 18.17

While Rāma, Kṛṣṇa and Their cowherd friends were thus tending the cows in that Vṛndāvana forest, the demon Pralamba entered their midst. He had assumed the form of a cowherd boy with the intention of kidnapping Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma.

Essence. The demon Pralamba enters disguised as a cowherd boy, intending to kidnap Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma. Sometimes a demon arrives wearing the costume of a friend — and the regular friend whose place he took is innocently elsewhere.

BG 18.18

Since the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa, who had appeared in the Daśārha dynasty, sees everything, He understood who the demon was. Still, the Lord pretended to accept the demon as a friend, while at the same time seriously considering how to kill him.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa sees through Pralamba immediately but pretends to accept him as a friend while planning his end. The Lord does not always expose impostors at once — sometimes He plays along until the moment of right resolution.

BG 18.19

Kṛṣṇa, who knows all sports and games, then called together the cowherd boys and spoke as follows: “Hey cowherd boys! Let’s play now! We’ll divide ourselves into two even teams.”

Essence. Kṛṣṇa proposes a game: 'Let's divide into two even teams.' The Lord engineers the situation that will surface and end the demon — through ordinary play, not through dramatic confrontation.

BG 18.20

The cowherd boys chose Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma as the leaders of the two parties. Some of the boys were on Kṛṣṇa’s side, and others joined Balarāma.

Essence. The boys make Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma the team leaders, and join one or the other. Even the natural sorting in a group reveals devotion — whom we attach to in play exposes whom we love.

BG 18.21

The boys played various games involving carriers and passengers. In these games the winners would climb up on the backs of the losers, who would have to carry them.

Essence. Winners climb on the backs of losers, who carry them. The game uses defeat as transportation — losing has a built-in service role, and serving the winner is part of how the game works.

BG 18.22

Thus carrying and being carried by one another, and at the same time tending the cows, the boys followed Kṛṣṇa to a banyan tree known as Bhāṇḍīraka.

Essence. Carrying and being carried, while tending the cows, the boys follow Kṛṣṇa to the Bhāṇḍīraka banyan. The Lord leads even while everyone is busy with each other — the destination is set even when no one looks like they're going anywhere.

BG 18.23

My dear King Parīkṣit, when Śrīdāmā, Vṛṣabha and the other members of Lord Balarāma’s party were victorious in these games, Kṛṣṇa and His followers had to carry them.

Essence. Śrīdāmā, Vṛṣabha, and others on Balarāma's team win; Kṛṣṇa and His side must carry them. The Lord lets His side lose — defeat in His company is itself a form of grace.

BG 18.24

Defeated, the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa carried Śrīdāmā. Bhadrasena carried Vṛṣabha, and Pralamba carried Balarāma, the son of Rohiṇī.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa carries Śrīdāmā; Bhadrasena carries Vṛṣabha; Pralamba carries Balarāma. The Lord submits to His friends' strength out of love; a father falls for his small child happily. Submission can be the form love takes.

BG 18.25

Considering Lord Kṛṣṇa invincible, that foremost demon [Pralamba] quickly carried Balarāma far beyond the spot where he was supposed to put his passenger down.

Essence. Considering Kṛṣṇa invincible, Pralamba quickly carries Balarāma past the agreed drop-off, planning to attack Him out of His brother's sight. Some demons separate us from our protectors deliberately — distance from Kṛṣṇa is the demon's strategy.

BG 18.26

As the great demon carried Balarāma, the Lord became as heavy as massive Mount Sumeru, and Pralamba had to slow down. He then resumed his actual form — an effulgent body that was covered with golden ornaments and that resembled a cloud flashing with lightning and carrying the moon.

Essence. Balarāma becomes as heavy as Sumeru; Pralamba slows and resumes his true demoniac form. Truth surfaces under the weight of devotion — the disguise cannot hold when the Lord deliberately increases the load.

BG 18.27

When Lord Balarāma, who carries the plow weapon, saw the gigantic body of the demon as he moved swiftly in the sky — with his blazing eyes, fiery hair, terrible teeth reaching toward his scowling brows, and an amazing effulgence generated by his armlets, crown and earrings — the Lord seemed to become a little frightened.

Essence. Balarāma sees the gigantic demon — blazing eyes, fiery hair, terrible teeth, golden ornaments — and seems momentarily afraid. Even the Lord's expansion plays the role of being slightly shaken; theatre serves the pastime.

BG 18.28

Remembering the actual situation, the fearless Balarāma understood that the demon was tṛying to kidnap Him and take Him away from His companions. The Lord then became furious and struck the demon’s head with His hard fist, just as Indra, the king of the demigods, strikes a mountain with his thunderbolt weapon.

Essence. Remembering the real situation, fearless Balarāma fists the demon's head like Indra striking a mountain. Once the play of fear is over, the response is instant and decisive — recollection of identity dissolves illusion.

BG 18.29

Thus smashed by Balarāma’s fist, Pralamba’s head immediately cracked open. The demon vomited blood from his mouth and lost all consciousness, and then with a great noise he fell lifeless on the ground, like a mountain devastated by Indra.

Essence. Pralamba's head cracks open; he vomits blood, falls unconscious, and crashes lifeless like Indra's mountain. The Lord's blow is total — there is no surviving partial damage to a demon that He fully decides to end.

BG 18.30

The cowherd boys were most astonished to see how the powerful Balarāma had killed the demon Pralamba, and they exclaimed, “Excellent! Excellent!”

Essence. The boys, astonished, exclaim 'Excellent! Excellent!' Witnessing a victory calls for unrestrained praise — they did not analyze, they simply cheered the visible power.

BG 18.31

They offered Balarāma profuse benedictions and then glorified Him, who deserves all glorification. Their minds overwhelmed with ecstatic love, they embraced Him as if He had come back from the dead.

Essence. They offer benedictions and glorify Balarāma, embracing Him as if He'd returned from the dead. Their love treats every separation, even a brief tactical one, as a return to life. Reunion deserves a body's-worth of embrace.

BG 18.32

The sinful Pralamba having been killed, the demigods felt extremely happy, and they showered flower garlands upon Lord Balarāma and praised the excellence of His deed.

Essence. The demigods rain flower garlands on Balarāma and praise His excellent deed. Heaven keeps track of every demon-killing — what looks unnoticed on earth is being celebrated overhead.
