Chapter 61 · Class prep

BG 61.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Each of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s wives gave birth to ten sons, who were not less than their father, having all His personal opulence.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's wives each bore ten sons equal in opulence to their father. The Lord's unlimited expansion through family life reflects His infinite nature — divine fatherhood is not a limitation but a fullness.

BG 61.2

Because each of these princesses saw that Lord Acyuta never left her palace, each thought herself the Lord’s favorite. These women did not understand the full truth about Him.

Essence. Each queen believed she was Kṛṣṇa's favorite because He never seemed to leave her side. The Lord's unlimited capacity to reciprocate love makes every sincere devotee feel uniquely held, though His true nature exceeds our grasp.

BG 61.3

The Supreme Lord’s wives were fully enchanted by His lovely, lotuslike face, His long arms and large eyes, His loving glances imbued with laughter, and His charming talks with them. But with all their charms these ladies could not conquer the mind of the all-powerful Lord.

Essence. Even the queens' surpassing beauty could not conquer Kṛṣṇa, because He is full in Himself. Real love for the Lord begins when we stop trying to enchant Him and surrender to being enchanted by Him.

BG 61.4

The arched eyebrows of these sixteen thousand queens enchantingly expressed those ladies’ secret intentions through coyly smiling sidelong glances. Thus their eyebrows boldly sent forth conjugal messages. Yet even with these arrows of Cupid, and with other means as well, they could not agitate Lord Kṛṣṇa’s senses.

Essence. The queens' coy glances and Cupid-like arrows could not stir Kṛṣṇa's senses. The Lord is the master of His senses; His enjoyment with devotees is on a spiritual plane untouched by material lust.

BG 61.5

Thus these women obtained as their husband the master of the goddess of fortune, although even great demigods like Brahmā do not know how to approach Him. With ever-increasing pleasure, they felt loving attraction for Him, exchanged smiling glances with Him, eagerly anticipated associating with Him in ever-fresh intimacy and enjoyed in many other ways.

Essence. The queens obtained as their husband the Lord whom even Brahmā cannot approach, and their love for Him only grew. Devotion to Kṛṣṇa is ever-fresh — it never becomes stale, because He is inexhaustibly attractive.

BG 61.6

Although the Supreme Lord’s queens each had hundreds of maidservants, they chose to personally serve the Lord by approaching Him humbly, offering Him a seat, worshiping Him with excellent paraphernalia, bathing and massaging His feet, giving Him pān to chew, fanning Him, anointing Him with fragrant sandalwood paste, adorning Him with flower garlands, dressing His hair, arranging His bed, bathing Him and presenting Him with various gifts.

Essence. Though they had hundreds of maids, the queens personally served Kṛṣṇa — bathing His feet, fanning Him, dressing His hair. Love expresses itself by doing for the beloved what no one else can do for us.

BG 61.7

Among Lord Kṛṣṇa’s wives, each of whom had ten sons, I previously mentioned eight principal queens. I shall now recite for you the names of those eight queens’ sons, headed by Pradyumna.

Essence. Śukadeva announces the names of the principal queens' sons, beginning with Pradyumna. The careful preservation of these names reminds us that every soul in the Lord's family has individual significance.

BG 61.8-9

The first son of Queen Rukmiṇī was Pradyumna, and also born of her were Cārudeṣṇa, Sudeṣṇa and the powerful Cārudeha, along with Sucāru, Cārugupta, Bhadracāru, Cārucandra, Vicāru and Cāru, the tenth. None of these sons of Lord Hari was less than his father.

Essence. Rukmiṇī's ten sons, headed by Pradyumna, were each fully empowered like their father Hari. Spiritual lineage produces children whose qualities reflect the parent's devotion, not merely biology.

BG 61.10-12

The ten sons of Satyabhāmā were Bhānu, Subhānu, Svarbhānu, Prabhānu, Bhānumān, Candrabhānu, Bṛhadbhānu, Atibhānu (the eighth), Śrībhānu and Pratibhānu. Sāmba, Sumitra, Purujit, Śatajit, Sahasrajit, Vijaya, Citraketu, Vasumān, Draviḍa and Kratu were the sons of Jāmbavatī. These ten, headed by Sāmba, were their father’s favorites.

Essence. Satyabhāmā's and Jāmbavatī's sons are named — Sāmba and his brothers were their father's favorites, regarded as just like Kṛṣṇa Himself. The Lord embraces His devotees with the warmth of a doting father.

BG 61.13

The sons of Nāgnajitī were Vīra, Candra, Aśvasena, Citragu, Vegavān, Vṛṣa, Āma, Śaṅku, Vasu and the opulent Kunti.

Essence. Nāgnajitī's sons are listed — Vīra, Candra, Aśvasena and others. The Bhāgavatam records these names with care, honoring each soul's place in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.

BG 61.14

Śruta, Kavi, Vṛṣa, Vīra, Subāhu, Bhadra, Śānti, Darśa and Pūrṇamāsa were sons of Kālindī. Her youngest son was Somaka.

Essence. Kālindī's sons, including Śruta, Kavi, and Pūrṇamāsa, are named, ending with her youngest, Somaka. Even brief mentions in scripture point to lives lived in proximity to the Lord — an inheritance no wealth can match.

BG 61.15

Mādrā’s sons were Praghoṣa, Gātravān, Siṁha, Bala, Prabala, Ūrdhaga, Mahāśakti, Saha, Oja and Aparājita.

Essence. Mādrā (Lakṣmaṇā) bore Praghoṣa, Siṁha, Bala, Mahāśakti and others. The qualities encoded in such names — strength, victory, undefeatable — reflect the spiritual power devotees inherit from association with the Lord.

BG 61.16

Mitravindā’s sons were Vṛka, Harṣa, Anila, Gṛdhra, Vardhana, Unnāda, Mahāṁsa, Pāvana, Vahni and Kṣudhi.

Essence. Mitravindā's sons are named, including Vṛka, Harṣa and Pāvana. Each son of the Lord stands as a reminder that intimacy with Kṛṣṇa produces visible fruit in this world.

BG 61.17

Saṅgrāmajit, Bṛhatsena, Śūra, Praharaṇa, Arijit, Jaya and Subhadra were the sons of Bhadrā, together with Vāma, Āyur and Satyaka.

Essence. Bhadrā's sons — Saṅgrāmajit, Śūra, Arijit, Jaya, Subhadra and others — bear names of valor and victory. Devotional life produces warriors who can conquer the real enemies: lust, anger, and illusion.

BG 61.18

Dīptimān, Tāmratapta and others were the sons of Lord Kṛṣṇa and Rohiṇī. Lord Kṛṣṇa’s son Pradyumna fathered the greatly powerful Aniruddha in the womb of Rukmavatī, the daughter of Rukmī. O King, this took place while they were living in the city of Bhojakaṭa.

Essence. Rohiṇī, foremost of the remaining queens, bore Dīptimān and Tāmratapta, and Pradyumna fathered Aniruddha through Rukmavatī in Bhojakaṭa. The Lord's pastimes weave generations together, each link essential.

BG 61.19

My dear King, the sons and grandsons of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s children numbered in the tens of millions. Sixteen thousand mothers gave rise to this dynasty.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's descendants numbered in the tens of millions, all springing from sixteen thousand mothers. The Lord's love is generative — it expands rather than contracts when shared.

BG 61.20

King Parīkṣit said: How could Rukmī give his daughter to his enemy’s son? After all, Rukmī had been defeated by Lord Kṛṣṇa in battle and was waiting for an opportunity to kill Him. Please explain this to me, O learned one — how these two inimical parties became united through marriage.

Essence. Parīkṣit asks how Rukmī, Kṛṣṇa's defeated enemy, could give his daughter to Kṛṣṇa's son. The sincere seeker is unafraid to ask hard questions about apparent contradictions in the Lord's pastimes.

BG 61.21

Mystic yogīs can perfectly see that which has not yet happened, as well as things in the past or present, beyond the senses, remote or blocked by physical obstacles.

Essence. Mystic yogīs see past, present and future, and what is hidden by distance or obstruction. Parīkṣit appeals to Śukadeva's vision — true spiritual authority can address our deepest puzzles.

BG 61.22

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: At her svayaṁvara ceremony, Rukmavatī herself chose Pradyumna, who was the re-embodiment of Cupid. Then, although He fought alone on a single chariot, Pradyumna defeated the assembled kings in battle and took her away.

Essence. At her svayaṁvara, Rukmavatī chose Pradyumna, who, alone on one chariot, defeated all the assembled kings and carried her away. Genuine choice in love often requires the beloved to demonstrate fearless strength.

BG 61.23

Though Rukmī always remembered his enmity toward Lord Kṛṣṇa, who had insulted him, in order to please his sister he sanctioned his daughter’s marriage to his nephew.

Essence. Despite his enduring grudge against Kṛṣṇa, Rukmī sanctioned his daughter's marriage to please his sister Rukmiṇī. Even hardened hearts can soften under the weight of family affection.

BG 61.24

O King, Balī, the son of Kṛtavarmā, married Rukmiṇī’s young daughter, large-eyed Cārumatī.

Essence. Balī, son of Kṛtavarmā, married Cārumatī, Rukmiṇī's daughter. The simple mention encodes the broader marriages of all the queens' daughters, woven quietly into Kṛṣṇa's expanding household.

BG 61.25

Rukmī gave his granddaughter Rocanā to his daughter’s son, Aniruddha, despite Rukmī’s relentless feud with Lord Hari. Although Rukmī considered this marriage irreligious, he wanted to please his sister, bound as he was by the ropes of affection.

Essence. Rukmī gave his granddaughter Rocanā to Aniruddha though it violated worldly propriety, bound by affection for his sister. Love can lead us into actions reason calls unwise — and sometimes Kṛṣṇa is hidden inside that choice.

BG 61.26

On the joyous occasion of that marriage, O King, Queen Rukmiṇī, Lord Balarāma, Lord Kṛṣṇa and several of the Lord’s sons, headed by Sāmba and Pradyumna, went to the city of Bhojakaṭa.

Essence. Rukmiṇī, Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa and several sons traveled to Bhojakaṭa for Aniruddha's wedding. The Lord and His family show up bodily for the joys of their loved ones — presence is itself a form of love.

BG 61.27-28

After the wedding, a group of arrogant kings headed by the King of Kaliṅga told Rukmī, “You should defeat Balarāma at dice. He’s not expert at dice, O King, but still He’s quite addicted to it.” Thus advised, Rukmī challenged Balarāma and began a gambling match with Him.

Essence. Arrogant kings, led by the King of Kaliṅga, goaded Rukmī to challenge Balarāma at dice. Bad company stokes our worst impulses by flattering them with the language of strategy.

BG 61.29

In that match Lord Balarāma first accepted a wager of one hundred coins, then one thousand, then ten thousand. Rukmī won this first round, and the King of Kaliṅga laughed loudly at Lord Balarāma, showing all his teeth. Lord Balarāma could not tolerate this.

Essence. Rukmī won the first rounds, and the King of Kaliṅga mocked Balarāma openly. Mocking the Lord or His devotees plants seeds of destruction even when one feels momentarily victorious.

BG 61.30

Next Rukmī accepted a bet of one hundred thousand coins, which Lord Balarāma won. But Rukmī tried to cheat, declaring “I’m the winner!”

Essence. After winning a large wager Balarāma was cheated by Rukmī's false claim of victory. When others cheat us, our integrity is tested more than theirs — how we respond is the real measure.

BG 61.31

Shaking with anger like the ocean on the full-moon day, handsome Lord Balarāma, His naturally reddish eyes even redder in His fury, accepted a wager of one hundred million gold coins.

Essence. Balarāma's anger swelled like the full-moon ocean, His eyes reddening, yet He accepted an even larger wager. Righteous anger does not abandon the game — it engages more fully and more truthfully.

BG 61.32

Lord Balarāma fairly won this wager also, but Rukmī again resorted to cheating and declared, “I have won! Let these witnesses here say what they saw.”

Essence. Balarāma won again, but Rukmī cheated a second time and called on his friends as false witnesses. Surrounding ourselves with people who confirm our lies is one of the deepest forms of self-deception.

BG 61.33

Just then a voice from the sky declared, “Balarāma has fairly won this wager. Rukmī is surely lying.”

Essence. A voice from the sky declared that Balarāma had fairly won and that Rukmī was lying. Truth has a way of breaking through, even when human consensus is arranged against it.

BG 61.34

Urged on by the wicked kings, Rukmī ignored the divine voice. In fact destiny itself was urging Rukmī on, and thus he ridiculed Lord Balarāma as follows.

Essence. Goaded by wicked kings and pushed by destiny itself, Rukmī ignored the divine voice and ridiculed Balarāma. When we keep ignoring inner correction, our own karma begins driving us toward our undoing.

BG 61.35

[Rukmī said:] You cowherds who wander about the forests know nothing about dice. Playing with dice and sporting with arrows are only for kings, not for the likes of You.

Essence. Rukmī mocked Balarāma as a mere cowherd ignorant of dice and arrows. We often misjudge spiritual people by the categories of worldly power, and pride blinds us to who we are actually insulting.

BG 61.36

Thus insulted by Rukmī and ridiculed by the kings, Lord Balarāma was provoked to anger. In the midst of the auspicious wedding assembly, He raised His club and struck Rukmī dead.

Essence. Provoked beyond limit, Balarāma raised His club and struck Rukmī dead in the midst of the wedding hall. The Lord's tolerance is vast, but offenses heaped on devotees can finally call forth His justice.

BG 61.37

The King of Kaliṅga, who had laughed at Lord Balarāma and shown his teeth, tried to run away, but the furious Lord quickly seized him on his tenth step and knocked out all his teeth.

Essence. Balarāma chased the fleeing King of Kaliṅga and knocked out the very teeth he had bared in mockery. The faculties we misuse to insult the Lord are often the ones reclaimed first.

BG 61.38

Tormented by Lord Balarāma’s club, the other kings fled in fear, their arms, thighs and heads broken and their bodies drenched in blood.

Essence. The other kings fled in fear, their limbs and heads broken by Balarāma's club. Sometimes we only realize what we have aligned with when its consequences land on our own bodies.

BG 61.39

When His brother-in-law Rukmī was slain, Lord Kṛṣṇa neither applauded nor protested, O King, for He feared jeopardizing His affectionate ties with either Rukmiṇī or Balarāma.

Essence. When Rukmī was killed, Kṛṣṇa neither cheered nor protested, unwilling to wound either Rukmiṇī or Balarāma. The Lord holds competing loves with exquisite care; silence can be the most truthful response.

BG 61.40

Then the descendants of Daśārha, headed by Lord Balarāma, seated Aniruddha and His bride on a fine chariot and set off from Bhojakaṭa for Dvārakā. Having taken shelter of Lord Madhusūdana, they had fulfilled all their purposes.

Essence. The Daśārhas seated Aniruddha and his bride on a chariot and returned to Dvārakā, all their purposes fulfilled by taking shelter of Madhusūdana. Whatever begins and ends in the shelter of Kṛṣṇa is finally a success, however bumpy the middle.
