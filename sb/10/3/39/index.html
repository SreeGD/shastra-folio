Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.39

अजुष्टग्राम्यविषयावनपत्यौ च दम्पती । न वव्राथेऽपवगन मे मोहितौ देवमायया ॥ ३९ ॥

Important Words
  • ajuṣṭa-grāmya-viṣayau — engaged in marital relation (for the sake of a son like Me)
    A + juṣṭa (engaged) + grāmya (worldly, village-life) + viṣaya (sense-objects). Even apparently ordinary marital activity, when motivated by desire for Bhagavān as son, becomes part of bhakti. Gauḍīya principle: the action is the same; the antaḥkaraṇa changes its tattva.
  • apavargam — liberation
    Apa (away) + varga (class, condition) — literally 'apart from class', i.e., free from saṁsāra. Standard term for mukti. Crucial: they REFUSED apavargam. The Vaiṣṇava ideal is not liberation but nitya-seva. Caitanya's siddhānta: prema is higher than mukti.
  • deva-māyayā — by transcendental love
    Deva (divine) + māyā (here, the favorable internal energy, NOT the deluding external one). Compare the two māyās in Gauḍīya theology — bahiraṅga-māyā that deludes and svarūpa-śakti / yoga-māyā that orchestrates līlā. Their 'desire' was the operation of His own internal energy fixing them in vātsalya-bhakti.
  • mohitau — captivated
    Past participle dual of √muh — 'to be enchanted'. The Gauḍīya reading: this is bhakti-moha, not avidyā-moha. To be captivated by Bhagavān is not bondage but the highest freedom. Vraja-residents in Canto 10 will live in deepest deva-māyā-moha and be praised for it.
Being husband and wife but always sonless, you were attracted by sexual desires, for by the influence of devamāyā, transcendental love, you wanted to have Me as your son. Therefore you never desired to be liberated from this material world.
Purport

Vasudeva and Devakī had been dam-patī, husband and wife, since the time of Sutapā and Pṛśni, and they wanted to remain husband and wife in order to have the Supreme Personality of Godhead as their son. This attachment came about by the influence of devamāyā. Loving Kṛṣṇa as one’s son is a Vedic principle. Vasudeva and Devakī never desired anything but to have the Lord as their son, yet for this purpose they apparently wanted to live like ordinary gṛhasthas for sexual indulgence. Although this was a transaction of spiritual potency, their desire appears like attachment for sex in conjugal life. If one wants to return home, back to Godhead, one must give up such desires. This is possible only when one develops intense love for the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has said: niṣkiñcanasya bhagavad-bhajanonmukhasya pāraṁ paraṁ jigamiṣor bhava-sāgarasya (Cc. Madhya 11.8) If one wants to go back home, back to Godhead, one must be niṣkiñcana, free from all material desires. Therefore, instead of desiring to have the Lord come here and become one’s son, one should desire to become free from all material desires ( anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam ) and go back home, back to Godhead. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu teaches us in His Śikṣāṣṭaka: na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ kavitāṁ vā jagad-īśa kāmaye mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi “O almighty Lord, I have no desire to accumulate wealth, nor do I desire beautiful women, nor do I want any number of followers. I only want Your causeless devotional service, birth after birth.” One should not ask the Lord to fulfill any materially tainted desires.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Being husband and wife but always sonless, you were attracted by sexual desires, for by the influence of devamāyā, transcendental love, you wanted to have Me as your son. Therefore you never desired to be liberated from this material world.
Commentary

Maya here refers to affection for their son, for Bhagavatam 10.8.43 says: Vaisnavi vyatanon mayam putra sneha mayim vibhur (Krsna spread out his maya in the form of affection for a son)P. Mohitau here means that they were overcome with bliss. "Being childless and detached from material enjoyment, but overcome with bliss by parental affection, you did not pray for liberation."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Because of your love for me, you did not have any interest in other objects. But not only that, you had no interest in liberation, though you were qualified and though I could have given it to you. That is expressed in this verse. You had no desire for material pleasures. You were devoid of conceptions of body and place which are based on ahaṅkāra. You were without children. You had no possessiveness. You had conduct suitable for libration. Verse 34 mentioned that they were completely purified of contamination. This indicates that liberation was possible for them since only the Lord gives liberation. The Lord was called “king of benedictors” in verse 38. You, though qualified for liberation, did not ask me, who am qualified to give liberation. The cause is explained. You were bewildered by my mercy (māyayā), which suddenly produced affection in you for having me as your child. Or you were bewildered by my mercy which made me desire to be pampered as your son. Other sentiments are derided. An example is the following verse. ālokya-sārṣṭi-sāmīpya- sārūpyaikatvam apy uta dīyamānaṁ na gṛhṇanti vinā mat-sevanaṁ janāḥ A pure devotee does not accept any kind of liberation—sālokya, sārṣṭi, sāmīpya, sārūpya or ekatva—even though they are offered by the Lord. SB 3.29.13

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

You did not accept liberation. Since you were detached from material objects, without developing renunciation, you did not have a taste for liberation. Or even though you did not enjoy material objects, and did not have children, you accept me as a son, and not material enjoyment or many children. And you did not even accept liberation. The cause of all this was that you were not bewildered (amohitau) by māyā. Liberation is accepted by persons under illusion.

Personal Guidance
Even the desire to possess Kṛṣṇa as 'mine' through gṛhastha attachment is a covering — true return home requires niṣkiñcana surrender, not curated wishes.
Practical Applications
  • Audit one 'spiritualized' desire today — a relationship, role, or comfort you've quietly wrapped in Kṛṣṇa's name — and name the material attachment hiding inside it.
  • Before japa, recite Caitanya Mahāprabhu's na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ verse aloud and renounce one specific thing you were planning to ask Kṛṣṇa for.
  • Refuse the bargain of treating bhakti as a means to keep your present life intact; offer one indulgence (food, scrolling, conjugal fantasy) up today as evidence you want Him, not His gifts.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I dressing up a material attachment as a spiritual aspiration — wanting Kṛṣṇa to enter my situation rather than wanting to leave the situation for Him?
  • What 'sonless' ache am I trying to fill through gṛhastha indulgence, and what would it look like to bring that same longing directly to the Lord as niṣkiñcana, asking for nothing but His service?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your prayer life this week: when you ask Kṛṣṇa for help with an exam, a crush, an internship, or peer acceptance, are you asking Him to be a delivery service for the life you already want, or are you asking Him to make you His? Write down one prayer in each category and notice the ratio.
  • Identify the one 'sacred-sounding' want you use to justify normal teenage cravings — 'I want to be famous so I can preach,' 'I want this relationship because we'll do bhakti together,' 'I want money so I can donate.' Name it honestly to one trusted friend today; do not let yourself off the hook with the spiritual veneer.
  • Pick one social-media stream you scroll for viṣaya — bodies, status, lifestyle envy — and fast from it for seven days. Each time you reach for it, replace it with one verse of Caitanya's Śikṣāṣṭaka (start with na dhanaṁ na janaṁ). Track what changes in your mind by day seven.
householder
  • This week, sit with your spouse and ask: 'What are we really raising this family for?' If the honest answer is comfort, status, or legacy, name it. Then together draft one concrete change — a daily family kīrtana, a fixed donation, a service commitment — that reorients the household toward Kṛṣṇa rather than around Him.
  • Examine the next big family decision on your table — a house, a school, a career move, a child's marriage. Apply the niṣkiñcana test: would you make this same choice if it brought you no security, no prestige, no future return — only the chance to please Kṛṣṇa? If not, find the smaller version of the choice that passes the test, and make that one.
  • Stop using your children's well-being as a reason to defer your own sādhana. Vasudeva and Devakī's example shows that even the most exalted parental love is not a substitute for surrender. Fix a non-negotiable morning sādhana — japa, reading, ārati — and let your children see a parent who serves Kṛṣṇa first; that itself is the highest gift you give them.
senior
  • Make a written list of every material desire still alive in you — a grandchild, a paid-off house, a final achievement, a particular kind of death. Read Caitanya's prayer aloud over the list: na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ. Cross off, one by one, what you are willing to release this year. Keep the list where you will see it daily.
  • Choose one younger devotee — a child, grandchild, or student — and instead of bequeathing advice about worldly success, transmit one specific instruction from this verse: do not ask the Lord to fulfill materially tainted desires. Tell them how you learned this the hard way. Legacy as warning, not as trophy.
  • Begin a weekly practice of 'loosening the grip': each week, give away one possession, one role, or one expectation you have been holding. Offer it consciously to Kṛṣṇa as a small rehearsal for the final letting-go. By the time the body's transition comes, surrender should not be a sudden act but a long-practiced posture.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Vasudeva and Devakī's desire as a license for one's own sense gratification — forgetting that their 'attachment' was devamāyā operating in pure devotees, not a template for ordinary conjugal indulgence.
  • Praying to Kṛṣṇa to fulfill materially tainted desires (a child, a spouse, wealth, success) and calling it bhakti, when the verse explicitly warns that even wanting the Lord Himself as a means to material life keeps one bound.
  • Misreading niṣkiñcana as outward poverty or renunciation of duties, rather than the inner emptying of all desires except ahaitukī bhakti — leading to either showy renunciation or quiet resentment of one's āśrama responsibilities.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva and Devakī's desire to have Kṛṣṇa as their son is praised as devamāyā, yet Prabhupāda still says one must give up such desires to go back to Godhead. In your own life, where is the line between a sacred attachment that draws you toward Kṛṣṇa and a material attachment dressed up in spiritual language? Bring a real example and let your partner push back.
  • The verse names them as 'ajuṣṭa-grāmya-viṣayau' — engaged in conjugal life, yet without ordinary sons. Discuss honestly: in modern gṛhastha life, can sex and family life be a transaction of spiritual potency, or is that almost always a self-flattering rationalization? Where does each of you actually land, and why?
  • Caitanya Mahāprabhu's prayer asks for nothing — na dhanaṁ, na janaṁ, na sundarīṁ — only ahaitukī bhakti. If you prayed that prayer with full honesty tonight, which specific item on your current 'asking list' would you have to drop first? Share it; do not let your partner let you off easy.
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a sincere devotee who has been praying for years for a specific outcome — a child, a marriage, a job, the recovery of a parent — and frames it as 'I am asking Kṛṣṇa for this so I can serve Him better.' Student B is a senior Vaiṣṇava friend who has just heard SB 10.3.39 in class. B must lovingly but firmly probe whether A's prayer is truly devamāyā (like Pṛśni and Sutapā wanting the Lord as son) or anyābhilāṣa wearing devotional clothes. A must defend the prayer; B must apply niṣkiñcana and ahaitukī bhakti as the test. After 6 minutes, swap roles with a different 'sacred wish.' Then debrief: where did the test bite? What did each of you concede?

Objections & Responses
  • This verse sounds like it shames a married couple for wanting children and a normal sex life. Isn't that a regressive, life-denying message — especially coming to householders who are already doing their dharma?
    Read the verse carefully — Kṛṣṇa is not shaming Vasudeva and Devakī; He is revealing the inner mechanics of devamāyā so they can now move past it. Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism does not reject gṛhastha life; it honors it as one of the four āśramas. What the verse critiques is not marriage or children but the subtle clinging that keeps even great devotees circling back into material existence. Prabhupāda's point is structural, not moralistic: every desire other than ahaitukī bhakti — however refined, however 'spiritual' it looks — is still a tether. Householders are not asked to abandon family; they are asked to stop secretly bargaining with Kṛṣṇa using their family life as currency.
  • If wanting Kṛṣṇa as your son is itself an obstacle to liberation, then nothing personal — no relationship, no love, no specificity — is safe. This collapses into impersonalism: just want nothing, be nothing.
    Exactly the opposite. The verse does not tell Vasudeva and Devakī to stop loving Kṛṣṇa as their son — that vātsalya-rasa is eternal and is one of the highest reciprocations in the spiritual world. It corrects the framing that this loving relationship requires the apparatus of material gṛhastha life to continue. In the spiritual realm, the same personal love between them and Kṛṣṇa goes on forever, more intensely, without the mortal bodies, the sonlessness, the longing. Gauḍīya siddhānta is the most personalist of all schools: the goal is not to dissolve into 'the Self' but to recover one's eternal personal relationship with Kṛṣṇa — as servant, friend, parent, or beloved. Niṣkiñcana means free of material desires, not free of love.
Analogy

A couple loves a particular master so deeply that they cannot bear to leave his service for any reward — even liberation. Offered freedom from work, they ask instead for a child who looks just like him, so the master's face will be in their home forever. Pṛśni and Sutapā's deva-māyā is bhakti-bound attachment, not material lust; their 'sex desire' is a vector toward Kṛṣṇa, not away.

Stories
Hanumān refusing liberation

When Rāma offered Hanumān any benediction, Hanumān asked only to continue serving — not mokṣa. Tradition records that Hanumān actively refuses liberation, preferring eternal dāsya. The same logic operates in this verse: Pṛśni and Sutapā refuse apavargam (liberation) because mukti would end their seva.

Gauḍīya theology celebrates this refusal: nitya-dāsya is higher than mukti; eternal service is the goal, not impersonal merging. Pṛśni and Sutapā are operating with the topmost siddhānta even before formally hearing it.
