Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.28

My Lord, because You dispel all the fear of Your devotees, I request You to save us and give us protection from the terrible fear of Kaṁsa. Your form as Viṣṇu, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is appreciated by yogīs in meditation. Please make this form invisible to those who see with material eyes.
Purport

The word dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam is significant in this verse because the form of Lord Viṣṇu is meditated upon by yogīs ( dhyānāvasthita-tad-gatena manasā paśyanti yaṁ yoginaḥ ). Devakī requested the Lord, who had appeared as Viṣṇu, to conceal that form, for she wanted to see the Lord as an ordinary child, like a child appreciated by persons who have material eyes. Devakī wanted to see whether the Supreme Personality of Godhead had factually appeared or she was dreaming the Viṣṇu form. If Kaṁsa were to come, she thought, upon seeing the Viṣṇu form he would immediately kill the child, but if he saw a human child, he might reconsider. Devakī was afraid of Ugrasena-ātmaja; that is, she was afraid not of Ugrasena and his men, but of the son of Ugrasena. Thus she requested the Lord to dissipate that fear, since He is always ready to give protection ( abhayam ) to His devotees. “My Lord,” she prayed, “I request You to save me from the cruel hands of the son of Ugrasena, Kaṁsa. I am praying to Your Lordship to please rescue me from this fearful condition because You are always ready to give protection to Your servitors.” The Lord has confirmed this statement in the Bhagavad-gītā by assuring Arjuna, “You may declare to the world, My devotee shall never be vanquished.” While thus praying to the Lord for rescue, mother Devakī expressed her motherly affection: “I understand that this transcendental form is generally perceived in meditation by the great sages, but I am still afraid because as soon as Kaṁsa understands that You have appeared, he might harm You. So I request that for the time being You become invisible to our material eyes.” In other words, she requested the Lord to assume the form of an ordinary child. “My only cause of fear from my brother Kaṁsa is due to Your appearance. My Lord Madhusūdana, Kaṁsa may know that You are already born. Therefore I request You to conceal this four-armed form of Your Lordship, which holds the four symbols of Viṣṇu — namely the conchshell, the disc, the club and the lotus flower. My dear Lord, at the end of the annihilation of the cosmic manifestation, You put the whole universe within Your abdomen; still, by Your unalloyed mercy, You have appeared in my womb. I am surprised that You imitate the activities of ordinary human beings just to please Your devotee.” Devakī was so afraid of Kaṁsa that she could not believe that Kaṁsa would be unable to kill Lord Viṣṇu, who was personally present. Out of motherly affection, therefore, she requested the Supreme Personality of Godhead to disappear. Although because of the Lord’s disappearance Kaṁsa would harass her more and more, thinking that the child born of her was hidden somewhere, she did not want the transcendental child to be harassed and killed. Therefore she requested Lord Viṣṇu to disappear. Later, when harassed, she would think of Him within her mind.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
My Lord, because You dispel all the fear of Your devotees, I request You to save us and give us protection from the terrible fear of Kaṁsa. Your form as Viṣṇu, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is appreciated by yogīs in meditation. Please make this form invisible to those who see with material eyes.
Commentary

"You are fearless and your devotees are fearless. Then why, o Lord, do you keep your mother and father in fear?" This is the intention of this verse. Ghora here means great fear. Out of fear she does not mention the name of Kamsa directly but uses the word Ugrasena atmaja, son of Ugrasena. "You destroy the fears of your devotees, but why do you not remove our fear?" "O mother, I have appeared to kill Kamsa. Bring Kamsa here and I will kill him before your eyes." Fearing such talk from Krsna, Devaki, out of motherly affection, thinking that her small child could not kill Kamsa, and rather worrying about harm coming to the child from Kamsa, became more fearful. Her body began shaking. "It is not suitable to follow the strategy of showing fear before my child who is showing himself as God. Rather using sweet words is a better policy." Thinking in this way, she began to reason in another way in order to get her child to change his form. "O master, this splendid form, which is the object of meditation, should not be visible to human eyes."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Since you can produce fearlessness and have appeared as my son, please protect us. Kaṁsa’s men will directly attack you since they have no attraction for you, being your enemies. And Kaṁsa is the most ferocious, as indicated in verse 22, referring to Kaṁsa as the uncivilized one and suggested in this verse by calling him the son of Ugrasena (ferocious leader). And because he had thrown his father in prison the words “son of Ugrasena” further suggest his fearsome nature. It is also suggested that Kaṁsa was more fearful for appearing in his father’s line, since it is said that fear arising from friends relatives destroys the root . Therefore she says “Save us.” She speaks with compassion for she does not request the Lord to kill Kaṁsa. The word bho indicates extreme suffering. Out of motherly affection she speaks of some way to make the child disappear since she fears for the child also. Since the form of the Lord is the antaryāmī situated in all beings with four arms the child is also famous as the object of meditation (dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam) for those situated in the material world. The two armed form as the object of meditation is also seen but more rarely. Gūḍhaṁ paraṁ brahma manuṣya-liṅgam: Supreme Brahman personally resides in your houses in a human form. (SB 7.10.48) Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad also says śṛṅga-veṇu-dharam: he holds a horn and flute. Hide that from ignorant people (māṁsa-dṛśām) because they are unqualified. The reason is later explained in verse 31: people will ridicule her when it is said that she gave birth to the Lord.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Protect persons like us (naḥ), terrified of Kaṁsa, the son of Ugrasena--he whose associates (sena) were fierce (ugra). This hints at Kaṁsa’s fierce nature. It is also suggested that Kaṁsa was more fearsome due to appearing in his father’s line, since it is said that fear arising from friends and relatives destroys the root. Or protect us, but do not kill Kaṁsa since he is the son of Ugrasena, a maternal uncle. “Why are you afraid?” He is ferocious (ghorāt). Bho indicates great pain. The method of deliverance is suggested. You have a smile which destroys your servants’ fears (bhṛtya-vitrāsa-hāsi). Do not show your form to the ignorant (māṁsa-ḍṛśām) or to those like Kaṁsa who see only to eat (māṁsa-dṛśām). Why? That form is the object of meditation for sages only (dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam). We will not be fearful if he does not know about your birth, being unable to see the attractive form. Or simply disappear. Not showing the form means the person should disappear. This is a prayer made out of affection.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa is the abhayam-giver — when fear closes in, beg Him to meet you in the form your trembling heart can hold today.
Practical Applications
  • Name the specific 'Kaṁsa' you fear today — a person, outcome, or threat — and place it openly before Kṛṣṇa as Devakī did, asking for protection.
  • Refuse the impersonalist temptation to abstract Kṛṣṇa into 'the Self'; speak to Him directly, by name, the way a frightened mother speaks to her child's protector.
  • When His four-armed splendor feels too distant, ask Him to come close as the simple, holdable form your bhakti can actually serve right now.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like Devakī, asking Kṛṣṇa to 'tone down' His full presence because His undisguised reality threatens the safety I have built around a 'Kaṁsa' I still fear?
  • Whose 'māṁsa-dṛśā' — material gaze — am I unconsciously protecting Kṛṣṇa from in my own practice: relatives, colleagues, my own skeptical mind? And what does that hiding cost the depth of my relationship with Him?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Identify one place online — a group chat, a feed, a comment thread — where revealing your actual sādhana (japa, deity worship, scripture study) would invite ridicule from a 'Kaṁsa-ātmaja' figure; practice Devakī's discretion this week and keep that part of your life dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam, internal, while not denying it if directly asked.
  • When exam fear or peer-rejection fear spikes, name the specific person or outcome you actually fear (not the vague 'pressure'); then pray exactly as Devakī did — 'trāhi trastān' — naming Kṛṣṇa as bhṛtya-vitrāsa-hāsi, the one who removes a servant's fear, not as a generic universe.
  • Notice the temptation to want Kṛṣṇa to show up dramatically — a viral post, a miraculous result, an obvious sign — and instead train your eye to see Him in the 'ordinary child' form: the steady friend, the un-glamorous duty done, the quiet answer to prayer that nobody else would even register.
householder
  • In family decisions where you are tempted to broadcast your devotion (in-laws, colleagues, school parents), ask Devakī's question first: would the four-armed form serve them, or would the ordinary child? Calibrate disclosure to the listener's adhikāra, not to your need to be seen as devout.
  • Identify the specific 'Kaṁsa' in your professional or financial life — a particular person, deal, or dependency whose hostility you actually fear — and stop laundering it as generic stress; bring that named fear into your morning prayer and let Kṛṣṇa be the protector of that exact relationship, not an abstract refuge.
  • When raising children, resist the urge to make Kṛṣṇa flashy to compete with their entertainment; let Him appear in your home as an ordinary, daily presence — meals offered, a small altar, a verse at bedtime — trusting that what looks unimpressive to māṁsa-dṛś eyes is precisely the form that will lodge in their hearts.
senior
  • As bodily strength loosens, deliberately let your sādhana become more dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam and less performative — fewer external duties, more internal worship; the form of the Lord that meets you now is the one yogīs see in meditation, and that interior chamber is exactly what this stage is for.
  • Name your actual remaining fears — a specific illness, a particular grandchild's future, a legacy concern — rather than the polite 'fear of death'; bring each named fear to Kṛṣṇa as Devakī did, asking Him precisely as bhṛtya-vitrāsa-hāsi, the destroyer of the servant's fear, and notice which fears dissolve and which expose remaining attachment.
  • When mentoring younger family or students, practice Devakī's restraint: do not display the 'four-armed form' of all you have realized; offer them the ordinary-child form they can actually hold — a simple instruction, a shared meal, a verse — and trust the Lord to reveal more when their eyes are ready.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking Devakī's request for the Lord to conceal His four-armed form as scriptural license for impersonalism — as though the 'ordinary child' form were a metaphor for a formless Absolute, when in fact both forms are Kṛṣṇa personally and the verse honors His personhood, not its dissolution.
  • Reading 'bhṛtya-vitrāsa-hāsi' (He who dispels His devotees' fear) as a guarantee of material safety — expecting Kṛṣṇa to remove every external 'Kaṁsa' from one's life — rather than as the inward abhayam He grants the surrendered soul, which Devakī herself receives even while continuing to be harassed.
  • Imitating Devakī's protective concealment without her bhakti — keeping one's sādhana, japa, tilaka, or Kṛṣṇa-conscious convictions hidden from the world out of social fear, and calling that discretion 'humility,' when it is actually the māṁsa-dṛśā of others ruling one's own devotional life.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī asks Kṛṣṇa to hide His four-armed Viṣṇu form so He looks like an ordinary child. Was this a lapse of faith — she should have trusted His protection — or was it a higher expression of vātsalya-rasa? Defend your answer to a partner who takes the opposite view.
  • Prabhupāda says Devakī feared not Ugrasena but the son of Ugrasena (Kaṁsa-ātmaja). In your own life, where does fear actually originate — circumstances, or specific persons whose intent threatens you? Share a concrete instance and let the group press you on whether you have correctly identified the real source.
  • The verse holds two truths in tension: the Lord's form is dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam (only for yogīs in meditation) yet He stands present before material eyes. Discuss with one other person: where in your sādhana do you actually want Kṛṣṇa to remain hidden, and why? Surface a real disagreement, not a polite agreement.
Role Play

Two students sit across from each other. Student A is 'Devakī' — the Lord has just manifested in their life as something obviously transcendental (an undeniable answer to prayer, a clear instruction from guru, a protection in crisis). Student B is 'a relative-Kaṁsa' — a skeptical, powerful family member who would mock, sabotage, or exploit this manifestation if they recognized it. Student A must explain to Student B what happened, while deciding in real time how much of Kṛṣṇa's hand to reveal and how much to 'conceal as an ordinary child.' After five minutes, switch roles. Then the class discusses: when is concealment bhakti-yukta discretion, and when is it a failure of nerve?

Objections & Responses
  • Why would the Supreme Personality of Godhead need His mother to ask Him to hide? An omnipotent God would not require coaching from a frightened devotee — this reads like a folk story projected onto the divine.
    Precisely the point a Gauḍīya teacher would lean into. Kṛṣṇa is not a remote omnipotence to be defended philosophically — He is a person who relishes being managed by His devotees. He becomes bhṛtya-vitrāsa-hāsi (destroyer of the servant's fear) by responding to her cry, not by overriding it. His omnipotence includes the freedom to be guided by vātsalya. To strip the exchange of its personalism in the name of protecting God's dignity is the impersonalist mistake — it makes the Absolute smaller, not greater.
  • Asking Kṛṣṇa to become 'invisible to material eyes' sounds like religious privacy theater — keep the mystical stuff hidden so outsiders cannot scoff. Is bhakti really about concealment from non-believers?
    Devakī is not protecting the Lord's reputation; she is protecting the unprepared eye from a vision it would weaponize. Kaṁsa seeing Viṣṇu would not produce surrender — it would produce homicidal panic, because māṁsa-dṛśāḥ, fleshly eyes, cannot receive what dhyāna-dhiṣṇyam discloses. The principle is not secrecy but adhikāra: the same sun that ripens fruit blisters bare skin. A Gauḍīya teacher applies this to today — we do not parade kīrtana, deity-worship, or guru-instruction in front of those who can only ridicule, not because the truth is fragile, but because we owe them a doorway sized to their eyes.
