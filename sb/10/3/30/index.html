Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.30

O my Lord, You are the all-pervading Supreme Personality of Godhead, and Your transcendental four-armed form, holding conchshell, disc, club and lotus, is unnatural for this world. Please withdraw this form [and become just like a natural human child so that I may try to hide You somewhere].
Purport

Devakī was thinking of hiding the Supreme Personality of Godhead and not handing Him over to Kaṁsa as she had all her previous children. Although Vasudeva had promised to hand over every child to Kaṁsa, this time he wanted to break his promise and hide the child somewhere. But because of the Lord’s appearance in this surprising four-armed form, He would be impossible to hide.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O my Lord, You are the all-pervading Supreme Personality of Godhead, and Your transcendental four-armed form, holding conchshell, disc, club and lotus, is unnatural for this world. Please withdraw this form [and become just like a natural human child so that I may try to hide You somewhere].
Commentary

"As you pervade the whole universe and I reside within the universe, you also reside within me. How then has this weakness of heart arisen in me? It must be your fault." Thus she addresses the Lord as visvatma, soul of the universe. "Withdraw this uncommon form and assume a form of a normal human baby, and I will quickly hide you."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Doubting that he will agree to her request, she speaks again, unable to tolerate Kaṁsa. Hide that particular form with four arms decorated with conch, cakra, club and lotus. Show a different form. You cannot hide your present form from the people anywhere. O soul of the universe! Since you show unlimited forms simultaneously, this is not impossible for you! Hiding two of your arms and your Kaustubha and other ornaments, show a concealed form like that of other people. Then you will be able to hide anywhere among the people.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Withdraw this form since it is extraordinary. O soul of the universe, this is possible for you to do. The extraordinary form is described. It is endowed with the beauty of conch etc. Or it should be withdrawn because it is extraordinary, with the beauty of clothing etc. and conch, cakra, lotus and club. The deep meaning is this: it is possible to protect you by hiding you somewhere in the world. Or the reason for hiding is given in the previous verse. He should not know, because by not knowing about your birth (bhavad-dhetoḥ), he will not harm us. “Should I disappear completely?” This verse answers. No, just withdraw this particular form.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa veils His opulence so love can flow as parental affection — surrender to the Person who hides His majesty for our sake.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where you demand a 'four-armed' proof from Kṛṣṇa before trusting Him, and choose to relate to Him in His simpler, nearer form instead.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa in prayer not as an abstract all-pervading force but as the personal Lord who consents to be small enough for your heart to hold.
  • Refuse the impersonalist habit of inflating God beyond intimacy — let your sādhana today be one concrete act of personal service, not awe at His vastness.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my sādhana am I, like Devakī, secretly asking Kṛṣṇa to dim His majesty so that I can fit Him into a life I have already arranged on my own terms?
  • When the Lord shows up in a form that is alaukika — inconvenient, demanding, impossible to hide — what is my first instinct: to adjust my life to His reality, or to ask Him to adjust to mine?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the moments — a class, a kīrtana, a verse, a conversation — when something distinctly Kṛṣṇa-conscious shows up in 'four-armed' form: undeniably not-of-this-world. Resist the urge to immediately translate it down into something your friend group or Instagram can accept. Sit with it un-edited for at least a day before you decide how to talk about it.
  • Identify one 'Vasudeva promise' you are quietly carrying — an agreement made to a peer culture, a romantic partner, a future-self career script — that the appearance of Kṛṣṇa in your life is now asking you to break. Name it specifically to one trusted devotee this week.
  • When parents, professors, or close friends ask you to 'be normal' about your sādhana — eat what everyone eats, drink what everyone drinks, date how everyone dates — practice the Vasudeva move: outwardly humble, inwardly unwavering. Don't argue; just don't hand the child over.
householder
  • Where in your family life are you, like Devakī, asking Kṛṣṇa to come in a less inconvenient form — fitting Him into the gaps left by career, mortgage, schools — instead of letting His full claim reorganize the home? This week, change one concrete domestic arrangement (mealtime, morning hour, weekend) so that His four-armed form is not hidden but honored.
  • In raising children, distinguish real compassion from material compassion. Material compassion shields them from every discomfort and trains them for viṣaya. Real compassion gives them sva-dharma, restraint, and an early relationship with Kṛṣṇa — even at the cost of being the 'strict' parent. Pick one decision this month (screen time, peer group, festival observance) and choose by that test.
  • On money and career: Vasudeva had to keep functioning inside Kaṁsa's regime while his real loyalty was elsewhere. Examine where you are doing the same with integrity, and where you have quietly merged with the regime. One specific corrective action this quarter — a tithe redirected, an ethically marginal contract refused, a Sunday reclaimed from work.
senior
  • At this stage Kṛṣṇa is increasingly willing to appear in His undisguised form — through bereavements, the body's failures, the thinning of worldly hopes. Stop asking Him to withdraw it. Each morning, instead of praying for the form of life you used to have, sit for five minutes and welcome the form of Kṛṣṇa that is actually showing up now.
  • Mentor at least one younger devotee or family member specifically in the personalist line — that Kṛṣṇa is a person, surrender is to Him, and impersonal 'the universe / consciousness' language is a downgrade. Your accumulated authority is meant to be spent on this, not hoarded.
  • Loosen attachments deliberately, not sentimentally. Pick one possession, one role, or one identity this month and formally hand it over — to a successor, to a temple, to Kṛṣṇa. Practice now what death will require shortly; the dhīra is the one who has rehearsed.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading Devakī's request as a flaw and missing that it arises from pure vātsalya — and then using that misreading to justify our own self-protective requests, which spring from ahaṅkāra, not love.
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's four-armed aiśvarya form and His two-armed mādhurya form as if one cancels the other, instead of seeing that He withdraws the opulence to deepen intimate relationship — not to become 'just a symbol' or an impersonal presence.
  • Concluding that because Kṛṣṇa accommodates His devotee, we may freely ask Him to shrink His instructions to fit our comfort — confusing the Lord's loving reciprocation with permission to negotiate away surrender.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī asks Kṛṣṇa to withdraw His four-armed opulence and appear as an ordinary child. Share with your partner: where in your own life are you tempted to ask Kṛṣṇa to be 'less' — less demanding, less inconvenient, less visibly transcendental — so He fits into your existing arrangements? Push back on each other if the answer sounds tidy.
  • Vasudeva had promised Kaṁsa every child, yet here he plans to break that promise to protect this one. Discuss: when does ordinary dharma (keeping one's word, obeying authority) need to bend before the higher claim of serving Kṛṣṇa directly? Try to find a case where you and your partner genuinely disagree.
  • Devakī's request comes from intense parental affection (vātsalya), but it is also a request that the Lord conceal His own glory. Is this love a model to imitate, or a humbling example of how even an exalted devotee's mood can run ahead of pure surrender? Defend your reading to someone who takes the opposite view.
Role Play

Two students take roles. Student A is a college senior who has just had an unmistakable, uncomfortable encounter with Kṛṣṇa-consciousness — a verse that landed, a saṅga that exposed how shallow their current life is, a clear inner instruction to change course (drop a job offer, end a relationship that pulls them down, take initiation). Student B is their well-meaning parent or closest friend who responds exactly like Devakī here: 'Please, withdraw this form. Be a normal child. Don't be so visibly different. Hide this.' Run the conversation for five minutes. Then swap roles. Afterwards, name the specific 'four-armed form' of Kṛṣṇa in your own life that someone close to you is begging you to put away, and what a Gauḍīya response to that plea would sound like — firm, affectionate, not defensive.

Objections & Responses
  • If Devakī, who has just received the darśana every yogī craves, immediately asks Kṛṣṇa to hide His four-armed form, isn't this proof that 'pure devotion' is really just psychological projection — people shaping God into whatever shape their nerves can handle?
    Look more carefully at what is happening. Devakī is not denying the four-armed form, debating it, or reducing it to metaphor — she has just offered elaborate prayers acknowledging Him as viśvātman, the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Her request is not 'You are not really like this'; it is 'Please show me Your other form so I can serve You as my child.' That is the opposite of projection. Projection says, 'I will not accept what is actually there.' Vātsalya-bhakti says, 'I accept You completely, and our relationship has a particular flavor in which this aiśvarya obstructs the intimacy You Yourself desire with me.' Kṛṣṇa complies precisely because the request is itself an expression of pure relationship, not a managing-down of the Absolute. Compare this to a king visiting his mother in full royal regalia — her asking him to set the crown aside is not denial of his kingship; it is the deeper truth of their bond.
  • Asking Kṛṣṇa to 'withdraw' a transcendental form sounds like the devotee dictating to God. Doesn't real surrender mean accepting whatever He shows, exactly as He shows it?
    There is a crucial distinction between manipulation and intimacy. Surrender is not a flat passivity; it is entrance into a specific relationship (rasa) with Kṛṣṇa, and within that relationship, requests are part of the love. Arjuna in the eleventh chapter sees the viśva-rūpa and asks Kṛṣṇa to please return to the two-armed form — and the Gītā treats this not as failure of surrender but as the natural plea of a sakhā. Devakī here is in vātsalya-rasa. The śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma form is real and worshipable, but in her relationship the human-like form is more real still, because Kṛṣṇa Himself values being her son more than being worshipped as Nārāyaṇa. Surrender does not mean erasing yourself before an abstraction; it means letting Him draw out of you the relationship He has eternally wanted. If you find yourself confusing surrender with self-erasure, the corrective is to study how the great devotees actually speak to Him — and notice how often they ask, plead, even argue, all from within complete dependence.
