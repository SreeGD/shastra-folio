Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.12

अथैनमस्तौदवधार्य पूरुषं परं नताङ्ग: कृतधी: कृताञ्जलि: । स्वरोचिषा भारत सूतिकागृहं विरोचयन्तं गतभी: प्रभाववित् ॥ १२ ॥

Important Words
  • astaut — offered prayers
    Aorist of stu — to praise, glorify. This is stuti, vandana-bhakti in action, the fifth of the nine principal limbs. Vasudeva's first act of formal bhakti to his son is verbal praise — establishing the relationship by naming who has come.
  • avadhārya — having understood with certainty
    Gerund of ava-√dhṛ — to ascertain, conclude. Not 'guessing' but settled cognition. The Gauḍīya line emphasizes that bhakti must be grounded in sambandha-jñāna — knowing precisely whom one serves. Vasudeva first ascertains; then he prays.
  • pūruṣam — the Supreme Person
    Alternate form of puruṣa (person). Used here to mark the highest sense — the puruṣottama, supreme person referred to in BG 15. Bhagavān is not Force, not Principle, but Person. Personalism is the verse's spine.
  • kṛta-añjaliḥ — with folded palms
    Kṛta (made, done) + añjali (the hollowed palms joined in offering). The classical Vedic posture before a superior. In Vaiṣṇava practice, the añjali-mudrā is itself an offering — empty hands giving back what was never owned. Body precedes speech in true śaraṇāgati.
  • gata-bhīḥ — whose fear has departed
    Gata (gone) + bhī (fear). The instant of seeing Bhagavān ends fear. This is the verse's emotional pivot — Kaṁsa's prison no longer terrifies Vasudeva because the supreme protector is now visibly his son. Echoes BG 18.66: śaraṇāgati dissolves fear at its root.
  • prabhāva-vit — knower of His influence
    Prabhāva (potency, influence) + vit (knower). Vasudeva does not merely see; he comprehends what is happening. The Gauḍīya threefold knowledge (svarūpa, prabhāva, līlā) of Bhagavān begins with this prabhāva-jñāna.
O Mahārāja Parīkṣit, descendant of King Bharata, Vasudeva could understand that this child was the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Nārāyaṇa. Having concluded this without a doubt, he became fearless. Bowing down with folded hands and concentrating his attention, he began to offer prayers to the child, who illuminated His birthplace by His natural influence.
Purport

Struck with such great wonder, Vasudeva now concentrated his attention on the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Understanding the influence of the Supreme Lord, he was surely fearless, since he understood that the Lord had appeared to give him protection ( gata-bhīḥ prabhāva-vit ). Understanding that the Supreme Personality of Godhead was present, he appropriately offered prayers as follows.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Mahārāja Parīkṣit, descendant of King Bharata, Vasudeva could understand that this child was the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Nārāyaṇa. Having concluded this without a doubt, he became fearless. Bowing down with folded hands and concentrating his attention, he began to offer prayers to the child, who illuminated His birthplace by His natural influence.
Commentary

Fixing his mind on the Lord (krtadhih), Vasudeva thought of the Lord with reverence as God and affection as his son. : Though he had fear for his tender child being harmed by Kamsa’s weapons, out of parental love, that fear vanished when he realized that his child was the Supreme Lord (gata bhi prabhava vit)

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Immediately (atha), ascertaining that the Lord had appeared in his complete form (param), since he was absorbed in the Lord (kṛta-dhīḥ), he offered his respects with his limbs on the ground, or he bowed his head. Another reason for his conclusion is mentioned. His son lit up the darkness of the maternity room, but not beyond, by his effulgence actually equal to millions of suns and moons, giving intense joy to his mind and eyes. The child’s single body simultaneously spread effulgence and limited it. This indicated the Lord’s power. What was Vasudeva’s condition in offering prayers? He lost his fear of Kaṁsa. But would he not be afraid that Kaṁsa’s guards would hear? Vasudeva knew the power of the Lord (prabhava-vit). He thought “What can these inferior rascals or Kaṁsa do?” O descendant of Bharata! Śukadeva addresses Parīkṣit with affection, since he carries the good fortune of Vasudeva. Or bhārata can mean “Parīkṣit, absorbed (rata) in Kṛṣṇa because of his shining beauty (bhā).”

Personal Guidance
When you grasp Kṛṣṇa's prabhāva, fear dissolves — fold your hands, steady your mind, and offer yourself to the Lord who has come to protect you.
Practical Applications
  • Before today's most anxious task, pause, fold your hands, and silently acknowledge Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality already present and protecting you.
  • Notice one moment of fear today and replace it with gata-bhīḥ prabhāva-vit — recall a specific instance where the Lord arranged your protection.
  • Begin your morning sādhana on your knees with kṛta-añjali, offering prayers before asking anything, as Vasudeva did upon recognizing the Lord.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still trying to offer prayers and surrender from a posture of fear, instead of letting prabhāva-vit — recognition of Kṛṣṇa's protective influence — make me gata-bhīḥ?
  • What 'birthplace' in me — what cramped, prison-like circumstance — am I refusing to let Kṛṣṇa illuminate by His own sva-rociṣā, because I would rather light it with my own effort?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam or interview, fold your hands once in private and admit: my fear of failing is really fear that I am alone in this. Then offer the attempt to Krishna and notice whether the dhīḥ steadies — not the outcome.
  • When a friend group or social-media pile-on is pressuring you, practice Vasudeva's two moves: kṛta-dhīḥ (fix your intelligence on what you actually know to be true) before kṛtāñjaliḥ (any external response). Refuse to react until the inner dhīḥ is set.
  • Pick one situation this week where peer pressure usually flips you into fear of being left out. Treat it as Vasudeva's sūtikā-gṛham — a cramped, hostile room — and ask: what would change if I knew, with prabhāva-vit certainty, that Krishna is already present here?
householder
  • When the next family or financial crisis hits — a child's illness, a job threat, a money shock — pause before reacting and do Vasudeva's sequence: first conclude without doubt who is actually in charge (avadhārya), then act. Most householder panic skips the avadhārya and goes straight to scrambling.
  • Your home is the sūtikā-gṛham — the room where Krishna can appear or be ignored. This week, light a lamp and offer prayers in front of your spouse and children at least once, openly, so they see a working adult bow with folded hands. Do not lecture; just model kṛtāñjaliḥ.
  • Notice where you confuse material compassion (shielding family from every bodily discomfort) with real karuṇā (serving their souls). Identify one decision this month — about screens, schooling, money, or aging parents — where the two are pulling in opposite directions, and choose the soul-serving option.
senior
  • Vasudeva's fearlessness came from prabhāva-vit — knowing the Lord's protective influence. At this stage, mortality is no longer abstract. Each morning, recall one specific instance from your own life where Krishna's prabhāva became unmistakable, and let that memory, not anxiety, set the tone for the day.
  • Loosen one attachment this week that you have been quietly gripping — a possession, a role, an opinion about how your children should live — and offer it the way Vasudeva offered prayers: with folded hands, without negotiation. Notice the gata-bhīḥ that follows.
  • Mentor someone younger by telling them, plainly and without embellishment, one moment when you understood Krishna's position and your fear dropped. Do not invent or inflate — Vasudeva offered prayers only after avadhārya, after he was sure. Your job at this stage is to be a credible witness, not a performer.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking Vasudeva's fearlessness as a generic positive-thinking move ('don't be afraid') instead of the specific fruit of recognizing Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead present before him.
  • Treating the Lord's self-illumining presence (sva-rociṣā) as an inner 'light of consciousness' or 'divine spark within,' draining the verse of its personalist force — the child Nārāyaṇa lighting an actual room.
  • Rushing past the kṛta-dhīḥ kṛtāñjaliḥ posture — folded hands, fixed intelligence, bowed body — and offering prayers casually, as if reverence and bodily humility were optional once one 'understands' philosophically.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva became fearless (gata-bhīḥ) only after he understood Krishna's prabhāva — His real position. In your own life, where does fear actually come from: from the situation itself, or from forgetting who Krishna is? Share a recent moment where this distinction would have changed how you acted.
  • The verse pivots on kṛta-dhīḥ — 'fixed intelligence' — and kṛtāñjaliḥ — folded hands. One is internal, one is external. Which of these is harder for you, and why? Where in modern life do we keep the external posture of reverence while letting the internal dhīḥ wander, and where do we have inner conviction but refuse to bow physically?
  • Vasudeva offered prayers only after he was certain who the child was — not based on sentiment but on prabhāva-vit, knowing the Lord's influence. Disagree with each other: is it better to begin with bhakti and let understanding follow, or to establish understanding first and let bhakti follow? Defend a position you don't fully hold.
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A plays a friend who has just survived a near-fatal accident and is shaken — convinced the universe is hostile and random. Student B plays Vasudeva-the-householder, someone who has just witnessed how Krishna actually protects (gata-bhīḥ prabhāva-vit) and must speak from that conviction without sounding preachy, without inventing pastimes, and without collapsing into 'everything happens for a reason' platitudes. Student B has 4 minutes to share what changed in their fear after they understood Krishna's prabhāva. Then swap roles. Debrief: where did real karuṇā differ from material consolation?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just the standard 'recognize God and your fear vanishes' move — every religion has it. What's actually distinctive here, or is the Gauḍīya version just dressed up with Sanskrit?
    The distinction is precise and worth holding. Vasudeva does not merge into an Absolute, dissolve into 'the Self,' or take refuge in an impersonal 'consciousness.' He folds his hands (kṛtāñjaliḥ) — a posture only meaningful between two persons — and offers prayers to a specific child with a specific form. The fearlessness comes not from generic transcendence but from prabhāva-vit, knowing this Personality and His protective relationship with His devotee. Strip the personalism out and you don't get the same verse, you get a different philosophy. The Gauḍīya line insists fear ends in relationship, not in dissolution.
  • Vasudeva sees the four-armed Nārāyaṇa form glowing in the room — of course he becomes fearless. I have never had a vision like that. Telling me to be 'fearless because Krishna protects' when I have no such confirmation is asking me to fake a conviction I don't have.
    Fair pushback, and the verse itself answers it. Vasudeva is called prabhāva-vit — one who knows the Lord's influence — not pratyakṣa-darśī, one who has merely had a sighting. His fearlessness is grounded in understanding, not just in spectacle. We are given the same understanding through śāstra, through guru, and through the reports of devotees like Vasudeva himself. The path is not to manufacture a vision but to deepen the dhīḥ — the intelligence — through hearing and chanting, until the same conclusion settles in. Sentiment without dhīḥ collapses under pressure; dhīḥ produces stable fearlessness even without the vision.
Analogy

When you walk into a small shrine and the lamp is suddenly relit by some unseen hand, the awe that arises is not abstract — your hands fold of themselves, your knees bend before your mind decides. Vasudeva's prostration is precisely this. Real recognition of Bhagavān moves the body before the intellect catches up.

Stories
Arjuna folding his hands at the Viśvarūpa

When Kṛṣṇa showed Arjuna the universal form on Kurukṣetra, Arjuna's response in BG 11.14 was identical to Vasudeva's: 'praṇamya śirasā devaṁ kṛtāñjalir abhāṣata' — bowing his head with folded hands. The bodily posture preceded any prayer.

The Bhāgavatam is showing us that the recognition of Bhagavān, whether in cosmic form on a battlefield or as an infant in a prison, evokes the same physical surrender: nata-aṅga, kṛta-añjali, kṛta-dhīḥ — body bowed, palms joined, attention concentrated.

Sanātana Gosvāmī at Caitanya's feet

When Sanātana Gosvāmī first met Caitanya Mahāprabhu at Vārāṇasī, he prostrated full-length in the public street and refused to rise until the Lord touched him. He understood instantly who stood before him; his body knew before his words could form.

Vasudeva's prabhāva-vit — knowing the influence — is the same recognition. The mark of true understanding of Bhagavān is the body's spontaneous surrender, not philosophical articulation.
