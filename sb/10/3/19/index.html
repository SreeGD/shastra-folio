Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.19

O my Lord, learned Vedic scholars conclude that the creation, maintenance and annihilation of the entire cosmic manifestation are performed by You, who are free from endeavor, unaffected by the modes of material nature, and changeless in Your spiritual situation. There are no contradictions in You, who are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Parabrahman. Because the three modes of material nature — sattva, rajas and tamas — are under Your control, everything takes place automatically.
Purport

As stated in the Vedas: na tasya kāryaṁ karaṇaṁ ca vidyate na tat-samaś cābhyadhikaś ca dṛśyate parāsya śaktir vividhaiva śrūyate svābhāvikī jñāna-bala-kriyā ca “The Supreme Lord has nothing to do, and no one is found to be equal to or greater than Him, for everything is done naturally and systematically by His multifarious energies.” ( Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 6.8) Creation, maintenance and annihilation are all conducted personally by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and this is confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā ( mayādhyakṣeṇa prakṛtiḥ sūyate sa-carācaram ). Yet ultimately the Lord does not need to do anything, and therefore He is nirvikāra, changeless. Because everything is done under His direction, He is called sṛṣṭi-kartā, the master of creation. Similarly, He is the master of annihilation. When a master sits in one place while his servants work in different duties, whatever the servants are doing is ultimately an activity of the master, although he is doing nothing ( na tasya kāryaṁ karaṇaṁ ca vidyate ). The Lord’s potencies are so numerous that everything is nicely done. Therefore, He is naturally still and is not directly the doer of anything in this material world.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O my Lord, learned Vedic scholars conclude that the creation, maintenance and annihilation of the entire cosmic manifestation are performed by You, who are free from endeavor, unaffected by the modes of material nature, and changeless in Your spiritual situation. There are no contradictions in You, who are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Parabrahman. Because the three modes of material nature-sattva, rajas and tamas—are under Your control, everything takes place automatically.
Commentary

From your statements that you are the creator through prakrti it is understood that prakrti is your energy and non different from you. You are the material cause of the universe. But you are without contact with the gunas internally or externally. How is that possible? This verse answers the question. O Lord, the sruti says that creation, maintenance and destruction of the universe arise from you, who are without action, quality and change. But how can the creator be inactive? Though you are Brahma and Siva, the creator and destroyer, at the same time you are without action (aniha), You are the reservoir of opposite qualities. How? Because you are the shelter of the gunas, the action of creation and destruction are ascribed upon you. Though prakrti is the Lord’s energy ,it is his external energy. It is not his svarupa or svabhava. Thus you are not in contact with the gunas internally or externally. This conclusion is established here. Or, how can my son be Brahma and Siva? Actually you are not Brahma or Siva--you are the shelter of both. You says this yourself: brahmano hi pratithaham. Brahma says: adyo’vatarah purusah parasya... Since you are the shelter of Brahma and Siva (tad asrayatvat) you are called the creator and destroyer indirectly. The qualities of a person being sheltered are often ascribed to the shelterer. Just as when the country is fortunate, the king is called fortunate. In this way you are Brahma and Siva. This is also in accordance with the concept of rasa. The form of Krsna is distinct in terms of rasa, though though he is one with other forms of the Lord. Brahma, Siva and Krsna are one form: Rasenotkrsyate krsna rupam esa rasa sthiti. (bhaki rasamrta sindhu)

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Śṛīdhara Svāmī explains this verse. This explanation is based on that. Anīhāt means the Lord is without disturbance or without change since he is devoid of anything except his svarūpa. Though there are transformations by the guṇas, there is no fault in you since you are only its material cause. There may be a doubt that you can also be the indirect cause (nimitta) (since you are also the material cause). You are actually Brahman in nature (brahmaṇi), and only by attribution of guṇas does it seem that you undergo change. Or you are established as the possessor of unlimited qualities, as the faultless cause of everything since you are both the efficient and material cause as proved by your statement (tvattaḥ) prakṛtiś ca pratijṣā-dṛṣtantanurodhena: Brahman is also the material nature (prakṛti) because this view is not contradicted by the statements and examples given in the scriptures. (Brahma-sūtra 1.4.23) The fear of contradiction is refuted starting with words like vibho (O Lord!) The three words aguṇāt, akriyāt and anīhāṭ are explained as follows. Though you have no relationship with the three guṇas (aguṇāt), because you are the material cause, you seem to become the guṇas. But there is no possibility that you can deviate from your svarūpa (anīhāt). Because you are the efficient cause, you have no external or internal actions (akriyāt). Therefore you are called vibhu. Kṛtsna-prasakti-niravayava-śabda-vyāpako vā: the jīva is absorbed in activity with all his parts for it is said that the jīva has no parts. (Brahma-sūtra 2.1.26) Therefore statements about you are untrue, because we see that you are worshipped in different forms, and thus not situated in one place. But this statement does not apply to you, if you are accepted as the efficient cause. Śrutes tu śabda-mūlatvāt: the defects if the jīva do not apply to Brahman because of the declaration of scripture; scripture is the root of knowledge about spirit. (Brhama-sūtra 2.1.27) The wise speak from authority when they say that you are the cause, maintenance and destruction of everything. What doubt can there be if the śruti, the self-manifesting authority, says that you are beyond the four defects, that you are without material guṇas and that you are the cause of the universe? There is no contradiction here. You are the one form of supreme bliss without change (brāhmaṇi) but also the Lord possessing all inconceivable śaktis (īśvare). The cintāmaṇi or load stone remain unchanged though they attract other objects or pieces of iron. acintyāḥ khalu ye bhāvā tāns tarkeṇa yojayet prakṛitbhyaḥ paraṁ yac ca tad acintyasya lakṣaṇam Inconceivable states are not subject to material logic since they are beyond the material situation. That is the quality of the inconceivable. Mahābhārata There is no contradiction in you, who are the supreme cause with various inconceivable śaktis working together, as proved by the Purāṇas. All contradictions on both sides are eliminated by strongly establishing the inconceivable śakti. There is no alternative to this. There can be no opposing arguments because this śakti is supremely suitable to the Lord, being superior to all else, and since it is devoid of dependence on matter and ignorance, being supreme bliss beyond words and mind. “That’s alright. In the spiritual world, the Lord with his śakti is faultless with his six qualities. But as the cause of the material world possessing śaktis of the three guṇas, full of fault, he becomes faulty.” You are the shelter of the material śakti. Because this śakti is only a shadow, you cannot be contaminated by the faults of matter. On the other hand, the guṇas do not function unless they take shelter of you. Though the guṇas act as the cause, this is only a product of your inconceivable power.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“Why should I be worshipped?” The knowers of the Vedas or the Vedas say that the creation of the universe comes from you who are without desire, who depend on nothing else, who are devoid of relationship with material guṇas, and who are devoid of transformations like anger. Or Lord (vibho), endowed with all powers! You are able to carry out creation, maintenance and destruction of the universe, being superior to it, as previously described. The three descriptive terms mentioned indicate the Lord’s independence from prakṛti. But still you create (without desire), maintain (without guṇas) and destroy the world (without anger). “This looks like a contradiction.” Just as for the one person who is the Lord and Brahman there is no contradiction since all the powers belong to Brahman, beyond māyā, so there is no contradiction if the Lord produces the material universe. “There is no contradiction within the absolute truth, but there is contradiction in creating the universe which is māyā.” The creation takes place in you by the guṇas of māyā, since you are the shelter of those guṇas. There is no contradiction whether you have or do not have a relationship with the guṇas, Or you are continually served by the guṇas, since you are their shelter. No one else is. Because of that, there is no contradiction. You are to be worshipped because of your form of power.

Concepts
SattvaMaya
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa effortlessly directs creation, maintenance, and dissolution through His energies — surrender removes your imagined burden of being the doer.
Practical Applications
  • Notice one moment today when you grasp for control of an outcome, and consciously hand it back to Kṛṣṇa as the true director.
  • Before each task, whisper that Kṛṣṇa is the akartā who acts through His śaktis — then act with full effort but without the weight of proprietorship.
  • Refuse the ahaṅkāra-thought 'I made this happen' tonight when reviewing your day, and instead name three places His energies carried the work.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I straining and 'endeavoring' as if I were the doer, when the verse says even the cosmos itself unfolds under Kṛṣṇa's effortless glance — what would it look like to act today as His instrument rather than as the proprietor of outcomes?
  • Which of the three guṇas — sattva, rajas, or tamas — is currently driving the activity I most identify with as 'mine,' and how does recognizing that this guṇa is itself under Kṛṣṇa's control change the way I approach my next task?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam, formally place the result at Kṛṣṇa's feet — study with full intensity, then refuse the 3 a.m. spiral of 'what if I fail.' The fruit is His department; the effort is yours.
  • Notice this week how social media trains you to believe you are the doer — every post a bid for credit. For one full day, do something genuinely good (help a classmate, clean up a space) and tell no one. Feel what it is like to act without claiming.
  • When a friend group dynamic goes sideways and you feel powerless, remember: the guṇas are moving through everyone, including you. Your job is not to control the outcome but to act in sattva — speak truth gently, refuse gossip, take shelter in Kṛṣṇa instead of in your peer group's approval.
householder
  • In the next family financial decision (a job change, a school choice, a big purchase), do the homework rigorously, then explicitly hand the outcome to Kṛṣṇa before deciding. Watch how much of your stress was about pretending to be the controller.
  • When your child's life is not unfolding the way you planned — grades, friendships, choices — practice tvad-āśrayatvāt: take shelter in Him rather than in your parental ahaṅkāra. Your duty is faithful action (sva-dharma as parent), not engineered outcomes.
  • At work, when a project succeeds, resist the inner claim 'I did this' for thirty seconds before the next thought. When it fails, resist the inner claim 'I caused this collapse' for the same thirty seconds. Both are ahaṅkāra; both block real surrender.
senior
  • Look at the institutions, businesses, or family roles you built and are now releasing. Practice seeing them as His all along — you were a servant in the master's house. The release is lighter when you were never the owner.
  • When you mentor someone younger, refuse the temptation to take credit for their growth or blame for their stumbles. Plant, water, and step back — the unfolding is under His direction, not yours. This is the dhīra-bhāva applied to legacy.
  • As the body's transitions accelerate — health, energy, independence — meditate on anīhād aguṇād avikriyāt: He is changeless. Each loosening attachment is a chance to rest more fully in tvad-āśrayatvāt, taking shelter in the One who does not change while everything around you does.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking Kṛṣṇa's nirvikāra (changeless, non-endeavoring) nature for the impersonalist's inactive, attribute-less Brahman — and quietly drifting from devotion to a personal Lord into worship of a vague 'stillness' or 'consciousness.'
  • Using 'everything happens automatically under His control' as a license for fatalism or laziness — neglecting one's sva-dharma and sādhana on the pretext that Kṛṣṇa is doing it all anyway.
  • Reading 'free from endeavor' as meaning Kṛṣṇa is distant or uninvolved, and thereby losing the intimacy of the personalist relationship — forgetting that creation, maintenance, and annihilation are conducted personally by Him through His śaktis.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse says Kṛṣṇa is anīhā — without endeavor — yet creation, maintenance, and annihilation are personally His. Share with your partner: where in your own life have you confused 'doing nothing visible' with 'doing nothing real'? Where have you mistaken visible exertion for actual potency? Push back if your partner gives a tidy answer.
  • Prabhupāda's purport compares the Lord to a master who sits still while servants run his estate. Discuss honestly: does this metaphor land for you, or does it feel like a justification for absentee authority? If you resist it, name what you resist — and let the others press you on whether the resistance is philosophical or just modern egalitarian conditioning.
  • The verse insists there is 'no contradiction' in the Lord — guṇa-less yet master of guṇas, changeless yet originator of all change. Where in your own sādhana do you demand a non-contradictory God on your terms (only loving, only just, only personal, only impersonal)? What gets exposed when Kṛṣṇa refuses to be flattened?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a working professional who has just been passed over for a promotion despite a year of relentless effort; she is convinced the universe is unjust and that 'if there were really a God in charge, this would not have happened.' Student B is her older Vaiṣṇava friend who has been studying SB 10.3.19. Student B must, without quoting the verse academically, help Student A see that (a) the Lord is anīhā — His non-doing is not absence, (b) the guṇas operating 'automatically' under His control include the rajas of her workplace and her own ahaṅkāra of expecting fruits, and (c) tvad-āśrayatvāt — taking shelter in Him — is the only stable footing. After 6 minutes, switch roles with a new scenario: a parent whose child has fallen seriously ill. The teacher then asks each pair: where did you slip into material compassion? Where did you offer real compassion?

Objections & Responses
  • If Kṛṣṇa is 'free from endeavor' and everything happens automatically through the guṇas, then He is functionally identical to an impersonal law of nature. Why insist on a Person at all? This sounds like dressed-up deism.
    The verse itself anticipates this and refutes it: tvayīśvare brahmaṇi no virudhyate — there is no contradiction in You, the Supreme Person who is also Parabrahman. A law cannot direct; it can only describe. The guṇas do not self-organize into cosmos, sustenance, and dissolution — they are upacaryate, served, by taking shelter in Him (tvad-āśrayatvāt). Prabhupāda's master-and-servants image is exact: the master's stillness is not absence but sovereignty. The impersonalist collapses the master into the activity of the servants and then wonders where the master went. The Gauḍīya line holds both: He acts personally, and yet na tasya kāryaṁ karaṇaṁ ca vidyate — His potencies (śakti) do the work because they are His, not in spite of Him.
  • Saying 'everything takes place automatically because the guṇas are under His control' is a perfect recipe for fatalism — and worse, it lets the powerful off the hook. Famine, injustice, abuse: all just guṇas doing their thing under divine management?
    This is the same confusion Arjuna had on the battlefield, and the Gītā's whole pivot is the answer. The verse does not say you have no role; it says the Lord is the ultimate śṛṣṭi-kartā so that you can act without the crushing burden of imagining you are. That is the opposite of fatalism — it is the foundation of karma-yoga (BG 2.47, in your related verses). You still act; you act as His instrument, with nyāya, with kāruṇya, with pratijñā. Real compassion — serving the soul — requires you to oppose abuse, feed the hungry, speak the truth. What the verse forbids is the ahaṅkāra of thinking you, separately from Him, are the doer or the rescuer. Fatalism says 'nothing I do matters.' This verse says 'everything you do matters, but never as your independent act — always as service under His shelter.'
