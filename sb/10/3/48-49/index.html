Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.48-49

Important Words
  • hṛta-pratyaya — deprived of (sense-)cognition
    Hṛta (taken away) + pratyaya (cognition, awareness). Yoga-māyā literally 'takes away' the sense-awareness of the guards. Internal energy operating on consciousness — sambandha-tattva: She does not merely make them sleep; She removes their cognition entirely.
  • kṛṣṇa-vāhe — when (Vasudeva) was carrying Kṛṣṇa
    Kṛṣṇa + vāha (one who carries) + locative absolute. The simple physical fact: Vasudeva is carrying Kṛṣṇa. But vāha is freighted: in Vaiṣṇava poetry the bhakta's body becomes Kṛṣṇa's vehicle. Vasudeva is the first kṛṣṇa-vāha — the prototype.
  • śeṣaḥ — Ananta-Śeṣa
    Śeṣa — 'the remainder', 'what is left'. The eternal Ananta-Śeṣa who serves as Bhagavān's couch, umbrella, throne, brother. Gauḍīya identification: Śeṣa is the same as Saṅkarṣaṇa-tattva and appears as Balarāma in Kṛṣṇa-līlā, as Nityānanda in Caitanya-līlā. The first viṣṇu-tattva expansion.
  • phaṇaiḥ — by His hoods
    Phaṇa (serpent hood) + instrumental plural. Ananta's many hoods extended over Vasudeva and the child like an umbrella, shielding from rain. Iconographic moment beloved in Vaiṣṇava painting — the rain, the swollen Yamunā, Vasudeva crossing, Ananta canopying.
By the influence of Yoga-māyā, all the doorkeepers fell fast asleep, their senses unable to work, and the other inhabitants of the house also fell deeply asleep. When the sun rises, the darkness automatically disappears; similarly, when Vasudeva appeared, the closed doors, which were strongly pinned with iron and locked with iron chains, opened automatically. Since the clouds in the sky were mildly thundering and showering, Ananta-nāga, an expansion of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, followed Vasudeva, beginning from the door, with hoods expanded to protect Vasudeva and the transcendental child.
Purport

Śeṣa-nāga is an expansion of the Supreme Personality of Godhead whose business is to serve the Lord with all necessary paraphernalia. When Vasudeva was carrying the child, Śeṣa-nāga came to serve the Lord and protect Him from the mild showers of rain.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
By the influence of Yogamāyā, all the doorkeepers fell fast asleep, their senses unable to work, and the other inhabitants of the house also fell deeply asleep. When the sun rises, the darkness automatically disappears; similarly, when Vasudeva appeared, the closed doors, which were strongly pinned with iron and locked with iron chains, opened automatically. Since the clouds in the sky were mildly thundering and showering, Ananta-nāga, an expansion of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, followed Vasudeva, beginning from the door, with hoods expanded to protect Vasudeva and the transcendental child.
Commentary

Yoga maya through her amsa covered the knowledge gathering senses of the door keepers, and they fell into deep sleep. The doors were insurmountable because of thick panels, huge bolds and chains. But they opened by themselves (svayam vyavaryanta), just like the sun driving away darkness. The clouds began to rumble softly (upamsu). Sesa spread his hood like an umbrella. Brahmanada Purana says: sayyasana paridhana paduka chatra camaraih kim nabhus tasya krsnasya murtibhedais ca murtisu (As Krsna took different forms, You served Krsna in the form of bed, seat, shoes, umbrella and camara.)

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Two verses are taken together. Pratyaya means awareness. Dvāḥsthāḥ means the guards at the doors. The guards lost all awareness with no senses functioning. Viṣṇu Purāṇa says: mohitāś cābhavaṁs tata rakṣiṇo yoga-nidrayā mathurā-dvāra-pālāś ca vrajatyānakadundūbhau The jail guards and the guards at the city gate were all bewildered by yoga-māyā when Vasudeva went out. The bewilderment was arranged by māyā whereas revelation took place by the Lord. That is described next. Suddenly all the locked doors opened. Atha means suddenly. When Vasudeva came to the doors and saw all the guards unconscious, the doors locked with chains and bolts automatically became loosened. It was not possible to break those locks since they had huge bolts. kṛṣir bhū-vācakaḥ śabdo ṇaś ca nirvṛtivācakaḥ | tayor aikyaṁ paraṁ brahma kṛṣṇa ity abhidhīyate || Kṛṣ means “existence” and ṇa means “bliss.” Combined together, the two roots indicate that Kṛṣṇa is the supreme form of God. Vasudeva was carrying Kṛṣṇa, the form attractive to all with the highest bliss. Thus Vasudeva had no fatigue in carrying him. Rather he felt the highest bliss. Kṛṣṇa also means he who takes away all bondage of saṁsāra. This was the cause of the doors becoming unbound. The verb varyavante is a reflexive form. The doors unlocked themselves. But Kṛṣṇa was the actual cause. Even before Kṛṣṇa came, the doors became unlocked just as the sun destroys darkness. The clouds rumbled gently. Ananta, a servant of the Lord, perfected his service at this time. ṣayyāsana-parīdhānapādukācchatra cāmaraiḥ kim nābhus tasya kṛṣnasya mūrti-bhedaiś ca mūrtiṣu Ananta serves different forms of Kṛṣṇa using different forms—as a bed, seat, cloth, shoes, umbrella and cāmara. Brahmānda Purāṇa Ananta followed behind Vasudeva. varṣatāṁ jaladānāṁ ca toyam atyulbaṇaṁ niśi saṁcchādyānuyayau śeṣaḥ phaṇair ānakadundūbhim While the clouds showered terrible amounts of water in the night, Ananta, covering Vasudeva with his hoods, followed him. Vasudeva noticed that the great amount of rain did not strike his body. Though he was agitated with worry, this protection caused astonishment and joy immediately.

Personal Guidance
When you carry Kṛṣṇa, locked doors open and Śeṣa shelters you — surrender draws unseen help that no effort of yours could arrange.
Practical Applications
  • Begin one task today by mentally placing it in Kṛṣṇa's service first, then act — and notice which 'locked doors' quietly open.
  • Refuse to credit your own cleverness for a breakthrough today; name aloud the help that came unbidden, as Vasudeva did.
  • When obstacles loom before a duty you know is right, take the next step anyway, trusting that yoga-māyā arranges what you cannot.
Reflection Questions
  • What 'iron-bolted doors' in my own life have I been straining to force open through willpower, when the verse suggests the real work is to carry Kṛṣṇa first and let the obstacles open of themselves?
  • Where am I still trying to be the protective hood over what is actually Kṛṣṇa's mission — playing Śeṣa's part out of ahaṅkāra rather than letting Him use me as the instrument?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam or interview, do your preparation as Vasudeva carried the child — fully, carefully, both arms — and then walk toward the locked door (submit, enter the room) without first demanding a guarantee that it will open; notice afterward, honestly, which 'doors' opened that you did not arrange.
  • Pick one friend-group situation this week where staying silent is the 'guarded gate' — a casual cruelty in a chat, a cheating shortcut, a pile-on about someone absent — and speak the one inconvenient true sentence; treat the social fear as the iron chain that only loosens once you actually step toward the door.
  • For seven days, post nothing on social media that you would not be willing to read aloud to a teacher you respect; let the unposted reels and stories be your small offering, and watch how much mental bandwidth Yoga-māyā quietly returns to you for sādhana and study.
householder
  • Identify the one family or career decision you have been postponing because the 'doors look locked' — a hard conversation with a spouse, a job change, a financial boundary with a relative — and this week take the first concrete step (the email, the appointment, the transfer) in the spirit of Vasudeva walking with the child, without pre-negotiating the outcome.
  • Be Śeṣa-nāga for someone in your household this week without announcement: hold the umbrella over a child's exam stress, a spouse's work crisis, or an aging parent's fear, by quietly absorbing one task they assume is theirs — and refuse, when asked later, to itemise what you did.
  • In your monthly budget review, mark one expense that exists purely to impress others (a subscription, an upgrade, a class for the kids that is really for the neighbours) and redirect that exact rupee amount to seva or to a fund for your children's spiritual education; let adambhitva, not appearances, set the line item.
senior
  • Choose one attachment you have been gripping with iron chains — a property, a grudge, a role in the family, a self-image as 'the one who decides' — and this month take a single visible step to loosen it (transfer a paper, write the letter of forgiveness, hand over the decision); treat the loosening itself as Yoga-māyā's work, not yours.
  • Identify one younger person — a grandchild, a junior colleague, a neighbour's child — and become their Śeṣa for this season: not by giving advice, but by quietly holding space (a weekly call, a standing invitation, a small fund) over which they can walk through their own locked doors; do not tell them you are doing it.
  • Each night before sleep, mentally rehearse SB 10.3.48-49 as your own death scene — the doorkeepers of the senses falling asleep, the locked doors of the body opening, the Lord being carried out — and ask Kṛṣṇa, by name, to be the One carried and the One carrying; let this, not estate planning, be the central preparation of your remaining years.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the auto-opening doors as a license for passivity — concluding 'if it's Kṛṣṇa's will, things will arrange themselves' and skipping Vasudeva's actual walk through the rain with the child in his arms.
  • Mistaking the showing-up of Yoga-māyā and Śeṣa as proof of one's personal spiritual advancement, when the verse shows arrangement is made for Kṛṣṇa's sake, not for the carrier's comfort or status.
  • Flattening Śeṣa-nāga into a poetic image or impersonal 'protective force' instead of honoring him as a distinct personal expansion of the Lord engaged in seva — drifting into the impersonalism the tradition explicitly rejects.
Discussion Prompts
  • Yoga-māyā puts the guards to sleep and unlocks the iron chains the moment Vasudeva steps forward — but only after he has picked up the child and walked toward the door. Where in your own life are you waiting for the doors to open before you start walking, and where have you actually seen them open only after you moved? Share an instance and let the group push back if it sounds like spiritual bypassing.
  • Śeṣa-nāga shows up unannounced, holds his hoods over the Lord and the carrier, and never asks for credit. In group seva or family life, who is the Śeṣa in your circle right now — and, harder question, where are you the one expecting the umbrella but unwilling to be one? Disagree honestly if someone romanticises silent service.
  • Vasudeva is in chains in a prison cell and still has more śraddhā than most of us in our comfortable rooms. Is faith easier in adversity or in ease? Defend your position with one concrete episode from your own life, and let the group challenge whether you are confusing faith with desperation, or comfort with complacency.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a hospital nurse on the night shift who has just been told by a senior doctor to falsify a medication log to cover a colleague's mistake — refusal could cost the job, the family's rent, and the kids' school fees. Student B is A's spouse, a sincere practitioner, woken at 2 a.m. by A's call. A is gripped by fear of the locked doors: legal, financial, social. B must respond not with platitudes ('Krishna will provide') and not with worldly calculation ('just do what's safest'), but in the spirit of SB 10.3.48-49 — speak to the duty in front of A (sva-dharma as a nurse, as a parent), point to where Yoga-māyā has already opened doors A is not noticing, and distinguish real compassion (for the patient, for A's own soul) from material compassion (for everyone's bodily comfort, including A's own). Run it for five minutes; then swap roles. Class debriefs: where did the counsel slide into impersonalist reassurance? Where did it slide into worldly pragmatism? Where did it actually point to Kṛṣṇa?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just a magical-doors story. Iron locks don't pop open and snakes don't grow protective hoods. Treating it as literal infantilises the audience; treating it as metaphor empties it of meaning. Either way the verse has nothing to teach a serious modern person.
    The Bhāgavatam is not asking you to stop being intelligent — it is asking you to stop being small. The verse reports that when the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself, in the form of the newborn child, is being carried, the laws of the prison reorganise around Him. That is not magic; that is hierarchy. Material nature is not the top of the chain — Yoga-māyā is, and she answers to Kṛṣṇa. You can refuse the report, but then you are also refusing the personalist claim of the whole tradition: that there is a Person behind the laws, not just laws. The practical teaching does not depend on whether you have ever seen a chain fall off. It depends on whether you have ever taken one step toward sva-dharma in fear and noticed that something you did not arrange went your way. Most of us have. The verse names that experience and tells us Whose hand it is.
  • Vasudeva's situation is dramatic and unique. My life is paying EMIs, raising teenagers, and managing a difficult boss. Comparing my Tuesday to a divine birth in a prison feels self-aggrandising and lets me off the hook from doing the boring work of just being responsible.
    Good — that suspicion is healthy, and it protects you from the cheap version of bhakti that turns every traffic jam into a 'test from Kṛṣṇa.' But notice what Vasudeva actually does in this verse: he does not levitate, he does not chant a mantra to break chains, he does not wait for a vision. He picks up the child and walks toward the door. The locked door is the boss, the EMI, the teenager. Your sva-dharma is exactly that boring work. The teaching is not that your Tuesday equals a divine birth; it is that the same Lord who arranged Vasudeva's exit is the Lord to whom your boring Tuesday can be offered. Adambhitva — non-hypocrisy — means you don't decorate your life with cosmic drama. You just carry the child you have been given, in the direction of duty, and let Yoga-māyā do her work without you having to narrate it.
Analogy

When the king passes through his palace, every lock opens, every guard sleeps, every gate swings wide — not because the king commanded but because the palace KNOWS its master. Kṛṣṇa, carried by Vasudeva, has the prison-palace recognize Him: doors yield, guards sleep, Ananta lifts His hoods overhead. The cosmos cannot help but escort its Person.

Stories
Ananta-Śeṣa as Lakṣmaṇa, Balarāma, Nityānanda

Ananta-Śeṣa, the thousand-hooded serpent, repeatedly appears as Bhagavān's intimate companion: as Lakṣmaṇa accompanying Rāma, as Balarāma elder-brother to Kṛṣṇa, and (Gauḍīya tradition adds) as Nityānanda Prabhu accompanying Caitanya. The same eternal associate, in service-form across yugas.

This verse shows that same Ananta extending His hoods over baby Kṛṣṇa and Vasudeva during the river-crossing, foreshadowing the lifelong companionship as Balarāma. The Vaiṣṇava world is populated by recurring associates serving Bhagavān across His descents.

Rāma's escort through Daṇḍakāraṇya

When Rāma walked through Daṇḍakāraṇya during the exile, the forest itself responded — sages emerged with prepared seats, animals followed, rivers offered passage. Daṇḍaka knew its Lord.

The pattern of this verse — doors opening, rivers parting, serpents canopying — is the same. Bhagavān's path is paved by His own nature. Material obstacles dissolve before Him, not by violence but by recognition.
