Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.40

गते मयि युवां लब्ध्वा वरं मत्सद‍ृशं सुतम् । ग्राम्यान् भोगानभुञ्जाथां युवां प्राप्तमनोरथौ ॥ ४० ॥

Important Words
  • gate mayi — when I had departed
    Gata (gone) + mayi (in Me — locative absolute). Locative absolute construction: 'When I had gone'. Bhagavān describes His own withdrawal after granting the benediction. He goes; the fruit ripens; He returns. Pattern of līlā: descent, dispatch, return.
  • mat-sadṛśam — exactly like Me
    Mat (Me) + sadṛśa (similar to). Their precise request. Verse 41 will reveal that no one is truly sadṛśa to Bhagavān except Himself — so He fulfilled the request by becoming their son in person. The literal answer was higher than the literal request.
  • grāmyān bhogān — ordinary worldly enjoyments
    Grāmya (village, worldly) + bhoga (enjoyment) + accusative plural. Sex life, family life, household enjoyments. The Bhāgavata does not romanticize this; it names it plainly. Yet because their orientation was mat-cittatva, the same grāmya-bhoga produced Bhagavān as fruit.
  • manorathau — the desire of their hearts (fulfilled)
    Manas (mind) + ratha (chariot) — literally 'the chariot of the mind', i.e., the heart's deepest movement. Dual: both husband and wife shared one manoratha. Gauḍīya principle: when both partners share kṛṣṇa-manoratha, the union itself becomes seva.
After you received that benediction and I disappeared, you engaged yourselves in sex to have a son like Me, and I fulfilled your desire.
Purport

According to the Sanskrit dictionary Amara-kośa, sex life is also called grāmya-dharma, material desire, but in spiritual life this grāmya-dharma, the material desire for sex, is not very much appreciated. If one has a tinge of attachment for the material enjoyments of eating, sleeping, mating and defending, one is not niṣkiñcana. But one really should be niṣkiñcana. Therefore, one should be free from the desire to beget a child like Kṛṣṇa by sexual enjoyment. This is indirectly hinted at in this verse.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
After you received that benediction and I disappeared, you engaged yourselves in sex to have a son like Me, and I fulfilled your desire.
Commentary

Desiring in this way for a son, you then engaged in sexual intercourse. Vyavyah means sex act according to the amara kosa.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

I arranged that you became slack in renunciation in order to fulfill my purpose. With joyful minds, you then collected material objects with a desire to be qualified for a wealthy son like me. You (yuvām) began enjoying objects previously enjoyed because you (yuvām) had a desire. The first yuvām can also mean yuvām mayi gate: when I left you. The word dattvā instead of labdhvā is seen in Śrīdhara’s commentary but that version is not seen anywhere. The meaning would be “having given the boon and departed.” The word varam indicates the means. Having attained the means of getting a son like me, you engaged in material life.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The benediction is described: having a son like me. Later you enjoyed material life because your desire was fulfilled. You had no fear at all. Or as ingredients for the birth of a son, you gathered items like oil, betel nut, bed, and cloth.

Personal Guidance
Even a desire as noble as wanting a son like Kṛṣṇa is still grāmya-dharma — the niṣkiñcana surrenders even sacred desires to Him.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today the one 'spiritual' desire you secretly use to justify material enjoyment, and offer it honestly to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse to measure your sādhana by what you receive — eat, sleep, and work today as a niṣkiñcana, claiming nothing as your own.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's names once today with no petition attached — not for a son, a result, or a benediction, only for Him.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I treating a benediction from Kṛṣṇa as license for grāmya-bhoga — using a spiritual gift to ratify a material craving I had not yet renounced?
  • What attachment to eating, sleeping, mating, or defending am I still rationalizing as 'natural,' and what would it cost me to recognize that even one such tinge keeps me from being niṣkiñcana?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the moments this week when 'I want to do something meaningful' quietly collapses into 'I want to look impressive on Instagram, get into the right friend group, or land the right internship' — that slide from sevā-language into grāmya-bhoga is the exact move SB 10.3.40 is naming.
  • Pick one form of viṣaya that runs your day — late-night scrolling, hookup culture talk in the group chat, snack-and-Netflix loops during exam stress — and practice one week of explicit ahaṅkāra-checking: 'Am I the body wanting this, or the soul?' Write the answer down each night.
  • Before you next say 'I want a relationship so I can grow spiritually together,' sit with the verse: even Devakī and Vasudeva's desire for a son like Kṛṣṇa is gently called grāmya-dharma. Ask honestly whether you want a partner, or want bhakti, and don't let the two collapse into one excuse.
householder
  • When you make the next big family decision — second child, bigger house, a new car, a school upgrade — write down the actual driver in one honest sentence before reaching for the devotional justification. SB 10.3.40 trains you to see that even 'for Kṛṣṇa' can be grāmya-bhoga in disguise.
  • Run a quarterly money-and-time audit with your spouse: what fraction of household resources actually flows toward sādhana, dāna, and serving Vaiṣṇavas, versus eating, sleeping, mating, defending? Don't aim for guilt — aim for accuracy, and shift one concrete percentage point this quarter.
  • Raise children inside gṛhastha-dharma without selling them the lie that family life is the spiritual summit. Tell them, in age-appropriate language, that mom and dad are also still working toward niṣkiñcana — model the direction, not just the role.
senior
  • Take inventory of the grāmya-bhoga you are still quietly defending in late life — the favorite food, the comfortable chair, the grandchild-attachment dressed as love — and pick one to consciously loosen this month as tapas, not as deprivation.
  • When mentoring younger devotees or family members, do not romanticize your own gṛhastha years as the high point. Tell the truth: the children, the house, the career were grāmya-bhoga sanctified by duty, and the real work was always bhakti — and still is.
  • Use the nearness of mortality as the verse uses Kṛṣṇa's disappearance: the benediction has been received, the household chapter is closing, what remains is direct surrender. Each evening, formally hand the day's remaining attachments to Kṛṣṇa before sleep.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as a sentimental story about Devakī and Vasudeva 'getting their wish,' and missing Prabhupāda's pointed warning that desiring even a Kṛṣṇa-like son through sex is still grāmya-dharma to be transcended.
  • Mistaking niṣkiñcana for outward poverty or ascetic theatrics, while quietly nursing the same household appetites — clinging to the fruits of bhoga while wearing the costume of vairāgya.
  • Twisting the verse into a blanket condemnation of family life or procreation, instead of hearing its real edge: that any tinge of attachment to material enjoyment, however refined or 'spiritualized,' disqualifies one from full surrender.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says even the desire to beget a son like Kṛṣṇa through sex life is not appreciated for one who would be niṣkiñcana. Share with your partner: where in your own life have you tried to baptize a grāmya-bhoga (eating, sleeping, mating, defending) by attaching a 'spiritual' purpose to it? Be specific. Push back if your partner's example sounds too clean.
  • Devakī and Vasudeva were given the supreme benediction — Kṛṣṇa Himself as son — yet the verse gently points out that the means was still grāmya-dharma. Discuss: does this verse imply that even sanctified family life is a concession, not the ideal? Does your group disagree on this? Where does the disagreement actually sit — on śāstra, or on personal stake?
  • The purport contrasts the niṣkiñcana with one who has 'a tinge of attachment' to bodily enjoyments. Most of us have far more than a tinge. Pair up and name one specific viṣaya you would not be willing to give up today even if a sādhu told you to. Then ask each other honestly: is that attachment serving bhakti, or being protected by bhakti-language?
Role Play

Two students play a married couple in their early thirties. They have one child. They are seriously practicing devotees and now considering a second child, and they tell themselves the reason is 'to raise another devotee for Kṛṣṇa.' A third student plays a senior Vaiṣṇava friend who has just heard them say this and gently challenges the framing — referencing SB 10.3.40 and Prabhupāda's note on grāmya-dharma. The couple must defend their reasoning honestly without hiding behind devotional vocabulary; the senior friend must distinguish between condemning gṛhastha life (which is not the point) and exposing self-deception about grāmya-bhoga dressed up as sevā. Run for eight minutes, then swap roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds anti-family and anti-sex. Devakī and Vasudeva were married, they had a child, and that child was Kṛṣṇa Himself — so why does Prabhupāda turn around and say even their desire was not 'very much appreciated'? It feels like the tradition can never let householders simply be householders.
    The verse and purport are not condemning Devakī and Vasudeva — Kṛṣṇa is in fact fulfilling their desire, and gṛhastha-āśrama is one of the four legitimate āśramas in varṇāśrama. What Prabhupāda is naming is a finer point: even the most sanctified material desire is still a material desire, and the niṣkiñcana stage lies beyond it. Householder life is not being rejected; the romanticization of householder life as the spiritual summit is being rejected. A Gauḍīya teacher upholds gṛhastha-dharma fully and at the same time refuses to let the student mistake grāmya-dharma for the goal. Both can be true.
  • The framing of sex as grāmya-dharma — 'village-dharma,' something low — is exactly the kind of body-shaming, sex-negative religion modern people are walking away from. Why should anyone today take this seriously?
    Grāmya-dharma is not a moral insult; it is a precise diagnostic term from the Amara-kośa. It names the level at which an activity functions — the level of bodily, common-to-all-creatures life (eating, sleeping, mating, defending). The Gītā's whole project is to ask: am I the body, or am I the soul situated in this body? If I am the soul, then organizing my entire life around bodily enjoyments — even with a partner I love, even with children I love — leaves the actual self un-fed. This is not shame; it is accuracy. A Gauḍīya teacher does not tell the student to hate the body or hate the spouse. The teacher tells the student: do not mistake the menu of grāmya-bhoga for the food of the soul, which is bhakti to Kṛṣṇa.
Analogy

An artisan, after receiving a master's promise, returns to his workshop and resumes the very work that always seemed mundane — but now every motion is filled with new meaning, because the master himself will inhabit the next piece. Pṛśni and Sutapā's grāmya-bhoga, after the boon, is artisanal: ordinary action saturated with divine purpose.

Stories
Prahlāda's birth from Hiraṇyakaśipu and Kayādhū

Prahlāda was born when Nārada had instructed his mother Kayādhū during pregnancy — the child in the womb absorbed bhakti directly. The mechanism: parental orientation and the bhāva at the time of conception shape the soul that arrives.

The Bhāgavata is here naming the same mechanism positively. Pṛśni and Sutapā's union, undertaken with mat-cittatva, prepared the field for Bhagavān Himself to arrive. The Gauḍīya line takes this seriously: sambandha matters in physical generation as much as in spiritual.
