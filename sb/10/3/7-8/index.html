Important Words
  • janārdane — the Supreme Person, Viṣṇu
    Locative of janārdana — He who 'urges' (ardana) the living beings (jana) toward Himself, or He who removes the distresses of His devotees. A name freighted with Vaiṣṇava intimacy: the Lord is not distant authority but the one who personally moves and rescues.
  • deva-rūpiṇyām — in her form of the same divine category
    Compound: deva-rūpiṇī — 'one whose form is divine'. Prabhupāda glosses this as ānanda-cinmaya-rasa-pratibhāvitā — Devakī is no ordinary mother; she is Bhagavān's eternal associate, aprākṛta in essence. The Gauḍīya frame: Kṛṣṇa's parents in every līlā are themselves Vaikuṇṭha residents in the appropriate role.
  • sarva-guhā-śayaḥ — the one who lies in every heart
    Sarva (all) + guhā (cave, heart-cavity) + śayaḥ (lying). The Paramātmā doctrine in three words. Gauḍīya reading: Bhagavān is simultaneously the Paramātmā in every heart AND the localized Person now visible to Vasudeva — acintya-bhedābheda live on the page.
  • induḥ iva — like the moon
    Iva — 'like' — but in Gauḍīya rasa-śāstra this is no idle metaphor. The moon, indu, is repeatedly Kṛṣṇa's image (mukha-candra, the lotus-faced moon). The simile already prepares the reader for the topmost rasa — the mādhurya of the moonlike face that will captivate Vraja.
  • tamaḥ-udbhūte — in the dense darkness
    Tamas (darkness, the lowest guṇa) + udbhūta (manifest, arisen). The verse deliberately frames Kṛṣṇa's appearance against maximal tamas — midnight, prison, Kaṁsa's terror. The pattern is doctrinal: bhakti's light shines brightest where saṁsāra's darkness is most concentrated.
The demigods and great saintly persons showered flowers in a joyous mood, and clouds gathered in the sky and very mildly thundered, making sounds like those of the ocean’s waves. Then the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, who is situated in the core of everyone’s heart, appeared from the heart of Devakī in the dense darkness of night, like the full moon rising on the eastern horizon, because Devakī was of the same category as Śrī Kṛṣṇa.
Purport

As stated in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.37) : ānanda-cinmaya-rasa-pratibhāvitābhis tābhir ya eva nija-rūpatayā kalābhiḥ goloka eva nivasaty akhilātma-bhūto govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi This verse indicates that Kṛṣṇa and His entourage are of the same spiritual potency ( ānanda-cinmaya-rasa ). Kṛṣṇa’s father, His mother, His friends the cowherd boys, and the cows are all expansions of Kṛṣṇa, as will be explained in the brahma-vimohana-līlā. When Brahmā took away Kṛṣṇa’s associates to test the supremacy of Lord Kṛṣṇa, the Lord expanded Himself again in the forms of the many cowherd boys and calves, all of whom, as Brahmā saw, were viṣṇu-mūrtis. Devakī is also an expansion of Kṛṣṇa, and therefore this verse says, devakyāṁ deva-rūpiṇyāṁ viṣṇuḥ sarva-guhā-śayaḥ. At the time for the Lord’s appearance, the great sages and the demigods, being pleased, began to shower flowers. At the seashore, there was the sound of mild waves, and above the sea there were clouds in the sky which began to thunder very pleasingly. When things were adjusted like this, Lord Viṣṇu, who is residing within the heart of every living entity, appeared in the darkness of night as the Supreme Personality of Godhead before Devakī, who appeared as one of the demigoddesses. The appearance of Lord Viṣṇu at that time could be compared to the rising of the full moon in the sky on the eastern horizon. The objection may be raised that since Lord Kṛṣṇa appeared on the eighth day of the waning moon, there could be no rising of the full moon. In answer to this it may be said that Lord Kṛṣṇa appeared in the dynasty which is in the hierarchy of the moon; therefore, although the moon was incomplete on that night, because of the Lord’s appearance in the dynasty wherein the moon is himself the original person, the moon was in an overjoyous condition, so by the grace of Kṛṣṇa he could appear as a full moon. To welcome the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the waning moon became a full moon in jubilation. Instead of deva-rūpiṇyām, some texts of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam clearly say viṣṇu-rūpiṇyām. In either case, the meaning is that Devakī has the same spiritual form as the Lord. The Lord is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha, and Devakī is also sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha. Therefore no one can find any fault in the way the Supreme Personality of Godhead, sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha, appeared from the womb of Devakī. Those who are not in full knowledge that the appearance and disappearance of the Lord are transcendental ( janma karma ca me divyam ) are sometimes surprised that the Supreme Personality of Godhead can take birth like an ordinary child. Actually, however, the Lord’s birth is never ordinary. The Supreme Personality of Godhead is already situated within the core of everyone’s heart as antaryāmī, the Supersoul. Thus because He was present in full potency in Devakī’s heart, He was also able to appear outside her body. One of the twelve great personalities is Bhīṣmadeva ( svayambhūr nāradaḥ śambhuḥ kumāraḥ kapilo manuḥ prahlāda, janako bhīṣmaḥ ). In Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.9.42) , Bhīṣma, a great authority to be followed by devotees, says that the Supreme Personality of Godhead is situated in the core of everyone’s heart, just as the sun may be on everyone’s head. Yet although the sun may be on the heads of millions and millions of people, this does not mean that the sun is variously situated. Similarly, because the Supreme Personality of Godhead has inconceivable potencies, He can be within everyone’s heart and yet not be situated variously. Ekatvam anupaśyataḥ ( Īśopaniṣad 7 ). The Lord is one, but He can appear in everyone’s heart by His inconceivable potency. Thus although the Lord was within the heart of Devakī, He appeared as her child. According to the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, therefore, as quoted in the Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī, the Lord appeared like the sun ( anugrahāsaya ). The Brahma-saṁhitā (5.35) confirms that the Lord is situated even within the atom ( aṇḍāntara-stha-paramāṇu-cayāntara-stham ). He is situated in Mathurā, in Vaikuṇṭha and in the core of the heart. Therefore one should clearly understand that He did not live like an ordinary child in the heart or the womb of Devakī. Nor did He appear like an ordinary human child, although He seemed to do so in order to bewilder asuras like Kaṁsa. The asuras wrongly think that Kṛṣṇa took birth like an ordinary child and passed away from this world like an ordinary man. Such asuric conceptions are rejected by persons in knowledge of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Ajo ’pi sann avyayātmā bhūtānām īśvaro ’pi san ( Bg. 4.6 ). As stated in Bhagavad-gītā, the Lord is aja, unborn, and He is the supreme controller of everything. Nonetheless, He appeared as the child of Devakī. This verse describes the inconceivable potency of the Lord, who appeared like the full moon. Understanding the special significance of the appearance of the Supreme Godhead, one should never regard Him as having taken birth like an ordinary child.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The demigods and great saintly persons showered flowers in a joyous mood, and clouds gathered in the sky and very mildly thundered, making sounds like those of the ocean's waves. Then the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, who is situated in the core of everyone's heart, appeared from the heart of Devakī in the dense darkness of night, like the full moon rising on the eastern horizon, because Devakī was of the same category as Śrī Kṛṣṇa.
Commentary

The clouds are described as anu sagaram (following the ocean). That means they imitated the roaring of the ocean. But previously it was stated that all directions were overjoyed, and the cloudless sky was filled with stars. So how could there be rumbling of clouds? Therefore it is described that deep in the night, everything become covered in darkness (tama udbhute). There was thunder at that time because thick clouds covered the sky after two praharas of night. The Lord is addressed as Janardana because he is being requested (ardana) by the devotees, munis and devatas (jana), "Oh Lord, now is the time for your birth.". When the time came for the Lord’s birth, from the womb of Devaki, whose eternal body was full of knowledge and bliss like the Lord’s ( deva rupinyam), appeared Visnu, who sleeps in the heart of all souls and in Mathura and Vaikuntha, invisible to the common man, like an inaccessible cave in mountain (sarva guhasayah). But he was not born with pain like a normal child. He appeared like the full moon rising in the eastern sky. The subject of the simile (moon) at that time began to act out the role. Though the moon was eight days waning at the time, the moon thought in bliss, "The Lord is ornamenting our dynasty of the moon with his birth in this world." Then it became round like the full moon. In this way also Visnu appeared in full form, along with all his amsas. According to the Hari Vamsa the birth took place in the eighth month of pregnancy, before the expected time, in order to bewilder Kamsa. In the Kha Manikya, an astrological work, the birth is described: The moon, mars, mercury and Saturn were exalted. The ascendent was Taurus. Jupiter was in pisces, sun in leo, venus in libra and rahu in scorpio. At midnight, on Wednesday with the moon in rohini naksatra, Lord Krsna appeared.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Verse seven is a continuation of the description at his birth. The word mudā is repeated in this verse from the previous verse. With joy the devatās and sages met together. The rumbling of clouds imitated the sounds of the ocean. Viṣṇu Purāṇa says sindhavo nija-śabdena vādyaṁ cakrur manoharam: the ocean produced attractive music with its roaring. When did all this take place? The last verse answers. It happened at midnight when it became completely dark. Tama-udbhūte means “becoming dark.” It was dark because it was the eighth day of the waning moon in Bhadrā month. The Lord’s effulgence comparable to the moon would defeat the darkness. This creates a literary ornament called adbhūta upameya. However since one does not seen the full moon rise at midnight the comparison is somewhat inapplicable. The cause of the drums sounding is given: the Lord was about to be born. Since Janārdana can mean “at the request of men,” he appeared because the devatās made their request. Kaṁsa and others were ignorant of all of this because of the influence of māyā who was simultaneously being born to Yaśodā in Gokula. There is another meaning of jāyamāne ’jane tasmin nedur dundubhayaḥ samam. When the moon entered the constellation of Rohiṇī, the constellation of Kṛṣṇa, who is not known (ajane) to be born at any other time, at the end of Dvāparā yuga, the drums sounded. Time became most auspicious, endowed with all good qualities of all the seasons. All this was accomplished by his śakti which can do the impossible when the Lord desires, or because it is his nature. Ajana-janmarkṣam (unknown birth constellation) also indicates the secret nature of his birth. This is for the purpose of showing the greatness of his birth (since a great person should not reveal his birth constellation.) The good qualities are then explained: the constellations were peaceful. Peaceful aspects are shown in the items down to the description of the minds of the devotees, excluding the description of the prosperity of the earth. Great auspiciousness is shown by the sounding of drums, the prosperity of the earth and the devatās showering flowers. The descriptions of the directions, rivers, forest and air also indicate auspiciousness. The qualities of spring and autumn seasons are depicted. Blossoming lotuses are features of the day, but on this occasion they bloomed at night. And sacrifices which are daytime activities are described as well. These qualities describe the situation in Satya-yuga. The clouds imitated the music of the ocean. This indicates that they were given consciousness by the Lord. The spring season spread in all directions. Since it was already stated in verse 2 that the sky was clear, there were no clouds. Viṣṇu Purāṇa says mandaṁ jagarjur jaladāḥ puṣpa-vṛṣṭi-muco dvijāḥ: the clouds rumbled softly and the sages released showers of flowers. Thus the clouds only appeared the moment that the moon entered Rohiṇī constellation. Devakī is described as having a form of sac-cid-ānanda like Bhagavān (deva-rūpiṇyām). Therefore there is not fault that the Lord appeared within her. Some versions have the words viṣṇu-rūpiṇyām instead. The Lord plays undisturbed (āśayaḥ) in his special places (guha) in all jīvas and in Vaikuṇṭha. These places are called guha (secret place or cave) because they are difficult to approach and difficult to understand. The Lord appeared complete with all his parts or expansions (puṣkalaḥ)-- with all his expansions such as those situated in the hearts of all beings as antaryāmī. The antaryāmīs appeared in the form of Kṛṣṇa in the hearts of great souls after Kṛṣṇa made his appearance. tam imam aham ajaṁ śarīra-bhājāṁ hṛdi hṛdi dhiṣṭhitam ātma-kalpitānām | pratidṛśam iva naikadhārkam ekaṁ samadhigato ’smi vidhūta-bheda-mohaḥ || Freed of the illusion of difference in the Lord’s various forms, I have attained the Lord who is one though appearing to be many like the sun seen by many people, who is the charioteer, but who is also in my heart, the unborn and is situated in the hearts of all the jīvas, who create their own bodies. SB 1.9.42 Even the rulers of Vaikuṇṭha appeared with Kṛṣṇa. This is described in Hari-vaṁśa in the story of Garuḍa after stealing the crown. There is also a description in the Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta. There is not fault in this. They all remain situated in Kṛṣṇa, hiding their respective powers which reveal their distinctive characters. Madhvācārya quotes Padma Purāṇa in this connection. sa devo bahudā bhūtvā nirguṇaḥ puruṣottamaḥ ekībhūya punaḥ śete nirdoṣo harir ādikṛt The Supreme Lord with no material qualities becomes many. Becoming one again the faultless Lord, the original creator, remains. Though the Lord appeared like the moon rising in the east, spreading light in all directions, it was explained that it was suitable that he appeared within Devakī since she was also sac-cid-ānanda. Viṣṇu Purāṇa says: tato ‘khila-jagat-padma-bodhāyācyuta-bhānunā devakī-pūrva-sandhyāyām āvirbhūtam mahātmanā The great Lord, the invincible sun appeared in the eastern direction in the evening within Devakī to awaken the lotus of the universe. To fool Kaṁsa, Kṛṣṇa appeared in the eight month of pregnancy. Hari-vaṁśa says: garbha-kāle tv asampūrṇe aṣṭame māsi te striyau devakī ca yaśodā ca suṣuvāte samaṁ tadā Without the pregnancy period being completed, in the eight month Devakī and Yaśodā gave birth at the same time.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Person already enthroned in every heart, appears outwardly when the inward chamber — like Devakī — is purified to receive Him.
Practical Applications
  • Begin the day by remembering that Kṛṣṇa is already seated in your heart as antaryāmī — speak to Him there before you speak to anyone else.
  • Identify one habit that keeps your inner 'Devakī' impure (a grudge, a screen-loop, a hidden indulgence) and refuse it once today as an offering of welcome.
  • At nightfall, pause for one minute in deliberate darkness and silence, and invite Kṛṣṇa to appear in your awareness as the rising moon of the heart.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still relating to Kṛṣṇa as an 'ordinary child' of history — admiring His story but not trusting that the same antaryāmī is in the core of my own heart right now?
  • What in me would have to become deva-rūpiṇī — of the same spiritual category — for me to actually receive Him, rather than merely think about Him?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam or audition, instead of the usual 'I've got this' self-talk, take 30 seconds to remember that Kṛṣṇa is already seated in the core of your heart as antaryāmī — you are not performing alone, and the outcome is not yours to manufacture.
  • On social media this week, notice every time you treat someone as 'just a body' — by their looks, their follower count, their aesthetic. Stop on three of those and silently acknowledge: this is also a soul in whose heart Kṛṣṇa is seated. Watch how it changes your scrolling.
  • When peer pressure hits — to drink, to hook up, to mock someone, to skip class — remember that the Lord did not live in Devakī's womb 'like an ordinary child.' You are also not ordinary. Your sva-dharma as a student is not negotiable based on who's in the room.
householder
  • Tonight, look at your spouse and your children with the deva-rūpiṇī lens — they are not just your responsibilities, they are sac-cid-ānanda souls Kṛṣṇa has placed in your care. Make one decision this week (a purchase, a discipline, a conversation) on the basis of their spiritual welfare, not just material comfort.
  • When career or money anxiety spikes, remember aṇḍāntara-stha-paramāṇu-cayāntara-stham — the Lord is already inside every atom of your situation, including your bank account and your boss's calendar. Perform your kartavya cleanly today and formally offer the day's results at your evening sandhyā or before sleep.
  • Identify one possession or status-marker you are quietly clinging to — the second car, the school district, the title — and practice aparigraha by giving something away this month (dāna), not symbolically but at a level you actually feel.
senior
  • Kṛṣṇa's appearance was not an ordinary birth, and your departure will not be an ordinary death — janma karma ca me divyam applies, by His grace, to His devotees too. Begin or deepen a daily practice (japa, Bhāgavata reading, kīrtana) explicitly aimed at remembering Him at the moment of leaving.
  • The waning moon became full to welcome Kṛṣṇa. In this season, where your own faculties may feel waning, ask: whom can I 'make full' by mentoring? Identify one younger person — a grandchild, a junior at the temple, a neighbor — and commit to a regular hour of transmission, not advice.
  • Walk through your home and name three attachments — a piece of furniture, a photo album, a savings instrument — and decide who they should go to and when. Practice loosening now, while you can do it as an offering rather than as a wrenching at the end. This is samatā in real time.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's appearance as mythology or symbolism — a 'beautiful story' — instead of a transcendental (divyam) event that reveals His inconceivable potency.
  • Sliding into impersonalism by emphasizing 'He is in everyone's heart' as if that were the whole truth, and quietly dropping the personal Kṛṣṇa who appeared from Devakī's womb.
  • Asuric reductionism — assuming He took birth and died like an ordinary man — which Prabhupāda explicitly identifies as the conception of those bewildered by His human-like pastimes.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse says Devakī was 'of the same spiritual category' as Kṛṣṇa — deva-rūpiṇī. Share with your partner: do you actually believe a soul can be elevated to the same sac-cid-ānanda nature as the Lord by surrender and service, or does that sound like inflation? Where does your skepticism live?
  • Prabhupāda insists the Lord 'did not live like an ordinary child in the womb of Devakī' — His birth is divyam, transcendental. Argue the opposite position to your partner: what would be lost, devotionally, if we conceded that Kṛṣṇa took birth like any other infant? Make the strongest case you can, then drop it.
  • The waning moon became full to welcome Kṛṣṇa. In your own family or community, name one moment where someone 'incomplete' (junior, untrained, low-status) was suddenly empowered because the Lord's work required it. Where in your own life are you waiting to be 'full' before you serve, instead of letting the service make you full?
Role Play

Two students sit across from each other. Student A is a college sophomore who has just told a campus skeptic friend (Student B) that Kṛṣṇa appeared from Devakī's heart 'like the full moon on the eastern horizon' on the eighth day of the waning moon. Student B, a science major, says: 'So your scripture can't even get the lunar phase right, and now you want me to believe a baby teleported out of someone's heart?' Student A must respond — not by retreating into 'it's symbolic,' and not by hand-waving — but by actually conveying what Prabhupāda explains: that Kṛṣṇa appeared in the candra-vaṁśa, that the moon himself rejoiced and appeared full, and that the Lord is aṇḍāntara-stha-paramāṇu-cayāntara-sthaḥ — situated even within the atom. After 4 minutes, swap roles. Then debrief: where did the defense get brittle? Where did it land?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole 'appeared from her heart, like the full moon' description sounds like myth-making after the fact — devotional poetry dressed up as event. Why should a serious modern reader treat it as anything but a beautiful story?
    A Gauḍīya teacher would not concede the false choice between 'literal ordinary event' and 'beautiful story.' The Bhāgavatam is reporting a transcendental event, divyam janma, witnessed and transmitted by paramparā — Vyāsa, Śuka, Sūta — and corroborated across the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Brahma-saṁhitā. The Lord is already antaryāmī in everyone's heart; for Him to manifest outwardly from Devakī's heart is not a violation of physics, it is the inconceivable potency (acintya-śakti) by which He is simultaneously within the atom and within Vaikuṇṭha. Asuras like Kaṁsa were meant to think He was an ordinary child — that bewilderment is part of the līlā. So the modern reader is in good company; the question is whether one wants to remain in Kaṁsa's category of perception or accept Bhīṣma's.
  • Saying Devakī is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha 'like the Lord' sounds dangerously close to the impersonalist claim that we're all God. How is this not just Advaita with extra steps?
    The distinction is precise and worth holding: deva-rūpiṇī / viṣṇu-rūpiṇī means Devakī shares the same spiritual category — eternal, conscious, blissful — but she is never identical with Kṛṣṇa. She is His eternal associate, an expansion of His internal potency, related to Him as mother. Advaita collapses the lover and the Beloved into one undifferentiated brahman; Gauḍīya siddhānta (acintya-bhedābheda) preserves the eternal distinction so that the rasa of vātsalya, sakhya, mādhurya can exist. Devakī's glory is precisely that she is His mother — not that she is Him. Krṣṇa's associates are of His potency; they are never Him. That is the whole difference between liberation as merging and liberation as eternal loving service.
Analogy

On the eastern horizon, the full moon does not announce itself — it simply rises, and the night that was dark is suddenly silvered. Kṛṣṇa's appearance in Devakī's chamber is exactly such a rising. The mother is the eastern sky; the son is the moon; the dense midnight is the world's worst hour, and the very darkness becomes the frame for His radiance.

Stories
Nṛsiṁha bursting from the pillar

Hiraṇyakaśipu, having terrorized the worlds, demanded Prahlāda show him this Viṣṇu. The boy pointed to a pillar. The Lord, who had always been present, manifested visibly — neither created nor moved, but suddenly seen.

The parallel to Devakī's chamber: Viṣṇu is sarva-guhā-śaya, already in every heart. His 'appearance' is not a new arrival but a self-disclosure to the eyes He chooses. The pillar in Hiraṇyakaśipu's hall and the womb of Devakī are both stages on which the eternal becomes visible.

Caitanya appearing during the eclipse

When Caitanya Mahāprabhu took birth in Navadvīpa, the moon was eclipsed and the whole town was chanting Hari's name along the Gaṅgā to lift the inauspicious shadow. Into that darkness, the golden Lord appeared.

Kṛṣṇa in midnight darkness; Gaurahari during a lunar eclipse; both descents redeem their dark hour. The Gauḍīya line reads the pattern as deliberate — Bhagavān comes precisely where the world is darkest, and His appearance is the answer to that darkness.
