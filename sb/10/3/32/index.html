Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.32

Important Words
  • pūrva-sarge — in a previous millennium
    Pūrva (previous) + sarga (creation, cosmic cycle) + locative. Bhagavān remembers multiple cosmic cycles — locating the present līlā within His larger narrative. Time is His; sarga is His unit; He speaks across it.
  • pṛśniḥ — (in your past birth, named) Pṛśni
    Devakī's previous name. The Bhāgavata is establishing that Bhagavān's parents are eternal associates who appear in different sargas with different names. Sambandha-jñāna: relationships precede this particular birth.
  • svāyambhuve — in Svāyambhuva (Manu's millennium)
    Svāyambhuva — the first manvantara. Bhāgavata cosmography names specific eons. The Gauḍīya line takes this as historical, not allegorical.
  • sati — O chaste one
    Vocative of satī — the chaste, the true, the faithful. The same root as the Sat in sac-cid-ānanda. Devakī is sat — her chastity is part of why she is qualified to be Bhagavān's mother. Address establishes the relational ground.
  • akalmaṣaḥ — spotlessly pious
    A- (privative) + kalmaṣa (sin, impurity). Sutapā (Vasudeva's previous incarnation) was pure. The Bhāgavata is naming the qualification of Bhagavān's father — not karma-grant but karma-free purity earned by tapas.
The Supreme Personality of Godhead replied: My dear mother, best of the chaste, in your previous birth, in the Svāyambhuva millennium, you were known as Pṛśni, and Vasudeva, who was the most pious Prajāpati, was named Sutapā.
Purport

The Supreme Personality of Godhead made it clear that Devakī had not become His mother only now; rather, she had been His mother previously also. Kṛṣṇa is eternal, and His selection of a father and mother from among His devotees takes place eternally. Previously also, Devakī had been the Lord’s mother and Vasudeva the Lord’s father, and they were named Pṛśni and Sutapā. When the Supreme Personality of Godhead appears, He accepts His eternal father and mother, and they accept Kṛṣṇa as their son. This pastime takes place eternally and is therefore called nitya-līlā. Thus there was no cause for surprise or ridicule. As confirmed by the Lord Himself in Bhagavad-gītā (4.9) : janma karma ca me divyam evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti so ’rjuna “One who knows the transcendental nature of My appearance and activities does not, upon leaving the body, take his birth again in this material world, but attains My eternal abode, O Arjuna.” One should try to understand the appearance and disappearance of the Supreme Personality of Godhead from Vedic authorities, not from imagination. One who follows his imaginations about the Supreme Personality of Godhead is condemned. avajānanti māṁ mūḍhā mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam paraṁ bhāvam ajānanto mama bhūta-maheśvaram ( Bg. 9.11 ) The Lord appears as the son of His devotee by His paraṁ bhāvam. The word bhāva refers to the stage of pure love, which has nothing to do with material transactions.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The Supreme Personality of Godhead replied: My dear mother, best of the chaste, in your previous birth, in the Svāyambhuva millennium, you were known as Pṛśni, and Vasudeva, who was the most pious Prajāpati, was named Sutapā.
Commentary

The Lord said, "O mother, not only in this birth, but in previous births I have appeared in your womb. Why are you thinking yourself so fallen? You are not an ordinary human." Thus in four verses he speaks. "During the reign of Svayambhuva you were (abhut) Prsni, O sati (Devaki). He (ayam) Vasudeva was Sutapa."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Overjoyed by their words, he consoles his parents and praises them by explaining the cause of his appearance. Out of special affection for his mother he speaks to Devakī. Sati can mean being present (in Svāyambhūva period) or can be a vocative to Devakī. O chaste woman (sati)! As a wife you were most chaste for many lives. Vasudeva was free of attachment and hatred. Thus both were highly qualified.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa eternally chooses His devotees as His parents — your relationship with Him is nitya-līlā, not a one-time event but a bond ripened over lifetimes of tapas.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one austerity (tapas) you can begin today — a fast, an early rising, a refused indulgence — offered specifically to deepen your relationship with Kṛṣṇa, not for self-improvement.
  • Refuse to imagine Kṛṣṇa's appearance and pastimes through your own speculation; today, read one passage about His birth from Prabhupāda's purport and accept it on Vedic authority.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa today as a person with a history with you — not as 'the Self' or 'consciousness' — and ask Him to reveal the eternal bond that precedes this birth.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I treating my relationships — especially with parents, children, or spouse — as merely material accidents, when this verse insists Kṛṣṇa Himself selects His eternal associates across yugas?
  • What austerity (tapas) am I unwilling to undertake now that Pṛśni and Sutapā undertook for ages to earn the privilege of receiving the Lord — and what does my unwillingness reveal about what I actually want?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a friendship from school or your hostel ends abruptly — a falling out, someone moving, a breakup — instead of declaring 'we were never really close,' sit with the possibility that the soul-connection isn't measured by this semester. Write the person one honest message acknowledging what you actually received from them; do not send it if it would be self-serving, but write it.
  • Notice this week how often social media frames identity as 'the version of you right now' — your current aesthetic, your current opinions, your current friend group. Once a day, before you open Instagram or TikTok, pause for thirty seconds and remind yourself: I am the same ātman who was a five-year-old and will be a sixty-year-old. Then open the app and see what shifts.
  • Pick one elder in your family — a grandparent, an uncle, a teacher — and ask them this week about something from before you were born. Listen as if you are meeting a soul whose journey began long before this body, because that is literally what is in front of you. Do not perform interest; actually be interested.
householder
  • Tonight when you see your child, look past the homework, the tantrum, the report card — and remember that this is an eternal soul who has come into your home as son or daughter in this birth. Adjust one specific thing you say to them this week so it speaks to that soul rather than to their behavior or performance.
  • Sit with your spouse and name one decision (a job change, a move, a financial choice) that you have been making purely on bodily-comfort logic. Re-examine it through the question Prabhupāda raises: does this serve the soul of everyone involved, or only the body? You do not have to reverse the decision — but do not pretend you weighed the soul if you did not.
  • The verse shows Kṛṣṇa choosing parents who underwent serious tapas. Audit your home for one small, sustainable austerity you can adopt as a couple — a shared fast day, a screen-free evening, a fixed time for reading the Bhāgavatam together. Make it doable enough that you will still be doing it in six months.
senior
  • Sutapā and Pṛśni's identity from a previous yuga is recovered and named by Kṛṣṇa Himself. As you approach the close of this body, stop curating the legacy of *this lifetime* — the achievements, the photo albums, the obituary draft — and ask instead what soul-work you are carrying forward. Spend an hour writing not your résumé but what your sādhana has actually become.
  • Identify one attachment you have been quietly preserving — a property, a position in a family dispute, a grudge, a possession — and begin loosening it this month. Not as performance of detachment for the relatives, but quietly, as Sutapā would have. Tell no one you are doing it.
  • Find one younger person — a grandchild, a neighbor, a junior at the temple — and offer them not advice but presence: read one verse with them weekly, and answer their questions about it without lecturing. Mentoring in this tradition is the transmission of bhāva across bodies; you have been receiving it for sixty years, and now is when you pass it on.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading nitya-līlā as poetic metaphor — softening 'eternal pastime' into 'a nice idea about reincarnation' and thereby missing that Kṛṣṇa's appearance, parents, and pastimes are literally transcendental and eternal.
  • Imagining one can casually 'become close to Kṛṣṇa' without the sustained tapas of a Sutapā — skipping the discipline (sādhana, vairāgya, austerity) that actually qualifies a soul for such an eternal relationship.
  • Speculating about past-life identities and previous births of oneself or others on the model of this verse, rather than receiving this truth from Vedic authority alone — Prabhupāda explicitly warns against imagination here.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kṛṣṇa tells Devakī she was His mother in a previous life as Pṛśni — meaning the relationship is nitya-līlā, eternal. Share with your partner: where in your own life do you treat a sacred relationship (with guru, with family, with Kṛṣṇa) as if it began at birth and ends at death? What changes if you sit with the possibility that it predates this body and continues past it?
  • Sutapā and Pṛśni are described here as having undergone severe tapas to earn Kṛṣṇa as their son. Yet Prabhupāda says the Lord appears 'by paraṁ bhāvam' — pure love, not material transaction. Debate in your group: was their austerity the *cause* of Kṛṣṇa coming, or merely the *occasion*? Where do you yourself secretly treat sādhana as a transaction?
  • Prabhupāda warns that 'one who follows his imaginations about the Supreme Personality of Godhead is condemned' and points us back to Vedic authorities. In an age that prizes personal interpretation, how do you draw the line between healthy reflection on the verse and impermissible imagination about the Lord's pastimes? Where have you crossed it?
Role Play

Two students play a pair of long-time friends meeting at a cafe. Friend A has just lost a parent and is grieving. Friend B has recently begun studying the Bhāgavatam and wants to share what this verse opened up for them — the eternity of the soul-relationship, that this parent-child bond is not severed by death the way bodies are severed. But Friend A is raw and not in a philosophical mood, and might hear it as cold or dismissive. Play it for five minutes. The challenge for Friend B: speak from real compassion (serving the soul) without bypassing the bodily grief, and without quoting verses as a shield. After, the class debriefs: where did Friend B slip into platitude? Where did real comfort actually land?

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like a fairy tale used to flatter Devakī — 'don't worry, you've been my mom before.' Why should a modern reader take literal past-life identities seriously rather than as pious narrative?
    Fair pushback — and the Gauḍīya line does not ask you to take it as poetic flattery. Kṛṣṇa Himself in Gītā 4.5 says, 'Many births of Mine have passed, and yours also, Arjuna; I know them all, you do not.' The eternity of the soul (BG 2.20) and the eternity of Kṛṣṇa's līlā are the same teaching applied in two directions. If the soul is eternal — which the Gītā argues on grounds you can actually examine in your own experience of continuous selfhood through childhood, youth, and age — then relationships of the soul are also not bounded by one body. The verse is not flattery; it is the personal corollary of an ontological claim. You can disagree with the ontology, but inside the ontology the past-life identification is not decorative.
  • Prabhupāda says Sutapā and Pṛśni performed severe austerities to obtain Kṛṣṇa as their son, and that the Lord appears by paraṁ bhāvam, pure love. Isn't this a contradiction — either it's earned by tapas or it's freely given by love?
    It looks like a contradiction only if you import a transactional frame. Tapas in this tradition is not a coin you pay; it is the gradual hollowing out of viṣaya-attachment so that bhāva — pure love — has room to surface. Sutapā and Pṛśni's austerity did not *purchase* Kṛṣṇa; it cleared away everything that wasn't longing for Him, until the longing itself was what He responded to. The same point Kṛṣṇa makes at BG 18.66 — surrender, not technique, is what He answers. Tapas without bhāva produces only a hardened ego (we have all met that person). Bhāva without tapas tends to be sentiment that the next pleasure dissolves. The two are not alternatives; tapas is the floor, bhāva is the room you build on it.
Analogy

A skilled goldsmith remembers the maker's stamp on every piece he has ever forged; presenting a new ornament, he begins, 'You and I have done this work before — this is not our first ornament together.' Kṛṣṇa, beginning His reply to Devakī, recalls Pṛśni and Sutapā — your previous incarnation, in this very work of being My parents.

Stories
Kṛṣṇa reminding Arjuna of past lives

In BG 4.5, Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna: 'Many births of Mine have passed, and yours also; I remember them all, you do not.' The pattern here is identical. Bhagavān remembers His descents and the eternal relationships of those who serve Him; the devotee's memory is veiled by jīva-condition.

The Gauḍīya line: Bhagavān's memory is the fixed record of every līlā; the bhakta's memory is being revealed back to her by His telling. This is sambandha-jñāna at work — knowing one's eternal relationship to Him through His own narration.
