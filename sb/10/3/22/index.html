Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.22

अयं त्वसभ्यस्तव जन्म नौ गृहे श्रुत्वाग्रजांस्ते न्यवधीत् सुरेश्वर । स तेऽवतारं पुरुषै: समर्पितं श्रुत्वाधुनैवाभिसरत्युदायुध: ॥ २२ ॥

Important Words
  • asabhyaḥ — uncivilized
    A- (privative) + sabhya (one who belongs to the sabhā — assembly of cultured persons). The Sanskrit is sharper than 'uncivilized' — Kaṁsa is one who has expelled himself from the company of those who recognize dharma. Prabhupāda's gloss: asura means uncivilized; sura means civilized. The categories are moral, not ethnic.
  • sura-īśvara — Lord of the suras
    Sura (the civilized, the devotee-class) + īśvara (Lord). Kṛṣṇa is the Lord specifically of those who recognize dharma. Bhagavān has a side; impartiality at the cosmic level does not mean equal regard for sura and asura at the practical level.
  • abhisarati — will come immediately
    Abhi (toward) + √sṛ (go, rush). Forceful verb — 'rushes toward'. In Sanskrit poetry abhisara is also the term for a tryst-bound heroine running to her beloved; the irony here is that Kaṁsa 'abhisaras' Kṛṣṇa with raised weapons, not with prema. Even hostile attention to Bhagavān is a kind of intense fixation.
  • udāyudhaḥ — with raised weapons
    Ud (up) + āyudha (weapon). The asura's posture before Bhagavān: armed and lifted-up. Contrast with Vasudeva's posture three verses earlier: nata-aṅgaḥ, body bowed. Bhagavān's appearance reveals the body-language of every heart.
  • agrajān te — Your elder brothers
    Agra-ja (first-born) + accusative plural + te (Your). Devakī's anguish: Kaṁsa already killed six of her sons. The verse carries her mother's grief into the appeal — she has the receipts of asura-violence and asks Kṛṣṇa to not be the seventh.
O my Lord, Lord of the demigods, after hearing the prophecy that You would take birth in our home and kill him, this uncivilized Kaṁsa killed so many of Your elder brothers. As soon as he hears from his lieutenants that You have appeared, he will immediately come with weapons to kill You.
Purport

Kaṁsa has here been described as asabhya, meaning “uncivilized” or “most heinous,” because he killed the many children of his sister. When he heard the prophecy that he would be killed by her eighth son, this uncivilized man, Kaṁsa, was immediately ready to kill his innocent sister on the occasion of her marriage. An uncivilized man can do anything for the satisfaction of his senses. He can kill children, he can kill cows, he can kill brāhmaṇas, he can kill old men; he has no mercy for anyone. According to the Vedic civilization, cows, women, children, old men and brāhmaṇas should be excused if they are at fault. But asuras, uncivilized men, do not care about that. At the present moment, the killing of cows and the killing of children is going on unrestrictedly, and therefore this civilization is not at all human, and those who are conducting this condemned civilization are uncivilized asuras. Such uncivilized men are not in favor of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement. As public officers, they declare without hesitation that the chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement is a nuisance, although Bhagavad-gītā clearly says, satataṁ kīrtayanto māṁ yatantaś ca dṛḍha-vratāḥ. According to this verse, it is the duty of the mahātmās to chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra and try to spread it all over the world to the best of their ability. Unfortunately, society is in such an uncivilized state that there are so-called mahātmās who are prepared to kill cows and children and stop the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement. Such uncivilized activities were actually demonstrated in opposition to the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement’s Bombay center, Hare Kṛṣṇa Land. As Kaṁsa was not expected to kill the beautiful child of Devakī and Vasudeva, the uncivilized society, although unhappy about the advancement of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement, cannot be expected to stop it. Yet we must face many difficulties in many different ways. Although Kṛṣṇa cannot be killed, Vasudeva, as the father of Kṛṣṇa, was trembling because in affection he thought that Kaṁsa would immediately come and kill his son. Similarly, although the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement and Kṛṣṇa are not different and no asuras can check it, we are afraid that at any moment the asuras can stop this movement in any part of the world.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O my Lord, Lord of the demigods, after hearing the prophecy that You would take birth in our home and kill him, this uncivilized Kaṁsa killed so many of Your elder brothers. As soon as he hears from his lieutenants that You have appeared, he will immediately come with weapons to kill You.
Commentary

"Though I know all your glories, I cannot cross the ocean of unintelligence. So now I will inform you all evil Kamsa." Anticipating that his son will say, "No father, he will not kill me because he will be overcome with the sweetness of my form," Vasudeva answers, "He is uncivilized, without taste for rasa. His servants will inform him of your birth and he will come here. I can see him come now. So hide your form. I am afraid of what will happen when he comes desiring to kill you."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The meaning is according to Śrīdhara Svāmī. Though you will kill the demons, bewildered by parental affection I give you this information. Or because he is the worst of the lot, and because we, your devotees are suffering so much, you should take action now and deal with him first. It is necessary because of his evil actions. Abhyahanat is sometimes seen instead of nyavadhīt. It means “crush on the stone.” Abhisarati though in the present tense, indicates action not yet completed. Uttering “O lord of the devatās” Vasudeva indicates that such activity of Kaṁsa is suitable for the demons who are enemies of the devatās, his devotees.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Those who are not devotees cannot understand your glories, though you directly appeared in this room to benefit the whole world. Previously Kaṁsa killed your elder brothers by throwing them on stone. Now he will come running with weapons in his hands. O lord of the devatās! Vasudeva indicates that such activity of Kaṁsa is suitable for the demons who are enemies of the devatās, his devotees. Therefore be alert.

Concepts
Purusha
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa cannot be killed and His movement cannot be stopped, yet asuric opposition is real — serve Him with steady courage anyway.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where fear of asuric opposition — ridicule, pushback, social cost — is making you soften your stand for Kṛṣṇa, and refuse to soften it.
  • Speak one clear word in defense of cows, children, brāhmaṇas, or the holy name when the moment arises, instead of staying conveniently silent.
  • Refuse to outsource your protection to your own cleverness — chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra deliberately today as Vasudeva trembled yet trusted Kṛṣṇa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life have I been quietly behaving like Kaṁsa — sacrificing innocents (people, principles, time, integrity) for the sake of my own sense-gratification or self-preservation, and what 'prophecy' am I trying to outrun?
  • Vasudeva trembled in affection even though Kṛṣṇa cannot be killed — where am I confusing protective fear for devotion, and what does it ask of me to act with steady duty rather than anxious affection when the asuric forces seem to close in?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a group chat or social feed turns into a pile-on against a classmate — even one who 'deserves' it — refuse to add the next message. That moment of crowd-cruelty is the asabhya pattern in miniature; protecting the defenseless starts with the chat you're actually in today.
  • Pick the one person in your year who is routinely talked over — the quiet kid, the foreign student, the unfashionable one — and have one real conversation with them this week. Vedic kṣamā says the weak are excused even when at fault; in your context, that means actively making space for the voiceless before they ask.
  • When you're afraid your faith looks weird on campus (chanting, japa beads, prasāda, no dating, no drinking), notice the trembling — that's Vasudeva's affection, not weakness. Don't hide it and don't perform it; just keep one daily practice visible without apology, and let Kṛṣṇa handle the social fallout.
householder
  • Audit one recurring household expense or career choice this week through the asabhya/sabhya lens: is this money or time funding cruelty somewhere upstream — factory dairy, exploitative labor, content that degrades children — and what is one substitution you can actually sustain? Authenticity (adambhitva) means the kitchen and the bank statement match the altar.
  • When you feel the Vasudeva-trembling for your children's future — their school, their friends, the culture coming for them — convert anxiety into one concrete protection this month: a family kīrtana evening, a weekly reading, a clear screen-time boundary. Effort is yours; outcomes are Kṛṣṇa's.
  • In a workplace conflict where someone genuinely is behaving asuric, practice nyāya without ahaṅkāra: name the behavior precisely, once, in the right forum — then drop it. No corridor gossip, no moral superiority at home. Righteous judgment is surgical, not a lifestyle.
senior
  • Identify one 'Kaṁsa' you've been carrying for decades — a person, an institution, a wound — and formally release them in your daily sit this week. At this stage, vairāgya is not theoretical; unfinished resentment is the heaviest thing you'll try to carry across the threshold.
  • Mentor one younger devotee or family member specifically on how to act with udyama while trembling — share an actual story from your life where you served Kṛṣṇa scared. Your lived non-anxiety in the face of obstruction is teaching the next generation can't get from books.
  • Quietly arrange your affairs — will, possessions, unfinished apologies — as Vasudeva arranged the transfer of the child: with care, without drama, trusting the Lord with the part you cannot control. Legacy in the Gauḍīya line is not what you leave behind; it is what you have already handed to Him.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'asabhya' as a label for distant historical villains (Kaṁsa, ancient tyrants) rather than recognizing the same asuric pattern in one's own willingness to harm cows, children, elders, or brāhmaṇas — directly or by participating in systems that do — for the sake of personal comfort.
  • Treating Vasudeva's trembling as a failure of faith and concluding that a real devotee should feel no fear — when in fact the verse honors the natural affection of a parent and shows that fearful affection and steady surrender can coexist; the misstep is using 'spiritual bypass' to suppress legitimate concern instead of channeling it into protective action.
  • Spiritualizing the verse into pure allegory and missing its concrete civilizational claim — that protecting cows, women, children, elders and brāhmaṇas is the mark of human civilization — and thereby excusing oneself from any practical, costly stand against the asuric currents of one's own time and place.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva trembles for Kṛṣṇa even though Kṛṣṇa cannot be killed — Prabhupāda links this to our own fear that asuras can stop the saṅkīrtana movement. Where in your own seva does affection slide into anxiety, and is that anxiety a form of bhakti or a subtle lack of faith? Push back on each other — don't let the group settle on a tidy answer.
  • Prabhupāda calls a society that kills cows and children 'uncivilized' and names its leaders asuras. In your own circle, naming someone an asura feels harsh, even un-Vaiṣṇava. When is such naming nyāya (righteous judgment) and when is it our own ahaṅkāra dressed up as discrimination? Bring a real example you actually disagree about.
  • Vedic civilization says cows, women, children, elders, and brāhmaṇas should be excused even when at fault. Modern instinct says equal accountability for all. Argue the Vaiṣṇava position to a partner who genuinely doesn't accept it — and notice where you yourself flinch.
Role Play

Two students sit across from each other. Student A is a devotee who has just learned that the city council in their town is moving to ban public Hare Kṛṣṇa saṅkīrtana in parks, calling it a 'public nuisance' — exactly the language Prabhupāda flags in the purport. Student B is a thoughtful, secular neighbor and friend who broadly likes the devotees personally but agrees with the council: 'It's loud, it's religious, it doesn't belong in a public space.' Student A must (1) not retreat into platitudes, (2) not demonize Student B, (3) speak from the verse — Kaṁsa-like obstruction is expected, the movement cannot ultimately be checked, yet Vasudeva-like care and effort are still required. After 5 minutes, swap roles. Then debrief: where did Student A drift into either appeasement or aggression, and what would steady udyama in Kṛṣṇa's service have actually looked like?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling people who disagree with you 'uncivilized asuras' is exactly the kind of religious othering that causes violence. Prabhupāda's purport sounds like the rhetoric of every fundamentalism.
    Notice what Prabhupāda actually marks as asuric: the killing of cows, children, elders, brāhmaṇas — the deliberate harming of those who cannot defend themselves. He is not naming people asura because they hold a different view; he is naming a specific civilizational pattern by its fruits. The Gauḍīya line is rigorously personalist — every jīva is part-and-parcel of Kṛṣṇa, including Kaṁsa, whom Kṛṣṇa Himself liberates. Real karuṇā is serving the soul, not flattering the ahaṅkāra. Refusing to name cruelty as cruelty, in the name of niceness, is the material compassion the Gītā explicitly pivots away from. The check on this is not silence but humility: we name the pattern, not the eternal worth of the person.
  • If Kṛṣṇa cannot be killed and the movement cannot ultimately be stopped, why did Vasudeva tremble — and why should we exert ourselves at all? Isn't the whole purport just contradiction?
    This is the heart of the verse. Vasudeva trembles in vātsalya — paternal affection — not in philosophical doubt. He knows tattva and yet he feels. Prabhupāda is showing us that bhakti is not a flatline of detached certainty; it includes legitimate care for what we love, expressed as effort. Karma-yoga (BG 2.47) gives us the form: the right to action, never to its fruits. We act with full udyama to protect Kṛṣṇa's movement, His devotees, His cows — and we surrender the outcome to Him, knowing He is never actually in danger. Vairāgya is not the absence of effort; it is the absence of proprietorship over results. Trembling Vasudeva who still hides the child is the model — not the cool spectator who does nothing because 'Kṛṣṇa will handle it.'
Analogy

A wolf who has already eaten the lambs hears that the shepherd has arrived in the field — his ears prick, his teeth bare, he runs not away but TOWARD the meadow with bared fangs. Kaṁsa is exactly this wolf. The news of Kṛṣṇa's birth will not cause him to repent; it will draw him sword-in-hand to the cradle. Asurā nature does not soften before Bhagavān; it sharpens.

Stories
Hiraṇyakaśipu hearing of Prahlāda's devotion

When Hiraṇyakaśipu heard that his own son Prahlāda was a devotee of Viṣṇu, his response was not curiosity but escalating attempts to kill the boy — poison, fire, the elephants, the cliff. SB 7 chronicles each attempt and each rescue.

The asura-mind, hearing news of bhakti or of Bhagavān, does not soften — it organizes violence. Devakī's anxiety in this verse is not paranoid; it is realistic. The Bhāgavata-narrative pattern is consistent: asuras hear and arm themselves.

Rāvaṇa hearing of Rāma's approach

When word reached Laṅkā that Rāma had crossed the ocean with the vānara army, Rāvaṇa did not consider returning Sītā. His court split; Vibhīṣaṇa urged surrender, was banished, and joined Rāma. Rāvaṇa armed himself, even knowing the auguries against him.

The pattern repeats: bhakti and avatāra polarize. Some surrender (Vibhīṣaṇa); the rest harden (Rāvaṇa, Kaṁsa). Bhagavān's appearance is never neutral data; it sorts the world.
