Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.44

Important Words
  • prāk-janma — previous births
    Prāk (former, eastern, prior) + janma. Bhagavān's prior 'births' — the appearances as Pṛśnigarbha and Vāmana. Note: He uses janma colloquially though He is ajan-māne, unborn. Even Bhagavān adopts the language of birth to communicate intimately.
  • smaraṇāya — for the purpose of remembering
    Smaraṇa (remembrance, smṛti) + dative. The purpose of the form-display is smaraṇa. Smaraṇaṁ is the third of the nine limbs of bhakti — remembering Kṛṣṇa. Bhagavān deliberately triggers smaraṇa in Devakī by showing the Viṣṇu-form.
  • martya-liṅgena — with the mark of a mortal
    Martya (mortal) + liṅga (mark, sign, form) + instrumental. The 'mark' of mortality — ordinary infant body. The word liṅga here means distinguishing sign, not gender; Bhagavān is wearing the disguise of mortality.
  • jñānam — this knowledge
    Jñāna — knowledge, specifically the recognition of who He is. Without the four-armed display, this specific jñāna would not have arisen. Gauḍīya distinction: vaidha-bhakti needs jñāna of who Bhagavān is; rāga-bhakti in Vraja proceeds without explicit jñāna — both paths are valid, and this is the vaidha-aiśvarya-mixed branch.
I have shown you this form of Viṣṇu just to remind you of My previous births. Otherwise, if I appeared like an ordinary human child, you would not believe that the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, has indeed appeared.
Purport

Devakī did not need to be reminded that the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Viṣṇu, had appeared as her son; she already accepted this. Nonetheless, she was anxious, thinking that if her neighbors heard that Viṣṇu had appeared as her son, none of them would believe it. Therefore she wanted Lord Viṣṇu to transform Himself into a human child. On the other hand, the Supreme Lord was also anxious, thinking that if He appeared as an ordinary child, she would not believe that Lord Viṣṇu had appeared. Such are the dealings between devotees and the Lord. The Lord deals with His devotees exactly like a human being, but this does not mean that the Lord is one of the human beings, for this is the conclusion of nondevotees ( avajānanti māṁ mūḍhā mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam ). Devotees know the Supreme Personality of Godhead under any circumstances. This is the difference between a devotee and a nondevotee. The Lord says, man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ namaskuru: “Engage your mind always in thinking of Me, become My devotee, offer obeisances and worship Me.” A nondevotee cannot believe that simply by thinking of one person, one can achieve liberation from this material world and go back home, back to Godhead. But this is a fact. The Lord comes as a human being, and if one becomes attached to the Lord on the platform of loving service, one’s promotion to the transcendental world is assured.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
I have shown you this form of Viṣṇu just to remind you of My previous births. Otherwise, if I appeared like an ordinary human child, you would not believe that the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, has indeed appeared.
Commentary

I have appeared in the four armed form because you would not recognize me as God if I appeared in my complete, original, two armed human-like form.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Bhava means “that in which something exists” or in other words an object. There would be no discernment (jṣānam) of me as the object of your previous worship. Instead of jāyate sometimes janyate is seen. Or bhava can mean the form with four arms shown so that they would remember the Lord’s three appearances to fulfill the boon. This indicates that this form is not the principle form. My form is Svayam Bhagavān, parambrahma with human form. My birth (bhava) would not be recognized by another method (anyathā), by appearing with human characteristics and two arms as the secret parabrahman in a human form. Though it is my chief form with either two or four arms, I did not show it first. That this is the chief form is shown in the following verses. Brahmā speaks the following: adyaiva tvad ṛte ’sya kiṁ mama na te māyātvam ādarśitam eko ’si prathamaṁ tato vraja-suhṛd-vatsāḥ samastā api tāvanto ’si catur-bhujās tad akhilaiḥ sākaṁ mayopāsitās tāvanty eva jaganty abhūs tad amitaṁ brahmādvayaṁ śiṣyate Have you not shown me today that both you yourself and everything within this creation are manifestations of your inconceivable potency? First you appeared alone, and then you manifested yourself as all of Vṛndāvana’s calves and cowherd boys, your friends. Next you appeared as an equal number of four-handed Viṣṇu forms, who were worshiped by all living beings, including me, and after that you appeared as an equal number of complete universes. Finally, you have now returned to your unlimited form as the Supreme Absolute Truth, one without a second. SB 10.14.18 Śukadeva speaks: na cāntar na bahir yasya na pūrvaṁ nāpi cāparam pūrvāparaṁ bahiś cāntar jagato yo jagac ca yaḥ The Supreme Lord has no beginning and no end, no exterior and no interior, no front and no rear. In other words, he is all-pervading. Because He is not under the influence of the element of time, for him there is no difference between past, present and future; he exists in his own transcendental form at all times. SB 10.9.13 Śukadeva speaks, with realization of Kṛṣṇa’s sweetness: gopyas tapaḥ kim acaran yad amuṣya rūpaṁ lāvaṇya-sāram asamordhvam ananya-siddham dṛgbhiḥ pibanty anusavābhinavaṁ durāpam ekānta-dhāma yaśasaḥ śrīya aiśvarasya What austerities must the gopīs have performed! With their eyes they always drink the nectar of Kṛṣṇa’s form, which is the essence of loveliness and is not to be equaled or surpassed. That loveliness is the only abode of beauty, fame and opulence. It is self-perfect, ever fresh and extremely rare. SB 10.44.14 The women of Mathurā speak: yan martya-līlaupayikaṁ sva-yoga- māyā-balaṁ darśayatā gṛhītam vismāpanaṁ svasya ca saubhagarddheḥ paraṁ padaṁ bhūṣaṇa-bhūṣaṇāṅgam Kṛṣṇa possesses that form suitable for human pastimes to show the full capacity of his yoga-māyā. That form astonishes even the lord of Vaikuṇṭha. It is the pinnacle of auspicious qualities and enhances the beauty of his ornaments. SB 3.2.12 Uddhava also makes similar statements.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

You would not understand my birth (mad-bhavam). Citsukha explains ahaṁ jāto ‘smīti jṣānaṁ manuṣya-rūpeṇa na syāj janmataḥ: knowledge that I have been born would not arise if I had a human form from birth.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa reveals His Viṣṇu form to confirm He is the Supreme Person, not an ordinary child — devotees recognize Him under every disguise.
Practical Applications
  • When the mind whispers 'Kṛṣṇa is just a charming village boy,' refuse the demotion and remember: this is the same Lord who showed Devakī His four-armed Viṣṇu form.
  • Notice today where you avajānanti — quietly downgrade the Lord into a symbol, an idea, or 'one path among many' — and correct that drift in your next round of japa.
  • Speak about Kṛṣṇa to one person today as a Person, not a principle, holding the conviction Devakī held: my Lord has personally come.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my sādhana am I quietly demanding that Kṛṣṇa show me a four-armed Viṣṇu form before I will trust Him — refusing to recognize Him in the 'ordinary human child' shape He has actually given me (the holy name, the Deity, the words of the ācārya)?
  • When Kṛṣṇa's dealings in my life feel too human, too coincidental, too unspectacular to be Him — what is the avajānanti māṁ mūḍhā reflex inside me that needs the spectacular before it will bow?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When you post or share something about your sādhana online and get one mocking comment, notice the pull to delete it or soften the next post. That pull is the same fear Devakī had about her neighbors. Choose one place this week — a class discussion, a chat, a story — to keep your conviction visible without arguing about it.
  • In your friend group there is probably one person everyone has quietly reduced to a stereotype ('the religious one,' 'the intense one'). You have done to them what nondevotees do to Kṛṣṇa — flattened a person into a category because of how they appear. Spend one real conversation this week looking past the label.
  • Before an exam or a high-stakes audition, you are tempted to hide that you pray, chant, or offer your work — because peers will think it is a crutch. Do it anyway, privately if needed. Kṛṣṇa showed the four-armed form so the connection to His earlier appearances would not be lost; you offer your effort so the connection to Him is not lost in the pressure.
householder
  • Your children watch how seriously you treat the deity, the altar, the Bhāgavatam — and they will calibrate their śraddhā to yours. Do not domesticate Kṛṣṇa into 'just a nice family tradition' for their comfort. Let one practice in the home this month be unmistakably reverent — full ārati, full prostration, full attention — so they see Viṣṇu, not just a baby.
  • In a work or money decision this week, you will be tempted to present your reasoning in purely secular terms to a spouse, partner, or colleague because you fear the devotional reasoning will not land. Try the harder thing: name the actual reason. 'I am not taking this contract because the work conflicts with my sādhana' — said plainly, not apologetically.
  • When relatives reduce your guru, your tradition, or Kṛṣṇa to 'one path among many' at the dinner table, you usually let it slide for peace. That is the avajānanti mistake happening at your table. You don't need to argue — but do not nod along either. Silence that looks like agreement teaches your children the wrong thing.
senior
  • You have lived through enough bodies-within-this-body — child, youth, parent, professional — to know experientially that the self has continued through each. Use this verse's prāg-janma-smaraṇa principle to do the inverse: trace the continuity backward through THIS life as preparation for trusting it across the next transition.
  • Your grandchildren and younger relatives will receive their first impression of Kṛṣṇa from how you, the elder, speak of Him. Do not collapse Him into vague 'God talk' to seem broad-minded. Speak of Him personally — by name, with specifics from līlā you actually know — so the next generation inherits a Person, not an abstraction.
  • As physical capacity narrows, the temptation is to let sādhana also narrow into something private and sentimental. Resist. Keep one practice publicly visible in the household — chanting aloud, offering food, daily Bhāgavatam reading — so that when you depart, those around you remember not a quiet old devotee but a clear witness to Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating the four-armed Viṣṇu darśana as the 'real' Kṛṣṇa and the human-like form as a lesser concession — inverting the verse, which actually shows that the human-like dealings are the intimate norm and the opulent form is the reminder given for our weak faith.
  • Concluding from 'the Lord deals like a human being' that Kṛṣṇa is therefore one of us — a great soul, an avatar among avatars, a symbol — instead of holding both: fully approachable and fully the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
  • Waiting for a personal four-armed vision before committing to man-manā bhava mad-bhakto, as if surrender required prior proof — when the verse teaches that the proof was given once for the devotee's sake and the practice of remembrance must now proceed on śraddhā, not on spectacle.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī asks Kṛṣṇa to hide His four-armed form because she fears the neighbors will not believe. Where in your own life have you softened or hidden your devotional convictions because you feared others would not believe? Was that protective wisdom, or was it a quiet betrayal of śraddhā? Push back on each other — don't let your partner off easy.
  • Prabhupāda says nondevotees, seeing the Lord act like a human, conclude He IS one of the human beings (avajānanti māṁ mūḍhāḥ). Discuss a concrete moment when YOU made that mistake — reduced Kṛṣṇa, the guru, or a sādhu to merely human because of how they appeared. What did it cost you? Disagree if you think this 'reduction' is sometimes appropriate humility.
  • The verse turns on a paradox: without the four-armed darśana, mad-bhavaṁ jñānaṁ (knowledge of My nature) does not arise through ordinary human appearance. Yet today we have no such darśana — only books, deity, and the holy name. Argue with each other: is our situation harder than Devakī's, or easier? Where do you actually disagree?
Role Play

Two students play the scene immediately after this verse. Student A is Devakī, who has just been shown the four-armed Viṣṇu form and asked Kṛṣṇa to become an ordinary child. A neighbor (Student B) walks in the next morning and asks, 'I heard something strange happened in your room last night — were you chanting too loudly again?' Devakī must respond: she knows what she saw, but she also knows the neighbor will dismiss any literal account. Does she tell the truth and accept ridicule? Does she deflect to protect the secret? Does she try to plant a seed without revealing everything? Run the scene twice, swapping roles, then debrief: where in your own life are you 'Devakī the morning after' — holding a conviction nobody around you will accept?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole verse is just retroactive mythology — the storytellers needed to explain why Kṛṣṇa looked like a normal baby in every other story, so they invented a 'He showed His Viṣṇu form first, then became a child' transition. It is a narrative patch, not a revelation.
    Notice what the objection assumes: that the text is a human composition trying to be internally consistent. The Gauḍīya line receives Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam as Vyāsa's mature revelation, the ripened fruit of the Vedic tree, spoken by Śuka to Parīkṣit at the moment of his death — not a stitched-together folk tradition. But set aside the source-claim for a moment and look at the verse's own logic: Kṛṣṇa Himself names the problem the objector is raising. He says, in effect, 'If I had appeared as an ordinary child, you would not believe.' The verse anticipates the very skepticism the objector now voices about the verse. That is not the structure of a patch; that is the structure of a teaching that knows its audience. Sit with that before dismissing it.
  • Why does an all-powerful God need to convince His own mother by a special effects display? If Devakī is truly His devotee, faith should be enough — the four-armed form is a crutch, and the verse exposes a God who is insecure about being recognized.
    Read the verse alongside the purport. Prabhupāda points out that BOTH parties were anxious — Devakī about the neighbors, Kṛṣṇa about her. This is not insecurity; this is the dealings between devotees and the Lord, where the Lord voluntarily participates in the mood of reciprocation. Kṛṣṇa is not proving Himself; He is meeting Devakī in her vātsalya-bhāva, where she relates to Him as a mother. A mother needs to remember her son across births — that is the concern of the verse (prāg-janma-smaraṇāya). The 'crutch' framing imports a transactional, prove-it-to-me model of faith. The Gauḍīya understanding is that the Lord's appearances and concealments are līlā — loving exchange, not evidence-gathering. The objection mistakes intimacy for inadequacy.
Analogy

A teacher reveals to her returning students the full diploma she will award them at the end of the course — not to keep it on display, but so they will trust the curriculum that follows. Bhagavān showed the four-armed Viṣṇu-form to Devakī precisely so she would believe what came next; otherwise, the ordinary infant in her arms could not be recognized as Lord.

Stories
Kṛṣṇa showing the Viśvarūpa to Arjuna

In BG 11, Kṛṣṇa showed Arjuna the cosmic form — but only briefly, then returned to His two-armed Pārtha-sārathi form, the one Arjuna preferred. The display was for credentialing; the relationship was sustained through the ordinary form.

Devakī's situation is exactly parallel. The four-armed Viṣṇu-form was the credential; the human-child form is the relationship. Aiśvarya establishes who; mādhurya/vātsalya sustains the love. The Bhāgavatam knows this rhythm by heart.
