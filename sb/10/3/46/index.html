Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.46

Important Words
  • tūṣṇīm — silent
    Adverb — silently, quietly. The pause after instruction. In Vaiṣṇava narrative, Bhagavān's silences are themselves significant — the space in which He transitions between modes of self-revelation. Tūṣṇīm here is the seam between aiśvarya-darśana and vātsalya-līlā.
  • hariḥ — Hari, the remover (of sins, hearts)
    Hari — from √hṛ (to take away). One of Bhagavān's principal names. He takes away inauspiciousness, takes away the hearts of those who see Him. In Caitanya tradition Hari-nāma is the yuga-dharma. Subtle: this is the moment Hari WITHDRAWS His own aiśvarya — turning the verb back on Himself.
  • ātma-māyayā — by His own internal energy
    Ātmā (own) + māyā. The same yoga-māyā / svarūpa-śakti that arranges all līlā. The transformation from aiśvarya-form to infant is not material; it is svarūpa-śakti's work. Distinguish from the bahiraṅga-māyā that deludes the jīva.
  • prākṛtaḥ-śiśuḥ — an ordinary-seeming child
    Prākṛta (natural, ordinary, of prakṛti) + śiśu (infant). He appears prākṛta — but Prabhupāda emphasizes that He is ALWAYS aprākṛta in essence. The 'prākṛta-ness' is concession to līlā-frame, not change in svarūpa. Crucial against Māyāvāda misreading: His ordinary form is not less than His extraordinary form; both are full Bhagavān.
Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: After thus instructing His father and mother, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, remained silent. In their presence, by His internal energy, He then transformed Himself into a small human child. [In other words, He transformed Himself into His original form: kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam.]
Purport

As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (4.6) , sambhavāmy ātma-māyayā: whatever is done by the Supreme Personality of Godhead is done by His spiritual energy; nothing is forced upon Him by the material energy. This is the difference between the Lord and an ordinary living being. The Vedas say: parāsya śaktir vividhaiva śrūyate svābhāvikī jñāna-bala-kriyā ca ( Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 6.8) It is natural for the Lord to be untinged by material qualities, and because everything is perfectly present in His spiritual energy, as soon as He desires something, it is immediately done. The Lord is not a prākṛta-śiśu, a child of this world, but by His personal energy He appeared like one. Ordinary people may have difficulty accepting the supreme controller, God, as a human being because they forget that He can do everything by spiritual energy ( ātma-māyayā ). Nonbelievers say, “How can the supreme controller descend as an ordinary being?” This sort of thinking is materialistic. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī says that unless we accept the energy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead as inconceivable, beyond the conception of our words and mind, we cannot understand the Supreme Lord. Those who doubt that the Supreme Personality of Godhead can come as a human being and turn Himself into a human child are fools who think that Kṛṣṇa’s body is material, that He is born and that He therefore also dies. In Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Third Canto, Fourth Chapter, verses 28 and 29, there is a description of Kṛṣṇa’s leaving His body. Mahārāja Parīkṣit inquired from Śukadeva Gosvāmī, “When all the members of the Yadu dynasty met their end, Kṛṣṇa also put an end to Himself, and the only member of the family who remained alive was Uddhava. How was this possible?” Śukadeva Gosvāmī answered that Kṛṣṇa, by His own energy, destroyed the entire family and then thought of making His own body disappear. In this connection, Śukadeva Gosvāmī described how the Lord gave up His body. But this was not the destruction of Kṛṣṇa’s body; rather, it was the disappearance of the Supreme Lord by His personal energy. Actually, the Lord does not give up His body, which is eternal, but as He can change His body from the form of Viṣṇu to that of an ordinary human child, He can change His body to any form He likes. This does not mean that He gives up His body. By spiritual energy, the Lord can appear in a body made of wood or stone. He can change His body into anything because everything is His energy ( parāsya śaktir vividhaiva śrūyate ). As clearly said in Bhagavad-gītā (7.4) , bhinnā prakṛtir aṣṭadhā: the material elements are separated energies of the Supreme Lord. If He transforms Himself into the arcā-mūrti, the worshipable Deity, which we see as stone or wood, He is still Kṛṣṇa. Therefore the śāstra warns, arcye viṣṇau śilā-dhīr guruṣu nara-matiḥ. One who thinks that the worshipable Deity in the temple is made of wood or stone, one who sees a Vaiṣṇava guru as an ordinary human being, or one who materially conceives of a Vaiṣṇava as belonging to a particular caste is nārakī, a resident of hell. The Supreme Personality of Godhead can appear before us in many forms, as He likes, but we must know the true facts: janma karma ca me divyam evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ ( Bg. 4.9 ). By following the instructions of sādhu, guru and śāstra — the saintly persons, the spiritual master and the authoritative scriptures — one can understand Kṛṣṇa, and then one makes his life successful by returning home, back to Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: After thus instructing His father and mother, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, remained silent. In their presence, by His internal energy, He then transformed Himself into a small human child. [In other words, He transformed Himself into His original form: kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam [SB 1.3.28].
Commentary

According to the Maha Samhita, atma mayaya means "by ones own free will." Babhuva prakrtah sisuh here means that he became, not a material baby, but his eternally perfect form as a baby. Prakrta means prakrti, svarupa and svabhava. In referring to great souls on earth, they are called prakrti siddha. Thus the word here means svabhava or svarupa. It cannot mean material in this context because there are statements like the following which contradict this. Na cantar na bahir yasya... in this statement it is clear that He who mother Yasoda bound up was like a material (prakrta mata) child, but actually he wasn’t prakrta, he was aprakrta, spiritual. Therefore the Lord showed his original spiritual form as a human-like baby.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Then what happened? After saying this (iti), preparing to withdraw (hari—to take away) and hide his four armed form, the Lord became silent. While his parents watched, without others being given this opportunity, Bhagavān, endowed with six qualities, by his own śakti of knowledge (ātmā-māyayā), his śakti of excellent intelligence, he became a normal child. According to Trikāṇḍa-śeṣa, māyā means illusion or intelligence. Nirghaṇṭu says māyā means knowledge. Or it can mean desire according to Mahā-saṁhitā. ātmā-māyā tad icchā syat guṇa-māyā jaḍātmikā: māyā means desire or matter made of guṇas, without consciousness. According to Viśva-koṣa, māyā means deceit or mercy. Thus by my desire or by my mercy I manifested the state of a baby. The word prākrṭaḥ means “as if material” by taking a secondary interpretation. Or prākrṭaḥ can mean natural, not something newly created. Prakrti can mean svarūpa or svabhāva. The usage is found in a sentence like prakṛti-siddham idaṁ hi mahātma: the great soul has a perfected nature. Thus the Lord manifested the form of a baby who was svabhāva-siddha, perfect by his very nature. In the statement babandha prākrtaṁ yatha: Yaśodā bound Kṛṣṇa as if he were an ordinary child (SB 10.9.14) Kṛṣṇa is shown to be non-material by comparing him to a material child. Kṛṣṇa, revealing his desired pastimes, is always the abode of all śaktis. By those śaktis he shows pastimes desired by his devotees. To accomplish this, they should all be qualified. Thus they are all svabhāva-siddha. Garga will say bahūni santi nāmāni rūpāṇi ca sutasya te: for this son of yours there are many forms and names. (SB 10.8.15)

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

By his special śakti made of eternity, knowledge and bliss equal to himself (ātma-māyayā) or by his mercy, he became a baby--someone having a baby nature (prākṛtaḥ) or having an attractive form with two arms, newly born (śiśuḥ). He was attractive to the mind (hariḥ) and most beautiful (bhagavān). His weapons disappeared and he had an umbilical cord. Otherwise the people of Gokula would be astonished and Nanda would not have given charity to counteract the impurity. He became a baby quickly, while his parents looked on. Or he showed the most astonishing power of his māyā, and thus the parents stared. Or by seeing that event with their vision, they saw everything with affection. With their other vision they had seen the four armed form. Arjuna and Nārada see the four armed form. That is seen in the Gītā when Kṛṣṇa reveals the universal form. Or the two watched, anticipating the baby form. Some persons say that Kṛṣṇa was first born with two arms. To make his parents understand about his birth he then displayed four arms. This form is thus called astonishing (adbhūtam). Later he again returned to his natural form as a baby as when he was born.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa is never a prākṛta-śiśu — He chooses His form by ātma-māyā; honor Him as svayaṁ bhagavān, not a material child.
Practical Applications
  • Today, when you bow before the arcā-mūrti, refuse the thought 'this is stone or wood' — address Kṛṣṇa directly, by name, as the same Person who became the child of Vasudeva and Devakī.
  • Notice one moment when you reduce Kṛṣṇa, guru, or a Vaiṣṇava to their material covering, and immediately correct the vision by recalling janma karma ca me divyam.
  • Spend five minutes meditating on ātma-māyayā — that whatever Kṛṣṇa does flows from His own spiritual energy — and offer one task today with that same posture of inner, not forced, action.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life do I quietly insist that Kṛṣṇa must conform to my human categories — and what would it cost my pride to admit His ātma-māyā is beyond my mind's reach?
  • When I bow before the arcā-mūrti or hear of Kṛṣṇa as a small child in Vasudeva's arms, what part of me secretly thinks 'this is only stone' or 'only a baby' — and how does that doubt show up in how I treat guru and Vaiṣṇavas?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice how often you judge a classmate or professor by their 'packaging' — the brand they wear, the accent they speak in, the Instagram they post. That is exactly the materialist error the purport names: śilā-dhīr in social form. Today, pick one person you have written off by appearance and have a real conversation with them.
  • Before exams or interviews, you will be tempted to think 'I am the doer; my prep determines the result.' SB 10.3.46 shows even Kṛṣṇa's appearance as a child is by ātma-māyā, not material cause. Offer your study session at the start: 'Kṛṣṇa, the energy to do this is Yours.' Then study harder than before — not less.
  • On social media you constantly perform a 'self' that is not quite you. The Lord can take any form by His own energy and remain Himself; you take one form and lose yourself. This week, post nothing for three days, and write down what you wanted to perform and why. Bring that to your next class discussion.
householder
  • Your children watch how you treat the Deity at home and how you treat the pūjārī, the guru, and visiting Vaiṣṇavas. They are learning the arcye viṣṇau śilā-dhīr lesson silently from you. This week, do one act of Deity service or guru-sevā where your child is present and can ask questions afterward.
  • In family financial decisions, you are constantly tempted to reduce people to their material category — this in-law's caste, that colleague's salary bracket, this neighbor's community. Catch yourself once this week mid-calculation, and consciously re-see that person as an eternal soul whom Kṛṣṇa is also reaching toward. Then make the decision again.
  • When your spouse or aging parent's body changes — illness, weight, memory, energy — you feel the pull to identify them with that body. Remember Kṛṣṇa changing form by ātma-māyā while remaining Himself: the soul of your loved one is unchanged. Sit with them today for fifteen minutes with no agenda, looking past the body.
senior
  • You are watching your own body change in ways you cannot reverse. The Lord changes form at will and is never diminished; you change form unwillingly and feel diminished. The difference is ownership, not change itself. Spend time daily with the verse janma karma ca me divyam — let the contrast of His divya birth with your forced one sharpen your vairāgya rather than your self-pity.
  • Mentor a younger practitioner this season specifically on this point: Kṛṣṇa is a person, not an impersonal Absolute, and the arcā-vigraha is Him, not a symbol of Him. You have decades of hearing this; the youth around you are surrounded by impersonalist drift. Your testimony, not your argument, is what will land.
  • As you loosen attachments to property, position, and even family role, do not loosen attachment to sādhu, guru, and śāstra — those three are precisely how the Lord is staying close to you in this stage. Renew one of them this month: re-read a Prabhupāda purport you marked years ago, write a letter to your guru, or visit a Vaiṣṇava you have not seen in too long.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's appearance as a 'prākṛta-śiśu' as though He underwent a real material transformation — collapsing ātma-māyā (His spiritual energy) into mahā-māyā (the material energy), and thereby reducing Him to one more conditioned being.
  • Demanding that the Lord's pastimes be intellectually 'explainable' before granting them faith — forgetting Jīva Gosvāmī's warning that without accepting acintya-śakti, the Supreme cannot be understood at all.
  • Applying the verse's lesson abstractly ('God can do anything') while still committing the very offense the purport names — seeing the Deity as wood or stone, the guru as an ordinary man, or a Vaiṣṇava through caste-vision — and missing that this is the nārakī mentality the verse is meant to dissolve.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kṛṣṇa transforms Himself into a 'prākṛta-śiśu' by ātma-māyā, yet Prabhupāda insists His body is never material. In your own words, where is the line between 'appears as' and 'is'? Share with your partner one place in your own life where you confuse the appearance of something spiritual with its substance — and where your partner might disagree with how you drew that line.
  • The purport says one who sees the arcā-mūrti as stone, or the guru as an ordinary man, is nārakī — a resident of hell. That is severe language. Discuss honestly: have you ever caught yourself making one of those three calculations (Deity-as-stone, guru-as-man, Vaiṣṇava-as-caste)? What pulled you back, or what kept you stuck? Push your partner if their answer sounds too clean.
  • Jīva Gosvāmī says we cannot understand the Lord unless we accept His energy as acintya — inconceivable. Modern education trains us to refuse anything inconceivable. Where is the actual point of friction for you: is it intellectual pride, fear of being fooled, or something else? Disagree productively if your partner names a different friction than yours.
Role Play

Two students sit in a temple room before the arcā-mūrti. Student A has just brought a college friend, Student B, to darśana for the first time. Walking out, Student B says, 'Look, I respect your tradition, but that's a marble statue. You bow, you offer food, you dress it — it's beautiful theater, but you and I both know it's stone.' The teacher assigns Student A to respond — not by quoting verses at Student B, but by drawing on SB 10.3.46: Kṛṣṇa, by ātma-māyā, became what looked like an ordinary human child in front of Vasudeva and Devakī; the same personal energy makes Him present in arcā-vigraha. Student A must speak as a fellow practitioner, not a debater, and must address the warning 'arcye viṣṇau śilā-dhīr… nārakī saḥ' without sounding like a threat. After three minutes, switch roles. Then debrief: where did the conversation actually shift, and where did it stay stuck?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just mythology dressing up an obvious contradiction. If Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme controller, why does He need to 'transform' into a child at all? The whole 'ātma-māyā' move sounds like a theological loophole to protect the doctrine from the awkward fact that He looks like an ordinary baby.
    The verse is not patching a contradiction; it is naming the very point a materialist will miss. An ordinary being is forced into a body by karma — Kṛṣṇa enters a form by His own will, sambhavāmy ātma-māyayā. That is the whole difference. Calling it a 'loophole' presumes the default state is materiality and divinity is the exception that needs explaining. The Vedic position is the opposite: spiritual energy is primary, matter is its separated expression (bhinnā prakṛtir aṣṭadhā). What looks like a contradiction to us is only the strain of fitting an acintya reality into our small categories. Jīva Gosvāmī's whole point: drop the demand that everything be conceivable to your mind, and the 'contradiction' dissolves.
  • Even granting the philosophy, the warning that someone who sees the Deity as stone is 'a resident of hell' is exactly the kind of fear-based religion modern people are walking away from. Can't we keep the beautiful parts and lose the threats?
    Hear what the śāstra is actually doing. It is not threatening — it is diagnosing. If you genuinely treat the arcā-mūrti as stone, the guru as an ordinary man, and a Vaiṣṇava as a caste-member, you have already cut yourself off from the only channels through which Kṛṣṇa is reaching toward you in this age. That self-inflicted cut is the 'hell' — not a punishment imposed from outside, but the natural consequence of refusing the medicine. A doctor who says 'if you keep drinking, your liver will fail' is not being fear-based; he is being honest. Soften the language and you also soften the seriousness of what is at stake — your own return home, back to Godhead. The Gauḍīya teacher does not deliver this warning to scare; he delivers it because he loves you enough not to flatter you.
Analogy

A king who has shown his royal robes to convince a witness then steps behind a screen and reappears in traveler's clothes — same person, different presentation. Kṛṣṇa, having instructed His parents, falls silent and becomes the ordinary infant. Aiśvarya was displayed for sambandha; mādhurya now begins for līlā.

Stories
Kṛṣṇa's transition into Vraja-līlā

From this verse forward — the descent into ordinary infanthood — the entire rest of Canto 10 unfolds. The Vraja-līlā that begins in chapter 8 (Yaśodā's vātsalya, the butter-thefts, the killing of demons sent by Kaṁsa, the rāsa-līlā) all begins in the silence of this moment.

The Gauḍīya line treasures verse 46 as the entry-point to mādhurya-leaning līlā. Bhagavān folds away the catuḥ-bhuja credential and chooses dvi-bhuja sweetness. From here it is the cowherd boy's story, and the Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana would unpack every moment that follows.
