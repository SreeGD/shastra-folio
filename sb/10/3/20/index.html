Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.20

स त्वं त्रिलोकस्थितये स्वमायया बिभर्षि शुक्लं खलु वर्णमात्मन: । सर्गाय रक्तं रजसोपबृंहितं कृष्णं च वणन तमसा जनात्यये ॥ २० ॥

Important Words
  • tri-loka-sthitaye — for the maintenance of the three worlds
    Tri (three) + loka (world) + sthiti (maintenance) + dative. The threefold cosmos — bhūr, bhuvaḥ, svaḥ — is held in being by Viṣṇu specifically. The white-Viṣṇu form is the sustaining function. Pure personalist cosmology: maintenance is the work of a Person.
  • sva-māyayā — by His own (internal) energy
    Sva (own) + māyā. Important distinction: this is NOT the māyā that deludes the jīva (which is bahiraṅga-māyā) but Bhagavān's own (svarūpa-śakti / yoga-māyā). The trimūrti forms are projections of His internal energy for cosmic governance — līlā, not delusion.
  • śuklam — the white (form)
    Śukla — white, pure. The color of sattva, of Viṣṇu, of dharma in its purest expression. Iconographically the white form of Viṣṇu maintains; in Gauḍīya theology this whiteness is the satya-yuga manifestation, distinct from but issuing from the original Kṛṣṇa-svarūpa.
  • raktam — the reddish (form)
    Rakta — red, the color of rajas. Brahmā's reddish complexion corresponds to the creative function in the mode of passion. Creation requires activity, drive, multiplication — all rajasic. Bhagavān assumes the color of the function He performs.
  • kṛṣṇam ca — and the dark (form)
    Kṛṣṇa here means 'dark-colored' — Śiva's complexion at the time of annihilation, the color of tamas. The same syllables that name svayaṁ bhagavān also name the destructive function's color, but the Gauḍīya gloss is firm: Kṛṣṇa-the-name belongs primarily to the all-attractive Person; the kṛṣṇa-varṇa of annihilation is a different reference.
My Lord, Your form is transcendental to the three material modes, yet for the maintenance of the three worlds, You assume the white color of Viṣṇu in goodness; for creation, which is surrounded by the quality of passion, You appear reddish; and at the end, when there is a need for annihilation, which is surrounded by ignorance, You appear blackish.
Purport

Vasudeva prayed to the Lord, “You are called śuklam. Śuklam, or ‘whiteness,’ is the symbolic representation of the Absolute Truth because it is unaffected by the material qualities. Lord Brahmā is called rakta, or red, because Brahmā represents the qualities of passion for creation. Darkness is entrusted to Lord Śiva because he annihilates the cosmos. The creation, annihilation and maintenance of this cosmic manifestation are conducted by Your potencies, yet You are always unaffected by those qualities.” As confirmed in the Vedas, harir hi nirguṇaḥ sākṣāt: the Supreme Personality of Godhead is always free from all material qualities. It is also said that the qualities of passion and ignorance are nonexistent in the person of the Supreme Lord. In this verse, the three colors mentioned — śukla, rakta and kṛṣṇa — are not to be understood literally, in terms of what we experience with our senses, but rather as representatives of sattva-guṇa, rajo-guṇa and tamo-guṇa. After all, sometimes we see that a duck is white, although it is in tamo-guṇa, the mode of ignorance. Illustrating the logic called bakāndha-nyāya, the duck is such a fool that it runs after the testicles of a bull, thinking them to be a hanging fish that can be taken when it drops. Thus the duck is always in darkness. Vyāsadeva, however, the compiler of the Vedic literature, is blackish, but this does not mean that he is in tamo-guṇa; rather, he is in the highest position of sattva-guṇa, beyond the material modes of nature. Sometimes these colors ( śukla-raktas tathā pītaḥ ) are used to designate the brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. Lord Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu is celebrated as possessing a blackish color, Lord Śiva is whitish, and Lord Brahmā is reddish, but according to Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī in the Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī-ṭīkā, this exhibition of colors is not what is referred to here. The real understanding of śukla, rakta and kṛṣṇa is as follows. The Lord is always transcendental, but for the sake of creation He assumes the color rakta as Lord Brahmā. Again, sometimes the Lord becomes angry. As He says in Bhagavad-gītā (16.19) : tān ahaṁ dviṣataḥ krūrān saṁsāreṣu narādhamān kṣipāmy ajasram aśubhān āsurīṣv eva yoniṣu “Those who are envious and mischievous, who are the lowest among men, are cast by Me into the ocean of material existence, into various demoniac species of life.” To destroy the demons, the Lord becomes angry, and therefore He assumes the form of Lord Śiva. In summary, the Supreme Personality of Godhead is always beyond the material qualities, and we should not be misled into thinking otherwise simply because of sense perception. One must understand the position of the Lord through the authorities, or mahājanas. As stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.3.28) , ete cāṁśa-kalāḥ puṁsaḥ kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
My Lord, Your form is transcendental to the three material modes, yet for the maintenance of the three worlds, You assume the white color of Viṣṇu in goodness; for creation, which is surrounded by the quality of passion, You appear reddish; and at the end, when there is a need for annihilation, which is surrounded by ignorance, You appear blackish.
Commentary

Concerning this there is a question. It is well known that the creation takes place through Brahma. Then, how are you also the creator? Brahma and others are your forms alone. That is the intention of this verse. You, by your svarupa (svamayaya), manifest yourself as pure (sukla) Visnu. Here sukla cannot mean white, because Visnu is black. At the time of destruction of all people you are black in color and for creation in the mode of passion you are red. But we cannot say that Visnu is nourished or influenced by sattva guna and therefore described as white. Visnu does not have connection with sattva guna as Brahma and Siva have with the other gunas. Visnu is close to sattva but does not touch it. In 10.88.55 it is said: Harir hi nirgunah saksat, sattvadayo na santise and sruti says. saksi cetah kevalo nirgunas ca

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“Why do you say I am the cause and destruction? These actions are done by Brahmā and Śiva.” The verse indicates that he does not speak contrary. Viṣṇu is white. This means he is pure, since he is beyond the guṇas, and merely assists sattva by closeness. With your form (varṇam) as Viṣṇu you appear in the world for maintaining it by your mercy (sva-māyayā). You appear as Brahmā full of rajas for creating, with the color red, indicating passion to nourish the world. You appear as Śiva full of tamas with anger indicated by the color black. Black does not indicate his form. Colors seen by the eye cannot determine the guṇas. Baka and other demons, full of tamas, were white. Vyāsa, Śukadeva and others, full of sattva, were blackish in complexion. The colors are also used to denote the varṇas like brāhmaṇa. Kṣīrodakaśāyi Viṣṇu the guṇa avatāra of sattva is blackish. Śiva is white. Their expansions are of various colors. Thus, though Brahmā is described as red bodied, that is not the meaning here. Viṣṇu is beyond the guṇas. He is not in the guṇas like others. harir hi nirguṇaḥ sākṣāt puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ Viṣṇu is beyond the guṇas, he is completely beyond matter. SB 10.88.3 śivaḥ śakti-yutaḥ śaśvat tri-liṅgo guṇa-saṁvṛtaḥ vaikārikas taijasaś ca tāmasaś cety ahaṁ tridhā Śiva is always united with his personal energy, the material nature. Manifesting himself in three features in response to the entreaties of nature’s three modes, he thus embodies the threefold principle of material ego in goodness, passion and ignorance. SB 10.88.5 Thus Viṣṇu cannot be said to be covered by sattva-guṇa as the other two are covered by rajas and tamas. Viṣṇu appears with the desire to protect. Even though it said that they all are not affected by transformation of the guṇas, since it is explained that the goal is to destroy the tamas in people (if Śiva is in tamas, how could he destroy it?) Viṣṇu has a form of sac-cid-ānanda at all times. Otherwise there would be contradiction to thousands of other of statements concerning Viṣṇu and his devotees.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“Why do you say I am the cause of creation and destruction? These actions are done by Brahmā and Śiva.” You accept sattva. This means that you accept to be the presiding deity of sattva, because the deity and the guṇa are considered non-diffferent. Or since you are without contradiction, or are the cause of creation etc. (saḥ), you are generally have a white form (varṇam) as the manvantara avatāras like Yajṇa. By your mercy (māyayā) though you are by nature the protector, you accept these forms. Since protection is your nature, maintenance is mentioned first. The Lord is distinguished from these forms as the source or avatārī by the word saḥ. You accept a dark blue and reddish form (kṛṣṇam) for destruction of the universe (janātyaye). Creation, maintenance and destruction arise from you.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa stays untouched by the guṇas even while wielding them as creation, maintenance, and destruction — fix your eye on the Person, not the colors.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today which guṇa is coloring your mood — rajas-restlessness, tamas-dullness, sattva-clarity — and refuse to mistake that color for your real self or for Kṛṣṇa.
  • When you meet someone whose surface (caste, complexion, role, temperament) tempts a quick judgment, pause and address the eternal soul behind the varṇa, as Vasudeva addressed the Lord beyond His colors.
  • Before sleep, hand back the day's three movements — what you built, what you sustained, what ended — to Kṛṣṇa, acknowledging He alone is the unchanging doer through the modes.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I mistaking the colors of the guṇas — the moods of sattva, rajas, or tamas passing through me — for my actual identity, and what would change if I held them as Kṛṣṇa's potencies rather than as 'me'?
  • When I encounter someone whose outer 'color' (caste, role, temperament, appearance) seems to mark them as low or high, where am I falling into bakāndha-nyāya — the duck's logic — and judging by surface rather than by their position in relation to Kṛṣṇa?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When an exam result, a breakup, or a friend-group shift hits, name the guṇa at work out loud — 'this is rajas churning,' 'this is tamas closing in' — and remember Kṛṣṇa is the one untouched person behind all three. Don't take the mode personally.
  • Audit your social-media feed for a week with the bakāndha-nyāya test: which accounts have you been chasing for their śukla appearance (clean aesthetic, calm voice) that are actually feeding you tamas? Unfollow three.
  • Before a high-stakes test or interview, pray specifically to Kṛṣṇa as the personal Lord behind the rajas of striving — not to 'the universe,' not to 'my best self.' Notice how the prayer changes when the addressee is a person, not a force.
householder
  • When a family decision involves creation (a new child, a business launch), maintenance (a marriage, a job kept), or ending (selling a home, leaving a role), explicitly identify which of the three you are in, and offer that specific phase to Kṛṣṇa as His potency working through you.
  • Teach your children the difference between Kṛṣṇa and the demigods using this verse — that Brahmā and Śiva are real and to be respected, but Kṛṣṇa alone is the personal Supreme. A five-minute dinner conversation is enough; do it this week before the culture does the teaching for you.
  • When a financial setback or a child's failure tempts you to read it as cosmic injustice, recall harir hi nirguṇaḥ — the Lord is not staining Himself with your tamas. The setback is His arrangement (nyāya), not His indifference. Act with kṣamā, then act.
senior
  • Your body is now visibly in the kṛṣṇa-varṇa phase — the verse names it without flinching, and so should you. Each morning, acknowledge that annihilation is a potency of the same Lord who maintained you for sixty years; the manager has not changed.
  • Mentor a younger person specifically on the personalist distinction — that surrender is to Kṛṣṇa, a person, not to 'the light' or 'the source.' Your generation is the last that can transmit this without translation loss; don't postpone it.
  • Loosen one attachment this month that you have been preserving out of rajas-residue (a property, a title, a grudge, a possession). Offer it to the Lord who presides over endings, and notice that vairāgya at this stage is not loss but alignment with the season Kṛṣṇa Himself has set.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading śukla, rakta, and kṛṣṇa literally — as skin tones or aesthetic preferences — instead of as representations of sattva-, rajo-, and tamo-guṇa, and then importing modern racial or aesthetic prejudices into one's understanding of the Lord and His devotees.
  • Concluding that because Kṛṣṇa 'assumes' the modes for creation, maintenance, and annihilation, He is therefore touched or limited by them — collapsing the personal Supreme into an impersonal field of guṇas, when the verse and purport insist harir hi nirguṇaḥ sākṣāt: He remains transcendental throughout.
  • Using 'beyond the modes' as a license to neglect one's varṇāśrama duties or to treat Brahmā and Śiva as interchangeable demigods on Kṛṣṇa's level, rather than recognizing that creation and annihilation are conducted by His potencies while He alone is svayaṁ bhagavān.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva insists Kṛṣṇa is nirguṇa even while assuming the white, red, and black colors of maintenance, creation, and annihilation. Share with your partner: when in your own life have you mistaken Kṛṣṇa's working through the guṇas (a hard reversal, a sudden ascent) as His personal disposition toward you? Where did that misreading take you?
  • Prabhupāda warns against bakāndha-nyāya — the duck logic of judging by surface color. Pair up and challenge each other: name one person, teaching, or institution you have written off based on its 'color' (its guṇa-appearance), and one you have over-trusted for the same reason. Disagree honestly if your partner's read seems naïve.
  • If Brahmā (rakta, creation), Viṣṇu (śukla, maintenance), and Śiva (kṛṣṇa, annihilation) are all functions through which the one Lord acts, what does it mean — practically, not poetically — to say surrender is to Kṛṣṇa personally and not to 'the divine functions'? Push your group: where does this distinction actually change a decision you would make this week?
Role Play

Two students play roommates. Roommate A has just lost a parent and is sinking; a well-meaning friend (Roommate B) keeps offering 'the universe has a plan,' 'energies are shifting,' 'your loved one is now pure light.' Roommate A finally says, 'Stop. I need something real.' Roommate B must now attempt to share the substance of SB 10.3.20 — that creation, maintenance, and annihilation are Kṛṣṇa's potencies, that He personally remains untouched and untouchable by tamas, and that the loss is not a cosmic accident but His arrangement — without lapsing into impersonalist platitudes or inventing pastimes. After 4 minutes, swap roles. Debrief: where did the language drift toward 'the Self' or 'energy'? Where did it land as Kṛṣṇa, the person?

Objections & Responses
  • If Kṛṣṇa 'becomes' Brahmā for creation and 'becomes' Śiva for destruction, isn't this just polytheism dressed up — or worse, a theology where God is responsible for every evil because tamas is His own color?
    Vasudeva is precise: sva-māyayā — by His own potency. The Lord does not personally enter rajas or tamas; He deploys these guṇas through empowered functionaries (Brahmā, Śiva) while remaining harir hi nirguṇaḥ sākṣāt, untouched. This is not polytheism: Kṛṣṇa is bhagavān svayam (SB 1.3.28), and Brahmā and Śiva are His aṁśa-kalāḥ — portions, not rivals. As for evil: annihilation is not malice but mercy, the cleansing the cosmos requires. The Lord arranges it without being soiled by it, the way a surgeon cuts without becoming the disease.
  • This whole color scheme sounds like a pre-modern attempt to map deities onto the guṇas — quaint mythology. A modern devotee can drop the color symbolism and keep the ethics.
    Prabhupāda anticipates exactly this move and refuses it. He cites Sanātana Gosvāmī to say the colors are not the literal pigments of Viṣṇu, Brahmā, and Śiva, but designations of sattva, rajas, and tamas — the actual operative forces behind every event in your life: the rise of an idea (rajas), the holding of a marriage (sattva), the death of a friend (tamas). Drop the symbolism and you lose the diagnostic. The verse is not decoration; it is how a devotee reads the day. And the personalist core — that one Lord, Kṛṣṇa, presides over all three through His potencies — is what keeps the ethics from collapsing into either fatalism or self-help.
Analogy

A master painter dips one brush into three pigments — white for the dawn, red for the noon, black for the dusk — and paints a single landscape in three movements. The brush, the hand, the painter never change colors; only what is painted does. Bhagavān assumes Viṣṇu (white), Brahmā (red), Śiva (black) for creation's three phases; the colors are on the canvas of līlā, not on Him.

Stories
Mohinī-mūrti at the churning of the milk ocean

Bhagavān assumed the Mohinī form (SB 8.8–9) to retrieve the nectar from the asuras and distribute it to the devas. He took a form whose color and gender were quite other than His normal Vaikuṇṭha appearance, yet His svarūpa never altered. The līlā required that mūrti; the inner identity was unchanged.

This verse's logic is the same: the white, red, and black colors of trimūrti are functional forms taken by Bhagavān's sva-māyā for cosmic work. The Person behind the colors is one — Viṣṇu, who in His original Vraja form is Kṛṣṇa, śyāma-sundara.
