Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.33

When both of you were ordered by Lord Brahmā to create progeny, you first underwent severe austerities by controlling your senses.
Purport

Here is an instruction about how to use one’s senses to create progeny. According to Vedic principles, before creating progeny one must fully control the senses. This control takes place through the garbhādhāna-saṁskāra. In India there is great agitation for birth control in various mechanical ways, but birth cannot be mechanically controlled. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (13.9), janma-mṛtyu-jarā-vyādhi-duḥkha-doṣānudarśanam: birth, death, old age and disease are certainly the primary distresses of the material world. People are trying to control birth, but they are not able to control death; and if one cannot control death, one cannot control birth either. In other words, artificially controlling birth is not any more feasible than artificially controlling death. According to Vedic civilization, procreation should not be contrary to religious principles, and then the birthrate will be controlled. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (7.11) , dharmāviruddho bhūteṣu kāmo ’smi: sex not contrary to religious principles is a representation of the Supreme Lord. People should be educated in how to give birth to good children through saṁskāras, beginning with the garbhādhāna-saṁskāra; birth should not be controlled by artificial means, for this will lead to a civilization of animals. If one follows religious principles, he automatically practices birth control because if one is spiritually educated he knows that the after-effects of sex are various types of misery ( bahu-duḥkha-bhāja ). One who is spiritually advanced does not indulge in uncontrolled sex. Therefore, instead of being forced to refrain from sex or refrain from giving birth to many children, people should be spiritually educated, and then birth control will automatically follow. If one is determined to make spiritual advancement, he will not beget a child unless able to make that child a devotee. As stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (5.5.18) , pitā na sa syāt: one should not become a father unless one is able to protect his child from mṛtyu, the path of birth and death. But where is there education about this? A responsible father never begets children like cats and dogs. Instead of being encouraged to adopt artificial means of birth control, people should be educated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness because only then will they understand their responsibility to their children. If one can beget children who will be devotees and be taught to turn aside from the path of birth and death ( mṛtyu-saṁsāra-vartmani ), there is no need of birth control. Rather, one should be encouraged to beget children. Artificial means of birth control have no value. Whether one begets children or does not, a population of men who are like cats and dogs will never make human society happy. It is therefore necessary for people to be educated spiritually so that instead of begetting children like cats and dogs, they will undergo austerities to produce devotees. This will make their lives successful.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
When both of you were ordered by Lord Brahmā to create progeny, you first underwent severe austerities by controlling your senses.
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Vai indicates that this is well known or remembered. They were obedient in following Brahmā’s orders. Their senses became peaceful. They understood many (paramam) austerities. Śrīdhara Svāmī explains that tepāthe means “they performed austerities.” This follows the rule tapstapaḥ karmakasya: the agent of the verb tap becomes similar to the object when the object is the word tapas (rather than “to heat”). (Pāṇini 1.3.88) tepāthe tapaḥ means “they performed austerities” and tepāthe then simply means “performed”

Personal Guidance
Before creating progeny, the great souls first mastered their senses through tapas — Kṛṣṇa-conscious procreation begins with atma-nigraha, never mechanical control.
Practical Applications
  • Refuse the modern lie that birth and outcomes can be mechanically engineered, and instead direct that energy into one act of sense-control today — fasting from one indulgence as offered tapas.
  • Before any creative or generative act today (work, words, planning a family), pause for japa or a few minutes of Bhāgavatam reading so the act flows from a Kṛṣṇa-fixed mind, not a restless one.
  • Speak to one householder or seeker in your life about responsible procreation as a sādhana — that a true father or mother begets souls they can guide past mṛtyu-saṁsāra, not children raised like cats and dogs.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my own life am I trying to 'mechanically control' an outcome — through technique, willpower, or arrangement — when the verse points to tapas and indriya-saṁyama as the real preparation?
  • If I were honest, am I willing to bring forth anything (a child, a project, a teaching, a relationship) only when I can shepherd it toward Kṛṣṇa — or am I producing 'progeny' of every kind without that responsibility?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before scrolling tonight, sit for ten minutes and do one round of japa — practice sanniyamya indriya-grāmam on the one sense (sight, attention) that runs you most.
  • Pick one exam, audition, or application this term and prepare for it with explicit tapas: a fixed sleep time, no social media after 9pm, and a small fast on the day before. Treat the preparation itself as a Prajāpati-style 'creation of progeny' — you are creating a future self.
  • In your friend group, name the one peer-pressure pattern (drinking, hookup talk, performative cynicism) where you abandon atma-nigraha to fit in. Decide today what your specific line is and tell one trusted friend, so it isn't only inside your head.
householder
  • If you are considering a child, do not just check finances and timing — set a six-month period of joint sādhana (daily japa together, Ekādaśī fasting, weekly Bhāgavatam reading) as your couple's garbhādhāna-saṁskāra preparation, regardless of what fertility plan you follow.
  • Audit one major family decision this month — a school for the kids, a job change, a house purchase — and ask: is this decision being made from indriya-pull (status, comfort, fear) or from a place of tapas? Sit with your spouse and rank the motives honestly.
  • Tonight, with each child, replace one screen-time slot with a story from Kṛṣṇa's pastimes or a verse you both memorize. You are not just parenting a body; you are responsible for delivering this soul from mṛtyu-saṁsāra-vartmani.
senior
  • Identify one indriya-attachment that has quietly tightened in later life — food preferences, news-watching, the need to be consulted — and undertake a deliberate, week-long tapas to loosen it. Mortality will loosen it eventually; better to do it now, consciously.
  • Mentor at least one younger person (grandchild, junior colleague, neighbor) not by giving advice but by modeling sanniyamya indriya-grāmam in front of them — your restraint at the dinner table, with your phone, in conversation, will teach more than any lecture.
  • Begin distributing — possessions, responsibilities, opinions — as a daily practice of tyāga. Each week, give away one object, hand off one role, and release one strong opinion. Treat this as your final 'progeny': the example of a senior who lived under regulation, not under appetite.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reducing this verse to a polemic about birth control and missing its core instruction: that any act of creation must be preceded by tapas and sense-restraint, not just procreation.
  • Confusing external suppression of the senses with sanniyamya — performative restraint without the inner austerity Brahmā's sons actually undertook.
  • Treating 'controlling the senses before creating progeny' as an archaic ritual concern, rather than as the personal standard the verse sets for any responsibility one assumes — that one must first be qualified by tapas to take it on.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says artificial birth control produces 'a civilization of animals,' while spiritual education produces natural restraint. Where in your own life have you tried to control an outcome mechanically that actually called for tapas and inner training? Disagree with each other honestly — when is mechanical control legitimate, and when is it a substitute for sense-control?
  • The verse holds up the Prajāpatis, who underwent severe austerity before begetting progeny. How much austerity is realistic before a major life act — marriage, having a child, taking on a disciple, starting a venture? Push each other: is 'I'm not ready spiritually' sometimes just an excuse, and is 'I'm ready enough' sometimes just impatience?
  • Prabhupāda writes pitā na sa syāt — one should not become a father unless able to deliver the child from birth and death. Apply this beyond biological fatherhood: as teacher, mentor, manager, elder sibling. Where in your life are you 'begetting' something (a student, a project, a follower) that you are not yet spiritually equipped to protect? Disagree on whether the standard is achievable or aspirational.
Role Play

A married couple in their early thirties comes to a temple counselor. They have been together five years and feel social pressure — parents, friends, social media — to have a child this year. The husband is keen; the wife is hesitant because she senses neither of them has any sādhana, and she fears raising a child 'like cats and dogs.' Assign three roles: the husband (eager, rationalizing), the wife (intuitive, unsure how to articulate her hesitation in non-religious terms), and the counselor (a Gauḍīya devotee who must cite SB 10.3.33 and the principle of garbhādhāna-saṁskāra without sounding judgmental or anti-family). The counselor must guide them toward a concrete period of tapas and atma-nigraha before conception — not 'don't have a child,' but 'prepare to beget a devotee.' Run it for ten minutes, then swap roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This is reactionary — it sounds like the verse is being used to attack contraception and women's reproductive autonomy. Surely the ṛṣis weren't legislating modern family planning.
    The verse is not a policy statement on contraception; it is a teaching on the sacredness of procreation. The Prajāpatis underwent tapas before creating progeny because they understood that bringing a soul into a body is a spiritual act with consequences for that soul. Prabhupāda's critique is not of women or of family size — it is of a culture that treats sex as recreation and the resulting child as an inconvenience to be mechanically prevented or, failing that, raised without spiritual education. A Gauḍīya teacher would say: the question is not whether you use a method, but whether your sexual life is dharma-aviruddha — not contrary to religious principles. Atma-nigraha is the real instruction; the rest is downstream.
  • Tapas before creating progeny sounds beautiful in mythology, but in 2026 most couples can barely afford rent. Telling them to undergo austerities before having a child is privileged spiritual advice that ignores economic reality.
    Tapas in this verse is not about renouncing income or living in a forest. It is sanniyamya indriya-grāmam — controlling the senses. That is available to anyone, on any budget, in any apartment. A young couple with no savings can still practice atma-nigraha: regulated sleep, regulated speech, regulated consumption of media, japa together, fasting on Ekādaśī, and most directly, restraint and intentionality in their sexual life. The verse doesn't ask for wealth before a child; it asks for self-mastery. In fact, Prabhupāda's whole point is that material abundance without tapas produces miserable children, and modest means with tapas produces devotees. The economics are almost the inverse of what the objection assumes.
