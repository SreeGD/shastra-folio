Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.13

Important Words
  • viditaḥ asi — You are known
    Vidita (known, past participle of vid) + asi (You are). The simplest possible statement of jñāna-grounded bhakti. Vasudeva does not say 'I worship'; he says 'I now know'. Sambandha-jñāna precedes abhidheya-bhakti.
  • puruṣaḥ — the Supreme Person
    Puruṣa — person, the conscious enjoyer over against prakṛti (matter). In Sāṅkhya the duality is enumerated; in Vaiṣṇava reading there is one supreme puruṣa, and the jīvas are His parts and parcels. The word locks Bhagavān into the personalist register.
  • prakṛteḥ-paraḥ — beyond material nature
    Prakṛti (material energy) + paraḥ (beyond, transcendent). Kṛṣṇa is acintya-bhedābheda with prakṛti — She is His energy, simultaneously one with Him and different — but He is paraḥ, never reducible to Her. Key anti-Māyāvāda formulation.
  • kevala-anubhava-ānanda-svarūpaḥ — of the nature of pure conscious bliss
    Kevala (pure, sole) + anubhava (experience, consciousness) + ānanda (bliss) + svarūpa (own form). Bhagavān is sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha — His very form is being-knowledge-bliss. Not 'has' these qualities but IS them. Definitional verse for personalist Vedānta.
  • sarva-buddhi-dṛk — the seer of all minds
    Sarva (all) + buddhi (intelligence) + dṛk (seer). The antaryāmī. Echoes the Paramātmā doctrine: Bhagavān witnesses every cognition in every heart. Combine with the previous compound and you have the Gauḍīya theology: He is bliss-form AND in-dwelling witness simultaneously.
Vasudeva said: My Lord, You are the Supreme Person, beyond material existence, and You are the Supersoul. Your form can be perceived by transcendental knowledge, by which You can be understood as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. I now understand Your position perfectly.
Purport

Within Vasudeva’s heart, affection for his son and knowledge of the Supreme Lord’s transcendental nature both awakened. In the beginning Vasudeva thought, “Such a beautiful child has been born, but now Kaṁsa will come and kill Him.” But when he understood that this was not an ordinary child but the Supreme Personality of Godhead, he became fearless. Regarding his son as the Supreme Lord, wonderful in everything, he began offering prayers appropriate for the Supreme Lord. Completely free from fear of Kaṁsa’s atrocities, he accepted the child simultaneously as an object of affection and as an object of worship by prayers.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Vasudeva said: My Lord, You are the Supreme Person, beyond material existence, and You are the Supersoul. Your form can be perceived by transcendental knowledge, by which You can be understood as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. I now understand Your position perfectly.
Commentary

Your intention in showing this form is as follows: "My father has fear for me because of Kamsa, so therefore I will make him fearless by showing him that I am God." Accordingly, I have become fearless on understanding that you are God." With this understanding Vasudeva speaks this verse. "I can understand that you are the supreme person, Karnodasayi Visnu, who activates prakrti by his glance. You are the soul of Brahman realization. You are the pervading supersoul (sarva buddhi drk). You are all these things because you are directly svayam bhagavan."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Śrīdhara Svāmī explains this verse. In what way are you known? He addresses the Lord with respect (bhavān) since he understands the Lord’s position as mentioned in the verse. “It is astonishing that you say that I am known as the Supreme Lord. It is well known that the Lord is beyond prakṛti.” But I know this directly by seeing you (sākṣāt). Or Vasudeva speaks in joy on directly seeing the Lord even though he is rare to attain. “You are known directly” means “I have attained you today.” The knowing is described. You are the witness of prakṛti—you are beyond prakṛti-- and you are the bliss or supreme Brahman possessing as well impersonal features (kevalānubhava). You are also the witness of all intelligence, the Paramātmā (sarva-buddhi-dṛk). Since you include these aspects in yourself, you are Bhagavān (ānanda). I see this directly with my eyes. How fortunate I am!

Personal Guidance
Vasudeva sees through fear by recognizing his newborn as Kṛṣṇa Himself — the Supreme Person beyond prakṛti, witness of every mind.
Practical Applications
  • When dread of an outcome grips you today, name Kṛṣṇa as puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ and refuse the assumption that material force has the final word.
  • Before one ordinary task, pause and remember that He is sarva-buddhi-dṛk — the seer of every thought — and act as one observed by the Lord.
  • Hold both intimacy and reverence at once: speak to Kṛṣṇa today as personally near, and bow to Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, never collapsing one into the other.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still relating to Kṛṣṇa as if He were a small, vulnerable presence I must protect with my own intelligence — instead of recognizing Him as puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ, beyond the very anxieties I am projecting onto Him?
  • Vasudeva held his son simultaneously as an object of affection and as the Supreme Personality of Godhead — which side of that double vision do I habitually drop, and what does my life look like when only one side is present?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam, instead of bargaining with anxiety, try Vasudeva's move: name one thing you have been fearing (a result, a rejection, a ranking) and offer it to Kṛṣṇa as sarva-buddhi-dṛk — the One who already sees every intelligence in the room, including the examiner's. Then walk in and write your paper.
  • On social media this week, notice every time you treat someone — a crush, an influencer, a friend you envy — as if they were puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ, the supreme reference point of your worth. Replace one such scroll session with five minutes of remembering that Kṛṣṇa alone holds that position, and feel how the comparison loop loosens.
  • Pick one friend or sibling you usually argue with. For one full day, practice seeing them the way Vasudeva learned to see his son — not as the supreme Lord, but as someone in whom the same Supersoul (sarva-buddhi-dṛk) is witnessing. Notice whether your tone changes without you forcing it.
householder
  • When you next look at your child — especially in a moment of frustration or worry — pause and remember Vasudeva: he held both deep affection and the awareness that his son was the Supreme Person. Today, treat one parenting interaction as worship without ceasing to be parenting; do not let one cancel the other.
  • In your next significant family financial or career decision, name out loud (to your spouse, or in writing) the fear driving the calculation — fear of a 'Kaṁsa' (loss, failure, judgment). Then explicitly turn the decision over to Kṛṣṇa as puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ, beyond all material contingencies, and choose from that ground rather than from fear.
  • Once this week, sit with your spouse for ten minutes and confess together one area where you have been treating a person, asset, or outcome as your real shelter. Then jointly offer it to Kṛṣṇa, the way Vasudeva accepted his son simultaneously as object of affection and object of worship — letting affection remain, but relocating shelter.
senior
  • Each morning, before reading the news or checking on the family, sit and recall: I am about to spend a day in a body that is closer to its end than its beginning, and Kṛṣṇa is kevalānubhavānanda-svarūpa — pure experiential bliss. Spend five minutes letting that contrast settle, so the day's losses and aches do not have the last word.
  • Identify one attachment — a property, a role in the family, an old grievance, a saved object — that you have been quietly clinging to as if it were puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ. This week, take one concrete step to loosen it: gift it, hand over the role, forgive the person, or simply name aloud that it is not your shelter.
  • Find one younger person — child, grandchild, student, neighbor — and tell them, plainly, the story of one fear you carried for decades and how recognizing Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person dissolved or reframed it. Vasudeva's fearlessness was not private; let your hard-won clarity become someone else's inheritance.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading kevalānubhavānanda-svarūpaḥ as 'pure consciousness' or 'inner bliss within me' — collapsing Kṛṣṇa's personal transcendental form into an impersonal feeling-state I can access without surrender.
  • Treating Vasudeva's recognition as a one-time intellectual realization to be checked off, rather than the ongoing pivot from fear-of-Kaṁsa thinking ('what will the world do to what I love?') to fearlessness rooted in Kṛṣṇa's supremacy.
  • Using the verse's 'beyond material existence' to manufacture a false detachment from one's actual duties and affections — whereas Vasudeva did not abandon his role as father; he saw the same person as both son and Supreme Lord, and acted accordingly.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva sees his newborn son and recognizes him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead — yet he still feels paternal affection. Share with your partner: where in your own life have you tried to choose between personal love and reverence for Kṛṣṇa, when the verse suggests both can coexist? Where do you and your partner disagree on whether one must dampen affection to deepen worship?
  • The verse says Kṛṣṇa is puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ — beyond material nature — and yet appears as a vulnerable child in a prison cell. Discuss: does this contradict the impersonalist intuition that the Absolute must be formless to be supreme? Push back on each other; do not settle too quickly on the 'right' Gauḍīya answer.
  • Vasudeva moved from fear of Kaṁsa to fearlessness the moment he saw clearly. In your group, name one fear that still rules you, and ask each other honestly: is this fear because I have not yet seen Kṛṣṇa as sarva-buddhi-dṛk — the witness of all intelligence — or am I using 'I haven't realized it yet' as an excuse to keep the fear?
Role Play

A teacher splits the class into pairs. One person plays Vasudeva in the prison cell at the moment of Kṛṣṇa's birth; the other plays a skeptical modern friend who has been allowed (imaginatively) into the cell and says: 'Vasudeva, this is your son. He is hungry, He can be killed. Stop calling Him the Supreme Person and start acting like a father — hide Him, feed Him, do something practical.' Vasudeva must respond from the verse — affirming that this child is puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ AND simultaneously his beloved son, and that recognizing the first is precisely what makes the second safe. Switch roles after five minutes. Debrief: where did the 'father' role feel forced into either pure sentiment or pure philosophy, and how does the verse hold both?

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like delusion — a frightened father convinces himself his baby is God so he can stop being terrified. Isn't this just a coping mechanism dressed up as theology?
    If Vasudeva had merely soothed himself with a story, the result would be denial, not the lucid prayers that follow. The verse marks a recognition (vidito 'si bhavān sākṣāt — 'You are directly known'), not a wish. Test it the way Vasudeva does: real recognition of Kṛṣṇa produces fearlessness AND clearer action, not paralysis or fantasy. Coping mechanisms collapse under pressure; Vasudeva's fearlessness held through carrying the child across the Yamunā. The Gauḍīya tradition does not ask you to believe by force — it asks you to perform sādhana, hear from sādhus, and let recognition arise the way it arose in Vasudeva, by Kṛṣṇa's own self-disclosure to a purified heart.
  • Why insist on the personal form? Isn't 'beyond material existence' enough? Calling Him a Person seems like a cultural leftover — the deeper truth must be impersonal.
    The verse itself refuses that move. It says puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ — the Person beyond prakṛti — and kevalānubhavānanda-svarūpaḥ — whose very form is pure experiential bliss. Impersonalism treats personhood as a limitation, but here personhood is the svarūpa, the essential form, of the Absolute. To strip the Person away in the name of depth is to subtract, not deepen. Ask yourself honestly: who actually loves an abstraction? Vasudeva's prayers, the Gītā's surrender, Caitanya Mahāprabhu's chanting — none of it works without a Person to address. The personalist line is not cultural residue; it is what makes bhakti possible at all.
Analogy

When you peer at a deep well, you can describe the rope, the bucket, the stone rim — but the water itself is only known by drawing it and tasting. Vasudeva says: 'My Lord, You are beyond prakṛti, You are pure consciousness-bliss; this I now know.' The well is the body before him; the water is the svarūpa he has tasted by recognition.

Stories
Brahmā's hymn at the start of Bhāgavatam Canto 10

When Brahmā realized Kṛṣṇa's true position after the brahma-vimohana-līlā (10.13–14), he offered an extended stuti establishing that Kṛṣṇa is sākṣāt puruṣa, prakṛti-paraḥ — the very language Vasudeva uses here.

The pattern is consistent across the canto: every realized being who encounters Kṛṣṇa arrives at the same Vedāntic conclusion — He is the supreme person, transcendental to matter, the very form of consciousness and bliss. Different witnesses, identical siddhānta.
