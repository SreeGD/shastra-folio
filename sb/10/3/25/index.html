Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.25

Important Words
  • dvi-parārdha-avasāne — at the end of the two parārdhas (Brahmā's full life)
    Dvi (two) + parārdha (half of Brahmā's life) + avasāna (end). Bhāgavata cosmology measures time in scales that dwarf human imagination — and Bhagavān outlasts even those. The verse is positioning Him as eternal in the absolute sense, not the relative.
  • kāla-vegena — by the force of time
    Kāla (time) + vega (force, momentum) + instrumental. Time itself is Bhagavān's energy (BG 11.32, kālo'smi). Yet here time is the agent of annihilation — His own potency dissolving His own manifestation. Reflexive cosmic action.
  • ekaḥ — one alone
    Eka — one, alone. Crucial Vaiṣṇava reading: Bhagavān remains 'one' in the sense of being the only existent — but with his eternal sva-rūpa, energies, and associates. NOT 'one' in the Advaitin sense of dissolving difference. Acintya-bhedābheda preserves both eka and ananta.
  • aśeṣa-saṁjñaḥ — known by various names (Ananta, Śeṣa)
    Aśeṣa (without remainder, also a play on the name Śeṣa) + saṁjñā (name, designation). He is named differently in different phases — Ananta-Śeṣa during dissolution. The Vaiṣṇava plurality of holy names is itself a doctrinal fact: He is one, His names are countless, and each name is fully Him.
After millions of years, at the time of cosmic annihilation, when everything, manifested and unmanifested, is annihilated by the force of time, the five gross elements enter into the subtle conception, and the manifested categories enter into the unmanifested substance. At that time, You alone remain, and You are known as Ananta Śeṣa-nāga.
Purport

At the time of annihilation, the five gross elements — earth, water, fire, air and ether — enter into the mind, intelligence and false ego ( ahaṅkāra ), and the entire cosmic manifestation enters into the spiritual energy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who alone remains as the origin of everything. The Lord is therefore known as Śeṣa-nāga, as Ādi-puruṣa and by many other names. Devakī therefore prayed, “After many millions of years, when Lord Brahmā comes to the end of his life, the annihilation of the cosmic manifestation takes place. At that time the five elements — namely earth, water, fire, air and ether — enter into the mahat-tattva. The mahat-tattva again enters, by the force of time, into the nonmanifested total material energy, the total material energy enters into the energetic pradhāna, and the pradhāna enters into You. Therefore after the annihilation of the whole cosmic manifestation, You alone remain with Your transcendental name, form, quality and paraphernalia. “My Lord, I offer my respectful obeisances unto You because You are the director of the unmanifested total energy, and the ultimate reservoir of the material nature. My Lord, the whole cosmic manifestation is under the influence of time, beginning from the moment up to the duration of the year. All act under Your direction. You are the original director of everything and the reservoir of all potent energies.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
After millions of years, at the time of cosmic annihilation, when everything, manifested and unmanifested, is annihilated by the force of time, the five gross elements enter into the subtle conception, and the manifested categories enter into the unmanifested substance. At that time, You alone remain, and You are known as Ananta Śeṣa-nāga.
Commentary

For one who is not destroyed during mahapralaya, what is the question of fear? The five elements(mahabhutam) merge into adibhutam or ahankara. Ahankara merges into vyaktam or mahat tattva. The mahat tattva merges into avyakta or prakrti. Then only you remain. Those things mentioned in the previous verse, the forms of Narayan and others, brahma jyoti and the dhama (sattamatram) also remain. The Lord’s associates, place and cloth are all eternal. You are called sesa because you remain (avasesa). This is a way of deriving the name Sesa.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Time is the cause of destruction. You alone remain. Since Vaikuṇṭha is non-different from the Lord it and other spiritual objects are included in the statement. Or you alone remain with those forms designated as unlimited that also remain in Vaikuṇṭha after pralaya. Or the word aśeṣa can also be taken as śeṣa (remain). Thus they inhabitants of Vaikuṇṭha also remain after destruction. Or śeṣa can refer to all the objects of Vaikuṇṭha that remain after destruction. You alone, along with the objects of Vaikuṇṭha understood (saṁjṣaḥ) to be non-different from you, remain. You alone exist. This does not include the jīvas of the material world. tattvānāṁ bhagavaṁs teṣāṁ katidhā pratisaṅkramaḥ tatremaṁ ka upāsīran ka u svid anuśerate How many types of destruction are there for the various elements? Who remains serving the Lord during destruction, and who goes to sleep? SB 3.7.37 However the devotees are in a special position: martyo mṛtyu-vyāla-bhītaḥ palāyan lokān sarvān nirbhayaṁ nādhyagacchat tvat pādābjaṁ prāpya yadṛcchayādya susthaḥ śete mṛtyur asmād apaiti No one in this material world has become free from the four principles birth, death, old age and disease, even by fleeing to various planets. But now that you have appeared, my Lord, death is fleeing in fear of you, and the living entities, having obtained shelter at your lotus feet by your mercy, are sleeping in full mental peace. SB 10.3.27

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Even at the time of universal destruction you remain playing with your associates. By the force of time everything is destroyed. At that time the associate Śeṣa remains. This stands for Garuḍa and others as well. They remain or they stand (śiśyate) even at destruction. Or that which is known as śeṣa, because it remains—everything in Vaikuṇṭha—is left. Yatheṣṭa-viniyogārhaṁ śeṣa-śabdena kathyate: all that which is utilized for the Lord’s pleasure is called śeṣa. Those things which are eternally with the Lord are called śeṣa.

Concepts
Purusha
Personal Guidance
When all manifestation dissolves at pralaya, Kṛṣṇa alone remains as Ananta Śeṣa — so anchor your shelter in Him today, not in what time will swallow.
Practical Applications
  • Name three things you are leaning on for security today (job, body, savings) and explicitly hand each one to Kṛṣṇa as borrowed, not owned.
  • When a fear of loss arises, recall that even the five gross elements collapse into Him at annihilation — and offer that fear at His feet.
  • Refuse to invest your identity in any role or possession that pralaya will erase; spend ten minutes in japa rooting your sense of self in Him alone.
Reflection Questions
  • When I picture the dvi-parārdha dissolution — every gross element folding back into the unmanifest, every category I rely on for identity erased — what part of my present self-conception cannot survive that contraction, and why am I still investing in it?
  • If Kṛṣṇa alone remains as Ananta Śeṣa-nāga when everything else is withdrawn, what would it look like, today, to relate to Him as the one who *will* remain — rather than to the people, roles, and securities that will not?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When your Instagram or college-application persona feels like the most permanent thing about you, sit for two minutes and remember: even pradhāna dissolves. The 'brand' you are building will not survive one cosmic night. Let that loosen the grip — not into laziness, but into honesty about what you are actually here to study and serve.
  • Before your next exam, offer the result formally to Kṛṣṇa as Ananta Śeṣa — the One who remains when even mahat-tattva is gone. Then study harder, not less. Notice how detachment from the score sharpens, rather than dulls, the preparation.
  • When a friendship breaks or a situationship ends and you feel like your world is annihilated, name it accurately: a small dissolution, not THE dissolution. The Self in you that is grieving has already passed through many bodies and many friend groups. Write down three earlier 'endings' you survived — and recognize the same dhīra in you now.
householder
  • When making the next big family decision (a move, a school choice, a job change), bring it to evening sandhyā and consciously place the outcome at the feet of Ananta Śeṣa — the Lord who alone remains. Decide with full effort and full detachment from the result; this is the householder's karma-yoga, not renunciate's escape.
  • Audit your financial planning this month against the verse: how much of your savings, insurance, and estate-planning anxiety assumes that the structures of this world are more permanent than they are? Keep the prudence; drop the panic. The pradhāna itself dissolves — your 401(k) is even shorter-lived.
  • Teach your children — by what you do, not what you say — that you are training them as eternal souls passing through this stage of life, not as résumés to be optimized. One concrete act this week: skip one achievement-oriented activity and replace it with reading or hearing about Kṛṣṇa together.
senior
  • Your body, like the five gross elements in the verse, will enter the subtle, and the subtle will enter the unmanifest. Sit with that truth daily for five minutes — not morbidly, but as preparation. The dhīra does not flinch from this passage; he uses the remaining time to deepen surrender.
  • Identify one possession, role, or title you have been quietly clinging to as 'mine forever' — the house, the chair on the board, the position in the family hierarchy. This week, make one concrete act of loosening: hand over a responsibility, give away an heirloom with warm words, name a successor. Practice the dissolution before it is forced.
  • Mentor a younger devotee or family member by speaking plainly about Ananta Śeṣa — the One who remains. Do not philosophize at them; tell them what you have learned about what does and does not survive. Your testimony at this stage carries weight that no book can match.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating cosmic annihilation as a poetic abstraction — admiring the scale of pralaya while never letting it touch one's daily attachments to body, status, or possessions; the verse becomes cosmology instead of vairāgya.
  • Sliding into impersonalism: imagining that what 'remains' at annihilation is a formless light or pure consciousness, rather than the personal Lord with His transcendental name, form, quality, and paraphernalia, as Devakī explicitly prays.
  • Using the inevitability of dissolution as an excuse for fatalism or passivity ('nothing lasts, so nothing matters'), missing that the verse's purpose is to redirect effort toward the one Person who does remain — not to dismiss action altogether.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī describes a moment when even mahat-tattva and pradhāna dissolve into Kṛṣṇa, while He alone remains as Ananta Śeṣa. Share with your partner: what is one 'permanent' structure in your life — a job, a relationship, a body of work — that you have been quietly treating as eternal? Where does the disagreement land between you about whether contemplating its dissolution is liberating or paralyzing?
  • The verse pivots on a personalist claim: at annihilation, what remains is not a luminous void or impersonal Self, but bhavān — You, with name, form, quality and paraphernalia. Discuss: is it harder for you to surrender to a Person than to an abstraction? Push each other on which is intellectual evasion and which is genuine bhakti.
  • Prabhupāda's purport says the entire cosmos enters the spiritual energy of the Supreme. If everything ultimately reposes in Him, does this make daily karma less serious or more serious? Defend opposite positions in your pair before trying to reconcile them through the lens of sva-dharma.
Role Play

Two students role-play a hospice scene. Student A is a 78-year-old grandfather who has just received a terminal diagnosis and is panicking that 'everything I built — the company, the house, the family name — will be gone in twenty years.' Student B is a grandchild who has been studying the Bhāgavatam and wants to share the truth of SB 10.3.25 — that at the time of cosmic annihilation, even mahat-tattva dissolves, and only Kṛṣṇa remains as Ananta Śeṣa. Student B must NOT use it to bypass the grandfather's grief or to score philosophical points; they must offer real karuṇā (serving the soul) rather than material compassion (just soothing the body's fear). After 5 minutes, swap roles. Then debrief: where did the verse land as comfort, and where did it land as evasion?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just cosmic nihilism dressed up in Sanskrit. If everything I love — my children, my work, the planet itself — eventually dissolves into Kṛṣṇa, why bother with any of it? It collapses into apathy.
    Notice the verse does not say everything becomes nothing — it says everything enters Him, and He remains with His transcendental name, form, quality, and paraphernalia. That is not nihilism; that is the opposite. Nothing precious is lost; it is held. Apathy comes from believing things are *unreal* now and *gone* later. The Gauḍīya teaching is that things are real because they rest in Him — and that recognition does not collapse engagement, it purifies it. You serve your children not to make them permanent (you cannot) but because the soul in them is eternal and the service is offered to Kṛṣṇa. That is karma-yoga, not apathy.
  • Modern cosmology gives us the Big Bang, heat death, entropy. Why drape it in mythological language about Brahmā's lifespan and Ananta Śeṣa? Isn't this pre-scientific dressing on facts science already explains better?
    Science describes the *mechanism* of dissolution; it is silent on what, if anything, remains as the substrate and on whether that substrate is personal. SB 10.3.25 is not competing with cosmology — it is answering a question cosmology cannot pose: at the limit of all manifestation, is there a Person, or only equations? Devakī's prayer is empirical in its own register — it comes from a tradition of seers, not speculators. The diacritics and the names (Ananta Śeṣa, Ādi-puruṣa) are not decoration; they are precise designations of what the impersonalist tradition tries to flatten into 'consciousness' or 'the Absolute.' Honor the science where it speaks; do not let it silence the question it cannot answer.
Analogy

Consider an ocean: rivers pour into it, waves rise and crash, ice forms and melts. At the end of a kalpa, when the rivers cease and the waves still and the ice has all melted, only the ocean remains, unmoved and one. Ananta-Śeṣa, on whom Viṣṇu reclines after the cosmic dissolution, is exactly this remaining ocean — and Bhagavān reclines upon Himself.

Stories
Mārkaṇḍeya's vision of the floodwaters

The sage Mārkaṇḍeya (SB 12.9) was granted a vision of the cosmic dissolution. He saw the entire universe drowned in water; only a child-form of Bhagavān lay floating on a banyan leaf, the navel-lotus of all creation. Mārkaṇḍeya entered the child's mouth and saw all the worlds within.

This verse is recalling that state. Beyond duration of any Brahmā's life, beyond the dissolution of mahat-tattva itself, ekaḥ śiṣyate — He alone remains, names changing (Ananta, Śeṣa, Garbhodakaśāyī), but svarūpa the same.
