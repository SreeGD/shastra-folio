Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.27

No one in this material world has become free from the four principles birth, death, old age and disease, even by fleeing to various planets. But now that You have appeared, My Lord, death is fleeing in fear of You, and the living entities, having obtained shelter at Your lotus feet by Your mercy, are sleeping in full mental peace.
Purport

There are different categories of living entities, but everyone is afraid of death. The highest aim of the karmīs is to be promoted to the higher, heavenly planets, where the duration of life is very long. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (8.17) , sahasra-yuga-paryantam ahar yad brahmaṇo viduḥ: one day of Brahmā equals 1,000 yugas, and each yuga consists of 4,300,000 years. Similarly, Brahmā has a night of 1,000 times 4,300,000 years. In this way, we may calculate Brahmā’s month and year, but even Brahmā, who lives for millions and millions of years ( dvi-parārdha-kāla ), also must die. According to Vedic śāstra, the inhabitants of the higher planetary systems live for 10,000 years, and just as Brahmā’s day is calculated to equal 4,300,000,000 of our years, one day in the higher planetary systems equals six of our months. Karmīs, therefore, try for promotion to the higher planetary systems, but this cannot free them from death. In this material world, everyone from Brahmā to the insignificant ant must die. Therefore this world is called martya-loka. As Kṛṣṇa says in Bhagavad-gītā (8.16) , ābrahma-bhuvanāl lokāḥ punar āvartino ’rjuna: as long as one is within this material world, either on Brahmaloka or on any other loka within this universe, one must undergo the kāla-cakra of one life after another ( bhūtvā bhūtvā pralīyate ). But if one returns to the Supreme Personality of Godhead ( yad gatvā na nivartante ), one need not reenter the limits of time. Therefore, devotees who have taken shelter of the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord can sleep very peacefully with this assurance from the Supreme Personality of Godhead. As confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā (4.9) , tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti: after giving up the present body, a devotee who has understood Kṛṣṇa as He is need not return to this material world. The constitutional position for the living entity is eternity ( na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre, nityaḥ śāśvato ’yam ). Every living entity is eternal. But because of having fallen into this material world, one wanders within the universe, continually changing from one body to another. Caitanya Mahāprabhu says: brahmāṇḍa bhramite kona bhāgyavān jīva guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja (Cc. Madhya 19.151) Everyone is wandering up and down within this universe, but one who is sufficiently fortunate comes in contact with Kṛṣṇa consciousness, by the mercy of the spiritual master, and takes to the path of devotional service. Then one is assured of eternal life, with no fear of death. When Kṛṣṇa appears, everyone is freed from fear of death, yet Devakī felt, “We are still afraid of Kaṁsa, although You have appeared as our son.” She was more or less bewildered as to why this should be so, and she appealed to the Lord to free her and Vasudeva from this fear. In this connection, it may be noted that the moon is one of the heavenly planets. From the Vedic literature we understand that one who goes to the moon receives a life with a duration of ten thousand years in which to enjoy the fruits of pious activities. If our so-called scientists are going to the moon, why should they come back here? We must conclude without a doubt that they have never gone to the moon. To go to the moon, one must have the qualification of pious activities. Then one may go there and live. If one has gone to the moon, why should he return to this planet, where life is of a very short duration?

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
No one in this material world has become free from the four principles birth, death, old age and disease, even by fleeing to various planets. But now that You have appeared, My Lord, death is fleeing in fear of You, and the living entities, having obtained shelter at Your lotus feet by Your mercy, are sleeping in full mental peace.
Commentary

Since those who take shelter of your lotus feet become fearless, what to speak of those who meet you face to face. Wandering throughout the universe, one cannot become free from fear. By the devotion attained by great mercy (yadrcchaya), one attains your lotus feet which are abjam or Dhanvatari, Lord of medicine. By this medicine the person becomes healthy. (Medini says that abja means lotus, dhanvantari, conch and moon.) "O Lord, origin of all, though I am your devotee, and have been accepted by you as your mother, I am still afflicted by fear of Kamsa."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Taking shelter of your feet one becomes fearless. Thus one becomes happy. That is expressed in this verse. No one qualified with death (martyaḥ) can attain freedom from fear of death. You are the best of all people (ādya,) since you are the controller of death! Attaining your lotus feet, one becomes fearless (susthaḥ) and tranquil (śete). Death flees from your lotus feet or from the peaceful person. Mention of lotus feet suggests that the Lord is the highest goal. Just by his influence, fear disappears. There is another meaning. Today (adya), now that you have appeared, all people will become fearless and tranquil. Present tense is used for the near future. This is because death flees from you. The rest is the same. It is also suggested that Kṛṣṇa is Parabrahman. Her fearlessness is emphasized by what she expresses. Because you remain at pralaya and because of your control of time causing all destruction, you destroy death for those who surrender to you. How can it not be possible? In accordance with the common theme of her prayers, verse 24 does not show that she has attraction for impersonal Brahman.

Personal Guidance
No planet, no plan, no power escapes mṛtyu — only shelter at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet lets the soul sleep without fear.
Practical Applications
  • Name one 'higher planet' you are secretly chasing — promotion, longevity, security — and refuse to treat it as your refuge today.
  • Before sleep tonight, place the day and the body explicitly at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet and rest in the assurance that mṛtyu has no claim on the surrendered soul.
  • When fear of loss, illness, or death surfaces, stop and chant Kṛṣṇa's name once aloud instead of reaching for the next distraction.
Reflection Questions
  • To which 'higher planet' — promotion, security, longevity, even a spiritual achievement — am I still fleeing in the hope it will exempt me from death, instead of taking shelter at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet?
  • Where in my life do I sleep restlessly because I have not surrendered that domain to Him — and what would it look like to actually 'sleep peacefully' there, knowing mṛtyu has fled in His presence?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the next time you 'flee to another loka' — a new app, a new friend group, transferring colleges, swapping majors — to escape a fear you haven't named. Pause and ask: am I running toward something, or just away?
  • Before exams or a big interview this week, take two minutes to formally place the outcome at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet. Not as a magic-trick request to pass, but as the actual relinquishing of the fear that your worth equals your result.
  • Audit one feed (Instagram, LinkedIn, whatever) for 'higher-loka' promises — the influencer life, the FAANG offer, the perfect aesthetic. Name out loud that even those lokas end in death, and unfollow three accounts that quietly preach otherwise.
householder
  • When making the next big family decision — school district, job change, mortgage, medical choice for a parent — explicitly distinguish: am I optimizing for a longer or richer stay in martya-loka, or am I making a choice that also serves the soul of everyone involved? Write both answers down before deciding.
  • Build a household practice — even five minutes after dinner — where the family together offers the day's actions and worries to Kṛṣṇa. Children learn shelter (tvat-pādābjaṁ prāpya) by watching parents take it, not by being lectured about it.
  • Examine your insurance, savings, and retirement planning honestly. Provide responsibly — that is sva-dharma — but notice where the planning has quietly become a project of conquering death through spreadsheets. Cap the worry; do not cap the duty.
senior
  • Each morning, name one attachment — a possession, a role, an opinion about how children should live — that you are ready to loosen this week. Loosening now is gentler than having it pried from you later.
  • Mentor at least one younger person specifically on the difference between material compassion (smoothing their bodily life) and real compassion (pointing them to shelter). Your age gives you authority to say what a peer cannot.
  • Instead of fearing the body's decline, treat each new limitation as Kṛṣṇa's quiet instruction to transfer weight from the body's projects to the soul's shelter — more japa, more hearing, more remembrance, less rearranging of a house death will empty anyway.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating 'fearlessness of death' as a psychological mood to cultivate rather than the natural fruit of taking shelter at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet — manufacturing calm instead of surrendering.
  • Reading mṛtyur asmād apaiti as a promise of bodily safety or worldly insulation, and then feeling betrayed when difficulties still come, rather than understanding it as freedom from punar-janma.
  • Misusing 'sleeping in peace' as license for spiritual complacency — neglecting sādhana on the assumption that shelter has already been secured, when shelter is something to be taken daily, not banked.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī says death itself flees in fear of Kṛṣṇa, yet she still trembles before Kaṁsa. Where in your life do you intellectually accept Kṛṣṇa's protection while practically still organizing your day around fear? Be specific — name the fear, name the hedge.
  • Prabhupāda is blunt: the so-called scientists never went to the moon, because no one returns from a 10,000-year pious-life destination to this short-lived planet. Does this reading offend, amuse, or persuade you? What does your reaction reveal about whose authority — śāstra or modern consensus — actually governs your worldview?
  • The verse contrasts running between lokas (palāyan) with taking shelter at the lotus feet (tvat-pādābjaṁ prāpya). In the last month, where have you been running — switching jobs, cities, relationships, apps, identities — under the illusion that the next loka will solve what only surrender can solve?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A plays a recently retired professional who has just received a serious medical diagnosis and is panicking — booking experimental treatments, researching life-extension protocols, considering a move to a 'healthier' country. Student B plays a Gauḍīya friend who has been invited over for tea. Without preaching, dismissing the medical reality, or offering cheap comfort, Student B must help Student A see the difference between fleeing to another loka (palāyan) and taking shelter at the lotus feet (tvat-pādābjaṁ prāpya) — and must distinguish material compassion (just calm them about the body) from real compassion (serve the soul). Swap roles after seven minutes. Debrief: where did the 'helper' slip into platitudes, and where did real shelter get pointed to?

Objections & Responses
  • This verse is escapism. 'Death flees in fear of You' is wishful thinking — devotees still die of cancer, accidents, and old age just like everyone else. Calling this 'freedom from death' is a semantic trick.
    The verse never claims the devotee's body becomes immortal — martya-loka remains martya-loka. What it claims is that the soul, having taken shelter (tvat-pādābjaṁ prāpya), no longer reenters the kāla-cakra: tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti (BG 4.9). 'Death flees' means the death that matters — the forced rebirth into another body, another womb, another cycle of forgetting — loses its grip. The body still drops; the bondage does not return. That is not a semantic trick; it is a precise distinction between the death of the costume and the death of the wearer. Compare BG 2.21: how can one who knows the soul as imperishable kill or be killed?
  • Telling people 'sleep peacefully because Kṛṣṇa will save you' sounds like the same opiate Marx criticized — a way to make oppressed people stop fighting their actual conditions. Kaṁsa is still on the throne; why are we telling Devakī to relax?
    Look at the verse carefully: Devakī is not told to relax about Kaṁsa. She herself names her continuing fear in the very next breath, and the Lord does act — Kaṁsa is killed. Surrender in the Gauḍīya line is not passivity; it is the reordering of whom you ultimately depend on. The karmī fights Kaṁsa hoping a longer life on a higher loka will solve the death problem — and dies anyway. The devotee fights Kaṁsa too (Kṛṣṇa Himself does), but without the delusion that political victory is final victory. The peace Prabhupāda points to is not the peace of giving up the struggle; it is the peace of no longer mistaking the struggle for the goal. That is the opposite of opium — opium numbs you to reality; this wakes you up to which reality is actually permanent.
