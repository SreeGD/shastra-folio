Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.3.14

स एव स्वप्रकृत्येदं सृष्ट्वाग्रे त्रिगुणात्मकम् । तदनु त्वं ह्यप्रविष्ट: प्रविष्ट इव भाव्यसे ॥ १४ ॥

Important Words
  • sva-prakṛtyā — by His own energy
    Sva (own) + prakṛti (energy, nature). Bhagavān creates not from some external matter but by His own energy — a direct allusion to BG 9.10: 'mayādhyakṣeṇa prakṛtiḥ sūyate sa-carācaram'. Acintya-bhedābheda: prakṛti is His and is simultaneously identical-and-different from Him.
  • tri-guṇa-ātmakam — consisting of the three modes
    Tri (three) + guṇa (mode) + ātmaka (composed of). Sattva, rajas, tamas — the threefold rope of material nature. The world is constitutionally tri-guṇic; only Bhagavān and the liberated jīva are nirguṇa. Foundational Sāṅkhya-Vaiṣṇava taxonomy.
  • apraviṣṭaḥ — not having entered
    A- (privative) + praviṣṭa (entered). The paradox-word of the verse. Bhagavān has NOT actually entered the material creation, even though to ordinary perception He appears to have done so. This is the negative side of acintya-bhedābheda — the transcendent never collapses into the immanent.
  • praviṣṭaḥ iva — as if entered
    Praviṣṭa (entered) + iva ('as if', the simile particle). The positive side: He does APPEAR to enter; the līlā is real, the descent is real, but His pūrṇatva is not compromised. Iva marks the precise locus of acintya — both 'not entered' and 'as if entered' are true.
  • bhāvyase — are so understood
    Passive of √bhū — 'You are conceived/understood (as such)'. The verb places the entering in the perception of the perceiver, not in Bhagavān's nature. From our vantage He seems to enter; from His own He does not move. The verb itself encodes the doctrine.
My Lord, You are the same person who in the beginning created this material world by His personal external energy. After the creation of this world of three guṇas [sattva, rajas and tamas], You appear to have entered it, although in fact You have not.
Purport

In Bhagavad-gītā (7.4) the Supreme Personality of Godhead clearly explains: bhūmir āpo ’nalo vāyuḥ khaṁ mano buddhir eva ca ahaṅkāra itīyaṁ me bhinnā prakṛtir aṣṭadhā This material world of three modes of nature — sattva-guṇa, rajo-guṇa and tamo-guṇa — is a composition of earth, water, fire, air, mind, intelligence and false ego, all of which are energies coming from Kṛṣṇa, yet Kṛṣṇa, being always transcendental, is aloof from this material world. Those who are not in pure knowledge think that Kṛṣṇa is a product of matter and that His body is material like ours ( avajānanti māṁ mūḍhāḥ ). In fact, however, Kṛṣṇa is always aloof from this material world. In the Vedic literature, we find the creation described in relationship to Mahā-Viṣṇu. As stated in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.35) : eko ’py asau racayituṁ jagad-aṇḍa-koṭiṁ yac-chaktir asti jagad-aṇḍa-cayā yad-antaḥ aṇḍāntara-stha-paramāṇu-cayāntara-sthaṁ govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi “I worship the primeval Lord, Govinda, the original Personality of Godhead. By His partial plenary expansion as Mahā-Viṣṇu, He enters into material nature. Then He enters every universe as Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, and He enters all the elements, including every atom of matter, as Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu. Such manifestations of cosmic creation are innumerable, both in the universes and in the individual atoms.” Govinda is partially exhibited as antaryāmī, the Supersoul, who enters this material world ( aṇḍāntara-stha ) and who is also within the atom. The Brahma-saṁhitā (5.48) further says: yasyaika-niśvasita-kālam athāvalambya jīvanti loma-vilajā jagad-aṇḍa-nāthāḥ viṣṇur mahān sa iha yasya kalā-viśeṣo govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi This verse describes Mahā-Viṣṇu as a plenary expansion of Kṛṣṇa. Mahā-Viṣṇu lies on the Causal Ocean, and when He exhales, millions of brahmāṇḍas, or universes, come from the pores of His body. Then, when Mahā-Viṣṇu inhales, all these brahmāṇḍas disappear. Thus the millions of brahmāṇḍas controlled by the Brahmās and other demigods come and go in this material world through the breathing of Mahā-Viṣṇu. Foolish persons think that when Kṛṣṇa appears as the son of Vasudeva, He is limited like an ordinary child. But Vasudeva was aware that although the Lord had appeared as his son, the Lord had not entered Devakī’s womb and then come out. Rather, the Lord was always there. The Supreme Lord is all-pervading, present within and without. Praviṣṭa iva bhāvyase: He only seemed to have entered the womb of Devakī and to have now appeared as Vasudeva’s child. The expression of this knowledge by Vasudeva indicates that Vasudeva knew how these events took place. Vasudeva was certainly a devotee of the Lord in full knowledge, and we must learn from devotees like him. Bhagavad-gītā (4.34) therefore recommends: tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā upadekṣyanti te jñānaṁ jñāninas tattva-darśinaḥ “Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized soul can impart knowledge unto you because he has seen the truth.” Vasudeva begot the Supreme Personality of Godhead, yet he was in full knowledge of how the Supreme Lord appears and disappears. He was therefore tattva-darśī, a seer of the truth, because he personally saw how the Supreme Absolute Truth appeared as his son. Vasudeva was not in ignorance, thinking that because the Supreme Godhead had appeared as his son, the Lord had become limited. The Lord is unlimitedly existing and all-pervading, inside and outside. Thus there is no question of His appearance or disappearance.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
My Lord, You are the same person who in the beginning created this material world by His personal external energy. After the creation of this world of three guṇas [sattva, rajas and tamas], You appear to have entered it, although in fact You have not.
Commentary

"O father, you know that I have been born with limitation in your house. But do you know the truth about me?" To destroy this doubt, Vasudeva expresses his realization. Sa eva means "That form of yours about which I previously spoke, that is you." "You create this universe by your material energy and seem to enter and not enter simultaneously. Because you are seen inside the universe, we cannot say that you do not enter. Because you are seen outside the universe, we cannot say you do not enter there. You seemed to have entered my house, as you are also present everywhere. You seemed have been born, but we also cannot say that, because you are always existing. Thus I know that Kamsa cannot do anything to you, as you are all pervading."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

In order to hide the fact that the Lord has become his son, in four verses, out of humility he indicates that the Lord does not enter the world even though contrary statements have already been made. “Devakī then kept within herself the Supreme Lord, the cause of all causes, the foundation of the entire cosmos.” (SB 10.2.19) “O Devakī, by your good fortune and ours, the Supreme Lord with all his plenary portions, such as Baladeva, is now within your womb.” (SB 10.2.41) Because you are pervading everywhere, you make an appearance but you do not enter. But the real meaning is “I have attained you as the Lord, and also as my son.” According to my previously stated realizations you are Svayam Bhagavān. Because of this, you do not enter matter. It is impossible. “You do not enter” has the sense of “being unbearable” as in the verb parājī which means “to conquer and to find unbearable” as in parājerasoḍhaḥ. (Pāṇini 1.43.26) In spite of this, you have certainly entered the womb of Devakī because of your nature, because you are controlled by prema, and because you have inconceivable śakti (sva-prakṛtyā). An example is given. Previously as Kāraṇārṇava-śāyī, you created the universe of three guṇas and then as Garbodhakaśāyī, you entered into the universe. Though this is not completely applicable to the present situation, since you entered the universe, why can you not enter Devakī’s womb? Though you entered the universe because of the bhakti of Brahmā and others, this cannot be prema, since the universe is gross matter. And you do not touch the universe since it is made of the three guṇas. Thus you are indifferent to it. Thus, though you have entered, it is as if you did not enter. This is proper since matter is opposite of your nature. My case is similar. I have attained you as my son, since you entered her womb in your spiritual form. You have not entered but seemed to have entered her. As you have entered elsewhere, in that manner you have done in this case also (bhāvyase). It is said: na cāntar na bahir yasya na pūrvaṁ nāpi cāparam: the Lord is not inside or outside and does not have past or future. (SB 10.9.13) Though you are the supreme truth, you show your form within a small area because of prema for your devotees and by that, you give them bliss. Even those who experience Brahman derive bliss from this form. The only cause is your inconceivable śakti which acts like a jewel, mantra or special herb. Because of that śakti, instigated by your devotees’ prema, you enter her womb while absorbed in bliss, engaging in pastimes like a human, acting like her son. Thus the Lord fulfilled is promise to his devotee. It should be remembered that Devakī did austerities even in this life to gain the Lord as he son. Now she attained him. This is mentioned in Viṣṇu-dharmottara.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Just as you created the universe made of three guṇas and entered it, so you seem to have entered Devakī as her son by your inconceivable energy, yet you have not entered. You become Devakī’s son. Though you create the universe (eva), out your nature of affection (sva-prakṛtyā), you seemed to have entered my house or Devakī’s womb. The word iva suggests that Lord actually does not enter by meditation. Or iva is just a figure of speech. Though (hi) you do not enter after creating the universe (anu), you seem to enter.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa creates this tri-guṇa world by His own energy and only seems to enter it — He is always within, without, and untouched by matter.
Practical Applications
  • Before reacting to today's hardest moment, pause and remember Kṛṣṇa is already present there as antaryāmī — act as if seen by Him.
  • Refuse the materialist reflex of treating Kṛṣṇa as a product of matter; affirm aloud in your morning sādhana that He is transcendental, not limited like our bodies.
  • Approach a senior devotee today with a real question, offering praṇāma and service, in the mood of Vasudeva learning tattva from a tattva-darśī.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I treating Kṛṣṇa as 'limited' to a particular form, role, or moment — failing to see Him as simultaneously within and beyond every situation I face today?
  • Vasudeva saw the truth as a tattva-darśī because he had submissively inquired and served — which living devotee am I refusing to approach in that posture, and what pride is keeping me at a distance?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam or interview, sit for two minutes and reflect: the Lord pervades this room as antaryāmī and is also untouched by your anxiety — your nerves are happening in the field of guṇas, not in the soul. Then walk in and write the paper.
  • Notice how social media trains you to treat people as bodies (likes, looks, follower counts). Pick one classmate this week whom you usually scroll past and speak to them as a soul — ask one real question, and listen for the answer with the awareness that Kṛṣṇa is present within them as Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu.
  • When friend-group drama hits — gossip, breakups, betrayal — practice praviṣṭa iva: you are in the situation but not of it. The drama is in the three guṇas; you are not. Don't fake aloofness, but refuse to identify your soul with the latest group-chat crisis.
householder
  • When making a major family decision (school for a child, a job change, a move), explicitly remember that you are a steward, not the proprietor — Kṛṣṇa is the antaryāmī within every member of your household, and you are arranging things on His behalf, not yours.
  • In your work this week, notice the pull to think 'I made this happen.' Counter it deliberately: the result came from a field of energies (prakṛti) that Kṛṣṇa set into motion. You acted; you did not cause. Apply this especially to money — receive income as prasāda, not as conquest.
  • Raise your children with the explicit teaching, in age-appropriate language, that they are not the body. Vasudeva is the model: he begot the Lord and yet was tattva-darśī. Approach your children's milestones with the same dual vision — celebrate the body's growth while never forgetting the soul within.
senior
  • Sit each morning with the verse's image of Mahā-Viṣṇu exhaling and inhaling universes. Your one body — entered and now preparing to be left — is on that same scale. Practice releasing identification with this body before the body releases you.
  • Identify one possession, one role, or one relationship to which you are still secretly clinging as 'mine,' and consciously offer it back to Kṛṣṇa this week. The Lord never actually entered the womb; you never actually owned any of this. Loosen the grip while you still can.
  • Mentor a younger devotee or family member as Vasudeva-as-tattva-darśī, not as a sentimental elder. Teach them by your bearing that you know the soul is unchanged through the body's transitions — let your steadiness in the face of aging and approaching death be the lesson, more than your words.
Common Pitfalls
  • Drifting into impersonalism: using 'He has not really entered' as evidence that Kṛṣṇa is some abstract all-pervading principle, rather than seeing that the same personal Lord is fully present everywhere as Paramātmā while remaining the supreme Person with form, name, and pastimes.
  • Misreading 'aloof from this material world' as license for personal aloofness — withdrawing from sva-dharma and responsibility under the banner of 'transcendence,' when the verse is about Kṛṣṇa's nature, not a prescription for the sādhaka to detach from his duties.
  • Trying to 'figure out' how Kṛṣṇa enters and does not enter through one's own speculation, instead of receiving this knowledge praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā — the very method the purport prescribes — and thereby remaining a mūḍha who 'avajānanti' the Lord even while studying the verse.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva sees Kṛṣṇa as his son and simultaneously as the all-pervading Lord who never actually 'entered' Devakī's womb. Share with your partner a relationship in your own life — a parent, child, spouse, teacher — where you only see the body and forget the soul. What concretely changes if you take seriously that the Lord is present within and without that person, and you are not?
  • The verse insists Kṛṣṇa creates the world of three guṇas yet remains aloof from it (praviṣṭa iva — only seemingly entered). In small groups, debate honestly: is this a cop-out by theologians who don't want to deal with the problem of evil, or is there something experientially real about a cause that is not contaminated by its effects? Bring an example from your own work or family life where you have or have not been able to act without becoming entangled.
  • Prabhupāda quotes BG 4.34 — 'approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively, render service.' Vasudeva himself models this tattva-darśī posture. Where in your current life are you trying to figure out tattva by your own reasoning instead of by praṇipāta, paripraśna, sevā? Push back against each other: is submissive inquiry actually compatible with modern critical thinking, or are we kidding ourselves?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a hospital chaplain visiting a dying patient (played by Student B) who says, 'My body is everything I am. When it goes, I go. Don't give me your scriptures.' Student A must respond not with arguments about the soul in the abstract, but from the verse's specific insight — that the Lord Himself is praviṣṭa iva, only seemingly entered into matter, and that the patient's situation mirrors this. Student A may NOT quote Sanskrit at the patient or lecture; they must speak as a fellow practitioner. After 4 minutes, swap roles. Then debrief: where did the 'real compassion vs. material compassion' distinction surface? Where did it collapse into platitudes?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just theological gymnastics. Saying Kṛṣṇa 'enters but does not enter' is a contradiction dressed up in Sanskrit. Either He's in the world or He isn't. You can't have both.
    The contradiction dissolves once you stop assuming the Lord is bound by the same containment-logic as a material object. A fire heats a room without the fire becoming the room; the sun illumines a sewer without the sun becoming polluted. The Brahma-saṁhitā describes Govinda as simultaneously in every aṇḍa and every paramāṇu while remaining in His own dhāma — this is acintya-bhedābheda, inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference. We do not resolve it by logic alone; we resolve it by hearing from a tattva-darśī like Vasudeva, who directly saw it. The contradiction is in our framework, not in the Lord.
  • Why insist Kṛṣṇa is a person? Saying 'the Supreme Personality of Godhead' sounds anthropomorphic and provincial — surely the deeper truth is the impersonal Absolute, and personalism is a concession for those who cannot grasp it.
    The Gauḍīya line reverses that hierarchy precisely. Brahman, the impersonal effulgence, is the bodily radiance of the Person — not the other way around. The verse itself is addressed by Vasudeva to a specific person: 'You are the same person who created.' If the ultimate were impersonal, Vasudeva's address would be a mistake, and so would the entire Bhāgavatam. Personalism is not a concession to weakness; impersonalism is a partial realization that stops short of the fullness. Kṛṣṇa exhibits Himself as antaryāmī, as Mahā-Viṣṇu, as Devakī's son — these are not reductions of an impersonal absolute, they are expansions of the original Person.
Analogy

The sun seems to enter a room through a window, but truly the sun never enters anywhere — its light pervades the room because the sun is high overhead, and the window only frames what was already present. Vasudeva's verse is exactly this: Kṛṣṇa appears to enter creation, yet by His own nature He is everywhere already; the 'entering' is darśana, not displacement.

Stories
Brahmā's repeated 'I am the creator' delusion

In SB 2.9, Brahmā mistakenly thought himself the original creator, until he heard the syllables 'ta-pa' and realized he himself was being created and instructed by Bhagavān. The recognition that Viṣṇu is the real source — Brahmā only the instrument — is precisely Vasudeva's recognition here.

The Bhāgavata is fond of this teaching loop: created beings repeatedly mistake themselves for first cause; revelation corrects them by pointing to the Lord who appears to enter the creation but never actually does.
