Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.39

न तेऽभवस्येश भवस्य कारणं विना विनोदं बत तर्कयामहे । भवो निरोध: स्थितिरप्यविद्यया कृता यतस्त्वय्यभयाश्रयात्मनि ॥ ३९ ॥

O Supreme Lord, You are not an ordinary living entity appearing in this material world as a result of fruitive activities. Therefore Your appearance or birth in this world has no other cause than Your pleasure potency. Similarly, the living entities, who are part of You, have no cause for miseries like birth, death and old age, except when these living entities are conducted by Your external energy.
Purport

As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (15.7) , mamaivāṁśo jīva-loke jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ: the living entities are parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord, and thus they are qualitatively one with the Lord. We can understand that when the Supreme Lord appears or disappears as an incarnation, there is no other cause than His pleasure potency. We cannot force the Supreme Personality of Godhead to appear. As He says in Bhagavad-gītā (4.7) : yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham “Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion — at that time I descend Myself.” When there is a need to diminish a burden created by the demons, the Supreme Godhead can do it in many ways because He has multifarious energies. There is no need for Him to come as an incarnation, since He is not forced to do anything like ordinary living entities. The living entities come to this material world in the spirit of enjoyment, but because they want to enjoy without Kṛṣṇa ( kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha haiyā bhoja-vāñchā kare ), they suffer birth, death, old age and disease under the control of the illusory energy. When the Supreme Personality of Godhead appears, however, no such causes are involved; His descent is an act of His pleasure potency. We should always remember this distinction between the Lord and the ordinary living entity and not uselessly argue that the Lord cannot come. There are philosophers who do not believe in the Lord’s incarnation and who ask, “Why should the Supreme Lord come?” But the answer is, “Why should He not come? Why should He be controlled by the desire of the living entity?” The Lord is free to do whatever He likes. Therefore this verse says, vinā vinodaṁ bata tarkayāmahe. It is only for His pleasure that He comes although He does not need to come. When the living entities come to this world for material enjoyment, they are entangled in karma and karma-phala by the Lord’s illusory energy. But if one seeks shelter at the Lord’s lotus feet, one is again situated in his original, liberated state. As stated here, kṛtā yatas tvayy abhayāśrayātmani: one who seeks shelter at the lotus feet of the Lord is always fearless. Because we are dependent on the Supreme Personality of Godhead, we should give up the idea that without Kṛṣṇa we can enjoy freedom in this material world. This idea is the reason we have become entangled. Now it is our duty to seek shelter again at the Lord’s lotus feet. This shelter is described as abhaya, or fearless. Since Kṛṣṇa is not subject to birth, death, old age or disease, and since we are part and parcel of Kṛṣṇa, we also are not subject to birth, death, old age and disease, but we have become subject to these illusory problems because of our forgetfulness of Kṛṣṇa and our position as His eternal servants ( jīvera ‘svarūpa’ haya-kṛṣṇera ‘nitya-dāsa’ ). Therefore, if we practice devotional service by always thinking of the Lord, always glorifying Him and always chanting about Him, as described in text 37 ( śṛṇvan gṛṇan saṁsmarayaṁś ca cintayan ), we will be reinstated in our original, constitutional position and thus be saved. The demigods, therefore, encouraged Devakī not to fear Kaṁsa, but to think of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who was already within her womb.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Supreme Lord, You are not an ordinary living entity appearing in this material world as a result of fruitive activities. Therefore Your appearance or birth in this world has no other cause than Your pleasure potency. Similarly, the living entities, who are part of You, have no cause for miseries like birth, death and old age, except when these living entities are conducted by Your external energy.
Commentary

We have said that you have appeared for protecting us, but that is only a pretense. You perform your birth, and activities independently with your own purpose. Though you are unborn (abhavasya) you appear in the world. We cannot determine the cause, because (yatah) the creation, maintenance and destruction (bhavo nirodhah sthitih) are carried out by maya (avidyaya) which takes shelter of you (tvayi asrayatmani) . The lord is called abhaya or instilling fearlessness, because by remembering him the fear of the demons like Kamsa disappears. Therefore there is no necessity of the Lord personally appearing to kill Kamsa.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Bata expresses joy. Even your devotees in this world have no birth, what to speak of you. Śrīdhaṛa explains as follows. We cannot speculate about the cause of your birth in this world, except that it is your pastime. O Lord who does not take shelter of fear! O eternally liberated Lord! What to speak of your having no birth, the birth, maintenance and destruction created by ignorance do not exist in the jīvas dedicated to you. Or, O fearless Lord! Since your only purpose is pastimes, the creation, maintenance and destruction exist eternally by māyā, composes of vidyā and avidyā, which exist under your shelter (āśrayātmani tvayi). You do not even make effort personally for the maintenance which is included in that list. We cannot infer any cause for your personal appearance except your desire for pastimes. Only one who has fear must make effort to destroy sinful persons. There is a great difference between pastimes of creation and destruction of the universe and the pastimes carried out by your svarūpa for enjoyment. The wives of Kāliya say avyākṛta-vihārāya: please let us offer our obeisances unto you, whose pastimes are inconceivable. (SB 10.16.47) Śukadeva is described as ajita-rucira-līlākṛṣṭa-sāraḥ: his heart was attracted to the sweet pastimes of Kṛṣṇa. (SB 12.12.69)

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Bata expresses joy. You have no birth (abhavasya), or you arrange so that your devotees or all people have no birth. We can only speculate the cause of your appearance. It is only your play. Even the killing of the demons to relieve the burden of the earth is only your play. Or “How can appearing to relieve the earth of its burden by killing demons with effort be considered play?” This verse answers. It is only your play because creation and destruction take place by māyā (avidyayā), which exists within you. It is your śakti. Killing demons is one of its goals. Or “I have appeared to relieve the earth of its burden on your request. That is what I am doing. Why speak of play?” You actually appear for pastimes in Gokula. Or “There are great pastimes of creation of the universe. How can this be called play?” Creation and destruction take place by your śakti of māyā for the jīvas (ātmani). It is real, but temporary. But your forms and pastimes are eternity, knowledge and bliss.

Concepts
Jiva
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa descends only by His pleasure potency; we suffer birth and death only by māyā — take shelter at His lotus feet and be fearless.
Practical Applications
  • Notice each fear that surfaces today and silently re-take shelter (śaraṇāgati) at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet as the only abhaya-āśraya.
  • Refuse the lie that you can enjoy independently of Kṛṣṇa — name one 'independent enjoyment' you are chasing and offer it back to Him before acting.
  • Practice text 37's threefold sādhana today: hear something about Kṛṣṇa, glorify Him aloud once, and remember Him at a fixed midday pause.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still arguing — like the philosophers Prabhupāda names — that Kṛṣṇa 'should not' descend, by quietly insisting my situation is too ordinary, too broken, or too late for His personal intervention?
  • Which specific fear (of failure, of aging, of someone like Kaṁsa in my own life) am I refusing to hand over to abhayāśraya — the fearless shelter — and what does my refusal cost me each day?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before the next exam or audition or interview, sit for two minutes and explicitly notice: this fear in my chest is the body's fear, not mine. Then walk in and do the work — not pretending to be calm, but knowing the fear is not the soul.
  • Pick one social-media account whose posts spike your anxiety or envy this week, and unfollow it today. Each scroll is feeding the illusion that your value is in the body and its image; abhaya-śaraṇa cannot coexist with that feed.
  • When a friend group pressures you toward something you know is bahirmukha — a party, a substance, a cruelty dressed up as a joke — practice naming Kṛṣṇa silently before you respond. Make the small refusals now; they build the muscle for the large ones later.
householder
  • When you next make a major family decision — a job change, a school for your child, a financial commitment — write at the top of the page: 'I am not the cause; Kṛṣṇa's pleasure is.' Then decide. Notice how the grip of needing to control the outcome loosens.
  • Sit with your spouse this week and name one fear each of you is carrying about the children's future — health, marriage, success. Do not problem-solve; instead, agree on one practice (a chanting round together, reading one verse before dinner) that places the children, in your hearts, at His abhaya lotus feet rather than only in your hands.
  • When financial worry strikes — a bill, a market dip, a layoff rumor — interrupt the spiral with this verse's logic: birth, death, old age, disease are not solved by money; the absence of money simply makes their illusion harder to ignore. Then act practically, but from śaraṇa, not from terror.
senior
  • Each morning, name aloud one attachment — a possession, a role, a grievance — and offer it back to Kṛṣṇa. Aging is the natural unhooking; cooperate with it instead of resisting. The vinoda of the Lord includes the gentle stripping that makes śaraṇa possible.
  • Identify one younger person — child, grandchild, mentee — who is operating bahirmukha, chasing enjoyment without Kṛṣṇa, and who would actually receive a word from you. Do not preach. Tell them one specific thing this verse has meant in your own life, especially as you face the body's decline. Real karuṇā is giving them what avoids the next birth, not just what comforts this one.
  • Write your own death — one page, in plain language: where you hope to be, what you hope to be chanting, who you hope to have already forgiven. Read it once a week. The verse's promise of abhaya is not for the abstract future; it is rehearsed now, in this body, while there is still time.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'Kṛṣṇa appears by His pleasure potency' as a remote cosmological fact, and missing that the same verse hands me a personal escape route from birth, death, and old age through śaraṇāgati right now.
  • Confusing the verse's compassion (karuṇā) with material sympathy — pitying people for their bodily distress while never offering them the one thing that actually dissolves avidyā, namely hearing, glorifying, and remembering Kṛṣṇa (śṛṇvan gṛṇan saṁsmarayaṁś ca cintayan).
  • Treating 'I am part and parcel of Kṛṣṇa, therefore not subject to birth and death' as a license for impersonal self-affirmation ('I am eternal consciousness'), instead of as a call to re-enter the relational position of nitya-dāsa — eternal servant of the Person.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda writes that Kṛṣṇa's descent has 'no other cause than His pleasure potency,' while ours is forced by karma under māyā. Share with your partner one area of your life right now where you have been pretending you came here freely — that you 'chose' this job, this relationship, this struggle — when honestly, you were pushed by avidyā. Where does this distinction sting, and where does it free you?
  • The verse calls Kṛṣṇa abhayāśraya — the shelter that is fearless. Name one fear you carried into the room today. Then debate: is taking shelter at His lotus feet a real solution to that specific fear, or does it sound like spiritual bypassing of a problem you should actually solve materially? Push back on each other; do not let the group settle on a polite consensus.
  • Prabhupāda says the demigods encouraged Devakī 'not to fear Kaṁsa, but to think of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who was already within her womb.' Discuss: is this a recipe for passivity in the face of real tyranny — Kaṁsas in our own families, workplaces, governments — or is it the only sane response? Where would you draw the line between abhaya-śaraṇa and naive inaction?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a devotee whose elderly mother has just been diagnosed with a serious illness; A is terrified — of the loss, of the medical bills, of facing mortality in the family. Student B is a slightly more senior devotee-friend who has been asked to listen and respond. A must voice the fear honestly (not piously). B must respond using only the logic of this verse: that birth, death, old age, and disease belong to the jīva under avidyā, that Kṛṣṇa as abhayāśraya is the actual shelter, and that real karuṇā here is to help A's mother remember Kṛṣṇa — not to manufacture material reassurance. Switch roles after five minutes. Then debrief as a group: where did B slip into platitudes? Where did B's words actually meet A's fear?

Objections & Responses
  • This is convenient theology. Kṛṣṇa gets to descend 'just for fun' (vinodam) while we are crushed under birth, death, old age, and disease. If He is truly compassionate, why does He not simply lift avidyā from everyone? The asymmetry feels like a cosmic insider deal.
    The asymmetry is not Kṛṣṇa's doing — it is ours. He has not imposed avidyā on the jīva; the jīva turned away (kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha haiyā bhoja-vāñchā kare) wanting to enjoy independently. Kṛṣṇa preserves our freedom to make that choice — that is real respect, not cruelty. And He does lift avidyā the moment we seek shelter; the door is not locked from His side. His descent in līlā is not a privilege withheld from us; it is precisely the demonstration that the spiritual platform exists and is reachable. To call it an 'insider deal' is to keep insisting the servant should be equal to the master — which is the very ahaṅkāra that put us here.
  • 'Take shelter and become fearless' sounds like a psychological trick — repeating a mantra until anxiety subsides. A modern reader sees this as cope, not metaphysics. How is abhaya-śaraṇa different from any other coping mechanism?
    A coping mechanism manages the symptom while leaving the cause untouched; abhaya-śaraṇa removes the cause. The fear of birth, death, old age, and disease is not free-floating anxiety — it is the accurate signal of a soul mis-identified with a perishable body. Therapy, breathwork, even Stoic resignation can dim the signal; only re-establishing the eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa addresses what the signal is pointing at. Notice the verse's grammar: tvayy abhayāśrayātmani — the shelter is in Him, not in your own mental adjustment. That is the precise difference. A cope locates the fix in your nervous system; bhakti locates it in a real, personal Other. Test it the way Prabhupāda asks — śṛṇvan, gṛṇan, saṁsmaran, cintayan, daily, for a year — and judge whether it resembles a trick.
