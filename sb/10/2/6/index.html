Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.6

भगवानपि विश्वात्मा विदित्वा कंसजं भयम् । यदूनां निजनाथानां योगमायां समादिशत् ॥ ६ ॥

Important Words
  • bhagavān — the Supreme Personality of Godhead
    Bhagavān is the technical Gauḍīya term for the fullest realization of the Absolute — possessor of the six opulences (ṣaḍ-aiśvarya). The verse opens the rescue scene with the highest name precisely because rescue is His most personal act.
  • viśvātmā — the Supersoul of everyone
    Viśva-ātmā — the soul of all. The same Lord who is bhagavān in His full form is paramātmā in every heart. This is acintya-bhedābheda in a single word: He is the universal indweller without ceasing to be the personal Lord.
  • kaṁsa - jam — produced by Kaṁsa
    Fear has a cause; the Bhāgavatam never speaks of generic anxiety. Devotees fear something specific (Kaṁsa), and the Lord answers that specific fear with a specific deputation. Pastoral theology is concrete, never vague.
  • nija - nāthānām — of those who have accepted Him as their Lord
    Nija-nātha — 'one's own Lord.' The Gauḍīyas read this as the technical posture of śaraṇāgati: the devotee who has accepted Kṛṣṇa as goptā (protector) earns the active protection described in this verse. Acceptance is the trigger.
  • yoga - māyām — Yoga-māyā, the spiritual potency
    Yoga-māyā is the internal energy that arranges the Lord's pastimes — distinct from mahā-māyā, the external illusory energy. The whole rest of the chapter (and Canto 10) is choreographed by her; here she receives her first order.
To protect the Yadus, His personal devotees, from Kaṁsa’s attack, the Personality of Godhead, Viśvātmā, the Supreme Soul of everyone, ordered Yoga-māyā as follows.
Purport

The words bhagavān api viśvātmā viditvā kaṁsajaṁ bhayam are commented upon by Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī. Bhagavān svayam is Kṛṣṇa ( kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam ). He is Viśvātmā, the original Supersoul of everyone, because His plenary portion expands as the Supersoul. This is confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā (13.3) : kṣetra-jñaṁ cāpi māṁ viddhi sarva-kṣetreṣu bhārata. Lord Kṛṣṇa is the kṣetra jña, or Supersoul, of all living entities. He is the original source of all expansions of the Personality of Godhead. There are hundreds and thousands of plenary expansions of Viṣṇu, such as Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha and Vāsudeva, but here in this material world, the Viśvātmā, the Supersoul for all living entities, is Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (18.61) , īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānāṁ hṛd-deśe ’rjuna tiṣṭhati: “The Supreme Lord is situated in the heart of all living entities, O Arjuna.” Kṛṣṇa is actually Viśvātmā by His plenary expansion as viṣṇu-tattva, yet because of His affection for His devotees, He acts as Supersoul to give them directions ( sarvasya cāhaṁ hṛdi sanniviṣṭo mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṁ ca ). The affairs of the Supersoul pertain to Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, but Kṛṣṇa took compassion on Devakī, His devotee, because He understood her fear of Kaṁsa’s persecution. A pure devotee is always fearful of material existence. No one knows what will happen next, for one may have to change his body at any moment ( tathā dehāntara-prāptiḥ ). Knowing this fact, a pure devotee acts in such a way that he will not have his life spoiled by being obliged to accept another body and undergo the tribulations of material existence. This is bhayam, or fear. Bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syāt ( Bhāg. 11.2.37 ). This fear is due to material existence. Properly speaking, everyone should always be alert and fearful of material existence, but although everyone is prone to be affected by the ignorance of material existence, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is always alert to the protection of His devotees. Kṛṣṇa is so kind and affectionate toward His devotees that He helps them by giving them the intelligence by which to exist in this material world without forgetting Him even for a moment. The Lord says: teṣām evānukampārtham aham ajñāna-jaṁ tamaḥ nāśayāmy ātma-bhāvastho jñāna-dīpena bhāsvatā “Out of compassion for them, I, dwelling in their hearts, destroy with the shining lamp of knowledge the darkness born of ignorance.” ( Bg. 10.11 ) The word yoga means “link.” Any system of yoga is an attempt to reconnect our broken relationship with the Supreme Personality of Godhead. There are different types of yoga, of which bhakti-yoga is the best. In other yoga systems, one must undergo various processes before attaining perfection, but bhakti-yoga is direct. The Lord says in Bhagavad-gītā (6.47) : yoginām api sarveṣāṁ mad-gatenāntarātmanā śraddhāvān bhajate yo māṁ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ “Of all yogīs, he who always abides in Me with great faith, worshiping Me in transcendental loving service, is most intimately united with Me in yoga and is the highest of all.” For the bhakti-yogī, a human body is guaranteed in his next existence, as stated by Lord Kṛṣṇa ( śucīnāṁ śrīmatāṁ gehe yoga-bhraṣṭo ’bhijāyate ). Yoga-māyā is the spiritual potency of the Lord. Out of affection for His devotees, the Lord always stays in spiritual touch with them, although otherwise His Māyā potency is so strong that she bewilders even exalted demigods like Brahmā. Therefore the Lord’s potency is called Yoga-māyā. Since the Lord is Viśvātmā, He immediately ordered Yoga-māyā to give protection to Devakī.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
To protect the Yadus, His personal devotees, from Kaṁsa's attack, the Personality of Godhead, Viśvātmā, the Supreme Soul of everyone, ordered Yogamāyā as follows.
Commentary

Krsna, svayam bhagavan, gave orders to Yogamaya, who is the fifth of his nine spiritual energies headed by Vimala.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Svayam Bhagavān, Kṛṣṇa, the aṁśī of all forms of God (viśvātmā), knew that the Yadus feared Kaṁsa. The word api is used to distinguish Kṛṣṇa who speaks at this time from Kṣīrodakaśāyī (who is his aṁśa) who spoke to Brahmā previously. He knew of their suffering without having to be informed of it. Therefore he spoke to Yogamāyā to relieve the Yadus of fear. He ordered her in a stimulating way (samyak ādiśat). Why? They took refuge in him alone (nija-nāthānām). Therefore out of affection for them he did this. Or the phrase can mean that they were the best (nāthānām) of all his devotees (nija). Yoga means a special śakti of the Lord and it is called māyā because it has the ability of bewilder even Brahmā. It is superior to the śakti which causes the material universe (jagat-kāraṇa-śakti). Another name of Yogamāyā is Ekānaṁśā. Another meaning of the sentence by interpreting the word api is that it indicates the supreme position of Bhagavān in comparison with māyā. Since he was in the supreme position he ordered Yogamāyā.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The word api is used to distinguish Kṛṣṇa, who speaks at this time, from Kṣīrodakaśāyī (who is his aṁśa) who spoke to Brahmā previously. He knew because he was all pervading (viśvātmā). Or knowing the Yadu’s fear because he was antaryāmī, he acted to destroy their fear without being asked. He ordered Yogamāyā in a stimulating way (sam ādiśat). Why? The lords of the Yadus were his relatives (nija-nāthānām). He was affectionate to his devotees. Or because he was the lord (viśvātmā), he gave orders to Yogamāyā, who was dependent on him. Yoga means a special śakti of the Lord. It is called māyā because it can bewilder even Brahmā.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa, Viśvātmā, dispatches Yoga-māyā to shield His devotees — surrender keeps you inside the circle of His personal protection.
Practical Applications
  • Name one fear of material existence (bhayam) you carry today, and hand it to Kṛṣṇa in your morning prayer instead of carrying it alone.
  • Refuse the impersonalist drift: address Kṛṣṇa by name when you chant or pray today, not 'the universe' or 'the Self'.
  • Notice one moment today when intelligence arises to keep you from forgetting Him — thank Him for it as the Supersoul's direct help.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life have I been trying to protect myself through my own arrangements, instead of crying out to Kṛṣṇa as Devakī did and trusting that He will dispatch Yoga-māyā on my behalf?
  • What 'Kaṁsa' am I refusing to name as a real fear — the next forced change of body, situation, or relationship — and how has my refusal to face it kept me from sincere shelter-taking?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When exam season or a friend-group betrayal triggers panic, name it as bhayam — fear born of identifying with the temporary body and its situation — and chant Hare Kṛṣṇa for ten minutes before opening any social media. Test whether the shaking actually subsides; do not take it on faith.
  • Pick one peer you secretly compete with and, for one week, treat them as another spark of the same Viśvātmā that lives in you — text them a genuine note when they win something. Notice what dies in you (ahaṅkāra) and what stays (you).
  • On Instagram you are constantly invited to perform a 'self' to be protected by likes. Devakī's protection came from yoga-māyā, not from posting her crisis. For one week, do not post anything about your inner life — bring those things instead to your japa and to one trusted devotee. Watch whether your interior gets stronger or weaker.
householder
  • Your kids and spouse are not yours; they are souls Kṛṣṇa has placed in your care, the way Devakī was placed under Yoga-māyā's protection. Once a week, before a major family decision (school, money, move), explicitly pray: 'Kṛṣṇa, You are Viśvātmā in their hearts too — guide me to serve, not to control.' Then act, and release the result.
  • The Kaṁsa in a householder's life is usually financial fear — job loss, market crash, kids' education costs. When it grips you, recall bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syāt: this fear is structural to material existence, not a sign you have failed. Do the next concrete responsible action (update the resume, call the advisor) and offer the outcome to Kṛṣṇa at your evening sādhana. Refuse to let the fear extend past the work it is meant to fuel.
  • Make your home a place where Yoga-māyā can act: install regular family kīrtana, prasāda, and reading — even fifteen minutes daily. Children raised in this atmosphere are protected in ways no school district or savings account can match, because Kṛṣṇa Himself takes interest in homes that take interest in Him.
senior
  • At 60+ you have already 'transitioned through' infant, child, youth, householder bodies — the soul is unchanged, as the dhīra knows. Sit each morning and feel that continuity; let it weaken your grip on this current body's preferences (food, comfort, routine) one notch at a time.
  • Kṛṣṇa protected Devakī because she was His devotee; the protection you most need now is not from illness or loneliness but from forgetting Him at the moment of death (ante nārāyaṇa-smṛtiḥ). Reorganize your day so that japa, hearing Bhāgavatam, and the holy name are the spine — and meals, errands, and even family calls are arranged around that spine, not the reverse.
  • Your real legacy is not the inheritance, the photo albums, or the stories — it is the bhakti you transmit. Identify one younger person (grandchild, neighbor, junior colleague) and, without preaching, share one verse, one prasādam meal, one kīrtana evening per month. Yoga-māyā can use even a small offering through a sincere elder to plant a seed that protects a soul for lifetimes.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Yoga-māyā as an impersonal force or 'cosmic energy' rather than the Lord's personal spiritual potency, dispatched by Kṛṣṇa Himself out of affection for His devotees.
  • Concluding that because Kṛṣṇa is Viśvātmā in everyone's heart, He is equally close to all — and therefore neglecting the specific intimacy He reserves for surrendered devotees like Devakī, who actually receive His protective intervention.
  • Misreading the verse's bhayam as worldly anxiety to be soothed away, when Prabhupāda explicitly says a pure devotee should remain alert and fearful of material existence (bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syāt) — the cure is shelter in Kṛṣṇa, not the elimination of vigilance.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says a pure devotee is 'always fearful of material existence' (bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syāt), yet Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna 'do not fear.' Pair up: where does each of you draw the line between healthy bhaya of material existence and faithless anxiety? Push back on each other when one of you collapses the distinction.
  • Kṛṣṇa, who is Viśvātmā for everyone, takes special action to protect Devakī because she is His devotee. Discuss in threes: does this make Kṛṣṇa 'partial'? Is impartiality even a virtue in a personal relationship with God, or is it a borrowed Enlightenment idea we should drop?
  • The purport says bhakti-yoga is 'direct' while other yogas require many preliminary processes. In your group, name one 'preliminary process' (study, meditation technique, ethical self-improvement) you have been treating as the main thing. Are you willing to demote it to the role of supporter of bhakti, or do you secretly think it is more reliable than chanting and hearing? Be honest with each other.
Role Play

Two students play out this scene: A is a devotee whose elderly mother has just been diagnosed with a serious illness. A's faith is shaken — 'If Kṛṣṇa protects His devotees, why is she suffering? Devakī got Yoga-māyā; what do we get?' B is a slightly more senior devotee in the same congregation who must respond — without platitudes, without denying A's grief, and without abandoning the verse's claim that Kṛṣṇa actively protects His devotees. B must distinguish material protection (the body) from real protection (the soul's progress and remembrance of Kṛṣṇa at the time of death), and must do so while sitting with A in real distress, not lecturing. After 5 minutes, swap roles. Then debrief: where did B slip into either cold theology or sentimental reassurance?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just mythological favoritism. A 'Supreme Soul of everyone' who then plays favorites for His 'personal devotees' is a contradiction — and morally suspect. Why should I trust a God who allegedly loves all but acts only for some?
    The contradiction dissolves once you see that Kṛṣṇa is Viśvātmā by His plenary expansion as Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu — He is impartially present in every heart, giving everyone the intelligence and remembrance their karma allows (sarvasya cāhaṁ hṛdi sanniviṣṭo). What changes for the devotee is not Kṛṣṇa's availability but the devotee's reciprocation. Yoga-māyā is dispatched not because Devakī earned a prize but because she has turned toward Him in love, and love by its nature responds personally. The sun shines on every field; only the field that has been ploughed and seeded yields a crop. To call this 'favoritism' is to demand that a personal God behave like an impersonal force — which is exactly the philosophical move the Gauḍīyas refuse.
  • Yoga-māyā sounds like a convenient narrative device — whenever the story needs a miracle, the spiritual potency shows up. Isn't this just pre-modern people explaining away coincidence?
    Notice what the purport actually claims: yoga-māyā is the Lord's spiritual potency, distinct from the māyā that bewilders even Brahmā. The point is not 'magic intervention' but that the Lord stays in spiritual touch with His devotees through a potency that is non-different from Him. You are free to remain skeptical of the Mahā-purāṇa's narrative, but the philosophical claim is testable in your own life: do you find that as you take to sādhana — chanting, hearing, serving — circumstances arrange themselves toward your spiritual progress in ways that exceed your planning? Pure devotees across centuries report exactly this. Yoga-māyā is the name for that organizing intelligence. The verse is not asking you to believe in a deus ex machina; it is asking you to begin practicing and watch what happens.
Analogy

The general does not himself row the boat that ferries his soldiers to safety; he gives the order, and a trusted lieutenant moves. Kṛṣṇa as Viśvātmā stands at the center; Yoga-māyā is the trusted lieutenant who carries out the rescue of His own.
