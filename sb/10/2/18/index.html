Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.18

ततो जगन्मङ्गलमच्युतांशंसमाहितं शूरसुतेन देवी । दधार सर्वात्मकमात्मभूतंकाष्ठा यथानन्दकरं मनस्त: ॥ १८ ॥

Important Words
  • jagat - maṅgalam — auspicious for the entire universe
    Jagat-maṅgala — the worlds' welfare itself. The Lord's transfer is not a private family event; the cosmos benefits. Gauḍīya theology: every act of bhakti is a cosmic intervention, however small it looks.
  • acyuta - aṁśam — the infallible Lord with all His expansions
    Acyuta — 'never fallen.' He retains every opulence even in the womb. Aṁśa here is plural in implication — all His plenary expansions accompany Him. Pūrṇāvatāra-tattva is being safeguarded against the misreading of partial descent.
  • sarva - ātmakam — the soul of all
    Sarva-ātmaka — composed of all souls, the soul of all. The same Lord who is in every heart is now being carried in one specific heart. The verse refuses any zero-sum reading of immanence and pastime.
  • ātma - bhūtam — the cause of all causes
    Ātma-bhūta — 'become the self.' Gauḍīyas read this not as Advaitin merger but as: He is the very being-ness of every being. Jīvas have ātmā; He is ātmā-bhūta, the source of even the having.
  • ānanda - karam — the bliss-maker
    Ānanda-kara — He who produces ānanda. The verse compares Him to the rising moon — but the moon is only borrowed bliss; He is the bliss itself. Sat-cit-ānanda, with ānanda foregrounded.
Thereafter, accompanied by plenary expansions, the fully opulent Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is all-auspicious for the entire universe, was transferred from the mind of Vasudeva to the mind of Devakī. Devakī, having thus been initiated by Vasudeva, became beautiful by carrying Lord Kṛṣṇa, the original consciousness for everyone, the cause of all causes, within the core of her heart, just as the east becomes beautiful by carrying the rising moon.
Purport

As indicated here by the word manastaḥ, the Supreme Personality of Godhead was transferred from the core of Vasudeva’s mind or heart to the core of the heart of Devakī. We should note carefully that the Lord was transferred to Devakī not by the ordinary way for a human being, but by dīkṣā, initiation. Thus the importance of initiation is mentioned here. Unless one is initiated by the right person, who always carries within his heart the Supreme Personality of Godhead, one cannot acquire the power to carry the Supreme Godhead within the core of one’s own heart. The word acyutāṁśam is used because the Supreme Personality of Godhead is ṣaḍ-aiśvarya-pūrṇa, full in the opulences of wealth, strength, fame, knowledge, beauty and renunciation. The Supreme Godhead is never separated from His personal opulences. As stated in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.39) , rāmādi-mūrtiṣu kalā-niyamena tiṣṭhan: the Lord is always situated with all His plenary expansions, such as Rāma, Nṛsiṁha and Varāha. Therefore the word acyutāṁśam is specifically used here, signifying that the Lord is always present with His plenary expansions and opulences. There is no need to think of the Lord artificially as yogīs do. Dhyānāvasthita-tad-gatena manasā paśyanti yaṁ yoginaḥ ( Bhāg. 12.13.1 ). Yogīs meditate upon the Supreme Person within the mind. For a devotee, however, the Lord is present, and His presence need only be awakened through initiation by a bona fide spiritual master. The Lord did not need to live within the womb of Devakī, for His presence within the core of her heart was sufficient to carry Him. One is here forbidden to think that Kṛṣṇa was begotten by Vasudeva within the womb of Devakī and that she carried the child within her womb. When Vasudeva was sustaining the form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead within his heart, he appeared just like the glowing sun, whose shining rays are always unbearable and scorching to the common man. The form of the Lord situated in the pure, unalloyed heart of Vasudeva is not different from the original form of Kṛṣṇa. The appearance of the form of Kṛṣṇa anywhere, and specifically within the heart, is called dhāma. Dhāma refers not only to Kṛṣṇa’s form, but to His name, His form, His quality and His paraphernalia. Everything becomes manifest simultaneously. Thus the eternal form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead with full potencies was transferred from the mind of Vasudeva to the mind of Devakī, exactly as the setting sun’s rays are transferred to the full moon rising in the east. Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, entered the body of Devakī from the body of Vasudeva. He was beyond the conditions of the ordinary living entity. When Kṛṣṇa is there, it is to be understood that all His plenary expansions, such as Nārāyaṇa, and incarnations like Lord Nṛsiṁha and Varāha, are with Him, and they are not subject to the conditions of material existence. In this way, Devakī became the residence of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is one without a second and the cause of all creation. Devakī became the residence of the Absolute Truth, but because she was within the house of Kaṁsa, she looked just like a suppressed fire, or like misused education. When fire is covered by the walls of a pot or is kept in a jug, the illuminating rays of the fire cannot be very much appreciated. Similarly, misused knowledge, which does not benefit the people in general, is not very much appreciated. So Devakī was kept within the prison walls of Kaṁsa’s palace, and no one could see her transcendental beauty, which resulted from her conceiving the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Commenting upon this verse, Śrī Vīrarāghava Ācārya writes: vasudeva-devakī jaṭharayor hṛdayayor bhagavataḥ sambandhaḥ. The Supreme Lord’s entrance into the womb of Devakī from the heart of Vasudeva was a heart-to-heart relationship.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Thereafter, accompanied by plenary expansions, the fully opulent Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is all-auspicious for the entire universe, was transferred from the mind of Vasudeva to the mind of Devakī. Devakī, having thus been initiated by Vasudeva, became beautiful by carrying Lord Kṛṣṇa, the original consciousness for everyone, the cause of all causes, within the core of her heart, just as the east becomes beautiful by carrying the rising moon.
Commentary

Then the Lord entered into Devaki from Vasudeva. This is described in this verse. She took into her mind that person who is the very form of auspiciousness for the whole universe, who does not deviate, and who includes all expansions such as Narayana and narasimha (jagan mangalam acyutamsam), who gives happiness (ka) to the minds (atma) of all the devotees and Siva (sarva), who appears (bhuta) on his own, independently (atma). The Lord appeared in devaki’s mind without the effort that yogis exert. Manastah dadhara mean she held with the mind. By saying this, material conception and birth is ruled out. A suitable example is given to support this. It was just like the eastern direction (kastha) holding the full moon (anandakara). It is understood that several days later she could perceive Krsna in her womb for it is said later " disty amba te kuksi gatah parah puman. (By fortune, o lady, the supreme Lord has entered your womb.)

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

She held the Lords (sarvātmakam) who appeared within her (ātmā-bhūtam) as her son. This indicates that the Lord does not take birth like jīvas. Madhva quotes from Tantra-bhagavata. aheyam anupādeyaṁ yad rūpam nityam avyayam sa evāpekṣa-rūpasya vyaktim evan janārdanaḥ agṛhnād vyasṛjac ceti kṛṣna-rāmādikāṁ tanum paṭhyate bhagavān īṣo mūḍa-buddhi-vyapekṣayā tamaṣā hy upagūḍasya yat tamaḥ pānam īśituḥ etat puruṣa-rūpasya grahaṇaṁ samudīryate kṛṣṇa-rāmādi rūpāṇāṁ loke vyakti-vyapekṣayā The foolish person covered by ignorance says that the Lord whose forms like Rāma and Kṛṣṇa are eternal and unchanging, incomparable, and without any faults accepts and gives up bodies. On seeing the appearance of Kṛṣṇa or Rāma in the world he declares that they accept material human forms. Mahā-varāha Purāṇa says: sarve nityāḥa śāśvatāś ca dehās tasya parātmanaḥ heyopadheya-rahitā naiva prakṛti-jāḥ kvacit paramānanda-sandohā jṣāna-mātraś ca sarvataḥ All the forms of God have eternal bodies without any faults. They never arise from matter. They are completely filled with the highest bliss and knowledge. This is the definition of the Lord. He was like the moon which gives bliss because of its sweet rays. The rest is according to the explanation of Śrīdhara Svāmī. Or, acyutāṁśam can mean “he who is not lacking even on part: he who is complete with all his parts, Bhagavān.” Therefore he is the root form (ātmā-bhūtam) who makes his appearance as if offering himself (samāhitam). It was previously mentioned that the seventh child would be the cause of joy and lamentation. The appearance of Kṛṣṇa was a cause of only joy. He was the cause of joy (ka) for all jīvas (sarvātma). Or he would appear personally (ātmā-bhūtam), unlike other forms which become the object of yogīs’ meditation with great effort because he appeared on his own (samāhitam).

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa is received not by birth or biology but heart-to-heart through dīkṣā — a bona fide guru transmits Him from his heart into yours.
Practical Applications
  • Identify whether you have taken shelter of a bona fide spiritual master who carries Kṛṣṇa in his heart, and if not, begin the search seriously today.
  • Refuse the yogic notion that you must imagine or manufacture Kṛṣṇa within — instead, prepare your heart through hearing and chanting so His already-present form can be awakened.
  • Notice where, like Devakī within Kaṁsa's prison, your inner cultivation is hidden by hostile surroundings, and protect that inner fire with daily sādhana rather than display.
Reflection Questions
  • Whose heart am I actually drawing Kṛṣṇa from — am I trying to 'meditate Him up' on my own, or am I receiving Him through a bona fide channel of dīkṣā?
  • Like Devakī in Kaṁsa's prison, where in my life is my inner cultivation of Kṛṣṇa being kept hidden by hostile surroundings — and am I tending that inner fire anyway, or letting the walls extinguish it?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice how much of your 'spiritual life' lives in your own head — playlists, reels, books, late-night thoughts. This week, find one living person senior to you in bhakti (a temple devotee, a teacher, an elder in your family) and actually sit with them for an hour; bhakti is transmitted, not downloaded.
  • Devakī carried the Lord even inside Kaṁsa's prison. Your campus, your hostel, your group chat is your Kaṁsa's palace — stop waiting until you 'get out' to begin sādhana. Fix one daily anchor (sixteen rounds is a long arc; start with one round and one verse) that travels with you regardless of environment.
  • Watch the pull to perform your inner life on social media — the suppressed fire is more powerful than the displayed one. For one month, keep your japa, your reading, and any darśana strictly off your feed; let your devotion stay covered like Devakī in the prison and observe what changes inside.
householder
  • Your home is the heart-chamber where Kṛṣṇa is meant to be carried. Concretely: install or re-install your home altar this week, and commit with your spouse to one fixed family program — a short ārati, one verse read aloud, prasāda offered — every single day, treating the household itself as Devakī's heart.
  • On the question of dīkṣā: if you have not yet formally taken shelter of a bona fide spiritual master in the paramparā, stop deferring it as 'something for later when life settles.' Identify the line, the teacher, the community, and take a real step this quarter — your children are watching whether bhakti, in your life, is theoretical or transmitted.
  • In your career and money decisions, ask whether what you are building is a Kaṁsa's palace (impressive walls hiding a suppressed fire) or a Vasudeva's heart (modest outwardly, but actually carrying the Lord). Make one concrete adjustment — a job, a purchase, a school decision — where you choose the second over the first this year.
senior
  • Your role now is Vasudeva's role — to transfer, heart to heart, what you have been carrying. Identify by name two or three younger people (children, grandchildren, juniors, mentees) and commit to a regular, unhurried transmission — read one verse with them weekly, tell them the pastimes Prabhupāda told you, let your bhakti actually leave your body before it does.
  • Loosen the grip on the 'prison walls' of property, position, and reputation that you have spent decades building. This year, formally hand over one major responsibility (a business, a trust, a household role) to the next generation — not because you are incapable, but to free your heart to be, like Devakī's, only the residence of the Lord.
  • Mortality is now close enough to be honest about. Each evening, before sleep, place Kṛṣṇa consciously in the core of your heart with the same deliberateness with which Vasudeva transferred Him to Devakī — so that when the body finally falls, the transfer at the moment of death is only the continuation of a transfer you have already been practicing for years.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating initiation as a social ceremony or sentimental milestone, rather than the actual transmission by which the Lord is established in the heart — and then wondering why no inner transformation follows.
  • Imitating the yogī's manufactured meditation — trying to 'visualize' Kṛṣṇa into the heart by mental effort — instead of awakening the personal Lord, complete with His name, form, qualities and paraphernalia, through the mercy of a bona fide guru.
  • Reducing acyutāṁśam to a bare 'inner divine spark' or impersonal presence, forgetting that Kṛṣṇa is never separated from His ṣaḍ-aiśvarya and His plenary expansions — and thereby drifting into the impersonalist substitute the verse explicitly forbids.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda insists Kṛṣṇa was transferred from Vasudeva to Devakī by dīkṣā, not by ordinary conception, and that without initiation by a bona fide spiritual master one cannot carry the Lord in the heart. Where does this leave the modern practitioner who reads, chants, and feels devotion but has not formally accepted a guru? Push each other — is unintiated bhakti merely preparatory, or is the verse making a stronger claim?
  • Devakī, even while carrying the Supreme Personality of Godhead in her heart, looked outwardly like a 'suppressed fire' inside Kaṁsa's prison. Share with your partner one area of your present life that feels like Kaṁsa's prison — and disagree honestly about whether the right response is to escape it, transform it, or simply carry Kṛṣṇa within it as Devakī did.
  • The purport draws a sharp line between the yogī who must artificially construct the Lord's form in meditation and the devotee in whom the Lord's presence is awakened by the spiritual master. Does this distinction still hold today, when meditation apps, visualization techniques, and 'consciousness work' are everywhere? Where do members of your group catch themselves drifting into yogic self-effort instead of receptive devotion?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A plays a sincere seeker who has been chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa for two years, reads Prabhupāda's books daily, and feels Kṛṣṇa in the heart — but has never formally taken initiation, arguing that 'my relationship with Kṛṣṇa is direct, I do not need a middleman.' Student B plays a senior devotee who has just heard SB 10.2.18 in class and must, without being preachy or condescending, explain why this verse specifically forbids that view — that the heart-to-heart transmission Vīrarāghava Ācārya describes (vasudeva-devakī jaṭharayor hṛdayayor bhagavataḥ sambandhaḥ) requires a living link. Run it for seven minutes, then switch roles. Afterward, each pair reports the strongest objection they could not answer.

Objections & Responses
  • This whole verse reads like mythology dressed up as theology — a baby mystically transferred from one mind to another, sustained without a womb. A modern reader cannot take this literally, and once we read it as metaphor the theological claims about initiation collapse with it.
    The verse is not asking you to first accept a biological miracle and then derive a doctrine from it. It is describing the descent of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is by definition not subject to the conditions of material existence — Prabhupāda is explicit that Kṛṣṇa 'was beyond the conditions of the ordinary living entity.' The metaphysics comes first: if Kṛṣṇa is acyuta and ṣaḍ-aiśvarya-pūrṇa, then the manner of His appearance need not, and will not, conform to ordinary gestation. The 'mythological' reading flattens this into a folk tale precisely because it has already smuggled in the assumption that only the empirically ordinary is real. The Gauḍīya answer is that the verse is teaching us the opposite: the Absolute can act personally and specifically without being reduced to our categories, and this is exactly why dīkṣā — a heart-to-heart transmission, not a biological one — is the model for how He enters a devotee.
  • Saying that one cannot carry Kṛṣṇa in the heart without being initiated by a bona fide spiritual master sounds like institutional gatekeeping. It conveniently makes the institution indispensable and dismisses every sincere bhakta outside the line.
    Hear the verse carefully — it is not the institution that is indispensable, it is the living link. Vasudeva did not transfer Kṛṣṇa to Devakī because he held an office; he did it because he himself was actually carrying the Lord 'like the glowing sun' in his unalloyed heart. Prabhupāda's standard is exactly the same: 'unless one is initiated by the right person, who always carries within his heart the Supreme Personality of Godhead.' That cuts against institutional complacency as sharply as it cuts against do-it-yourself spirituality. The point is not that a stamped certificate gives you Kṛṣṇa, nor that your private sincerity does — it is that bhakti is by nature received, transmitted heart to heart, the way a flame is lit from a flame. To object to that is, in the end, to insist that the disciple be his own source — and a source you generate yourself is, by definition, not the Supreme.
Analogy

The full moon does not light itself; it borrows the sun's rays and gives back a light the earth can bear. Devakī, receiving the Lord from Vasudeva's heart into her own, is the eastern horizon — beautiful not by her own light but by what is about to rise from her.
