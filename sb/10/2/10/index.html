Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.10

अर्चिष्यन्ति मनुष्यास्त्वां सर्वकामवरेश्वरीम् । धूपोपहारबलिभि: सर्वकामवरप्रदाम् ॥ १० ॥

Important Words
  • manuṣyāḥ — human beings
    Plural, generic — 'ordinary people.' The verse classifies its audience: not devotees, not yogīs, but karma-kāṇḍa-rooted human society, which needs a śakti who fulfills material desires. The Gauḍīya frame: demigod-worship is for those not yet ready for Kṛṣṇa.
  • sarva - kāma - vara - īśvarīm — controller of all desired boons
    Long compound — sarva-kāma (all material desires), vara (boons), īśvarī (controller). She is the queen of fulfillment for the kāma-realm. Crucially, Kṛṣṇa Himself is naming her domain — limiting it as much as authorizing it.
  • balibhiḥ — by sacrificial offerings
    Bali — animal sacrifice. Prabhupāda's purport flags this: people will worship Durgā with goats and buffaloes, but Vaiṣṇavas do not. The verse acknowledges the practice without endorsing it as the highest dharma.
  • sarva - kāma — of all material desires
    Repeated within the verse for emphasis. Sarva-kāma is the realm of pravṛtti-mārga — the path of fulfillment of desire. The Gītā contrasts this with niṣkāma-karma; the Bhāgavatam quietly names where each path leads.
  • pradām — the bestower
    From pra-√dā, 'to grant.' Yoga-māyā in her Durgā form does give what is asked — the verse does not deny the efficacy of demigod-worship. The Gauḍīya teaching is not that demigods fail, but that what they grant is impermanent.
By sacrifices of animals, ordinary human beings will worship you gorgeously, with various paraphernalia, because you are supreme in fulfilling the material desires of everyone.
Purport

As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (7.20) , kāmais tais tair hṛta jñānāḥ prapadyante ’nya-devatāḥ: “Those whose minds are distorted by material desires surrender unto demigods.” Therefore the word manuṣya, meaning “human being,” here refers to one who does not know the actual goal of life. Such a person wants to enjoy the material world by taking birth in a highly elevated family with the benefits of education, beauty and immense wealth, which in this material world are desirable. One who has forgotten the real aim of life may worship the goddess Durgā, māyā-śakti, under various names, for different purposes, and in different places. As there are many holy places for the worship of Kṛṣṇa, there are also many holy places in India for the worship of Durgādevī, or Māyādevī, who took birth as the daughter of Yaśodā. After cheating Kaṁsa, Māyādevī dispersed herself to various places, especially in Vindhyācala, to accept regular worship from ordinary men. A human being should actually be interested in understanding ātma-tattva, the truth of ātmā, the spirit soul, and Paramātmā, the supreme soul. Those who are interested in ātma-tattva worship the Supreme Personality of Godhead ( yasmin vijñāte sarvam evaṁ vijñātaṁ bhavati ) ( Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.3). However, as explained in the next verse of this chapter, those who cannot understand ātma-tattva ( apaśyatām ātma-tattvam ) worship Yoga-māyā in her different features. Therefore Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (2.1.2) says: śrotavyādīni rājendra nṛṇāṁ santi sahasraśaḥ apaśyatām ātma-tattvaṁ gṛheṣu gṛha-medhinām “Those persons who are materially engrossed, being blind to the knowledge of ultimate truth, have many subject matters for hearing in human society, O Emperor.” Those who are interested in remaining in this material world and are not interested in spiritual salvation have many duties, but for one who is interested in spiritual salvation, the only duty is to surrender fully unto Kṛṣṇa ( sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ). Such a person is not interested in material enjoyment.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
By sacrifices of animals, ordinary human beings will worship you gorgeously, with various paraphernalia, because you are supreme in fulfilling the material desires of everyone.
Commentary

Your amsa, maha maya, will be taken by Vasudeva to cheat Kamsa. You will be worshipped in various forms in Vindhacala and other places. All men will worship these forms, because you are the supreme (varam) goddess (isvarim) of those who have all types of material desires.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Three verses elaborate. Because men have desires and are qualified with that dharma, you bestow benedictions. Hari-vaṁśa says pradāṁ te kariṣyāmi mat prabhāva-amaṁ bhuvi Yena sarvasya lokasya devi devī bhavisyasi O Devī! I give you power equal to mine in this world by which you will become the goddess of all men.

Personal Guidance
Worship aimed at material desires keeps you a 'manuṣya' chasing fruits; the soul's only real duty is surrender to Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Notice every prayer or wish you offered today and ask whether it sought a material fruit or Kṛṣṇa Himself, and re-aim it.
  • Refuse to treat Kṛṣṇa as one more demigod for boons — speak to Him today only as the goal, not the means.
  • Spend ten minutes studying ātma-tattva (read one purport on the soul) instead of consuming one round of entertainment.
Reflection Questions
  • Which 'sarva-kāma' — which bundle of material desires — am I quietly hoping the spiritual path will fulfill, rather than dissolve?
  • Where in my worship or sādhana am I behaving like the manuṣya of this verse — approaching the Divine with elaborate offerings but a transactional heart, asking for fruits instead of seeking ātma-tattva?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your last ten prayers, wishes, or 'manifestations' — the ones whispered before exams, before opening grades, before checking who replied to your message. How many were requests to a sarva-kāma-vara-pradām figure (the universe, luck, a deity, your own willpower) for a material outcome? Write them down; the list itself is the lesson.
  • Pick one upcoming ambition — a grade, a placement, an admission, a relationship — and practice asking for the strength to do your duty (kartavya) toward it rather than for the result. Notice how much harder this prayer is, and how much it exposes about what you actually trust.
  • On social media this week, watch how often spiritual or aesthetic imagery (temples, deities, 'good vibes') is deployed to chase material kāma — followers, validation, aesthetic identity. Resolve not to post one such image yourself for seven days, and observe what withdrawal feels like. That withdrawal is your own manuṣya-mind meeting the verse.
householder
  • Look honestly at the last big family decision — a school for a child, a house, a job change, a financial move. Was it a gṛhastha decision (household life ordered toward Kṛṣṇa) or a gṛha-medhī decision (household life as the end in itself)? Name the difference out loud to your spouse this week; do not solve it yet, just name it.
  • In your worship at home, separate two registers and keep them separate for a month: prayers of petition for the family's material needs (health, school, income) and prayers of offering with no agenda. Place the second one first — before the dīpa is lit, before the children are called — even if it is only two minutes.
  • Practice dāna and aparigraha together as a couple: choose one possession or one recurring expense this month that exists purely to fulfill kāma, and give the equivalent away. Tell your children why, in plain language. You are teaching them, by demonstration, that the household is not the highest court of appeal.
senior
  • List the material boons you spent decades pursuing — career, status, the children's success, the house, the savings. Mark each one: did getting it free you, or did it generate the next desire? Sit with the pattern. This is the verse showing you your own life from the inside.
  • When you mentor a younger person this season, resist the temptation to bless them only with sarva-kāma-vara — 'may you get the job, the match, the promotion.' Bless them once, in your own words, with ātma-tattva: with the wish that they come to know the soul and the Supreme Soul. Watch how unfamiliar that blessing feels in your own mouth.
  • Choose one attachment — a possession, a role, an identity ('the one who provides,' 'the matriarch,' 'the expert') — and begin its formal handover this month. Not renunciation as performance; quiet, practical loosening. Use the time recovered for hearing and chanting. The body's transitions are coming; let the grip soften before they are pried open.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as a dismissal of Durgādevī or other devatās rather than as a precise diagnosis of the worshipper's motive — the pitfall is contempt for others' worship instead of scrutiny of one's own desires.
  • Assuming that because one worships Kṛṣṇa (and not a demigod), one is automatically free of the manuṣya-mentality — yet still approaching Kṛṣṇa with the same shopping-list of sarva-kāma, just addressed to a different name.
  • Mistaking the verse's critique of kāmya worship as a license to abandon ritual, offering, and arcana altogether, when the real correction is to retain the offerings (dhūpa, upahāra, bali of one's labor) but reorient them from material desire toward ātma-tattva and surrender.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda calls the worshippers in this verse manuṣya — 'human beings' — but redefines the word to mean those who have forgotten the actual goal of life. Where in your own week are you a 'manuṣya' in that diminished sense, approaching the sacred with a shopping list of material wants? Be specific with your partner; do not let each other off with generalities.
  • The verse names Māyādevī as sarva-kāma-vara-pradām — she really does grant material desires. So worshipping her 'works.' Discuss with your group: if it works, what exactly is wrong with it? Push back on each other until you can articulate the difference between getting what you asked for and getting what you actually need.
  • Prabhupāda contrasts the gṛha-medhī (one absorbed in household affairs as an end in themselves) with one who seeks ātma-tattva. Yet the householder āśrama is honored in our tradition. Where is the line between a legitimate gṛhastha life and the gṛha-medhī drift? Share one decision from the past month where you can feel the line in your own life.
Role Play

Two students are paired. Student A plays a sincere friend who has just returned from a Durgā-pūjā at a famous temple, glowing about the atmosphere and convinced she received a job offer because of the goddess's grace. She asks Student B — known in the friend group as 'the Krishna person' — whether she should keep going every year. Student B must respond as a Gauḍīya practitioner: honoring Māyādevī's actual position (the verse says she does grant such boons; she is Kṛṣṇa's internal energy who took birth as Yaśodā's daughter), not insulting the friend's experience, but gently turning the conversation toward ātma-tattva and the Personality of Godhead behind the energy. The teacher should stop the scene if Student B becomes either dismissive ('that's just demigod worship') or impersonalist ('it's all the same divine'). Run it twice, swapping roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This verse and purport sound condescending — it labels millions of sincere Durgā devotees as people who 'do not know the actual goal of life.' Isn't that exactly the kind of religious arrogance the modern world is right to reject?
    Notice that Prabhupāda is not insulting Māyādevī — he is honoring her as Kṛṣṇa's energy, the daughter of Yaśodā, worshipped at genuine holy places like Vindhyācala. The discrimination is not between 'our god' and 'their god'; it is between asking for the gift and asking for the Giver. A child who only ever asks his mother for sweets is not wicked, but he has not yet understood his mother. The Gītā verse Prabhupāda cites — kāmais tais tair hṛta-jñānāḥ — is diagnostic, not contemptuous. We say the same thing about ourselves whenever we approach Kṛṣṇa with a material agenda. The verse is a mirror first, a critique second.
  • If Māyādevī genuinely fulfills material desires, and material life is what most people actually want, then this whole framework is just spiritual elitism dressed up as compassion. Why shouldn't ordinary people get the births, beauty, and wealth they're asking for?
    They should — and the verse confirms they do. Nothing in the purport denies that the boons come. The question is not whether the system works but where it ends. Take birth, beauty, education, wealth: walk each one to its terminus. Each ends in old age, disease, and another body, with the desires intact and the bill compounding. Prabhupāda's compassion is precisely not to interrupt anyone's pūjā but to whisper: there is one more thing you can ask for that does not have to be asked for again. That is ātma-tattva, and finally surrender to Kṛṣṇa Himself — sarva-dharmān parityajya. To withhold that whisper out of politeness would be the real elitism: deciding in advance that other people cannot bear the higher offer.
Analogy

When a great prince passes through a town, lesser officials are stationed at the gate to receive offerings the prince Himself does not require. The masses bring their goats and gold to the gate-officers and go home satisfied. Yoga-māyā as Durgā is stationed at the gate; Kṛṣṇa stands in the inner court, accessible only to those who pass through.

Stories
Caitanya at the Sākṣi-Gopāla shrine

When Caitanya Mahāprabhu travelled through South India, He paid full respects to the local Deities — Veṅkaṭeśvara, Raṅganātha, the goddess at Kanyākumārī — but at every stop He turned the conversation toward kṛṣṇa-bhakti. He did not deny the demigods their station; He showed where their station led.

This verse foretells the same hierarchy. Yoga-māyā as Durgā receives the world's sacrifices for material desires (sarva-kāma-vara-pradām), but the verse is set inside Kṛṣṇa's own instruction to her — establishing that demigod-worship is real but penultimate. Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā 9.
