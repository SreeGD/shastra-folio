Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.8

Within the womb of Devakī is My partial plenary expansion known as Saṅkarṣaṇa or Śeṣa. Without difficulty, transfer Him into the womb of Rohiṇī.
Purport

The first plenary expansion of Kṛṣṇa is Baladeva, also known as Śeṣa. The Śeṣa incarnation of the Supreme Personality of Godhead supports the entire universe, and the eternal mother of this incarnation is mother Rohiṇī. “Because I am going into the womb of Devakī,” the Lord told Yoga-māyā, “the Śeṣa incarnation has already gone there and made suitable arrangements so that I may live there. Now He should enter the womb of Rohiṇī, His eternal mother.” In this connection, one may ask how the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is always situated transcendentally, could enter the womb of Devakī, which had previously been entered by the six asuras, the ṣaḍ-garbhas. Does this mean that the ṣaḍ-garbhāsuras were equal to the transcendental body of the Supreme Personality of Godhead? The following answer is given by Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura. The entire creation, as well as its individual parts, is an expansion of the energy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore, even though the Lord enters the material world, He does not do so. This is explained by the Lord Himself in Bhagavad-gītā (9.4-5): mayā tatam idaṁ sarvaṁ jagad avyakta-mūrtinā mat-sthāni sarva-bhūtāni na cāhaṁ teṣv avasthitaḥ na ca mat-sthāni bhūtāni paśya me yogam aiśvaram bhūta-bhṛn na ca bhūta-stho mamātmā bhūta-bhāvanaḥ “By Me, in My unmanifested form, this entire universe is pervaded. All beings are in Me, but I am not in them. And yet everything that is created does not rest in Me. Behold My mystic opulence! Although I am the maintainer of all living entities, and although I am everywhere, My Self is the very source of creation.” Sarvaṁ khalv idaṁ brahma. Everything is an expansion of Brahman, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, yet everything is not the Supreme Godhead, and He is not everywhere. Everything rests upon Him and yet does not rest upon Him. This can be explained only through the acintya-bhedābheda philosophy. Such truths cannot be understood, however, unless one is a pure devotee, for the Lord says in Bhagavad-gītā (18.55) , bhaktyā mām abhijānāti yāvān yaś cāsmi tattvataḥ: “One can understand the Supreme Personality as He is only by devotional service.” Even though the Lord cannot be understood by ordinary persons, this principle should be understood from the statement of the śāstras. A pure devotee is always transcendentally situated because of executing nine different processes of bhakti-yoga ( śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaranaṁ pāda-sevanam/ arcanaṁ vandanaṁ dāsyaṁ sakhyam ātma-nivedanam ). Thus situated in devotional service, a devotee, although in the material world, is not in the material world. Yet a devotee always fears, “Because I am associated with the material world, so many contaminations affect me.” Therefore he is always alert in fear, which gradually diminishes his material association. Symbolically, mother Devakī’s constant fear of Kaṁsa was purifying her. A pure devotee should always fear material association, and in this way all the asuras of material association will be killed, as the ṣaḍ-garbhāsuras were killed by Kaṁsa. It is said that from the mind, Marīci appears. In other words, Marīci is an incarnation of the mind. Marīci has six sons: Kāma, Krodha, Lobha, Moha, Mada and Mātsarya (lust, anger, greed, illusion, madness and envy). The Supreme Personality of Godhead appears in pure devotional service. This is confirmed in the Vedas: bhaktir evainaṁ darśayati. Only bhakti can bring one in contact with the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The Supreme Personality of Godhead appeared from the womb of Devakī, and therefore Devakī symbolically represents bhakti, and Kaṁsa symbolically represents material fear. When a pure devotee always fears material association, his real position of bhakti is manifested, and he naturally becomes uninterested in material enjoyment. When the six sons of Marīci are killed by such fear and one is freed from material contamination, within the womb of bhakti the Supreme Personality of Godhead appears. Thus the seventh pregnancy of Devakī signifies the appearance of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. After the six sons Kāma, Krodha, Lobha, Moha, Mada and Mātsarya are killed, the Śeṣa incarnation creates a suitable situation for the appearance of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. In other words, when one awakens his natural Kṛṣṇa consciousness, Lord Kṛṣṇa appears. This is the explanation given by Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Within the womb of Devakī is My partial plenary expansion known as Saṅkarṣaṇa or Śeṣa. Without difficulty, transfer Him into the womb of Rohiṇī.
Commentary

Mamakam dhama means that Baladeva is my amsa. Sesa is his portion. This is shown in verses such as sb 10.65.28; yasyaikamsensa vidhrta jagati jagatah pateh: O master of the universe, Rama! By one of your parts you hold up the earth." "Though Rohini is the eternal mother of Balarama, because of my request, he appeared in Devaki’s womb first. After establishing Sesa, his amsa there, as my bed and seat, He desired to go to the womb of his own mother." How is it possible that the first six material children could appear in the womb of Devaki, who is the pure spiritual energy of the pure Lord? Just as the material universes exist within the Supreme Lord but do not contaminate the Lord, so the six children could exist within Devaki without contaminating her. This is proven in the Gita: mat sthani sarva bhutani na caham tesv avastithah... The conjunction of material and spiritual in the pastimes is there to illustrate the methodical nature of bhakti. While devotees perform sadhana bhakti in the form of hearing and chanting internally there exists material enjoyment of the six objects of the senses (sound, touch, form, taste, smell and thought) as well, which is a secondary effect of bhakti. The devotee then cries out pitifully, "OH, because of this enjoyment I will fall into the material well of darkness. " By manifesting fear of material enjoyment, gradually the tendency to enjoy matter subsides. Then bhakti in the form of hearing and chanting the names, and glorifying the forms, qualities and pastimes of the Lord and serving in various ways, becomes prominent. Finally the Lord himself, the ocean of all wonderful forms and qualities appears within the devotee. Bhakti is suddha sattva by nature, and directly manifests the Lord. The sruti says: bhaktir evainam darsayati. Devotion makes the Lord reveal himself. As it is said that Marici appeared from the mind, he can be considered the incarnation of the mind. His six children are the six objects of the senses: sound, touch, form, taste, smell, and thought. As she possesses the power to make the Lord appear, Devaki can be considered the incarnation of bhakti. Kamsa can be considered the incarnation of fear, as it is often said "out of fear of Kamsa". Just as fear of the material world removes the six sense objects from the womb of bhakti, so Kamsa destroyed the six children of Devaki. Just as prema, characterized by intense service to the Lord, necessarily appears in the womb of bhakti after the desire for sense objects is extinguished, so Ananta, the form of service to the Lord, appeared as the seventh child of Devaki. Just as the Lord himself appears after prema bhakti appears, so Bhagavan himself appears as the eighth child of Devaki after Sesa appears.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Place the embryo (garbham), the form (dhāma) of Saṅkarṣaṇa, who is my portion (mama), who is known as Śeṣa into Rohiṇī’s womb. Śeṣa means “he who remains.’ Dhāma can also mean the shelter, since he possesses the ādhāra-śakti, the power to maintain everything. Take the embryo invisibly and with joy (sam), by all means (ni).

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Place the embryo (garbham), the form (dhāma) of Saṅkarṣaṇa, who is my portion (mama), who is known as Śeṣa, into Rohiṇī’s womb. Śeṣa means “he who remains.’ Dhāma can also mean the shelter, since he possesses the ādhāra-śakti, the power to maintain everything. Take the embryo invisibly and with joy (sam), by all means (ni). “If he is called Śeṣa, why transfer him out of fear of Kaṁsa?” He is my shelter (dhāma māmakam). Or transfer him to Gokula (dhāma). Without him I cannot fulfill the happiness of my pastimes there.

Personal Guidance
Devakī's constant fear of Kaṁsa is bhakti's purifying alertness — when the six inner asuras (kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya) are slain, Kṛṣṇa appears within.
Practical Applications
  • Name today which of the six — kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, or mātsarya — is most active in you, and refuse it one specific indulgence before sundown.
  • Cultivate Devakī's sat-bhaya: each time material association tempts you (a scroll, a grudge, a craving), pause and say aloud, 'This contaminates the womb where Kṛṣṇa would appear.'
  • Begin the day with one of the nine processes of bhakti — śravaṇaṁ or kīrtanaṁ — for ten unbroken minutes, treating it as Śeṣa preparing the ground for the Lord's arrival in your consciousness.
Reflection Questions
  • Which 'asuras' — kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya — is still gestating in the womb of my consciousness, and what specific material association keeps feeding it?
  • Where in my life am I mistaking comfortable association with the material world for safety, when in fact it is the very fearlessness Devakī did not have that is blocking Kṛṣṇa's appearance in me?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your phone today the way Devakī watched Kaṁsa: which apps and group chats are the six asuras (lust, anger, greed, illusion, madness, envy) sneaking in through? Don't delete anything dramatically — just notice, with the same alert fear Devakī had, what is entering the womb of your attention.
  • Before your next exam or interview, instead of pumping yourself up with 'I've got this,' try the opposite: acknowledge that the outcome is being arranged by a higher energy (Yoga-māyā), and your job is only to show up cleanly prepared. Notice whether this lowers or raises your performance.
  • Identify one friendship where you are the Rohiṇī — the safer, steadier womb — for someone who is in a Devakī-like situation (toxic family, hostile dorm, controlling relationship). Without preaching, be that protected space for them this week.
householder
  • Treat your home as the womb in which Kṛṣṇa-consciousness is gestating for your children. This week, name out loud one specific 'Kaṁsa' you are choosing to keep out — a show, a habit of speech, a category of conversation — and one 'Rohiṇī-arrangement' you are putting in place (a kīrtana time, a meal together, a verse on the fridge).
  • In your next significant family or career decision, explicitly ask: am I trying to be Yoga-māyā here — orchestrating outcomes — or am I willing to be Rohiṇī, the receptive arrangement Kṛṣṇa transfers things into? Discuss with your spouse where the line is.
  • Pick the one of the six sons of Marīci (kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya) that most disturbs your household — usually krodha or lobha for working parents — and for one week treat its arising as a fire alarm for sādhana, not a personal failing. Each time it arises, chant one round.
senior
  • You have already 'transferred wombs' many times — childhood body to youth to middle age to now. Sit with that lineage of transitions today and feel that the same Self has been carried through each one without difficulty by Yoga-māyā. Let this rehearse you for the final transfer.
  • Identify a younger devotee or family member who is in a hostile environment for their spiritual life. Become consciously their Rohiṇī this season: the safe womb they can transfer their tender bhakti into. Mentor without controlling.
  • Of the six asuras, mada (pride in past accomplishments) and mātsarya (envy of those still in their prime) tend to grip seniors most. Name the one that is yours, and treat its arising not as shameful but as the very contamination Devakī's fear was meant to burn off — let it sharpen your chanting rather than depress you.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the transfer of Śeṣa as mere mythological mechanics and missing the inner instruction — that the Lord's appearance in the heart requires a prior arranging by His expansion through purificatory fear of material contamination.
  • Cultivating a vague 'spiritual fear' as a mood, while still nurturing the six sons of Marīci in daily habits — being afraid in theory of māyā but unafraid in practice of lust, anger, greed, illusion, madness, and envy.
  • Concluding from 'everything is Brahman' that the Lord's descent into Devakī's womb proves He is materially contaminated like the ṣaḍ-garbhāsuras — collapsing acintya-bhedābheda into flat impersonalism and losing the personal Kṛṣṇa to whom surrender is owed.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī's constant fear of Kaṁsa is presented as purifying, not as a defect. Share with your partner: where in your life is fear functioning as a purifying alarm bell, and where is it just neurosis? How do you tell the difference without rationalizing one as the other?
  • Prabhupāda names the six sons of Marīci — kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya — as the asuras Kaṁsa kills. Pick the one that has the strongest grip on you right now, and disagree with each other honestly: is fearing material association actually a workable strategy against it, or does avoidance just feed the very thing you're trying to escape?
  • The verse says Śeṣa is transferred from Devakī's womb to Rohiṇī's 'without difficulty' by Yoga-māyā. In your own sādhana, what have you been trying to muscle through with willpower that the verse suggests is actually arranged by Kṛṣṇa's energy? Where does the line fall between your effort and His arrangement — and do you and your partner draw that line in the same place?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a sincere devotee who has just received a job offer in a new city — better pay, but it would mean leaving an established sanga and moving into an environment full of material association (corporate culture, colleagues uninterested in spiritual life, evening entertainment). Student A is genuinely afraid of the contamination but also worries this fear is just cowardice or attachment to comfort. Student B is a senior devotee Student A trusts. Student B must respond using the verse's logic — that fear of material association can itself be purifying like Devakī's, that the six asuras (kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya) need to be confronted not just avoided, and that Kṛṣṇa arranges the womb in which He will appear. Do not give a yes/no answer. Help Student A discern which fear is bhakti-fear and which is bodily fear. Swap roles after eight minutes.

Objections & Responses
  • Teaching people to 'always fear material association' sounds psychologically harmful. Modern wellness teaches us that anxiety is the disease, not the cure. Won't this verse just produce scrupulous, paranoid devotees?
    The fear Prabhupāda describes is not anxiety in the clinical sense — it is sajātīya-bhaya, the wakefulness of someone who knows what is at stake. A new mother does not have an anxiety disorder because she is alert to what could harm her infant; she is simply awake to the value of what she is carrying. Devakī carried Kṛṣṇa Himself, and her fear of Kaṁsa was the natural alertness of one who knows the worth of what is within. The Gauḍīya line distinguishes this carefully from rajasic worry about outcomes or tamasic dread. Bhakti-fear does not paralyze; it sharpens. Notice that Devakī continues her duties throughout. The test is fruit: does your 'spiritual fear' make you more present, more careful with the holy name, more honest about your associations — or does it spiral inward? If the latter, that is not what this verse describes.
  • The whole transfer-of-the-embryo story is biologically absurd. Treating it as literal makes the tradition look credulous; treating it as allegory means we should just teach the moral and drop the narrative.
    The verse does not ask you to choose between literal history and inner meaning — Vyāsadeva, through Śukadeva, gives both at once, and Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's commentary that Prabhupāda relays here shows precisely how. Yoga-māyā, Kṛṣṇa's internal potency, performs the transfer; this is not biology, it is acintya-śakti, the inconceivable energy by which the Lord enters the material world without being touched by it (Gītā 9.4–5, quoted right in the purport). To 'drop the narrative' is to lose the very thing the allegory is anchored in — that Kṛṣṇa is a person who acts, who sends Yoga-māyā, who arranges the appearance of His own brother. Impersonalists will always want the moral without the Lord. The Gauḍīya answer is that the pastime and the inner meaning are non-different — acintya-bhedābheda — and the strength of one depends on holding the other.
