Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.33

Important Words
  • tāvakāḥ — Your own (devotees)
    Tāvaka — possessive adjective, 'belonging to You.' The verse names devotees not by their qualifications but by their possession by the Lord. Bhakti is in the genitive — His, not theirs.
  • bhraśyanti — they fall (do not)
    Negated here — na te bhraśyanti. The same root as the impersonalists' falling in v.32, deliberately echoed and then denied. Two falls, two outcomes; the difference is sambandha.
  • baddha - sauhṛdāḥ — bound by friendly affection
    Baddha-sauhṛda — 'bondage of friendship.' The Gauḍīyas treasure this paradoxical phrase: real bhakti is a bondage that liberates, because the bonds are made of love. Compare the rope of Yaśodā at SB 10.9.
  • abhiguptāḥ — protected
    From abhi-√gup — 'to hide, to protect.' The same root as goptā (protector). The Lord hides His own — covers them, conceals their lapses, ensures their continuation. Protection in the Bhāgavatam is intimate, not mechanical.
  • nirbhayāḥ — fearless
    Nir-bhaya — without fear. The verse's gift to its reader: protected devotees are fearless not because of confidence in themselves but because of the goptā. Abhaya is offered as a state, not as a feeling.
O Mādhava, Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord of the goddess of fortune, if devotees completely in love with You sometimes fall from the path of devotion, they do not fall like nondevotees, for You still protect them. Thus they fearlessly traverse the heads of their opponents and continue to progress in devotional service.
Purport

Devotees generally do not fall down, but if circumstantially they do, the Lord, because of their strong attachment to Him, gives them protection in all circumstances. Thus even if devotees fall down, they are still strong enough to traverse the heads of their enemies. We have actually seen that our Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement has many opponents, such as the “deprogrammers,” who instituted a strong legal case against the devotees. We thought that this case would take a long time to settle, but because the devotees were protected by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, we unexpectedly won the case in one day. Thus a case that was expected to continue for years was settled in a day because of the protection of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who has promised in Bhagavad-gītā (9.31) , kaunteya pratijānīhi na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati: “O son of Kuntī, declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes.” In history there are many instances of devotees like Citraketu, Indradyumna and Mahārāja Bharata who circumstantially fell down but were still protected. Mahārāja Bharata, for example, because of his attachment to a deer, thought of the deer at the time of death, and therefore in his next life he became a deer ( yaṁ yaṁ vāpi smaran bhāvaṁ tyajaty ante kalevaram ). Because of protection by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, however, the deer remembered his relationship with the Lord and next took birth in a good brahminical family and performed devotional service ( śucīnāṁ śrīmatāṁ gehe yoga-bhraṣṭo ’bhijāyate ). Similarly, Citraketu fell down and became a demon, Vṛtrāsura, but he too was protected. Thus even if one falls down from the path of bhakti-yoga, one is ultimately saved. If a devotee is strongly situated in devotional service, the Supreme Personality of Godhead has promised to protect him ( kaunteya pratijānīhi na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati ). But even if a devotee circumstantially falls down, he is protected by Mādhava. The word Mādhava is significant. Mā, mother Lakṣmī, the mother of all opulences, is always with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and if a devotee is in touch with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, all the opulences of the Lord are ready to help him. yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇo yatra pārtho dhanur-dharaḥ tatra śrīr vijayo bhūtir dhruvā nītir matir mama ( Bg. 18.78 ) Wherever there is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, and His devotee Arjuna, Pārtha, there is victory, opulence, extraordinary power and morality. The opulences of a devotee are not a result of karma-kāṇḍa-vicāra. A devotee is always protected by all of the Supreme Lord’s opulences, of which no one can deprive him ( teṣāṁ nityābhiyuktānāṁ yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham ). Thus a devotee cannot be defeated by any opponents. A devotee, therefore, should not deviate knowingly from the path of devotion. The adherent devotee is assured all protection from the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Mādhava, Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord of the goddess of fortune, if devotees completely in love with You sometimes fall from the path of devotion, they do not fall like nondevotees, for You still protect them. Thus they fearlessly traverse the heads of their opponents and continue to progress in devotional service.
Commentary

Why do the jnanis get criticized so heavily? Bharata, Indradyumna and Citraketu, the chief devotees, do not get criticism even though they fell. This verse answers the question. The devotees on the path of bhakti do not fall like the pretenders of liberation—what to speak of giving up the Lord’s lotus feet. Even if they fall, in that state they remain firmly attached to your lotus feet. Though Citraketu, Bharata, and Indradyumna fell, in the bodies of Vrta and others, the prema increased a hundred fold. One should see their fall down as a cause for increasing prema. And their fall is not like the fall of the aspirants for liberation, for even in the fallen state they remain firmly attached to your feet. They remain faithful to the Lord, thinking that their fall has been arranged by the Lord for their own benefit, for the Lord promises that his devotee will never perish. Being thus convinced of the Lord’s protection, they tread over the heads of the devas causing obstacles, that is they conquer over all obstacles. Another meaning can be that the devotes hold on their own heads the feet of the Lord which destroy all obstacles.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Those who worship you, without knowledge of ātmā-tattva, giving up sva-dharma and even committing sins, do not fall. This verse is a contrast to the previous verse. Tathā na (not like that) is similar to saying “You are a fool, but I am not like that.” Or tathā can mean “on the other hand.” Persons who have at some time taken shelter of you (tāvakāḥ) never fall from the path, or if they do, they do not fall again. On the contrary they have achieved firm prema (baddha-sauhrḍāḥ) and therefore are completely (abhi) protected by you. O dearest of Lakṣmī (mādhava)! These devotees automatically possess all wealth. Or Mādhava can mean “O Lord who appeared in the Madhu dynasty!” You are most merciful. O master (prabho)! You are endowed with all śaktis. By the Lord’s powers, the devotees also possess such powers. Or because of having your power from the beginning, even if they disrespect your lotus feet, they do not fall like the jṣānīs mentioned in the previous verse. They are bound with prema for you. The devotees move without fear (vicaranti). They ascend to Vaikuṇṭha, making a ladder of the various paths which act as obstacles (vināyakanīkapa). The devotees regret the obstacles occurring within bhakti itself (caused by themselves) and by this they gain the great mercy of the Lord.

Personal Guidance
Even if a devotee of Mādhava slips, He personally protects them — bound by love, they fearlessly cross the heads of every opponent.
Practical Applications
  • Name one fall or failure you are still ashamed of, and trust that Mādhava — bound by your love — has already begun protecting you through it.
  • Refuse the inner verdict that a single slip disqualifies you from bhakti; renew your sādhana today exactly where you stopped.
  • When an 'opponent' rises today — a critic, a temptation, a fear — chant Kṛṣṇa's name once and walk forward, treating His promise na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati as literal.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I quietly presuming on Mādhava's protection — using 'a devotee never perishes' as license to take shortcuts in sādhana I would never tolerate in a fellow practitioner?
  • Like Bharata clinging to the deer, what specific affection am I cultivating right now that could become the very attachment I carry into my next moment, next day, next life?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When you blow an exam, get dumped, or wreck a friendship — the situations that feel like 'falling' at your age — notice whether you run away from chanting and association, or toward it. Mādhava's protection works through that returning, not around it.
  • Stop curating a 'devotee aesthetic' on social media while privately watching whatever you want. The verse is about baddha-sauhṛda — a real bond — not a public image. A small private vow kept (one round chanted before opening Instagram) builds that bond; a big public post does not.
  • Pick one person in your friend group who openly mocks your practice. Don't argue with them, don't hide from them — simply continue. The verse says devotees 'fearlessly traverse the heads of their opponents.' In your life right now this looks like: keep your tilak, keep your japa, keep your prasāda, and let the social cost be paid. You will discover Mādhava's protection only by exposing yourself to need it.
householder
  • When a major family decision goes sideways — a job lost, a child struggling in school, a financial hit — resist the householder reflex of 'I should have managed this better.' Read this verse with your spouse and ask together: where is Mādhava's protection in this circumstantial fall, and what is He preserving that we cannot yet see?
  • Teach your children the Bharata Mahārāja story this week — not as a fairy tale, but as a serious lesson about where the mind goes at the moment of death. Then ask them (and yourself): what attachment in our home life is shaping the mind that will face that moment? Adjust one concrete thing: a screen habit, a possession, a relationship pattern.
  • Householder life will throw you into compromises your brahmacārī self would have refused — late nights at work, skipped maṅgala-ārati, prasāda eaten in a hurry. Don't pretend you haven't compromised; also don't despair. Make one non-negotiable daily anchor (sixteen rounds, or one chapter of Bhāgavatam aloud to the family) that survives every compromise. That anchor is the baddha-sauhṛda the verse protects.
senior
  • At your stage, 'falling from the path' rarely looks like dramatic collapse — it looks like dimming enthusiasm, skipped programs, a quiet cynicism toward the younger devotees. Name your specific drift this week, out loud to a godbrother or godsister, and ask them to hold you to a single corrective.
  • Bharata Mahārāja's deer is the verse's gift to you specifically: at the time of death, the mind goes where it has been trained to go. Audit your attachments now — the grandchild, the property, the unfinished projects, the grievances — and begin the work of consciously offering each one to Mādhava. Not renouncing them (you still love them) but loosening the grip so the mind is free to remember Him at the end.
  • Mentor at least one younger devotee through their own 'fall' this year — without lecturing, without minimizing. Tell them honestly about a time you fell and how Mādhava drew you back. Your fearlessness in admitting the fall is itself the proof of the verse, and it is the legacy worth leaving — far more than any material inheritance.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as blanket insurance and easing off daily sādhana — Prabhupāda is explicit that the devotee 'should not deviate knowingly,' so treating protection as a safety net for chosen negligence inverts the teaching.
  • Romanticizing the 'fall' of Bharata or Citraketu as proof that detours are harmless or even picturesque, instead of seeing them as costly diversions that only Kṛṣṇa's mercy redeemed.
  • Claiming the dramatic deliverance ('won the case in one day') as personal entitlement — expecting miraculous rescues from self-made messes rather than steady, resourceful (dākṣya) action under Kṛṣṇa's shelter.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a time when you 'fell off the path' — skipped sādhana for weeks, broke a vow, drifted into viṣaya. Did you experience anything that felt like Mādhava's protection pulling you back, or did you have to claw your way back yourself? Be honest about which it was, and where the line is between protection and presumption.
  • The verse promises devotees fearlessly traverse the heads of their opponents. In your actual life, who or what are your 'opponents' right now — and are you fearless before them, or quietly terrified? If terrified, what does that say about whether you've truly built baddha-sauhṛda (loving bond) with Kṛṣṇa, or only the appearance of it?
  • Prabhupāda cites Bharata Mahārāja becoming a deer because he thought of a deer at death. Disagree with the person next to you: is that 'protection' (he eventually returned to bhakti) or is that 'punishment' (he had to suffer animal birth first)? What does your answer reveal about how you understand the Lord's mercy?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A plays a long-time devotee who has secretly stopped chanting rounds for six months, is ashamed, and has come to confide in a senior godbrother. Student B plays the senior godbrother. Student A wants reassurance: 'Kṛṣṇa protects His devotees, right? I haven't really fallen.' Student B must hold two things at once — the genuine consolation of SB 10.2.33 (yes, Mādhava protects, do not despair, do not run from Him) AND Prabhupāda's warning at the close of the purport ('a devotee should not deviate knowingly from the path of devotion'). Practice neither crushing A with guilt nor offering the cheap grace of 'don't worry, you're protected.' Then switch roles. Debrief: where did you slip into either harshness or sentimentality?

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like a spiritual 'get out of jail free' card. If devotees are protected even when they fall, doesn't that just license sloppy practice? Why bother with strict sādhana if Mādhava will catch me anyway?
    Read the purport's last line carefully — Prabhupāda explicitly closes that door: 'a devotee should not deviate knowingly from the path of devotion.' The protection is for the one who falls circumstantially, not the one who falls calculatedly. The very attitude of 'I can fall because He'll catch me' is itself the rupture of baddha-sauhṛda — the loving bond the verse names as the precondition for protection. A child who runs into traffic trusting the parent will catch them has already broken the relationship the trust was based on. Protection follows attachment to Him; it does not follow our cleverness about His mercy.
  • Citraketu became Vṛtrāsura — a demon who fought Indra. Bharata became a deer. If this is what 'protection' looks like, it sounds indistinguishable from ordinary karmic consequence. Where's the actual difference?
    The difference is not in the outward form of the next birth — it is in the trajectory. A non-devotee falling into demonic or animal birth typically continues the descent; the saṁskāras of bhakti are absent, so there is nothing to pull them back. Citraketu as Vṛtrāsura still glorified Kṛṣṇa at the moment of death; Bharata as a deer remembered his prior sādhana and became cautious, then took birth in a brahminical family and resumed bhakti (śucīnāṁ śrīmatāṁ gehe yoga-bhraṣṭo 'bhijāyate, BG 6.41). The protection is the seed of devotion that survives the fall and germinates again. Karma without bhakti is a closed loop; karma with bhakti has an exit, and Mādhava holds the door.
Analogy

A child of the king may fall while learning to walk, but the gardener does not pick him up — the father does, because the child is his. So the devotee who stumbles is not handed back to the demons; the Lord Himself bends down. The fall is real; the abandonment is impossible.

Stories
Ajāmila's last syllables

Ajāmila, the brāhmaṇa who had spent his life with a prostitute and fathered sons by her, called out at the moment of death to his youngest son — whose name happened to be Nārāyaṇa. The Viṣṇudūtas came, drove the Yamadūtas away, and protected him.

The Bhāgavatam (SB 6.1-3) treats this not as a fluke but as the precise meaning of the present verse: even one who had completely fallen from the brahminical path, having once had a real relationship with the holy name, is not allowed to be dragged off by Yama. Tāvakāḥ — His own — are protected even mid-fall.
