Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.4-5

Important Words
  • anurundhānāḥ — exactly following
    From anu-√rudh, 'to fall in line behind.' The verse marks a quiet schism in the Yadu dynasty itself — some followed Kaṁsa's policy, some followed Kṛṣṇa's. Genealogy does not save you; alignment does.
  • saptamaḥ — the seventh
    After six sons killed, the seventh is the turning-point. Number-symbolism here is deliberate: six are the senses (five plus mind) consumed by Kaṁsa's policy; the seventh is the soul itself — Ananta, who cannot be killed.
  • vaiṣṇavam — of Lord Viṣṇu
    Saṅkarṣaṇa is explicitly named vaiṣṇava-dhāma — Viṣṇu's own plenary expansion, second of the catur-vyūha. The verse anchors Kṛṣṇa's descent inside the Pāñcarātric catur-vyūha doctrine the Gauḍīyas inherit from the Mādhva line.
  • anantam — by the name Ananta
    From an-anta, 'without end.' Saṅkarṣaṇa as Ananta-Śeṣa is the bed on whom Viṣṇu reclines — the very ground of dhāma. Devakī's womb is, for these moments, the Kṣīrodaka ocean.
  • harṣa - śoka - vivardhanaḥ — increasing both joy and lamentation
    The compound is a single grammatical unit: one cause, two simultaneous emotions. Gauḍīya theology calls this saṁmilita-bhāva — opposite emotions co-arising in a single devotional moment. It is the seed of mādhurya-rasa, which Caitanya later perfected.
Some of their relatives, however, began to follow Kaṁsa’s principles and act in his service. After Kaṁsa, the son of Ugrasena, killed the six sons of Devakī, a plenary portion of Kṛṣṇa entered her womb as her seventh child, arousing her pleasure and her lamentation. That plenary portion is celebrated by great sages as Ananta, who belongs to Kṛṣṇa’s second quadruple expansion.
Purport

Some of the chief devotees, such as Akrūra, stayed with Kaṁsa to satisfy him. This they did for various purposes. They all expected the Supreme Personality of Godhead to appear as the eighth child as soon as Devakī’s other children were killed by Kaṁsa, and they were eagerly awaiting His appearance. By remaining in Kaṁsa’s association, they would be able to see the Supreme Personality of Godhead take birth and display His childhood pastimes, and Akrūra would later go to Vṛndāvana to bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā. The word paryupāsate is significant because it indicates that some devotees wanted to stay near Kaṁsa in order to see all these pastimes of the Lord. The six children killed by Kaṁsa had formerly been sons of Marīci, but because of having been cursed by a brāhmaṇa, they were obliged to take birth as grandsons of Hiraṇyakaśipu. Kaṁsa had taken birth as Kālanemi, and now he was obliged to kill his own sons. This was a mystery. As soon as the sons of Devakī were killed, they would return to their original place. The devotees wanted to see this also. Generally speaking, no one kills his own nephews, but Kaṁsa was so cruel that he did so without hesitation. Ananta, Saṅkarṣaṇa, belongs to the second catur-vyūha, or quadruple expansion. This is the opinion of experienced commentators.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Some of their relatives, however, began to follow Kaṁsa's principles and act in his service. After Kaṁsa, the son of Ugrasena, killed the six sons of Devakī, a plenary portion of Kṛṣṇa entered her womb as her seventh child, arousing her pleasure and her lamentation. That plenary portion is celebrated by great sages as Ananta, who belongs to Kṛṣṇa's second quadruple expansion.
Commentary

Being eager to see the Lord, some of the Yadus such as Akrura became cooperative with Kamsa and lived with him in Mathura. After Kamsa killed the first six children, the seventh appeared in Devaki’s womb. He is famous as Ananta. What is his description? He is described as vaisnava dhama, That means an amsa of Krsna. That he appeared in the womb was a cause of jubilation, but that he would be killed by Kamsa was a cause of lamentation.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Some of the Yadus’ relatives followed Kaṁsa. This means that they skillfully were able to control him. Or, some devotees did this because they were waiting to see Kṛṣṇa. This will later be revealed by Akrūra when he goes to Vraja. Paryupāsate should be paryupāsata. The six children who were killed were previously devatā sons of Marīci but by offending Brahmā they became demons. They were born to Kālanemi, son of Hiraṇyakaśipu, as his six sons known as the ṣaḍ-garbha. Rejecting Hiraṇyakaśipu they began worshipping Brahmā. Angry at that, Hiraṇyakaśipu cursed them to be killed by their father. Remembering this incident, the Lord had Yogamāyā put them in the womb of Devakī, to be killed by Kaṁsa, who was previously their father Kālameni. This is explained in Hari-vamśa. Later it will be said that they appeared from Hiraṇyakaśipu (hiraṇyakaśipor jātā nītās te yoga-māyayā ( SB 10.85.48). To harmonize the two statements, the meaning should be that they were born as Kālameni’s sons, in Hiraṇyakaśipu’s lineage. Thus in the later verse it is simply stated they appeared (jātāḥ) from Hiraṇyakaśipu, and that does not mention they were his sons. This is also stated in Mahābhārata. Or, there can be different versions in different kalpas. Though they were killed, it is explained in Chapter Eighty-five that they attained liberation from hellish condition. Thus no one can accuse the Lord of being indifferent to their killing. Because of his anger Śukadeva does not mention Kaṁsa’s name but instead calls him “son of Ugrasena.” The wise call the seventh child Saṅkarṣaṇa (anantam), the second member of the caturvyūha, whose body was filled with Kṛṣṇa (vaiṣṇavam).

Personal Guidance
Even amid Kaṁsa's tyranny, devotees stay close to witness Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — the Lord arranges every cruelty and joy to set the stage for His appearance.
Practical Applications
  • Notice one situation today where you've labeled circumstances 'bad' and ask what pastime of Kṛṣṇa they may be setting the stage for.
  • Like Akrūra remaining near Kaṁsa, stay engaged in your difficult post today rather than fleeing — serve with the inner aim of seeing Kṛṣṇa act through it.
  • Refuse the impulse to resolve every harṣa-śoka swing in your day; sit with both pleasure and lamentation as Devakī did, awaiting the Lord within.
Reflection Questions
  • Like Akrūra remaining in Kaṁsa's court to witness Kṛṣṇa's appearance, where am I being asked to stay inside an uncomfortable or compromising situation — not to serve it, but to be present for the Lord's work that will unfold there?
  • Devakī's womb carried both harṣa and śoka simultaneously when Ananta entered — where in my own life am I refusing to hold joy and lamentation together, demanding that surrender feel like only one or the other?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Identify the 'Kaṁsa-court' in your life right now — the friend group, the group chat, the campus scene whose values you actually disagree with. Are you there as Akrūra (present with a clear devotional purpose) or just because leaving is socially expensive? Write down which one, honestly, this week.
  • Devakī carried both joy and grief in the same pregnancy. Next time a result comes in — an exam grade, an internship rejection, a like-count on a post — practice holding the swing without immediately posting, venting, or numbing. Sit with the harṣa-śoka for ten minutes and offer it.
  • The six previous sons had been cursed and were working off karma through Kaṁsa's hand — a hidden order behind apparent injustice. When something feels unfair this semester (a grade, a breakup, being left out), resist the impulse to immediately narrate yourself as victim. Ask instead: what is being burned off here, and what is being prepared?
householder
  • You almost certainly work or live inside at least one 'Kaṁsa-system' — a workplace, an industry, an extended-family dynamic whose values are not yours. Apply Akrūra's three tests this month: is there a specific service only you can render here, is your inner alignment intact, and are you actually ready to act when the moment comes? If not all three, plan your exit; if yes, stay with explicit intent.
  • Devakī's joy-and-grief is the householder's daily condition — the same child who delights you also exposes your selfishness; the same career that funds your sādhana also drains it. Stop trying to resolve that tension into pure joy. Each evening, name one harṣa and one śoka of the day and offer both to Kṛṣṇa, the way Devakī carried both at once.
  • When raising your children, do not flatten the spiritual story into 'be nice.' Tell them about Kaṁsa, about Devakī's loss, about Akrūra's strange patience — let them inherit a tradition with real moral weight, not a sanitized version that will not survive their first contact with adult cruelty.
senior
  • Akrūra waited years inside Kaṁsa's court for one specific act of service — bringing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā. In this season of life, identify the one or two acts of service that may be the real reason you are still here, and stop scattering your energies. The rest is paryupāsate — sitting near, ready.
  • Devakī lost six children before the seventh was even conceived. You have likely lost more than you expected by now — friends, parents, capacities, perhaps a child. The tradition does not ask you to call that loss good; it asks you to carry harṣa and śoka together, as she did, without letting either calcify into bitterness or denial. Practice this in your daily remembrance.
  • Mentor a younger householder or youth this year specifically on the difference between real compassion (serving the soul) and material compassion (smoothing over bodily discomfort). Your age gives you authority on this that no one in their twenties has. Do not waste it on generic advice — give them the Gītā's pivot, in your own words, from your own losses.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading Akrūra's outward service to Kaṁsa as license to stay entangled in any compromising association — forgetting that his presence there was specifically Kṛṣṇa-conscious, purposeful, and temporary, not a justification for ordinary attachment.
  • Treating the killing of Devakī's six sons as mere narrative cruelty and missing the deeper teaching — that what looks like loss in this world is often the soul returning to its original position, which only the devotee's vision can perceive.
  • Conflating Ananta-Saṅkarṣaṇa with Kṛṣṇa Himself, or dismissing Him as a 'lesser' expansion — both errors collapse the personal, hierarchical structure of the catur-vyūha into either impersonalism or demigod-worship.
Discussion Prompts
  • Akrūra and other devotees stayed inside Kaṁsa's court — eating his food, attending his meetings — purely to be near where Kṛṣṇa would appear. Where in your own life are you tolerating an environment you find distasteful because the spiritual payoff is real, and where are you just rationalizing comfort? Push each other on the difference.
  • Devakī's seventh pregnancy is described as harṣa-śoka-vivardhanaḥ — simultaneously increasing her joy and her lamentation. Share a recent situation where bhakti has produced both feelings in you at once. Does the modern instinct to 'resolve' that tension actually serve the soul, or does Devakī's example suggest something else?
  • The verse says 'some' relatives followed Kaṁsa's principles to serve him — implying others did not, and were presumably killed or exiled. If you had been in that family, which group would you honestly have joined, and on what grounds? Don't let each other off with the pious answer.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a devotee working at a tech company whose product they've come to believe is harming users (think social media engagement traps). They are considering quitting on principle. Student B is an older Vaiṣṇava friend who points out that Akrūra stayed in Kaṁsa's court — not to enjoy it, but because the Lord's work required someone to be positioned there. Student B argues for staying with paryupāsate-like intent: present, internally non-aligned, awaiting the moment of useful service. Student A must distinguish this from self-deceiving rationalization. Run the conversation for ten minutes; then swap and run it again with the roles reversed. Debrief: what tests would you apply to know which side of that line you are actually on?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole story is mythological — a tyrant king killing infants because of a prophecy, a 'plenary portion' descending into a womb. Why are we extracting life-guidance from a fairy tale instead of just naming the moral (don't be cruel) and moving on?
    The Bhāgavatam is not asking you to believe a fairy tale; it is presenting Kṛṣṇa-līlā as the deepest layer of reality, recorded by liberated sages. But even if a skeptic brackets the metaphysics, notice what gets lost by reducing the verse to 'don't be cruel': you lose Akrūra's strategic patience inside an evil court, you lose Devakī's harṣa-śoka as a model for devotion under pressure, you lose Ananta as the second catur-vyūha — the structural fact that Kṛṣṇa's expansions precede and prepare His appearance. The 'just take the moral' approach is precisely the impersonalist drift the tradition warns against. We hold the personal Kṛṣṇa, His pastimes, and His devotees as real — and only then do the practical lessons land with weight.
  • The devotees stayed near Kaṁsa to 'see the pastimes.' Isn't that just spiritual voyeurism — proximity to power dressed up as devotion? Plenty of people today justify staying in toxic systems by saying they're 'doing God's work from inside.'
    A fair pushback, and the tradition itself names the test. Akrūra's staying was paryupāsate — sitting near with worshipful intent, awaiting a specific service that only he could render (later, going to Vṛndāvana to bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma). Three marks distinguish him from the modern rationalizer. First, the service was assigned by the situation, not chosen for prestige — he was not enjoying Kaṁsa's wealth. Second, his internal alignment never shifted — he never began defending Kaṁsa, only enduring him. Third, when the moment of real service came, he acted, even at risk. Apply those three tests to anyone (including yourself) claiming to do God's work from inside a Kaṁsa-court. If the service is vague, the alignment has drifted, and the moment of action keeps getting postponed — it is voyeurism, or worse, complicity. Bhakti is exacting on this point.
Analogy

Before a great king's birth, his elder brother is sometimes sent ahead as the standard-bearer. Ananta-Saṅkarṣaṇa enters Devakī's womb as the Lord's own standard going up the flagpole — and her simultaneous joy and grief are the people in the courtyard, half cheering, half weeping, knowing the procession has begun.

Stories
Akrūra in Kaṁsa's court

Akrūra was the noblest of the Yadus — a devotee who could have fled with the rest, but who chose to remain inside Kaṁsa's Mathurā. He served the tyrant outwardly, kept his sādhana inwardly, and was the one entrusted with bringing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma from Vraja to Mathurā.

The Bhāgavatam later (10.38) shows him meditating on the dust of Kṛṣṇa's feet as his chariot rolls toward Vṛndāvana, tears falling on the reins. His example glosses this verse: 'some relatives' stayed not from cowardice or collusion but because the Lord needed an inside witness.
