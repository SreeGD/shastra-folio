Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.11-12

Important Words
  • nāmadheyāni — names
    Nāma-dheya — the act of name-giving. The verse acknowledges name-pluralism as a fact of religious geography while keeping the underlying identity (Yoga-māyā) intact. Gauḍīyas read it as license for inclusive recognition, not for syncretism.
  • vaiṣṇavī iti — the name Vaiṣṇavī
    Of all her names, the Vaiṣṇavas pick this one — Vaiṣṇavī, 'belonging to Viṣṇu.' Prabhupāda emphasizes that the goddess in her highest mood is the Lord's own potency, not an independent deity.
  • kṛṣṇā — the name Kṛṣṇā (feminine)
    Note the long ā — the feminine form. She is the śakti of the Śaktimān; her name echoes His. This is a structural hint of Rādhā-tattva, though Rādhā is hlādinī-śakti distinct from Yoga-māyā.
  • mādhavī — the name Mādhavī
    From Mādhava ('husband of Lakṣmī,' a name of Kṛṣṇa). Her name is a derivative of His; the feminine takes its meaning from the masculine. Gauḍīya theology: every śakti is named for the Śaktimān she serves.
  • māyā — the name Māyā
    Here she is openly called Māyā — but the verse has already established her as Yoga-māyā (internal energy), not mahā-māyā (external). The same word covers both potencies; only context discriminates.
Lord Kṛṣṇa blessed Māyādevī by saying: In different places on the surface of the earth, people will give you different names, such as Durgā, Bhadrakālī, Vijayā, Vaiṣṇavī, Kumudā, Caṇḍikā, Kṛṣṇā, Mādhavī, Kanyakā, Māyā, Nārāyaṇī, Īśānī, Śāradā and Ambikā.
Purport

Because Kṛṣṇa and His energy appeared simultaneously, people have generally formed two groups — the śāktas and the Vaiṣṇavas — and sometimes there is rivalry between them. Essentially, those who are interested in material enjoyment are śāktas, and those interested in spiritual salvation and attaining the spiritual kingdom are Vaiṣṇavas. Because people are generally interested in material enjoyment, they are interested in worshiping Māyādevī, the energy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Vaiṣṇavas, however, are śuddha-śāktas, or pure bhaktas, because the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra indicates worship of the Supreme Lord’s energy, Harā. A Vaiṣṇava prays to the energy of the Lord for the opportunity to serve the Lord along with His spiritual energy. Thus Vaiṣṇavas all worship such Deities as Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, Sītā-Rāma, Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa and Rukmiṇī-Dvārakādhīśa, whereas durgā-śāktas worship the material energy under different names. The names by which Māyādevī is known in different places have been listed by Vallabhācārya as follows. In Vārāṇasī she is known as Durgā, in Avantī she is known as Bhadrakālī, in Orissa she is known as Vijayā, and in Kulahāpura she is known as Vaiṣṇavī or Mahālakṣmī. (The representatives of Mahālakṣmī and Ambikā are present in Bombay.) In the country known as Kāmarūpa she is known as Caṇḍikā, in Northern India as Śāradā, and in Cape Comorin as Kanyakā. Thus she is distributed according to various names in various places. Śrīla Vijayadhvaja Tīrthapāda, in his Pada-ratnāvalī-ṭīkā, has explained the meanings of the different representations. Māyā is known as Durgā because she is approached with great difficulty, as Bhadrā because she is auspicious, and as Kālī because she is deep blue. Because she is the most powerful energy, she is known as Vijayā; because she is one of the different energies of Viṣṇu, she is known as Vaiṣṇavī; and because she enjoys in this material world and gives facilities for material enjoyment, she is known as Kumudā. Because she is very severe to her enemies, the asuras, she is known as Caṇḍikā, and because she gives all sorts of material facilities, she is called Kṛṣṇā. In this way the material energy is differently named and situated in different places on the surface of the globe.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Lord Kṛṣṇa blessed Māyādevī by saying: In different places on the surface of the earth, people will give you different names, such as Durgā, Bhadrakālī, Vijayā, Vaiṣṇavī, Kumudā, Caṇḍikā, Kṛṣṇā, Mādhavī, Kanyakā, Māyā, Nārāyaṇī, Īśānī, Śāradā and Ambikā.
Commentary

Kurvanti (present tense) here indicates the future. "Men will call you by various names and establish many temples for you." This means "By my and your appearance on earth some men will become vaisnavas and others will become saktas."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The two verses are taken together. The future tense is meant. Men, situated on the earth, meaning all men, will make temples and give you different names. According to their particular desires with different attitudes towards your deity forms, they will give you names like Durgā. According to different places you will be known by different names. In some places you will be known by one name, in other places by two names and in other places by many names. The word ca indicates that these are the main names among all the names.

Personal Guidance
Māyādevī wears many names across the earth, but she remains Kṛṣṇa's energy — a Vaiṣṇava worships her only as Harā, the Lord's servant.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today when you are tempted to petition material energy under any of her names for enjoyment, and redirect that prayer to Kṛṣṇa through the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra.
  • Refuse the śākta-impulse to chase facilities for bodily enjoyment, and instead ask Harā for one concrete opportunity to serve Kṛṣṇa today.
  • Speak respectfully of Durgā, Kālī, and Caṇḍikā as Kṛṣṇa's energy — never as independent supreme deities — when the topic comes up with family or friends.
Reflection Questions
  • Under which of Māyādevī's many names — security, beauty, prestige, family safety, intellectual mastery — am I most comfortably worshiping the material energy while telling myself I am being spiritual?
  • What would it actually look like, this week, for me to approach Harā as the Lord's spiritual energy and ask for service, rather than approaching her as Durgā and asking for material protection and provision?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit one week of your prayers — including the silent ones before exams, before posting, before texting a crush. How many were 'Kṛṣṇa, give me this result' (śākta) versus 'Kṛṣṇa, let me serve You through this' (Vaiṣṇava)? Write the count down; don't moralize, just see.
  • Pick one upcoming situation where you are tempted to ask the universe for a specific outcome — an admission, a follower count, a relationship. Practice nishkama-karma: do the work fully, and consciously decline to bargain with the energy for the fruit.
  • When friends say 'all gods are the same, pick whichever vibe you like,' resist both reactions — neither nodding along nor lecturing. Instead ask them, and yourself, 'same for what purpose? enjoyment or surrender?' Let that one question do the work.
householder
  • Notice the family rituals where you are quietly approaching the Lord's energy for material results — a son's job, a daughter's match, a promotion, a court case. Keep doing them with full sincerity, but add one line at the end: 'and above all, let this household become Yours.' Re-aim the same act.
  • When making a household financial or career decision this month, apply discriminative wisdom: ask not only 'will this give us more comfort?' but 'will this give us more capacity to serve?' If the answer to the second is no, it is a śākta decision wearing a Vaiṣṇava costume — be honest about it.
  • Teach your children the names in this verse without flattening them. Tell them Durgā, Kālī, Caṇḍikā are real, powerful, and serve Kṛṣṇa — not rival gods, not the same as Kṛṣṇa. Children can hold this distinction earlier than we credit them; muddled parents produce muddled bhakti.
senior
  • Look back at the last forty years of your prayers and vows. How much was addressed, in effect, to Māyādevī under one of her many names — for protection, prosperity, progeny, removal of obstacles? No regret; just clarity. Then redirect what remains of your prayer-life toward Kṛṣṇa Himself.
  • Practice tyāga concretely with the possessions, titles, and relationships you have accumulated — these are gifts of the Lord's energy, due to be returned. Begin handing them over now, in daana and in succession planning, rather than letting māyā collect them at the end.
  • When mentoring younger family members or students, do not preach against the goddesses or other paths — Prabhupāda did not. Simply keep pointing, again and again, to the difference between asking the energy for facility and asking the Energetic for service. Let your final years be that one clear signal.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating the long list of names (Durgā, Kālī, Caṇḍikā, Ambikā, etc.) as proof that 'all paths are one' and that worship of the material energy is equivalent to worship of Kṛṣṇa — the verse distinguishes śāktas from Vaiṣṇavas, it does not collapse them.
  • Disrespecting Māyādevī or her worshipers as a way of feeling like a 'real' Vaiṣṇava — Kṛṣṇa Himself blesses her and assigns her these names; the Vaiṣṇava becomes a śuddha-śākta by serving the Lord with His energy, not by despising the energy.
  • Reducing 'I want spiritual life' to a slightly more refined shopping list addressed to Māyādevī — praying for a peaceful home, a good marriage, health, and success and labeling that as bhakti, when in substance it is still durgā-śākta worship dressed in Vaiṣṇava clothes.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda calls Vaiṣṇavas 'śuddha-śāktas' because the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra addresses Harā, Kṛṣṇa's energy. Where in your own sādhana have you been approaching Māyādevī (asking the energy for facility) when you thought you were approaching Kṛṣṇa? Be specific — what did you actually pray for last week?
  • The verse lists fourteen names for the same Māyādevī across fourteen regions. Modern interfaith voices use this kind of list to argue 'all paths lead to the same goal.' Prabhupāda explicitly distinguishes śāktas from Vaiṣṇavas and says they sometimes have rivalry. Where do you draw the line between honoring difference and falling into a flat 'all the same' that this verse refuses?
  • Prabhupāda says people are 'generally interested in material enjoyment' and therefore worship Māyādevī under her many names. Look at your own circle — family, coworkers, the people you spiritually admire. Who is actually a Vaiṣṇava in the technical sense Prabhupāda gives, and who is, gently, a śākta? Does naming this honestly feel like discrimination or like discriminative wisdom?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A plays a sincere relative who has just returned from a trip to Vārāṇasī, full of devotion to Durgā, and says, 'Durgā, Kṛṣṇa, Nārāyaṇa — it is all one śakti, all one energy, the names don't matter. Even your Bhāgavatam lists fourteen names for the same goddess.' Student B plays the Vaiṣṇava family member who has to respond in the next two minutes — without insulting the relative's faith, without flattening the distinction Prabhupāda draws, and without retreating into 'well, you do you.' Switch roles. Then debrief: where did each of you slip into either rudeness or false agreement?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole verse and purport sound sectarian. Prabhupāda is essentially saying 'our worship is pure, theirs is material.' Isn't that exactly the kind of religious one-upmanship the modern world is trying to leave behind?
    The distinction is not sectarian, it is diagnostic. Prabhupāda is not ranking communities by birth or label — he is naming what each worshiper is actually asking for. If I approach the Lord's energy for a job, a spouse, a healthy child, a long life, I am, by definition, a śākta in that moment, regardless of which Deity I stand in front of. If I approach the same energy asking only for the chance to serve Kṛṣṇa, I am a śuddha-śākta. The line runs through each of our hearts before it runs between traditions. Far from being arrogant, this verse forces a Vaiṣṇava to constantly check whether his own prayer is actually devotional or just dressed-up viṣaya. Real respect for other paths begins with the honesty to see what is being asked for, in them and in us.
  • Why would Kṛṣṇa Himself bless His own māyā with all these glorious names and worldwide worship if māyā is the very thing keeping souls bound? It seems contradictory — the Lord is sponsoring the illusion.
    Māyā is not Kṛṣṇa's enemy; she is His faithful servant, doing exactly the work He assigns. Souls who insist on enjoying separately from Kṛṣṇa need a manager, and she is that manager — strict, generous, and impartial. By blessing her with these names, Kṛṣṇa is arranging that those who are not yet ready for direct surrender still have a structured way to grow, receive their karma, and slowly become exhausted with material enjoyment. The Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra calls out to Harā precisely because she is the one who can release the conditioned soul back to her Lord. The 'contradiction' dissolves once you see māyā as Kṛṣṇa's arrangement of tough love, not as a rival kingdom.
Analogy

A single river is named differently by every village it flows through — Gaṅgā in Hardwar, Bhāgīrathī in the Himālayas, Padmā in Bengal — but the water is one. Yoga-māyā's many names are these village-names of one Śakti; the Vaiṣṇava remembers the river's source.
