Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.17

स बिभ्रत् पौरुषं धाम भ्राजमानो यथा रवि: । दुरासदोऽतिदुर्धर्षो भूतानां सम्बभूव ह ॥ १७ ॥

Important Words
  • pauruṣam — pertaining to the Supreme Person
    Adjective from puruṣa — 'belonging to the Person.' The dhāma Vasudeva carries is not abstract effulgence but specifically the Person's effulgence. Gauḍīya theology: brahmajyoti is the bodily glow of Bhagavān, not an independent absolute.
  • dhāma — spiritual effulgence / abode
    Third occurrence in this chapter — dhāma is the chapter's keyword. The same word names Saṅkarṣaṇa (v.4-5), the Lord's plenary expansion (v.8), and here His radiance. All three senses ride on one word; the Sanskrit refuses to flatten them.
  • raviḥ — the sun
    The standard Vaiṣṇava analogy: Bhagavān is like the sun, His effulgence (Brahman) like sunlight, His localized presence (Paramātmā) like the sun-rays entering each room. Vasudeva is described as like the sun — the analogy of analogies.
  • durāsadaḥ — difficult to approach
    From dur-ā-√sad — 'hard to sit beside.' Even Kaṁsa, the demon-king, cannot bring his hostile attention to bear on a man carrying the Lord. The devotee's shield is not effort but the Lord's own presence in him.
  • ati - durdharṣaḥ — exceedingly unassailable
    Ati intensifies dur-dhṛṣ — 'extremely impossible to overpower.' This is the practical promise of the verse: bhakti renders the devotee untouchable not by strength of arm but by the Lord's residence.
While carrying the form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead within the core of his heart, Vasudeva bore the Lord’s transcendentally illuminating effulgence, and thus he became as bright as the sun. He was therefore very difficult to see or approach through sensory perception. Indeed, he was unapproachable and unperceivable even for such formidable men as Kaṁsa, and not only for Kaṁsa but for all living entities.
Purport

The word dhāma is significant. Dhāma refers to the place where the Supreme Personality of Godhead resides. In the beginning of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.1.1) it is said, dhāmnā svena sadā nirasta-kuhakaṁ satyaṁ paraṁ dhīmahi. In the abode of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, there is no influence of material energy ( dhāmnā svena sadā nirasta-kuhakam ). Any place where the Supreme Personality of Godhead is present by His name, form, qualities or paraphernalia immediately becomes a dhāma. For example, we speak of Vṛndāvana-dhāma, Dvārakā-dhāma and Mathurā-dhāma because in these places the name, fame, qualities and paraphernalia of the Supreme Godhead are always present. Similarly, if one is empowered by the Supreme Personality of Godhead to do something, the core of his heart becomes a dhāma, and thus he becomes so extraordinarily powerful that not only his enemies but also people in general are astonished to observe his activities. Because he is unapproachable, his enemies are simply struck with wonder, as explained here by the words durāsado ’tidurdharsaḥ. The words pauruṣaṁ dhāma have been explained by various ācāryas. Śrī Vīrarāghava Ācārya says that these words refer to the effulgence of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Vijayadhvaja says that they signify viṣṇu-tejas, and Śukadeva says bhagavat-svarūpa. The Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī says that these words indicate the influence of the Supreme Lord’s effulgence, and Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura says that they signify the appearance of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
While carrying the form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead within the core of his heart, Vasudeva bore the Lord's transcendentally illuminating effulgence, and thus he became as bright as the sun. He was therefore very difficult to see or approach through sensory perception. Indeed, he was unapproachable and unperceivable even for such formidable men as Kaṁsa, and not only for Kaṁsa but for all living entities.
Commentary

Vasudeva was holding the power (dhama) of the Lord (paurusam) . Or it can mean "Vasudeva became effulgent on seeing the appearance of the Lord within himself." According to the Visvakosa, dhama means, body, house, ray, place, birth and influence. Durasadah means that other living entities could not approach him. Ati durdharsa means that Kamsa and others could not defeat him.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Vasudeva took on the effulgence of the Lord (pauruṣaṁ dhāma) because the Lord’s power appeared in his mind. Or dhāma can mean manifestation or power. Viśva-kośa says that dhāṁa means body, house, rays, place, birth or power. People could not approach him or look at him (durāsadaḥ) and it was impossible to defeat him. This clearly (ha) happened in a pronounced way (sam).

Personal Guidance
When you carry Kṛṣṇa in the core of your heart, His dhāma shines through you — even your fiercest enemies cannot touch what He protects.
Practical Applications
  • Begin the day by consciously installing Kṛṣṇa in the core of your heart through japa, and return to that seat whenever you feel exposed or afraid.
  • Notice when you try to protect yourself through image, argument, or aggression — and instead let the Lord's presence within be your real shield today.
  • Refuse to measure your strength by what others can see; serve quietly in one task today, trusting that the dhāma you carry within is the only durāsada power that matters.
Reflection Questions
  • What in my life — a service, a responsibility, a relationship — am I empowered by Kṛṣṇa to carry, and am I bearing it with the gravitas of a dhāma or treating it as merely my own clever doing?
  • Where do I shrink from being 'durāsada' — unapproachable to flattery, gossip, or compromise — because I would rather be liked by Kaṁsa-like influences than guarded by the Lord's effulgence within?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick the one social-media app that most easily 'approaches' you — the one that reads your moods and pulls you in. For one week, log out of it on your phone entirely; let your heart be a little less easy to walk into. Notice what tries to fill the space.
  • Before your next exam or big submission, spend two minutes silently placing Kṛṣṇa in the core of your heart and offering the work to Him. Then go in. Watch how differently peer panic, comparison, and the urge to cheat or copy actually land on you when you are not the one carrying the result.
  • Identify the one friend or group chat that most easily talks you out of your sva-dharma as a student — late nights, gossip, pulling you down when you try to study or chant. You do not need to break the friendship; you need to become slightly durāsada in that one direction. Practice one clear, kind 'no' this week without explanation or apology.
householder
  • Before any significant family decision this week — a purchase, a school choice, a difficult conversation with your spouse — pause and consciously place Kṛṣṇa in the core of your heart first. Make the decision from that seat, not from fear, in-laws' opinions, or LinkedIn-style comparison with other families.
  • Your children and colleagues are constantly trying to 'read' you — to find which lever (guilt, flattery, anger, money) gets the response they want. For one week, practice being lovingly durāsada to manipulation: warm in presence, unmoved by leverage. Notice which family member is most disturbed by this; that is your diagnostic.
  • Make one room or corner of your home an actual dhāma — Deities or a picture of Kṛṣṇa, a clean āsana, your japa-mālā, no phones. Sit there for fifteen minutes every morning before the household wakes. The home that has a dhāma at its center cannot be approached by māyā in the same way as one that does not.
senior
  • You are now closer than ever to the next 'transition' the body will make. Begin treating your heart, not your bank account or property file, as the most important asset to be in order — daily japa, hearing Bhāgavatam, association of devotees. This is the dhāma you actually carry across.
  • Younger family members are watching to see whether a life of sādhana produces something visibly different at the end. Without preaching, let your composure around illness, loss, and money decisions become its own quiet teaching — a pauruṣaṁ dhāma they cannot quite argue with.
  • Identify the two or three attachments — a property dispute, an old grievance, an identity as 'the one who provides' — that still make you very approachable to anxiety and anger. Name them aloud before the Deity each morning and consciously hand them to Kṛṣṇa. Loosening these now is how the heart becomes light enough to actually hold Him at the time of leaving.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading Vasudeva's effulgence as a metaphor for personal charisma or self-confidence, and then cultivating ahaṅkāra-driven 'presence' instead of carrying Kṛṣṇa within the heart.
  • Assuming that because the Lord empowers His devotee, one can court danger or provoke Kaṁsa-figures recklessly — forgetting that Vasudeva still moved with vairāgya, hrīḥ, and discretion, not bravado.
  • Treating 'unapproachable to sensory perception' as license to become aloof, cold, or socially withdrawn from family and duties, when the verse points to spiritual inaccessibility to envy and exploitation, not to ordinary human warmth.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva became 'unapproachable' the moment Kṛṣṇa entered the core of his heart. Share with your partner: when have you seen someone whose inner life made them genuinely difficult to manipulate or sway — and how did that differ from mere stubbornness or arrogance? Where would you, honestly, still be easy to approach in the wrong way?
  • The verse says Vasudeva was unperceivable 'even for such formidable men as Kaṁsa.' Disagree productively: is this a literal protective effulgence, or is the Bhāgavatam describing a sober psychological reality — that those carrying Kṛṣṇa within cannot be read by purely materially-motivated people? Pick a side and defend it.
  • Prabhupāda says the heart of an empowered devotee itself becomes a dhāma. If that is true, what specifically in your daily routine is currently making your heart un-dhāma-like — what guests are you hosting there instead? Be concrete with your partner; no spiritual abstractions allowed.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a devotee who has been quietly deepening sādhana for six months — more japa, less social media, fewer late-night outings. Student B is an old friend (the 'Kaṁsa' figure in miniature — not evil, just materially calibrated) who senses the change and shows up to 'check in,' actually trying to pull A back into the old group dynamics: 'You've gotten weird, you used to be fun, are you in a cult?' A must respond from the dhāma-bhāva of this verse — not defensive, not preachy, not apologetic, but genuinely durāsada (hard to grab onto) without being cold. Run it for five minutes, then swap. Debrief: where did A leak? Where did A's effulgence actually show?

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like spiritual narcissism dressed up in Sanskrit. 'I am so full of God that ordinary people can't even perceive me' — isn't this exactly the kind of self-flattery that makes religious people insufferable?
    A fair worry, and the verse itself protects against it. Notice: Vasudeva does not declare his own effulgence — the Bhāgavatam describes it, and Vasudeva himself behaves with extraordinary humility, even bowing to Kaṁsa to save the child. The dhāma is not his; he is carrying Kṛṣṇa. The moment a sādhaka thinks 'I have become unapproachable,' the dhāma has already left, because ahaṅkāra has moved back in. The test of real pauruṣaṁ dhāma is the opposite of narcissism — it is the quiet inability to be flattered, frightened, or bought. If someone is loudly inaccessible, that is just rajas with a tilak. Real durāsada looks, from the outside, simply like a person you cannot quite use.
  • The verse implies Kṛṣṇa is a localized person who 'enters' a heart and emits literal light. Modern readers will say this is mythological — surely the deeper meaning is that we all already have a divine spark, and 'Kṛṣṇa within' is just a colorful way of pointing to that universal Self.
    The Gauḍīya line will respectfully but firmly decline that translation. Prabhupāda is precise here: dhāma means the place where the Supreme Personality of Godhead resides, and Kṛṣṇa is a person — sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha — not a metaphor for an impersonal Self we already are. If 'Kṛṣṇa within' simply meant the divine spark every jīva already carries, then Vasudeva would have looked like the sun his entire life, and so would Kaṁsa. The whole point of the verse is that something new happened — the Supreme Person, by His own will, took residence — and that this is categorically different from the eternal ātman every living being already possesses. Collapsing the two erases bhakti, because you cannot surrender to, serve, or love a 'spark within yourself.' You can only do that with a Person who is other than you and dearer than you.
Analogy

When a lamp of pure ghee is placed inside a clay pot, the pot itself grows hot to the touch; no one can put a hand on it without recoiling. Vasudeva, carrying the Lord in his heart, becomes that pot — Kaṁsa's eyes cannot rest on him. The brightness is not Vasudeva's; he is only the vessel.
