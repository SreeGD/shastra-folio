Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.41

दिष्टय‍ाम्ब ते कुक्षिगत: पर: पुमा- नंशेन साक्षाद् भगवान् भवाय न: । माभूद् भयं भोजपतेर्मुमूर्षो- र्गोप्ता यदूनां भविता तवात्मज: ॥ ४१ ॥

Important Words
  • amba — O mother
    Ambā — mother. The demigods address Devakī directly with the most tender vocative. From cosmic praise to maternal salutation in two verses — the Bhāgavatam's range is its theology.
  • kukṣi - gataḥ — gone into the womb
    Kukṣi — womb. Gata — gone. The technical word for what has happened: the Lord has entered her body. The compound is utterly factual; the demigods do not theologize her — they inform her.
  • paraḥ - pumān — the Supreme Person
    Para-puruṣa — the highest Person. Same phrasing as the Puruṣa-sūkta and the Bhagavad-gītā's Puruṣottama. The Gauḍīyas anchor Kṛṣṇa as the puruṣa of all puruṣa-stutis, including this one.
  • goptā — the protector
    Goptā — protector, guardian, herdsman. One of the six limbs of śaraṇāgati is goptṛtve varaṇam, 'accepting Him as guardian.' Kṛṣṇa is explicitly named goptā here — and the very next verses see Him about to be born as a gopa, a cowherd. The wordplay is the prophecy.
  • tava ātmajaḥ — your son
    Ātma-ja — 'born of the self,' a tender word for a son. The Supreme Soul (sarva-ātmā) is paradoxically becoming Devakī's ātma-ja. Acintya-bhedābheda once more: He is the soul of all souls, and yet specifically her son. The pronoun tava (your) makes the cosmic ātmā the property of one mother.
O mother Devakī, by your good fortune and ours, the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself, with all His plenary portions, such as Baladeva, is now within your womb. Therefore you need not fear Kaṁsa, who has decided to be killed by the Lord. Your eternal son, Kṛṣṇa, will be the protector of the entire Yadu dynasty.
Purport

The words paraḥ pumān aṁśena signify that Kṛṣṇa is the original Supreme Personality of Godhead. This is the verdict of the śāstra ( kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam ). Thus the demigods assured Devakī, “Your son is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and He is appearing with Baladeva, His plenary portion. He will give you all protection and kill Kaṁsa, who has decided to continue his enmity toward the Lord and thus be killed by Him.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O mother Devakī, by your good fortune and ours, the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself, with all His plenary portions, such as Baladeva, is now within your womb. Therefore you need not fear Kaṁsa, who has decided to be killed by the Lord. Your eternal son, Kṛṣṇa, will be the protector of the entire Yadu dynasty.
Commentary

To console Devaki, they now praise her. "The Lord has appeared in your womb along with Baladeva (amsena)." Or the sentence can also mean "The Lord (saksat bhagavan), whose as amsa is the para puman, the one who glances over prakrti (Mahavisnu), has appeared in you womb to bestow auspiciousness. (bhavaya)."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Having praised the Lord while being invisible to others, the devatās then pacified Devakī who was astonished by their presence. O mother! She was most respectable because she was the mother of the Lord. Though it was said that she held the Lord in her mind, the Lord then appeared in her womb (kukṣi-gataḥ). Thus it became suitable for the devatās to address her as a mother. The Supreme Lord Svayam Bhagavān, endowed with all powers--not his expansion , appeared in her womb along with Balarāma (aṁśena). It should be understood that he appeared within her in order to be born. He has appeared for our benefit. What to speak of appearing for your and Vasudeva’s benefit, he has appeared for us (naḥ) also. Or he who previously appeared for our benefit as expansions like Matsya and other forms has not appeared in your womb directly as Svayam Bhagavān. This is real good fortune. “But I am afraid of the evil acts of Kaṁsa.” To her fear, they reply that he is about to die (mumūrṣoḥ). His actions will soon bring about his death. Do not fear him. “But I do not know how much disturbance he will cause.” They reply that he is the protector of all the Yadus, what to speak of being the protector of Vasudeva and you who are close to him. In this way the devatās constantly praised her. adṛṣṭaiḥ puruṣair strībhir devakīm devatā-ganāḥ vibhrāṇāṁ vapuṣā viṣṇuṁ tuṣṭuvus tām aharniśam The devatās along with unseen men and women praised Devakī day and night since she held Viṣṇu with his body within her. Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa Himself, with Baladeva, has entered Devakī's womb as protector — when the Lord is present, fear of any Kaṁsa-like threat dissolves.
Practical Applications
  • Name one 'Kaṁsa' in your life today — a fear or hostile force — and consciously hand its outcome over to Kṛṣṇa instead of strategizing alone.
  • Begin the day by remembering that Kṛṣṇa is svayam bhagavān, not one deity among many, and direct your first prayer to Him personally by name.
  • Speak a word of fearless reassurance to someone anxious in your family today, rooted in the truth that the Lord protects His devotees.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still bracing against a 'Kaṁsa' — some looming threat — as if Kṛṣṇa were not already present within the situation as its eventual protector?
  • If I truly accepted that Kṛṣṇa Himself, with His plenary portions, is intimately near (as close as a child in the womb), what fear would I have to put down today, and why am I still holding it?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Identify your current 'Kaṁsa' — the professor, the ex, the group chat, the algorithm — and write down, in one sentence, what specifically you are afraid it will do to you. Then write underneath: 'This person/thing is mumūrṣu — they have already chosen their own outcome by their relationship to Kṛṣṇa. My job is not to defeat them, my job is to take shelter.' Read it before you open Instagram tomorrow morning.
  • In your friend group there is at least one conversation where you have been quietly performing impersonalism — 'the universe has my back,' 'trust the process,' 'everything happens for a reason' — because saying 'Kṛṣṇa' would cost you social capital. This week, name Him once, by name, in front of someone whose opinion you fear. Notice what dies in you and what is born.
  • Devakī did not earn Kṛṣṇa appearing in her womb by being impressive; she earned it by lifetimes of bhakti. You will not crack your exam, your application, your audition by performing harder. Each morning this week, before you study, offer the day's effort to Kṛṣṇa out loud — 'I am the doer of right action, not the owner of the result' — and then work as hard as you would have anyway. The work doesn't change. You do.
householder
  • Your household has a Kaṁsa — a financial pressure, a difficult in-law, a school administrator, a health diagnosis. Sit with your spouse this week and name it together. Then read this verse aloud to each other and ask: what action does honest karuṇā require us to take for our children's souls (not just their comfort and résumés), and where have we been confusing the two?
  • Vasudeva and Devakī did not raise Kṛṣṇa themselves — they handed Him to Nanda and Yaśodā for His protection. Where in your parenting are you holding too tightly because you cannot trust that Kṛṣṇa is the actual goptā of your child? Pick one decision this month — a school, a friendship, a discipline issue — and make it from the posture of 'I am a temporary caretaker of a soul Kṛṣṇa already protects,' not 'I am the sole bulwark between my child and ruin.'
  • At the dinner table this week, replace one 'how was your day' with one verse — this one, or another from the related list — read aloud, with one sentence of what it means to you. Do not lecture, do not test the children. Just put Kṛṣṇa, by name, into the room where money and homework and laundry are usually the only gods. Let the awkwardness be the sādhana.
senior
  • You are now closer to the age Devakī was when she finally held Kṛṣṇa than to the age she was when she first lost a child. The whole arc of fear-and-protection she lived through is, in compressed form, your own life. Write a single page to a younger person — child, grandchild, mentee — naming the Kaṁsas you feared most and how Kṛṣṇa was, in fact, the goptā. Do not sanitize. Let the failures be in there too. This is your verse-41 to them.
  • The body is now visibly mumūrṣu — it has decided, by its very nature, to die. The verse's comfort is not that this will be reversed but that the soul has a Protector who is unaffected by the body's decision. Each evening, before sleep, lie down and rehearse leaving: 'I am not this body that is preparing to dissolve. I am the soul of whom Kṛṣṇa is the eternal goptā.' Make the bed a daily cremation ground and the japa-mālā the only thing you carry across.
  • Loosen one attachment this month that you have been postponing — a property, a position on a board, a grudge, a possession your children secretly wish you would let go of. Hand it over the way Vasudeva handed the infant across the Yamunā: not with despair, with trust. Tell one person you did it and why, so that your vairāgya becomes a teaching, not a private virtue.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating 'do not fear' as a vague affirmation of positive thinking, rather than a specific assurance grounded in Kṛṣṇa being the original Supreme Personality of Godhead (paraḥ pumān) — not an impersonal force or one deity among many.
  • Reading the verse as license for passivity — 'Kṛṣṇa will handle Kaṁsa, so I do nothing' — instead of recognizing that Devakī still had to carry, protect, and cooperate; surrender to His protection does not cancel one's own sva-dharma.
  • Diluting 'aṁśena sākṣād bhagavān' into a generic 'divinity is within everyone,' collapsing the specific descent of Kṛṣṇa with Baladeva into a sentimental impersonalism the verse explicitly refutes.
Discussion Prompts
  • The demigods tell Devakī, 'Do not fear — Kṛṣṇa Himself is in your womb.' Yet immediately afterward she still hides the child, and Vasudeva still smuggles Him to Gokula. If Kṛṣṇa's presence is the antidote to fear, why does the family still take protective action? Where in your own life do you wrongly treat 'taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa' as a substitute for doing the practical work in front of you — and where do you do the opposite, working frantically as if His protection were optional?
  • Paraḥ pumān aṁśena — Kṛṣṇa is the original Person, and Baladeva is His plenary portion. Discuss with your partner: what is actually at stake in insisting Kṛṣṇa is a person and not 'the divine spark within' or 'pure consciousness'? Push each other — where have you, in your own speech or thinking, quietly drifted into impersonalism because it sounds more sophisticated to modern ears?
  • The verse calls Kaṁsa mumūrṣu — 'one who has decided to die' — because of his enmity toward the Lord. The demigods do not say Kṛṣṇa will kill him; they say Kaṁsa has chosen his own destruction. Where in your life are you, like Kaṁsa, quietly choosing a destruction by refusing to surrender something? Be specific with your partner — and let them push back if your answer sounds noble rather than honest.
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a recently widowed mother whose adult son has just been diagnosed with a serious illness; she is paralyzed by fear. Student B is a Vaiṣṇava friend who has come to sit with her. The teacher's instruction: B must console A using the spirit of this verse — not by minimizing the danger ('don't worry, everything will be fine'), and not by spiritual bypass ('it's all māyā'), but by helping A locate where Kṛṣṇa actually stands in this situation as the goptā, the protector, while still honoring that protection does not mean the body will be spared. After 5 minutes, swap roles: now A is a young professional who has just been passed over for a promotion by a manipulative colleague, and B must speak to her about Kaṁsa, mumūrṣu, and what it means that the Lord protects His devotees from the *real* danger, not always the apparent one. Debrief: where did each of you slide into platitude? Where did the verse actually do work?

Objections & Responses
  • This is a beautiful family story, but it treats Kṛṣṇa as a literal baby in a literal womb. Surely a mature reader should take the 'Supreme Personality of Godhead' inside Devakī as a symbol — the divine potential within every mother, every person — not a historical claim about one infant in Mathurā.
    The verse will not let you do that. Paraḥ pumān aṁśena is precise: the original Person, with His plenary portion Baladeva, is in this specific womb at this specific moment. Prabhupāda points to kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam — Kṛṣṇa is not a symbol of divinity, He is its source. The 'mature' move of dissolving Him into a universal principle is, in our line, the immature move; it is the ahaṅkāra refusing a Person it cannot patronize. The Gauḍīyas are unembarrassed realists about this: a real Person took a real birth in a real prison cell, and the soul who can accept that on its own terms — without translating it into something more flattering to the modern intellect — is the one who has actually grown up. Try the opposite experiment for a week: read the verse as literally true and see what in you resists. That resistance is the teacher.
  • The demigods tell Devakī not to fear because Kṛṣṇa is in her womb. Fine for her — she had the Lord physically present. The rest of us don't. So this verse offers no real comfort to me when my own Kaṁsa shows up: a hostile boss, a sick child, a collapsing marriage. What am I supposed to do, pretend?
    Notice what the verse actually promises: not that Kaṁsa will be removed before he frightens you, but that Kaṁsa is already mumūrṣu — already self-condemned by his enmity toward the Lord — and that Kṛṣṇa will be the goptā, the protector, of His own. The same arrangement is in force for you. Kṛṣṇa is not less present in your life than He was in Devakī's; He is present through the holy name, through the verse you are reading right now, through the devotee sitting next to you. The shift the verse asks of you is not pretense, it is re-identification: stop locating yourself as the body the boss can fire or the heart the spouse can break, and start locating yourself as the soul Kṛṣṇa has already claimed as His own. That is karuṇā in its real form — not the demigods sparing Devakī's feelings, but telling her the truth about who is actually in the room. Sit with that truth for ten minutes a day, and the Kaṁsas in your life begin to look, accurately, like men who have decided to die.
Analogy

After a long parade of generals and kings, the demigods turn to address a single trembling mother in the prison and tell her plainly: your son is the king the parade is for. Theology becomes a postcard, an intimate note slipped under Devakī's door — fear not, He whom we have just praised is the child in your womb.
