Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.2.20

तां वीक्ष्य कंस: प्रभयाजितान्तरांविरोचयन्तीं भवनं शुचिस्मिताम् । आहैष मे प्राणहरो हरिर्गुहांध्रुवं श्रितो यन्न पुरेयमीद‍ृशी ॥ २० ॥

Important Words
  • ajita - antarām — having the unconquerable one within
    Ajita — 'unconquerable,' a standard name of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa. Antara — within. Devakī's brightness is technically described: she has Ajita inside her. The name predicts Kaṁsa's defeat in advance.
  • śuci - smitām — with a pure smile
    Śuci — pure, bright. Smita — smile. The Bhāgavatam loves to note the devotee's smile in distress; Kuntī, Bhīṣma at death, Prahlāda before Hiraṇyakaśipu — all smile. Devakī's smile inside the prison is in that lineage.
  • prāṇa - haraḥ — the taker of life
    Prāṇa-hara — 'remover of breath.' Kaṁsa names the Lord by His function with respect to him personally. Even the demon recognizes the Lord; what differs from the devotee is what he does with the recognition.
  • hariḥ — Hari
    Hari — from √hṛ, 'to take away.' He takes the devotee's sins, He takes the demon's life. The same verbal root yields opposite outcomes by the disposition of the addressee. Doctrinally tender: name-power depends on relationship.
  • śritaḥ — has taken shelter
    From √śri — 'to take shelter, to lean upon.' The same root underlies śaraṇāgati (āśraya, saṁśraya). Even the Lord 'takes shelter' in Devakī's womb — He performs the very act He requires of His devotees.
Because the Supreme Personality of Godhead was within her womb, Devakī illuminated the entire atmosphere in the place where she was confined. Seeing her jubilant, pure and smiling, Kaṁsa thought, “The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, who is now within her, will kill me. Devakī has never before looked so brilliant and jubilant.”
Purport

The Lord says in Bhagavad-gītā (4.7) : yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham “Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion — at that time I descend Myself.” In this age, at the present moment, there are inordinate discrepancies in the discharge of human duties. Human life is meant for God-realization, but unfortunately the materialistic civilization is stressing only the senses of the body, not understanding the living force within the body. As clearly stated in Bhagavad-gītā ( dehino ’smin yathā dehe ), within the body is the body’s proprietor, the living force, which is more important. But human society has become so fallen that instead of understanding the living force within the body, people have become busy with external things. This is a discrepancy in human duties. Therefore Kṛṣṇa has taken birth or taken shelter within the womb of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement. Men of Kaṁsa’s class, therefore, are very much afraid and are busy trying to stop this movement, especially in the Western countries. One politician has remarked that the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is spreading like an epidemic and that if not checked immediately, within ten years it may capture governmental power. There is, of course, such potency in the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement. As stated by authorities (Cc. Ādi 17.22), kali-kāle nāma-rūpe kṛṣṇa-avatāra: in this age, Kṛṣṇa has appeared in the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra. The Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is spreading like wildfire all over the world, and it will go on doing so. Men who are like Kaṁsa are very much afraid of the movement’s progress and acceptance by the younger generation, but as Kṛṣṇa could not be killed by Kaṁsa, this movement cannot be checked by men of Kaṁsa’s class. The movement will go on increasing more and more, provided the leaders of the movement remain firmly Kṛṣṇa conscious by following the regulative principles and the primary activities of chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra regularly.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Because the Supreme Personality of Godhead was within her womb, Devakī illuminated the entire atmosphere in the place where she was confined. Seeing her jubilant, pure and smiling, Kaṁsa thought, "The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, who is now within her, will kill me. Devakī has never before looked so brilliant and jubilant."
Commentary

Devaki, who was holding Visnu within her womb (ajitantaram) , filled the room with light. She smiled out of genuine bliss (suci smitam), not in order to deceive Kamsa as previously. Seeing her, Kamsa then addressed her. Hari can mean lion. "Like the lion who kills (pranahari) the elephant, Hari, who will take my life, has taken shelter in the womb of Devaki. Certainly this is so, because (yat) she did not appear effulgent previously (pura)."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Kaṁsa personally saw her. Her effulgence is described in Viṣṇu Purāṇa: na śeke devakīṁ draṣṭum kaścid apy atitejasā jājvalyamānāṁ tāṁ dṛṣṭvā manāṁsi kṣobham āyayuḥ No one could look at Devakī because of her effulgence. Seeing her shining form they became disturbed in mind. Because of the bliss of the Lord she had a pure (śuci) smile. It was not a deceptive smile which she previously showed. Seeing her, Kaṁsa spoke. The Lord has taken shelter of her womb or heart (gūham.) A secondary meaning is: like a snake the Lord has taken shelter of a hole out of fear of me, a lion.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa within radiates through Devakī — when you shelter Him in your heart, your very presence threatens the Kaṁsa within and around you.
Practical Applications
  • Chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra attentively today so that, like Devakī, your face carries the tejas of one who shelters Him within.
  • Notice the 'Kaṁsa-voice' inside that wants to suppress your sādhana out of fear, and refuse it by sitting for japa anyway.
  • Speak about Kṛṣṇa once today to someone in your life — trust that the same potency that frightened Kaṁsa is still active in His name.
Reflection Questions
  • Devakī's tejas radiated outward because Kṛṣṇa was within her — what 'inner cargo' am I currently carrying (entertainment, anxieties, calculations) that is silently radiating from me and shaping how others perceive me?
  • Kaṁsa correctly diagnosed Devakī's brilliance but drew the demonic conclusion — where in my own life have I sensed a higher presence in someone or something and reacted with threat or suspicion rather than shelter?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your social media for one week with a single question: when you post, are you trying to radiate something real, or are you trying to manufacture the appearance of radiance for an audience? Devakī did not know she was glowing — Kaṁsa noticed. Stop curating; start chanting japa before you open the app.
  • Identify the Kaṁsa in your friend group — the one who reflexively mocks anyone who studies hard, prays, refuses to drink, or takes things seriously. Now identify the Kaṁsa in yourself: which friend's seriousness about Kṛṣṇa or studies makes you want to drag them down to your level? Confess that to a senior devotee this week.
  • Before your next exam or interview, take five minutes to chant and remember that the body is not the proprietor. You are not your GPA, your offer letter, or your follower count. Then do the work with full effort and zero attachment to the result — this is the karma-yoga the Gītā has been teaching since 2.47, applied to your actual Tuesday.
householder
  • When your spouse, child, or co-worker shows a flash of genuine spiritual seriousness — a desire to chant more, to give to a temple, to take a vow — watch your own first reaction carefully. If it is anxiety, calculation, or the urge to talk them down ('let's be practical'), you have just met the Kaṁsa in yourself. Name it, do not act on it, and instead support their step.
  • Make one concrete family decision this month — about money, about your child's education, about your career — where you visibly choose the soul's interest over the body's comfort. Tell your spouse what you are doing and why, in plain language, without preaching. Real compassion serves the soul; material compassion just removes discomfort.
  • Light a lamp in your home shrine and chant audibly so your children hear it — not as performance, but as the household's actual atmosphere. Devakī's prison cell glowed because of who was within. Ask honestly: what is within the womb of your home, and what is it radiating to the children growing up inside it?
senior
  • You have already passed through infancy, youth, householder life, and are now in a body the Gītā calls a transition, not an annihilation. Begin loosening one specific attachment this month — a possession, a grudge, a role you still cling to — and offer it explicitly to Kṛṣṇa. Practice the dhīra-bhāva of BG 2.13, not as theory, but as your actual daily disposition.
  • Identify two younger people — one in your family, one outside — who would benefit from hearing how Kṛṣṇa consciousness has actually held you through old age, illness, and loss. Do not lecture; tell the story plainly. Prabhupāda's purport ties the movement's continuation to leaders remaining firmly Kṛṣṇa conscious — your steadiness now is part of that transmission.
  • Sit with the verse's image: a body becomes radiant because of who is within. As your body weakens, ask whether the same is becoming more true or less true of you. If less, increase your chanting and reduce your consumption of news, opinion, and complaint — these are the materialistic 'external things' Prabhupāda names as the discrepancy of human duties.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as praise of Devakī's personal radiance and aspiring to 'glow' for its own sake, rather than recognizing the radiance as a sign that Kṛṣṇa Himself was within — confusing the lamp with the flame.
  • Applying the Kaṁsa-Kṛṣṇa contrast only to external 'opponents of the movement' while never auditing the Kaṁsa-like reflexes (envy, fear of being eclipsed, urge to suppress what is purer) operating within one's own heart.
  • Treating tejas, dhairya, and anahankāra as personality traits to cultivate through self-improvement techniques, instead of as natural symptoms that arise when one shelters Kṛṣṇa within through chanting and the regulative principles.
Discussion Prompts
  • Devakī's body literally radiated because Kṛṣṇa was within her womb. In your own life, name one person you know whose presence visibly changes a room — and one person who drains it. What is actually being transmitted, and is it tejas or something else? Push each other: is 'good vibes' the same thing as the tejas Prabhupāda points to here, or is that a sentimental flattening?
  • Kaṁsa sees Devakī's brilliance and immediately reads it as a threat to himself. Share a time when someone else's spiritual progress made you uncomfortable, envious, or dismissive. Don't let your partner off with a polite answer — name the specific person and the specific feeling. What does the Kaṁsa-reaction reveal about the ahaṅkāra still operating in you?
  • Prabhupāda claims the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is 'Kṛṣṇa taking shelter within the womb' of the saṅkīrtana movement, and that Kaṁsa-class men try to stop it. Where do you find yourself genuinely siding with the Kaṁsa-class — wanting the movement softened, made more respectable, less embarrassing in public? Be honest with each other: where does your own reputation matter more to you than the movement's potency?
Role Play

Two students are paired. Student A is a longtime devotee who has just told their non-devotee sibling (Student B) that they are taking initiation, moving into the temple's outreach program, and giving up a lucrative job offer. Student B plays the sibling — affectionate, intelligent, secular, and genuinely worried that A is being 'captured' by what looks, from outside, like the kind of movement Prabhupāda's politician feared. Student B must press the real objections: cult dynamics, wasted education, family duty, the suspicion that A's new 'radiance' is just brainwashing. Student A must respond not with slogans but in the spirit of this verse — without attacking the sibling's good intentions, without retreating into defensiveness, and without softening Kṛṣṇa into a generic 'spiritual path.' After ten minutes, swap roles. Then debrief together: where did A sound like a real practitioner, and where did A sound like a recruiter? Where did B's objections actually land?

Objections & Responses
  • Prabhupāda's purport reads like movement triumphalism — 'spreading like wildfire,' politicians afraid of us, governmental power within ten years. That was 1970s rhetoric and it didn't come true. Doesn't this date the purport and weaken the verse's authority?
    The historical prediction is not the point — the structural claim is. Prabhupāda is reading the verse as a pattern: wherever Kṛṣṇa takes shelter (in a womb, in a saṅkīrtana movement, in a sincere chanter), Kaṁsa-class consciousness reflexively tries to extinguish that light. That pattern is empirically observable today — in ourselves first, in institutions second. The 'ten years to governmental power' line is incidental; the diagnosis of materialistic civilization mistaking the body for the proprietor is exactly as accurate now as then. Read the purport as Prabhupāda reading the verse, not as Prabhupāda predicting elections. And note the conditional he closes with — 'provided the leaders remain firmly Kṛṣṇa conscious by following the regulative principles.' He explicitly ties the movement's potency to sādhana, not to demographics.
  • Devakī's radiance came from carrying Kṛṣṇa physically in her womb. That's a one-time pastime of the Supreme Lord. It is sentimental and frankly self-flattering for ordinary chanters to apply this to themselves and claim that we, too, are carrying Kṛṣṇa and shining.
    The objection is healthy — it guards against the impersonalist drift where every devotee imagines themselves a little Devakī. But the Gauḍīya answer is precise: we do not claim Devakī's position; we claim the principle Prabhupāda draws from the verse. Kali-kāle nāma-rūpe kṛṣṇa-avatāra — in this age Kṛṣṇa has appeared as the holy name. When the holy name takes shelter on the tongue and in the heart of a sincere chanter, something does change in the atmosphere around that chanter — not because the chanter is exalted, but because Kṛṣṇa Himself is non-different from His name. The tejas is His, not ours. The moment we begin to take credit for the radiance, we have already lost it — that is the ahaṅkāra Kaṁsa himself models in this very chapter. So the verse is not a flattery; it is a warning about whose light it actually is.
Analogy

A lit lamp held behind a thin paper screen still shows through; the watcher in the next room knows the lamp is there even before he sees it. Devakī's joyous brightness is the lamp through the paper; Kaṁsa's terror is the watcher's certainty that the flame within will burn him.
