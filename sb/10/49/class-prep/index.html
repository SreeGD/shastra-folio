BG 49.1-2

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Akrūra went to Hastināpura, the city distinguished by the glory of the Paurava rulers. There he saw Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Bhīṣma, Vidura and Kuntī, along with Bāhlika and his son Somadatta. He also saw Droṇācārya, Kṛpācārya, Karṇa, Duryodhana, Aśvatthāmā, the Pāṇḍavas and other close friends.

Essence. Akrūra goes to Hastināpura, the Pauravas' glorious city, and sees Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Bhīṣma, Vidura, Kuntī, Bāhlika, Somadatta, Droṇa, Kṛpa, Karṇa, Duryodhana, Aśvatthāmā, Pāṇḍu's sons and others. The mission begins with greeting everyone — comprehensive contact precedes intelligence-gathering.

BG 49.3

After Akrūra, the son of Gāndinī, had appropriately greeted all his relatives and friends, they asked him for news of their family members, and he in turn asked about their welfare.

Essence. Akrūra appropriately greets relatives and friends, and they ask after their family members while he asks about their welfare. Mutual inquiry is the form mature visits take; both sides ask, neither only reports.

BG 49.4

He remained in Hastināpura for several months to scrutinize the conduct of the weak-willed King, who had bad sons and who was inclined to give in to the whims of mischievous advisers.

Essence. He remains in Hastināpura several months to scrutinize the conduct of the weak-willed King with bad sons who gives in to mischievous advisers. Real investigation takes time; one visit doesn't reveal the full picture.

BG 49.5-6

Kuntī and Vidura described to Akrūra in detail the evil intentions of Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s sons, who could not tolerate the great qualities of Kuntī’s sons — such as their powerful influence, military skill, physical strength, bravery and humility — or the intense affection the citizens had for them. Kuntī and Vidura also told Akrūra about how the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra had tried to poison the Pāṇḍavas and carry out other such plots.

Essence. Kuntī and Vidura describe the evil intentions of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons, who cannot tolerate the qualities of Kuntī's sons or the citizens' affection for them. Envy of others' virtues drives the wicked; jealousy is a diagnostic.

BG 49.7

Kuntīdevī, taking advantage of her brother Akrūra’s visit, approached him confidentially. While remembering her birthplace, she spoke with tears in her eyes.

Essence. Kuntī, taking advantage of her brother's visit, approaches Akrūra confidentially, remembering her birthplace with tears. Some conversations require explicit confidentiality and the right relative; family contact unlocks what status cannot.

BG 49.8

[Queen Kuntī said:] O gentle one, do my parents, brothers, sisters, nephews, family women and girlhood friends still remember us?

Essence. Do my parents, brothers, sisters, nephews, family women and girlhood friends still remember us? Kuntī asks specifically — the deep ache is to be remembered by name.

BG 49.9

Does my nephew Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality and the compassionate shelter of His devotees, still remember His aunt’s sons? And does lotus-eyed Rāma remember them also?

Essence. Does my nephew Kṛṣṇa — the Supreme Personality, compassionate shelter of devotees — still remember His aunt's sons? Does lotus-eyed Rāma? Family love and theology coexist: He is both nephew and Supreme.

BG 49.10

Now that I am suffering in the midst of my enemies like a doe in the midst of wolves, will Kṛṣṇa come to console me and my fatherless sons with His words?

Essence. Now suffering among enemies like a doe among wolves, will Kṛṣṇa come to console me and my fatherless sons with His words? The vulnerable imagine specific arrivals; longing has a shape.

BG 49.11

Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa! O great yogi! O Supreme Soul and protector of the universe! O Govinda! Please protect me, who have surrendered to You. I and my sons are being overwhelmed by trouble.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa! O great yogi, Supreme Soul, protector of the universe! Govinda! Please protect me, who have surrendered to You; I and my sons are overwhelmed. Crisis prayer uses many names and explicit surrender.

BG 49.12

For persons fearful of death and rebirth, I see no shelter other than Your liberating lotus feet, for You are the Supreme Lord.

Essence. For those fearful of death and rebirth, I see no shelter other than Your liberating lotus feet — for You are the Supreme Lord. Kuntī states the only alternative theology she will accept.

BG 49.13

I offer my obeisances unto You, Kṛṣṇa, the supreme pure, the Absolute Truth and the Supersoul, the Lord of pure devotional service and the source of all knowledge. I have come to You for shelter.

Essence. I offer obeisances to You, supreme pure, Absolute Truth and Supersoul, Lord of pure devotional service, source of all knowledge — I have come to You for shelter. The address moves from family to philosophical to refuge — three registers in one prayer.

BG 49.14

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus meditating on her family members and also on Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of the universe, your great-grandmother Kuntīdevī began to cry out in grief, O King.

Essence. Meditating on family and on Kṛṣṇa, your great-grandmother Kuntīdevī cries out in grief. Grief breaks through during meditation; family-thinking and Kṛṣṇa-thinking can both bring tears.

BG 49.15

Both Akrūra, who shared Queen Kuntī’s distress and happiness, and the illustrious Vidura consoled the Queen by reminding her of the extraordinary way her sons had taken birth.

Essence. Akrūra and Vidura console the Queen by reminding her of the extraordinary way her sons were born — they came from heavenly gods and cannot be vanquished like ordinary mortals. Consolation reminds people of facts they already know but have forgotten in distress.

BG 49.16

The ardent affection King Dhṛtarāṣṭra felt for his sons had made him act unjustly toward the Pāṇḍavas. Just before leaving, Akrūra approached the King, who was seated among his friends and supporters, and related to him the message that his relatives — Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Balarāma — had sent out of friendship.

Essence. Dhṛtarāṣṭra's ardent affection for his sons made him act unjustly toward the Pāṇḍavas. Before leaving, Akrūra approaches the King and delivers the message from Kṛṣṇa and relatives. Honest message is delivered to the wrongdoer, not hidden from him.

BG 49.17

Akrūra said: O my dear son of Vicitravīrya, O enhancer of the Kurus’ glory, your brother Pāṇḍu having passed away, you have now assumed the royal throne.

Essence. O son of Vicitravīrya, enhancer of the Kurus' glory, your brother Pāṇḍu having passed away, you have now assumed the throne. Even ironic address can be politically gracious; Akrūra speaks the King's titles before his sins.

BG 49.18

By religiously protecting the earth, delighting your subjects with your noble character, and treating all your relatives equally, you will surely achieve success and glory.

Essence. By religiously protecting the earth, delighting subjects with noble character, and treating all relatives equally, you will achieve success and glory. The standard for legitimate rule is named: dharma, conduct, equality.

BG 49.19

If you act otherwise, however, people will condemn you in this world, and in the next life you will enter the darkness of hell. Remain equally disposed, therefore, toward Pāṇḍu’s sons and your own.

Essence. If you act otherwise, people will condemn you here and in the next life you will enter hell's darkness. Be equally disposed toward Pāṇḍu's sons and your own. The warning is direct; karma operates on both timelines.

BG 49.20

In this world no one has any permanent relationship with anyone else, O King. We cannot stay forever even with our own body, what to speak of our wife, children and the rest.

Essence. In this world no one has any permanent relationship with anyone; we cannot stay forever even with our own body, what to speak of wife, children, and the rest. Impermanence is named bluntly to break attachment.

BG 49.21

Every creature is born alone and dies alone, and alone one experiences the just rewards of his good and evil deeds.

Essence. Every creature is born alone, dies alone, and alone experiences the just rewards of good and evil deeds. Karma is individual; companionship doesn't extend to the consequences.

BG 49.22

In the guise of dear dependents, strangers steal the sinfully acquired wealth of a foolish man, just as the offspring of a fish drink up the water that sustains the fish.

Essence. In the guise of dear dependents, strangers steal a fool's sinfully acquired wealth — like fish offspring drinking the water that sustains them. Even our 'beloved' beneficiaries can be the agents of our loss.

BG 49.23

A fool indulges in sin to maintain his life, wealth and children and other relatives, for he thinks, “These things are mine.” In the end, however, these very things all abandon him, leaving him frustrated.

Essence. A fool indulges in sin to maintain life, wealth and children, thinking 'these are mine' — but they all abandon him, leaving frustration. The very objects we sin to protect ultimately leave us.

BG 49.24

Abandoned by his so-called dependents, ignorant of the actual goal of life, indifferent to his real duty, and having failed to fulfill his purposes, the foolish soul enters the blindness of hell, taking his sinful reactions with him.

Essence. Abandoned by dependents, ignorant of the real goal, indifferent to true duty, the foolish soul enters hell's darkness with sinful reactions. The exit-with-no-resources picture is grim; this is the destination if course doesn't change.

BG 49.25

Therefore, O King, looking upon this world as a dream, a magician’s illusion or a flight of fancy, please control your mind with intelligence and become equipoised and peaceful, my lord.

Essence. Therefore, O King, looking upon this world as a dream, a magician's illusion, or a flight of fancy, control your mind with intelligence and become equipoised and peaceful. The remedy: reframe perception, employ intelligence, find equipoise.

BG 49.26

Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: O master of charity, I can never be satiated while hearing your auspicious words. Indeed, I am like a mortal who has obtained the nectar of the gods.

Essence. Dhṛtarāṣṭra: I cannot be satiated while hearing your auspicious words — I am like a mortal with the nectar of the gods. Even those who will not change can authentically appreciate good teaching while not following it.

BG 49.27

Even so, gentle Akrūra, because my unsteady heart is prejudiced by affection for my sons, these pleasing words of yours cannot remain fixed there, just as lightning cannot remain fixed in a cloud.

Essence. Even so, my unsteady heart is prejudiced by affection for my sons; your pleasing words cannot remain fixed there, like lightning unable to stay in a cloud. Self-aware failure is still failure — knowing isn't curing.

BG 49.28

Who can defy the injunctions of the Supreme Lord, who has now descended in the Yadu dynasty to diminish the earth’s burden?

Essence. Who can defy the injunctions of the Supreme Lord, now descended in the Yadu dynasty to diminish the earth's burden? Dhṛtarāṣṭra hides behind theology to excuse what was his to refuse.

BG 49.29

I offer my obeisances to Him, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who creates this universe by the inconceivable activity of His material energy and then distributes the various modes of nature by entering within the creation. From Him, the meaning of whose pastimes is unfathomable, come both the entangling cycle of birth and death and the process of deliverance from it.

Essence. I offer obeisances to Him who creates the universe by inconceivable material energy and distributes the modes by entering creation. Even the failed king can speak true praise; correct words coexist with corrupt heart.

BG 49.30

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Having thus apprised himself of the King’s attitude, Akrūra, the descendant of Yadu, took permission from his well-wishing relatives and friends and returned to the capital of the Yādavas.

Essence. Having apprised himself of the King's attitude, Akrūra takes permission from well-wishers and returns to the Yādava capital. Reading the room is part of the mission; he doesn't push further when nothing more can be done.

BG 49.31

Akrūra reported to Lord Balarāma and Lord Kṛṣṇa how Dhṛtarāṣṭra was behaving toward the Pāṇḍavas. Thus, O descendant of the Kurus, he fulfilled the purpose for which he had been sent.

Essence. Akrūra reports to Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa how Dhṛtarāṣṭra is behaving toward the Pāṇḍavas, fulfilling the purpose for which he was sent. Service ends with accurate reporting back; the mission is incomplete without it.
