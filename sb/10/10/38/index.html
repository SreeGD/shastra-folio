Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.10.38

वाणी गुणानुकथने श्रवणौ कथायां हस्तौ च कर्मसु मनस्तव पादयोर्न: । स्मृत्यां शिरस्तव निवासजगत्प्रणामे द‍ृष्टि: सतां दर्शनेऽस्तु भवत्तनूनाम् ॥ ३८ ॥

Important Words
  • vāṇī — the power of speech
    Nominative of vāṇī. Gauḍīya: speech is the first sense engaged in bhakti — chanting, kīrtana, kathā. Caitanya's siddhānta begins with the tongue (jihva) as both diagnostic and therapeutic organ.
  • guṇa-anukathane — in talking about Your pastimes
    Compound: guṇa (qualities, pastimes) + anukathana (continual telling). Gauḍīya: anu- prefixes indicate the disciple's repetition of what the guru speaks. The siddhas' speech becomes paramparā-anukathana.
  • smṛtyām — in remembrance
    Locative of smṛti — same word from SB 10.10.20–22, where Nārada granted them smṛti during the tree-curse. The smṛti that began as memory of sin now becomes meditation on Kṛṣṇa's feet. Same faculty, redirected.
  • nivāsa-jagat-praṇāme — in obeisance to the whole world as Your dwelling
    Compound: nivāsa (dwelling) + jagat (world) + praṇāma (obeisance). Gauḍīya acintya-bhedābheda in one phrase: the world is His dwelling — non-different in being His energy, different in not being Him personally. The head bows to the world as His vyakta-rūpa.
  • bhavat-tanūnām — who are nondifferent from You
    Compound: bhavat (Your) + tanu (body). Vaiṣṇavas are addressed as 'Your own bodies.' Gauḍīya: vaiṣṇava-aparādha is the gravest offense precisely because the devotee is Kṛṣṇa's tanu. The eyes that see Vaiṣṇavas see Kṛṣṇa Himself.
Henceforward, may all our words describe Your pastimes, may our ears engage in aural reception of Your glories, may our hands, legs and other senses engage in actions pleasing to You, and may our minds always think of Your lotus feet. May our heads offer our obeisances to everything within this world, because all things are also Your different forms, and may our eyes see the forms of Vaiṣṇavas, who are nondifferent from You.
Purport

Here the process of understanding the Supreme Personality of Godhead is given. This process is bhakti. śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṁ pāda-sevanam arcanaṁ vandanaṁ dāsyaṁ sakhyam ātma-nivedanam ( Bhāg. 7.5.23) Everything should be engaged in the service of the Lord. Hṛṣīkeṇa hṛṣīkeśa-sevanaṁ bhaktir ucyate ( Nārada-pañcarātra ). Everything — the mind, the body and all the sense organs — should be engaged in Kṛṣṇa’s service. This is to be learned from expert devotees like Nārada, Svayambhū and Śambhu. This is the process. We cannot manufacture our own way of understanding the Supreme Personality of Godhead, for it is not that everything one manufactures or concocts will lead to understanding God. Such a proposition — yata mata, tata patha — is foolish. Kṛṣṇa says, bhaktyāham ekayā grāhyaḥ: “Only by executing the activities of bhakti can one understand Me.” ( Bhāg. 11.14.21 ) This is called ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānuśīlanam, remaining engaged favorably in the service of the Lord.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Henceforward, may all our words describe Your pastimes, may our ears engage in aural reception of Your glories, may our hands, legs and other senses engage in actions pleasing to You, and may our minds always think of Your lotus feet. May our heads offer our obeisances to everything within this world, because all things are also Your different forms, and may our eyes see the forms of Vaiṣṇavas, who are nondifferent from You.
Commentary

As we are your followers and Narada’s servants, you should be very merciful to us. We now pray form something rarely given to others. The particle ca should be understood after each item. May our words (vani) always be engaged in describing your qualities (guna kathane), and nothing else (ca). may our minds be engaged in remembering your lotus feet. May our heads pay respects to the living entities (jagat), the devotees such as Narada. Or nivasa jagat can be a vocative, o abode of the universe (jagat nivasa), may our eyes be engaged in seeing your forms (bhavat tanuman) and those of your devotees such as Narada (satam).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“Since Nārada made you devotees, go to him.” Then give us all the methods of bhakti. They pray in this way, which is the nature of bhakti. May our words or voice speak continuously of the qualities of your feet, of your affection for your devotees and of your beauty-- not about yoga or sacrifices. May our minds be thinking of your feet, with devotion-- not just thinking. May our two ears be engaged in hearing topics about you with full attention (with two ears). Similarly both hands should engage in action for you fully. Let us hear all topics—including how you enable Brahmā to carry out creation. Since the ears are not completely independent of what they hear, at least in speaking, we should speak only about the sweet qualities of the Lord because of their rasa. The hands and other organs (ca) should be engaged in actions like deity worship and related services. The head should offer respects to you, O dweller in the universe! Śiras is mentioned last since this is the main limb of the body. The word tava is repeated to indicate that by offering respects to the Lord respects are offered to the world, since the Lord is everything, and thus perfection in offering respects is achieved, Or may our heads offer respects to the Vaiṣṇavas, the living beings (jagat) who are your dwelling place, for it is said śiras tu tasyobhaya-liṅgam ānamet: an actual head is one that bows down to the Lord in his manifestations among the moving and nonmoving creatures. (SB 10.80.4) Or may our heads offer respects to the people of Vraja where you dwell, even though they are far away for us. May our eyes see devotees like Nārada who are your body. Or may our eyes directly see you in your deity forms and the devotees. However to see him directly in their house was impossible. Out of fear they did not pray for this. Touching and smelling are also understood though not mentioned in the verse.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“Since Nārada made you devotees, go to him.” Then give us all the methods of bhakti. They pray in this way, which is the nature of bhakti. Or having attained closeness to Vāsudeva, we ask you for another mercy. Let us be devotees and supply other things as well. May our words or voice speak continuously of the qualities of your feet, of your affection for your devotees and of your beauty-- not about yoga or sacrifices. Mention of the feet indicates desire for service. Dry jṣāna is discarded. May our two ears be engaged in hearing topics about you with full attention (with two ears). They speak in the plural (naḥ), thinking of blessings for their relatives as well. The ears should hear your topics and not act independently. Because of qualification for describing the Lord’s glories, they mention guṇānu-kathana. The hands and other organs (ca) should be engaged in actions like deity worship and related services. The head should offer respects to you, O dweller in the universe! Śiras is mentioned last since this is the main limb of the body and is used in offering respects with eight limbs. Other limbs should also be included when mention is made of the head. Or it is not possible to offer respects with just the head, and one engages the hands and other limbs when serving the Lord, even when doing kīrtana once. O lord who lives within the universe (nivāsa-jagat). The word tava is repeated to indicate that by offering respects to the Lord respects are offered to the world, since the Lord is everything, and thus perfection in offering respects is achieved. Our eyes should be engaged in seeking devotees, in which you always manifest yourself or our eyes should see the devotees within whom forms of the Lord like śālagrāmas are worshipped. Or our eyes should see the devotees and the forms of the Lord. They do not pray that the Lord directly appear in their homes out of fear. In this way six types of bhakti are mentioned: kīrtana, śvaraṇa, pūjā, paricaryā, smaraṇa and vandana. Sakhya and ātma-nivedana are generally included in the goal (sādhya) and are thus not mentioned in a prayer about sādhana. Or these two are naturally the result of the other six. Or the two are included in smaraṇa, being particular bhāvas. Karmārpaṇa and dāsya is included in kīrtana and other processes. Normally speaking words agitate all the senses. They pray for kīrtana because by doing that the other senses become happily engaged in their respective actions. Or kīrtana is the main process because the Lord prefers it. Nāhaṁ vasāmi vaikuṇṭhe: I do not reside in Vaikuṇṭha. Or kīrtana produces a special rasa. Seeing the devotees and deities is the same as seeing the Lord directly. Since seeing the Lord directly is the end result of all processes of bhakti, this is mentioned last. The other processes mentioned in the middle one can do according to one’s taste.

Personal Guidance
Engage every sense — speech, hearing, hands, mind, head, eyes — in Kṛṣṇa's service; bhakti is the only authentic process to know Him.
Practical Applications
  • Audit one sense today (tongue, ears, or eyes) and redirect it from viṣaya to a concrete act of bhakti — speak of His pastimes, hear a kīrtana, behold a Vaiṣṇava.
  • Refuse the yata mata, tata patha attitude in your own life: drop one self-invented 'spiritual practice' and replace it with a śāstra-given limb of bhakti — śravaṇa, kīrtana, or smaraṇa.
  • Before sleep tonight, offer praṇāma with your head to the Vaiṣṇavas you encountered, recognizing each as bhavat-tanu — His own form.
Analogy

A whole orchestra dedicated to one melody — the speaker on speech, the hearer on hearing, the hands on rhythm, the mind on the absent beat, the eyes on the conductor. The siddhas pledge every sense to Kṛṣṇa's līlā by name: vāṇī to His pastimes, śravaṇau to His kathā, hastau to His mission, manas to His feet, śiras to praṇāma, dṛṣṭi to His Vaiṣṇavas. Each instrument is given its part.

Stories
Hanumān's six-sense surrender

Hanumān, asked how he had crossed the ocean and burned Laṅkā, replied that his speech was Rāma's name, his hearing Rāma's glories, his hands Rāma's work, his mind Rāma's feet, his head Rāma's praṇāma, and his eyes Rāma's form. Every sense had a single destination.

SB 10.10.38 enumerates the same pledge in the same form, sense by sense. The verse becomes the template for the morning prayer of the Vaiṣṇava: pañca-indriya samarpaṇa as the entry-fee of the day.
