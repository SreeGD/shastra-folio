Chapter 26 · Class prep

BG 26.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: The cowherd men were astonished when they saw Kṛṣṇa’s activities, such as lifting Govardhana Hill. Unable to understand His transcendental potency, they approached Nanda Mahārāja and spoke as follows.

Essence. The cowherds, awestruck by Kṛṣṇa lifting Govardhana, approach Nanda to make sense of it. After a miracle, perplexity often arrives at home — the wise consult their elders rather than try to settle it alone.

BG 26.2

[The cowherd men said:] Since this boy performs such extraordinary activities, how could He warrant a birth among worldly men like us — a birth that for Him would seem contemptible?

Essence. 'Since this boy performs such extraordinary acts, how could He warrant a birth among ordinary humans?' They sense He is too much for the form He has taken. Astonishment becomes a doorway to deeper recognition.

BG 26.3

How could this seven-year-old boy playfully hold up the great hill Govardhana with one hand, just as a mighty elephant holds up a lotus flower?

Essence. 'How could a seven-year-old lift Govardhana with one hand, as an elephant lifts a lotus?' The image itself — child holding mountain like flower — is the meditation. Sometimes the right response is just to keep picturing the impossible.

BG 26.4

As a mere infant who had hardly yet opened His eyes, He drank the breast milk of the powerful demoness Pūtanā and then sucked out her very life air as well, just as the force of time sucks out the youth of one’s body.

Essence. As an infant He drank Pūtanā's breast milk and sucked out her life-air, as time sucks out youth. Kṛṣṇa is time itself in His infant form. The fiercest force in the cosmos plays in a baby's body.

BG 26.5

Once, when only three months old, little Kṛṣṇa was crying and kicking up His feet as He lay beneath a huge cart. Then the cart fell and turned upside-down simply because it was struck by the tip of His toe.

Essence. At three months, kicking under a cart, He overturned it with His toe-tip. Even the kicking infant displayed cosmic strength — His earliest gestures are already pastimes.

BG 26.6

At the age of one, while sitting peacefully He was taken up into the sky by the demon Tṛṇāvarta. But baby Kṛṣṇa grabbed the demon’s neck, causing him great pain, and thus killed him.

Essence. At one year, taken up into the sky by Tṛṇāvarta, baby Kṛṣṇa grabbed the demon's neck and killed him. Even being kidnapped became an opportunity to dispatch evil — every situation, however hostile, the Lord turns to His purpose.

BG 26.7

Once, His mother tied Him with ropes to a mortar because she had caught Him stealing butter. Then, crawling on His hands, He dragged the mortar between a pair of arjuna trees and pulled them down.

Essence. Tied to a mortar for stealing butter, He crawled and pulled down two huge arjuna trees. The discipline imposed on Him becomes the vehicle of His next pastime — bondage in His case opens new movement.

BG 26.8

Another time, when Kṛṣṇa was tending the calves in the forest together with Balarāma and the cowherd boys, the demon Bakāsura came with the intention of killing Kṛṣṇa. But Kṛṣṇa seized this inimical demon by the mouth and tore him apart.

Essence. Bakāsura, the crane-demon, came intending to kill Kṛṣṇa, but Kṛṣṇa tore him apart by the mouth. Mouth-attacks — verbal, ingestive — meet with mouth-defenses; the Lord uses the attacker's own weapon to finish them.

BG 26.9

Desiring to kill Kṛṣṇa, the demon Vatsa disguised himself as a calf and entered among Kṛṣṇa’s calves. But Kṛṣṇa killed the demon and, using his body, enjoyed the sport of knocking kapittha fruits down from the trees.

Essence. Vatsa disguised as a calf entered the herd; Kṛṣṇa killed him and used his body to knock down kapittha fruits. Even a defeated demon becomes a tool — the Lord wastes no enemy.

BG 26.10

Together with Lord Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa killed the jackass demon and all his friends, thereby securing the safety of the Tālavana forest, which abounded with fully ripened palm fruits.

Essence. With Balarāma He killed Dhenuka and his friends, securing the safe Tālavana with its ripe fruits. The Lord clears terrorists who keep good things inaccessible to ordinary people; security restored is feast restored.

BG 26.11

After arranging for the mighty Lord Balarāma to kill the terrible demon Pralamba, Kṛṣṇa saved Vraja’s cowherd boys and their animals from a forest fire.

Essence. After arranging for Balarāma to kill Pralamba, Kṛṣṇa saved the cowherds from the forest fire. The Lord coordinates with His expansions — His brother takes one task, He takes another; nothing falls through the cracks.

BG 26.12

Kṛṣṇa chastised the most poisonous serpent, Kāliya, and after humbling him He drove him forcibly from the lake of the Yamunā. In this way the Lord made the water of that river free of the snake’s powerful poison.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa chastised Kāliya and drove him forcibly from the Yamunā lake, freeing the river from poison. The Lord doesn't just neutralize the threat; He restores the river's original sweetness for ordinary use.

BG 26.13

Dear Nanda, how is it that we and all the other residents of Vraja cannot give up our constant affection for your son? And how is it that He is so spontaneously attracted to us?

Essence. 'How is it that we and all Vraja can't give up our love for your son? And how is He so spontaneously attracted to us?' Mutual irresistible attraction is the marker of the all-attractive Lord meeting genuine devotion.

BG 26.14

On the one hand this boy is only seven years old, and on the other we see that He has lifted the great hill Govardhana. Therefore, O King of Vraja, a doubt about your son arises within us.

Essence. 'On one hand seven years old, on the other He lifted Govardhana. A doubt arises about your son.' Honest doubt voiced to a trusted elder is part of devotional inquiry — they didn't suppress it, they asked.

BG 26.15

Nanda Mahārāja replied: O cowherd men, just hear my words and let all your doubts concerning my son be gone. Some time ago Garga Muni spoke to me as follows about this boy.

Essence. 'Hear my words and let your doubts go. Garga Muni told me about this boy.' Nanda answers with received teaching, not personal speculation. The right answer to doubt is often a remembered word from a sage.

BG 26.16

[Garga Muni had said:] Your son Kṛṣṇa appears as an incarnation in every millennium. In the past He assumed three different colors — white, red and yellow — and now He has appeared in a blackish color.

Essence. 'Your son appears every millennium — past colors white, red, yellow; now blackish.' The Lord's incarnations form a series; recognizing the pattern lets us read the present incarnation against the catalog.

BG 26.17

For many reasons, this beautiful son of yours sometimes appeared previously as the son of Vasudeva. Therefore, those who are learned sometimes call this child Vāsudeva.

Essence. 'For many reasons He sometimes appeared as Vasudeva's son — hence called Vāsudeva.' Names carry histories; understanding why a name is used connects us to a chain of pastimes.

BG 26.18

For this son of yours there are many forms and names according to His transcendental qualities and activities. These are known to me, but people in general do not understand them.

Essence. 'Many forms and names according to His qualities and activities — known to me but not to common people.' Initiate knowledge of His variety is a gift not all receive — and a responsibility to share when invited.

BG 26.19

To increase the transcendental bliss of the cowherd men of Gokula, this child will always act auspiciously for you. And by His grace only, you will surpass all difficulties.

Essence. 'This child will always act auspiciously for you; by His grace alone you will surpass all difficulties.' Difficulties remain; what changes is who passes through them with you. Promise is companionship, not absence of trouble.

BG 26.20

O Nanda Mahārāja, as recorded in history, when there was an irregular, incapable government, Indra having been dethroned, and when honest people were being harassed and disturbed by thieves, this child appeared in order to curb the rogues and to protect the people and enable them to flourish.

Essence. 'When government was irregular, Indra dethroned, honest people harassed by thieves, this child appeared to curb rogues and protect the people.' The Lord's appearance has political and social purpose — He arrives when civic order has failed.

BG 26.21

Demons cannot harm the demigods, who always have Lord Viṣṇu on their side. Similarly, any person or group attached to all-auspicious Kṛṣṇa cannot be defeated by enemies.

Essence. 'Demons can't harm demigods who have Viṣṇu; no group attached to Kṛṣṇa can be defeated.' The promise of invincibility applies to the group, not the individual ego — collective devotion is sturdier than solo virtue.

BG 26.22

Therefore, O Nanda Mahārāja, this child of yours is as good as Nārāyaṇa. In His transcendental qualities, opulence, name, fame and influence, He is exactly like Nārāyaṇa. Thus you should not be astonished by His activities.

Essence. 'This child is as good as Nārāyaṇa — same qualities, opulence, name, fame, influence. Don't be astonished by His activities.' Once you place Him as Nārāyaṇa, surprise dissolves into recognition.

BG 26.23

[Nanda Mahārāja continued:] After Garga Ṛṣi spoke these words to me and returned home, I began to consider that Kṛṣṇa, who keeps us free from trouble, is actually an expansion of Lord Nārāyaṇa.

Essence. After Garga left, Nanda began considering Kṛṣṇa, who keeps trouble away, as an expansion of Nārāyaṇa. Mature reflection on a sage's word, over time, settles into stable conviction.

BG 26.24

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued:] Having heard Nanda Mahārāja relate the statements of Garga Muni, the residents of Vṛndāvana became enlivened. Their perplexity was gone, and they worshiped Nanda and Lord Kṛṣṇa with great respect.

Essence. Hearing Garga's statements through Nanda, the residents are enlivened; perplexity gone, they worship Nanda and Kṛṣṇa with respect. Resolution of doubt produces visible enlivenment and increase of worship.

BG 26.25

Indra became angry when his sacrifice was disrupted, and thus he caused rain and hail to fall on Gokula, accompanied by lightning and powerful winds, all of which brought great suffering to the cowherds, animals and women there. When Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is by nature always compassionate, saw the condition of those who had only Him as their shelter, He smiled broadly and lifted Govardhana Hill with one hand, just as a small child picks up a mushroom to play with it. Holding up the hill, He protected the cowherd community. May He, Govinda, the Lord of the cows and the destroyer of Indra’s false pride, be pleased with us.

Essence. Indra raged, rain and hail tormented Gokula, and Kṛṣṇa, smiling, lifted Govardhana with one hand as a child picks up a mushroom. May Govinda — Lord of cows, destroyer of Indra's pride — be pleased with us.
