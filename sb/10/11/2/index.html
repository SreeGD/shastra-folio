Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.11.2

भूम्यां निपतितौ तत्र दद‍ृशुर्यमलार्जुनौ । बभ्रमुस्तदविज्ञाय लक्ष्यं पतनकारणम् ॥ २ ॥
bhūmyāṁ nipatitau tatra
[bhoomyaam nipatitau tatra]
dadṛśur yamalārjunau
[dadrishur yamalaarjunau]
babhramus tad avijñāya
[babhramus tad avijnaaya]
lakṣyaṁ patana-kāraṇam
[lakshyam patana-kaaranam]

Important Words
  • babhramuḥ — they became bewildered
    Bhrama — the technical word for confusion in Vedānta. Gauḍīya commentators distinguish bhrama (illusion) from pramāda and vipralipsā: the gopas are not lying or careless; they are simply unable to see through prakṛti to its underlying puruṣa. This is the natural condition of those still seeing Kṛṣṇa through paternal affection rather than tattva.
  • avijñāya — but they could not trace out
    A-vi-jñā — the prefix 'a' negates real knowing; 'vi' intensifies. They had perception (lakṣya) but no vijñāna — the discriminating knowledge that would link effect to its true cause. The Gauḍīya distinction: jñāna of Kṛṣṇa's act requires anugraha, not just sense data.
  • lakṣyam — the visible mark, what could be seen
    From lakṣ — 'to mark, to perceive.' The gopas had the lakṣya (effect) but lacked the lakṣaṇa (defining sign) that would identify Kṛṣṇa as cause. Effects are public; causes, when Kṛṣṇa is the agent, are revealed only by His grace.
  • patana - kāraṇam — the cause of the falling
    Kāraṇa here is the philosophical 'cause' (nimitta-kāraṇa). The verse pivots on this word: Bhagavān is the para-kāraṇa of all events, but His causation is acintya — beyond logical inference. Gauḍīya siddhānta: His role as kāraṇa is known by śravaṇa from Him and His devotees, not deduced.
There they saw the fallen yamala-arjuna trees on the ground, but they were bewildered because even though they could directly perceive that the trees had fallen, they could not trace out the cause for their having done so.
Purport

Considering all the circumstances, had this been done by Kṛṣṇa? He was standing on the spot, and His playmates described that this had been done by Him. Had Kṛṣṇa actually done this, or were these merely stories? This was a cause of bewilderment.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
There they saw the fallen yamala-arjuna trees on the ground, but they were bewildered because even though they could directly perceive that the trees had fallen, they could not trace out the cause for their having done so.
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

They saw the two trees completely uprooted (ni), lying on the ground. Though it was possible to see that the mortar had pulled the trees down they were unaware of that, since they were far away and confused and the branches covered up the view. Aware of the situation, they were in doubt. Seeing the situation, they would first become overcome with fear, not trying to see the cause of the trees falling. Even knowing the cause, they would not be inquisitive. Coming closer, they saw Kṛṣṇa covered with the branches and said in pain, “Who has done this? This is astonishing. Why have they done this?” Hari-vaṁśa describes the incident. kenemau pātitau vṛkṣau ghoṣasyāyatanopamau vinā vātaṁ vinā varṣa vidyut-prapatanaṁ vinā vinā hastikṛtaṁ doṣam kenemau pātitau drumau How did these two trees, big as a cowherd’s house fall, without wind or rain, without being struck by lightning, without being attacked by elephants?

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

They saw the two trees completely uprooted (ni), lying on the ground. Though it was possible to understand (lakṣyam) the cause of the trees falling there, they were bewildered and could not understand. Hari-vaṁśa describes the incident. kenemau pātitau vṛkṣau ghoṣasyāyatanopamau vinā vātaṁ vinā varṣa vidyut-prapatanaṁ vinā vinā hastikṛtaṁ doṣam kenemau pātitau drumau

Personal Guidance
When Kṛṣṇa's hand is hidden behind ordinary appearances, the bewildered see only effects; the devotee learns to trust His unseen agency.
Practical Applications
  • Notice one event today where you instinctively reach for a material cause, and pause to ask where Kṛṣṇa's hand might be hidden in it.
  • Refuse to dismiss as 'mere story' the accounts of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes simply because they exceed your perception — hear them from guru and śāstra with humility.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa directly about one situation that bewilders you today, and ask Him to reveal the patana-kāraṇam — the cause behind the fall.
Analogy

A child sees a lamp suddenly bloom into light and cannot find the hand that lit it — only the brightness is evident. So the gopas see the fallen trees but cannot trace the hand of Kṛṣṇa, because Bhagavān's agency, being acintya, leaves visible effects without visible causes for those not yet awakened to His personality.

Stories
Bewilderment before Bhagavān's hand

Throughout the Bhāgavatam, this same pattern repeats: Kaṁsa cannot trace why his weapons keep failing; Duryodhana cannot trace why his side keeps faltering despite superior numbers; even the demigods, in the churning of the Milk Ocean, cannot trace why their poison is suddenly absorbed by Śiva. The lakṣya is seen; the patana-kāraṇam is hidden.

The Gauḍīya commentators read this as a recurring tutorial: until one approaches by guru and śāstra, Bhagavān's hand will remain hidden behind 'natural' explanations. The cure is not sharper perception but humbler hearing.
