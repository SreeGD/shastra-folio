Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.11.25

चक्रवातेन नीतोऽयं दैत्येन विपदं वियत् । शिलायां पतितस्तत्र परित्रात: सुरेश्वरै: ॥ २५ ॥

Important Words
  • cakra - vātena — by the whirlwind-demon
    Cakra-vāta — circling wind, the meteorological form Tṛṇāvarta took. The instrumental marks the means: even when an asura attacks, he must take a natural form to do so. Gauḍīya teaching: demons in līlā are always 'natural causes' to the unawakened — the devotee learns to see the daitya behind the wind.
  • daityena — by the demon
    Daitya — a son of Diti; the standard category of asura in śāstra. The verse moves from the natural cause (whirlwind) to the personal cause (daitya) — the same movement of seeing the Gauḍīya commentators want the reader to make in his own life.
  • śilāyām — on a slab of stone
    Śilā — rock, stone. The stone on which Tṛṇāvarta crashes echoes Govardhana — the stone Kṛṣṇa would later lift — and śālagrāma-śilā, the worshipable form of Viṣṇu. Stone in the Bhāgavatam is rarely accidental: it carries the weight of Bhagavān's stability.
  • paritrātaḥ — was saved
    Pari-trā — to protect all around. The 'pari' intensifies: protection on every side, not just from one direction. Gauḍīya teaching: when Hari's rakṣaṇa works, it is total — the devotee is held safe in every dimension simultaneously.
  • sura - īśvaraiḥ — by the lords of the gods (Viṣṇu and associates)
    Sureśvara — usually 'the lord of the gods' (Viṣṇu) or 'the chiefs among the gods.' Gauḍīya commentators read it as Viṣṇu Himself with His pārṣadas (eternal associates). Even Kṛṣṇa's līlā-protection in Vraja moves through this Vaikuṇṭha-channel.
Then again, the demon Tṛṇāvarta, in the form of a whirlwind, took the child away into the dangerous sky to kill Him, but the demon fell down onto a slab of stone. In that case also, by the mercy of Lord Viṣṇu or His associates, the child was saved.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Then again, the demon Tṛṇāvarta, in the form of a whirlwind, took the child away into the dangerous sky to kill Him, but the demon fell down onto a slab of stone. In that case also, by the mercy of Lord Viṣṇu or His associates, the child was saved.
Commentary

Asau refers to krsna.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

He was taken by the demon into the sky, full of danger, since it was high, without any support (vi—padam—without a base). He was saved by associates sent by the Lord. The word api in the next verse indicates that in this case also the Lord (along with his associates) saved him.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Ayam indicates that the event happened soon after, or concerning the same person. Or it indicates affection, by showing the inauspicious act of Tṛnāvarta. He was taken by the demon into the sky, full of danger, since it was high, without any support (vi—padam—without a base). Or he took him in the sky which was dangerous (vipadam) because if he fell he would be killed. Though it is not suitable to speak of the Lord in this manner, it was done to invoke a sense of fear. Or it is stated out of anxiety arising from great affection according to aniṣṭāśaṅkīnī bandhu-hṛdayāni bhavanti: a relative or intimate friend is always fearful of some injury to his beloved. (Abhijṣāna-śakuntalā-nāṭaka) This should be understood in later statements like na mriyeta (verse 26). Though he fell, he fell on stone. Above and below there was danger. He was saved by the best devatās (sureśvaraiḥ). Or thinking of the great power of the Lord to protect in all circumstances, out of respect the Supreme Lord is indicated by using the plural (sureśvaraiḥ).

Personal Guidance
Even when overwhelming forces seize you, Kṛṣṇa's rakṣaṇa is silently at work — the demon falls, the child is saved.
Practical Applications
  • Name one 'whirlwind' currently lifting you off the ground — a deadline, a craving, a fear — and consciously place it in Kṛṣṇa's hands before acting on it today.
  • When a situation feels beyond your control, refuse the impulse to grip harder; instead, chant one round of japa and let the daityena exhaust itself against the śilā of His protection.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's name aloud once today in a moment of ordinary safety, acknowledging that the unremarkable survival of this hour is itself sureśvarair paritrāṇa.
Analogy

A great wind lifts a child off His feet and seems to carry Him into nothingness — and then the wind itself shatters, and the child stands on stone, unharmed. Whatever appears to seize Kṛṣṇa's devotee is silently undone by His associates; the daitya exhausts itself against the śilā of His protection, and the child remains.

Stories
Tṛṇāvarta's whirlwind

Tṛṇāvarta, sent by Kaṁsa as a whirlwind-demon, lifted the infant Kṛṣṇa into the dust-darkened sky. The Vrajavāsīs lost sight of Him; Yaśodā fainted. But Kṛṣṇa, grasping the demon's throat, made Himself unbearably heavy until Tṛṇāvarta crashed onto a slab of stone and died, while the child was found unharmed (SB 10.7.20–32).

Gauḍīya commentators emphasize the verse's phrase 'sureśvarair paritrātaḥ' — saved by the lords of the gods (i.e. by Viṣṇu and His associates). Even when Bhagavān Himself is the protected, the protection is staged through His own personal energies, dramatizing the doctrine that all rakṣaṇa originates in Viṣṇu and flows through His associates.
