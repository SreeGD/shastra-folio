Chapter 24 · Class prep

BG 24.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: While staying in that very place with His brother Baladeva, Lord Kṛṣṇa happened to see the cowherd men busily arranging for a sacrifice to Indra.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa sees Nanda and the cowherd men arranging an elaborate sacrifice to Indra. The Lord notices when His own devotees are about to misdirect their devotion — He doesn't ignore it, but plans to redirect it.

BG 24.2

Being the omniscient Supersoul, the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa already understood the situation, yet He still humbly inquired from the elders, headed by His father, Nanda Mahārāja.

Essence. Omniscient Kṛṣṇa already understood, yet humbly asks the elders. He doesn't display knowledge; He invites the explanation. Genuine inquiry from one who knows is itself a form of teaching.

BG 24.3

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] My dear father, kindly explain to Me what this great endeavor of yours is all about. What is it meant to accomplish? If this is a ritual sacrifice, then for whose satisfaction is it intended and by what means is it going to be executed?

Essence. 'My dear father, please explain — for whose satisfaction is this sacrifice, by what means executed?' The Lord names the standard questions a serious inquirer asks. Clarity about audience and method comes before performance.

BG 24.4

Please tell Me about it, O father. I have a great desire to know and am ready to hear in good faith. Certainly, no secrets are to be kept by saintly personalities, who see all others as equal to themselves, who have no conception of “mine” or “another’s” and who do not consider who is a friend, who is an enemy and who is neutral.

Essence. 'I'm ready to hear in good faith. Saintly persons keep no secrets — they see all as equal and don't divide friend, enemy, neutral.' The Lord asks to be admitted into the conversation as a saintly hearer.

BG 24.5

One who is neutral may be avoided like an enemy, but a friend should be considered like one’s own self.

Essence. 'A neutral person may be avoided as an enemy, but a friend should be considered like one's own self.' The Lord builds the case that He, as son, must be included as friend in the family's decisions.

BG 24.6

When people in this world perform activities, sometimes they understand what they are doing and sometimes they don’t. Those who know what they are doing achieve success in their work, whereas ignorant people do not.

Essence. 'People sometimes act knowingly, sometimes not — and those who know what they're doing succeed.' Tradition is not enough; understanding what we're doing matters. Blind ritual rarely yields its claimed results.

BG 24.7

Such being the case, this ritualistic endeavor of yours should be clearly explained to Me. Is it a ceremony based on scriptural injunction, or simply a custom of ordinary society?

Essence. 'Is this ceremony based on scripture, or simply on social custom?' Distinguishing scriptural authority from social convention is a permanent question every honest practitioner must ask.

BG 24.8

Nanda Mahārāja replied: The great Lord Indra is the controller of the rain. The clouds are his personal representatives, and they directly provide rainwater, which gives happiness and sustenance to all creatures.

Essence. Nanda explains: Indra controls rain; clouds are his representatives; rain sustains all life. The traditional view names a real benefactor — but stops short of asking who oversees the benefactor.

BG 24.9

Not only we, my dear son, but also many other men worship him, the lord and master of the rain-giving clouds. We offer him grain and other paraphernalia of worship produced through his own discharge in the form of rain.

Essence. 'Many men worship Indra, the lord of rain-clouds. We offer him grain and items produced from his own rain.' The economy of worship returns to its source — we give what was given to us.

BG 24.10

By accepting the remnants of sacrifices performed to Indra, people sustain their lives and accomplish the threefold aims of religiosity, economic development and sense gratification. Thus Lord Indra is the agent responsible for the fruitive success of industrious people.

Essence. Accepting Indra-sacrifice remnants sustains life and accomplishes dharma, artha, kāma. Indra, says Nanda, is responsible for the success of industrious people. This is the traditional, partial truth that Kṛṣṇa will deepen.

BG 24.11

This religious principle is based on sound tradition. Anyone who rejects it out of lust, enmity, fear or greed will certainly fail to achieve good fortune.

Essence. 'This religious principle is based on sound tradition. Reject it from lust, enmity, fear, or greed and you'll lose good fortune.' Nanda warns of the danger of casual deviation — but tradition is not the final word.

BG 24.12

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: When Lord Keśava [Kṛṣṇa] heard the statements of His father, Nanda, and other senior residents of Vraja, He addressed His father as follows, to arouse anger in Lord Indra.

Essence. Hearing Nanda, Kṛṣṇa speaks — intending to provoke Indra so He can humble his pride and start the Govardhana-pūjā festival. The Lord deliberately stirs a controlled crisis to teach a larger lesson.

BG 24.13

Lord Kṛṣṇa said: It is by the force of karma that a living entity takes birth, and it is by karma alone that he meets his destruction. His happiness, distress, fear and sense of security all arise as the effects of karma.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa argues: birth, destruction, happiness, fear — all arise from karma. He temporarily voices karma-vāda to shake His listeners' assumption that Indra controls outcomes. Sometimes the teacher voices a half-truth to dislodge a different half-truth.

BG 24.14

Even if there is some supreme controller who awards all others the results of their activities, He must also depend upon a performer’s engaging in activity. After all, there is no question of being the bestower of fruitive results unless fruitive activities have actually been performed.

Essence. 'Even a supreme controller must depend on the performer's action. Without activity, no reward to give.' The Lord points out that any system of just dessert requires the actor — so why worship the dispenser separately?

BG 24.15

Living beings in this world are forced to experience the consequences of their own particular previous work. Since Lord Indra cannot in any way change the destiny of human beings, which is born of their own nature, why should people worship him?

Essence. Beings reap consequences of their own previous work; Indra cannot change destiny. Why worship him? The argument exposes the illogic of expecting a demigod to override the karma he doesn't own.

BG 24.16

Every individual is under the control of his own conditioned nature, and thus he must follow that nature. This entire universe, with all its demigods, demons and human beings, is based on the conditioned nature of the living entities.

Essence. Every being follows its own conditioned nature; the universe runs on the natures of its participants. If conditioning is the engine, why worship demigods? Kṛṣṇa pushes the materialist line further.

BG 24.17

Because it is karma that causes the conditioned living entity to accept and then give up different high- and low-grade material bodies, this karma is his enemy, friend and neutral witness, his spiritual master and controlling lord.

Essence. Karma — accepting and giving up bodies — is the enemy, friend, witness, master of conditioned beings. Even Indra is bound by it. The argument reaches the point that 'work itself' rules everything.

BG 24.18

Therefore one should seriously worship work itself. A person should remain in the position corresponding to his nature and should perform his own duty. Indeed, that by which we may live nicely is really our worshipable deity.

Essence. 'Worship work itself; stay in your position; do your duty. That which lets us live well is our worshipable deity.' Kṛṣṇa playfully proposes the modern view, exposing its limits by stating it plainly.

BG 24.19

If one thing is actually sustaining our life but we take shelter of something else, how can we achieve any real benefit? We would be like an unfaithful woman, who can never achieve any actual benefit by consorting with her paramour.

Essence. 'If one thing sustains us but we shelter in another, what real benefit? Like an unfaithful woman with her paramour — no real prosperity comes.' Misdirected dependence yields illusion of safety, never the thing itself.

BG 24.20

The brāhmaṇa maintains his life by studying and teaching the Vedas, the member of the royal order by protecting the earth, the vaiśya by trade, and the śūdra by serving the higher, twice-born classes.

Essence. Brāhmaṇas live by study and teaching; kṣatriyas by protecting; vaiśyas by trade; śūdras by service. Lord Kṛṣṇa names the dharma of each — every life has its prescribed work, born of nature and meant for service.

BG 24.21

The occupational duties of the vaiśya are conceived in four divisions: farming, commerce, cow protection and moneylending. Out of these, we as a community are always engaged in cow protection.

Essence. Vaiśya duties divide into farming, commerce, cow protection, moneylending — and our community is always engaged in cow protection. Specific identification of one's role within a tradition is itself part of devotion.

BG 24.22

The causes of creation, maintenance and destruction are the three modes of nature — namely goodness, passion and ignorance. In particular, the mode of passion creates this universe and through sexual combination causes it to become full of variety.

Essence. Creation, maintenance, destruction arise from the three modes — and passion creates the variegated cosmos. Kṛṣṇa briefly voices atheistic Sāṅkhya's mechanism to show how easy it is to skip the Designer.

BG 24.23

Impelled by the material mode of passion, the clouds pour down their rain everywhere, and by this rain all creatures gain their sustenance. What has the great Indra to do with this arrangement?

Essence. 'The clouds rain everywhere by the mode of passion; all creatures gain sustenance. What has Indra to do with it?' Lord Kṛṣṇa exaggerates the mechanistic view to make Nanda hear how thin it sounds without a Person behind it.

BG 24.24

My dear father, our home is not in the cities or towns or villages. Being forest dwellers, we always live in the forest and on the hills.

Essence. 'Our home is not towns or cities — we are forest dwellers on hills.' Identity drives worship; our actual place determines our authentic deities. Govardhana, not distant Indra, is the locally relevant power to honor.

BG 24.25

Therefore may a sacrifice for the pleasure of the cows, the brāhmaṇas and Govardhana Hill begin! With all the paraphernalia collected for worshiping Indra, let this sacrifice be performed instead.

Essence. 'Let a sacrifice begin for the cows, brāhmaṇas, and Govardhana Hill — using all the Indra-paraphernalia.' Redirect existing devotional infrastructure toward truer recipients; don't waste preparation, repurpose it.

BG 24.26

Let many different kinds of food be cooked, from sweet rice to vegetable soups! Many kinds of fancy cakes, both baked and fried, should be prepared. And all the available milk products should be taken for this sacrifice.

Essence. 'Cook many foods — sweet rice, vegetable soups, fancy cakes, all milk products.' The Govardhana-pūjā is a feast, not a deprivation. Devotional festivals are generous and richly cooked.

BG 24.27

The brāhmaṇas who are learned in the Vedic mantras must properly invoke the sacrificial fires. Then you should feed the priests with nicely prepared food and reward them with cows and other gifts.

Essence. 'Have Vedic brāhmaṇas invoke the fires properly, feed them well, reward them with cows and gifts.' Procedural integrity and proper compensation for the learned servants of dharma are part of the festival's substance.

BG 24.28

After giving the appropriate food to everyone else, including such fallen souls as dogs and dog-eaters, you should give grass to the cows and then present your respectful offerings to Govardhana Hill.

Essence. 'Feed everyone — even dogs and dog-eaters. Then give grass to cows and offerings to Govardhana.' The festival's circle includes the lowest; nobody is forgotten before the sacred hill receives its honor.

BG 24.29

After everyone has eaten to his satisfaction, you should all dress and decorate yourselves handsomely, smear your bodies with sandalwood paste and then circumambulate the cows, the brāhmaṇas, the sacrificial fires and Govardhana Hill.

Essence. After eating, dress beautifully, smear sandalwood, then circumambulate cows, brāhmaṇas, fires, and Govardhana. Aesthetic care of body and movement around the sacred completes the worship.

BG 24.30

This is My idea, O father, and you may carry it out if it appeals to you. Such a sacrifice will be very dear to the cows, the brāhmaṇas and Govardhana Hill, and also to Me.

Essence. 'This is My idea; carry it out if it appeals to you. The sacrifice will be dear to cows, brāhmaṇas, Govardhana, and to Me.' The Lord leaves room for consent — He doesn't override; He proposes.

BG 24.31

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is Himself powerful time, desired to destroy the false pride of Lord Indra. When Nanda and the other senior men of Vṛndāvana heard Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s statement, they accepted His words as proper.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, the powerful time itself, wants to destroy Indra's pride. The elders accept His words. Even radical proposals from a child are accepted when spoken by the Lord — devotional consent overrides social hierarchy.

BG 24.32-33

The cowherd community then did all that Madhusūdana had suggested. They arranged for the brāhmaṇas to recite the auspicious Vedic mantras, and using the paraphernalia that had been intended for Indra’s sacrifice, they presented offerings to Govardhana Hill and the brāhmaṇas with reverential respect. They also gave grass to the cows. Then, placing the cows, bulls and calves in front of them, they circumambulated Govardhana.

Essence. The cowherds do exactly as Madhusūdana suggested — Vedic mantras, offerings to Govardhana and brāhmaṇas, grass to cows, and circumambulation with cows in front. Implementation of the Lord's plan is detailed and concrete.

BG 24.34

As the beautifully ornamented cowherd ladies followed along, riding on wagons drawn by oxen, they sang the glories of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and their songs mingled with the brāhmaṇas’ chanting of benedictions.

Essence. The ornamented cowherd ladies ride wagons, singing Kṛṣṇa's glories, blending with brāhmaṇas' benedictions. The festival is collective music — women's songs and priestly mantras together.

BG 24.35

Kṛṣṇa then assumed an unprecedented, huge form to instill faith in the cowherd men. Declaring “I am Govardhana Mountain!” He ate the abundant offerings.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa assumes a huge form, declares 'I am Govardhana Mountain!' and eats the offerings. The Lord shows the identity of Govardhana with Himself — devotion to the holy place is devotion to the Person.

BG 24.36

Together with the people of Vraja, the Lord bowed down to this form of Govardhana Hill, thus in effect offering obeisances to Himself. Then He said, “Just see how this hill has appeared in person and bestowed mercy upon us!

Essence. Kṛṣṇa bows with the villagers to Govardhana — bowing in effect to Himself. 'See how the hill appeared in person and bestowed mercy!' The Lord demonstrates worship by joining it, not by receiving it from above.

BG 24.37

“This Govardhana Hill, assuming any form he wishes, will kill any residents of the forest who neglect him. Therefore let us pay our obeisances to him for the safety of ourselves and our cows.”

Essence. 'This hill, assuming any form, can kill those who neglect him. Let us bow for safety of self and cows.' Govardhana is benevolent but not to be ignored — sacred presence demands ongoing acknowledgment.

BG 24.38

The members of the cowherd community, having thus been inspired by Lord Vāsudeva to properly execute the sacrifice to Govardhana Hill, the cows and the brāhmaṇas, returned with Lord Kṛṣṇa to their village, Vraja.

Essence. Inspired by Lord Vāsudeva, the cowherds finish the sacrifice and return to Vraja with Him. But Indra, hearing of it, is enraged — the consequence of redirecting devotion is the next test.
