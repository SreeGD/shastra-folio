Chapter 46 · Class prep

BG 46.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: The supremely intelligent Uddhava was the best counselor of the Vṛṣṇi dynasty, a beloved friend of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa and a direct disciple of Bṛhaspati.

Essence. Uddhava — supremely intelligent best counselor of the Vṛṣṇis, beloved friend of Kṛṣṇa, direct disciple of Bṛhaspati — is introduced. The right messenger for a delicate mission must be intelligent, intimate with the Lord, and trained by qualified teachers.

BG 46.2

The Supreme Lord Hari, who relieves the distress of all who surrender to Him, once took the hand of His fully devoted, dearmost friend Uddhava and addressed him as follows.

Essence. Hari, who relieves distress of all surrendered, takes Uddhava's hand — His dearest friend — and addresses him. The Lord physically holds the messenger's hand before sending him; commissioning includes touch.

BG 46.3

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] Dear gentle Uddhava, go to Vraja and give pleasure to Our parents. And also relieve the gopīs, suffering in separation from Me, by giving them My message.

Essence. Dear gentle Uddhava, go to Vraja and give pleasure to Our parents; also relieve the gopīs suffering in separation by giving them My message. The Lord's care reaches the people He left behind through trusted messengers.

BG 46.4

The minds of those gopīs are always absorbed in Me, and their very lives are ever devoted to Me. For My sake they have abandoned everything related to their bodies, renouncing ordinary happiness in this life, as well as religious duties necessary for such happiness in the next life. I alone am their dearmost beloved and, indeed, their very Self. I personally sustain such devotees, who for My sake give up all worldly duties.

Essence. The gopīs' minds are always absorbed in Me, lives ever devoted; for My sake they abandoned bodily relations, ordinary happiness, and religious duties for the next life. Total surrender includes letting go even of orderly future-life planning.

BG 46.5

My dear Uddhava, for those women of Gokula I am the most cherished object of love. Thus when they remember Me, who am so far away, they are overwhelmed by the anxiety of separation.

Essence. For those women I am the most cherished object of love; remembering Me far away, they are overwhelmed by separation. The dearer the Lord is, the more painful His absence — devotion intensifies vulnerability.

BG 46.6

Simply because I have promised to return to them, My fully devoted cowherd girlfriends struggle to maintain their lives somehow or other.

Essence. Simply because I have promised to return, My fully devoted girlfriends struggle to maintain their lives. Promises from Kṛṣṇa become life-support for devotees — the word 'I will return' is enough to keep someone alive.

BG 46.7

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus addressed, O King, Uddhava respectfully accepted his master’s message, mounted his chariot and set off for Nanda-gokula.

Essence. Uddhava respectfully accepts the message, mounts his chariot, and sets off for Nanda-gokula. Acceptance is followed by immediate departure — agreement plus locomotion equals service.

BG 46.8

The fortunate Uddhava reached Nanda Mahārāja’s pastures just as the sun was setting, and since the returning cows and other animals were raising dust with their hooves, his chariot passed unnoticed.

Essence. Uddhava reaches Nanda's pastures at sunset; returning cows raise dust that hides his chariot. The Lord's messenger can arrive unnoticed amid ordinary evening activity; entry need not be announced.

BG 46.9-13

Gokula resounded on all sides with the sounds of bulls in rut fighting with one another for fertile cows; with the mooing of cows, burdened by their udders, chasing after their calves; with the noise of milking and of the white calves jumping here and there; with the loud reverberation of flute-playing; and with the singing of the all-auspicious deeds of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma by the cowherd men and women, who made the village resplendent with their wonderfully ornamented attire. The cowherds’ homes in Gokula appeared most charming with their abundant paraphernalia for worship of the sacrificial fire, the sun, unexpected guests, the cows, the brāhmaṇas, the forefathers and the demigods. On all sides lay the flowering forest, echoing with flocks of birds and swarms of bees and beautified by its lakes crowded with swans, kāraṇḍava ducks and bowers of lotuses.

Essence. Gokula resounds with fighting bulls, mooing cows chasing calves, milking sounds, jumping calves, flute reverberations and song — even in grief at Kṛṣṇa's absence, Vraja's life-rhythm continues by His internal potency. Ordinary life carries on alongside spiritual longing.

BG 46.14

As soon as Uddhava arrived at Nanda Mahārāja’s home, Nanda came forward to meet him. The cowherd King embraced him in great happiness and worshiped him as nondifferent from Lord Vāsudeva.

Essence. Nanda meets Uddhava, embraces him in great happiness, and worships him as nondifferent from Vāsudeva — for Uddhava resembles Kṛṣṇa. Devotees see the Lord in those who carry His likeness; recognizing Him in others is a devotional skill.

BG 46.15

After Uddhava had eaten first-class food, been seated comfortably on a bed and been relieved of his fatigue by a foot massage and other means, Nanda inquired from him as follows.

Essence. Uddhava is fed first-class food, given a comfortable bed, and his fatigue is relieved by foot massage and other means. Hospitality to a messenger is multi-step; serving the Lord's representative is a full ritual.

BG 46.16

[Nanda Mahārāja said:] My dear most fortunate one, does the son of Śūra fare well, now that he is free and has rejoined his children and other relatives?

Essence. Nanda asks: does the son of Śūra fare well, now free and reunited with his children and relatives? Even joyful reunion in Mathurā is worth asking about; love wants news of the loved one's situation.

BG 46.17

Fortunately, because of his own sins, the sinful Kaṁsa has been killed, along with all his brothers. He always hated the saintly and righteous Yadus.

Essence. Fortunately, because of his own sins, sinful Kaṁsa has been killed with his brothers — he always hated the saintly Yadus. Devotees acknowledge that the wicked's downfall is their own karma, not random fortune.

BG 46.18

Does Kṛṣṇa remember us? Does He remember His mother and His friends and well-wishers? Does He remember the cowherds and their village of Vraja, of which He is the master? Does He remember the cows, Vṛndāvana forest and Govardhana Hill?

Essence. Does Kṛṣṇa remember us? Does He remember His mother, friends, cowherds, village, cows, forest, Govardhana Hill? Nanda lists the specific objects of memory — devotional love asks specific, almost child-like questions.

BG 46.19

Will Govinda return even once to see His family? If He ever does, we may then glance upon His beautiful face, with its beautiful eyes, nose and smile.

Essence. Will Govinda return even once to see His family? If He does, we may glance upon His beautiful face, eyes, nose, smile. Hope for even a brief visit sustains those left behind; one visit would suffice.

BG 46.20

We were saved from the forest fire, the wind and rain, the bull and serpent demons — from all such insurmountable, deadly dangers — by that very great soul, Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. We were saved from forest fire, wind and rain, bull and serpent demons — all insurmountable dangers — by that great soul, Kṛṣṇa. Memory of past rescues fuels current devotion; the list itself is prayer.

BG 46.21

As we remember the wonderful deeds Kṛṣṇa performed, His playful sidelong glances, His smiles and His words, O Uddhava, we forget all our material engagements.

Essence. As we remember Kṛṣṇa's wonderful deeds, playful glances, smiles and words, we forget all material engagements. Memory of Kṛṣṇa is itself a way to release material absorption — not by denying it but by being absorbed elsewhere.

BG 46.22

When we see the places where Mukunda enjoyed His sporting pastimes — the rivers, hills and forests He decorated with His feet — our minds become totally absorbed in Him.

Essence. When we see the rivers, hills, and forests Mukunda decorated with His feet, our minds become totally absorbed in Him. Specific places carry the residue of His presence; tīrtha is real.

BG 46.23

In my opinion, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma must be two exalted demigods who have come to this planet to fulfill some great mission of the demigods. Such was foretold by Garga Ṛṣi.

Essence. In my opinion, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma must be two exalted demigods come for some great mission, as Garga Ṛṣi foretold. Even the most intimate devotees can guess too low; love does not always equal exact theology.

BG 46.24

After all, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma killed Kaṁsa, who was as strong as ten thousand elephants, as well as the wrestlers Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika and the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa. They killed them all sportingly, as easily as a lion disposes of small animals.

Essence. After all, They killed Kaṁsa (strong as ten thousand elephants), Cāṇūra, Muṣṭika, and the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa — sportingly, like a lion handling small animals. Public deeds suggest a divinity Nanda still hedges about; evidence keeps accumulating.

BG 46.25

With the ease of a royal elephant breaking a stick, Kṛṣṇa broke a powerful, giant bow three tālas long. He also held a mountain aloft for seven days with just one hand.

Essence. With the ease of a royal elephant breaking a stick, Kṛṣṇa broke a giant bow three tālas long, and held a mountain for seven days with one hand. Iconic specific deeds enter the daily speech of devotees; remembered events shape memory.

BG 46.26

Here in Vṛndāvana, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma easily destroyed demons like Pralamba, Dhenuka, Arista, Tṛṇāvarta and Baka, who had themselves defeated both demigods and other demons.

Essence. Here in Vṛndāvana, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma easily destroyed Pralamba, Dhenuka, Ariṣṭa, Tṛṇāvarta, Baka — demons who defeated even demigods. The Lord's home turf saw destruction of demons no one else could touch.

BG 46.27

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus intensely remembering Kṛṣṇa again and again, Nanda Mahārāja, his mind completely attached to the Lord, felt extreme anxiety and fell silent, overcome by the strength of his love.

Essence. Remembering Kṛṣṇa intensely again and again, Nanda feels extreme anxiety and falls silent, overcome by love. Intense memory is not always blissful; sometimes it silences the mouth with the weight of love.

BG 46.28

As mother Yaśodā heard the descriptions of her son’s activities, she poured out her tears, and milk flowed from her breasts out of love.

Essence. As Yaśodā hears descriptions of her son's activities, she pours out tears, and milk flows from her breasts out of love. Bodily responses to remembered love are real; vatsalya rasa is physical.

BG 46.29

Uddhava then joyfully addressed Nanda Mahārāja, having clearly seen the supreme loving attraction he and Yaśodā felt for Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead .

Essence. Uddhava joyfully addresses Nanda, having seen their supreme loving attraction to Kṛṣṇa. The advanced devotee rejoices to witness others' love for the Lord; transcendental emotion is contagious.

BG 46.30

Śrī Uddhava said: O respectful Nanda, certainly you and mother Yaśodā are the most praiseworthy persons in the entire world, since you have developed such a loving attitude toward Lord Nārāyaṇa, the spiritual master of all living beings.

Essence. Uddhava says: certainly you and Yaśodā are the most praiseworthy in the world, having developed such loving attitude toward Nārāyaṇa, the spiritual master of all. Recognition by the wise lands directly: 'you are the most praiseworthy.'

BG 46.31

These two Lords, Mukunda and Balarāma, are each the seed and womb of the universe, the creator and His creative potency. They enter the hearts of living beings and control their conditioned awareness. They are the primeval Supreme.

Essence. Mukunda and Balarāma are each seed and womb of the universe, creator and His creative potency; entering hearts they control conditioned awareness. They are the primeval Supreme. Uddhava upgrades Nanda's theology gently — they are not 'two demigods.'

BG 46.32-33

Anyone, even a person in an impure state, who absorbs his mind in Him for just a moment at the time of death burns up all traces of sinful reactions and immediately attains the supreme transcendental destination in a pure, spiritual form as effulgent as the sun. You two have rendered exceptional loving service to Him, Lord Nārāyaṇa, the Supersoul of all and the cause of all existence, the great soul who, although the original cause of everything, has a humanlike form. What pious deeds could still be required of you?

Essence. Anyone, even impure, who absorbs mind in Him for a moment at death burns all sin and attains the supreme destination in a sun-bright spiritual form. You two have rendered direct service. Final-moment absorption is decisive — but not random; it rests on cultivated devotion.

BG 46.34

Infallible Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of the devotees, will soon return to Vraja to satisfy His parents.

Essence. Infallible Kṛṣṇa, Lord of devotees, will soon return to Vraja to satisfy His parents. Uddhava starts delivering Kṛṣṇa's actual message; reassurance after philosophical context.

BG 46.35

Having killed Kaṁsa, the enemy of all the Yadus, in the wrestling arena, Kṛṣṇa will now surely fulfill His promise to you by coming back.

Essence. Having killed Kaṁsa, the enemy of all Yadus, in the wrestling arena, Kṛṣṇa will fulfill His promise by coming back. The death of the obstacle precedes the fulfilling of the promise; the order is sequential.

BG 46.36

O most fortunate ones, do not lament. You will see Kṛṣṇa again very soon. He is present in the hearts of all living beings, just as fire lies dormant in wood.

Essence. O most fortunate ones, do not lament — you will see Kṛṣṇa soon; He is present in the hearts of all beings like fire dormant in wood. Outer return is preceded by inner accessibility; He is already in the heart.

BG 46.37

For Him no one is especially dear or despicable, superior or inferior, and yet He is not indifferent to anyone. He is free from all desire for respect and yet gives respect to all others.

Essence. For Him no one is dear or despicable, superior or inferior, yet He is not indifferent; free from desire for respect, He gives respect to all. The Lord's universal equanimity coexists with His full responsiveness.

BG 46.38

He has no mother, no father, no wife, children or other relatives. No one is related to Him, and yet no one is a stranger to Him. He has no material body and no birth.

Essence. He has no mother, father, wife, children, or relatives; no one is related to Him and no one is a stranger; He has no material body or birth. The Lord's family arrangement is not biological — yet He plays parents, wife, friends.

BG 46.39

He has no work to do in this world that would oblige Him to take birth in pure, impure or mixed species of life. Yet to enjoy His pastimes and deliver His saintly devotees, He manifests Himself.

Essence. He has no work obliging Him to take birth in any species; yet to enjoy pastimes and deliver devotees, He manifests Himself. The Lord's appearances are voluntary; the alternative is not necessary.

BG 46.40

Although beyond the three modes of material nature — goodness, passion and ignorance — the transcendental Lord accepts association with them as His play. Thus the unborn Supreme Lord utilizes the material modes to create, maintain and destroy.

Essence. Although beyond the three modes, the transcendental Lord accepts association with them as play, using them to create, maintain, and destroy. The Lord 'plays as if a resident of this world'; transcendence and engagement are simultaneous.

BG 46.41

Just as a person who is whirling around perceives the ground to be turning, one who is affected by false ego thinks himself the doer, when actually only his mind is acting.

Essence. Just as a whirling person sees ground turning, one affected by false ego thinks himself the doer when only the mind acts. Sense of agency is itself a kind of dizziness; correcting requires noticing the whirl.

BG 46.42

The Supreme Lord Hari is certainly not your son alone. Rather, being the Lord, He is the son, Soul, father and mother of everyone.

Essence. Hari is certainly not your son alone; being the Lord, He is the son, Soul, father, and mother of everyone. Specific intimate relationships do not exclude universal scope; both are true.

BG 46.43

Nothing can be said to exist independent of Lord Acyuta — nothing heard or seen, nothing in the past, present or future, nothing moving or unmoving, great or small. He indeed is everything, for He is the Supreme Soul.

Essence. Nothing exists independent of Acyuta — past, present, future, moving, unmoving, great, small; He is everything because He is Supreme Soul. Uddhava grounds Nanda's grief in metaphysics; no separation from Kṛṣṇa is metaphysically possible.

BG 46.44

While Kṛṣṇa’s messenger continued speaking with Nanda, the night ended, O King. The women of the cowherd village rose from bed and, lighting lamps, worshiped their household deities. Then they began churning the yogurt into butter.

Essence. While Uddhava speaks with Nanda, night ends; Vraja's women rise, light lamps, worship household deities, and begin churning yogurt to butter. Daily worship and household work begin at sunrise; devotional life is timed.

BG 46.45

As they pulled on the churning ropes with their bangled arms, the women of Vraja shone with the splendor of their jewels, which reflected the lamps’ light. Their hips, breasts and necklaces moved about, and their faces, anointed with reddish kuṅkuma, glowed radiantly with the luster of their earrings reflecting from their cheeks.

Essence. Pulling churning ropes, the women shine with jewels reflecting lamp light; hips, breasts, necklaces moving, kuṅkuma-anointed faces glowing. Even labor at dawn is beautiful; the body becomes radiant in service.

BG 46.46

As the ladies of Vraja loudly sang the glories of lotus-eyed Kṛṣṇa, their songs blended with the sound of their churning, ascended to the sky and dissipated all inauspiciousness in every direction.

Essence. The ladies loudly sing the glories of lotus-eyed Kṛṣṇa; songs blend with churning, ascend to the sky, dissipating inauspiciousness everywhere. Sung devotion at work spreads beyond the singer — collective good follows.

BG 46.47

When the godly sun had risen, the people of Vraja noticed the golden chariot in front of Nanda Mahārāja’s doorway. “Who does this belong to?” they asked.

Essence. When the sun rises, Vraja's people notice the golden chariot at Nanda's door and ask 'who does this belong to?' Visible signs of a guest's arrival become village news; ordinary life notices and inquires.

BG 46.48

“Perhaps Akrūra has returned — he who fulfilled Kaṁsa’s desire by taking lotus-eyed Kṛṣṇa to Mathurā.

Essence. Perhaps Akrūra has returned — he who fulfilled Kaṁsa's desire by taking Kṛṣṇa to Mathurā. The gopīs speak angrily; bitterness about past loss persists as the first interpretation of present arrivals.

BG 46.49

“Is he going to use our flesh to offer funeral oblations for his master, who was so satisfied with his service?” As the women were speaking in this way, Uddhava appeared, having finished his early-morning duties.

Essence. 'Is he going to use our flesh for his master's funeral?' The gopīs imagine the worst as Uddhava appears, finishing his morning duties. They will be pleasantly surprised; bitter speculation is interrupted by unexpected grace.
