Chapter 44 · Class prep

BG 44.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus addressed, Lord Kṛṣṇa made up His mind to accept the challenge. He paired off with Cāṇūra, and Lord Balarāma with Muṣṭika.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa accepts the challenge and pairs off with Cāṇūra, Balarāma with Muṣṭika. The Lord doesn't refuse the unfair match; sometimes accepting absurd terms is the right move when one knows the outcome.

BG 44.2

Seizing each other’s hands and locking legs with each other, the opponents struggled powerfully, eager for victory.

Essence. Seizing each other's hands and locking legs, the opponents struggle powerfully for victory. Engagement is total; spiritual combat does not happen at arm's length.

BG 44.3

They each struck fists against fists, knees against knees, head against head and chest against chest.

Essence. They strike fists against fists, knees against knees, head against head, chest against chest. Real fights involve every part of the body; symbolic fights skip the bruises.

BG 44.4

Each fighter contended with his opponent by dragging him about in circles, shoving and crushing him, throwing him down and running before and behind him.

Essence. Each fighter drags his opponent in circles, shoves, crushes, throws him down, runs before and behind. Combat takes many forms; flexibility of tactic matters.

BG 44.5

Forcefully lifting and carrying each other, pushing each other away and holding each other down, the fighters hurt even their own bodies in their great eagerness for victory.

Essence. Lifting and carrying, pushing and holding down, the fighters hurt their own bodies in their eagerness for victory; though Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma did not actually suffer harm, it appeared so to mundane vision. Real bhakti can look like self-harm to outsiders.

BG 44.6

My dear King, all the women present, considering the match an unfair fight between the strong and the weak, felt extreme anxiety due to compassion. They assembled in groups around the arena and spoke to one another as follows.

Essence. The women present consider the match unfair — strong against weak — and feel extreme anxiety from compassion, gathering and speaking together. Sometimes the wise response of bystanders is collective worry voiced aloud.

BG 44.7

[The women said:] Alas, what a greatly irreligious act the members of this royal assembly are committing! As the King watches this fight between the strong and the weak, they also want to see it.

Essence. Alas, the women say, what an irreligious act the royal assembly is committing — and watching! Even bystanders who watch wrongdoing share in its sin; spectatorship is participation.

BG 44.8

What comparison can there be between these two professional wrestlers, with limbs as strong as lightning bolts and bodies resembling mighty mountains, and these two young, immature boys with exceedingly tender limbs?

Essence. What comparison can there be between professional wrestlers with mountain-like bodies and these two young tender boys? When you see imbalance plainly, naming it is part of dharma.

BG 44.9

Religious principles have certainly been violated in this assembly. One should not remain for even a moment in a place where irreligion is flourishing.

Essence. Religious principles are violated here; one should not remain even a moment where irreligion flourishes. The instruction is severe — leave promptly, do not linger to register objection later.

BG 44.10

A wise person should not enter an assembly if he knows the participants there are committing acts of impropriety. And if, having entered such an assembly, he fails to speak the truth, speaks falsely or pleads ignorance, he will certainly incur sin.

Essence. The wise should not enter an assembly of impropriety, and if entered must speak truth — silence, falsehood, or false ignorance incurs sin. Speech matters; refusal to speak truth in a wrong setting becomes complicity.

BG 44.11

Just see the lotus face of Kṛṣṇa as He darts around His foe! That face, covered with drops of perspiration brought on by the strenuous fight, resembles a lotus covered with dew.

Essence. See Kṛṣṇa's lotus face, perspiration covering it like dew on a lotus, as He darts around His foe! Even amid combat, the Lord's beauty is what arrests the devotee's attention.

BG 44.12

Don’t you see the face of Lord Balarāma, with its eyes copper-red from His anger toward Muṣṭika and its beauty enhanced by His laughter and His absorption in the fight?

Essence. See Balarāma's eyes copper-red with anger toward Muṣṭika, beauty enhanced by His laughter and absorption in the fight. The elder Lord's anger doesn't disfigure His face; it intensifies it.

BG 44.13

How pious are the tracts of land in Vraja, for there the primeval Personality of Godhead, disguising Himself with human traits, wanders about, enacting His many pastimes! Adorned with wonderfully variegated forest garlands, He whose feet are worshiped by Lord Śiva and goddess Ramā vibrates His flute as He tends the cows in the company of Balarāma.

Essence. How pious is Vraja's land, where the primeval Lord — disguised with human traits — wanders enacting His pastimes adorned with forest garlands. The simple rural setting outclasses the royal arena because of who walks there.

BG 44.14

What austerities must the gopīs have performed! With their eyes they always drink the nectar of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s form, which is the essence of loveliness and is not to be equaled or surpassed. That form is the only abode of beauty, fame and opulence. It is self-perfect, ever fresh and extremely rare.

Essence. What austerities the gopīs must have performed to always drink the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's form — the only abode of beauty, fame and opulence, self-perfect and ever-fresh. Intimate access rests on hidden tapasya.

BG 44.15

The ladies of Vraja are the most fortunate of women because, with their minds fully attached to Kṛṣṇa and their throats always choked up with tears, they constantly sing about Him while milking the cows, winnowing grain, churning butter, gathering cow dung for fuel, riding on swings, taking care of their crying babies, sprinkling the ground with water, cleaning their houses, and so on. By their exalted Kṛṣṇa consciousness they automatically acquire all desirable things.

Essence. Vraja's ladies — minds attached to Kṛṣṇa, throats choked with tears — constantly sing of Him while milking cows, winnowing grain, churning butter, gathering cow dung, riding swings. Devotion infuses every domestic act.

BG 44.16

When the gopīs hear Kṛṣṇa playing His flute as He leaves Vraja in the morning with His cows or returns with them at sunset, the young girls quickly come out of their houses to see Him. They must have performed many pious activities to be able to see Him as He walks on the road, His smiling face mercifully glancing upon them.

Essence. When the gopīs hear Kṛṣṇa's flute as He leaves with cows or returns at sunset, they quickly come out to see Him — they must have performed many pious acts to see Him on the road. Even fleeting darśana is an enormous reward.

BG 44.17

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued:] As the women spoke thus, O hero of the Bhāratas, Lord Kṛṣṇa, the master of all mystic power, made up His mind to kill His opponent.

Essence. As the women speak, Kṛṣṇa, master of mystic power, makes up His mind to kill His opponent. The compassion of devotees moves the Lord to decisive action; their words enter the timing of His judgment.

BG 44.18

Out of affection for the two Lords, Their parents [Devakī and Vasudeva] became overwhelmed with sorrow when they heard the women’s fearful statements. They grieved, not knowing their sons’ strength.

Essence. Out of affection, Devakī and Vasudeva become sorrowful at the women's fearful statements; not knowing their sons' strength, they grieve. Even parents of the Lord can underestimate Him when they hear panic around them.

BG 44.19

Lord Balarāma and Muṣṭika, expertly displaying numerous wrestling techniques, battled each other in the same way that Lord Kṛṣṇa and His opponent did.

Essence. Balarāma and Muṣṭika fight using numerous wrestling techniques in parallel with Kṛṣṇa's battle. Both Lords engage simultaneously; the universe's correction often happens on multiple fronts at once.

BG 44.20

The harsh blows from the Supreme Lord’s limbs fell like crushing lightning bolts upon Cāṇūra, breaking every part of his body and causing him more and more pain and fatigue.

Essence. The harsh blows from the Lord's limbs fall like crushing lightning on Cāṇūra, breaking every part of his body. The Lord's casual strength is, to His enemies, devastating.

BG 44.21

Furious, Cāṇūra attacked Lord Vāsudeva with the speed of a hawk and struck His chest with both fists.

Essence. Furious, Cāṇūra attacks like a hawk with both fists to Kṛṣṇa's chest. Even strong demons make a last attempt — the spirited fighter goes down swinging.

BG 44.22-23

No more shaken by the demon’s mighty blows than an elephant struck with a flower garland, Lord Kṛṣṇa grabbed Cāṇūra by his arms, swung him around several times and hurled him onto the ground with great force. His clothes, hair and garland scattering, the wrestler fell down dead, like a huge festival column collapsing.

Essence. No more shaken than an elephant struck by a flower garland, Kṛṣṇa grabs Cāṇūra by the arms, swings him around several times, and hurls him to the ground dead, clothes and garland scattering. The Lord receives the demon's hardest blow as decoration.

BG 44.24-25

Similarly, Muṣṭika struck Lord Balabhadra with his fist and was slain. Receiving a violent blow from the mighty Lord’s palm, the demon trembled all over in great pain, vomited blood and then fell lifeless onto the ground, like a tree blown down by the wind.

Essence. Similarly Muṣṭika strikes Balabhadra and is slain by a single palm-blow; he trembles, vomits blood, and falls like a tree blown down by wind. Symmetric resolution: each demon meets his fall in his own ring.

BG 44.26

Confronted next by the wrestler Kūṭa, Lord Balarāma, the best of fighters, playfully and nonchalantly killed him with His left fist, O King.

Essence. Confronted next by Kūṭa, Balarāma playfully and nonchalantly kills him with His left fist. Once major opponents are down, secondary ones are dispatched without ceremony.

BG 44.27

Then Kṛṣṇa kicked in Śala’s head and tore Tośala in half, and both wrestlers fell down dead.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa kicks in Śala's head and tears Tośala in half; both fall dead. The clean-up after the main fight is rapid and unsentimental.

BG 44.28

Cāṇūra, Muṣṭika, Kūṭa, Śala and Tośala having been killed, the remaining wrestlers all fled for their lives.

Essence. With Cāṇūra, Muṣṭika, Kūṭa, Śala and Tośala killed, the remaining wrestlers flee. Once the alpha demons fall, the rest of the demonic crew loses its nerve.

BG 44.29

Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma then called Their young cowherd boyfriends to join Them, and in their company the Lords danced about and sported, Their ankle bells resounding as musical instruments played.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma call Their cowherd boyfriends to dance and sport, ankle bells resounding to music. Victory is celebrated with friends, not pondered alone; bhakti celebrates with the body.

BG 44.30

Everyone except Kaṁsa rejoiced at the wonderful feat Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma had performed. The exalted brāhmaṇas and great saints exclaimed, “Excellent! Excellent!”

Essence. Everyone except Kaṁsa rejoices; exalted brāhmaṇas and saints exclaim 'Excellent!' while Kaṁsa's priests grieve. Celebration of dharma exposes who is on which side; rejoicing is itself diagnostic.

BG 44.31

The Bhoja king, seeing that his best wrestlers had all been killed or had fled, stopped the musical performance originally meant for his pleasure and spoke the following words.

Essence. Seeing his wrestlers dead or fled, the Bhoja king stops the music meant for his pleasure. Tyrants cut the music when their performance turns against them; cancellation reveals the actual purpose.

BG 44.32

[Kaṁsa said:] Drive the two wicked sons of Vasudeva out of the city! Confiscate the cowherds’ property and arrest that fool Nanda!

Essence. Kaṁsa orders: drive out Vasudeva's wicked sons, confiscate cowherds' property, arrest Nanda! In panic, tyranny issues sweeping orders that target the innocent — the response to losing demons in is to attack civilians.

BG 44.33

Kill that most evil fool Vasudeva! And also kill my father, Ugrasena, along with his followers, who have all sided with our enemies!

Essence. Kill Vasudeva! Kill my father Ugrasena and his followers, who sided with our enemies! Tyranny in defeat turns even on its own family.

BG 44.34

As Kaṁsa thus raved so audaciously, the infallible Lord Kṛṣṇa, intensely angry, quickly and easily jumped up onto the high royal dais.

Essence. As Kaṁsa rants, the infallible Kṛṣṇa, intensely angry, quickly jumps onto the royal dais. The Lord doesn't argue; He climbs.

BG 44.35

Seeing Lord Kṛṣṇa approaching like death personified, the quick-witted Kaṁsa instantly rose from his seat and took up his sword and shield.

Essence. Seeing Kṛṣṇa approach like death personified, quick-witted Kaṁsa rises and takes up sword and shield. Even the wicked have reflexes; the question is whether reflex without dharma can save anyone.

BG 44.36

Sword in hand, Kaṁsa moved quickly from side to side like a hawk in the sky. But Lord Kṛṣṇa, whose fearsome strength is irresistible, powerfully seized the demon just as the son of Tārkṣya might capture a snake.

Essence. Sword in hand, Kaṁsa moves like a hawk; Kṛṣṇa seizes him like Garuḍa catching a snake. Fast as the enemy is, the Lord's grip is faster — every comparison to lower predators is exceeded by Him.

BG 44.37

Grabbing Kaṁsa by the hair and knocking off his crown, the lotus-naveled Lord threw him off the elevated dais onto the wrestling mat. Then the independent Lord, the support of the entire universe, threw Himself upon the King.

Essence. Grabbing Kaṁsa by the hair, knocking off his crown, Kṛṣṇa throws him from the dais onto the mat, then throws Himself on him and strikes him repeatedly. The crown comes off first; even royal symbols don't protect when the Lord decides.

BG 44.38

As a lion drags a dead elephant, the Lord then dragged Kaṁsa’s dead body along the ground in full view of everyone present. O King, all the people in the arena tumultuously cried out, “Oh! Oh!”

Essence. Like a lion dragging a dead elephant, Kṛṣṇa drags Kaṁsa's body so everyone sees it is over; the arena cries 'Oh! Oh!' The Lord makes endings public to leave no doubt; clarity about death prevents new myth-making.

BG 44.39

Kaṁsa had always been disturbed by the thought that the Supreme Lord was to kill him. Therefore when drinking, eating, moving about, sleeping or simply breathing, the King had always seen the Lord before him with the disc weapon in His hand. Thus Kaṁsa achieved the rare boon of attaining a form like the Lord’s.

Essence. Kaṁsa, always disturbed by the thought of being killed by the Lord, had constantly seen Him with disc in hand while drinking, eating, sleeping, breathing — and thus achieved the rare boon of dying liberated. Even hateful meditation reaches the Lord; the mind's object matters more than the mood.

BG 44.40

Kaṁsa’s eight younger brothers, led by Kaṅka and Nyagrodhaka, then attacked the Lords in a rage, seeking to avenge their brother’s death.

Essence. Kaṁsa's eight younger brothers — led by Kaṅka and Nyagrodhaka — attack the Lords in rage to avenge their brother. Networks of demons share each other's grudges; cutting one head exposes the family.

BG 44.41

As they ran swiftly toward the two Lords, ready to strike, the son of Rohiṇī slew them with His club just as a lion easily kills other animals.

Essence. As they rush to strike, Rohiṇī's son slays them with His club like a lion easily killing other animals. Aftermath enemies are dispatched with less ceremony than the main; momentum carries the work to completion.

BG 44.42

Kettledrums resounded in the sky as Brahmā, Śiva and other demigods, the Lord’s expansions, rained down flowers upon Him with pleasure. They chanted His praises, and their wives danced.

Essence. Kettledrums resound; Brahmā, Śiva, and demigods rain flowers and chant His praises while their wives dance. The cosmic audience celebrates what mortals only began to see; victories ripple through dimensions.

BG 44.43

My dear King, the wives of Kaṁsa and his brothers, aggrieved by the death of their well-wishing husbands, came forward with tearful eyes, beating their heads.

Essence. Kaṁsa's wives and his brothers' wives come weeping, beating their heads in grief. Even tyrants are loved by someone; the cost of demonic life lands on innocents who survive.

BG 44.44

Embracing their husbands, who lay on a hero’s final bed, the sorrowful women loudly lamented while shedding constant tears.

Essence. Embracing their husbands' bodies on a hero's bed, the sorrowful women loudly lament with constant tears. Lamentation is permitted and ritualized; bodies of even wicked men are embraced before farewell.

BG 44.45

[The women cried out:] Alas, O master, O dear one, O knower of religious principles! O kind and compassionate protector of the shelterless! By your being slain we have also been slain, together with your household and offspring.

Essence. Alas, master, dear, knower of religious principles, kind protector of the shelterless — by your being slain, we are slain with our household. The mourners idealize what they lost; grief edits memory.

BG 44.46

O great hero among men, bereft of you, its master, this city has lost its beauty, just as we have, and all festivity and good fortune within it have come to an end.

Essence. O hero, bereft of you the city has lost its beauty; festivity and good fortune have ended. The death of a tyrant feels like the death of festival to those whose joy depended on him.

BG 44.47

O dear one, you have been brought to this state because of the terrible violence you committed against innocent creatures. How can one who harms others attain happiness?

Essence. O dear, you have been brought to this state by the terrible violence you did to innocent creatures — how can one who harms others be happy? Grief eventually speaks plain wisdom even about the dead.

BG 44.48

Lord Kṛṣṇa causes the appearance and disappearance of all beings in this world, and He is their maintainer as well. One who disrespects Him can never prosper happily.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa causes appearance and disappearance of all beings and is also their maintainer; one who disrespects Him never prospers happily. Even mourners arrive at the theological point: disrespecting the Source forecloses happiness.

BG 44.49

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: After consoling the royal ladies, Lord Kṛṣṇa, sustainer of all the worlds, arranged for the prescribed funeral rites to be performed.

Essence. After consoling the royal ladies, Kṛṣṇa, sustainer of all worlds, arranges the prescribed funeral rites. The victor consoles the mourners and pays for the funeral; defeat of an enemy doesn't end His responsibility.

BG 44.50

Then Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma released Their mother and father from bondage and offered obeisances to them, touching their feet with Their heads.

Essence. Then Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma release Their parents from bondage and offer obeisances, touching their feet with Their heads. Even God, after killing tyrants, bows to His parents — filial reverence outlasts cosmic deeds.

BG 44.51

Devakī and Vasudeva, now knowing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to be the Lords of the universe, simply stood with joined palms. Being apprehensive, they did not embrace their sons.

Essence. Now knowing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma as Lords of the universe, Devakī and Vasudeva stand with joined palms, apprehensive, not embracing their sons. Knowledge of someone's divinity can momentarily inhibit the most natural human embrace.
