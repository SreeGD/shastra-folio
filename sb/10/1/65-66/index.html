Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.65-66

Important Words
  • matvā surān — thinking of [them] as demigods
    Matvā (gerund of man, 'having thought') + surān (acc. pl. of sura, 'demigods'). Kaṁsa thinks correctly — they are demigods. The Bhāgavata's diagnosis: asuras can think correctly and still act demoniacally. Cognition is not character.
  • garbha-sambhūtam viṣṇum — the children born from the womb, [thinking of them] as Viṣṇu
    Garbha (womb) + sambhūta (originating) + viṣṇu (acc.). Kaṁsa fears Viṣṇu specifically — the form whose hand killed his previous birth as Kālanemi. The Bhāgavata names what the asura cannot escape: the past birth's killer is the next birth's threat.
  • sva-vadham prati — fearing his own death from Viṣṇu
    Sva (own) + vadha (death) + prati (toward). 'With reference to his own death.' Asuras are oriented toward their own vadha — they live in continuous reverse-engineering of their end. Devotees, by contrast, live toward Bhagavān; the end takes care of itself.
  • nigaḍaiḥ — by iron shackles
    Plural of nigaḍa, 'iron chain.' Vasudeva and Devakī are physically chained — preparing the stage for Kṛṣṇa's appearance in chains in chapter 3 (10.3.46–47 — the chains fell at His arrival). The Bhāgavata's narrative chains are also doctrinal: the body in bondage will be visited by Bhagavān.
  • jātam jātam ahan — each one born, he killed
    Jāta (born), repeated for emphasis. Ahan = aorist of han, 'killed.' The verse's syntactic violence: jātam-jātam-ahan, born-born-killed. Six syllables that document six killings. Bhāgavata's narrative grammar carries the trauma.
After the departure of the great saint Nārada, Kaṁsa thought that all the members of the Yadu dynasty were demigods and that any of the children born from the womb of Devakī might be Viṣṇu. Fearing his death, Kaṁsa arrested Vasudeva and Devakī and chained them with iron shackles. Suspecting each of the children to be Viṣṇu, Kaṁsa killed them one after another because of the prophecy that Viṣṇu would kill him.
Purport

Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, in his notes on this verse, has mentioned how Nārada Muni gave Kaṁsa this information. This incident is described in the Hari-vaṁśa. Nārada Muni went to see Kaṁsa by providence, and Kaṁsa received him very well. Nārada, therefore, informed him that any one of the sons of Devakī might be Viṣṇu. Because Viṣṇu was to kill him, Kaṁsa should not spare any of Devakī’s children, Nārada Muni advised. Nārada’s intention was that Kaṁsa, by killing the children, would increase his sinful activities so that Kṛṣṇa would soon appear to kill him. Upon receiving the instructions of Nārada Muni, Kaṁsa killed all the children of Devakī one after another. The word ajana-śaṅkayā indicates that Lord Viṣṇu never takes birth ( ajana ) and that He therefore appeared as Kṛṣṇa, taking birth just like a human being ( mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam ). Kaṁsa attempted to kill all the babies born of Devakī and Vasudeva, although he knew that if Viṣṇu were born, He would not be killed. Actually it came to pass that when Viṣṇu appeared as Kṛṣṇa, Kaṁsa could not kill Him; rather, as foretold, it was He who killed Kaṁsa. One should know in truth how Kṛṣṇa, who takes His birth transcendentally, acts to kill the demons but is never killed. When one perfectly understands Kṛṣṇa in this way, through the medium of śāstra, one becomes immortal. As the Lord says in Bhagavad-gītā (4.9) : janma karma ca me divyam evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti so ’rjuna “One who knows the transcendental nature of My appearance and activities does not, upon leaving the body, take his birth again in this material world, but attains My eternal abode, O Arjuna.”

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
After the departure of the great saint Nārada, Kaṁsa thought that all the members of the Yadu dynasty were demigods and that any of the children born from the womb of Devakī might be Viṣṇu. Fearing his death, Kaṁsa arrested Vasudeva and Devakī and chained them with iron shackles. Suspecting each of the children to be Viṣṇu, Kaṁsa killed them one after another because of the prophecy that Viṣṇu would kill him.
Commentary

Kamsa heard that Visnu who had been his enemy in a previous life, would now be born in the womb of Devaki and would kill him. Hearing from the mouth of Narada, Kamsa believed this. Out of fear of Visnu (ajana, one who has no birth) he killed the children as soon as they were born.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Nārada went particularly far away (vinirgame) because of shame at having directly put Vasudeva in prison, and because Kaṁsa feared he may return to prevent his actions. Kaṁsa understood that the Yadus were the Lord’s associates and that Viṣṇu born with all powers (saṁbhūtam) from Devakī to kill him. Past tense is used for the future event because the time was close or because the event was certain. Or Kaṁsa thought that Viṣṇu who possesses all powers (sambhūtam) was in Devakī’s womb. Another version has sambhūtim. Kaṁsa understood from Nārada that the eighth child of Devakī would kill him and that the relatives were making preparations to kill him. ttatraiṣa devakī yā te mathurāyāṁ pitṛṣvasā yo ‘syā garbho ‘ṣṭamaḥ kaṁsa sa te mṛtyur bhaviṣyati devānāṁ caiva sarvasvaṁ tridivasya gatiś ca saḥ paraṁ rahasyaṁ vedānāṁ sa te mṛtyur bhaviṣyati parato ‘pi paras teṣam svayaṁbhūś ca divaukasām tatas tv etan mahadbhūtaṁ divyam te kathayāmy aham ślāghaśca sa hi te mṛtyur bhūta-pūrvaś ca taṁ smara O Kaṁsa! You will be killed by the eighth child of Devakī, your sister, in Mathurā. He who is the goal of the devatās and the secret of the Vedas will kill you. He is superior to the devatās and without birth. I am telling you this astonishing, divine news. He killed you in the past. Remember that. Piṭrṣvasā means sister related through the father. Some say that Āhuka, Devaka’s father raised Devakī, his granddaughter as his own child. Nigṛhya means imprisoned in Kaṁsa’s house. Ajana means a person who is without birth. Kaṁsa thought he would be born like a normal child. This word thus indicates Kaṁsa’s foolishness. Or it indicates fear of something which was perhaps non-existent.

Personal Guidance
Kaṁsa's fearful slaughter only hastened Kṛṣṇa's appearance — the demon's defenses cannot stop the Lord, who takes birth (ajana) only to protect and to slay.
Practical Applications
  • Notice where fear of losing your position drives you to harm others, and refuse one such pre-emptive 'killing' today — a cutting word, a guarded silence, a withheld opportunity.
  • Read or recite BG 4.9 (janma karma ca me divyam) once today and consciously meditate that Kṛṣṇa's birth and acts are transcendental, not ordinary.
  • Identify one 'iron shackle' you have placed on a devotee or family member out of suspicion, and deliberately loosen it through a concrete act of trust.
Reflection Questions
  • Kaṁsa believed that by killing each newborn he could outwit a prophecy from the Lord — where in my own life am I expending enormous effort to control an outcome that is not actually mine to determine?
  • The verse hinges on ajana — Viṣṇu, the unborn, who appears nonetheless as a child of Devakī; what assumption am I still carrying that Kṛṣṇa must conform to my categories of how the divine should or should not act?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Identify your own 'Kaṁsa-list' — the people or possibilities you treat as threats to the future you have planned (the rival classmate, the friend who is doing better than you on Instagram, the parent's expectation, the GPA that 'must' stay above a number). Write the list. Notice that fear of losing your imagined future is the engine.
  • This week, when peer pressure tells you to 'shackle' someone with gossip, exclusion, or a cutting comment to protect your own social position, name it silently as the Kaṁsa-impulse and refuse it once. Just once. Tell a sādhu-saṅga friend afterwards.
  • Pick one decision you are currently making out of fear of an outcome (choosing a major, ghosting someone, hiding a struggle from your parents) and ask: what would I do if I trusted that Kṛṣṇa, not my prediction, governs the result? Then do that thing today — not after graduation, today.
householder
  • Audit one 'iron shackle' you have placed in your home this month — the controlling rule with your teenager, the financial leash on your spouse, the silent treatment after a disagreement. Remove it deliberately, and notice what fear surfaces in you when you do. That fear is your work.
  • In a major family decision this week (school for a child, a job change, money for an aging parent), explicitly distinguish material compassion (their comfort, their career, their bodily ease) from real compassion (their soul's progress toward Kṛṣṇa). Choose the action that serves the second even when it costs the first. Do this once, with full awareness, before you generalize it.
  • Each evening for one week, sit with your spouse for five minutes and offer the day's actions and their results to Kṛṣṇa together. Not as ritual — as the practical refusal of the Kaṁsa-illusion that you are the controller of your family's fate.
senior
  • Name the prophecies you have been living under — 'I will be forgotten,' 'my children will not call,' 'my body will fail and I will be a burden.' These are your private versions of Kaṁsa's prophecy. Write them down and read them aloud once. The naming itself loosens their grip.
  • Choose one attachment you have been quietly chaining — a property, a role in the family, a grudge you have nursed for decades, a skill you refuse to hand on because then 'they won't need me.' Begin the act of release this week: transfer one document, train one successor, speak one forgiveness. Tyāga is practiced in concrete handovers, not in feelings.
  • Mentor one younger person not toward worldly success but toward Kṛṣṇa — share one verse, one purport, one story from your own sādhana. Do this without expecting they will become 'devotional' on your timeline. The offering is the point; the fruit belongs to Him.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading Kaṁsa as merely 'the bad guy' and missing the mirror — the same fearful, calculating ahaṅkāra that chains Devakī and Vasudeva operates in us whenever we try to eliminate every threat to our position rather than surrender to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Treating Nārada's role as cruel or duplicitous; the student concludes 'so deception is fine if the end is good' instead of recognizing that only a pure devotee, acting under Kṛṣṇa's direct arrangement, can play such a role — it is not a template for our conduct.
  • Taking ajana-śaṅkayā as license for impersonalism — 'see, Viṣṇu has no real birth, so His form is symbolic' — when the verse and purport teach the opposite: He genuinely appears in a transcendental form (mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam), and knowing this in tattva is what liberates.
Discussion Prompts
  • Nārada deliberately fed Kaṁsa information that he knew would lead Kaṁsa to commit more sin and hasten Kṛṣṇa's appearance. Share with your partner: was this compassion or cruelty? Where in your own life have you confused 'being nice' (material compassion for the body) with actually serving someone's soul? Push each other — don't settle for an easy reconciliation.
  • Kaṁsa heard the prophecy and responded with iron shackles and infanticide. We rarely chain anyone, but we do react to perceived threats with control — micromanaging a spouse, surveilling a child's phone, hoarding money against an imagined future. Name one specific 'iron shackle' you have placed on someone you love because of fear of your own loss. Did your partner help you see one you hadn't named?
  • The verse insists that Viṣṇu is aja — never born — yet appears to take birth. Some in your group will find this a beautiful mystery; others will call it a contradiction or a face-saving theological move. Argue both sides honestly for ten minutes. What is actually at stake for a sādhaka if Kṛṣṇa's birth is 'just like ours' versus divyam (transcendental)?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a parent whose adult child has just announced they are leaving a stable career to pursue full-time bhakti service in a temple far from home. Student A has spent twenty years building security for this child and feels the prophecy of their own old-age abandonment coming true. Student B is a senior devotee Student A has come to for counsel. Student A must voice the Kaṁsa-impulse honestly — the urge to 'shackle,' to threaten withdrawal of inheritance, to predict ruin, to manufacture obstacles 'for the child's own good.' Student B must distinguish, in real time, between material compassion (protecting the child's body and career) and real compassion (serving the child's soul), without becoming preachy. Run for eight minutes, then swap roles. Debrief: where did the Kaṁsa-logic sound most reasonable? Where did it expose itself as fear of personal loss dressed up as love?

Objections & Responses
  • Nārada Muni knowingly provoked Kaṁsa into killing six infants. By any modern ethical standard — and frankly by the Gītā's own emphasis on ahiṁsā — that makes Nārada complicit in mass murder. How can a Gauḍīya teacher defend this without sounding like an apologist for cruelty?
    Stay with the discomfort — don't rush past it. The Gauḍīya answer is not 'the ends justify the means.' It is that Nārada sees what we do not: those infants are not annihilated, they are liberated souls returning to Kṛṣṇa's eternal abode (the Bhāgavatam later identifies them as Marīci's sons completing a curse). Kaṁsa's body-vision sees murder; Nārada's tattva-vision sees the soul's passage, exactly the dhīra-dṛṣṭi the Gītā cultivates. The objection assumes the body is the person — the very assumption the verse is dismantling. A Gauḍīya teacher does not deny the horror of Kaṁsa's act; he points out that calling Nārada complicit requires us to first agree with Kaṁsa about what a person is. We don't.
  • This whole framing — 'Viṣṇu is never born but appears to be born' — sounds like theological cope. Either Kṛṣṇa was born like a human or He wasn't. Saying both feels like a way to keep the comforting story while protecting the doctrine.
    It feels that way only if we assume birth means one thing. The verse teaches the opposite: divyam janma — a category of birth that is not the soul's forced entry into a body driven by past karma, but a free, conscious descent of the Lord in His own eternal form. Mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam — He takes a humanlike form, He does not become bound by it. The test is not philosophical neatness; it is the verse Prabhupāda quotes: janma karma ca me divyam evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ — one who knows this in tattva is not born again. That is a falsifiable claim about your own life, not a doctrinal hedge. A Gauḍīya teacher would ask: are you willing to let the verse work on you long enough to test it, or are you settling the question before you have lived with it?
Analogy

After Nārada's departure, Kaṁsa concludes that all the Yadus are demigods and any child of Devakī may be Viṣṇu. He arrests Vasudeva and Devakī in iron shackles and, suspecting each newborn to be Viṣṇu, kills them one by one. The verse describes the asura's reasoning under existential threat — accurate at every step, and lethal at every step. The Bhāgavata teaches: correct information without surrender produces only more efficient cruelty.
