Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.24

वासुदेवकलानन्त: सहस्रवदन: स्वराट् । अग्रतो भविता देवो हरे: प्रियचिकीर्षया ॥ २४ ॥

Important Words
  • vāsudeva-kalā anantaḥ — the plenary expansion of Lord Kṛṣṇa known as Anantadeva or Saṅkarṣaṇa Ananta
    Vāsudeva-kalā = a portion of Vāsudeva; Ananta = endless. The same Person who is the infinite serpent-bed of Mahā-Viṣṇu now appears as a child. Gauḍīya theology: the infinite voluntarily takes finite form to serve mādhurya.
  • sahasra-vadanaḥ — having thousands of hoods
    Sahasra (thousand) + vadana (mouth/face). Ananta-Śeṣa's cosmic form has thousands of hoods on which the universes rest. The same Person — whose hoods bear the worlds — will be wrestled by Kṛṣṇa's playmates in Vraja.
  • svarāṭ — fully independent
    Sva (own) + rāj (ruler) — 'self-ruling.' Used in the Bhāgavata's first verse (janmādy asya yataḥ ... svarāṭ) of the Absolute. Saṅkarṣaṇa is svarāṭ — independent in Himself — yet voluntarily subordinates Himself in līlā as Balarāma. Independence used for service.
  • priya-cikīrṣayā — with the desire to act for the pleasure
    Priya (beloved, pleasure) + cikīrṣā (desire to do; from kṛ). The whole motive of Balarāma's appearance is named: to do what pleases Kṛṣṇa. The eternal servant-mood (dāsya) coloring even sakhya-rasa with Kṛṣṇa.
The foremost manifestation of Kṛṣṇa is Saṅkarṣaṇa, who is known as Ananta. He is the origin of all incarnations within this material world. Previous to the appearance of Lord Kṛṣṇa, this original Saṅkarṣaṇa will appear as Baladeva, just to please the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa in His transcendental pastimes.
Purport

Śrī Baladeva is the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself. He is equal in supremacy to the Supreme Godhead, yet wherever Kṛṣṇa appears, Śrī Baladeva appears as His brother, sometimes elder and sometimes younger. When Kṛṣṇa appears, all His plenary expansions and other incarnations appear with Him. This is elaborately explained in Caitanya-caritāmṛta. This time, Baladeva would appear before Kṛṣṇa as Kṛṣṇa’s elder brother.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The foremost manifestation of Kṛṣṇa is Saṅkarṣaṇa, who is known as Ananta. He is the origin of all incarnations within this material world. Previous to the appearance of Lord Kṛṣṇa, this original Saṅkarṣaṇa will appear as Baladeva, just to please the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa in His transcendental pastimes.
Commentary

Svarat usually means independent, he who exists (raj or rat) by himself (sva), but here it means he who exists with his own brother Krsna (sva). Baladeva (deva) who is existing with his own brother (svarat) will appear first (agratah bhavita). Ananta is an amsa or kala of Baladeva. This is supported with verses like: evamsena sahasra vadano’nantah (whose portion is the thousand mouthed ananta) and yasaikamsena vidhrta jagati jagatah pateh ( O mighty armed balarama, Who by one portion supports the universe.) Here Baladeva is described as Ananta who is a portion of Vasudeva.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

His elder brother will appear first. His brother’s qualifications are described. Vāsudeva refers to Kṛṣṇa as part of the caturvyūha in Dvārakā. His kalā or expansion there is Saṅkarśaṇa. He is unlimited (anantaḥ) and has a thousand heads as the expansion Śeṣa in order to praise the qualities of Kṛṣṇa. āma rāma mahā-bāho na jāne tava vikramam yasyaikāṁśena vidhṛtā jagatī jagataḥ pate Rāma, Rāma, O mighty-armed one! I know nothing of your prowess. With a single portion of yourself you hold up the earth, O Lord of the universe. SB 10.65.28 The “single portion of yourself” refers to Śeṣa. Svarāṭ means he who exists (rājate) with Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa, the highest svarūpa (sva). This means that he does not exist without Kṛṣṇa. He is called deva because he is to be worshipped by all, since he is Svayam Bhagavān’s elder brother. “But he should be the younger brother of Svayam Bhagavān like Lakṣmaṇa, the younger brother of Rāma.” He takes this role of an older brother to please Kṛṣṇa. Being able to destroy the Lord’s pain by being obedient and at the same time an equal, he has a special friendly relationship with Kṛṣṇa as his devotee. In the Bṛhad-sahasra-nāma-stotra, among the names of Balarāma there is pūrva-bhakti-khedācyutāgrajaḥ: he became the elder brother of the Lord because of Lord’s pain in previous pastimes of Rāma. In those previous pastimes he could not relieve Rāma of distress because he was younger. This means that in some kalpas he does not appear in a dependant position. Since Lakṣmaṇa is dependent, he cannot freely remove Rāma’s distress. “Being elder, I desire to give him happiness independently.” Thus Lakṣmaṇa laments in devotion, because he is not able to relieve the Lord’ pain. He desired to give happiness to Hari, out of affection since Hari is most attractive. Thus he appears as the elder brother.

Personal Guidance
Baladeva, Kṛṣṇa's own first expansion as Saṅkarṣaṇa-Ananta, descends as elder brother solely for hareḥ priya-cikīrṣayā — to please Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Begin one task today with the explicit motive 'to please Kṛṣṇa,' and notice how it reorders your priorities mid-stream.
  • Speak to your elder brother, sister, or senior with the awareness that their service is itself a form of Baladeva-tattva — assist them rather than compete.
  • Refuse the impersonalist reflex that flattens Kṛṣṇa into 'the divine'; address Him by name in your japa today and feel the personal relation.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I trying to stand alone in service to Kṛṣṇa, when this verse shows that even the Lord's own expansion appears first to support His pastimes — whose accompanying service have I been refusing to recognize?
  • Baladeva descends only hareḥ priya-cikīrṣayā — solely to please Kṛṣṇa. When I examine my own sādhana today, what subtle second motive (recognition, peace of mind, advancement) is competing with that single purpose?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Identify one friend or sibling whose success you secretly compete with on social media. For one week, deliberately act in priya-cikīrṣayā toward them — promote their work, show up for their thing, without posting about your own role in it. Notice the ahaṅkāra that resists.
  • In your next group project or study circle, take the Baladeva position on purpose: be the elder-brother figure who clears the path for someone else to shine, even if you could have led. Journal what shifted in your motivation by the end of the week.
  • When a peer 'beats' you — better grade, better internship, better relationship — practice the dhīra response: 'Kṛṣṇa is acting through that result; my role is right action and to please Him, not to be the star.' Catch yourself the moment envy starts and re-aim there.
householder
  • In one family decision this week (school, money, where to spend a weekend), consciously yield your preference to your spouse's or child's flourishing — not as suppression, but as priya-cikīrṣayā. At day's end, offer the yielding to Kṛṣṇa rather than banking it as a grievance.
  • Examine your career ambition: where are you the protagonist demanding to be Kṛṣṇa, and where could you be the Baladeva — the one who quietly makes another's success possible (a colleague, a younger sibling, your own children)? Pick one such relationship and recommit to it for the next quarter.
  • Read SB 10.1.24 with your spouse or older child this week. Discuss together: what would it mean for our household if we treated one another's flourishing as the form of our own service to Kṛṣṇa, rather than as a transaction?
senior
  • You are nearer to the role Baladeva models — the elder who appears 'before' the younger to prepare the ground. Identify one younger person (child, grandchild, junior at the temple) whose path you can clear without asking for credit, and commit to a concrete act of clearing this month.
  • Audit the attachments you are still holding to being the central figure — in family decisions, in inheritance, in who is consulted first. Loosen one this week as an offering: step back deliberately so a younger person can step forward, in the mood of priya-cikīrṣayā.
  • Use this verse as a meditation on your own approaching transition. Just as Baladeva appears 'before' Kṛṣṇa to please Him, you are now the senior making space for what comes next. Each evening, offer the day — its diminishments included — to Kṛṣṇa, asking only that your remaining time be spent pleasing Him through serving His devotees.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Baladeva as a lesser or secondary deity rather than as the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself appearing in the mood of a servitor — the verse establishes equality in supremacy, with difference only in līlā-rasa.
  • Reading 'Ananta,' 'Saṅkarṣaṇa,' and 'Baladeva' as separate or competing identities and getting lost in cataloging expansions, instead of seeing the one personal Lord serving Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — drifting into impersonal or merely scholastic study (failed svādhyāya).
  • Romanticizing the 'elder brother who serves the younger' as a sentimental image while ignoring its concrete demand: that real anahaṅkāra means voluntarily taking the supporting role in one's own family, saṅga, or work today, not merely admiring it in the līlā.
Discussion Prompts
  • Baladeva appears as Kṛṣṇa's elder brother purely to please Him — share with your partner one relationship in your life where you genuinely subordinate your preference to another's pleasure, and one where you only pretend to. What is the actual difference you feel inside?
  • The verse says Saṅkarṣaṇa is 'equal in supremacy' to Kṛṣṇa yet takes the role of servitor-brother. Does this strike you as inspiring or troubling? Argue the case that voluntary subordination is higher than mutual equality, and let someone in the group push back honestly.
  • Where in modern life — career, parenting, sādhana — do we confuse 'being the protagonist of my own story' with svadhyāya, and where does Baladeva's example of priya-cikīrṣayā (acting only to please another) cut against that instinct? Bring a concrete example, not a theory.
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is the older sibling who has just been told by their parents that the family is moving cities so the younger sibling (Student B) can attend a specialized program — A's own college plans, friend group, and a relationship will all be disrupted. Student B did not ask for this and feels guilty. Run the conversation twice: first, A speaks from ahaṅkāra ('I'm sacrificing everything for you') — notice how it lands. Second, A speaks in the mood of priya-cikīrṣayā — wanting the younger one's flourishing, with no ledger. The group then debriefs: what was different in the body, the tone, the silence afterward? Where did each of you feel the pull back to ahaṅkāra?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just mythology dressed up as theology — one God 'expanding' into a brother to play with Himself sounds like a fairy tale, not something a serious adult should organize their life around.
    The objection assumes the standard of 'serious' is impersonal abstraction — a featureless Absolute, or no God at all. The Gauḍīya line answers that the highest reality is not less personal than us but infinitely more so. A God who can expand into a brother, a friend, a beloved — without diminishment — is philosophically richer than a static One, because it accounts for relationship as ultimate, not derivative. Baladeva is not Kṛṣṇa playing with a mirror; He is the eternal Other through whom Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is tasted. Try the experiment from the inside: live one week as if your relationships are the stage on which the Supreme is rehearsing His own līlā, and see whether 'fairy tale' or 'serious adult' feels truer.
  • If Baladeva is Himself the Supreme Personality of Godhead, why would He 'serve' Kṛṣṇa? Doesn't this just create a hierarchy inside God — and isn't the Advaitin view (one undifferentiated Absolute) cleaner?
    Cleaner, yes — and emptier. The Advaitin solution dissolves the lover and the beloved into a single substance, and with them dissolves prema, bhakti, and every relationship that gives life its taste. The Gauḍīya answer is that Baladeva's 'service' is not subordination imposed from outside; it is the internal structure of love itself, where the lover wishes only the beloved's pleasure (priya-cikīrṣayā) and the beloved is magnified, not diminished, by being loved. Within Godhead this is eternal, voluntary, and mutually full. Hierarchy here is not a defect to be smoothed away — it is the very shape of rasa. Remove it and you do not get a higher truth; you get a sterile one.
Analogy

Saṅkarṣaṇa Ananta will appear as Balarāma before Kṛṣṇa — like the elder brother walking into the wedding hall first to ensure the seating, before the groom arrives. He goes ahead not because he is greater but because his service is to make the Lord's appearance comfortable. The eternal brother-servant has already entered before the curtain rises.
