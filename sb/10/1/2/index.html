Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.2

यदोश्च धर्मशीलस्य नितरां मुनिसत्तम । तत्रांशेनावतीर्णस्य विष्णोर्वीर्याणि शंस न: ॥ २ ॥

Important Words
  • dharma-śīlasya — strictly attached to religious principles
    Compound: dharma + śīla (character, habit). The Yadus' qualification was not occasional piety but a settled habit of dharma. Kṛṣṇa enters a family already trained in dharma — sthāyī-bhāva of dharma prepares the ground for prema.
  • muni-sattama — O best of all munis
    Sat + tama = superlative, 'best.' Parīkṣit addresses Śukadeva by his qualification, not merely his name. Hearing kṛṣṇa-kathā is potent only when the speaker is qualified; the disciple's first task is to recognize and address that qualification.
  • aṁśena — with His plenary expansion Baladeva
    Instrumental of aṁśa, 'portion, plenary part.' Baladeva is non-different from Kṛṣṇa in tattva yet distinct in līlā as the original brother and servitor. The Gauḍīya line reads this as the simultaneous oneness-and-difference at the level of Bhagavān Himself — acintya-bhedābheda visible even in Vraja.
  • viṣṇoḥ — of Lord Viṣṇu
    Genitive. Read in Gauḍīya siddhānta as referring here to Kṛṣṇa Himself — the source — not to a separate Viṣṇu-tattva. The verse confirms Kṛṣṇa as Viṣṇu's source rather than His servant or expansion (cf. CC Ādi 2).
  • vīryāṇi — the glorious activities
    Plural of vīrya, 'potency, prowess.' Not principles, not symbols — concrete, narratable feats. Parīkṣit is specifically asking for līlā, the personal pastimes, because vīryāṇi reveal the Person in a way doctrine alone cannot.
  • śaṁsa — kindly describe
    Imperative of śaṁs, 'to praise, recite.' The same root that gives śāstra. Parīkṣit's request is not 'inform me' but 'praise Him aloud to me' — śravaṇam is intended to be sung, not surveyed.
O best of munis, you have also described the descendants of Yadu, who were very pious and strictly adherent to religious principles. Now, if you will, kindly describe the wonderful, glorious activities of Lord Viṣṇu, or Kṛṣṇa, who appeared in that Yadu dynasty with Baladeva, His plenary expansion.
Purport

The Brahma-saṁhitā (5.1) explains that Kṛṣṇa is the origin of the viṣṇu-tattva. īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ sac-cid-ānanda-vigrahaḥ anādir ādir govindaḥ sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam “Kṛṣṇa, who is known as Govinda, is the supreme controller. He has an eternal, blissful, spiritual body. He is the origin of all. He has no other origin, for He is the prime cause of all causes.” yasyaika-niśvasita-kālam athāvalambya jīvanti loma-vilajā jagad-aṇḍa-nāthāḥ viṣṇur mahān sa iha yasya kalā-viśeṣo govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi “The Brahmās, the heads of the innumerable universes, live only for the duration of one breath of Mahā-Viṣṇu. I worship Govinda, the original Lord, of whom Mahā-Viṣṇu is but a portion of a plenary portion.” ( Brahma-saṁhitā 5.48 ) Govinda, Kṛṣṇa, is the original Personality of Godhead. Kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam . Even Lord Mahā-Viṣṇu, who by His breathing creates many millions upon millions of universes, is Lord Kṛṣṇa’s kalā-viśeṣa, or plenary portion of a plenary portion. Mahā-Viṣṇu is a plenary expansion of Saṅkarṣaṇa, who is a plenary expansion of Nārāyaṇa. Nārāyaṇa is a plenary expansion of the catur-vyūha, and the catur-vyūha are plenary expansions of Baladeva, the first manifestation of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore when Kṛṣṇa appeared with Baladeva, all the viṣṇu-tattvas appeared with Him. Mahārāja Parīkṣit requested Śukadeva Gosvāmī to describe Kṛṣṇa and His glorious activities. Another meaning may be derived from this verse as follows, Although Śukadeva Gosvāmī was the greatest muni, he could describe Kṛṣṇa only partially ( aṁśena ), for no one can describe Kṛṣṇa fully. It is said that Anantadeva has thousands of heads, but although He tries to describe Kṛṣṇa with thousands of tongues, His descriptions are still incomplete.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O best of munis, you have also described the descendants of Yadu, who were very pious and strictly adherent to religious principles. Now, if you will, kindly describe the wonderful, glorious activities of Lord Viṣṇu, or Kṛṣṇa, who appeared in that Yadu dynasty with Baladeva, His plenary expansion.
Commentary

You have also previously described the pious nature of Yadu. Though Yadu disobeyed his father’s order, because of his pure devotion, his exalted character is praised in the Ninth and Eleventh cantos. Pariksit addresses Sukadeva as the king of the munis—muni sattama, because as a muni he is completely knowledgeable, and as a devotee (sat), he is the chief since he established the supreme position of bhakti. "Please describe the exploits (viryani) of he who has appeared (avatirnasya), of that person whose amsa is Visnu in Vaikuntha." Another meaning is "Please describe the exploits partially (amsena), since it is impossible to describe the Lord’s pastimes completely."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

You have also described the details of Yadu’s dynasty and the astonishing activities of its kings to a great degree, since Yadu was filled with bhakti (dharma-śīlasya). Dharma means bhakti because it is said dharmo mad-bhakti-kṛt proktaḥ: dharma means bhakti to me. (SB 11.19.27) Yadu’s steady bhakti is described in the Eleventh Canto. Kuntī has also said yadoḥ priyasyānvavāye: you have taken birth to please King Yadu, one of your dearest devotees. (SB 1.8.32) Parīkṣit particularly mentions Yadu, because his dynasty became the best by the appearance of Kṛṣṇa, and he has in mind many questions concerning Kṛṣṇa. He addresses Śukadeva as the best of sages. He is a devotee of the Lord, and better than that he is absorbed in Kṛṣṇa. Best of all, he has intense prema at the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa. He thus indicates Śukadeva’s high qualification for what he spoke previously and for what he will speak in the upcoming chapters. The second line of the verse states Parīkṣit’s desire. Please describe to us the activities of Kṛṣṇa (viṣṇoḥ) who appeared in this world from his place known as Goloka in Yadu’s dynasty (tatra), since he was a great devotee. Śrīdhara Svāmī explains that the word aṁśena (Kṛṣṇa is an expansion of Viṣṇu) has been used for the common people in the assembly. The Gītā (9.25) says nāhaṁ prakāśaḥ sarvasya yogamāyā-samāvṛtaḥ: covered by my māyā I do not reveal myself to all people. The phrase can also mean “he appeared with a nature not easily understood by all.” Or it can mean “he appeared along with Balarāma, his aṁśa.” Such meanings must be given, because otherwise there is a contradiction to other statements in the Bhāgavatam: darśayām āsa lokaṁ svaṁ gopānāṁ tamasaḥ param Kṛṣṇa revealed to the cowherd men his abode, which is beyond material darkness. SB 10.28.14 kṛṣṇaṁ ca tatra cchandobhiḥ stūyamānaṁ su-vismitāḥ They were amazed to see Kṛṣṇa there, surrounded by the personified Vedas, who were offering him prayers. SB 10.28.17 Also of course there are the words kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam which will be explained in detail later. Brahmā also says: goloka-nāmni nija-dhāmni tale ca tasya devī-maheśa-hari-dhāmasu teṣu teṣu | te te prabhāva-nicayā vihitāś ca yena govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi || I worship the Supreme Lord Govinda by whom respective powers are given to the abodes of Durgā, Śiva and Viṣṇu, which are situated below his own planet of Goloka. Brahma-saṁhitā 5.43 rāmādi-mūrtiṣu kalā-niyamena tiṣṭhan nānāvatāram akarod bhuvaneṣu kintu | kṛṣṇaḥ svayaṁ samabhavat paramaḥ pumān yo govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi || I worship the Supreme Lord Govinda who, though appearing in the worlds in eternal forms such as Rāma and Nṛsiṁha with their characteristic powers, also appears personally in the supreme form of Kṛṣṇa with the most attractive pastimes. Brahma-saṁhitā 5.39 These verses defeat all doubts. There are other verses as well. The word viṣṇu is used to indicate Kṛṣṇa in this verse because it indicates that he as the highest position of pervasion (viṣṇu means the pervader). Please describe with excellence (śaṁsa) as if in verses of praise, the greatly powerful activites of Kṛṣṇa. This statement hints at the supremely astonishing nature of Krṣṇa’s activities among the activities of all forms of the Lord. The word naḥ (us) is used in the plural out of respect, with great desire to hear, or out of consideration of the other people listening. Or it can indicate humility, to receive mercy. The word naḥ can also modify viṣṇoḥ, indicating “Kṛṣṇa belonging to us Pāṇḍavas.” This would indicate his devotion to Kṛṣṇa and his great desire to hear about his activities.

Personal Guidance
Hearing Kṛṣṇa's pastimes is the highest activity — far surpassing even the noblest worldly histories of pious dynasties.
Practical Applications
  • Replace fifteen minutes of news or social media today with a chapter from Kṛṣṇa Book or the Tenth Canto.
  • When you tell stories at dinner, include one pastime of Kṛṣṇa alongside the day's events — train your tongue toward what truly nourishes.
  • Before sleep, ask Kṛṣṇa specifically (not a vague 'higher power') to let you hear about Him tomorrow with deeper attention.
Reflection Questions
  • When I sit to hear about Kṛṣṇa, am I actually hungry for His personal vīryāṇi, or am I settling for moral lessons and 'pious dynasty' content that leaves Him at a safe distance?
  • Whose 'glories' am I most eager to recount in my day — my family's lineage, my teachers', my own accomplishments — and how does that compete for the space Parīkṣit reserved for Kṛṣṇa-kathā?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Your feed is full of 'pious Yadus' — founder biographies, productivity gurus, athletes, even devotee influencers. Track for one week how many minutes you spend hearing about admirable humans versus hearing about Kṛṣṇa directly. Then ask: what shifted, and what didn't.
  • Parīkṣit, a king with seven days to live, asked for stories not strategy. When you face a real crunch — exam week, breakup, job rejection — what do you actually reach for? Discuss whether kṛṣṇa-kathā is on that list or only your backup-of-the-backup.
  • The verse honors the Yadus as dharma-śīla before moving to Kṛṣṇa. Don't skip dharma to leap into rasa — are you maintaining basic honesty, study discipline, regulation around screens and relationships? The leap to advanced hearing without that base tends to produce sentimentality, not bhakti.
householder
  • You hear endless 'pious Yadu' content at work — leadership podcasts, parenting experts, financial sages. The verse asks: when do you and your spouse sit together and hear vīryāṇi of Kṛṣṇa? If the honest answer is 'rarely', plan one fixed weekly slot this week — not as program, as oxygen.
  • Children imitate what parents consume. If your kids see you absorbed in news and market updates but never in Bhāgavata-kathā, you are passing on the Yadu-dynasty stage and skipping the Kṛṣṇa stage. Discuss with your spouse what one piece of household media diet you will replace this month.
  • Money and career decisions often get framed as dharmic — supporting family, giving in charity, all true and to be honored like the Yadus here. But Parīkṣit's question reminds us — dharma is the floor, not the ceiling. Is there any decision in the next year where you would let kṛṣṇa-kathā and association weigh more than the optimal financial move?
senior
  • Parīkṣit was your contemporary in spirit — told he had limited time and immediately asked for what matters. You may not have a curse on your head, but the body is giving its own notice. What are you still postponing in your hearing — what 'after I finish this project' is still in the way?
  • Legacy-thinking often defaults to the Yadu mode — what good lineage, values, wealth do I leave behind? Worthy, but the verse points further — what kṛṣṇa-kathā have you transmitted? Consider recording a few of your most beloved Bhāgavata passages or memories of hearing them, for grandchildren who may not ask now but will one day need it.
  • Loosening attachments at this stage is not done by force of will but by the heart finding something sweeter. Increase the dose of līlā-śravaṇa — Tenth Canto, Caitanya-caritāmṛta — and watch attachments thin out on their own. If they are not thinning, the dose is too low, not the principle wrong.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa as merely one more avatāra of Viṣṇu rather than the source (kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam), which subtly demotes Him into an impersonal 'aspect' and dries up rasa in hearing.
  • Substituting study of cultural, ethical, or 'pious-Yadu' material for direct śravaṇam of Kṛṣṇa's personal activities, and calling that bhakti-cultivation.
  • Hearing Kṛṣṇa-līlā as mythology or symbol — extracting 'principles' while skipping the Person — so that even Daśama-skandha becomes a self-improvement text rather than meeting with Bhagavān.
Discussion Prompts
  • Parīkṣit Mahārāja first hears about pious Yadus and then asks for Kṛṣṇa's vīryāṇi. In pairs, debate: is it spiritually mature to 'graduate' from stories of saintly devotees to hearing of Kṛṣṇa directly, or does that attitude itself betray a subtle pride? Bring evidence from your own hearing habits.
  • The verse implies a hierarchy: hearing of righteous men is good, but hearing of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa is supreme. Discuss as a group where the modern devotional community actually spends its hearing time — sociology, biography, philosophy, kīrtana, kathā of Kṛṣṇa. Where is the imbalance, and is anyone willing to name it honestly?
  • Jīva Gosvāmī establishes that Kṛṣṇa is the source and Viṣṇu the expansion, but many of us were first attracted through a Viṣṇu-tattva form (Nṛsiṁha, Rāma, Jagannātha, the deity in our home temple). Pair up and share: has the siddhānta of kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam actually changed your worship, or is it a doctrine you accept without it touching your heart?
Role Play

A long-time temple member (player A) approaches the class facilitator (player B) after a Sunday program and says, 'I've been hearing the Tenth Canto for thirty years. I'm tired of the stories. Can we please move to Vedānta-sūtra study or something more intellectual?' Player B must respond drawing on this verse — why did Parīkṣit, on the verge of death and already self-realized, still ask specifically for vīryāṇi, the līlā? Run the scene for four minutes, then swap roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This verse just shows sectarian one-upmanship — the speaker wants to hear about his preferred deity Kṛṣṇa over the dynasty stories. Every tradition does this with its own iṣṭa. Why should I take it as universal truth rather than as devotional partisanship?
    A fair challenge, and Gauḍīya ācāryas faced it openly. The claim is not 'my deity is best because I love Him' but a tattva-siddhānta argued from śāstra — kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam (SB 1.3.28) — and demonstrated by Jīva Gosvāmī through the relations of aṁśa and aṁśī, expansion and source. Parīkṣit is not boosting a favorite; he is dying in seven days and asking for what alone can carry the soul across. The test is not theological argument but phala — what does sustained hearing of Kṛṣṇa-līlā actually do to a person's heart over years? Try it as an experiment, not a creed.
  • If Viṣṇu is non-different from Kṛṣṇa, as the purport says, then asking specifically for Kṛṣṇa's pastimes seems like splitting hairs. Worship any form, get the same result. Why this insistence on the original?
    Non-different in tattva, yes — one ghee — but with different rasa. A father is non-different from himself whether he is at the office or playing on the floor with his child, yet the child experiences something in the second form that the office form cannot give. Kṛṣṇa in Vraja reveals mādhurya — sweetness, accessibility, intimacy — that the awe-laden Vaikuṇṭha form, by His own choice, holds back. Worshiping any viṣṇu-tattva yields liberation, but Kṛṣṇa-līlā specifically yields prema, and prema is what the soul actually hungers for beneath its desire for liberation.
Analogy

Hearing the Yadu dynasty's pious deeds is like reading the architect's notes; hearing Kṛṣṇa's vīryāṇi is entering the house and meeting the owner. Parīkṣit has read the notes — he now asks for the Person who designed and dwells in them. The dynasty is the doorway; Kṛṣṇa is the room.

Stories
Jīva Gosvāmī on kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam

When opponents argued that Kṛṣṇa was simply one more avatāra of Viṣṇu, Jīva Gosvāmī in his Krama-sandarbha turned to the very next verse Parīkṣit will hear later in the Bhāgavata — ete cāṁśa-kalāḥ puṁsaḥ kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam (SB 1.3.28) — and showed grammatically that 'tu' establishes Kṛṣṇa as the source, not the expansion.

This verse's request for the vīryāṇi of Viṣṇu 'who appeared with Baladeva' is read by the Gauḍīya line in that light: the Viṣṇu spoken of here is Kṛṣṇa Himself, the original Person. Parīkṣit asks specifically for vīryāṇi — the personal exploits — not the cosmological functions Viṣṇu shares with other forms.
