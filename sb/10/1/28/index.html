Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.28

Since that time, the city of Mathurā had been the capital of all the kings of the Yadu dynasty. The city and district of Mathurā are very intimately connected with Kṛṣṇa, for Lord Kṛṣṇa lives there eternally.
Purport

It is understood that Mathurā City is the transcendental abode of Lord Kṛṣṇa; it is not an ordinary material city, for it is eternally connected with the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Vṛndāvana is within the jurisdiction of Mathurā, and it still continues to exist. Because Mathurā and Vṛndāvana are intimately connected with Kṛṣṇa eternally, it is said that Lord Kṛṣṇa never leaves Vṛndāvana ( vṛndāvanaṁ parityajya padam ekaṁ na gacchati ). At present, the place known as Vṛndāvana, in the district of Mathurā, continues its position as a transcendental place, and certainly anyone who goes there becomes transcendentally purified. Navadvīpa-dhāma is also intimately connected with Vrajabhūmi. Śrīla Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura therefore says: śrī gauḍa-maṇḍala-bhūmi, yebā jāne cintāmaṇi, tāra haya vrajabhūme vāsa “Vrajabhūmi” refers to Mathurā-Vṛndāvana, and Gauḍa-maṇḍala-bhūmi includes Navadvīpa. These two places are nondifferent. Therefore, anyone living in Navadvīpa-dhāma, knowing Kṛṣṇa and Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu to be the same personality, lives in Vrajabhūmi, Mathurā-Vṛndāvana. The Lord has made it convenient for the conditioned soul to live in Mathurā, Vṛndāvana and Navadvīpa and thus be directly connected with the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Simply by living in these places, one can immediately come in contact with the Lord. There are many devotees who vow never to leave Vṛndāvana and Mathurā. This is undoubtedly a good vow, but if one leaves Vṛndāvana, Mathurā or Navadvīpa-dhāma for the service of the Lord, he is not disconnected from the Supreme Personality of Godhead. At any rate, we must understand the transcendental importance of Mathurā-Vṛndāvana and Navadvīpa-dhāma. Anyone who executes devotional service in these places certainly goes back home, back to Godhead, after giving up his body. Thus the words mathurā bhagavān yatra nityaṁ sannihito hariḥ are particularly important. A devotee should fully utilize this instruction to the best of his ability. Whenever the Supreme Lord personally appears, He appears in Mathurā because of His intimate connection with this place. Therefore although Mathurā and Vṛndāvana are situated on this planet earth, they are transcendental abodes of the Lord.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Since that time, the city of Mathurā had been the capital of all the kings of the Yadu dynasty. The city and district of Mathurā are very intimately connected with Kṛṣṇa, for Lord Kṛṣṇa lives there eternally.
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

From that time (tataḥ), Mathurā (sā), most famous and with indescribable glories, was the capital of the Yadu kings. In spite of the curse on Yayāti, by excellent governing with the great power of a person like Śūrasena, they stayed there as kings. But not only was it their capital. Svayam Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa endowed with all powers, who attracts all by his extraordinarily sweet form, qualities and pastimes, remains in that vicinity eternally because Mathurā agitates even Kṛṣṇa’s mind. Since he resides there eternally it is false to say that he disappears and goes elsewhere by the force of time. This means that Mathurā also is eternal. In Padma Purāṇa, Nirvāṇa-khaṇḍa the Lord says: aho na jānanti narā durāśayāḥ purīṁ madīyāṁ paramāṁ sanātanīm surendranāgendra-munīndra-saṁstutaṁ manorāṁ tāṁ mathurāṁ parākṛtim Unfortunate people do not know my supreme eternal city, attractive Mathurā, with a spiritual form, praised by the best of the devatās, nāgas and sages. tāsāṁ madhye sākṣād brahma gopāla-purī hi In the center of those is the city of Kṛṣṇa, fully spiritual. Gopāla Tāpanī Upaniṣad prāpya mathurāṁ purīṁ ramyāṁ sadā brahmādi-sevitām śaṅkha-cakra-gadāśārṅga-rakisītāṁ muṣalādibhiḥ yatrāsau saṁsthitaḥ kṛṣṇas tribhiḥ śaktyā samāhitaḥ rāmāniruddha-pradyumnai rukmiṇyā sahito vibhuḥ He went to Mathurā served by Brahmā and others and protected by the conch, cakra, club, bow and pestle. There the almighty Kṛṣṇa, endowed with his full potency, resided in the company of his three plenary expansions—Balarāma, Aniruddha and Pradyumna and Rukmiṇī. Gopala-tāpanī Upaniṣad The three śaktis are Balarāma, Aniruddha and Pradyumna. Rukmiṇī is also included. This is more attractive than Vaikuṇṭha. Padma Purāṇa says aho madhupurī dhanyā vaikuṇṭhāt ca garīyasī: fortunate Mathurā is greater than Vaikuṇṭha. Because they are all the abodes of Kṛṣṇa Goloka and his other abodes are all one.

Personal Guidance
Mathurā is no ordinary city — it is Kṛṣṇa's eternal abode; living, serving, or even orienting yourself toward the dhāma directly connects you to Him.
Practical Applications
  • Set Mathurā-Vṛndāvana as the literal direction of your sādhana today — face it in mind during japa and remember Kṛṣṇa is nityaṁ sannihita there.
  • Refuse the assumption that holy places are mere geography; speak of Mathurā, Vṛndāvana, and Navadvīpa as transcendental, even when others reduce them to tourism.
  • If you cannot travel to the dhāma, serve a devotee or project connected to it today — Prabhupāda confirms service abroad does not disconnect you from the Lord.
Reflection Questions
  • If Mathurā and Vṛndāvana are eternally non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself, what does it reveal about my heart that I treat pilgrimage as tourism — collecting photos and souvenirs — rather than as direct contact with the Lord?
  • Where in my life am I clinging to a 'good vow' (like never leaving a holy place, or a fixed sādhana routine) in a way that would actually obstruct service to Kṛṣṇa if He asked me to move?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Your phone, your dorm, your hostel room — these are the 'place' you actually inhabit most hours. Audit one week: what are you letting eternally reside in that space — reels, group chats, exam stress — and where could you make even a small corner (a picture of Kṛṣṇa, a Bhāgavatam, ten minutes of japa before scrolling) into your personal connection to the dhāma?
  • When friends plan a trip, they instinctively pick beaches, hill stations, or concert cities. Quietly propose Vṛndāvana or Māyāpura for one short trip this year — even three days. Notice which friends are curious and which dismiss it; that itself will teach you who is walking which direction in life.
  • If you cannot travel to Mathurā now because of studies or money, take Prabhupāda's principle seriously: a devotee leaving the dhāma for service is not disconnected, and one approaching it through sādhana is connected. Pick one daily anchor — a morning verse, attending an online class from a Vṛndāvana temple, hearing kīrtana while commuting — that keeps the dhāma running underneath your semester.
householder
  • Bring this verse into the next family decision about where to live, where to send the children to school, where to buy property. Stop asking only 'good schools, safe area, resale value' and add the question Prabhupāda's purport forces: how close can we reasonably bring our family to dhāma, devotee association, and a temple — and what are we willing to trade for that closeness?
  • If physical relocation is genuinely not possible in this stage of life, build a 'dhāma-room' in the home — a clean, quiet space with the deity, Bhāgavatam, and tulasī — and protect it the way you would protect your child's study corner. Children raised stepping into such a room daily absorb the abode without a plane ticket.
  • Plan one family yātrā to Mathurā-Vṛndāvana or Navadvīpa within the next two years and start the savings line for it now, the same way you save for school fees or insurance. Treat it as non-negotiable family infrastructure, not a vacation.
senior
  • At this stage the question 'where will I spend my last years and where will I leave this body?' is no longer abstract. Prabhupāda says executing devotional service in the dhāma carries one back to Godhead. Begin a serious, unsentimental conversation — with spouse, with children, with your guru — about whether some form of retirement to or near the dhāma is feasible, and what would have to be arranged for it to be so.
  • If full relocation is not possible, structure long stays — a month in Vṛndāvana, a month in Māyāpura — into the calendar each year while the body still cooperates. Loosen the grip on house, possessions, and routine enough that these stays are not 'disruptions' but the spine of the year.
  • Mentor a younger devotee or family member specifically into the glory of the dhāma: tell them what you have seen there, take them on a yātrā at your expense if you can, and leave behind in your will or letters a clear instruction about your wish to be connected to Mathurā or Navadvīpa even at the time of death. This is the legacy this verse asks of an elder.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Mathurā-Vṛndāvana as merely historically or culturally significant — a heritage site of Krishna's childhood — rather than as His eternally manifest transcendental abode existing right now on this earth.
  • Concluding that since the Lord is everywhere, physical residence in or pilgrimage to the dhāma is unnecessary — using impersonalist logic to dismiss the specific potency Prabhupāda emphasizes in this verse.
  • Using residence in the dhāma as a badge of spiritual status while neglecting actual devotional service, or refusing service opportunities outside the dhāma under the false piety of 'I vowed never to leave Vṛndāvana.'
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says Mathurā and Vṛndāvana are not ordinary material cities but transcendental abodes of Kṛṣṇa. In your pair, take opposing positions: one of you argue that physically going to dhāma is essential for any serious devotee; the other argue that one can access the dhāma fully through service wherever one is placed. Where does the disagreement actually come from — geography, faith, or convenience?
  • The verse says Kṛṣṇa nityaṁ sannihito — eternally present in Mathurā. Yet Prabhupāda also says a devotee who leaves the dhāma 'for the service of the Lord' is not disconnected. Discuss with your partner: how do you tell the difference, in your own life, between leaving the dhāma for service and leaving it for sense gratification dressed up as duty? Bring a real example from your own circumstances, not a hypothetical.
  • Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura equates Vrajabhūmi with Gauḍa-maṇḍala, and Prabhupāda extends the same status to Navadvīpa. Does this 'non-difference' of holy places dilute the unique glory of Mathurā, or does it widen Kṛṣṇa's mercy? Argue both sides and notice which one you instinctively defend — and why.
Role Play

Two students play a married couple. One has just received a transferable, well-paying job offer in another country; the other has been planning for three years to relocate the family to Vṛndāvana to raise the children near the dhāma. They have ninety days to decide. A third student plays their senior Vaiṣṇava friend whom they have come to consult. Enact the conversation: each spouse must articulate, using this verse and Prabhupāda's purport, why their preferred direction is service to Kṛṣṇa and not sense gratification. The senior friend must not simply validate either side — they must press both with the distinction Prabhupāda draws between residing in the dhāma and leaving the dhāma for the Lord's service. End the scene with the senior friend offering one concrete sādhana the couple can adopt this week, regardless of which way the decision finally goes.

Objections & Responses
  • Calling a specific patch of land in north India 'transcendental' and 'eternally non-different from God' sounds like the same sacred-geography claim every religion makes about its own turf. Why should I take Mathurā's status more seriously than any other pilgrim's claim about Mecca, Jerusalem, or Bodh Gaya?
    The Gauḍīya claim is not that Mathurā is sacred because devotees feel it to be — it is that Kṛṣṇa Himself, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, descends and resides there, and the śāstra (here Śukadeva speaking to Parīkṣit) testifies to it. The test is not comparative geography but whether one accepts the personalist conclusion of the Vedic literature: that the Absolute is a person, that He has a specific abode, and that He has revealed it. If one rejects that premise, no amount of argument about Mathurā will land. If one accepts it, the question is no longer 'why this place?' but 'how do I take shelter of what He Himself has indicated?' We do not say other places are unreal — we say Kṛṣṇa has personally identified this one.
  • If simply living in Mathurā or Navadvīpa purifies a person, then the locals there should all be liberated, and most are not. Meanwhile sincere devotees thousands of miles away are clearly advancing. Doesn't this make the geography claim empty in practice?
    Prabhupāda is precise: he says one who 'executes devotional service' in these places goes back to Godhead — not one who merely has a postal address there. The dhāma is a tremendous opportunity, not an automatic dispenser. Offense in the dhāma is heavier, not lighter, precisely because the place is potent. And note what Prabhupāda adds: a devotee leaving the dhāma for the Lord's service is not disconnected. The principle is not real estate but relationship — taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa's chosen abode through bhakti, whether one is physically there or carrying it in the heart through sādhana, association of devotees, and service. Distance from Mathurā is not the disqualifier; absence of devotional service is.
