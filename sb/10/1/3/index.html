Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.3

अवतीर्य यदोर्वंशे भगवान् भूतभावन: । कृतवान् यानि विश्वात्मा तानि नो वद विस्तरात् ॥ ३ ॥

Important Words
  • avatīrya — after descending
    Gerund of ava-tṝ, 'to cross down.' Avatāra is not 'incarnation' in the sense of being forced into matter — it is a voluntary descent. Kṛṣṇa is the descent (avatārī) Himself, not a deity who is incarnated into something foreign.
  • bhagavān — the Supreme Personality of Godhead
    From bhaga (opulence) + -vat (possessing) — defined in Viṣṇu Purāṇa (6.5.47) as one who possesses in full all six opulences. Gauḍīya siddhānta reserves bhagavān (as the highest realization) for the personal feature, above Brahman and Paramātmā realizations.
  • bhūta-bhāvanaḥ — the cause of the cosmic manifestation
    Bhūta (beings, manifested) + bhāvana (causing to be). Kṛṣṇa is not 'within' creation as one of its parts; He is the One by whose will all bhūtas come to be. Yet that same Person stands in Yadu-vaṁśa as a relative.
  • viśva-ātmā — the Supersoul of the entire universe
    Paramātmā feature — Kṛṣṇa as the inner controller. Gauḍīya distinction: viśva-ātmā does not mean 'I am the universe.' It means Bhagavān has a localized aspect in every heart, distinct from the jīva-ātmā, witnessing without becoming one with.
  • vada — kindly say
    Imperative of vad, 'to speak.' Knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is transmitted by speech, not by silent inference. Bhāgavata is śabda-pramāṇa — the Person must be spoken into the hearer's ear, not inferred from the world.
  • vistarāt — elaborately
    Ablative of vistara, 'expansion, detail.' Parīkṣit refuses summary. The flavor (rasa) of līlā lives in the particulars — what Yaśodā wore, what time of day, whose voice called from across the river. Highlight reels do not carry rasa.
The Supersoul, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the cause of the cosmic manifestation, appeared in the dynasty of Yadu. Please tell me elaborately about His glorious activities and character, from the beginning to the end of His life.
Purport

In this verse the words kṛtavān yāni indicate that all the different activities Kṛṣṇa performed while present on earth are beneficial to human society. If religionists, philosophers and people in general simply hear the activities of Kṛṣṇa, they will be liberated. We have described several times that there are two kinds of kṛṣṇa-kathā, represented by Bhagavad-gītā, spoken personally by Kṛṣṇa about Himself, and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, spoken by Śukadeva Gosvāmī about the glories of Kṛṣṇa. Anyone who becomes even slightly interested in kṛṣṇa-kathā is liberated. Kīrtanād eva kṛṣṇasya mukta-saṅgaḥ paraṁ vrajet ( Bhāg. 12.3.51 ). Simply by chanting or repeating kṛṣṇa-kathā, one is liberated from the contamination of Kali-yuga. Caitanya Mahāprabhu therefore advised, yāre dekha, tāre kaha ‘kṛṣṇa’-upadeśa (Cc. Madhya 7.128). This is the mission of Kṛṣṇa consciousness: to hear about Kṛṣṇa and thus be liberated from material bondage.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The Supersoul, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the cause of the cosmic manifestation, appeared in the dynasty of Yadu. Please tell me elaborately about His glorious activities and character, from the beginning to the end of His life.
Commentary

"Previously you described very briefly how Krsna was taken from the prison to Vraja, but you could not describe everything in that description. O muni, you described it in only two verses. Now I pray that you describe this incident in many verses." Krsna is the one who descends to immerse the jivas (bhuta) in prema (bhuta bhavana -causing bhava for the living entities). Other verses in SB support this: nrlokam ramayam asa murtya sarvanga ramyaya He gave pleasure to mankind through his beautiful form. avitrptadrsam nrnam, (SB 9.24.64) Mankind was unsatisfied after tasting his sweetness. They developed a desire constantly taste. svamurtya lokalavanya...etc. (SB 11.1.6) The sweetness of Krsna’s beauty steals the functions of a l the senses. They no longer desire any other form. Drowning them in the ocean of his beauty, he gives them prema. He is called visvatma, soul of the universe, or paramatma because he is the object of love rather than the body or the jiva. This is explained in brahma’s prayers 10.14. "Please explain extensively (vistarat) since my intelligence is slow to grasp the subject."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“But all his activities are most astonishing.” Then describe all of those activities. This verse indicates the purpose of the Lord. Bhagavān, full of all powers, is the protector of all beings by his mercy (bhūta-bhāvanaḥ). He is a benefactor of all (viśvātmā) by his very nature, because he alone supports and inspires everyone with his śakti of consciousness and other factors. This also means that as the highest form, he is the ultimate form of prema. Therefore you should glorify all his activities whatever (yāni), in great detail, to bring out this purpose. Do not summarize them as previously. Though it is impossible to describe all the details, he requests this out of great eagerness.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa, cause of all causes and Supersoul in every heart, descended in the Yadu dynasty out of kindness — hear His activities in full.
Practical Applications
  • Read one chapter of Kṛṣṇa Book or Tenth Canto today, hearing His pastimes from beginning with attention.
  • Pause before a decision and silently acknowledge Kṛṣṇa as the Supersoul in your heart guiding remembrance and forgetfulness.
  • Tell one family member or friend a specific Kṛṣṇa pastime you heard, keeping the vistarāt (elaborate) hearing alive in speech.
Reflection Questions
  • When I retell or remember Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, where do I notice my mind reducing Him to symbol or principle rather than meeting Him as the supremely attractive person in Vraja?
  • What in my own heart resists letting bhūta-bhāvanaḥ — the maintainer seated within — actually maintain me, so that I keep trying to be my own cause?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Parīkṣit was 60 when he received this — but he had been groomed for kṛṣṇa-kathā since childhood. What are you absorbing daily that will form your reflexes when a real crisis hits at 30 or 40?
  • The Yadu dynasty was Kṛṣṇa's chosen family. You don't choose your birth family, but you DO choose your peer group, your influences, and eventually your spouse. Are you choosing in a way that makes Kṛṣṇa's appearance in your life more or less likely?
  • Don't dismiss the Tenth Canto pastimes as 'kid stuff' you'll get to later. Reading Kṛṣṇa's childhood līlās in your own youth — when you still have the capacity for wonder — forms a foundation that becomes very hard to lay down after thirty.
householder
  • Kṛṣṇa appeared in a dynasty — within family, marriage, political alliances, and economic responsibilities. Householder life is not a detour from spiritual life; it is the very setting in which Kṛṣṇa Himself chose to perform His pastimes. Honor your varṇāśrama duties as the field of your bhakti.
  • Make daily kṛṣṇa-kathā non-negotiable in the home — even ten minutes of Bhāgavatam reading with your spouse and children. The Yadus heard about Kṛṣṇa because Kṛṣṇa was in their home; your home becomes 'Yadu-like' to the degree His pastimes are heard there.
  • Parīkṣit asked vistarāt — elaborately, not in summary. As a householder you will be tempted by 'highlight reels' and short-form content. Resist this in your spiritual diet. Sit with one verse and its purport for an extended period; do not optimize bhakti for efficiency.
senior
  • You are now closer in years to Parīkṣit's situation than ever before — a definite remaining span, responsibilities winding down. The verse is a direct model: turn the remaining time toward systematic hearing of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes from beginning to end.
  • Use your authority in the family to ensure the next two generations actually hear kṛṣṇa-kathā, not merely inherit a vague 'Hindu' identity. The Yadu dynasty's glory was that Kṛṣṇa appeared there; your family's real wealth is whether His pastimes are spoken in it.
  • Resist the impersonalist drift that often seduces seniors — 'God is everywhere, all paths lead to the same place, no need for forms and stories.' Prabhupāda's purport insists Kṛṣṇa is the most beautiful person within the three worlds. At this stage, deepen your relationship with the personal Kṛṣṇa; do not dilute it.
Common Pitfalls
  • Hearing 'Supersoul of all' and quietly sliding into impersonalism, as if Kṛṣṇa's personal form in Yadu-vaṁśa were a lower expression of a higher abstraction.
  • Skimming the līlā for moral lessons while skipping the vistarāt — the elaborate, particular details Parīkṣit specifically asked for.
  • Embellishing pastimes from imagination or sentiment rather than staying within what guru, sādhu, and śāstra (Bhāgavatam, ācāryas) actually transmit.
Discussion Prompts
  • Parīkṣit asks to hear Kṛṣṇa's pastimes 'from beginning to end' (vistarāt) even while facing death in seven days. Discuss: when you have limited time left for something important (a deadline, an illness in the family, retirement), do you also reach for hearing about Kṛṣṇa, or for distraction? What does your honest answer reveal?
  • The verse calls Kṛṣṇa bhūta-bhāvanaḥ — the well-wisher and maintainer of all beings — yet He appeared specifically in the Yadu dynasty. Discuss whether Kṛṣṇa's partiality to His devotees and family contradicts His being the well-wisher of all, or whether it actually demonstrates it.
  • Kṛṣṇa is viśvātmā, seated in every heart, yet He also descends in a personal form in Vṛndāvana and Mathurā. Some in the group may feel 'God in the heart' is enough; others insist on the personal form. Surface the disagreement honestly — what is actually lost if we stop at the Supersoul and never approach the personal Kṛṣṇa?
Role Play

Two friends meet for chai. One has just started reading the Tenth Canto and is enthusiastic about Kṛṣṇa's childhood pastimes. The other says, 'I respect your bhakti, but stories of a blue cowherd boy stealing butter feel mythological to me. I prefer the Gītā's philosophy — that feels universal.' Role-play the conversation. The bhakti friend should not get defensive or preachy; he should explain why Parīkṣit, on the verge of death, asked for these very pastimes vistarāt rather than for more philosophy.

Objections & Responses
  • If Kṛṣṇa is viśvātmā, the Supersoul in everyone's heart, then why do I need to hear external stories about Him appearing in some dynasty thousands of years ago? Isn't internal meditation enough?
    Prabhupāda's purport itself answers this: the same Kṛṣṇa who is in the heart as Supersoul is also the cause of all knowledge AND forgetfulness. Left to internal meditation alone, we project our own conditioning onto the 'inner voice' — that is why the same heart can rationalize lust, greed, and even violence as 'inner guidance.' Hearing the actual pastimes (śravaṇam) of the historical Kṛṣṇa in the Yadu dynasty gives the heart-Kṛṣṇa a definite form, name, and character to confirm or correct our perception. Parīkṣit, a self-realized soul who could meditate perfectly, still asked for the external narration vistarāt — that should settle the question.
  • Why should I, a modern person, take seriously a king asking a sage for stories? This is just devotional folklore, not something that addresses my actual problems at work, in my marriage, or with my health.
    Parīkṣit is not asking for entertainment — he has been cursed to die in seven days and is using his final hours with strategic precision. He is asking the question every honest person eventually asks: when everything I have built is about to be taken from me, what is worth attending to? The verse is the considered choice of a dying man with full clarity. Your problems are real but temporary; the Bhāgavatam begins from the standpoint that you, too, are running out of time. Hearing kṛṣṇa-kathā is what trains the consciousness to face that fact without panic.
Analogy

Kṛṣṇa is like the sun: at once present in every drop of dew as a tiny reflection (Paramātmā in every heart) and shining in one place in the sky as Himself (Bhagavān descending in the Yadu dynasty). To say 'the sun is everywhere in the dewdrops' is true; but you still raise your face when the sun itself rises. Parīkṣit, knowing both, raises his face.

Stories
Brahmā baffled in Vṛndāvana

Later in this very Canto (SB 10.13), Lord Brahmā — knowing Kṛṣṇa to be the Supersoul of the cosmos — tested whether the cowherd boy in Vraja was really that same Person. He stole the calves and the boys.

Kṛṣṇa expanded Himself into every missing calf and every missing boy, taking on their exact features for one year. When Brahmā returned and saw himself outdone, he fell at the feet of the cowherd child and prayed: 'I cannot understand You — You who are simultaneously the cause of the cosmic manifestation and a child rolling in the dust of Vraja.'

Parīkṣit's request here — to hear Kṛṣṇa as bhūta-bhāvanaḥ and viśva-ātmā who appeared in a dynasty — receives its concrete answer there. The cause of all causes ate butter from His mother's hand.
