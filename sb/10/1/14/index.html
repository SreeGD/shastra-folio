Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.14

Śukadeva Gosvāmī, the chief among the pure devotees .
Sūta Gosvāmī said: O son of Bhṛgu, after Śukadeva Gosvāmī, the most respectable devotee, the son of Vyāsadeva, heard the pious questions of Mahārāja Parīkṣit, he thanked the King with great respect. Then he began to discourse on topics concerning Kṛṣṇa, which are the remedy for all sufferings in this Age of Kali.
Purport

In this verse the words kṛṣṇa-caritaṁ kali-kalmaṣa-ghnam indicate that the activities of Lord Kṛṣṇa are certainly the greatest panacea for all miseries, especially in this Age of Kali. It is said that in Kali-yuga people have only short lives, and they have no culture of spiritual consciousness. If anyone is at all interested in spiritual culture, he is misled by many bogus svāmīs and yogīs who do not refer to kṛṣṇa-kathā. Therefore most people are unfortunate and disturbed by many calamities. Śrīla Vyāsadeva prepared Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam at the request of Nārada Muni in order to give relief to the suffering people of this age ( kali-kalmaṣa-ghnam ). The Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is seriously engaged in enlightening people through the pleasing topics of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. All over the world, the message of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and Bhagavad-gītā is being accepted in all spheres of life, especially in advanced, educated circles. Śrīla Śukadeva Gosvāmī is described in this verse as bhāgavata-pradhānaḥ, whereas Mahārāja Parīkṣit is described as viṣṇu-rātam. Both words bear the same meaning; that is, Mahārāja Parīkṣit was a great devotee of Kṛṣṇa, and Śukadeva Gosvāmī was also a great saintly person and a great devotee of Kṛṣṇa. Combined together to present kṛṣṇa-kathā, they give great relief to suffering humanity. anarthopaśamaṁ sākṣād bhakti-yogam adhokṣaje lokasyājānato vidvāṁś cakre sātvata-saṁhitām “The material miseries of the living entity, which are superfluous to him, can be directly mitigated by the linking process of devotional service. But the mass of people do not know this, and therefore the learned Vyāsadeva compiled this Vedic literature, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, which is in relation to the Supreme Truth.” ( Bhāg. 1.7.6 ) People in general are unaware that the message of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam can give all of human society relief from the pangs of Kali-yuga ( kali-kalmaṣa-ghnam ).

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Sūta Gosvāmī said: O son of Bhṛgu [Śaunaka Ṛṣi], after Śukadeva Gosvāmī, the most respectable devotee, the son of Vyāsadeva, heard the pious questions of Mahārāja Parīkṣit, he thanked the King with great respect. Then he began to discourse on topics concerning Kṛṣṇa, which are the remedy for all sufferings in this age of Kali.
Commentary

Suta Goswami speaks. " O son of Bhrgu, Saunaka. Sukadeva began describing the pastimes of Krsna which destroy the sorrows of the jivas of kali yuga (kali kalmasa ghnam), or which destroy the worst of sins." Another meaning can be "Sukadeva began describint the pastimes of Krsna to Pariksit (visnu ratam), the destroyer of the sins of Kali.) Sukadeva is described as bhagavata pradhanah, the chief among the devotees, or one who has as his object of respect the book bhagavata or devotee bhagavata.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Pleased by his words, Śukadeva begins to speak. Out of joy, Sūta addresses Śaunaka as “giver of joy to Bhṛgu.” After hearing such words (evam), after the questions of Parīkṣit (atha), the great soul known as the son of Vyāsa, who compiled the Vedas and knew their meaning, the son with strong vows (not to be bewildered by māyā), spoke. He is known as bhagavān since bhagavān is defined as follows: utpattiṁ pralayaṁ caiva bhūtānām āgatiṁ gatim vetti vidyām avidyām c sa vācyo bhagavān iti Bhagavāṇ refers to a person who knows about the creation and destruction of the universe, the appearance and disappearance of the living entities, knowledge and ignorance. Viṣṇu Purāṇa Or, the word bhagavān is used since the Lord and his devotees are considered non-different, being given equal respect. Sādhavo hṛdayam mahyaṁ sādhūnāṁ hrḍayas tv aham: the pure devotee is my very heart: what pains him pains me; I am the heart of the pure devotee: what pleases him pleases me. (SB 9.4.68) Viṣṇu-rātam means Parīkṣit, protected from the brahmāstra by the Lord who entered into the womb his mother and then given (rātam) to Yudhiṣṭhira. Or viṣṇu-rātam means “he who was accepted by Viṣṇu.” This indicates his high position as a devotee. Śukadeva then respected Parīkṣit with great praise and began speaking about the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, Bhagavān, who attracts the hearts of all people. Those topics destroy the grave sins of the people of Kaliyuga, since they destroy Kali himself, the cause of all sins. Or those topics destroy the sin of quarreling about the nature of truth, the goal and the sādhana, arising from inability to determine the greatness of the Lord. All quarrels cease on understanding the greatness of the Lord by hearing about his activities. Or the topics destroy the root of all suffering (Kali) and all sin (kalmaṣam). They destroy the suffering in the material world. Or, Parīkṣit was the destroyer of all sins in the form of Kali. How could the secret topics be revealed in an assembly of persons of various mentalities? Śukadeva was the best among the devotees or the person who possessed the highest scripture, Bhāgavatam. harer guṇākṣipta-matir bhagavān bādarāyaṇiḥ adhyagān mahad ākhyānaṁ nityaṁ viṣṇu-jana-priyaḥ Śukadeva, the son of Vyāsa, whose mind became distracted from trance by the qualities of Kṛṣṇa, and who became attracted to the Vaiṣṇavas, studied this great Bhāgavatam. SB 1.7.11 He was completely absorbed in Kṛṣṇa. Because he was so elevated he was able to induce all listeners to become similar. It is said concerning Prahlāda: sa uttama-śloka-padāravindayor niṣevayākiṣcana-saṅga-labdhayā tanvan parāṁ nirvṛtim ātmano muhur duḥsaṅga-dīnasya manaḥ śamaṁ vyadhāt His bad associates become absorbed in the Lord and experienced the highest bliss by their association with a devotee who had nothing to do with anything material and by engaging in the service of the Lord's lotus feet. SB 7.4.42 The masculine case in pradhānaḥ is poetic license (usually it is neuter).

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa-kathā from a pure devotee is the direct cure for the calamities of Kali-yuga — hear it, and the heart is cleansed.
Practical Applications
  • Devote a fixed portion of today to hearing or reading kṛṣṇa-kathā from a Gauḍīya ācārya — not generic spirituality, but the actual pastimes and teachings of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse the counterfeit remedies of Kali — doom-scrolling, bogus svāmīs, self-help substitutes — and replace one such slot with Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.
  • Speak some kṛṣṇa-kathā to one other person today, however briefly, so that hearing becomes chanting and the medicine is shared.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still seeking remedies for kali-kalmaṣa — anxiety, restlessness, lifelessness — in places other than kṛṣṇa-kathā, and what does that misplaced trust cost me each day?
  • Like Parīkṣit who was honored as viṣṇu-rātam for asking the right question, what is the one question about Kṛṣṇa I have been avoiding asking my own teacher or sādhu-saṅga, and why?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Replace fifteen minutes of doom-scrolling tonight with one purport from the Tenth Canto read aloud — notice, concretely, what shifts in your nervous system before the next class or exam.
  • In your friend group there is at least one 'bogus svāmī' equivalent — a influencer, a self-help author, a hyper-confident senior — whose worldview you have absorbed without auditing. Name them this week and stop quoting them until you have tested their claims against kṛṣṇa-kathā.
  • Before your next exam or interview, do not pray for the result; spend five minutes hearing about Kṛṣṇa instead. Treat the anxiety itself as kali-kalmaṣa and the hearing as the actual remedy, not a spiritual garnish on top of your real strategy.
householder
  • Institute a ten-minute family Bhāgavatam reading before dinner — not as another chore for the kids, but because you, the earner, need the kali-kalmaṣa-ghnam medicine more than they do. Let them see you receive it.
  • When the next major family decision arrives — a job change, a school choice, a financial stretch — sit with your spouse and ask: 'Does this move us closer to a household where kṛṣṇa-kathā is regularly heard, or further?' Make that the tie-breaker, not the salary delta.
  • Identify the one 'bogus' input shaping your parenting or career — a productivity guru, a parenting podcast, a finance personality — that never references the Personality of Godhead. Reduce its share of your attention this month and replace it with a single qualified speaker of Bhāgavatam.
senior
  • Your role now is closer to Śukadeva than to Parīkṣit — you are being asked to speak, not only to inquire. Identify one younger person (child, grandchild, neighbor) and commit to offering them kṛṣṇa-kathā weekly, even five minutes, even if they resist at first.
  • Mortality is the loudest kali-kalmaṣa at this stage. Each night, before sleep, hear or recite one verse about Kṛṣṇa rather than reviewing health worries or family grievances — train the mind toward the topic you want active at the moment of departure.
  • Audit the attachments you are still quietly tightening — property, reputation, being needed — and ask which of them a dhīra in your position would already be loosening. Offer one such attachment, concretely, into the service of spreading kṛṣṇa-kathā this year.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating kṛṣṇa-kathā as one self-help tool among many — slotted between podcasts and therapy — rather than as the singular kali-kalmaṣa-ghnam this verse declares it to be.
  • Drifting toward the bogus svāmīs and yogīs Prabhupāda warns of by accepting any 'spiritual' content that omits Kṛṣṇa, mistaking generic mindfulness or impersonal 'consciousness' talk for the medicine prescribed here.
  • Hearing the Bhāgavatam passively as entertainment or cultural duty, without the reverent posture of Parīkṣit and the responsive humility of Śukadeva that this verse models for every hearer-speaker exchange.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says people in Kali-yuga are 'misled by many bogus svāmīs and yogīs who do not refer to kṛṣṇa-kathā.' Name a teacher, podcast, or book you have personally found compelling that does NOT center Kṛṣṇa — and let the group push back on whether following it is actually helping or quietly harming you.
  • Parīkṣit is called viṣṇu-rātam (protected by Viṣṇu) and Śukadeva is bhāgavata-pradhānaḥ. The verse insists relief comes only when both meet. Where in your life are you trying to be the questioner without a qualified speaker — or trying to speak without first having sincerely inquired? Disagree openly about whether self-study can substitute for guru-bhakti.
  • The verse claims kṛṣṇa-kathā is the remedy for kali-kalmaṣa — the contamination of this age. Pick one specific 'kali-kalmaṣa' in your week (anxiety, doom-scrolling, irritation at family, financial fear) and argue: would ten focused minutes of Bhāgavatam hearing actually dissolve it, or are you secretly convinced the problem requires a material fix first?
Role Play

Two students sit facing each other. Student A is a college friend who has just spent six months with a popular 'non-dual' teacher who says 'Kṛṣṇa, Christ, Buddha — all pointers to the same impersonal awareness; the personality is a concession for the simple-minded.' Student A is genuinely peaceful, articulate, and invites Student B to a weekend retreat. Student B has been reading the Tenth Canto for three months. Without insulting the friend or collapsing into 'all paths are the same,' Student B must (1) honor what is real in A's experience, (2) clearly distinguish bhāgavata-pradhānaḥ kṛṣṇa-kathā from impersonalist drift, and (3) actually invite A to hear one verse of the Bhāgavatam. Run it for five minutes. Then swap roles. Debrief: where did the personalist line get blurred? Where did B sound preachy instead of like a fellow sufferer of Kali-yuga who has tasted relief?

Objections & Responses
  • This is just in-group marketing. Every tradition claims its own scripture is THE remedy for the age — Muslims say the Qur'an, Christians say the Gospel, Buddhists say the Dharma. 'Kṛṣṇa-kathā cures Kali-yuga' is a sectarian boast dressed up as cosmology.
    A fair challenge, and a Gauḍīya teacher will not answer it by shouting louder. Two things. First, the verse is not making a competitive claim against other revelations; it is making a diagnostic claim about a specific disease — the short life, scattered attention, and absence of spiritual culture that define this yuga — and prescribing a specific medicine: hearing about the Personality of Godhead from a realized speaker. Test the diagnosis on yourself before arguing the prescription. Second, the personalist point is not interchangeable. An impersonal Absolute cannot be heard about as caritam — activities, pastimes, relationships. Only a person has a caritam. So the claim is narrower and more testable than it sounds: where you find living kṛṣṇa-kathā spoken by a bhāgavata-pradhānaḥ to a viṣṇu-rātam, suffering measurably lifts. Sit in that for forty days and report back.
  • Calling other teachers 'bogus svāmīs and yogīs' is exactly the arrogance that makes religion ugly. Surely sincere seekers in any path are protected.
    Prabhupāda's sharpness here is not personal contempt; it is a doctor naming counterfeit medicine. The test he gives is precise and impersonal: does the teacher refer to kṛṣṇa-kathā, or not? A teacher who omits the Personality of Godhead may be sincere, charismatic, even kind — and still be handing you something that cannot dissolve kali-kalmaṣa, because the active ingredient is missing. Sincerity in the patient does not transmute placebo into penicillin. The Gauḍīya line is not asking you to despise other teachers; it is asking you to be ruthlessly honest about whether what you are receiving actually matches the prescription Vyāsadeva compiled, at Nārada's request, specifically for people like us in this age.
