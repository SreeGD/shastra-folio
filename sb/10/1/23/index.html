Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.23

वसुदेवगृहे साक्षाद् भगवान्पुरुष: पर: । जनिष्यते तत्प्रियार्थं सम्भवन्तु सुरस्त्रिय: ॥ २३ ॥

Important Words
  • sākṣāt — personally
    Sa + akṣa (eye), 'eye-to-eye, in person.' The verse asserts sākṣāt-bhagavān — not an emanation, not an aṁśa, but Kṛṣṇa Himself. This is the textual anchor for CC Ādi 2 — kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam, Kṛṣṇa is sākṣāt the Person.
  • bhagavān — the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who has full potency
    Used again here in the precise sense: the Person with all six opulences in full. The verse refuses any reading that would reduce Kṛṣṇa to an avatāra or partial expansion. He is the source of avatāras.
  • puruṣaḥ paraḥ — the original person, who is transcendental
    Puruṣaḥ (Person) + paraḥ (highest, transcendental). The Gītā uses paramaḥ puruṣaḥ (15.18) of Kṛṣṇa Himself. Para here distinguishes Him from the other puruṣas (Mahā-Viṣṇu, etc.) — He is the puruṣa above puruṣas.
  • tat-priya-artham — for His satisfaction
    Tat (His) + priya (pleasure, beloved) + artha (purpose). The reason the demigod wives must appear is named directly: His satisfaction. Bhakti's logic — the goal of all activity is Bhagavān's prīti — operates even in cosmic dispatch.
The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who has full potency, will personally appear as the son of Vasudeva. Therefore all the wives of the demigods should also appear in order to satisfy Him.
Purport

In Bhagavad-gītā (4.9) the Lord says, tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti: after giving up the material body, the devotee of the Lord returns home, back to Godhead. This means that the devotee is first transferred to the particular universe where the Lord is at that time staying to exhibit His pastimes. There are innumerable universes, and the Lord is appearing in one of these universes at every moment. Therefore His pastimes are called nitya-līlā, eternal pastimes. The Lord’s appearance as a child in the house of Devakī takes place continuously in one universe after another. Therefore, the devotee is first transferred to that particular universe where the pastimes of the Lord are current. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā, even if a devotee does not complete the course of devotional service, he enjoys the happiness of the heavenly planets, where the most pious people dwell, and then takes birth in the house of a śuci or śrīmān, a pious brāhmaṇa or a wealthy vaiśya ( śucīnāṁ śrīmatāṁ gehe yoga-bhraṣṭo ’bhijāyate ). Thus a pure devotee, even if unable to execute devotional service completely, is transferred to the upper planetary system, where pious people reside. From there, if his devotional service is complete, such a devotee is transferred to the place where the Lord’s pastimes are going on. Herein it is said, sambhavantu sura-striyaḥ. Sura-strī, the women of the heavenly planets, were thus ordered to appear in the Yadu dynasty in Vṛndāvana to enrich the pastimes of Lord Kṛṣṇa. These sura-strī, when further trained to live with Kṛṣṇa, would be transferred to the original Goloka Vṛndāvana. During Lord Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes within this world, the sura-strī were to appear in different ways in different families to give pleasure to the Lord, just so that they would be fully trained before going to the eternal Goloka Vṛndāvana. With the association of Lord Kṛṣṇa, either at Dvārakā-purī, Mathurā-purī or Vṛndāvana, they would certainly return home, back to Godhead. Among the sura-strī, the women of the heavenly planets, there are many devotees, such as the mother of the Upendra incarnation of Kṛṣṇa. It was such devoted women who were called for in this connection.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who has full potency, will personally appear as the son of Vasudeva. Therefore all the wives of the demigods should also appear in order to satisfy Him.
Commentary

Sura striya, the wives of the devas, refers to expansions of Krsna’s eternal female companions, who were present in the heavenly planets. Examples are the wives of the manvatara avataras such as Upendra or Vamana. They performed worship to attain friendship and intimacy with Krsna’s most dear lovers. Because of this worship they should take birth in different forms as priya sakhis of Krsna. In the Ujjvala nilamani it is said: nitya priyanam amsas tu -When Krsna appears in the family of the devas in partial form, his beloveds also appear as the forms of devis to please him. When Krsna appeared on earth, they took the form of young unmarried gopis, as priya sakhis of the nitya priya sakhis.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Having mentioned that Kṛṣṇa is the source of all forms of God, Viṣṇu now describes the esoteric goal of Svayam Bhagavān’s appearance: his blissful pastimes. Kṛṣṇa is described as puruṣaḥ paraḥ, the person to whom no one is superior, and as Bhagavān, endowed with all powers. By mentioning this, no one can say he is born like an ordinary jīva. He will appear (rather than be born) personally in Vasudeva’s house. In order to please him (tat-priyārtham) by your service, or in order to serve his dear consorts such as Rukmiṇī and Rādhā (tat-priyā), the wives of the devatās should make their appearance in the best possible way (sambhavantu). Or they should become qualified (sambhavantu). This means the Lord would bless them with suitable qualification and they would then appear.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa Himself descends as Vasudeva's son in eternal nitya-līlā — even sura-strīs are summoned to serve Him; nothing matters but readiness for His pastimes.
Practical Applications
  • Today, refuse the impersonalist drift in your thinking — speak of Kṛṣṇa by name as a Person, not as 'the divine' or 'consciousness.'
  • Take one concrete step in sādhana that prepares you for direct service — chant attentively, hear one verse, or render a small kāya-vāk-manas offering before sleep.
  • Notice one comfort or status you are quietly attached to, and consciously offer it to Kṛṣṇa as belonging to Him, not to your enjoyment.
Reflection Questions
  • This verse shows that even the sura-strī, residents of the heavenly planets, had to take birth in a particular family at a particular time to be 'fully trained' before going to Goloka — where am I treating my present circumstances (this family, this stage, this duty) as an obstacle to bhakti rather than as the precise training ground Kṛṣṇa has arranged for me?
  • Prabhupāda says the Lord's pastimes are nitya-līlā, going on continuously in some universe at every moment — what would change in how I read Kṛṣṇa's līlā, and how I sit for sādhana today, if I stopped relating to it as ancient history and started relating to it as something happening right now?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When you scroll today, notice how every feed is engineered to make YOU the one being satisfied — likes, validation, dopamine. Flip it once: spend ten minutes chanting or reading SB with the explicit intention 'this is to please Kṛṣṇa, not to make me feel spiritual.' Watch how strange and uncomfortable that is — that discomfort is the whole curriculum.
  • You are at the age where peer groups are forming around 'who you are becoming.' The sura-strīs were called down by name for a specific service. Pick ONE friendship or group where you are quietly performing for approval, and ask yourself: if Kṛṣṇa were the audience instead of these people, would I still say what I'm saying? Don't ghost the friends — change the audience in your own head.
  • Exam season makes you bargain with God: 'let me pass and I'll be devotional after.' This verse says the opposite — devotional service is not the reward you earn after success, it is the training that makes you fit for Kṛṣṇa's association. Today, do fifteen minutes of japa or śāstra-study BEFORE you study, with no transactional expectation, and let your studies be the offering, not the bribe.
householder
  • Your spouse, your kids, your career — all of it is your current 'universe' where Kṛṣṇa's pastimes can be entered through sva-dharma. Stop waiting for a future renunciation. This week, identify one decision (a job change, a school for the child, a money question) you have been making for your own satisfaction and re-decide it asking 'what does this look like as service?' — not as a slogan, but with the actual numbers and tradeoffs on the table.
  • Prabhupāda's purport says the yoga-bhraṣṭa takes birth in a śuci or śrīmān family. If you are a householder reading this, that is likely YOU — you got the human birth, the means, the access to śāstra. The question is no longer whether you were given the opportunity. It is whether your children will inherit a home where Kṛṣṇa is the center, or just a home where Kṛṣṇa is mentioned. Pick one daily family practice — ārati, a verse at dinner, a Sunday program — and install it this week, not next month.
  • Money and time are the two currencies a householder fights over. The sura-strīs were called for a specific service, not for general piety. Audit one month of your spending and one week of your calendar, and ask: what fraction is going to viṣaya (sense gratification dressed up as 'family needs') versus actual service? Don't moralize the answer — just see it clearly, and shift one line item.
senior
  • You are closer to the body's transition than the youth in the room, and this verse is a direct mercy for you: the devotee does not lose the pastimes by dying — he is transferred to the universe where they are currently happening. Today, write down what you are still afraid to lose at death. Read the list. Then read the verse. Notice which fears the verse actually answers and which are just ahaṅkāra clinging.
  • Mentoring is your sva-dharma now, but the modern temptation is to mentor for legacy — 'so they remember me.' The sura-strīs were called to satisfy Kṛṣṇa, not to be remembered. Pick one younger person — a grandchild, a junior devotee, a neighbor — and give them one hour this week of undivided attention with no agenda of being thanked or quoted later. Let the service be the whole point.
  • Loosen one attachment this month, deliberately. Not the easy ones (a possession you were already tired of) — a real one: a grudge you have nursed for decades, a role in the family you refuse to let your children take over, a self-image as 'the one who knows.' Offer it specifically to Kṛṣṇa. Santoṣa at this stage is not passive contentment; it is the active letting-go that makes you light enough to be transferred when the time comes.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'sura-strī were called to enrich Kṛṣṇa's pastimes' as a license for sentimental fantasy about being a gopī or playmate of the Lord, instead of doing the unglamorous training the present āśrama actually demands.
  • Concluding from 'even yoga-bhraṣṭas are transferred to pious families' that one can coast on partial sādhana — turning the verse's mercy into an excuse to slacken viṣaya-tyāga and daily sādhana now.
  • Slipping into impersonalist drift by treating 'returning home, back to Godhead' as merger or a state of consciousness, when the verse is explicitly about a personal Kṛṣṇa appearing in a specific house, in a specific dynasty, with specific associates.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse says the sura-strīs were called down specifically to please Kṛṣṇa — not for their own enjoyment or fulfillment. Where in your own sādhana do you secretly want to be 'called' for your own pleasure rather than for His? Share one place where this self-deception is operating right now.
  • Prabhupāda's purport claims that even an incomplete devotee enjoys the heavenly planets first and then takes birth in a śuci or śrīmān family. Does this sound like a consolation prize that lets us slack off, or like a serious warning about the cost of incompletion? Defend your reading against the opposite view in the room.
  • The pastimes are nitya-līlā — happening continuously in some universe at every moment. If this is literally true, what does it change about your day today? If it sounds mythological to you, name the specific resistance — and let someone in the group push back on it without being polite.
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A is a long-time devotee who has just been told by a senior Vaiṣṇava that, because their sādhana is incomplete, they will likely 'go to the heavenly planets first' rather than directly to Goloka. Student A is unsettled — it sounds like failure. Student B is a fellow practitioner who has read this verse and its purport. B's job, in five minutes, is NOT to comfort A with vague reassurance but to honestly help A see (a) that the arrangement is Kṛṣṇa's mercy, not punishment, (b) what concretely is missing from A's sādhana right now, and (c) one specific thing A can do this week to close that gap. Then swap roles. Debrief: where did the conversation drift into material consolation instead of real compassion?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling down 'wives of the demigods' to satisfy Kṛṣṇa sounds patriarchal and instrumentalizing — women as ornaments for a male deity's pastimes. Why should a modern reader take this seriously?
    The framing inverts the modern reading. The sura-strīs are not being used; they are being given the rarest opportunity in all the universes — direct association with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, training that culminates in eternal Goloka Vṛndāvana. In our material conditioning, 'satisfying another' sounds like degradation because we project our own exploitative relationships onto it. But in the spiritual realm, satisfying Kṛṣṇa IS the soul's own highest fulfillment — there is no split between His pleasure and the devotee's. The sura-strīs are advanced devotees from the heavenly planets being promoted, not commodities being deployed. The 'patriarchy' lens is a category error: it imports a material power-relation into a domain where Kṛṣṇa is not a 'male' in the bodily sense and the devotees are not 'females' in the bodily sense — both are eternal souls, and the rasa is svarūpa-based, not gender-political.
  • Innumerable universes, the Lord appearing in one of them every moment, eternal pastimes running in parallel — this is mythology dressed up as cosmology. A serious adult shouldn't need to believe this to live a moral life.
    Two responses, and the second is the one that actually matters. First: the 'serious adult' claim assumes the modern materialist cosmology is the neutral default and śāstra is the optional ornament. But that cosmology is itself a metaphysical commitment — one that cannot account for consciousness, for the persistence of the self, or for why moral life should bind us at all. Second, and more important: the verse is not asking you to assent to a cosmological diagram. It is telling you that Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are not a one-time historical event you missed by 5,000 years — they are eternally available, and a devotee whose sādhana ripens enters them. That is not mythology; that is the practical promise the whole tradition rests on. If you find yourself needing the cosmology to be 'symbolic' before you can practice, ask honestly whether you are protecting your modern self-image or seriously testing the claim. The Gauḍīya answer is: chant, hear, serve — and the cosmology will stop feeling like a hurdle and start feeling like a description of where you actually are.
Analogy

Kṛṣṇa will appear as Vasudeva's son — not as a borrowed body, not as a partial expansion, but personally, sākṣāt. The demigods' wives are dispatched to take birth as the women who will serve Him. The line of preparation is precise: where the Person Himself comes, the supporting cast must be in place beforehand. Yoga-māyā is the casting director.
