Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.64

एतत् कंसाय भगवाञ्छशंसाभ्येत्य नारद: । भूमेर्भारायमाणानां दैत्यानां च वधोद्यमम् ॥ ६४ ॥

Once the great saint Nārada approached Kaṁsa and informed him of how the demoniac persons who were a great burden on the earth were going to be killed. Thus Kaṁsa was placed into great fear and doubt.
Purport

It has already been discussed that mother earth implored Lord Brahmā to give her relief from the distress created by the burdensome demons and that Lord Brahmā informed her that Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself was going to appear. Kṛṣṇa says in Bhagavad-gītā (4.8) : paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge Whenever there is a burden created by the demons and whenever the innocent devotees are distressed by demoniac rulers, the Lord appears in due course of time to kill the demons with the assistance of His real representatives, who are technically called demigods. In the Upaniṣads it is stated that the demigods are different parts of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. As it is the duty of the parts of the body to serve the whole, it is the duty of Kṛṣṇa’s devotees to serve Kṛṣṇa as He wants. Kṛṣṇa’s business is to kill the demons, and therefore this should be a devotee’s business also. Because the people of Kali-yuga are fallen, however, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, out of kindness for them, did not bring any weapon to kill them. Rather, by spreading Kṛṣṇa consciousness, love of Kṛṣṇa, He wanted to kill their nefarious, demoniac activities. This is the purpose of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement. Unless the demoniac activities on the surface of the world are diminished or vanquished, no one can be happy. The program for the conditioned soul is fully described in Bhagavad-gītā, and one simply has to follow these instructions to become happy. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has therefore prescribed: harer nāma harer nāma harer nāmaiva kevalam kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā Let people chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra constantly. Then their demoniac tendencies will be killed, and they will become first-class devotees, happy in this life and in the next.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Once the great saint Nārada approached Kaṁsa and informed him of how the demoniac persons who were a great burden on the earth were going to be killed. Thus Kaṁsa was placed into great fear and doubt.
Commentary

Narada informed Kamsa (samsayam asa) how the devatas were appearing in the various families.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Nārada had all knowledge (bhagavān) and thus knew that Kaṁsa had become peaceful, thinking that the Lord would not appear. Or bhagavān indicates Nārada’s most merciful nature. He was acting for the benefit of the world by hastening the appearance of the Lord through speaking with Kaṁsa. He spoke to Kaṁsa: tripiṣṭapād āpatito mathuropavane sthitaḥ preṣayām āsa kaṁsāya sa dūtaṁ muni-puṅgavaḥ sa dūtaḥ kathanām āsa nāradāgamana nṛpe sa nāradasyāgamanaṁ śrutvā tvarita vikraṁaḥ nirgajāmāsuraḥ kaṁsaḥ svapuryāḥ padmalocanaḥ sa dadarśātithiṁ ślāghaṁ devarśīṁ vītakalmaṣam tejasā jvalam ākāraṁ vapuṣā sūryavarcasam so ‘bhivādyarsahe tasmai pūjāṁ cakre yathāvidhi āsanaṁ cāgnivaṛṇabhaṁ visṭrejyopajahāra saḥ nisasādāsane tasmin sa vai śakru-sakho muniḥ uvāca cograsenasya sutaṁ paramakopanam pūjito ‘haṁ tvayā viṛa vidhi-dṛṣṭena karmaṇā gate tvevaṁ mama vacaḥ śruyatāṁ gṛhyatāṁ ca vai anuśṛtya divo lokān ahaṁ brahma-purogamān gataḥ sūrya-sakham tāta vipulaṁ meru-paravatam so ‘ham kadācid devānāṁ samāje meru-mūrdhani saṁgṛhya vīṇāṁ samṣaktām āgacchaṁ brahmaṇaḥ sabhām tatra mantrayatām evaṁ devatānām māyā śrutaḥ bhavataḥ sānugasyeha vodhopāyaḥ sudāruṇaḥ Coming from Svarga to a grove in Mathurā, Nārada sent a messenger to Kaṁsa. The messenger told him of Nārada’s arrival. Hearing of his arrival, lotus-eyed Kaṁsa quickly came out of his city. He saw Nārada, shining like the sun, with no impurities, blazing with effulgence. He welcomed and worshipped Nārada properly and offered him a pure seat. Nārada sat on the seat and spoke to Ugrasena’s son who was full of anger. “You have worshipped me according to the rules. Please hear and understand my words. On the order of the devatās I went to huge Meru, friend of the sun. With my vīṇā I came to the peak of Meru, where the devatās were assembled. I heard the devatās discussing about a very cruel method of killing you and your associates”. Kaṁsa is described as lotus-eyed because his eyes were wide with surprise.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa descends to relieve the earth of demoniac burden — your share in this mission today is to chant His names and refuse to feed demoniac tendencies in yourself.
Practical Applications
  • Chant a fixed number of Hare Kṛṣṇa rounds today before any other 'urgent' task, treating it as your personal contribution to lifting the earth's burden.
  • Notice one demoniac tendency rising in you — envy, lust, deceit — and refuse to act on it, offering that restraint as service to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's name or a verse to one person today who is suffering under modern 'demoniac' pressures, sharing the medicine instead of merely commiserating.
Reflection Questions
  • Nārada's warning placed Kaṁsa in fear because his whole life was built on demoniac premises — where in my own life would a clear word from a sādhu about Kṛṣṇa's coming provoke fear rather than joy, and what does that fear reveal about what I am still protecting?
  • Prabhupāda says Kṛṣṇa's devotees share His business of relieving the earth's burden — what specific 'demoniac activity' in my own conduct or environment am I tolerating today instead of actively working to diminish through chanting and Kṛṣṇa consciousness?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a friend group is drifting into something clearly destructive — substances, cruelty toward an outsider, academic dishonesty — be the Nārada who walks in and names it, even if it costs you the group. Do not hide behind 'it's not my business.'
  • Audit your social media feed this week as if it were Kaṁsa's court: which accounts are demoniac burdens on your attention? Unfollow them with the same decisiveness this verse models — no negotiation, no 'but they're sometimes funny.'
  • Before exams or interviews, instead of praying for outcomes, take five minutes to chant Hare Kṛṣṇa and ask Kṛṣṇa to kill the demoniac tendencies in your own preparation — laziness, panic, comparison with classmates. That is the udyama this verse is really about.
householder
  • Identify one 'burden on the earth' inside your own home — a habit, a financial entanglement, a toxic relationship you have tolerated for the children's sake — and name it aloud to your spouse this week, the way Nārada named the coming reckoning to Kaṁsa. Schedule the hard conversation; do not wait for Kṛṣṇa to force it through circumstance.
  • In your career, distinguish work that genuinely serves Kṛṣṇa's order in society from work that merely thickens the demoniac burden. You may not be able to leave your job, but you can refuse one specific assignment this quarter that crosses the line — and absorb the cost as your sādhana.
  • Make your home a place where harināma is louder than the news cycle. Institute one daily family kīrtana, however short, as the household's working response to the demoniac noise your children are absorbing all day from screens and peers.
senior
  • Use the authority of your age to be the Nārada in your extended family — the one person willing to say to a younger relative that the path they are on will end badly. You have less to lose now than anyone; spend that capital before you cannot.
  • Reflect on which attachments still make you, like Kaṁsa, susceptible to 'great fear and doubt' when you hear of death. Each one is a demon still seated on the throne of your heart; offer it specifically into the chanting of the Holy Name each morning.
  • Mentor at least one younger devotee or seeker by transmitting not your opinions but the verses and the Name. Caitanya Mahāprabhu's method — no weapons, only saṅkīrtana — is the legacy a senior is uniquely positioned to leave.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'killing the demons' externally — scanning the world for villains to oppose — while ignoring the demoniac tendencies (lust, anger, greed, envy) that are the actual burden Kṛṣṇa came to lift from one's own heart.
  • Treating the harer nāma prescription as a soft, optional devotional flavor rather than as Caitanya Mahāprabhu's actual weapon for this age — chanting casually or ornamentally instead of constantly and with the seriousness of someone engaged in vadhodyamam against their own anarthas.
  • Sliding into impersonal or vague 'cosmic balance' language ('the universe restores itself,' 'evil is destroyed') and forgetting that it is Kṛṣṇa personally, on a definite schedule (sambhavāmi yuge yuge), who appears and that devotees are His personal instruments — not free agents pursuing a generic good cause.
Discussion Prompts
  • Nārada deliberately approaches Kaṁsa to inform him that the demons will be killed — provoking, not pacifying him. Where in your own life have you been tempted to soothe a 'Kaṁsa' (a person clearly going against dharma) instead of being the agent who hastens the resolution? Share a specific instance, then disagree with your partner if their 'kindness' looks like cowardice to you.
  • Prabhupāda says it is 'a devotee's business' to assist Kṛṣṇa in killing the demons — but Caitanya Mahāprabhu carried no weapon, only the Holy Name. Debate in your group: in your actual workplace or family, what does 'killing demoniac activities' look like this week — confrontation, withdrawal, or chanting? Push back when someone reaches for the easy answer.
  • The verse shows Kaṁsa thrown into 'great fear and doubt' the moment he hears the truth. Where has hearing śāstra recently produced fear or doubt in you rather than peace — and is that a sign you are closer to Kaṁsa's position than to Nārada's? Be willing to disagree about whether spiritual discomfort is always a good sign.
Role Play

Two students play a scene set in a modern office. Student A is a project lead who has discovered that a senior colleague is quietly falsifying client reports — clearly adharmic, but powerful and well-liked. Student B plays a 'Nārada' friend who has come to A's desk specifically to tell A the truth about what is coming: this will collapse, people will be hurt, and A's silence is itself participation. Student B must not soften the message to keep the friendship comfortable; Student A must react honestly — fear, denial, deflection, or resolve. After five minutes, swap roles and run it again with a family scenario (an elder relative enabling an addiction in a younger one). Debrief: when did 'Nārada' lose nerve? When did 'Kaṁsa' get heard instead of confronted? What would real Vaiṣṇava udyama have looked like?

Objections & Responses
  • Isn't it disturbing that a 'great saint' like Nārada actively goes to a tyrant and informs on people who are about to be killed? This sounds less like compassion and more like instigation — a modern reader can't accept a sage who behaves this way.
    The modern reader is reading Nārada through a sentimental lens of compassion as niceness. Nārada's compassion is for the soul, not the body — including Kaṁsa's soul. By informing Kaṁsa, he accelerates the very fear and doubt that will eventually bring Kaṁsa face to face with Kṛṣṇa, which is the only real liberation available to him. He also accelerates the relief of the earth and of the sādhus burdened by demoniac rule. This is precisely the pivot the Gītā insists on: real compassion is sometimes indistinguishable, on the surface, from provocation. A Gauḍīya teacher would say: do not measure a sage by whether he comforts the body; measure him by whether he serves Kṛṣṇa's plan.
  • The whole framing of 'demons who are a burden on the earth' has been used historically to justify violence against whoever someone decides is demoniac. Why should I take this verse seriously in a pluralistic age?
    The verse is not licensing you to identify demons and remove them — that is Kṛṣṇa's prerogative, executed in due course of time, with His own representatives. Notice what Prabhupāda actually prescribes for us in Kali-yuga: not weapons, but the Holy Name. Caitanya Mahāprabhu, knowing exactly how fallen and confused this age is, refused to give us a sword precisely because we cannot be trusted to wield it. Our 'killing of demons' is the killing of demoniac tendencies — beginning inside ourselves — through harināma. Anyone who reads this verse as a mandate for human violence has stopped reading at line one of the purport.
