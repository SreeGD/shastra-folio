Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.16

वासुदेवकथाप्रश्न: पुरुषांस्त्रीन् पुनाति हि । वक्तारं प्रच्छकं श्रोतृंस्तत्पादसलिलं यथा ॥ १६ ॥

exactly as the entire world is purified by the Ganges water emanating from the toe of Lord Viṣṇu .
The Ganges, emanating from the toe of Lord Viṣṇu, purifies the three worlds, the upper, middle and lower planetary systems. Similarly, when one asks questions about the pastimes and characteristics of Lord Vāsudeva, Kṛṣṇa, three varieties of men are purified: the speaker or preacher, he who inquires, and the people in general who listen.
Purport

It is said, tasmād guruṁ prapadyeta jijñāsuḥ śreya uttamam ( Bhāg. 11.3.21 ). Those interested in understanding transcendental subject matters as the goal of life must approach the bona fide spiritual master. Tasmād guruṁ prapadyeta. One must surrender to such a guru, who can give right information about Kṛṣṇa. Herein, Mahārāja Parīkṣit has surrendered to the right personality, Śukadeva Gosvāmī, for enlightenment in vāsudeva-kathā. Vāsudeva is the original Personality of Godhead, who has unlimited spiritual activities. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is a record of such activities, and Bhagavad-gītā is the record of Vāsudeva speaking personally. Therefore, since the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is full of vāsudeva-kathā, anyone who hears, anyone who joins the movement and anyone who preaches will be purified.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The Ganges, emanating from the toe of Lord Viṣṇu, purifies the three worlds, the upper, middle and lower planetary systems. Similarly, when one asks questions about the pastimes and characteristics of Lord Vāsudeva, Kṛṣṇa, three varieties of men are purified: the speaker or preacher, he who inquires, and the people in general who listen.
Commentary

The topics of Hari purifies three types of persons just like the caranamrta from the salagaram or deity (pada salilam yatha), which purifies three types of persons: those who put it on their head, those who drink it and those who do both. It can also mean that the topics are just like the Ganga, which purifies those in the upper, middle and lower worlds. The topics of Hari purify the speaker, the inquirer and the listener. It is most purifying for the speaker, then the inquirer, and then the listener, in that order.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Sages and I myself become purified of the contamination which arises from not hearing the Lord’s topics. He repeats the name Vāsudeva out of attraction for Kṛṣṇa. One will ask questions in order to increase steadiness. Talks about Kṛṣṇa definitely (hi) purify three types of persons (the main ones). By asking the question, the speaker becomes purified since he becomes established as the speaker at the time of asking him. Of course, how much more he will be purified when he speaks. What to speak of how much more the original questioner will be purified. And even those who hear the question as bystanders become purified. The questioner and speaker are single, since it is unsuitable that there be many questioners and speakers. But without consideration, all people are purified by things related to the Lord. An example is given. The water from the Gaṅgā or from a śālagrāma purifies the sprinkler of water (the questioner). It then purifies the object on which it is sprinkled (the speaker). It then purifies others who are present (the listeners). śruto ’nupaṭhito dhyāta ādṛto vānumoditaḥ sadyaḥ punāti sad-dharmo deva-viśva-druho ’pi hi Pure devotional service rendered to the Supreme Lord, consisting of hearing, chanting, meditating, accepting it with faith, or praising others who perform it, immediately purifies even those who are envious of the Lord and the universe. Personal Guidance
Hearing, asking, and speaking kṛṣṇa-kathā purify all three parties — like Gaṅgā from Viṣṇu's toe — so make vāsudeva-kathā the daily current of your life.
Practical Applications
  • Today, ask one sincere question about Kṛṣṇa to a devotee senior to you — do not let the day pass in only material conversation.
  • Read aloud one paragraph of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam or Bhagavad-gītā to someone in your household, trusting that speaker, hearer, and inquirer are all purified.
  • Refuse to treat kṛṣṇa-kathā as optional entertainment — schedule a fixed window for hearing from a bona fide source, the way you would schedule a bath.
Reflection Questions
  • When I speak, hear, or inquire about Vāsudeva-kathā, where do I notice the purification the verse promises — and where do I still walk away unchanged because I was performing the act rather than entering it?
  • Like Parīkṣit at the threshold of death turning to Śukadeva, what is the one question about Kṛṣṇa I have been postponing — and what fear or distraction keeps me from asking it of a bona fide guru today?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • This week, instead of doom-scrolling between classes, post one honest question about Kṛṣṇa or the Bhāgavatam in a group chat or to a senior devotee — not a hot take, an actual praśna you don't know the answer to.
  • In your friend group, identify the one person who always changes the subject when anything spiritual comes up. Don't argue with them; just ask them a real question about what they actually believe and listen without rebuttal — practice being the purified śrotā.
  • Before the next exam-stress spiral, pick a ten-minute SB or BG audio class and listen with full attention instead of background noise. Notice whether your nervous system settles — that's the salila of tat-pāda working, not a productivity hack.
householder
  • At one family meal this week, replace the usual logistics-and-news talk with a single Vāsudeva-kathā question your children can actually engage with. Not a lecture — a question. You become the vaktā, they become the pracchaka, the meal becomes Gaṅgā.
  • When a colleague or neighbor opens up about a real struggle — illness, divorce, a parent's death — resist the trained reflex toward purely material consolation. Offer one sentence of genuine kathā, calibrated to them, even at the cost of looking 'religious.' This is karuṇā over comfort.
  • Audit where the household's money and weekend hours actually go. Reallocate one recurring expense or one weekend slot per month explicitly toward sat-saṅga — a class, a visiting sādhu, hosting kīrtana. Make the budget reflect what you say you believe.
senior
  • You have stories, decades of saṁskāra, and the moral standing to be heard. Pick one younger relative or mentee and commit to a monthly conversation where you are the vaktā of vāsudeva-kathā — not advice about careers, but the verses and pastimes that have actually carried you.
  • Loosen one attachment this season — a possession, a position, a grudge — and use the loosening as the occasion to inquire more deeply, not less. As the body's grip weakens, let praśna sharpen: ask Śukadeva-equivalents in your life the questions you've been postponing.
  • Build the habit of dying as a śrotā. Each day, sit for a fixed period and simply hear Bhāgavatam — read aloud, chanted, or recorded — with the awareness that this is the salila preparing the passage. Mahārāja Parīkṣit modeled this; you have the āśrama for it now.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating vāsudeva-kathā as neutral information to be consumed, so the speaker grows proud, the inquirer grows clever, and the listener grows entertained — none of the three actually purified.
  • Asking questions about Kṛṣṇa from idle curiosity or debate rather than as a jijñāsu seeking śreya uttamam, and then wondering why the hearing leaves no taste.
  • Drifting into impersonalism — hearing 'Vāsudeva' as cosmic principle or inner Self rather than the original Personality of Godhead whose pastimes and characteristics are the specific subject the verse purifies us through.
Discussion Prompts
  • Pair up and identify a moment in the past month when you avoided a serious conversation about Kṛṣṇa with a friend or family member. What did you tell yourself in that moment, and what was the real reason? Where did your group disagree about whether speaking up would have been intrusive or actually compassionate?
  • The verse names three beneficiaries: speaker, inquirer, listener. In your experience, which of these three roles is hardest to occupy honestly — and why? Push back on each other: is the 'listener' role really purifying, or is it a passive seat people hide in to avoid the risks of asking or speaking?
  • Mahārāja Parīkṣit had seven days to live; Śukadeva had renunciation. What is the equivalent urgency in your life right now that should be driving your inquiry into vāsudeva-kathā — and be specific about what's actually crowding it out? Compare with your partner: is the obstacle external circumstance, or your own ahaṅkāra dressed as 'busy'?
Role Play

Three students take roles. Student A is a working professional whose college roommate (Student B) has just been diagnosed with a serious illness and texts: 'Help me make sense of this — I don't know what I believe anymore.' Student C is A's spouse, who privately warns: 'Don't push your Kṛṣṇa stuff on him right now, he's vulnerable, just be a friend.' Play out a fifteen-minute conversation in which A must decide: speak vāsudeva-kathā (real karuṇā, serving the soul) or offer only emotional support (material compassion for the body). After the role-play, the group discusses where A drifted into platitudes, where genuine inquiry was invited, and what the verse demands here.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like proselytizing dressed up in Sanskrit. Why would anyone be 'purified' by being talked at about someone else's God? Isn't this just religious imposition with extra steps?
    The verse is precise: purification comes from praśna — questions — not from monologue. Nobody is 'talked at.' The speaker is purified because the act of articulating Vāsudeva-kathā burns away their own pretense; the inquirer is purified because honest jijñāsā cuts through ahaṅkāra; the listener is purified because attentive śravaṇa is itself sādhana. Compare it to standing near the Gaṅgā: the river doesn't ask your permission, and yet contact purifies. If Kṛṣṇa is who He says He is — the supreme Personality of Godhead, not one deity among many — then withholding His kathā out of politeness isn't tolerance, it's the material compassion the Gītā explicitly rejects. Real karuṇā serves the soul, even when it's inconvenient.
  • I can read Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam alone. Why do I need a guru, a class, or a 'speaker'? The whole 'three persons purified' framework feels like an institutional sales pitch.
    Try it and watch what happens. Solo reading drifts — you import your own assumptions, soften the hard parts, skim what you don't want to hear, and end up with a Bhāgavatam shaped to your preferences. The verse before this one establishes that Parīkṣit, a self-realized king on the threshold of death, still surrendered to Śukadeva Gosvāmī. Tasmād guruṁ prapadyeta jijñāsuḥ śreya uttamam — the qualified inquirer surrenders to a guru not because the book is unclear but because the reader is. The three-person structure isn't institutional; it's diagnostic. If you can't find a speaker you trust or a question you're willing to ask aloud, that absence is itself the obstacle the verse is pointing at.
