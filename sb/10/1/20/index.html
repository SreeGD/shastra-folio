Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.1.20

तत्र गत्वा जगन्नाथं देवदेवं वृषाकपिम् । पुरुषं पुरुषसूक्तेन उपतस्थे समाहित: ॥ २० ॥

Important Words
  • jagannātham — unto the master of the entire universe
    Jagat (universe) + nātha (master). The same name later concretized in Lord Jagannātha of Puri — central to Caitanya Mahāprabhu's life, the deity at whose festival He danced and through whose maṅgala-ārati He served forty-eight years.
  • vṛṣākapim — the Supreme Person, Viṣṇu, who provides for everyone and diminishes everyone's suffering
    Vṛṣa (best, bull, dharma) + kapi (variously: best of bipeds, or one who shakes off). A Vedic epithet for Viṣṇu. The verse's choice of an old Vedic name signals that the demigods are approaching with Vedic protocol — the Puruṣa-sūkta itself is from Ṛg-veda 10.90.
  • puruṣam — the Supreme Person
    Puruṣa = person. The Gauḍīya line is emphatic: the Absolute is personal. The mantra they recite is named precisely Puruṣa-sūkta — the hymn to the Person — and Brahmā chants it not to an impersonal Brahman but to the puruṣa lying on the ocean.
  • puruṣa-sūktena — with the Vedic mantra known as Puruṣa-sūkta
    Ṛg-veda 10.90, the great hymn that describes the cosmos as the body of the Puruṣa. Vaiṣṇava commentators read it as the Vedic confirmation of Bhagavān's personal supremacy. Using this hymn here is doctrinally pointed.
  • samāhitaḥ — with full attention
    Past participle of sam-ā-dhā, 'to place together, concentrate.' Same root as samādhi. The demigods worship in samādhi-mood — fully gathered, not distractedly. Even at this exalted level, attention is the operative variable.
After reaching the shore of the Ocean of Milk, the demigods worshiped the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Viṣṇu, the master of the whole universe, the supreme God of all gods, who provides for everyone and diminishes everyone’s suffering. With great attention, they worshiped Lord Viṣṇu, who lies on the Ocean of Milk, by reciting the Vedic mantras known as the Puruṣa-sūkta.
Purport

The demigods, such as Lord Brahmā, Lord Śiva, King Indra, Candra and Sūrya, are all subordinate to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Aside from the demigods, even in human society there are many influential personalities supervising various businesses or establishments. Lord Viṣṇu, however, is the God of gods ( parameśvara ). He is parama-puruṣa, the Supreme Being, Paramātmā. As confirmed in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.1) , īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ sac-cid-ānanda-vigrahaḥ: “Kṛṣṇa, known as Govinda, is the supreme controller. He has an eternal, blissful, spiritual body.” No one is equal to or greater than the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and therefore He is described here by many words: jagannātha, deva-deva, vṛṣākapi and puruṣa. The supremacy of Lord Viṣṇu is also confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā (10.12) in this statement by Arjuna: paraṁ brahma paraṁ dhāma pavitraṁ paramaṁ bhavān puruṣaṁ śāśvataṁ divyam ādi-devam ajaṁ vibhum “You are the Supreme Brahman, the ultimate, the supreme abode and purifier, the Absolute Truth and the eternal divine person. You are the primal God, transcendental and original, and You are the unborn and all-pervading beauty.” Kṛṣṇa is ādi-puruṣa, the original Personality of Godhead ( govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi ). Viṣṇu is a plenary expansion of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and all the viṣṇu-tattvas are parameśvara, deva-deva.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
After reaching the shore of the ocean of milk, the demigods worshiped the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Viṣṇu, the master of the whole universe, the supreme God of all gods, who provides for everyone and diminishes everyone's suffering. With great attention, they worshiped Lord Viṣṇu, who lies on the ocean of milk, by reciting the Vedic mantras known as the Puruṣa-sūkta.
Commentary

After hearing from earth personified (tad upadhary), Brahma went to the milk ocean. The word atha is excessive usage if it means "then." It can be explained as follows: Brahma thought: My job is creation. Visnu’s job is maintenance. For this job of maintenance we should go to Him. He is resting on the milk ocean. We should go there and request his help. After considering in this way (atha), he left for the milk ocean. There were two tasks here: protection of the earth and destruction of the demons. Thinking that he could engage Indra in protecting the earth and Siva in destroying the demons, Brahma went to the milk ocean with the devas and Siva. Why should Brahma go to Visnu? The reason is given. Visnu is jagannatha, Lord of the universe. He is devadeva, the worshipable deity of all the devatas. It announces their position under the authority of Visnu inprotecting the earth. Visnu is Vrsakapi, he who showers all desires and he who removes all suffering.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

With devotion, they praised the Lord by the Puruṣa-sūkta, indicating the Mahā-puruṣa, creator of mahat-tattva. With great attention they praised the Lord with those verses to indicate his greatness and to reveal that he was proven by the Vedas. The Lord is described as the master of the universe, as the worshipable lord of all devatās: he is praised by the devatās so that he will protect the universe. He showers (vṛṣati) all desires and destroys (ākampayati) all suffering: therefore he is called Vṛṣākapi. This indicates that the devatās had a desire.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa as Viṣṇu is jagannātha and deva-deva — the one supreme shelter; even the demigods take refuge in Him, so why look elsewhere?
Practical Applications
  • Begin today's worship by addressing Kṛṣṇa directly as parameśvara, refusing to dilute Him into a generic 'higher power'.
  • Notice each time you instinctively run to a lesser authority — boss, market, demigod — and redirect that prayer to Lord Viṣṇu first.
  • Recite or hear a portion of the Puruṣa-sūkta or a Viṣṇu-stotra with samāhita attention before opening your phone tomorrow morning.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still treating some controller — boss, government, parent, my own intellect — as if they were parameśvara, when SB 10.1.20 calls me to recognize that all such authorities are themselves subordinate to Lord Viṣṇu?
  • When trouble comes, whom do I instinctively approach first for shelter, and what does that reflexive turn reveal about whether I have actually accepted Kṛṣṇa as deva-deva, the God of gods who alone diminishes everyone's suffering?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Today, name the 'demigods' of your campus or feed — the professor whose approval you crave, the influencer whose lifestyle you envy, the senior whose validation feels like oxygen — and consciously offer your obeisance to Kṛṣṇa above them before checking your phone in the morning.
  • Pick one moment in your day — before an exam, before posting, before a difficult conversation with a friend — and recite even one verse or the mahā-mantra samāhitaḥ, with the same attention the demigods gave the Puruṣa-sūkta. Notice the quality difference from your scrolling-mind.
  • When a peer says 'all gods are the same, just pick whichever vibes,' don't argue and don't cave. Quietly hold the personalist line in your own heart: Kṛṣṇa is a Person, not a vibe — and let your steadiness, not your debate skills, do the witnessing.
householder
  • Before a major family decision — a job change, a school for the children, a financial commitment — formally approach Kṛṣṇa first, the way the demigods approached the shore: not asking Him to rubber-stamp what you have already decided, but actually placing the matter at His feet samāhitaḥ before consulting spouse, parents, or planner.
  • In your home shrine or daily worship, deliberately distinguish Kṛṣṇa from the demigods you may also honor for prosperity or protection. Honor them as His servants, not as alternatives — and teach this distinction to your children explicitly, because their school and screens will not.
  • When your career rewards you and a 'demigod' (boss, client, market) seems to provide your family's income, name out loud at the dinner table that Lord Viṣṇu is the one who provides for everyone and diminishes suffering — the boss is an instrument. Re-anchor the children's sense of where security actually comes from.
senior
  • As you loosen attachments, deliberately loosen them upward: the people you once feared, served, and admired in your career — the 'demigods' of your working life — release them now from the throne they occupied in your psyche, and seat Kṛṣṇa there instead, before the body forces the lesson on you.
  • Make the Puruṣa-sūkta, the Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma, or your daily japa less rote and more samāhita. At this stage you have less time to be distracted; let the recitation be the demigods' kind — full attention, knowing you are addressing the master of the whole universe, not muttering through a checklist.
  • When mentoring younger devotees or grandchildren, do not present Kṛṣṇa as 'one of the great traditions' to keep peace at the table. Speak as Prabhupāda did — Kṛṣṇa is parama-puruṣa, ādi-puruṣa — and let your legacy be the personalist line preserved one more generation, not a tactful blur.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'God of gods' as polite Hindu pluralism — concluding that all paths and all deities lead to the same place — and missing that the verse explicitly subordinates Brahmā, Śiva, Indra, Candra and Sūrya to Lord Viṣṇu as parameśvara.
  • Drifting into impersonalism by treating jagannātha and puruṣa as cosmic principles or 'the Self,' when Prabhupāda's purport insists on Kṛṣṇa as ādi-puruṣa with a sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha — a personal, eternal, blissful form.
  • Imitating the demigods' approach as a transactional technique — reciting Puruṣa-sūkta or other mantras to extract relief from a specific problem — while skipping the samāhitaḥ part: the focused, surrendered attention that makes the worship real rather than mechanical.
Discussion Prompts
  • The demigods — Brahmā, Śiva, Indra — are themselves powerful, yet they go to the shore and bow before Lord Viṣṇu. Share with your partner: who are the 'demigods' in your own life — the influential, capable people you admire — and how does it change your devotion when you realize even they are subordinate to Kṛṣṇa? Where do you still secretly treat such figures as ultimate?
  • Prabhupāda emphasizes that Kṛṣṇa is parameśvara, deva-deva, and that no one is equal to or greater than Him. Modern sensibility resists hierarchy and calls every path equally valid. Discuss honestly: where do you find yourself softening this exclusivity to seem more open-minded, and what is lost in that softening?
  • The demigods recited the Puruṣa-sūkta samāhitaḥ — with full attention. Compare with your partner the difference between your most distracted japa or prayer and the rare moments of samāhita-bhāva. What concretely produces the difference, and what would it cost you to make samāhita-bhāva the rule rather than the exception?
Role Play

Two students. Student A is a thoughtful agnostic friend who says: 'I respect that you have your Kṛṣṇa, I have my version of the Divine, my Hindu uncle has Devī, my Christian roommate has Jesus — they are all equally valid faces of the same Absolute.' Student B must respond as a Gauḍīya practitioner drawing on SB 10.1.20 and Prabhupāda's purport — honoring the demigods and other paths without flattening Kṛṣṇa into 'one face among many,' and without becoming combative. The class then critiques: where did B drift into impersonalism? Where was B too aggressive? Where did B faithfully represent deva-deva?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling Viṣṇu 'God of gods' and ranking deities sounds like religious chauvinism. Isn't this just the same supremacy claim every tradition makes about its own deity?
    A Gauḍīya teacher would distinguish sectarian boasting from ontological description. The verse and purport do not deny the reality or power of Brahmā, Śiva, Indra, Candra, Sūrya — they are real, they have real domains, they are worthy of respect. What Prabhupāda is pointing out is structural: the demigods themselves go to the shore and worship Viṣṇu. The hierarchy is not asserted by devotees against outsiders; it is enacted by the demigods themselves in the verse. Brahma-saṁhitā 5.1 — īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ — and Arjuna's own realization in BG 10.12 (paraṁ brahma paraṁ dhāma) come not from triumphalism but from direct seeing. To flatten this into 'every tradition says that' is to refuse to look at what is actually being shown.
  • If Kṛṣṇa is supreme and provides for everyone and diminishes everyone's suffering, why do the demigods even need to approach Him with elaborate Vedic mantras? Either He already knows and acts, or the petition matters — you can't have both.
    A Gauḍīya teacher would gently point out the bhakti logic the objection misses. Kṛṣṇa does not need the Puruṣa-sūkta — the demigods need to recite it. The samāhita upatasthe — worshiping with full attention — is not information delivery to an ignorant deity; it is the soul placing itself in correct relation. He provides for everyone always; the prayer is how the receiver opens the hand. This is the whole point of sādhana: not to inform Kṛṣṇa, but to dissolve our ahaṅkāra enough that His already-flowing mercy can land. Bhakti is relationship, not transaction — and relationship requires the address, even when the Beloved already knows.
Analogy

The demigods chanting the Puruṣa-sūkta on the shore of the Ocean of Milk is like a delegation approaching a sovereign with the precise protocol of greeting written down by his own court: they bring no improvisations. The mantra was given by the Lord Himself for occasions like this; they use the words He gave. Pure devotion is rarely innovative.
