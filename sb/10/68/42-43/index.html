Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.68.42-43

जलयानमिवाघूर्णं गङ्गायां नगरं पतत् । आकृष्यमाणमालोक्य कौरवा: जातसम्भ्रमा: ॥ ४२ ॥ तमेव शरणं जग्मु: सकुटुम्बा जिजीविषव: । सलक्ष्मणं पुरस्कृत्य साम्बं प्राञ्जलय: प्रभुम् ॥ ४३ ॥

Important Words
  • jala - yānam iva āghūrṇam — tumbling about like a raft
    Jala-yāna = water-vessel; ā-ghūrṇa = tumbling, lurching. The simile is exact — a heavy city behaves like a raft when a plough's tip catches its underside.
  • śaraṇam jagmuḥ — they went for shelter
    Śaraṇāgati — the act at the center of Vaiṣṇava soteriology. The Kurus perform it, though only after their city tips. Even late śaraṇāgati saves; this is the Bhāgavatam's quiet teaching.
  • sa - kuṭumbaḥ — with their families
    Whole households take refuge — wives, children, brāhmaṇas. Surrender taken seriously is taken with one's whole world, not as a private gesture.
  • puraḥ - kṛtya — placing in front
    Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā are placed in front. The very boy they had bound now stands forward as their advocate to Balarāma. The bound becomes the intercessor — a perfect Bhāgavata reversal.
  • prāñjalayaḥ — with palms joined
    Añjali — the gesture of supplication. The Kurus' palms, raised against Sāmba moments ago, are now folded toward his uncle.
Seeing that their city was tumbling about like a raft at sea as it was being dragged away, and that it was about to fall into the Ganges, the Kauravas became terrified. To save their lives they approached the Lord for shelter, taking their families with them. Placing Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā in front, they joined their palms in supplication.
Purport

The city of Hastināpura began to roll about like a raft in a stormy sea. The frightened Kauravas, to quickly appease the Lord, immediately brought Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā and placed them in front.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Seeing that their city was tumbling about like a raft at sea as it was being dragged away, and that it was about to fall into the Ganges, the Kauravas became terrified. To save their lives they approached the Lord for shelter, taking their families with them. Placing Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā in front, they joined their palms in supplication.
Commentary

Seeing their city moving about like a boat, and about to fall into the Ganga, the Kauravas became fearful. There is an extra syllable in the first line of verse 43, as there may be nine instead of eight syllables in a line according to the Bhasavrtti. They put Laksmana and Samba in front in order to please Balarama quickly.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

First they saw the city wobbling, and then falling into the Gaṅgā. Then they saw Balarāma pulling it. Then they tried save their lives. They lost all pride. They approached Balarāma, taking shelter of him as the Lord (prabhum). Or, they approached him since he was capable (prabhum) of forgiving their offense. Or prabhum can modify sāmbam. Putting Sāmba in front as capable of stopping the disaster, they approached Balarāma. If the city fell in the water Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā would be in danger. Thus to please Balarāma they put Sāmba in front as an offering first. But Balarāma was not worried about Sāmba falling in the water. He pulled the city only to scare them.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

A family at sea suddenly sees their ship lurching, lash their children to themselves, and stagger to the captain crying for life — the Kurus, pride evaporated in one rocking minute, place Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā in front of them and fall at the feet they had insulted.

Stories
Gajendra's Surrender

When the elephant Gajendra was being dragged into the lake by the crocodile (SB 8.2-3), his strength exhausted, he lifted a single lotus to the Lord. Viṣṇu came at once.

The Kurus' situation is the same in structure: tilted city, families clutched, life slipping. The śaraṇāgati that follows in verses 44–48 is one of the Bhāgavatam's emergency surrenders — late, but real. Balarāma, like Viṣṇu for Gajendra, accepts it.
