Chapter 68 · Class prep

BG 68.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O King, Jāmbavatī’s son Sāmba, ever victorious in battle, kidnapped Duryodhana’s daughter Lakṣmaṇā from her svayaṁvara ceremony.

Essence. Sāmba boldly carried off Lakṣmaṇā from her svayaṁvara, following the kṣatriya tradition where a princess could be won by strength. Bold action, when aligned with dharma and reciprocation, has its proper place.

BG 68.2

The angry Kurus said: This ill-behaved boy has offended us, forcibly kidnapping our unmarried daughter against her will.

Essence. The Kurus saw Sāmba's action as offense, not initiative. Often when someone acts decisively in a way we didn't authorize, we label them rude rather than honest with ourselves about why we're upset.

BG 68.3

Arrest this ill-behaved Sāmba! What will the Vṛṣṇis do? By our grace they are ruling land that we have granted them.

Essence. The Kurus reminded themselves they had granted the Vṛṣṇis their lands — assuming superiority by patronage. Whenever we begin tallying favors we have done, we have already lost the spirit of relationship.

BG 68.4

If the Vṛṣṇis come here when they learn that their son has been captured, we will break their pride. Thus they’ll become subdued, like bodily senses brought under strict control.

Essence. The Kurus believed they could subdue the Vṛṣṇis like senses brought under control. Pride imagines others can be 'managed' the way a wise person manages himself — but other beings are not our senses.

BG 68.5

After saying this and having their plan sanctioned by the senior member of the Kuru dynasty, Karṇa, Śala, Bhūri, Yajñaketu and Suyodhana set out to attack Sāmba.

Essence. Even Bhīṣma sanctioned the plan, with the qualification that Sāmba should not be killed. Sometimes elder wisdom permits firmness while drawing a line at lethal escalation. Mercy can be folded into discipline.

BG 68.6

Seeing Duryodhana and his companions rushing toward him, Sāmba, the great chariot-fighter, took up his splendid bow and stood alone like a lion.

Essence. Sāmba stood alone like a lion against six warriors. There are moments when conviction means standing without backup; readiness to do so is itself a kind of devotion.

BG 68.7

Determined to capture him, the angry bowmen led by Karṇa shouted at Sāmba, ‘Stand and fight! Stand and fight!’ They came straight for him and showered him with arrows.

Essence. The Kurus shouted 'Stand and fight!' and rained arrows on a single opponent. Mob pressure tries to overwhelm by numbers what cannot be defeated by arguments. Numbers do not make truth.

BG 68.8

O best of the Kurus, as Kṛṣṇa’s son Sāmba was being unjustly harassed by the Kurus, that darling of the Yadu dynasty did not tolerate their attack, any more than a lion would tolerate an attack by puny animals.

Essence. Sāmba would not tolerate unjust attack — a lion does not yield to puny animals. There is dignity in refusing to accept abuse as if it were just. Self-respect is a quiet form of devotion.

BG 68.9-10

Twanging his wonderful bow, heroic Sāmba struck with arrows the six warriors headed by Karṇa. He pierced the six chariots with as many arrows, each team of four horses with four arrows, and each chariot driver with a single arrow, and he similarly struck the great bowmen who commanded the chariots. The enemy warriors congratulated Sāmba for this display of prowess.

Essence. Sāmba fought with such skill that even his enemies congratulated him. Genuine excellence is recognized even by opponents; integrity in performance creates respect across battle lines.

BG 68.11

But they forced him down from his chariot, and thereupon four of them struck his four horses, one of them struck down his chariot driver, and another broke his bow.

Essence. Through coordinated attack, the Kurus dismantled Sāmba piece by piece — horses, charioteer, bow. Even great individual strength can be overwhelmed by organized opposition. We are not meant to fight alone for long.

BG 68.12

Having deprived Sāmba of his chariot during the fight, the Kuru warriors tied him up with great difficulty and then returned victorious to their city, taking the young boy and their princess.

Essence. Sāmba was finally bound and brought to the city as a prisoner. Even the most skilled can be temporarily defeated; defeat is not the final word, only the next scene.

BG 68.13

O King, when the Yādavas heard news of this from Śrī Nārada, they became angry. Urged on by King Ugrasena, they prepared for war against the Kurus.

Essence. Nārada brought the news to the Yādavas, who armed for war. Devotee community responds to harm done to its members — but the response must still be shaped by wise leadership.

BG 68.14-15

Lord Balarāma, however, cooled the tempers of the Vṛṣṇi heroes, who had already put on their armor. He who purifies the age of quarrel did not want a quarrel between the Kurus and Vṛṣṇis. Thus, accompanied by brāhmaṇas and family elders, He went to Hastināpura on His chariot, which was as effulgent as the sun. As He went, He appeared like the moon surrounded by the ruling planets.

Essence. Balarāma cooled the Vṛṣṇi heroes' tempers. The one who purifies Kali Yuga did not want quarrel between Kurus and Vṛṣṇis; He went Himself, dignified as the moon among planets, to seek peace first.

BG 68.16

Upon arriving at Hastināpura, Lord Balarāma remained in a garden outside the city and sent Uddhava ahead to probe King Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s intentions.

Essence. Balarāma camped outside the city and sent Uddhava to probe intentions. Wise diplomacy gathers information before acting; not every entry needs to be a grand entrance.

BG 68.17

After he had offered proper respects to the son of Ambikā [Dhṛtarāṣṭra] and to Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Bāhlika and Duryodhana, Uddhava informed them that Lord Balarāma had arrived.

Essence. Uddhava offered proper respects to elders before delivering the message. Form and protocol still matter; they create the conditions in which serious matters can be heard.

BG 68.18

Overjoyed to hear that Balarāma, their dearmost friend, had come, they first honored Uddhava and then went forth to meet the Lord, carrying auspicious offerings in their hands.

Essence. The Kurus welcomed Uddhava and went out with auspicious gifts to receive Balarāma. They knew the Lord as their well-wisher; surface friendship was real even if it would soon be tested by deeper issues.

BG 68.19

They approached Lord Balarāma and worshiped Him with gifts of cows and arghya, as was fitting. Those among the Kurus who understood His true power bowed down to Him, touching their heads to the ground.

Essence. Those Kurus who understood Balarāma's true power bowed down. Recognition of the Lord's identity makes all the difference; without that recognition, even worship is missing its center.

BG 68.20

After both parties had heard that their relatives were doing well and both had inquired into each other’s welfare and health, Lord Balarāma forthrightly spoke to the Kurus as follows.

Essence. After mutual welfare inquiries, Balarāma spoke directly to the matter at hand. There is a time for pleasantries and a time for clarity; mature relationships make space for both.

BG 68.21

[Lord Balarāma said:] King Ugrasena is our master and the ruler of kings. With undivided attention you should hear what he has ordered you to do, and then you should do it at once.

Essence. Balarāma framed His message as coming from King Ugrasena, not Himself. Speaking under proper authority gives words weight and removes personal ego from the matter.

BG 68.22

[King Ugrasena has said:] Even though by irreligious means several of you defeated a single opponent who follows the religious codes, still I am tolerating this for the sake of unity among family members.

Essence. Ugrasena tolerated the Kurus' unjust victory 'for the sake of family unity.' Sometimes patience over offense is itself a form of leadership — but it must come with a clear request for correction.

BG 68.23

Upon hearing these words of Lord Baladeva’s, which were full of potency, courage and strength and were appropriate to His transcendental power, the Kauravas became furious and spoke as follows.

Essence. Balarāma's words were potent and appropriate, yet the Kauravas grew furious. Even right speech can provoke rage in those bound by pride. The response of others is not always a measure of our truthfulness.

BG 68.24

[The Kuru nobles said:] Oh, how amazing this is! The force of time is indeed insurmountable: a lowly shoe now wants to climb on the head that bears the royal crown.

Essence. The Kurus dismissed the Vṛṣṇis as a 'shoe trying to climb on the head.' Caste-pride and contempt are not new; they reveal more about the speaker than the one spoken of.

BG 68.25

It is because these Vṛṣṇis are bound to us by marital ties that we have granted them equality, allowing them to share our beds, seats and meals. Indeed, it is we who have given them their royal thrones.

Essence. The Kurus framed the Vṛṣṇis' equality as their own gift — beds, seats, meals, thrones, all 'granted' from above. Whenever we mistake shared humanity for our generosity, we have already corrupted the relationship.

BG 68.26

Only because we looked the other way could they enjoy the pair of yak-tail fans and the conchshell, white umbrella, throne, and royal bed.

Essence. The Kurus said they had 'looked the other way' as the Vṛṣṇis used royal symbols. Pretending that tolerance was generosity, while seething inside, is a recipe for sudden ugly outbursts.

BG 68.27

No longer should the Yadus be allowed to use these royal symbols, which now cause trouble for those who gave them, like milk fed to poisonous snakes. Having prospered by our grace, these Yādavas have now lost all shame and are daring to command us!

Essence. The Kurus compared their relationship with the Yadus to feeding milk to poisonous snakes. When we resent the success of those we helped, we have moved from love to ledger-keeping.

BG 68.28

How would even Indra dare usurp anything that Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Arjuna or the other Kurus have not given him? It would be like a lamb claiming the lion’s kill.

Essence. The Kurus boasted that not even Indra could take what they had not given. Pride loves to imagine itself as the source of all sanction. Reality eventually rearranges such delusions.

BG 68.29

Śrī Bādarāyaṇi said: O best of the Bhāratas, after the arrogant Kurus, thoroughly puffed up by the opulence of their high birth and relations, had spoken these harsh words to Lord Balarāma, they turned and went back to their city.

Essence. Puffed up by birth and connections, the Kurus walked away from the conversation. Pride often ends a discussion before the truth has been received; walking away can be a refusal disguised as dignity.

BG 68.30

Seeing the bad character of the Kurus and hearing their nasty words, the infallible Lord Balarāma became filled with rage. His countenance frightful to behold, He laughed repeatedly and spoke as follows.

Essence. Balarāma's countenance became frightful; He laughed repeatedly. The Lord's anger is not the same as ours — it can coexist with laughter, because it is grounded in love for dharma, not threatened ego.

BG 68.31

[Lord Balarāma said:] “Clearly the many passions of these scoundrels have made them so proud that they do not want peace. Then let them be pacified by physical punishment, as animals are with a stick.

Essence. When peace was refused, Balarāma chose physical punishment — 'as animals are with a stick.' Some pride genuinely will not respond to anything but consequence; refusing to act is not always kindness.

BG 68.32-33

“Ah, only gradually was I able to calm the furious Yadus and Lord Kṛṣṇa, who was also enraged. Desiring peace for these Kauravas, I came here. But they are so dull-headed, fond of quarrel and mischievous by nature that they have repeatedly disrespected Me. Out of conceit they dared to address Me with harsh words!

Essence. Balarāma reflected on how patiently He had calmed the Yadus and even Kṛṣṇa, hoping for peace. When patient peace-making is repeatedly mocked, the patient one is justified in escalating.

BG 68.34

“King Ugrasena, the lord of the Bhojas, Vṛṣṇis and Andhakas, is not fit to command, when Indra and other planetary rulers obey his orders?

Essence. Even Indra and the planetary rulers obey Ugrasena's orders. Worldly hierarchies do not reflect the real chain of command. The unseen order of the universe is not what status seekers imagine.

BG 68.35

“That same Kṛṣṇa who occupies the Sudharmā assembly hall and for His enjoyment took the pārijāta tree from the immortal demigods — that very Kṛṣṇa is indeed not fit to sit on a royal throne?

Essence. The Lord who occupies the Sudharmā hall and brought the pārijāta from heaven is dismissed as unfit for a throne? Insult to the Lord's intimates often masks insult to the Lord Himself.

BG 68.36

“The goddess of fortune herself, ruler of the entire universe, worships His feet. And the master of the goddess of fortune does not deserve the paraphernalia of a mortal king?

Essence. Lakṣmī herself worships Kṛṣṇa's feet. The master of the goddess of fortune does not need mortal paraphernalia to be Lord. Our trinkets are not what give Him stature.

BG 68.37

“The dust of Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet, which is the source of holiness for all places of pilgrimage, is worshiped by all the great demigods. The principal deities of all planets are engaged in His service, and they consider themselves most fortunate to take the dust of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa on their crowns. Great demigods like Lord Brahmā and Lord Śiva, and even the goddess of fortune and I, are simply parts of His spiritual identity, and we also carefully carry that dust on our heads. And still Kṛṣṇa is not fit to use the royal insignia or even sit on the royal throne?

Essence. Brahmā, Śiva, Lakṣmī, and Balarāma all carry the dust of Kṛṣṇa's feet on their heads. If they bow with such reverence, the disrespect of pride-blinded men is laughable in its scale.

BG 68.38

“We Vṛṣṇis enjoy only whatever small parcel of land the Kurus allow us? And we are indeed shoes, whereas the Kurus are the head?

Essence. Balarāma sarcastically echoes the Kurus' claim: 'We are shoes, you are the head?' Sometimes mirroring back another's words exposes their absurdity better than argument ever could.

BG 68.39

“Just see how these puffed-up Kurus are intoxicated with their so-called power, like ordinary drunken men! What actual ruler, with the power to command, would tolerate their foolish, nasty words?

Essence. The drunken pride of the Kauravas could not be tolerated by any real ruler. Some forms of folly must finally be answered with firm action; endless tolerance becomes complicity.

BG 68.40

“Today I shall rid the earth of the Kauravas!” declared the furious Balarāma. Thus He took His plow weapon and rose up as if to set the three worlds ablaze.

Essence. 'Today I shall rid the earth of the Kauravas!' Balarāma rose with His plow as if to set the three worlds ablaze. Even the most patient Lord has a limit; mercy and wrath are not opposites in Him.

BG 68.41

The Lord angrily dug up Hastināpura with the tip of His plow and began to drag it, intending to cast the entire city into the Ganges.

Essence. Balarāma dug up Hastināpura with His plow, dragging the city toward the Ganges. The Lord's tools are not symbolic; when He acts, the foundations of pride literally shift.

BG 68.42-43

Seeing that their city was tumbling about like a raft at sea as it was being dragged away, and that it was about to fall into the Ganges, the Kauravas became terrified. To save their lives they approached the Lord for shelter, taking their families with them. Placing Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā in front, they joined their palms in supplication.

Essence. Terrified, the Kauravas approached the Lord for shelter, placing Sāmba and Lakṣmaṇā in front. Crisis often returns us to surrender; what argument could not accomplish, fear sometimes does.

BG 68.44

[The Kauravas said:] O Rāma, Rāma, foundation of everything! We know nothing of Your power. Please excuse our offense, for we are ignorant and misguided.

Essence. 'O Rāma, Rāma, foundation of everything — we know nothing of Your power.' Real prayer often begins with confession of ignorance. Pretending we already know who God is keeps us from meeting Him.

BG 68.45

You alone cause the creation, maintenance and annihilation of the cosmos, and of You there is no prior cause. Indeed, O Lord, authorities say that the worlds are mere playthings for You as You perform Your pastimes.

Essence. The Lord alone causes creation, maintenance and annihilation, and the worlds are His playthings. When we glimpse this scale, our turf-wars look very small.

BG 68.46

O unlimited one of a thousand heads, as Your pastime You carry this earthly globe upon one of Your heads. At the time of annihilation You withdraw the entire universe within Your body and, remaining all alone, lie down to rest.

Essence. Balarāma, with His thousand heads, carries the earth as casual play and withdraws the universe at annihilation. The cosmic weight is no burden to Him; our problems are not too heavy for His attention.

BG 68.47

Your anger is meant for instructing everyone; it is not a manifestation of hatred or envy. O Supreme Lord, You sustain the pure mode of goodness, and You become angry only to maintain and protect this world.

Essence. The Lord's anger is for instruction, not hatred — meant to purify and protect. When divine reproof comes, it is not rejection; it is the most direct form of mercy available.

BG 68.48

We bow down to You, O Soul of all beings, O wielder of all potencies, O tireless maker of the universe! Offering You obeisances, we take shelter of You.

Essence. The Kauravas finally bow: 'O Soul of all beings, we take shelter of You.' Real shelter only becomes possible after pride has been dismantled. Surrender is the doorway, not the obstacle.

BG 68.49

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus propitiated by the Kurus, whose city was trembling and who were surrendering to Him in great distress, Lord Balarāma became very calm and kindly disposed toward them. “Do not be afraid,” He said, and took away their fear.

Essence. Balarāma became calm and removed their fear: 'Do not be afraid.' Once surrender is real, the Lord's protection is immediate. There is no long penalty period; mercy flows freely.

BG 68.50-51

Duryodhana, being very affectionate to his daughter, gave as her dowry 1,200 sixty-year-old elephants, 120,000 horses, 6,000 golden chariots shining like the sun, and 1,000 maidservants with jeweled lockets on their necks.

Essence. Duryodhana, affectionate to his daughter, gave a vast dowry — elephants, horses, chariots, maids. Even those in conflict with the Lord can be tender with their own; love is universal even when narrow.

BG 68.52

The Supreme Lord, chief of the Yādavas, accepted all these gifts and then departed with His son and daughter-in-law as His well-wishers bid Him farewell.

Essence. The Lord accepted the gifts and departed with His son and daughter-in-law. Once the matter was settled, He did not linger to lord it over the defeated. Reconciliation is the goal, not domination.

BG 68.53

Then Lord Halāyudha entered His city [Dvārakā] and met His relatives, whose hearts were all bound to Him in loving attachment. In the assembly hall He reported to the Yadu leaders everything about His dealings with the Kurus.

Essence. Returning to Dvārakā, Balarāma reported everything to the Yadu leaders. Devotee community requires honest reporting of what happened — successes, struggles, and how the Lord acted in between.

BG 68.54

Even today the city of Hastināpura is visibly elevated on its southern side along the Ganges, thus showing the signs of Lord Balarāma’s prowess.

Essence. Even today, Hastināpura's southern side is visibly elevated along the Ganges — the marks of Balarāma's plow remain on the land. The Lord's pastimes leave geographical traces; sacred land is real, not metaphorical.
