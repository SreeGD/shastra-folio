Chapter 63 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 63.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O descendant of Bharata, the relatives of Aniruddha, not seeing Him return, continued to lament as the four rainy months passed.

Essence. Aniruddha's relatives lamented His absence through the four rainy months. Even in a devoted family, separation from a loved one casts a shadow over whole seasons until the truth becomes known.

BG 63.2

After hearing from Nārada the news of Aniruddha’s deeds and His capture, the Vṛṣṇis, who worshiped Lord Kṛṣṇa as their personal Deity, went to Śoṇitapura.

Essence. Nārada brought news of Aniruddha's deeds and capture, and the Vṛṣṇis — worshipers of Kṛṣṇa — set out for Śoṇitapura. Trustworthy news from a saint turns waiting into purposeful action.

BG 63.3-4

With Lord Balarāma and Lord Kṛṣṇa in the lead, the chiefs of the Sātvata clan — Pradyumna, Sātyaki, Gada, Sāmba, Sāraṇa, Nanda, Upananda, Bhadra and others — converged with an army of twelve divisions and laid siege to Bāṇasura’s capital, completely surrounding the city on all sides.

Essence. Led by Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa, the Sātvata chiefs surrounded Bāṇa's city with twelve army divisions. When the Lord's family acts, it acts with full strength and complete commitment — no half measures.

BG 63.5

Bāṇāsura became filled with anger upon seeing them destroy his city’s suburban gardens, ramparts, watchtowers and gateways, and thus he went out to confront them with an army of equal size.

Essence. Bāṇa, enraged at the destruction of his gardens, ramparts and gates, came out with an equal-sized army to confront Kṛṣṇa. Pride confuses the breaking of our outer walls with an attack on us, when it may be a mercy.

BG 63.6

Lord Rudra, accompanied by his son Kārtikeya and the Pramathas, came riding on Nandi, his bull carrier, to fight Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa on Bāṇa’s behalf.

Essence. Lord Rudra, with Kārtikeya and the Pramathas, came on Nandi to fight on Bāṇa's behalf, knowing well Kṛṣṇa's supremacy. Even great souls may take an apparent opposing side to display the Lord's glory.

BG 63.7

A most astonishing, tumultuous and hair-raising battle then commenced, with Lord Kṛṣṇa matched against Lord Śaṅkara, and Pradyumna against Kārtikeya.

Essence. An astonishing, hair-raising battle began — Kṛṣṇa against Śaṅkara, Pradyumna against Kārtikeya. Some scenes in the spiritual world are beyond easy mental categories and must be received with wonder rather than judgment.

BG 63.8

Lord Balarāma fought with Kumbhāṇḍa and Kūpakarṇa, Sāmba with Bāṇa’s son, and Sātyaki with Bāṇa.

Essence. Balarāma fought Kumbhāṇḍa and Kūpakarṇa, Sāmba fought Bāṇa's son, Sātyaki fought Bāṇa. Each devotee is matched with the opponent that fits their strength — Kṛṣṇa never asks us to fight a battle beyond our scale.

BG 63.9

Brahmā and the other ruling demigods, along with Siddhas, Cāraṇas and great sages, as well as Gandharvas, Apsarās and Yakṣas, all came in their celestial airplanes to watch.

Essence. Brahmā, the demigods, Siddhas, Cāraṇas, Gandharvas and Apsarās came in celestial airplanes to watch. The Lord's pastimes draw witnesses from every realm — heaven itself pauses to behold what we often skim past.

BG 63.10-11

With sharp-pointed arrows discharged from His bow Śārṅga, Lord Kṛṣṇa drove away the various followers of Lord Śiva — Bhūtas, Pramathas, Guhyakas, Ḍākinīs, Yātudhānas, Vetālas, Vināyakas, Pretas, Mātās, Piśācas, Kuṣmāṇḍas and Brahma-rākṣasas.

Essence. With sharp arrows from Śārṅga, Kṛṣṇa drove off Śiva's followers — Bhūtas, Pramathas, Ḍākinīs, Yātudhānas, Vetālas, Piśācas. The Lord clears the field of disturbing presences before the central confrontation, just as He clears the heart for direct devotion.

BG 63.12

Lord Śiva, wielder of the trident, shot various weapons at Lord Kṛṣṇa, wielder of Śārṅga. But Lord Kṛṣṇa was not in the least perplexed: He neutralized all these weapons with appropriate counterweapons.

Essence. Śiva launched various weapons; Kṛṣṇa, unperplexed, neutralized each with a counter-weapon. Spiritual mastery is not the absence of attack but the steady, intelligent response to whatever is launched at us.

BG 63.13

Lord Kṛṣṇa counteracted a brahmāstra with another brahmāstra, a wind weapon with a mountain weapon, a fire weapon with a rain weapon, and Lord Śiva’s personal pāśupatāstra weapon with His own personal weapon, the nārāyaṇāstra.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa met brahmāstra with brahmāstra, wind with mountain, fire with rain, pāśupatāstra with nārāyaṇāstra. Wisdom answers each force with its exact remedy — the cure for one excess is not another excess but its precise opposite.

BG 63.14

After bewildering Lord Śiva by making him yawn with a yawning weapon, Lord Kṛṣṇa proceeded to strike down Bāṇāsura’s army with His sword, club and arrows.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa bewildered Śiva with a yawning weapon and then struck down Bāṇa's army with sword, club and arrows. Even great opponents have a moment when their guard drops; the Lord uses such openings with precise effect.

BG 63.15

Lord Kārtikeya was distressed by the flood of Pradyumna’s arrows raining down from all sides, and thus he fled the battlefield on his peacock as blood poured from his limbs.

Essence. Kārtikeya, drenched in blood, fled the battlefield on his peacock under Pradyumna's flood of arrows. Even a great warrior knows when retreat preserves more than persistence destroys.

BG 63.16

Kumbhāṇḍa and Kūpakarṇa, tormented by Lord Balarāma’s club, fell down dead. When the soldiers of these two demons saw that their leaders had been killed, they scattered in all directions.

Essence. Kumbhāṇḍa and Kūpakarṇa fell to Balarāma's club, and their soldiers scattered. When the leaders of negative patterns inside us collapse, the dependent habits often disperse on their own.

BG 63.17

Bāṇāsura was furious to see his entire military force being torn apart. Leaving his fight with Sātyaki, he charged across the battlefield on his chariot and attacked Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Bāṇa, furious at his army's collapse, abandoned his fight with Sātyaki and charged Kṛṣṇa directly. Wounded pride eventually picks the biggest opponent in the room — and so finally faces the Lord.

BG 63.18

Excited to a frenzy by the fighting, Bāṇa simultaneously pulled taut all the strings of his five hundred bows and fixed two arrows on each string.

Essence. Bāṇa simultaneously drew all five hundred of his bows, fixing two arrows on each. The display of overwhelming force is often the last theatre of a pride about to be undone.

BG 63.19

Lord Śrī Hari split every one of Bāṇāsura’s bows simultaneously, and also struck down his chariot driver, chariot and horses. The Lord then sounded His conchshell.

Essence. Hari split every bow of Bāṇa at once, struck down his driver, chariot and horses, and sounded His conchshell. The Lord can dismantle in a single moment what we have built over a lifetime, and announce a new chapter.

BG 63.20

Just then Bāṇāsura’s mother, Koṭarā, desiring to save her son’s life, appeared before Lord Kṛṣṇa naked and with her hair undone.

Essence. Bāṇa's mother Koṭarā appeared naked before Kṛṣṇa to save her son's life. A mother's love will set aside dignity, propriety and self-image to intervene for her child.

BG 63.21

Lord Gadāgraja turned His face away to avoid seeing the naked woman, and Bāṇāsura — deprived of his chariot, his bow shattered — took the opportunity to flee into his city.

Essence. Gadāgraja turned His face away from the naked woman, and Bāṇa fled into his city. The Lord's restraint of His own gaze creates the very space in which His opponent escapes — mercy operates through what He chooses not to do.

BG 63.22

After Lord Śiva’s followers had been driven away, the Śiva-jvara, who had three heads and three feet, pressed forward to attack Lord Kṛṣṇa. As the Śiva-jvara approached, he seemed to burn everything in the ten directions.

Essence. The Śiva-jvara, three-headed and three-footed, pressed forward to attack Kṛṣṇa, burning all directions. Personified fever — fierce, ash-showering — represents the consuming heat of misdirected energy.

BG 63.23

Seeing this personified weapon approach, Lord Nārāyaṇa then released His own personified fever weapon, the Viṣṇu-jvara. The Śiva-jvara and Viṣṇu-jvara thus battled each other.

Essence. Nārāyaṇa released His own personified Viṣṇu-jvara to meet Śiva-jvara, and the two fevers battled. The Lord can place precisely calibrated fire against fire when ordinary remedies will not suffice.

BG 63.24

The Śiva-jvara, overwhelmed by the strength of the Viṣṇu-jvara, cried out in pain. But finding no refuge, the frightened Śiva-jvara approached Lord Kṛṣṇa, the master of the senses, hoping to attain His shelter. Thus with joined palms he began to praise the Lord.

Essence. Overwhelmed by the Viṣṇu-jvara, the Śiva-jvara cried out and, finding no refuge, came to Kṛṣṇa with joined palms — even leaving his own master's side. When suffering becomes severe enough, even loyal followers of other masters reach for the supreme shelter.

BG 63.25

The Śiva-jvara said: I bow down to You of unlimited potencies, the Supreme Lord, the Supersoul of all beings. You possess pure and complete consciousness and are the cause of cosmic creation, maintenance and dissolution. Perfectly peaceful, You are the Absolute Truth to whom the Vedas indirectly refer.

Essence. Śiva-jvara bowed and praised the Lord of unlimited potencies, the Supersoul of all beings, pure consciousness, cause of creation and dissolution, perfectly peaceful, the Absolute Truth indirectly indicated by the Vedas. Once burned, even a fierce force learns to speak the truth of who Kṛṣṇa is.

BG 63.26

Time; fate; karma; the jīva and his propensities; the subtle material elements; the material body; the life air; false ego; the various senses; and the totality of these as reflected in the living being’s subtle body — all this constitutes your material illusory energy, māyā, an endless cycle like that of seed and plant. I take shelter of You, the negation of this māyā.

Essence. Time, fate, karma, the jīva, subtle elements, body, life air, false ego and senses together form māyā — an endless cycle of seed and plant. The Śiva-jvara takes shelter in the Lord who negates this whole machinery.

BG 63.27

With various intentions, You perform pastimes to maintain the demigods, the saintly persons and the codes of religion for this world. By these pastimes You also kill those who stray from the right path and live by violence. Indeed, your present incarnation is meant to relieve the earth’s burden.

Essence. The Lord performs pastimes to maintain demigods, saints and dharma, while killing those who stray and live by violence — His current descent is to relieve the earth's burden. Divine mercy and divine correction are not opposites; they serve a single purpose.

BG 63.28

I am tortured by the fierce power of Your terrible fever weapon, which is cold yet burning. All embodied souls must suffer as long as they remain bound to material ambitions and thus averse to serving Your feet.

Essence. Śiva-jvara confesses he is tortured by Kṛṣṇa's terrible fever weapon — cold yet burning — and acknowledges that embodied souls suffer as long as they remain bound to material ambitions, averse to the Lord's feet. Confession of bondage is the first step toward release.

BG 63.29

The Supreme Lord said: O three-headed one, I am pleased with you. May your fear of My fever weapon be dispelled, and may whoever remembers our conversation here have no reason to fear you.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa accepted the Śiva-jvara and granted that whoever remembers their conversation need have no fear of him. The Lord turns a defeated weapon into a protective blessing for His listeners.

BG 63.30

Thus addressed, the Māheśvara-jvara bowed down to the infallible Lord and went away. But Bāṇāsura then appeared, riding forth on his chariot to fight Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. The Māheśvara-jvara bowed and departed, and Bāṇa reappeared on his chariot to fight again. Some inner battles return for one more round even after their supporters have surrendered.

BG 63.31

Carrying numerous weapons in his thousand hands, O King, the terribly infuriated demon shot many arrows at Lord Kṛṣṇa, the carrier of the disc weapon.

Essence. With weapons in his thousand hands, the infuriated Bāṇa shot many arrows at Kṛṣṇa, the disc-bearer. When pride is wounded, it brings every available resource to bear at once — but always against the Lord.

BG 63.32

As Bāṇa continued hurling weapons at Him, the Supreme Lord began using His razor-sharp cakra to cut off Bāṇāsura’s arms as if they were tree branches.

Essence. The Lord used His razor-sharp cakra to cut off Bāṇa's arms as if they were tree branches. Kṛṣṇa does not destroy the demon but removes the instruments of his arrogance — sometimes mercy looks like loss.

BG 63.33

Lord Śiva felt compassion for his devotee Bāṇāsura, whose arms were being cut off, and thus he approached Lord Cakrāyudha [Kṛṣṇa] and spoke to Him as follows.

Essence. Lord Śiva, feeling compassion for his devotee whose arms were being severed, approached Kṛṣṇa to speak on Bāṇa's behalf. A true spiritual master intercedes for a follower even when the follower is being humbled.

BG 63.34

Śrī Rudra said: You alone are the Absolute Truth, the supreme light, the mystery hidden within the verbal manifestation of the Absolute. Those whose hearts are spotless can see You, for You are uncontaminated, like the sky.

Essence. Rudra praised Kṛṣṇa as the Absolute Truth, supreme light, mystery hidden within Vedic words, visible only to spotless hearts. Even Śiva — among the greatest — acknowledges that direct vision of Kṛṣṇa requires purity within.

BG 63.35-36

The sky is Your navel, fire Your face, water Your semen, and heaven Your head. The cardinal directions are Your sense of hearing, herbal plants the hairs on Your body, and water-bearing clouds the hair on Your head. The earth is Your foot, the moon Your mind, and the sun Your vision, while I am Your ego. The ocean is Your abdomen, Indra Your arm, Lord Brahmā Your intelligence, the progenitor of mankind Your genitals, and religion Your heart. You are indeed the original puruṣa, creator of the worlds.

Essence. Śiva describes the cosmic body of the Lord — sky as navel, fire as face, water as semen, sun as vision, ocean as abdomen, religion as heart — declaring Him the original puruṣa. Every visible feature of creation is a clue to its single Lord.

BG 63.37

Your current descent into the material realm, O Lord of unrestricted power, is meant for upholding the principles of justice and benefiting the entire universe. We demigods, each depending on Your grace and authority, develop the seven planetary systems.

Essence. Śiva acknowledges that Kṛṣṇa's descent is for upholding justice and benefiting the universe, with the demigods developing the seven planetary systems by His grace. All cosmic administration is downstream of one source.

BG 63.38

You are the original person, one without a second, transcendental and self-manifesting. Uncaused, you are the cause of all, and You are the ultimate controller. You are nonetheless perceived in terms of the transformations of matter effected by Your illusory energy — transformations You sanction so that the various material qualities can fully manifest.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa is the original person, one without a second, uncaused cause and ultimate controller, perceived through the transformations of māyā which He sanctions. The Lord is both beyond all and the secret enabler of what is happening here.

BG 63.39

O almighty one, just as the sun, though hidden by a cloud, illuminates the cloud and all other visible forms as well, so You, although hidden by the material qualities, remain self-luminous and thus reveal all those qualities, along with the living entities who possess them.

Essence. Like the sun illuminating the very cloud that veils it, the Lord, though hidden by the material qualities, remains self-luminous and reveals them all. Even what conceals Him exists only because He shines.

BG 63.40

Their intelligence bewildered by Your māyā, fully attached to children, wife, home and so on, persons immersed in the ocean of material misery sometimes rise to the surface and sometimes sink down.

Essence. Bewildered by māyā, attached to children, wife and home, beings rise and sink in the ocean of misery. Spiritual life begins by acknowledging that even our most cherished attachments are part of a turbulent current.

BG 63.41

One who has attained this human form of life as a gift from God, yet who fails to control his senses and honor Your feet, is surely to be pitied, for he is only cheating himself.

Essence. One who attains human life as God's gift yet fails to control the senses and honor the Lord's feet is pitiable — cheating only himself. The human form is the doorway; refusal to use it is a quiet self-betrayal.

BG 63.42

That mortal who rejects You — his true Self, dearmost friend, and Lord — for the sake of sense objects, whose nature is just the opposite, refuses nectar and instead consumes poison.

Essence. The mortal who rejects the Lord — true Self, dearest friend — for sense objects opposite in nature is refusing nectar to drink poison. The framing of choice is stark when seen through Śiva's eyes.

BG 63.43

I, Lord Brahmā, the other demigods and the pure-minded sages have all surrendered wholeheartedly unto You, our dearmost Self and Lord.

Essence. Śiva confesses that he, Brahmā, the other demigods and pure-minded sages have all wholeheartedly surrendered to the Lord as dearest Self. The greatest souls model surrender so we will not consider it beneath us.

BG 63.44

Let us worship You, the Supreme Lord, to be freed from material life. You are the maintainer of the universe and the cause of its creation and demise. Equipoised and perfectly at peace, You are the true friend, Self and worshipable Lord. You are one without a second, the shelter of all the worlds and all souls.

Essence. Śiva proposes worshiping the Lord — maintainer of the universe, equipoised, perfectly peaceful, true friend, Self and worshipable refuge — to be freed from material life. Worship of Kṛṣṇa is not one option among many; it is the path out.

BG 63.45

This Bāṇāsura is my dear and faithful follower, and I have awarded him freedom from fear. Therefore, my Lord, please grant him Your mercy, just as You showed mercy to Prahlāda, the lord of the demons.

Essence. Śiva pleads that Bāṇa is his dear faithful follower, granted fearlessness by him, and asks for the same mercy Kṛṣṇa once showed Prahlāda. A great soul leverages his own surrender to win mercy for those who depend on him.

BG 63.46

The Supreme Lord said: My dear lord, for your pleasure We must certainly do what you have requested of Us. I fully agree with your conclusion.

Essence. The Lord agreed: 'For your pleasure We must certainly do as you have asked.' The Supreme is delighted to honor the requests of a pure devotee, even sharing the dignity of address.

BG 63.47

I will not kill this demonic son of Vairocani, for I gave Prahlāda Mahārāja the benediction that I would not kill any of his descendants.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa promised not to kill the demonic son of Vairocani because He had given Prahlāda the benediction not to kill any of his descendants. Promises made to pure devotees extend protection across generations.

BG 63.48

It was to subdue Bāṇāsura’s false pride that I severed his arms. And I slew his mighty army because it had become a burden upon the earth.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa explains He cut off Bāṇa's arms to subdue false pride and slew his army because it had become a burden upon the earth. Divine action is precise, never gratuitous — it addresses a specific spiritual disease.

BG 63.49

This demon, who still has four arms, will be immune to old age and death, and he will serve as one of your principal attendants. Thus he will have nothing to fear on any account.

Essence. Bāṇa, retaining four arms, would become immune to old age and death and serve as one of Śiva's chief attendants — nothing to fear. A demon surrendered becomes an eternal associate; humiliation can be the start of a permanent role.

BG 63.50

Thus attaining freedom from fear, Bāṇāsura offered obeisances to Lord Kṛṣṇa by touching his head to the ground. Bāṇa then seated Aniruddha and His bride on their chariot and brought them before the Lord.

Essence. Freed from fear, Bāṇa touched his head to the ground before Kṛṣṇa and brought Aniruddha and His bride to the Lord. The defeated father-in-law presents the very couple he tried to separate — fear gone, relationships restored.

BG 63.51

At the front of the party Lord Kṛṣṇa then placed Aniruddha and His bride, both beautifully adorned with fine clothes and ornaments, and surrounded them with a full military division. Thus Lord Kṛṣṇa took His leave of Lord Śiva and departed.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa placed Aniruddha and His bride at the front of the party, surrounded by a full military division, and took leave of Śiva. After conflict resolves, the Lord still travels with full ceremony and protection — peace does not mean letting down all guard.

BG 63.52

The Lord then entered His capital. The city was lavishly decorated with flags and victory arches, and its avenues and crossways were all sprinkled with water. As conchshells, ānakas and dundubhi drums resounded, the Lord’s relatives, the brāhmaṇas and the general populace all came forward to greet Him respectfully.

Essence. The Lord entered Dvārakā amid flags, victory arches, sprinkled streets and the sound of conches and drums, greeted by relatives, brāhmaṇas and citizens. Communal celebration of the Lord's victories binds a society together.

BG 63.53

Whoever rises early in the morning and remembers Lord Kṛṣṇa’s victory in His battle with Lord Śiva will never experience defeat.

Essence. Whoever rises early and remembers Kṛṣṇa's victory over Śiva will never be defeated. The chapter ends by handing the reader a portable shelter — morning remembrance — anchored in this very pastime.
