Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.30.30

तस्या अमूनि न: क्षोभं कुर्वन्त्युच्चै: पदानि यत् यैकापहृत्य गोपीनां रहो भुङ्क्तेऽच्युताधरम् । न लक्ष्यन्ते पदान्यत्र तस्या नूनं तृणाङ्कुरै: खिद्यत्सुजाताङ्‍‍‍‍‍घ्रितलामुन्निन्ये प्रेयसीं प्रिय: ॥ ३० ॥

Important Words
  • tasyāḥ — of Her
    Genitive of feminine demonstrative. The chapter's twelfth or thirteenth oblique reference to the one-not-named. Gauḍīyas (Viśvanātha Cakravartī) read this systematic pronominal hiding as the Bhāgavatam's literary practice of Rādhā-saṅgopana — protective concealment.
  • rahaḥ-bhuṅkte — in seclusion She enjoys
    Phrase: rahaḥ (seclusion) + bhuṅkte (enjoys, present middle of √bhuj). Bhuj here means erotic enjoyment, not eating. The gopīs name the unnameable — She is at this moment enjoying His lips. Pain and theological precision in one word.
  • acyuta-adharam — the lips of Kṛṣṇa
    Compound: acyuta (the Infallible) + adhara (lower lip). The Bhāgavatam pairs the absolute name with the most physical anga — Vraja-rasa's paradox in three syllables. Bhagavān is here, and Bhagavān's lips are touchable.
  • sujāta-aṅghri-talām — whose tender foot-soles
    Compound: sujāta (well-formed, tender) + aṅghri (foot) + tala (sole). The gopīs imagine Her soles cut by the grass — and recognize, in their own bodies, why He lifted Her. The detail is both forensic and devotional.
  • preyasīm-priyaḥ — His dear beloved (lifted by her) dear one
    Phrase: preyasī (most-beloved-female) + priyaḥ (the Beloved-male). Two superlatives of the same root √prī, brought together. Rūpa Gosvāmī's Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi treats this preyasī-priyaḥ pairing as the very definition of mādhurya-rasa.
These footprints of that special gopī greatly disturb us. Of all the gopīs, She alone was taken away to a secluded place, where She is enjoying the lips of Kṛṣṇa. Look, we can’t see Her footprints over here! It’s obvious that the grass and sprouts were hurting the tender soles of Her feet, and so the lover lifted up His beloved.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
These footprints of that special gopī greatly disturb us. Of all the gopīs, She alone was taken away to a secluded place, where She is enjoying the lips of Kṛṣṇa. Look, we can't see Her footprints over here! It's obvious that the grass and sprouts were hurting the tender soles of Her feet, and so the lover lifted up His beloved.
Commentary

In this verse, the opposing group of gopis (prati paksa) speak. "The footprints of this woman give us great sorrow. She has stolen the lips of Acyuta which should be enjoyed by all of us gopis, and is drinking those lips by herself alone. Bringing Krsna under control by some unknown pious acts, she has made him reject us, who are filled with prema, and taken him far away."

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The gopīs in the opposing group speak. These footprints cause us great (ucchaiḥ) sorrow. She took him away for private enjoyment. He has given us up and come here because she has used magic powers to bring him here, acting like a thief. Otherwise why should he come here? She enjoys his lips which belong to all of us. Another version has dhanam (treasure) instead of raho. Śrīdhara Svāmī explains dhanam as meaning “all in all.” But raho is the commonest version. She enjoys the lips of the person who does not give her up and go elsewhere (acyuta). Out of envy they surmise that Kṛṣṇa lifted her up. That is Śrīdhara Svāmī’s opinion. They saw his foot prints without hers. Or the second verse can be spoken by Rādhā’s friends. Her tender feet were injured by the sprouts. Thus the lover lifted up the dearest gopī using his hands.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The friends of Candrāvalī speak. The footprints cause us great (ucchaiḥ) pain, or make us lose our composure, because of what was said about them. Or the footprints being offered on our heads with pride give us pain. She has taken him alone for private enjoyment. Or since she stole him, he has given up all the gopīs (generally) and come here. Otherwise why would he come here? She enjoys his lips which belong to all of us. Another version has dhanam (treasure) instead of raho. Śrīdhara Svāmī explains dhanam as meaning “all in all.” But raho is the commonest version. She enjoys the lips of the person who does not give her up and go elsewhere (acyuta). Or she enjoys the lips of he who does not give up that enjoyment. Or she enjoys the lips which were enjoyed by us already (cyuta). Or should those footprints which by passed us (ucchaiḥ) cause us disturbance? No. This is a statement made by all of them. He has lifted up this most loved one on his shoulders. This is understood from verse 39. They say this to slander her. All of them are dear to Kṛṣṇa but among all of them, he has affection for her the most. Therefore lifting her up is to be expected. Or he lifted her with both hands onto his lap. By embraces etc. it is understood that she was the dearest.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

A reader of paths can tell where a man bent to lift a child — the prints deepen, the stride shortens, and then suddenly only one pair continues. The gopīs read the dust and grow more agitated: Her prints stop here — the grass cut Her tender soles, He lifted Her into His arms. The Bhāgavatam's most heartbreaking forensics.
