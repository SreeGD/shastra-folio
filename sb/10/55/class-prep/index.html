Chapter 55 · Class prep

BG 55.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Kāmadeva [Cupid], an expansion of Vāsudeva, had previously been burned to ashes by Rudra’s anger. Now, to obtain a new body, he merged back into the body of Lord Vāsudeva.

Essence. Kāmadeva (Cupid), an expansion of Vāsudeva, previously burnt by Rudra's anger, merges back into Vāsudeva to obtain a new body. Even celestial figures must re-enter source to receive new form; renewal goes through Lord.

BG 55.2

He took birth in the womb of Vaidarbhī from the seed of Lord Kṛṣṇa and received the name Pradyumna. In no respect was He inferior to His father.

Essence. He takes birth in Vaidarbhī's womb from Kṛṣṇa's seed, named Pradyumna, in no respect inferior to His father. The son who carries his father's full quality is born from specific union; lineage matters and faithfully transmits.

BG 55.3

The demon Śambara, who could assume any form he desired, kidnapped the infant before He was even ten days old. Understanding Pradyumna to be his enemy, Śambara threw Him into the sea and then returned home.

Essence. Śambara, shape-shifting demon, kidnaps the infant before ten days old and throws Him into the sea. Demons time their attacks to the most vulnerable phase; what is precious can be stolen before defenses form.

BG 55.4

A powerful fish swallowed Pradyumna, and this fish, along with others, was caught in a huge net and seized by fishermen.

Essence. A powerful fish swallows Pradyumna; the fish is caught with others in a huge net by fishermen. Even thrown to sea, divine plan continues; the fish that swallows is itself caught by the net.

BG 55.5

The fishermen presented that extraordinary fish to Śambara, who had his cooks bring it to the kitchen, where they began cutting it up with a butcher knife.

Essence. Fishermen present the extraordinary fish to Śambara, whose cooks bring it to the kitchen and cut it with a butcher knife. The lost child returns to the very enemy's house; recovery moves through the heart of opposition.

BG 55.6

Seeing a male child in the belly of the fish, the cooks gave the infant to Māyāvatī, who was astonished. Nārada Muni then appeared and explained to her everything about the child’s birth and His entering the fish’s abdomen.

Essence. The cooks see a male child in the fish's belly and give Him to Māyāvatī, who is astonished; Nārada appears and explains the child's birth and his entering the fish. Even cooks deliver divine work; great mysteries unfold via ordinary hands and sage explanation.

BG 55.7-8

Māyāvatī was in fact Cupid’s renowned wife, Rati. While waiting for her husband to obtain a new body — his previous one having been burnt up — she had been assigned by Śambara to prepare vegetables and rice. Māyāvatī understood that this infant was actually Kāmadeva, and thus she began to feel love for Him.

Essence. Māyāvatī is actually Cupid's wife Rati, awaiting her husband's new body; assigned by Śambara to prepare vegetables and rice, she understands this is her husband. The waiting wife recognizes the new form of her returning beloved.

BG 55.9

After a short time, this son of Kṛṣṇa — Pradyumna — attained His full youth. He enchanted all women who gazed upon Him.

Essence. In a short time Pradyumna attains full youth, enchanting all who gaze upon Him. Rapid maturation of divine forms is normal; the lost child becomes the attractive young man.

BG 55.10

My dear King, with a bashful smile and raised eyebrows, Māyāvatī exhibited various gestures of conjugal attraction as she lovingly approached her husband, whose eyes were broad like the petals of a lotus, whose arms were very long and who was the most beautiful of men.

Essence. With bashful smile and raised eyebrows, Māyāvatī shows gestures of conjugal attraction to the lotus-eyed, long-armed Pradyumna — even before revealing identities. The reunion begins with confusing affection; love seeks form before category.

BG 55.11

Lord Pradyumna told her, “O mother, your attitude has changed. You are overstepping the proper feelings of a mother and behaving like a lover.”

Essence. Pradyumna says: O mother, your attitude has changed; you are overstepping the proper feelings of a mother and behaving like a lover. The young hero confronts the confusing intimacy directly; honesty about category mismatch is part of dharma.

BG 55.12

Rati said: You are the son of Lord Nārāyaṇa and were kidnapped from Your parents’ home by Śambara. I, Rati, am Your legitimate wife, O master, because You are Cupid.

Essence. Rati says: You are Nārāyaṇa's son kidnapped from home; I, Rati, am Your legitimate wife because You are Cupid. Identity reveal corrects category; the truth restores proper relationship.

BG 55.13

That demon, Śambara, threw You into the sea when You were not even ten days old, and a fish swallowed You. Then in this very place we recovered You from the fish’s abdomen, O master.

Essence. Śambara threw You into the sea at under ten days; a fish swallowed You; in this place we recovered You from its belly. The whole story is given to the hero; he learns his own backstory from his consort.

BG 55.14

Now kill this dreadful Śambara, Your formidable enemy. Although he knows hundreds of magic spells, You can defeat him with bewildering magic and other techniques.

Essence. Now kill this dreadful Śambara, Your formidable enemy; though he knows hundreds of spells, You can defeat him with bewildering magic and other techniques. Mission follows backstory; action equipped with technique.

BG 55.15

Your poor mother, having lost her son, cries for You like a kurarī bird. She is overwhelmed with love for her child, just like a cow that has lost its calf.

Essence. Your poor mother, having lost her son, cries for You like a kurarī bird, overwhelmed with love like a cow that has lost its calf. The wife reminds the hero of the mother's grief; love includes remembering both sides.

BG 55.16

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued:] Speaking thus, Māyāvatī gave to the great soul Pradyumna the mystic knowledge called Mahā-māyā, which vanquishes all other deluding spells.

Essence. Māyāvatī gives Pradyumna the mystic knowledge Mahā-māyā, which vanquishes all other deluding spells. Weapons are knowledge in spiritual combat; the right consort transmits the right knowledge.

BG 55.17

Pradyumna approached Śambara and called him to battle, hurling intolerable insults at him to foment a conflict.

Essence. Pradyumna approaches Śambara, calls him to battle, hurls intolerable insults to foment conflict. Strategic provocation can be part of a just confrontation; speech is initiating tactic.

BG 55.18

Offended by these harsh words, Śambara became as agitated as a kicked snake. He came out, club in hand, his eyes red with rage.

Essence. Offended, Śambara becomes agitated as a kicked snake, comes out with club, eyes red with rage. Insults precisely calibrated bring out what hiding would have concealed.

BG 55.19

Śambara whirled his club swiftly about and then hurled it at the wise Pradyumna, producing a sound as sharp as a thunder crack.

Essence. Śambara whirls his club and hurls it producing a thunder-crack sound. Demonic combat features dramatic noise; intimidation by sound precedes physical strike.

BG 55.20

As Śambara’s club came flying toward Him, Lord Pradyumna knocked it away with His own. Then, O King, Pradyumna angrily threw His club at the enemy.

Essence. Pradyumna knocks the flying club away with His own and angrily throws His club at the enemy. Counter-strike with the same weapon; the hero meets the form on its own terms.

BG 55.21

Resorting to the black magic of the Daityas taught to him by Maya Dānava, Śambara suddenly appeared in the sky and released a downpour of weapons upon Kṛṣṇa’s son.

Essence. Resorting to Daitya black magic taught by Maya Dānava, Śambara appears in the sky and releases a downpour of weapons on Kṛṣṇa's son. Demonic enemies escalate to magical means; conventional defense isn't enough.

BG 55.22

Harassed by this rain of weapons, Lord Raukmiṇeya, the greatly powerful warrior, made use of the mystic science called Mahā-māyā, which was created from the mode of goodness and which could defeat all other mystic power.

Essence. Harassed by the weapons-rain, Raukmiṇeya uses Mahā-māyā — created from goodness — which defeats all other mystic power. Goodness-rooted technique outclasses dark-magic technique; quality of source matters.

BG 55.23

The demon then unleashed hundreds of mystic weapons belonging to the Guhyakas, Gandharvas, Piśācas, Uragas and Rākṣasas, but Lord Kārṣṇi, Pradyumna, struck them all down.

Essence. Śambara unleashes hundreds of mystic weapons of Guhyakas, Gandharvas, Piśācas, Uragas, Rākṣasas; Kārṣṇi strikes them all down. When a demon empties the entire arsenal of dark beings, the hero still strikes them all down.

BG 55.24

Drawing His sharp-edged sword, Pradyumna forcefully cut off Śambara’s head, complete with red mustache, helmet and earrings.

Essence. Drawing His sharp sword, Pradyumna forcefully cuts off Śambara's head — complete with red mustache, helmet, earrings. Decisive ending names specific decorative details; complete severance is recorded.

BG 55.25

As the residents of the higher planets showered Pradyumna with flowers and chanted His praises, His wife appeared in the sky and transported Him through the heavens, back to the city of Dvārakā.

Essence. Higher planet residents shower flowers and chant praises; his wife appears in the sky and transports Him through the heavens back to Dvārakā. Victory leads to homeward flight; wife carries hero through the heavens.

BG 55.26

O King, Lord Pradyumna and His wife resembled a cloud accompanied by lightning as they descended from the sky into the inner quarters of Kṛṣṇa’s most excellent palace, which were crowded with lovely women.

Essence. Like a cloud accompanied by lightning, Pradyumna and his wife descend from the sky into the inner quarters of Kṛṣṇa's palace, crowded with lovely women. Heroic arrivals descend to mundane spaces; cosmic flight ends in domestic settings.

BG 55.27-28

The women of the palace thought He was Lord Kṛṣṇa when they saw His dark-blue complexion the color of a rain cloud, His yellow silk garments, His long arms and red-tinged eyes, His charming lotus face adorned with a pleasing smile, His fine ornaments and His thick, curly blue hair. Thus the women became bashful and hid themselves here and there.

Essence. Palace women think He is Kṛṣṇa — dark-blue, yellow silk, long arms, red-tinged eyes, charming lotus face. Father-son resemblance can be mistaken for identity; the family quality is unmistakable.

BG 55.29

Gradually, from the slight differences between His appearance and Kṛṣṇa’s, the ladies realized He was not the Lord. Delighted and astonished, they approached Pradyumna and His consort, who was a jewel among women.

Essence. From slight differences they realize He isn't the Lord; delighted and astonished, they approach Pradyumna and His consort. Discernment of small differences matters; the family resemblance is not identity.

BG 55.30

Seeing Pradyumna, sweet-voiced, dark-eyed Rukmiṇī remembered her lost son, and her breasts became moist out of affection.

Essence. Sweet-voiced, dark-eyed Rukmiṇī sees Pradyumna, remembers her lost son, and her breasts become moist with affection. The body responds to recognition before the mind confirms it.

BG 55.31

[Śrīmatī Rukmiṇī-devī said:] Who is this lotus-eyed jewel among men? What man’s son is He, and what woman carried Him in her womb? And who is this woman He has taken as His wife?

Essence. Who is this lotus-eyed jewel among men? What man's son is He, and what woman carried Him? Who is His wife? Specific questions accompany recognition; even the certain mother asks.

BG 55.32

If my lost son, who was kidnapped from the maternity room, were still alive somewhere, He would be of the same age and appearance as this young man.

Essence. If my lost son were still alive somewhere, He would be of the same age and appearance as this young man. The mother does the math; love calculates discreetly.

BG 55.33

But how is it that this young man so much resembles my own Lord, Kṛṣṇa, the wielder of Śārṅga, in His bodily form and His limbs, in His gait and the tone of His voice, and in His smiling glance?

Essence. He so much resembles my Lord Kṛṣṇa in form, limbs, gait, voice, smiling glance. The mother reads specific features; love is in the details.

BG 55.34

Yes, He must be the same child I bore in my womb, since I feel great affection for Him and my left arm is quivering.

Essence. He must be the child I bore — I feel great affection for Him, and my left arm is quivering. Body signs (left arm quivering) plus emotional response confirm identity; mature devotion trusts both signals.

BG 55.35

As Queen Rukmiṇī conjectured in this way, Lord Kṛṣṇa, the son of Devakī, arrived on the scene with Vasudeva and Devakī.

Essence. As she conjectures, Kṛṣṇa arrives with Vasudeva and Devakī. The Lord appears at the climax of recognition; arrivals are timed to conclusions.

BG 55.36

Although Lord Janārdana knew perfectly well what had transpired, He remained silent. The sage Nārada, however, explained everything, beginning with Śambara’s kidnapping of the child.

Essence. Janārdana knows all but remains silent; Nārada explains everything from Śambara's kidnapping. The Lord lets others narrate; sometimes the right speaker is the sage, not the Lord Himself.

BG 55.37

When the women of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s palace heard this most amazing account, they joyfully greeted Pradyumna, who had been lost for many years but who had now returned as if from the dead.

Essence. Hearing the amazing account, the palace women joyfully greet Pradyumna — lost for years and now returned as if from death. Communal reception of restored ones is part of the homecoming.

BG 55.38

Devakī, Vasudeva, Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma and all the women of the palace, especially Queen Rukmiṇī, embraced the young couple and rejoiced.

Essence. Devakī, Vasudeva, Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma and all the women — especially Rukmiṇī — embrace the young couple and rejoice. The whole multigenerational family gathers around the recovered son.

BG 55.39

Hearing that lost Pradyumna had come home, the residents of Dvārakā declared, “Ah, providence has allowed this child to return as if from death!”

Essence. Dvārakā's residents declare: 'Ah, providence has allowed this child to return as if from death!' Public commentary frames the event theologically; the city acknowledges providence by name.

BG 55.40

It is not astonishing that the palace women, who should have felt maternal affection for Pradyumna, privately felt ecstatic attraction for Him as if He were their own Lord. After all, the son exactly resembled His father. Indeed, Pradyumna was a perfect reflection of the beauty of Lord Kṛṣṇa, the shelter of the goddess of fortune, and appeared before their eyes as Cupid Himself. Since even those on the level of His mother felt conjugal attraction for Him, then what to speak of how other women felt when they saw Him?

Essence. It is not astonishing that the palace women felt ecstatic attraction for Pradyumna as if He were their own Lord — He exactly resembles His father. Maternal feeling and conjugal feeling can become confused when likeness is exact.
