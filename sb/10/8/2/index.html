Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.8.2

Important Words
  • pratyutthāya — standing up to receive him
    Gerund of prati-ud-sthā — 'rising up toward'. Vaiṣṇava-sadācāra prescribes physical rising the moment a sādhu appears. The body's motion trains the mind: pride cannot sit while the legs already stand.
  • kṛta - añjaliḥ — with folded hands
    Añjali (cupped palms) is the universal Vedic gesture of surrender. Joined hands resemble a closed lotus offered upward — the heart given before the speech. Gauḍīyas join this with daṇḍavat to enact ātma-nikṣepa.
  • ānarca — worshiped
    Reduplicated perfect of √arc — 'worshipped (and the act stands complete)'. Not a casual greeting; Nanda actually performs arcana-like reception, treating the sādhu as Bhagavān's mūrti.
  • adhokṣaja - dhiyā — with a vision perceiving the transcendental
    Adhokṣaja = 'that which is beyond (adhaḥ) the senses (akṣa) and their downward reach (ja)'. Dhī = sober intelligence. The verse's central word: Nanda's eyes see a body, but his buddhi sees Kṛṣṇa's representative. This is the rejection of pratyakṣa-only epistemology that Gauḍīyas press against empiricism.
  • praṇipāta - puraḥsaram — preceded by falling down in obeisance
    Praṇipāta (full prostration) puraḥsaram (with X going in front). The daṇḍavat goes first; only then comes praśna and sevā — the order Kṛṣṇa Himself prescribes in Gītā 4.34 (tad viddhi praṇipātena).
When Nanda Mahārāja saw Garga Muni present at his home, Nanda was so pleased that he stood up to receive him with folded hands. Although seeing Garga Muni with his eyes, Nanda Mahārāja could appreciate that Garga Muni was adhokṣaja; that is, he was not an ordinary person seen by material senses.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
When Nanda Mahārāja saw Gargamuni present at his home, Nanda was so pleased that he stood up to receive him with folded hands. Although seeing Gargamuni with his eyes, Nanda Mahārāja could appreciate that Gargamuni was adhokṣaja; that is, he was not an ordinary person seen by material senses.
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Nanda worshipped Garga with devotion, as if he were the Supreme Lord (adhikṣaja-dhiyā).

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Nanda worshipped Garga with devotion, as if he were the Supreme Lord (adhokṣaja-dhiyā), who is beyond knowledge of the senses. This means he thought of Garga as a great devotee. Adhokṣaja also means he who was as if born again under the axel when the cart fell. After that incident he was called adhokṣaja. Nanda thinking of Kṛṣṇa as his son, out of attachment, thinking of his safety, worshipped Garga. He offered respects on the ground, or with great humility in front of him.

Personal Guidance
True reception of a sādhu requires adhokṣaja-dṛṣṭi — seeing past the material covering to the transcendental servant of Kṛṣṇa within.
Practical Applications
  • When you next meet a Vaiṣṇava or your guru, physically stand, fold your hands, and offer praṇāma — let the body train the consciousness.
  • Before any darśana or saṅga, pause thirty seconds and consciously reject the urge to evaluate the devotee by mundane criteria (age, education, background).
  • Keep one photograph of your dīkṣā-guru or a senior Vaiṣṇava on your desk; offer a silent obeisance each morning before opening work.
Reflection Questions
  • When a sādhu or senior Vaiṣṇava enters my space, what does my first inward reaction reveal about whose 'representative' I actually take them to be?
  • In what specific situations do I let the external covering — age, appearance, social standing, mood — eclipse my adhokṣaja-dhī toward guru and devotees?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Adhokṣaja-dhiyā is essentially the same skill you already use when you look past someone's Instagram profile to who they really are — except here you are training yourself to look past the body to the soul and the soul's connection to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Standing up when an elder Vaiṣṇava enters the room is not an outdated custom your parents are imposing; it is a precise spiritual technology that rewires the ego in seconds — try it once consciously and observe what shifts inside you.
  • Nanda Mahārāja was wealthy, respected, and head of his community, yet he rose immediately for Garga Muni — real strength and confidence are shown by whom you are willing to bow to, not by whom you refuse to bow to.
householder
  • Your home is a tīrtha when a sādhu enters it; arrange your gṛhastha-āśrama — the cleanliness of the entrance, the āsana you offer, the prasāda you serve — so that your family witnesses pratyutthāna and kṛtāñjali as the natural protocol of the household.
  • Nanda balanced his responsibilities as Vraja's chief and a father with immediate, unhesitating reverence to a visiting brāhmaṇa-sādhu — your worldly position never exempts you from standing up; rather, it makes your humility more instructive to those under your care.
  • Train your children by example: when they see you fold your hands and offer obeisances before exchanging any words with a sādhu, they absorb adhokṣaja-dhiyā as a lived family culture rather than a doctrine you preach to them.
senior
  • With age, the body resists rising and bending — yet even the gesture of attempting pratyutthāna, supported by a chair or hand, carries the full weight of Nanda's mood and is honored by Kṛṣṇa accordingly.
  • Years of association with devotees may tempt familiarity that erodes reverence; the senior Vaiṣṇava's discipline is to greet each visiting sādhu with the same freshness of adhokṣaja-dhiyā as the very first time, refusing the subtle pride of seniority.
  • As a senior, your manner of receiving sādhus is now itself a teaching to younger devotees in the room — your folded hands and lowered head transmit varṇāśrama-dharma and Vaiṣṇava etiquette more powerfully than any class you could give.
Common Pitfalls
  • Performing elaborate outward reception of sādhus while inwardly evaluating them by mundane criteria like learning, charisma, or institutional rank.
  • Reserving adhokṣaja-dhī only for famous or initiating gurus while treating ordinary visiting Vaiṣṇavas as social guests to be managed.
  • Mistaking emotional excitement at meeting a devotee for the sober, reverential vision Nanda Mahārāja showed through praṇipāta and folded hands.
Discussion Prompts
  • Nanda Mahārāja perceived Garga Muni with adhokṣaja-dhiyā — vision attuned to that which is beyond sense perception. How do we cultivate this dual sight that honors both the human form of a sādhu and his transcendental position as a representative of Kṛṣṇa?
  • Nanda stood up, folded his hands, and offered praṇāma before any words were exchanged. What does this sequence of bodily reverence teach us about the role of physical etiquette (vaiṣṇava-sadācāra) in awakening inner bhāva?
  • Garga Muni was the family priest (purohita) of the Yadu dynasty, fulfilling a varṇāśrama duty, yet Nanda received him as adhokṣaja. How do we balance honoring a sādhu's social/āśrama position with recognizing his deeper spiritual identity?
Role Play

You are a gṛhastha living in a city apartment. A senior Vaiṣṇava sannyāsī whom you have never met personally, but who is the godbrother of your dīkṣā-guru, sends word that he will visit your home tomorrow afternoon for prasāda. Walk through how you would prepare your home, your family, and your own consciousness — from the cleaning of the entrance and āsana, to instructing your children, to the moment of his arrival when you open the door. Show how you would embody Nanda Mahārāja's mood of pratyutthāna, kṛtāñjali, and praṇipāta with adhokṣaja-dhiyā, rather than mere social hospitality.

Objections & Responses
  • Standing up, folding hands, and offering daṇḍavats to a visiting sādhu feels performative and even uncomfortable in modern settings, especially in front of non-devotee guests. Isn't sincere internal respect enough?
    Śrīla Prabhupāda repeatedly emphasized that bhakti is expressed through body, mind, and words together — kāyena manasā vācā. Nanda Mahārāja was the prosperous king of Vraja, yet he stood up immediately; external humility actually protects and nourishes internal devotion rather than contradicting it. Internal respect alone, without bodily expression, tends to gradually evaporate because the false ego quietly reasserts itself. The discomfort we feel in front of others is itself a diagnostic — it reveals where our sense of prestige still lives. Performing praṇāma in such moments is not theatre; it is medicine. Over time, what begins as deliberate practice ripens into spontaneous bhāva.
  • How can I treat every visiting sādhu as a representative of Kṛṣṇa when many so-called gurus and saintly persons today have been exposed as frauds or fallen? Doesn't blind reverence make us vulnerable to deception?
    The verse does not ask for blind reverence — it presupposes that Nanda recognized Garga Muni as a genuine sādhu through the standard tests: paramparā, scriptural alignment, and personal character. Śāstra itself instructs us first to examine (parīkṣya) and then to surrender (sevitavyaḥ). Discrimination and reverence are not opposites; they are sequential. Once we have honestly applied śāstric criteria — adherence to guru-paramparā, fidelity to Bhagavad-gītā and Bhāgavatam, freedom from the four regulative deviations, evidence of compassion and humility — then for that qualified sādhu we cultivate adhokṣaja-dhiyā. Caution toward imposters does not justify cynicism toward genuine Vaiṣṇavas.
Analogy

When a king sees his own son's tutor at the door, he rises before any servant can move — the body acts before thought, because love has already named the visitor. So Nanda, the wealthy ruler of Vraja, rises and folds his hands the instant Garga Muni appears, his eyes seeing a man but his buddhi already bowed at adhokṣaja's feet.

Stories
Advaita Ācārya receives Caitanya Mahāprabhu

When Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu first arrived at Advaita Ācārya's home in Śāntipura, the elder Advaita — a renowned scholar and householder of mature years — fell at the young sannyāsī's feet before any word was spoken. His learning did not delay him; his adhokṣaja-dṛṣṭi pierced the body and saw Bhagavān Himself.

Caitanya-caritāmṛta lingers on this reception as the template for Gauḍīya etiquette: pratyutthāna, kṛtāñjali, daṇḍavat — bodily reverence first, conversation afterward, exactly Nanda's sequence in this verse.
