Chapter 58 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 58.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Once, the supremely opulent Personality of Godhead went to Indraprastha to visit the Pāṇḍavas, who had again appeared in public. Accompanying the Lord were Yuyudhāna and other associates.

Essence. The supremely opulent Lord goes to Indraprastha to visit the Pāṇḍavas, who have again appeared in public, accompanied by Yuyudhāna. After hiding ends, visiting becomes possible; the Lord goes to His own when they can be visited.

BG 58.2

When the Pāṇḍavas saw that Lord Mukunda had arrived, those heroic sons of Pṛthā all stood up at once, like the senses responding to the return of the life air.

Essence. Seeing Mukunda arrive, the Pāṇḍavas all stand at once, like senses responding to the return of the life air. The Lord's arrival animates devotees the way prāṇa animates the body.

BG 58.3

The heroes embraced Lord Acyuta, and the touch of His body freed them of sin. Looking at His affectionate, smiling face, they were overwhelmed with joy.

Essence. The heroes embrace Acyuta and His body's touch frees them of sin; looking at His affectionate, smiling face, they are overwhelmed with joy. Embrace carries purification; the smile in the face is part of the encounter.

BG 58.4

After the Lord bowed down at the feet of Yudhiṣṭhira and Bhīma and firmly embraced Arjuna, He accepted obeisances from the twin brothers, Nakula and Sahadeva.

Essence. After bowing at Yudhiṣṭhira's and Bhīma's feet and firmly embracing Arjuna, He accepts obeisances from Nakula and Sahadeva. The Lord calibrates greetings by relationship; some get a bow, some a hug, some accept-bow-from.

BG 58.5

Faultless Draupadī, the Pāṇḍavas’ newly married wife, slowly and somewhat timidly approached Lord Kṛṣṇa, who sat on an exalted seat, and offered Him her obeisances.

Essence. Faultless Draupadī, the Pāṇḍavas' newly married wife, approaches Kṛṣṇa timidly and offers obeisances. Even great female devotees approach the Lord with reverence; timidity coexists with intimacy.

BG 58.6

Sātyaki also accepted a seat of honor after receiving worship and welcome from the Pāṇḍavas, and the Lord’s other companions, being duly honored, sat down in various places.

Essence. Sātyaki accepts a seat of honor after welcome; the Lord's companions, duly honored, sit in various places. Even the entourage is welcomed properly; comprehensive hospitality.

BG 58.7

The Lord then went to see His aunt, Queen Kuntī. He bowed down to her and she embraced Him, her eyes moist with great affection. Lord Kṛṣṇa inquired from her and her daughter-in-law, Draupadī, about their welfare, and they in turn questioned Him at length about His relatives [in Dvārakā].

Essence. The Lord goes to see His aunt Kuntī, bows; she embraces Him with moist eyes; mutual inquiry about welfare; the conversation flows in both directions. Aunt-and-nephew dynamics are honored, mutual inquiry is the standard.

BG 58.8

So overcome by love that her throat choked up and her eyes filled with tears, Queen Kuntī remembered the many troubles she and her sons had endured. Thus she addressed Lord Kṛṣṇa, who appears before His devotees to drive away their distress

Essence. Throat choked, eyes filled, Kuntī remembers the many troubles she and her sons endured and addresses Kṛṣṇa, who removes devotees' distress. Speech follows tears; vulnerable address comes after the body breaks open.

BG 58.9

[Queen Kuntī said:] My dear Kṛṣṇa, our welfare was assured only when You remembered us, Your relatives, and gave us Your protection by sending my brother to visit us.

Essence. Our welfare was assured only when You remembered us and gave us protection by sending my brother. Salvation is named through specific Mediating relatives; protection arrives via people the Lord sends.

BG 58.10

For You, the well-wishing friend and Supreme Soul of the universe, there is never any illusion of “us” and “them.” Yet even so, residing within the hearts of all, You eradicate the sufferings of those who remember You constantly.

Essence. For You, well-wishing Friend and Supreme Soul, there's no 'us' and 'them'; yet residing in all hearts, You eradicate sufferings of those who remember You constantly. Universal equality and personal responsiveness coexist.

BG 58.11

King Yudhiṣṭhira said: O supreme controller, I do not know what pious deeds we fools have done so that we can see You, whom the masters of yogic perfection rarely see.

Essence. Yudhiṣṭhira: I don't know what pious deeds we fools have done to see You — whom even yogic masters rarely see. Humility before unearned grace; the king names himself a fool.

BG 58.12

Requested by the King to stay with them, the almighty Lord remained happily in Indraprastha during the months of the rainy season, giving joy to the eyes of the city’s residents.

Essence. Requested to stay, the Lord remains happily in Indraprastha during the rainy season, delighting the city's residents. Extended stays bless cities; the Lord can be asked to remain.

BG 58.13-14

Once Arjuna, the slayer of powerful enemies, donned his armor, mounted his chariot flying the flag of Hanumān, took up his bow and his two inexhaustible quivers, and went to sport with Lord Kṛṣṇa in a large forest filled with fierce animals.

Essence. Arjuna donned armor, mounted his Hanumān-flagged chariot, took bow and two inexhaustible quivers, and went to sport with Kṛṣṇa in a forest of fierce animals. Sport for warriors involves equipment that ordinary play doesn't; deep play is also serious.

BG 58.15

With his arrows Arjuna shot tigers, boars and buffalo in that forest, along with rurus, śarabhas, gavayas, rhinoceroses, black deer, rabbits and porcupines.

Essence. With arrows Arjuna shoots tigers, boars, buffalo, rurus, śarabhas, gavayas, rhinoceroses, deer, rabbits, porcupines. The kṣatriya hunts; warrior duties are listed plainly, not euphemized.

BG 58.16

A crew of servants carried to King Yudhiṣṭhira the slain animals fit to be offered in sacrifice on some special occasion. Then, feeling thirsty and tired, Arjuna went to the bank of the Yamunā.

Essence. Servants carry slain animals to Yudhiṣṭhira for sacrifice; tired and thirsty, Arjuna goes to the Yamunā's bank. Hunting feeds sacrifice; tiredness leads to water; the day's logic is concrete.

BG 58.17

After the two Kṛṣṇas bathed there, they drank the river’s clear water. The great warriors then saw an attractive young girl walking nearby.

Essence. After both Kṛṣṇas bathe and drink the river's water, the warriors see an attractive young girl walking nearby. The Lord arranges meetings around routine bathing; sacred encounters interrupt mundane refreshment.

BG 58.18

Sent by his friend, Arjuna approached the exceptional young woman, who possessed beautiful hips, fine teeth and a lovely face, and inquired from her as follows.

Essence. Sent by his friend, Arjuna approaches the exceptional young woman with beautiful hips, fine teeth, lovely face, and inquires. Kṛṣṇa sends Arjuna to make the initial inquiry; devotional matchmaking has a chain.

BG 58.19

[Arjuna said:] Who are you, O fine-waisted lady? Whose daughter are you, and where do you come from? What are you doing here? I think you must be looking for a husband. Please explain everything, O beautiful one.

Essence. Who are you, fine-waisted lady? Whose daughter, where from, doing what? You must be looking for a husband — please explain. Friendly directness; questions move toward the obvious purpose.

BG 58.20

Śrī Kālindī said: I am the daughter of the sun-god. I desire to get as my husband the most excellent and munificent Lord Viṣṇu, and to that end I am performing severe penances.

Essence. Kālindī: I am the sun-god's daughter; I desire Lord Viṣṇu as husband and am performing severe penances. Identity, desire, and current discipline are stated plainly; she doesn't disguise her aim.

BG 58.21

I will accept no husband other than Him, the abode of the goddess of fortune. May that Mukunda, the Supreme Personality, the shelter of the helpless, be pleased with me.

Essence. I will accept no husband other than Him, the abode of Lakṣmī, the shelter of the helpless — may Mukunda be pleased with me. Exclusive choice plus self-doubt about the Lord's pleasure; the heart insists and worries simultaneously.

BG 58.22

I am known as Kālindī, and I live in a mansion my father built for me within the water of the Yamunā. There I will stay until I meet Lord Acyuta.

Essence. I live in a mansion my father built within the Yamunā's water; I will stay there until I meet Lord Acyuta. The daughter of the sun-god waits in a specific underwater mansion; patient location-based waiting is part of strong intent.

BG 58.23

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued:] Arjuna repeated all this to Lord Vāsudeva, who was already aware of it. The Lord then took Kālindī onto His chariot and went back to see King Yudhiṣṭhira.

Essence. Arjuna repeats this to Vāsudeva, who is already aware; the Lord takes Kālindī onto His chariot and returns to Yudhiṣṭhira. The Lord, already knowing, still listens to His friend's report; service involves repeating known truth.

BG 58.24

[Describing a previous incident, Śukadeva Gosvāmī said:] Upon the request of the Pāṇḍavas, Lord Kṛṣṇa had Viśvakarmā build them a most wonderful and amazing city.

Essence. Upon the Pāṇḍavas' request, Kṛṣṇa has Viśvakarmā build a most wonderful and amazing city. Requests to the Lord can yield architectural responses; sometimes the answer is a city, not a sentence.

BG 58.25

The Supreme Lord stayed in that city for some time to please His devotees. On one occasion, Śrī Kṛṣṇa wanted to give the Khāṇḍava forest as a gift to Agni, and so the Lord became Arjuna’s charioteer.

Essence. The Lord stays in the city to please His devotees; later, wanting to give the Khāṇḍava forest to Agni, He becomes Arjuna's charioteer. The Lord steers His devotees' chariots when major gifts are being delivered.

BG 58.26

Being pleased, O King, Lord Agni presented Arjuna with a bow, a set of white horses, a chariot, a pair of inexhaustible quivers, and armor that no fighter could pierce with weapons.

Essence. Pleased Agni presents Arjuna with bow, white horses, chariot, inexhaustible quivers, impenetrable armor. Service to a higher being via the Lord yields cascading divine gifts.

BG 58.27

When the demon Maya was saved from the fire by his friend Arjuna, Maya presented him with an assembly hall, in which Duryodhana would later mistake water for a solid floor.

Essence. When the demon Maya is saved from the fire by his friend Arjuna, Maya presents him with an assembly hall — where Duryodhana would later mistake water for solid floor. Acts of friendship even with demons yield long-term consequences across stories.

BG 58.28

Then Lord Kṛṣṇa, given leave by Arjuna and other well-wishing relatives and friends, returned to Dvārakā with Sātyaki and the rest of His entourage.

Essence. Given leave by Arjuna and other relatives, Kṛṣṇa returns to Dvārakā with Sātyaki and entourage. Visits end properly; permission is taken even by the Lord.

BG 58.29

The supremely auspicious Lord then married Kālindī on a day when the season, the lunar asterism and the configurations of the sun and other heavenly bodies were all propitious. In this way He brought the greatest pleasure to His devotees.

Essence. The Lord marries Kālindī on a day when season, lunar asterism, sun and other heavenly configurations are all propitious — bringing greatest pleasure to His devotees. The Lord respects auspicious timing; even He waits for the right muhūrta.

BG 58.30

Vindya and Anuvindya, who shared the throne of Avantī, were followers of Duryodhana’s. When the time came for their sister [Mitravindā] to select her husband in the svayaṁvara ceremony, they forbade her to choose Kṛṣṇa, although she was attracted to Him.

Essence. Vindya and Anuvindya, followers of Duryodhana, forbid their sister Mitravindā from choosing Kṛṣṇa at her svayaṁvara though she is attracted to Him. Family political enmities shape personal choices; brothers can block sisters' devotion.

BG 58.31

My dear King, Lord Kṛṣṇa forcibly took away Princess Mitravindā, the daughter of His aunt Rājādhidevī, before the eyes of the rival kings.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa forcibly takes Mitravindā, daughter of His aunt Rājādhidevī, before the rival kings' eyes. When forbidden by relatives but desired by the soul, the Lord can intervene forcibly.

BG 58.32

O King, Nagnajit, the very pious King of Kośala, had a lovely daughter named Satyā, or Nāgnajitī.

Essence. Pious Nagnajit, King of Kośala, has a lovely daughter named Satyā or Nāgnajitī. Setup of next bride introduces another good father; mothers and fathers of saints are themselves saintly.

BG 58.33

The kings who came as suitors were not allowed to marry her unless they could subdue seven sharp-horned bulls. These bulls were extremely vicious and uncontrollable, and they could not tolerate even the smell of warriors.

Essence. Suitors cannot marry unless they subdue seven sharp-horned bulls — vicious, uncontrollable, intolerant of warriors' smell. The test is impossible; only the right one can pass.

BG 58.34

When the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the master of the Vaiṣṇavas, heard of the princess who was to be won by the conqueror of the bulls, He went to the capital of Kauśalya with a large army.

Essence. Hearing of the princess won by the bull-conqueror, the Lord — master of Vaiṣṇavas — goes to Kauśalya with a large army. The Lord travels to specific tests; missions are deliberate.

BG 58.35

The King of Kośala, pleased to see Lord Kṛṣṇa, worshiped Him by rising from his throne and offering Him a seat of honor and substantial gifts. Lord Kṛṣṇa also greeted the King respectfully.

Essence. Pleased Nagnajit rises from his throne, offers seat of honor and substantial gifts; Kṛṣṇa greets the King respectfully. Mutual respect across royal protocols; ceremony serves relationship.

BG 58.36

When the King’s daughter saw that most agreeable suitor arrive, she immediately desired to have Him, the Lord of Goddess Rāma. She prayed, “May He become my husband. If I have kept my vows, may the sacred fire bring about the fulfillment of my hopes.

Essence. Seeing the agreeable suitor, the princess immediately desires Him; she prays: may He become my husband; if I have kept my vows, may the sacred fire bring fulfillment. Vow-based prayer for a specific person.

BG 58.37

“Goddess Lakṣmī, Lord Brahma, Lord Śiva and the rulers of the various planets place the dust of His lotus feet on their heads, and to protect the codes of religion, which He has created, He assumes pastime incarnations at various times. How may that Supreme Personality of Godhead become pleased with me?”

Essence. Lakṣmī, Brahmā, Śiva, planetary rulers place dust of His feet on their heads; to protect dharma He assumes pastime incarnations — how could He neglect me? The princess argues from cosmic theology to personal prayer.

BG 58.38

King Nagnajit first worshiped the Lord properly and then addressed Him: “O Nārāyaṇa, Lord of the universe, You are full in Your own spiritual pleasure. Therefore what can this insignificant person do for You?”

Essence. Nagnajit worships the Lord properly and addresses Him: 'O Nārāyaṇa, You are full in Your own pleasure; what can this insignificant person do for You?' Humble welcome doesn't presume capacity to give to the Lord.

BG 58.39

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O beloved descendant of Kuru, the Supreme Lord was pleased, and after accepting a comfortable seat He smiled and addressed the King in a voice as deep as the rumbling of a cloud.

Essence. Pleased, the Lord smiles and addresses the King in a voice deep as cloud-rumble. Pleasure of the Lord precedes His response; smile is part of speech.

BG 58.40

The Supreme Lord said: O ruler of men, learned authorities condemn begging for a person in the royal order who is executing his religious duties. Even so, desiring your friendship, I ask you for your daughter, though We offer no gifts in exchange.

Essence. Learned authorities condemn begging for a kṣatriya executing religious duties; yet desiring your friendship, I ask for your daughter, though We offer no gifts in exchange. The Lord names His own breach of decorum while asking; honest framing.

BG 58.41

The King said: My Lord, who could be a better husband for my daughter than You, the exclusive abode of all transcendental qualities? On Your body the goddess of fortune herself resides, never leaving You for any reason.

Essence. The King: who could be a better husband than You, the exclusive abode of all transcendental qualities? Lakṣmī herself never leaves Your body. The father affirms the match in stronger terms than the Lord proposed.

BG 58.42

But to ascertain the proper husband for my daughter, O chief of the Sātvatas, we previously set a condition to test the prowess of her suitors.

Essence. But to ascertain the proper husband, we previously set a condition to test prowess. Even when the answer is clearly yes, protocol must run; tests are honored.

BG 58.43

These seven wild bulls are impossible to tame, O hero. They have defeated many princes, breaking their limbs.

Essence. These seven wild bulls have defeated many princes, breaking their limbs. The cost of failure is named honestly; tests can have real consequences.

BG 58.44

If You can subdue them, O descendant of Yadu, You will certainly be the appropriate bridegroom for my daughter, O Lord of Śrī.

Essence. If You subdue them, You will be the appropriate bridegroom for my daughter, O Lord of Śrī. The condition is plainly stated; success means yes.

BG 58.45

Upon hearing these terms, the Lord tightened His clothing, expanded Himself into seven forms and easily subdued the bulls.

Essence. Hearing the terms, the Lord tightens His clothing, expands Himself into seven forms, and easily subdues the bulls. Practical preparation, multiple forms, and effortless completion; the Lord brings what is needed.

BG 58.46

Lord Śauri tied up the bulls, whose pride and strength were now broken, and pulled them with ropes just as a child playfully pulls wooden toy bulls.

Essence. Lord Śauri ties the bulls — pride and strength broken — and pulls them with ropes like a child plays with wooden toy bulls. What was vicious is now toy; the Lord plays with what defeated others.

BG 58.47

Then King Nagnajit, pleased and astonished, presented his daughter to Lord Kṛṣṇa. The Supreme Personality of Godhead accepted this suitable bride in the proper Vedic fashion.

Essence. Pleased and astonished Nagnajit presents his daughter; the Lord accepts the suitable bride in proper Vedic fashion. Astonishment yields offering, offering yields proper Vedic acceptance.

BG 58.48

The King’s wives felt the greatest ecstasy upon attaining Lord Kṛṣṇa as the dear husband of the royal princess, and a mood of great festivity arose.

Essence. The King's wives feel the greatest ecstasy at having the Lord as husband to their princess, and a mood of great festivity arises. Mothers' ecstasy frames the wedding; women's joy is named as central.

BG 58.49

Conchshells, horns and drums resounded, along with vocal and instrumental music and the sounds of brāhmaṇas, invoking blessings. The joyful men and women adorned themselves with fine clothing and garlands.

Essence. Conchshells, horns, drums, vocal and instrumental music, brāhmaṇas' blessings; joyful men and women in fine clothing and garlands. Multi-channel celebration; the senses are addressed in concert.

BG 58.50-51

As the dowry, powerful King Nagnajit gave ten thousand cows, three thousand young maidservants wearing golden ornaments on their necks and bedecked in fine clothing, nine thousand elephants, a hundred times as many chariots as elephants, a hundred times as many horses as chariots, and a hundred times as many manservants as horses.

Essence. Powerful Nagnajit gives ten thousand cows, three thousand maidservants in gold ornaments, nine thousand elephants, a hundred times as many chariots as elephants. Dowry is detailed; specific abundance is part of honoring the daughter.

BG 58.52

The King of Kośala, his heart melting with affection, had the bride and groom seated on their chariot, and then he sent them on their way surrounded by a great army.

Essence. Heart melting with affection, the King has bride and groom seated on their chariot and sends them off with a great army. Sendings off can be heart-melting; large escort honors the departure.

BG 58.53

When the intolerant kings who had been rival suitors heard what had happened, they tried to stop Lord Kṛṣṇa on the road as He took His bride home. But just as the bulls had broken the kings’ strength before, the Yadu warriors broke it now.

Essence. Intolerant rival kings try to stop Kṛṣṇa on the road; just as the bulls broke under His strength, the Yadu warriors break them now. Same dynamic recurs; what failed for the kings before fails again.

BG 58.54

Arjuna, wielder of the Gāṇḍīva bow, was always eager to please his friend Kṛṣṇa, and thus he drove back those opponents, who were shooting torrents of arrows at the Lord. He did this just as a lion drives away insignificant animals.

Essence. Arjuna, eager to please Kṛṣṇa, drives back opponents shooting arrows at the Lord like a lion driving away insignificant animals. Friend's eagerness becomes lion's protection; love for Kṛṣṇa fights well.

BG 58.55

Lord Devakī-suta, the chief of the Yadus, then took His dowry and Satyā to Dvārakā and continued to live there happily.

Essence. Devakī's son takes His dowry and Satyā to Dvārakā and continues to live happily. Even after dramatic acquisition, the Lord settles into ordinary continued happiness; events lead to stable life.

BG 58.56

Bhadrā was a princess of the Kaikeya kingdom and the daughter of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s paternal aunt Śrutakīrti. The Lord married Bhadrā when her brothers, headed by Santardana, offered her to Him.

Essence. Bhadrā, princess of Kaikeya, daughter of paternal aunt Śrutakīrti, is offered to the Lord by her brothers led by Santardana. Family-arranged marriage with family-aligned brothers is its own dignified path.

BG 58.57

Then the Lord married Lakṣmaṇā, the daughter of the King of Madra. Kṛṣṇa appeared alone at her svayaṁvara ceremony and took her away, just as Garuḍa once stole the demigods’ nectar.

Essence. The Lord marries Lakṣmaṇā, daughter of the King of Madra; appearing alone at her svayaṁvara, He takes her away like Garuḍa stealing the demigods' nectar. Solo arrival at high-stakes events is itself a statement.

BG 58.58

Lord Kṛṣṇa also acquired thousands of other wives equal to these when He killed Bhaumāsura and freed the beautiful maidens the demon was holding captive.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa also acquires thousands of other wives equal to these by killing Bhaumāsura and freeing the beautiful maidens he was holding captive. Marriage-by-liberation; freeing captives is its own form of union.
