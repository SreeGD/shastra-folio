Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.17.16

Important Words
  • acyutam — the infallible one
    Krṣṇa's name as 'never-falling.' Balarāma embraces Him precisely as Acyuta — He who has never been in danger. The choice of name explains the laugh: an elder brother who knows the younger cannot truly be in peril.
  • anubhāva-vit — knower of the (Lord's) potency
    Compound: anubhāva (perceived power, glory) + vid (one who knows). Balarāma is the only one in this verse who knows. The Bhāgavatam quietly distinguishes him from the cowherds and mothers: love coexists with full knowledge in him.
  • aṅkam-āropya — raising Him onto His lap
    Identical phrasing to mother Yaśodā two verses later (19) — Balarāma performs the same vātsalya-gesture toward his younger brother that Yaśodā performs toward her son. The chapter braids sakhya and vātsalya in adjacent verses.
  • premṇā — out of love
    Instrumental of prema. The technical term for unmotivated, unbreakable love — the Gauḍīya summit. Balarāma's embrace is named prema-driven explicitly; the cowherds' embrace in verse 14 was prīti-driven. The poet grades the rasas precisely.
  • vatsataryaḥ — the young female calves
    From vatsa (calf). The chapter scrupulously lists every category of Vraja-life that rejoices — even the youngest calves. Gauḍīyas: no rung of Vraja's ecology is omitted from Kṛṣṇa's circle of love.
Lord Balarāma embraced His infallible brother and laughed, knowing well the extent of Kṛṣṇa’s potency. Out of great feelings of love, Balarāma lifted Kṛṣṇa up on His lap and repeatedly looked at Him. The cows, bulls and young female calves also achieved the highest pleasure.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Lord Balarāma embraced His infallible brother and laughed, knowing well the extent of Kṛṣṇa's potency. Out of great feelings of love, Balarāma lifted Kṛṣṇa up on His lap and repeatedly looked at Him. The cows, bulls and young female calves also achieved the highest pleasure.
Commentary

Balarama smiled. "You have done just the right thing." Though he was knowledgeable of Krsna’s strength, he looked at him intently again and again (udaiksata). He looked to see if Krsna had incurred any injuries from fighting Kaliya.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

He then met with Balarāma who had given an opportunity for the people of Vraja who were grieving to meet him. That is described in this verse. The word ca indicates that Balarāma internally dried up because of the people’s sorrow. Later meeting Kṛṣṇa, who never loses his powers (acyutam), taking his hand, he laughed. Why did he laugh? He knew that Kṛṣṇa could accomplish anything by his will alone (anubhāva-vit). As a scolding he laughed, as if saying “You have done well, making your people of Vraja suffer like this!” Besides this, other men and women, bulls and calves also rejoiced (narā nāryāḥ vṛṣā vatsā). Instead of this gāvo vṛṣā vatsatarā is sometimes seen.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

After describing the actions of devotees having affection, Śukadeva describes the meeting with Balarāma, endowed with great affection and also knowledge of Kṛṣṇa’s powers. The word ca indicates that Balarāma also internally dried up. Later meeting Kṛṣṇa, who never loses his powers (acyutam), taking his hand, he laughed. Acyutam indicates that Kṛṣṇa was never devoid of his hair arrangements etc. or he never falls from his greatness. He knew the various powers (anubhāva) of Kṛṣṇa. His own powers to manifest the fire to destroy the universe was nothing compared to Kṛṣṇa’s powers. He remembered Kāliya squeezing him in his coils. Or he knew how Kṛsṇa could cleverly ascertain (anubhāva) how to release himself from Kāliya. Another version has nārāḥ nāryo vṛṣaḥ vatsāḥ. Nārāḥ refers to people in general, or to Nanda’s servants and the Pulina men. Another version has mṛgāḥ first. This indicates that the cows recovered first. Or the sequence indicates the intensity of bliss. Thus nārāḥ placed first indicates Nanda’s intense bliss, but other’s bliss depended on his bliss.

Personal Guidance
Analogy

Balarāma laughs. He alone in the whole gathering knew the outcome from the start — the cowherds wept, the mothers fainted, but the elder brother saw the whole līlā as theatre and embraced Kṛṣṇa like a man retrieving a friend from a successful prank. His laughter is the laughter of one who knows aiśvarya from inside, and lets the others have their rasa.

Stories
Balarāma's anubhāva-jñāna

Throughout Canto 10, Balarāma alone seems to know Kṛṣṇa's full identity without ever ceasing to be His elder brother. He laughs at Yaśodā's binding-rope in SB 10.11, He laughs here, and He will laugh when Brahmā steals the calves in 10.13.

Gauḍīyas place Balarāma at the apex of sakhya-rasa precisely because of this: he holds the knowledge of aiśvarya entirely inside the sweetness of brotherhood. He does not let omniscience interrupt affection.
