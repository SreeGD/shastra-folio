Important Words
  • paryaṅke — on the bed
    Locative of paryaṅka. The same paryaṅka of v.18 — from which Kṛṣṇa rose. Now Sudāmā sits on it. The migration of the royal seat from Lord to bhakta is the verse's quiet structural marvel.
  • avanijya — washing
    Gerund of ava-√nij. Pāda-prakṣālana — the ritual of foot-washing customarily done for a brāhmaṇa or guru. Here the Lord does it Himself, with His own hands. Gauḍīyas mark this as the chapter's emotional summit.
  • pāda - avanejanīḥ — the water which had washed his feet
    Compound: pāda + avanejanī (washing-water). The water that washed Sudāmā's feet — and Kṛṣṇa puts it on His own head. This single detail upends the entire normal direction of pāda-udaka veneration (cf. v.4).
  • śirasā — on His head
    Instrumental of śiras. The Lord's head — the highest part — receives the foot-water of His friend. Gauḍīyas note: the same head that is the universe's crown bows to receive His own devotee's foot-water.
  • loka — of all worlds
    Member of compound loka-pāvanaḥ — purifier of worlds. The cosmic purifier honors His friend by treating him as the source of purification. The role-reversal is total.
  • pāvanaḥ — the purifier
    From √pū. Kṛṣṇa is the One who purifies; yet here the purifier accepts purification from the bhakta. Gauḍīyas read this as audārya — the Lord's gracious self-abasement, central to Mahāprabhu's mood.
Lord Kṛṣṇa seated His friend Sudāmā upon the bed. Then the Lord, who purifies the whole world, personally offered him various tokens of respect and washed his feet, O King, after which He sprinkled the water on His own head. He anointed him with divinely fragrant sandalwood, aguru and kuṅkuma pastes and happily worshiped him with aromatic incense and arrays of lamps. After finally offering him betel nut and the gift of a cow, He welcomed him with pleasing words.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Lord Kṛṣṇa seated His friend Sudāmā upon the bed. Then the Lord, who purifies the whole world, personally offered him various tokens of respect and washed his feet, O King, after which He sprinkled the water on His own head. He anointed him with divinely fragrant sandalwood, aguru and kuṅkuma pastes and happily worshiped him with aromatic incense and arrays of lamps. After finally offering him betel nut and the gift of a cow, He welcomed him with pleasing words.
Commentary

Krsna put the water that he used to wash his feet (padavanejani) on his head.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

After embracing him a long time he made him sit on the bed by force, since Sudāmā was afraid to do so. This should be understood in other actions also that Kṛṣṇa personally did the services. He brought articles of worship and personally washed his feet. Though he was the Lord he took the water on his head. By sprinkling his head he purified the worlds. Though he was the Lord, and purifier of the worlds, he took the water on his head. These actions show Kṛṣṇa’s gentle character. He anointed him with fragrant substances ground together and made special patterns on his limbs (vi—alimpat). He also feed him milk rice and had him bathe. This is understood from later verses.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

After embracing him a long time, he made him sit on the bed by force, since Sudāmā was afraid to do so. It should be understood in other actions also that Kṛṣṇa personally did the services. Or gathering excellent articles, the worship is described as a respectful greeting. Or he personally gathered the items, washed his feet with water using golden pitchers given by Rukmiṇī, and placed the water on his head, because he was directly the Supreme Lord (bhagavān) who can purify the world just by his glance (loka-pāvanah). Or though he purifies the world he placed the water on his head, since he appears in this world to display unlimited qualities such as affection to brāhmaṇas (bhagavān). O king (rājan), the Lord does this because he is controlled by prema. Or the Lord was shining with affection (rājan). The scents were attractive (divya) with sandalwood and aguru ground together. Or he painted him with decorations made of sandalwood mixed with attractive fragrances. He worshipped him with incense etc. because he was a friend. He offered betel and a cow. After having him bathe, he offered a meal. That is explained in SB 10.81.12.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa seated Sudāmā on the bed, washed his feet, sprinkled the water on His own head, anointed him with sandalwood, kuṅkuma, incense, lamps, betel nut. The Lord serves His friend with the full sequence of honoring rites.
Reflection Questions
  • Whom in my life have I never honored with the full sequence — not just a quick hello but a real welcome?
  • What relationship would deepen with attention to the rites of welcome?
Analogy

A king washing his guest's feet and pouring the water on his own head reverses the entire pageantry of court: the host is the servant, the rag-clothed visitor is the deity. This is the Lord of Dvārakā doing what brāhmaṇas customarily do for Him — the rasa runs the other way.

Stories
Bali offered the Lord's foot-water

When Vāmana stepped over the universe in Bali's sacrifice, His foot-water (carried by Brahmā's kamaṇḍalu) became the Gaṅgā, sanctifying all worlds (SB 8.21). Even devas keep the foot-water on their heads.

In SB 10.80, Kṛṣṇa Himself does to Sudāmā what cosmic beings do to Him — sprinkles foot-water on His own head. Gauḍīyas read this as the līlā's signature: Bhagavān assumes the bhakta's position so the bhakta can experience the Bhagavān's.

Caitanya washing Vāsudeva Datta's feet

Caitanya-caritāmṛta records Mahāprabhu insisting on washing the feet of His own devotees on multiple occasions — and weeping while doing so. The mood is named in śāstra as bhakta-vatsala-cāpalya: the Lord's restlessness to honor His bhakta.

Sudāmā's foot-washing at Dvārakā is the prooftext Mahāprabhu's biographers cite: if the original Kṛṣṇa does it, the same Kṛṣṇa as Gauracandra will do it again.
