Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.5.27

भ्रातर्मम सुत: कच्चिन्मात्रा सह भवद्‌व्रजे । तातं भवन्तं मन्वानो भवद्‌भ्यामुपलालित: ॥ २७ ॥
bhrātar mama sutaḥ kaccin
[bhraatar mama sutah kachchin]
mātrā saha bhavad-vraje
[maatraa saha bhavad-vraje]
tātaṁ bhavantaṁ manvāno
[taatam bhavantam manvaano]
bhavadbhyām upalālitaḥ
[bhavadbhyaam upalaalitah]

Important Words
  • sutaḥ — son
    Vasudeva calls Baladeva 'my son' — but the rest of the verse asks whether Baladeva considers Nanda his father. The Sanskrit holds both: biological and rasa-relational fatherhood coexist without contradiction in the Vraja-līlā.
  • mātrā saha — with His mother
    Mātṛ + saha. The phrase quietly clarifies: Baladeva's mother (Rohiṇī) is with him at Nanda's house. Vasudeva is asking after the whole transferred family — Rohiṇī as much as Balarāma.
  • bhavat-vraje — in your Vraja
    Bhavat ('your, honored') + vraja. The possessive 'your Vraja' is striking — Vasudeva acknowledges that Vraja belongs to Nanda's stewardship in this līlā, even though Kṛṣṇa Himself is the eternal Lord of it. Vaiṣṇava humility: yielding the title even of what one's own son owns.
  • manvānaḥ — thinking, considering
    Present participle of √man ('to think, to consider'). Baladeva 'thinks of' Nanda as father — the verb is gentle, locating the relationship in Baladeva's own consciousness rather than imposing it from outside. Rasa-relations are first inner.
  • upalālitaḥ — fondled, cherished
    Past participle of upa + √lal ('to caress, fondle'). The specific tactile word for the affection given to a child — being held, stroked, played with. Vasudeva is asking whether his son is receiving real vātsalya, not just being adequately raised.
My son Baladeva, being raised by you and your wife, Yaśodādevī, considers you His father and mother. Is He living very peacefully in your home with His real mother, Rohiṇī?
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
My son Baladeva, being raised by you and your wife, Yaśodādevī, considers you His father and mother. Is he living very peacefully in your home with His real mother, Rohiṇī?
Commentary

"Is my child (Balarama) happy? Does he think of you as his father?"

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

How is my son whom you raised as your own son? Or, does he have great affection for you because he thinks of you as his father? That affection does not arise simply because of being born from a certain father, but by seeing and association. Or, is my son along with his mother staying in your Vraja? Have you not gone somewhere else because of fear of Kaṁsa? He speaks of Balarāma as his son because Vasudeva had already heard that he was born. Hari-vaṁśa says: prāg eva vasudevas tu vraje śuśrāva rohiṇīm prajātāṁ putram evāgre candrāt kāntatarānanam Vasudeva attained a son whose face was brighter than the moon from pregnant Rohiṇī in Vraja before Kṛṣṇa was born. Agre means before Kṛṣṇa’s birth. But it should be understood that his birth was almost at the same time. Vasudeva says: vardhamānāv ubhāv etau samāna-vayasau yathā śobhetāṁ govraje tasmin nanda gopa tathā kuru O Nanda! Do what is necessary so that the two growing boys of the same age may flourish. Hari-vaṁśa In other words the two boys performed pastimes like crawling at the same time.

Personal Guidance
Vasudeva's loving inquiry shows that even Kṛṣṇa's pastimes honor real bonds of fosterage — sevā in family is not lesser dharma, it is sacred ground.
Practical Applications
  • Today, speak to the person who quietly raised or sustained you — parent, foster figure, mentor — and acknowledge their care explicitly, without performance.
  • Refuse the modern reflex to rank biological over fostering ties; serve whichever family Kṛṣṇa has placed in front of you with full sevā-bhāva.
  • Notice when you are anxious about someone in another household and, like Vasudeva, transmute the worry into a concrete, respectful inquiry rather than silent rumination.
Analogy

A child's first-mother and milk-mother may be different women, but the child knows where his head goes when he is sleepy. Vasudeva asks gently whether Balarāma considers Nanda his father — not in the legal sense, but in the sense of where the sleepy head turns. The answer is yes; vātsalya does not run on biology.

Stories
Hanumān and his two fathers

Hanumān has two fathers in the tradition — Kesarī (the monkey king who married Añjanā) and Vāyu (the wind-god whose breath conceived him). The Rāmāyaṇa and later devotional literature honor both: Hanumān is called Vāyu-putra and Kesarī-suta, with no contradiction.

Gauḍīya ācāryas point to such cases (and to Baladeva's two mothers, Devakī and Rohiṇī) as confirmation that in the world of Bhagavān's pastimes, multiple parenthoods coexist rasa-fully. Vasudeva's question about Baladeva at Nanda's house operates in this same logic — kinship is determined by love, not biology alone.
