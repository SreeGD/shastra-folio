Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.5.11

गोप्य: सुमृष्टमणिकुण्डलनिष्ककण्ठ्य- श्चित्राम्बरा: पथि शिखाच्युतमाल्यवर्षा: । नन्दालयं सवलया व्रजतीर्विरेजु- र्व्यालोलकुण्डलपयोधरहारशोभा: ॥ ११ ॥

In the ears of the gopīs were brilliantly polished jeweled earrings, and from their necks hung metal lockets. Their hands were decorated with bangles, their dresses were of varied colors, and from their hair, flowers fell onto the street like showers. Thus while going to the house of Mahārāja Nanda, the gopīs, their earrings, breasts and garlands moving, were brilliantly beautiful.
Purport

The description of the gopīs, who were going to the house of Mahārāja Nanda to welcome Kṛṣṇa, is especially significant. The gopīs were not ordinary women, but expansions of Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure potency, as described in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.37,29): ānanda-cinmaya-rasa-pratibhāvitābhis tābhir ya eva nija-rūpatayā kalābhiḥ goloka eva nivasaty akhilātma-bhūto govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi (5.37) cintāmaṇi-prakara-sadmasu kalpa-vṛkṣa- lakṣāvṛteṣu surabhīr abhipālayantam lakṣmī-sahasra-śata-sambhrama-sevyamānaṁ govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi (5.29) Kṛṣṇa is always worshiped by the gopīs wherever He goes. Therefore Kṛṣṇa is so vividly described in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has also described Kṛṣṇa in this way: ramyā kācid upāsanā vrajavadhū-vargeṇa yā kalpitā. All these gopīs were going to offer Kṛṣṇa their presentations because the gopīs are eternal associates of the Lord. Now the gopīs were more jubilant because of the news of Kṛṣṇa’s appearance in Vṛndāvana.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In the ears of the gopīs were brilliantly polished jeweled earrings, and from their necks hung metal lockets. Their hands were decorated with bangles, their dresses were of varied colors, and from their hair, flowers fell onto the street like showers. Thus while going to the house of Mahārāja Nanda, the gopīs, their earrings, breasts and garlands moving, were brilliantly beautiful.
Commentary

Though the beauty of the gopis has been described already, because of their great devotion, and feeling unsatisfied with that description alone, Sukadeva gives further description. Sikha refers to their tied up hair. Vrajati means while going.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

They are described again with devotion. Śikhā refers to the top of their hair. They became especially beautiful in going to Nanda’s house. Though they were naturally beautiful, because of their prema for Kṛṣṇa, they became especially beautiful.

Personal Guidance
The gopīs' radiant rush to greet Kṛṣṇa shows that true bhakti is the whole self — body, ornaments, and heart — moving toward Him in eager love.
Practical Applications
  • Before any seva or darśana today, prepare yourself with the same care the gopīs took — clean clothes, clean body, clean intention — refusing to approach Kṛṣṇa carelessly.
  • Notice when 'going to greet Kṛṣṇa' (temple, japa, study) feels like an obligation, and deliberately walk into it with the gopīs' eagerness instead.
  • Offer one tangible thing today — a flower, a meal, a small gift — at Kṛṣṇa's altar, imitating the gopīs who never came empty-handed.
Reflection Questions
  • The gopīs adorned themselves not for display but to welcome Kṛṣṇa — what care do I take in my own preparation (body, dress, mood) before approaching Him in sādhana, and where have I let that preparation become careless or self-centered?
  • These eternal associates moved together toward Nanda's house with shared jubilation — where in my life am I trying to walk toward Kṛṣṇa in solitary self-importance instead of with the saṅga of devotees, and what ahaṅkāra is that protecting?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before posting a selfie or curating an outfit, pause and ask honestly: am I dressing for Kṛṣṇa, for my friends' approval, or for the algorithm? The gopīs dressed elaborately — but every bangle was for Him. Try one day this week where you get ready as if Kṛṣṇa is the only audience.
  • Notice how your friend group reacts to news. When something exciting drops in the group chat, you all run to it together. The gopīs ran together to Nanda's house at the news of Kṛṣṇa. Find one friend who will run with you toward kīrtana, a class, or a temple visit the way you currently run toward concerts and parties.
  • Your phone, your room, your backpack are all 'adornments' you curate. Today, place one tulasī leaf, one image of Kṛṣṇa, or one small offering somewhere visible — not as decoration, but as a daily reminder that the externals can be tuned toward Him without becoming fake or performative.
householder
  • When you dress for work, a wedding, or a family gathering, reframe one element of your preparation as offered to Kṛṣṇa — the tilaka before the meeting, the tulasī mālā under the shirt, the prayer before stepping out. The gopīs' ornaments were elaborate but never ego-centered; the same is possible in your professional life.
  • Decisions about your children's clothes, school events, and birthday parties are constant. Ask: am I performing for other parents, or am I raising children who will run toward Kṛṣṇa with the gopīs' jubilation? Choose one upcoming family decision where you consciously prioritize the second over the first.
  • When good news comes — a promotion, a baby, a house — your first instinct is to call family or post it. Before any of that, offer the news at the altar, the way the gopīs went straight to Nanda's house. Make Kṛṣṇa the first recipient of every important update for one full month and notice what it does to the household.
senior
  • Loosen attachment to your own jewelry, saris, watches, and keepsakes by giving one item this month to a younger devotee — not as inheritance, but as a living offering to their service of Kṛṣṇa. The gopīs' ornaments were never theirs; they were always His.
  • At this stage the body's adornments fade — hair thins, joints stiffen, mirrors are less kind. Use this as an invitation, not a loss. The gopīs' beauty was never the body's; it was the radiance of one running toward Kṛṣṇa. Each morning, do one small act of preparation (bathing, tilaka, fresh cloth) explicitly as your offering.
  • Mentor a younger devotee or grandchild this week by telling them, in your own voice, the news of Kṛṣṇa's appearance — a pastime from the Bhāgavatam, a memory of your guru, a moment from your own sādhana. Be the one who carries the news to Nanda's house for the next generation.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the gopīs' ornaments as mere material vanity and dismissing the verse — missing that their decoration is an offering, an expression of prema, not a display of viṣaya.
  • Treating the gopīs as ordinary village women and so flattening the verse into folklore, instead of recognizing them as expansions of Kṛṣṇa's pleasure potency whose every gesture is non-different from devotional service.
  • Imitating the gopīs' externals — their bhāva, their rasa, their intimacy — as a sādhana shortcut, when our actual stage calls for vinaya, vaidhī-bhakti, and the humility of one still cleaning the mirror of the heart.
Discussion Prompts
  • The gopīs adorned themselves elaborately before going to see Kṛṣṇa — earrings, bangles, garlands, colored cloth. Is bodily adornment for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure compatible with vairāgya, or does it contradict the teaching that the body is not the self? Where does your group actually disagree?
  • Prabhupāda insists the gopīs are not ordinary women but expansions of Kṛṣṇa's pleasure potency (hlādinī-śakti). In pairs, work out the practical difference between hearing this verse as 'a charming village scene' versus hearing it as 'eternal associates of the Lord in Goloka.' Does the difference change anything you would do tomorrow?
  • The verse shows the gopīs running together to Nanda's house at the news of Kṛṣṇa's appearance. Discuss: when news of Kṛṣṇa reaches you — a class, a kīrtana, a festival — do you respond with the gopīs' urgency, or with householder calculation? Push each other on the honest answer, not the pious one.
Role Play

A new student arrives at your nāma-hatta program dressed in plain street clothes. She sees several devotees in fresh dhotīs and saris, tilaka, neck-beads, flowers in their hair, and quietly asks you afterwards: 'I thought bhakti was internal. Why all the dressing up? Isn't this just another form of vanity, only with a Kṛṣṇa label on it?' One student plays the new seeker; another plays the senior devotee responding. A third observes and calls out any answer that drifts toward either (a) impersonalism ('it's all just symbolic, the externals don't matter') or (b) mere ritualism ('we do it because we're supposed to'). The teacher then opens the floor: how do we, like the gopīs, dress the body for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure rather than our own — and how can the listener tell the difference?

Objections & Responses
  • This verse is just a lavish description of women's jewelry and bodies — earrings, breasts, garlands, hair. It reads like a male poet's fantasy, not spiritual teaching. Why should serious students dwell on it?
    Read it as you would read the description of a Deity in the temple. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam describes the gopīs' ornaments because the gopīs themselves are Kṛṣṇa's eternal associates — ānanda-cinmaya-rasa-pratibhāvitābhiḥ, expansions of His pleasure potency, as Brahma-saṁhitā says. Their bodies are not material in the ordinary sense; their adornments are not for self-display but for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure. Treating the verse as erotic spectacle is precisely the misreading Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu warned against — ramyā kācid upāsanā vrajavadhū-vargeṇa yā kalpitā: there is no higher worship than that conceived by the gopīs. The verse trains the heart to see ornament-as-offering rather than ornament-as-vanity, which is a real distinction we have lost in modern life.
  • Devotion should be inward. The gopīs' jubilant rush, the showers of flowers from their hair, the bangles and lockets — this all sounds like religious enthusiasm that crosses into emotionalism. Isn't a steady, sober sādhana more mature?
    Steadiness and jubilation are not opposites in the Gauḍīya line; they are stages. The dhīra-bhāva of BG 2.13 keeps you anchored when the body changes. The gopīs' jubilation here is not sentimentality — it is the natural response of eternal associates at the news of Kṛṣṇa's appearance. Cold sobriety in the face of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes is not maturity; it is anartha, a covering over the soul's natural rasa. Prabhupāda did not teach a dry, stoic bhakti. He taught chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa with feeling, dancing in kīrtana, weeping for separation. The corrective to emotionalism is not impersonal flatness; it is sva-rūpa-siddhi — emotion rightly directed to Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, never to an impersonal Absolute or to one's own ego.
