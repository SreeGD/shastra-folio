BG 6.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: My dear King, while Nanda Mahārāja was on the way home, he considered that what Vasudeva had said could not be false or useless. There must have been some danger of disturbances in Gokula. As Nanda Mahārāja thought about the danger for his beautiful son, Kṛṣṇa, he was afraid, and he took shelter at the lotus feet of the supreme controller.

Essence. When danger looms, the pure devotee — like Nanda — does not scheme; he takes śaraṇa at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet at the very first sign of trouble.

Practical applications
  • At the first flicker of fear today — a worry about a child, a job, a diagnosis — pause and audibly take shelter of Kṛṣṇa before doing anything else.
  • Treat a trusted devotee's warning the way Nanda treated Vasudeva's words: do not dismiss it as exaggeration, act on it.
  • Recite padaṁ padaṁ yad vipadām once today and name the specific 'step' in your life where you have been pretending there is no danger.

BG 6.2

While Nanda Mahārāja was returning to Gokula, the same fierce Pūtanā whom Kaṁsa had previously engaged to kill babies was wandering about in the towns, cities and villages, doing her nefarious duty.

Essence. Even as Kṛṣṇa enters Gokula, Kaṁsa's Pūtanā is already at work — māyā never sleeps; the devotee must stay alert in śraddhā and vairāgya.

Practical applications
  • Notice one 'Pūtanā' in your day — a sweet-seeming influence (a feed, a flatterer, a habit) that quietly poisons bhakti — and refuse its milk today.
  • Before stepping out, take shelter of Kṛṣṇa's holy name once with full attention, knowing that hostile forces are already in motion in the towns and streets you will enter.
  • Guard the small and vulnerable in your care — children, juniors, your own beginner's faith — by screening what you allow near them, the way Yaśodā would screen a stranger at the door.

BG 6.3

My dear King, wherever people in any position perform their occupational duties of devotional service by chanting and hearing [śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ], there cannot be any danger from bad elements. Therefore there was no need for anxiety about Gokula while the Supreme Personality of Godhead was personally present.

Essence. Where śravaṇaṁ-kīrtanaṁ of Kṛṣṇa goes on within sva-karma, no rākṣasic force can take hold — chant, and stay at your post.

Practical applications
  • Before starting today's work, chant one round of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra and explicitly offer the day's duties as devotional service.
  • Notice the next moment of anxiety about your family or nation, and refuse to manage it mentally — instead, audibly chant Kṛṣṇa's names right then.
  • Do not abandon your sva-karma to chase 'spiritual' settings; perform today's ordinary occupational duty with śravaṇaṁ-kīrtanaṁ running alongside it.

BG 6.4

Once upon a time, Pūtanā Rākṣasī, who could move according to her desire and was wandering in outer space, converted herself by mystic power into a very beautiful woman and thus entered Gokula, the abode of Nanda Mahārāja.

Essence. Māyā does not knock as a demon — she enters Gokula disguised as beauty; train discriminative sight to recognize Pūtanā in pleasing forms.

Practical applications
  • Notice one 'beautiful' offer today — a flattering message, an alluring purchase, an easy shortcut — and ask whose interest it actually serves.
  • Before admitting any new influence (media, company, content) into your inner Gokula, pause and test it against Kṛṣṇa-consciousness rather than its surface charm.
  • Refuse to judge people, content, or invitations by their pleasing exterior; cultivate vairāgya by looking past form to intention and effect.

BG 6.5-6

Her hips were full, her breasts were large and firm, seeming to overburden her slim waist, and she was dressed very nicely. Her hair, adorned with a garland of mallikā flowers, was scattered about her beautiful face. Her earrings were brilliant, and as she smiled very attractively, glancing upon everyone, her beauty drew the attention of all the inhabitants of Vraja, especially the men. When the gopīs saw her, they thought that the beautiful goddess of fortune, holding a lotus flower in her hand, had come to see her husband, Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Pūtanā arrives dressed as Lakṣmī — māyā's most dangerous form is the one that mimics auspiciousness; only Kṛṣṇa-conscious discrimination sees through it.

Practical applications
  • Notice today one 'attractive' offering — a person, a feed, a deal — and ask whether it carries Kṛṣṇa within it or only borrows the look of śrī.
  • Refuse to judge people or proposals by surface beauty alone; pause and check the intent behind the smile before you let it into your home or heart.
  • Before opening your phone or door, chant Kṛṣṇa's name once and ask Him to give you the gopīs' eyes — quick to see Him, slow to be charmed by what merely resembles Him.

BG 6.7

While searching for small children, Pūtanā, whose business was to kill them, entered the house of Nanda Mahārāja unobstructed, having been sent by the superior potency of the Lord. Without asking anyone’s permission, she entered Nanda Mahārāja’s room, where she saw the child sleeping in bed, His unlimited power covered like a powerful fire covered by ashes. She could understand that this child was not ordinary, but was meant to kill all demons.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa lay in Nanda's bed with His tejas veiled like fire under ashes — the Lord enters our ordinary life cloaked, ready to slay every Pūtanā that comes for the soul.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where Kṛṣṇa is acting quietly in your life under the 'ashes' of ordinary circumstance, and thank Him by name for that hidden protection.
  • Refuse the demonic strategy of Pūtanā — masquerading affection that feeds on the innocent — and audit one influence (a feed, a friendship, a habit) that wears a mother's face but poisons your bhakti.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's holy name aloud before sleep tonight, entrusting your household and dependents to the same Lord who let no demon touch the cradle in Gokula.

BG 6.8

Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the all-pervading Supersoul, lying on the bed, understood that Pūtanā, a witch who was expert in killing small children, had come to kill Him. Therefore, as if afraid of her, Kṛṣṇa closed His eyes. Thus Pūtanā took upon her lap Him who was to be her own annihilation, just as an unintelligent person places a sleeping snake on his lap, thinking the snake to be a rope.

Essence. Like Pūtanā mistaking a sleeping snake for a rope, we keep clasping what will undo us — only Kṛṣṇa, the ananta, is safe to embrace.

Practical applications
  • Name one 'rope' you are presently nursing in your lap — a habit, ambition, or relationship — and ask honestly whether it is in fact antakam, a slow annihilation dressed as comfort.
  • Refuse the artificial affection that masks viṣaya: when someone or something offers sweetness with a hidden agenda, do not return the embrace, even politely.
  • Before sleep tonight, place on your 'lap' only Kṛṣṇa — chant His names with closed eyes, as the infant Kṛṣṇa Himself did, surrendering the day to the ananta who cannot be killed.

BG 6.9

Pūtanā Rākṣasī’s heart was fierce and cruel, but she looked like a very affectionate mother. Thus she resembled a sharp sword in a soft sheath. Although seeing her within the room, Yaśodā and Rohiṇī, overwhelmed by her beauty, did not stop her, but remained silent because she treated the child like a mother.

Essence. Outer beauty can hide inner poison; without Kṛṣṇa-conscious discrimination, even mothers stay silent before a sword in a soft sheath.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are being swayed by surface charm — a person, a feed, a pitch — and pause to ask what intention sits beneath the sheath.
  • Refuse to mistake māyā-mohita politeness for protection; speak up when something feels off around a child, a junior, or yourself, even if the form looks pleasing.
  • Each evening, take shelter of Kṛṣṇa through japa or Gītā 7.13, asking Him to be the Yoga-māyā that guards your home and heart from cruelty dressed as care.

BG 6.10

On that very spot, the fiercely dangerous Rākṣasī took Kṛṣṇa on her lap and pushed her breast into His mouth. The nipple of her breast was smeared with a dangerous, immediately effective poison, but the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, becoming very angry at her, took hold of her breast, squeezed it very hard with both hands, and sucked out both the poison and her life.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's wrath at Pūtanā is real compassion — He punishes the destroyer of children, teaching us that true mercy protects souls, not appearances.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you confuse niceness with compassion, and refuse to stay silent when a real harm is being done to someone's spiritual welfare.
  • Take one hidden 'poisoned offering' in your life — flattery, indulgence, distraction dressed as care — and squeeze it dry by naming it honestly to yourself before the Lord.
  • Speak to or pray for a child today with the awareness that Kṛṣṇa Himself defends the innocent; commit one concrete act to protect the young in your circle.

BG 6.11

Unbearably pressed in every vital point, the demon Pūtanā began to cry, “Please leave me, leave me! Suck my breast no longer!” Perspiring, her eyes wide open and her arms and legs flailing, she cried very loudly again and again.

Essence. Even the demoness Pūtanā, struck at every vital point by Kṛṣṇa, must cry out and release her grip — what we cling to in māyā cannot withstand His touch.

Practical applications
  • Name one viṣaya you have been 'feeding' as Pūtanā fed Kṛṣṇa, and today refuse it one concrete time — skip the scroll, the snack, the second look.
  • When an attachment screams 'leave me, leave me!' as it loosens, do not rescue it; let Kṛṣṇa finish the work and chant His names through the discomfort.
  • Before sleep, offer your day's vital points — ambitions, fears, resentments — to Kṛṣṇa's feet, asking Him to press out whatever is not bhakti.

BG 6.12

As Pūtanā screamed loudly and forcefully, the earth with its mountains, and outer space with its planets, trembled. The lower planets and all directions vibrated, and people fell down, fearing that thunderbolts were falling upon them.

Essence. Pūtanā's scream shakes the three worlds, yet baby Kṛṣṇa is unmoved — surrender to Him is the one shelter when reality itself seems to tremble.

Practical applications
  • When today's news or a personal crisis makes the ground feel like it's shaking, pause and chant Kṛṣṇa's names before reacting.
  • Notice where you give cosmic weight to a 'scream' — a boss's anger, a market drop, a diagnosis — and consciously place that fear at Kṛṣṇa's feet instead.
  • Refuse to mistake loud disturbances for ultimate reality; return to one fixed sādhana (japa, reading, offering) that does not bend to the noise.

BG 6.13

In this way the demon Pūtanā, very much aggrieved because her breast was being attacked by Kṛṣṇa, lost her life. O King Parīkṣit, opening her mouth wide and spreading her arms, legs and hair, she fell down in the pasturing ground in her original form as a Rākṣasī, as Vṛtrāsura had fallen when killed by the thunderbolt of Indra.

Essence. Māyā's disguises collapse at the touch of Kṛṣṇa — what looks nourishing but conceals death is exposed the moment He is allowed to act.

Practical applications
  • Name one 'Pūtanā' in your life today — a pleasure, feed, or relationship that wears a maternal face but quietly drains you — and cut contact with it before nightfall.
  • Before accepting any offering today (food, advice, attention), pause and ask whose interest it actually serves, then refuse what cannot be offered to Kṛṣṇa first.
  • When a glamour you have been chasing falls apart, refuse the urge to rebuild it; sit with the exposed Rākṣasī-form and thank Kṛṣṇa for the unmasking.

BG 6.14

O King Parīkṣit, when the gigantic body of Pūtanā fell to the ground, it smashed all the trees within a limit of twelve miles. Appearing in a gigantic body, she was certainly extraordinary.

Essence. Pūtanā's gigantic, tree-smashing corpse shows what false motherhood truly is — yet even she fell within Kṛṣṇa's range; demoniac scale dies at His touch.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are impressed by sheer scale — wealth, follower counts, institutional size — and remember that Pūtanā too was 'extraordinary.'
  • Refuse to confuse outward magnitude with spiritual potency; measure the people and projects in your life by their relation to Kṛṣṇa, not their footprint.
  • When a 'gigantic' fear or obstacle looms in your day, recall that it falls within Kṛṣṇa's twelve-mile pasture — name it aloud and continue your sādhana.

BG 6.15-17

The Rākṣasī’s mouth was full of teeth, each resembling the front of a plow, her nostrils were deep like mountain caves, and her breasts resembled big slabs of stone fallen from a hill. Her scattered hair was the color of copper. The sockets of her eyes appeared like deep blind wells, her fearful thighs resembled the banks of a river, her arms, legs and feet seemed like big bridges, and her abdomen appeared like a dried-up lake. The hearts, ears and heads of the cowherd men and women were already shocked by the Rākṣasī’s screaming, and when they saw the fierce wonder of her body, they were even more frightened.

Essence. Pūtanā's monstrous form unmasks māyā: what dazzles or terrifies the senses is hollow — only Kṛṣṇa's shelter steadies the heart.

Practical applications
  • Notice one image today (news, scroll, gossip) that hijacks your heart through fear or fascination, and consciously withdraw the senses (vairāgya) back to Kṛṣṇa's name.
  • When dread rises in the body, refuse to let it dictate action — chant the holy name aloud and let abhaya, not adrenaline, decide your next step.
  • Speak to someone who is shaken by a frightening appearance in their life and gently point past the form to the shelter of Kṛṣṇa, not to mere reassurance.

BG 6.18

Without fear, the child Kṛṣṇa was playing on the upper portion of Pūtanā Rākṣasī’s breast, and when the gopīs saw the child’s wonderful activities, they immediately came forward with great jubilation and picked Him up.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa is ṣaḍaiśvarya-pūrṇa — fearless on Pūtanā's breast as on Vaikuṇṭha's throne; take shelter of His unchanging fullness, not your shifting strength.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you try to manufacture abhaya through preparation or control, and instead take one small action grounded in dependence on Kṛṣṇa's protection.
  • When a 'Pūtanā' arrives in disguise — a sweet-seeming offer that would poison your sādhana — refuse it on the spot, trusting that Kṛṣṇa's presence inside you sees through it.
  • Before sleep, recall one moment from your day when you were 'small' and the situation was 'large,' and offer that moment to Kṛṣṇa as proof that His potency, not yours, carried you.

BG 6.19

Thereafter, mother Yaśodā and Rohiṇī, along with the other elderly gopīs, waved about the switch of a cow to give full protection to the child Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Protecting Kṛṣṇa is protecting the cow — Gauḍīya household dharma weaves śaraṇāgati, go-rakṣya, and the vatsalya of elders into one shelter.

Practical applications
  • Today, perform one concrete act of go-rakṣya — buy milk from a protected-cow source, support a gośālā, or learn one traditional use of cow products you have forgotten.
  • Before any task involving a child or dependent, pause and silently entrust them to Kṛṣṇa the way Yaśodā and Rohiṇī did — protection is offered, never owned.
  • Refuse the modern conceit that 'the household' is a small or unspiritual sphere; treat your kitchen, your elders, and your cows as the very arena where Kṛṣṇa is sheltered.

BG 6.20

The child was thoroughly washed with cow urine and then smeared with the dust raised by the movements of the cows. Then different names of the Lord were applied with cow dung on twelve different parts of His body, beginning with the forehead, as done in applying tilaka. In this way, the child was given protection.

Essence. Sanctify the body with what Kṛṣṇa has touched — protection comes from marking every limb with His names, not from our own arrangements.

Practical applications
  • Mark the start of your day by audibly placing Kṛṣṇa's names over your body and work — forehead, hands, heart — before checking any device.
  • Refuse to lean on secular safeguards alone today; pair every practical precaution (lock, seatbelt, plan) with a brief invocation of a name of the Lord.
  • Honor what the devotees honor — treat cow-related substances, prasāda, and Vaiṣṇava remnants as carriers of protection, not superstition to be argued away.

BG 6.21

The gopīs first executed the process of ācamana, drinking a sip of water from the right hand. They purified their bodies and hands with the nyāsa-mantra and then applied the same mantra upon the body of the child.

Essence. Before approaching Kṛṣṇa or the child entrusted to you, purify body and hands with viṣṇu-mantra — sacred care begins with self-purification.

Practical applications
  • Before sitting for japa or study today, perform ācamana with attention, not as ritual habit but as a deliberate purification of the instruments you will offer to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse to handle anything sacred — a Deity, a tulasī leaf, prasāda, a child in your care — until you have first cleaned your hands and quieted your mind with His name.
  • Notice where you have inherited 'Indian culture' as fashion but lost its purifying disciplines, and reclaim one small Vedic practice (ācamana, mantra before meals) starting tonight.

BG 6.22-23

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī informed Mahārāja Parīkṣit that the gopīs, following the proper system, protected Kṛṣṇa, their child, with this mantra.] May Aja protect Your legs, may Maṇimān protect Your knees, Yajña Your thighs, Acyuta the upper part of Your waist, and Hayagrīva Your abdomen. May Keśava protect Your heart, Īśa Your chest, the sun-god Your neck, Viṣṇu Your arms, Urukrama Your face, and Īśvara Your head. May Cakrī protect You from the front; may Śrī Hari, Gadādharī, the carrier of the club, protect You from the back; and may the carrier of the bow, who is known as the enemy of Madhu, and Lord Ajana, the carrier of the sword, protect Your two sides. May Lord Urugāya, the carrier of the conchshell, protect You from all corners; may Upendra protect You from above; may Garuḍa protect You on the ground; and may Lord Haladhara, the Supreme Person, protect You on all sides.

Essence. The gopīs invoke Kṛṣṇa's own forms to guard every limb of their child — true protection comes from placing one's body, head to toe, under His personal shelter.

Practical applications
  • Begin your morning by mentally offering each part of your body — feet, knees, heart, head — to Kṛṣṇa's protection before stepping into the day's work.
  • When sending a child or loved one out, pause and audibly invoke Kṛṣṇa's names over them rather than relying solely on locks, helmets, and apps.
  • Refuse the modern conceit that safety is engineered alone; treat the Vedic mantra-shelter as the first line of defense, not a quaint supplement.

BG 6.24

May Hṛṣīkeśa protect Your senses, and Nārāyaṇa Your life air. May the master of Śvetadvīpa protect the core of Your heart, and may Lord Yogeśvara protect Your mind.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's many forms — Hṛṣīkeśa, Nārāyaṇa, Yogeśvara — guard the senses, prāṇa, heart, and mind; surrender each to Him by name today.

Practical applications
  • Before engaging the senses today, silently invoke Hṛṣīkeśa as their true master and offer their use back to Him.
  • When fear or agitation grips the heart, call on the Lord of Śvetadvīpa and refuse to let the mind run unguarded.
  • Begin the day naming each layer — senses, prāṇa, heart, mind — and place each under Kṛṣṇa's specific protection rather than relying on your own vigilance.

BG 6.25-26

May Lord Pṛśnigarbha protect Your intelligence, and the Supreme Personality of Godhead Your soul. While You are playing, may Govinda protect You, and while You are sleeping may Mādhava protect You. May Lord Vaikuṇṭha protect You while You are walking, and may Lord Nārāyaṇa, the husband of the goddess of fortune, protect You while You are sitting. Similarly, may Lord Yajñabhuk, the fearful enemy of all evil planets, always protect You while You enjoy life.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's plenary expansions guard every act — playing, sleeping, walking, sitting, eating — so entrust each ordinary motion of the day to Him.

Practical applications
  • Before each transition today — rising, walking out, sitting at work, eating, lying down — silently call on Kṛṣṇa by one of His names as Govinda, Mādhava, or Nārāyaṇa.
  • Refuse the illusion of self-protection: when fear of accident, illness, or unseen forces arises, remember that Yajñabhuk is the real shield and that anxious self-management is wasted ahaṅkāra.
  • Treat one ordinary meal today as offered food eaten under Yajñabhuk's protection — pause, offer it to Kṛṣṇa, and eat without distraction or grievance.

BG 6.27-29

The evil witches known as Ḍākinīs, Yātudhānīs and Kuṣmāṇḍas are the greatest enemies of children, and the evil spirits like Bhūtas, Pretas, Piśācas, Yakṣas, Rākṣasas and Vināyakas, as well as witches like Koṭarā, Revatī, Jyeṣṭhā, Pūtanā and Mātṛkā, are always ready to give trouble to the body, the life air and the senses, causing loss of memory, madness and bad dreams. Like the most experienced evil stars, they all create great disturbances, especially for children, but one can vanquish them simply by uttering Lord Viṣṇu’s name, for when Lord Viṣṇu’s name resounds, all of them become afraid and go away.

Essence. In a world full of unseen disturbances, chant Lord Viṣṇu's names — His holy name itself is the protection no medicine or precaution can replace.

Practical applications
  • Begin the day by audibly chanting 'Viṣṇu, Viṣṇu, Viṣṇu' or the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra before reaching for your phone, planting His name as the first sound of your day.
  • When anxiety, bad dreams, or intrusive thoughts arise, refuse to fight them mentally — instead chant Kṛṣṇa's name aloud and let the disturbance retreat on its own.
  • While taking medicine, food, or any precaution today, remember auṣadhi cintayet viṣṇum — silently offer it to Viṣṇu, acknowledging Him as the real protector behind every remedy.

BG 6.30

Śrīla Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: All the gopīs, headed by mother Yaśodā, were bound by maternal affection. After they thus chanted mantras to protect the child, mother Yaśodā gave the child the nipple of her breast to suck and then got Him to lie down on His bed.

Essence. Yaśodā's anxious tending of baby Kṛṣṇa shows that bhakti expresses itself as concrete, embodied care — love verified by attention, not sentiment.

Practical applications
  • Tend to one dependent — child, elder, or junior — today with the same patient, hands-on attentiveness Yaśodā gave Kṛṣṇa, not delegated and not rushed.
  • Refuse the false choice between 'spiritual' acts and bodily care; offer the next ordinary nurturing task (feeding, settling, comforting) directly to Kṛṣṇa as sevā.
  • Notice where you stop at chanting or wishing and skip the practical follow-through, and finish at least one such task today before lying down to rest.

BG 6.31

Meanwhile, all the cowherd men, headed by Nanda Mahārāja, returned from Mathurā, and when they saw on the way the gigantic body of Pūtanā lying dead, they were struck with great wonder.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa silently destroys a Pūtanā in your life, you may return to find only the wreckage — wonder, then trace it back to His mercy.

Practical applications
  • Notice one disaster Kṛṣṇa quietly averted while you were elsewhere today, and name it aloud as His protection rather than your luck.
  • When you encounter the 'gigantic corpse' of a problem already resolved without you, refuse the impulse to credit chance — sit with the wonder and offer thanks.
  • Speak to one family member tonight about a danger Kṛṣṇa removed from your household, so the wonder of Nanda Mahārāja becomes shared kṛtajñatā, not private sentiment.

BG 6.32

Nanda Mahārāja and the other gopas exclaimed: My dear friends, you must know that Ānakadundubhi, Vasudeva, has become a great saint or a master of mystic power. Otherwise how could he have foreseen this calamity and predicted it to us?

Essence. Vasudeva's foresight came from sober study, not mystic display — clear seeing in Kṛṣṇa's service is itself the saint's true power.

Practical applications
  • Study one concrete situation in your life today — at work, at home — the way Vasudeva studied Kaṁsa, and warn whoever needs warning before the calamity lands.
  • Refuse to mistake genuine devotional discernment for mystical theatrics; honor the quiet, sober vision of those serving Kṛṣṇa around you.
  • Notice when you, like Nanda Mahārāja, attribute another's clear thinking to magic — and instead ask what sober attention they paid that you skipped.

BG 6.33

The inhabitants of Vraja cut the gigantic body of Pūtanā into pieces with the help of axes. Then they threw the pieces far away, covered them with wood and burned them to ashes.

Essence. What threatens the soul must be uprooted completely — half-measures with māyā let the serpent revive; finish what Kṛṣṇa has already slain.

Practical applications
  • Identify one habit Kṛṣṇa's mercy has already weakened in you, and today cut off its remaining 'limbs' — the contacts, apps, or settings that let it regrow.
  • Refuse the comfort of partial vairāgya: when you renounce a viṣaya, also burn the kindling around it so it cannot rejoin with air and revive.
  • Before sleep, name one rajas/tamas tendency you 'killed' this week and ask honestly whether you have truly burned it or merely buried it shallow.

BG 6.34

Because of Kṛṣṇa’s having sucked the breast of the Rākṣasī Pūtanā, when Kṛṣṇa killed her she was immediately freed of all material contamination. Her sinful reactions automatically vanished, and therefore when her gigantic body was being burnt, the smoke emanating from her body was fragrant like aguru incense.

Essence. Even unwilling, indirect service to Kṛṣṇa purifies absolutely — render Him service today, however small, and contamination begins to burn off.

Practical applications
  • Engage one sense in direct service to Kṛṣṇa today — hear a verse aloud, chant His name, or cook and offer a meal — and trust that it works on you even when you feel unmoved.
  • Refuse the lie that you must first be 'pure enough' to approach Kṛṣṇa; act now in śravaṇa-kīrtana and let the act itself do the cleansing.
  • Notice someone you have written off as too fallen — a co-worker, a family member — and find one way to involve them in hearing or chanting about Kṛṣṇa today.

BG 6.35-36

Pūtanā was always hankering for the blood of human children, and with that desire she came to kill Kṛṣṇa; but because she offered her breast to the Lord, she attained the greatest achievement. What then is to be said of those who had natural devotion and affection for Kṛṣṇa as mothers and who offered Him their breasts to suck or offered something very dear, as a mother offers something to a child?

Essence. Even Pūtanā, who came to kill, gained the highest goal by offering her breast to Kṛṣṇa — how much more those who serve Him with pure parental love.

Practical applications
  • Offer one ordinary act today — a meal, a task, a gesture — directly to Kṛṣṇa with conscious affection, not as ritual but as a mother feeds her child.
  • Notice when your service is mechanical or grudging, and deliberately add the bhāva of vātsalya — "this is for Him, as for my own dear one."
  • Refuse the doubt that your offering is too small; if Pūtanā's poisoned breast won sad-gatim, your sincere offering today is not wasted.

BG 6.37-38

The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is always situated within the core of the heart of the pure devotee, and He is always offered prayers by such worshipable personalities as Lord Brahmā and Lord Śiva. Because Kṛṣṇa embraced Pūtanā’s body with great pleasure and sucked her breast, although she was a great witch, she attained the position of a mother in the transcendental world and thus achieved the highest perfection. What then is to be said of the cows whose nipples Kṛṣṇa sucked with great pleasure and who offered their milk very jubilantly with affection exactly like that of a mother?

Essence. Even a witch who only mimicked motherhood toward Kṛṣṇa was promoted to Vaikuṇṭha — any service offered to Him, knowingly or not, never goes wasted.

Practical applications
  • Offer one ordinary act of care today — feeding someone, tending a cow, serving a guest — explicitly to Kṛṣṇa, trusting He receives even imperfect offerings.
  • Notice when you dismiss your own bhakti as 'not sincere enough' and remember Pūtanā: Kṛṣṇa weighs the form of the service, not only the motive behind it.
  • Refuse to write anyone off as beyond reclamation; if even Pūtanā attained jananī-gati, pray for the hardest heart you know rather than condemning it.

BG 6.39-40

The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is the bestower of many benedictions, including liberation [kaivalya], or oneness with the Brahman effulgence. For that Personality of Godhead, the gopīs always felt maternal love, and Kṛṣṇa sucked their breasts with full satisfaction. Therefore, because of their relationship as mother and son, although the gopīs were engaged in various family activities, one should never think that they returned to this material world after leaving their bodies.

Essence. Any genuine relationship with Kṛṣṇa — as son, friend, master, or beloved — ends saṁsāra; ordinary roles awaken in ignorance, but the same roles centered on Him guarantee return to Goloka.

Practical applications
  • Choose one daily relationship — child, parent, friend, spouse — and consciously redirect its central affection toward Kṛṣṇa, treating the human bond as a doorway, not the destination.
  • Before sleep tonight, name the people you call 'mine' and offer each one to Kṛṣṇa, refusing the ajñāna-sambhavaḥ illusion that these ties are ultimate.
  • Stay in your household duties (kṛṣṇera saṁsāra) but cut one anācāra — one sinful or sloppy habit — today, so family life itself becomes the path back to Godhead.

BG 6.41

Upon smelling the fragrance of the smoke emanating from Pūtanā’s burning body, many inhabitants of Vrajabhūmi in distant places were astonished. “Where is this fragrance coming from?” they asked. Thus they went to the spot where Pūtanā’s body was being burnt.

Essence. When a soul aggressor is consumed in Kṛṣṇa's service, even her remains exude divine fragrance — proximity to Him purifies what would otherwise reek.

Practical applications
  • Notice today one association or input you assume is 'toxic smoke' and ask whether contact with Kṛṣṇa's service could transmute it into something fragrant.
  • Offer one ordinary task — cooking, cleaning, a work email — explicitly to Kṛṣṇa and watch how its 'aroma' changes for those around you.
  • Refuse to judge a person solely by their outward role; investigate, like the Vrajavāsīs tracking the scent, what unseen contact with the Lord may be at work.

BG 6.42

When the inhabitants of Vraja who had come from distant places heard the whole story of how Pūtanā had come and then been killed by Kṛṣṇa, they were certainly astonished, and they offered their blessings to the child for His wonderful deed of killing Pūtanā. Nanda Mahārāja, of course, was very much obliged to Vasudeva, who had foreseen the incident, and simply thanked him, thinking how wonderful Vasudeva was.

Essence. Hearing how the infant Kṛṣṇa slew Pūtanā, Vraja blesses Him and Nanda marvels at Vasudeva — the devotee sees Kṛṣṇa's protection in every averted danger.

Practical applications
  • Recall one 'near-miss' from your past today and explicitly thank Kṛṣṇa for the protection you did not see at the time.
  • When someone shares good news of a danger averted, respond like the Vrajavāsīs — offer blessings and glorify Kṛṣṇa instead of crediting luck.
  • Speak to an elder devotee this week the way Nanda thanked Vasudeva — acknowledge concretely how their foresight has shielded your bhakti.

BG 6.43

O Mahārāja Parīkṣit, best of the Kurus, Nanda Mahārāja was very liberal and simple. He immediately took his son Kṛṣṇa on his lap as if Kṛṣṇa had returned from death, and by formally smelling his son’s head, Nanda Mahārāja undoubtedly enjoyed transcendental bliss.

Essence. Nanda's simple, loving heart receives Kṛṣṇa back as if from death — the affection of a pure devotee untouched by speculation about Yoga-māyā's pastimes.

Practical applications
  • When a loved one returns safely from danger today, pause and offer that wave of relief to Kṛṣṇa rather than crediting luck or your own vigilance.
  • Notice where you complicate simple devotion with speculation, and choose Nanda's udāra-dhīḥ — liberal, simple-hearted trust — in one concrete exchange today.
  • Greet someone you love with a small, formal gesture of affection (a touch on the head, a folded-hand praṇāma) and silently see Kṛṣṇa in them.

BG 6.44

Any person who hears with faith and devotion about how Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, killed Pūtanā, and who thus invests his hearing in such childhood pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, certainly attains attachment for Govinda, the supreme, original person.

Essence. Hearing Kṛṣṇa's wonderful childhood pastimes — like the slaying of Pūtanā — with faith awakens real attachment (rati) for Govinda Himself.

Practical applications
  • Today, read or hear the Pūtanā-mokṣa narration from Kṛṣṇa Book with śraddhā — not as folklore, but as the direct medicine for material entanglement.
  • Replace one segment of your usual scrolling or background noise with audio of Kṛṣṇa's bālya-līlā, and notice what shifts in your mood by evening.
  • Refuse the impersonalist habit of treating these pastimes as 'symbolic' — receive them as the literal activities of Govinda, the ādi-puruṣa, and let that faith deepen your hearing.
