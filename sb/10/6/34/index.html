Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.6.34

दह्यमानस्य देहस्य धूमश्चागुरुसौरभ: । उत्थित: कृष्णनिर्भुक्तसपद्याहतपाप्मन: ॥ ३४ ॥

[dahyamaanasya dehasya]

[dhoomash chaaguru-saurabhah]

[utthitah krishna-nirbhukta-]

Important Words
  • aguru - saurabhaḥ — the fragrance of aguru (incense)
    Compound — aguru (a fragrant heartwood) + saurabha (fragrance). The exclusive scent of temple worship and Deity sevā. The verse declares Pūtanā's body, post-Kṛṣṇa-contact, fit for a maṇḍira's incense brazier. Stunning theological inversion: the rākṣasī now smells like the ārati altar.
  • kṛṣṇa - nirbhukta — (because of being) drunk by Kṛṣṇa
    Compound — Kṛṣṇa + nirbhukta (entirely consumed). The reason is named explicitly. Gauḍīya doctrine: any contact in which Kṛṣṇa is the active partner — even if forced, even if hostile — purifies. Hence Caitanya Mahāprabhu's principle that even hating Kṛṣṇa intensely (dveṣa-bhakti) can deliver, as it did Kaṁsa and Śiśupāla.
  • āhata - pāpmanaḥ — her sinful reactions destroyed
    Compound — āhata (struck down) + pāpman (sin) + genitive. The pāpas are not negotiated, atoned, or worked off — they are 'struck down' instantly by contact with Bhagavān. Gauḍīya pratimukti-doctrine in compressed form.
  • sapadi — immediately
    Indeclinable — at once. Speed matters in the doctrine: nāma- or sparśa-contact with Kṛṣṇa does not wait for ripening; sapadi the result follows. Same word the Bhāgavata uses for the gajendra-mokṣa's instantaneousness.
  • dahyamānasya dehasya — (of) the body being burnt
    Adjacent genitive phrase — dahyamānasya (being burnt, present passive participle) + dehasya (of the body). The grammar is poignant — the very body that was āhata-pāpman has now become aguru-saurabha. The transformation happens on the pyre, in real time.
Because of Kṛṣṇa’s having sucked the breast of the Rākṣasī Pūtanā, when Kṛṣṇa killed her she was immediately freed of all material contamination. Her sinful reactions automatically vanished, and therefore when her gigantic body was being burnt, the smoke emanating from her body was fragrant like aguru incense.
Purport

Such are the effects of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If one somehow or other becomes Kṛṣṇa conscious by applying his senses in the service of the Lord, one is immediately freed from material contamination. Śṛṇvatāṁ sva-kathāḥ kṛṣṇaḥ puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ ( Bhāg. 1.2.17 ). Hearing about the activities of Kṛṣṇa is the beginning of purified life. Puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ: simply by hearing and chanting, one becomes purified. Therefore, in discharging devotional service, śravaṇa-kīrtana (hearing and chanting) is most important. Then, with purified senses, one begins to render service to the Lord ( hṛṣīkeṇa hṛṣīkeśa-sevanam ). Bhaktir ucyate: this is called bhakti. When Pūtanā was somehow or other, directly or indirectly, induced to render some service to the Lord by feeding Him with her breast, she was immediately purified, so much so that when her nasty material body was burnt to ashes, it gave off the fragrance of aguru, the most agreeably scented herb.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Because of Kṛṣṇa's having sucked the breast of the Rākṣasī Pūtanā, when Kṛṣṇa killed her she was immediately freed of all material contamination. Her sinful reactions automatically vanished, and therefore when her gigantic body was being burnt, the smoke emanating from her body was fragrant like aguru incense.
Commentary

This verse describes how Putana’s body became sanctified by the touch of Krsna’s mouth. Her body became purified of all sins (ahata papma) at the moment (sapadi) that Krsna began drinking from her breast (krsna nirbhukta).

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The smoke from the burning body was scented with aguru. The word ca indicates that even her body became fragrant because of being enjoyed by Kṛṣṇa, having been touched by Kṛṣṇa’s mouth. Kṛṣṇa, filled with unlimited attractions such as his fragrant spiritual body, had constantly drunk (nirbhukta) her milk along with her life airs. For anyone who smelled, immediately on smelling the smoke all sins were completely destroyed (ā-hata). This shows the power of the Lord’s remnants. What then to say of the power of pure items offered with devotion? This is expressed in the next verse also.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

The smoke from the burning body was scented with aguru. The word ca indicates that even her body became fragrant because of being enjoyed by Kṛṣṇa, having been touched by Kṛṣṇa’s mouth. Kṛṣṇa, filled with unlimited attractions such as his fragrant spiritual body, had constantly drunk (nirbhukta) her milk along with her life airs. For anyone who smelled the smoke, immediately all sins were completely destroyed (ā-hata). This should be said. Just as remnants of the Lord’ offering offered by those desiring liberation become pure nectar, destroying the great sickness of saṁsāra, a great poison obstructing the Lord, the body of Pūtana, the remnant of the lord’s enjoyment, became free of all faults and most purifying. Thus it is suitable that the smoke was fragrant.

Personal Guidance
Even unwilling, indirect service to Kṛṣṇa purifies absolutely — render Him service today, however small, and contamination begins to burn off.
Practical Applications
  • Engage one sense in direct service to Kṛṣṇa today — hear a verse aloud, chant His name, or cook and offer a meal — and trust that it works on you even when you feel unmoved.
  • Refuse the lie that you must first be 'pure enough' to approach Kṛṣṇa; act now in śravaṇa-kīrtana and let the act itself do the cleansing.
  • Notice someone you have written off as too fallen — a co-worker, a family member — and find one way to involve them in hearing or chanting about Kṛṣṇa today.
Analogy

Pūtanā's burning corpse exhaling aguru fragrance is the soaked rag thrown into a sandalwood fire and coming out smelling of sandal — the fire has not negotiated with the rag, it has consumed it, and the rag's own scent is replaced by what it touched. Service to Kṛṣṇa, however imperfect, is fire of that grade.

Stories
Valmīki the dacoit

Before he was the ādi-kavi who sang the Rāmāyaṇa, Vālmīki was a forest robber. Tradition records that the sage Nārada gave him the name of Rāma — even backwards, 'Marā, Marā' — and he chanted it for thousands of years until an anthill grew over him. He emerged as Vālmīki, his earlier name and karma burned off.

Pūtanā's aguru-saurabha in 10.6.34 is the same trajectory in a single afternoon's heat: contact with Kṛṣṇa burns the pāpa and leaves only fragrance behind.
