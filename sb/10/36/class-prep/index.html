Chapter 36 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 36.1

Śukadeva Goswāmī said: The demon Ariṣṭa then came to the cowherd village. Appearing in the form of a bull with a large hump, he made the earth tremble as he tore it apart with his hooves.

Essence. The demon Ariṣṭa arrives at the cowherd village in the form of a humped bull, making the earth tremble. Even Vṛndāvana is not free of intrusion — danger times its arrival to disrupt sacred moments like the rāsa dance.

BG 36.2

Ariṣṭāsura bellowed very harshly and pawed the ground. With his tail raised and his eyes glaring, he began to tear up the embankments with the tips of his horns, every now and then passing a little stool and urine.

Essence. Ariṣṭa bellows, glares, paws the ground, tears embankments, and even excretes carelessly. Demonic energy is ugly in detail — it is not just powerful, it is gross, undignified, and openly contemptuous.

BG 36.3-4

My dear King, clouds hovered about sharp-horned Ariṣṭāsura’s hump, mistaking it for a mountain, and when the cowherd men and ladies caught sight of the demon, they were struck with terror. Indeed, the strident reverberation of his roar so frightened the pregnant cows and women that they lost their fetuses in miscarriages.

Essence. Clouds mistake Ariṣṭa's hump for a mountain; the cowherds are terror-stricken; pregnant cows and women miscarry from his roar. Demonic disturbance has real, irreversible costs — it kills futures, not just present comfort.

BG 36.5

The domestic animals fled the pasture in fear, O King, and all the inhabitants rushed to Lord Govinda for shelter, crying, “Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa!”

Essence. Domestic animals flee and all inhabitants rush to Govinda crying, 'Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa!' In real danger devotees don't theorize — they run to a single shelter and call a single name.

BG 36.6

When the Supreme Lord saw the cowherd community distraught and fleeing in fear, He calmed them, saying, “Don’t be afraid.” Then He called out to the bull demon as follows.

Essence. Seeing the community distraught, Kṛṣṇa calms them with 'Don't be afraid,' then turns to address the bull demon directly. The Lord first comforts His own people and only then deals with the threat — order of operations is care first, combat second.

BG 36.7

You fool! What do you think you’re doing, you wicked rascal, frightening the cowherd community and their animals when I am here just to punish corrupt miscreants like you!

Essence. Kṛṣṇa calls Ariṣṭa 'fool' and 'wicked rascal' and announces He is here precisely to punish corrupt miscreants. The Lord names evil bluntly — politeness with demons is not divine etiquette.

BG 36.8

Having spoken these words, the infallible Lord Hari slapped His arms with His palms, further angering Ariṣṭa with the loud sound. The Lord then casually threw His mighty, serpentine arm over the shoulder of a friend and stood facing the demon.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa slaps His arms, throws an arm casually over a friend's shoulder, and faces the demon — showing contempt rather than fear. Confidence in the Lord lets us face threats with relaxed shoulders, not braced ones.

BG 36.9

Thus provoked, Ariṣṭa pawed the ground with one of his hooves and then, with the clouds hovering around his upraised tail, furiously charged Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Provoked, Ariṣṭa paws the ground and, with clouds around his raised tail, charges Kṛṣṇa furiously. Demons attack hardest when challenged; the threat's escalation is not a sign of failure but of contact with the truth.

BG 36.10

Pointing the tips of his horns straight ahead and glaring menacingly at Lord Kṛṣṇa from the corners of his bloodshot eyes, Ariṣṭa rushed toward Him at full speed, like a thunderbolt hurled by Indra.

Essence. Eyes bloodshot, horns lowered, Ariṣṭa rushes at Kṛṣṇa like Indra's thunderbolt. Even fully aimed at the Lord, the demon's speed and rage do not change the outcome — only delay it slightly.

BG 36.11

The Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa seized Ariṣṭāsura by the horns and threw him back eighteen steps, just as an elephant might do when fighting a rival elephant.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa seizes Ariṣṭa by the horns and throws him back eighteen steps, like a rival elephant. The Lord doesn't dodge — He engages with the demon's strength on its own terms and exceeds it.

BG 36.12

Thus repulsed by the Supreme Lord, the bull demon got up and, breathing hard and sweating all over his body, again charged Him in a mindless rage.

Essence. Repulsed, the bull demon rises, breathing hard and sweating, and charges again in mindless rage. Inner demons rarely give up after one defeat; sustained spiritual life expects repeated rounds.

BG 36.13

As Ariṣṭa attacked, Lord Kṛṣṇa seized him by the horns and knocked him to the ground with His foot. The Lord then thrashed him as if he were a wet cloth, and finally He yanked out one of the demon’s horns and struck him with it until he lay prostrate.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa seizes Ariṣṭa's horns again, knocks him down with His foot, thrashes him like a wet cloth, yanks out a horn, and beats him with it. When the Lord finishes a demon, He finishes it thoroughly — using its own weapon against it.

BG 36.14

Vomiting blood and profusely excreting stool and urine, kicking his legs and rolling his eyes about, Ariṣṭāsura thus went painfully to the abode of Death. The demigods honored Lord Kṛṣṇa by scattering flowers upon Him.

Essence. Vomiting blood, excreting, rolling his eyes, Ariṣṭa dies painfully while demigods shower flowers on Kṛṣṇa. Demonic death is ugly; the Lord's victory is honored from above. Material end-of-life and divine glorification are different stories happening at once.

BG 36.15

Having thus killed the bull demon Ariṣṭa, He who is a festival for the gopīs’ eyes entered the cowherd village with Balarāma.

Essence. After killing Ariṣṭa, Kṛṣṇa — 'a festival for the gopīs' eyes' — enters the cowherd village with Balarāma. The same hands that destroy demons are the ones the gopīs feast their eyes on; He is thunderbolt and rose, depending on the heart that meets Him.

BG 36.16

After Ariṣṭāsura had been killed by Kṛṣṇa, who acts wonderfully, Nārada Muni went to speak to King Kaṁsa. That powerful sage of godly vision addressed the King as follows.

Essence. Nārada Muni — whose name itself means 'seeing God' — comes to speak to Kaṁsa. Even when the Lord is doing miracles, His servants are independently moving plot lines forward; the Lord coordinates many devotees at once.

BG 36.17

[Nārada told Kaṁsa:] Yaśodā’s child was actually a daughter, and Kṛṣṇa is the son of Devakī. Also, Rāma is the son of Rohiṇī. Out of fear, Vasudeva entrusted Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to his friend Nanda Mahārāja, and it is these two boys who have killed your men.

Essence. Nārada tells Kaṁsa the truth — Yaśodā's child was actually a daughter, Kṛṣṇa is Devakī's son, and these are the boys who killed his men. Truth-telling accelerates the wicked toward their fate, even when delivered by a saint.

BG 36.18

Upon hearing this, the master of the Bhojas became furious and lost control of his senses. He picked up a sharp sword to kill Vasudeva.

Essence. Furious and out of control, Kaṁsa picks up a sharp sword to kill Vasudeva. The wicked respond to truth not with reflection but with renewed violence; this is how rage exposes itself.

BG 36.19

But Nārada restrained Kaṁsa by reminding him that it was the two sons of Vasudeva who would cause his death. Kaṁsa then had Vasudeva and his wife shackled in iron chains.

Essence. Nārada restrains Kaṁsa by reminding him only Vasudeva's sons will kill him; Kaṁsa then chains Vasudeva and Devakī. Even partial wisdom in the wicked produces only partial restraint — they still imprison those they cannot quite kill.

BG 36.20

After Nārada left, King Kaṁsa summoned Keśī and ordered him, “Go kill Rāma and Kṛṣṇa.”

Essence. Kaṁsa summons the horse demon Keśī and orders him to kill Rāma and Kṛṣṇa. Tyrants outsource their fears; whatever they cannot do themselves, they send agents to attempt.

BG 36.21

The King of the Bhojas next called for his ministers, headed by Muṣṭika, Cāṇūra, Śala and Tośala, and also for his elephant-keepers. The King addressed them as follows.

Essence. Kaṁsa calls his ministers — Muṣṭika, Cāṇūra, Śala, Tośala — and his elephant keepers to coordinate the plan. Evil is administrative; it convenes meetings and divides labor.

BG 36.22-23

My dear heroic Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika, please hear this. Rāma and Kṛṣṇa, the sons of Ānakadundubhi [Vasudeva], are living in Nanda’s cowherd village. It has been predicted that these two boys will be the cause of my death. When They are brought here, kill Them on the pretext of engaging Them in a wrestling match.

Essence. Kaṁsa tells Cāṇūra and Muṣṭika to kill Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma under the pretext of a wrestling match. Deception loves the disguise of fair play; appearances of sport often cover schemes of harm.

BG 36.24

Erect a wrestling ring with many surrounding viewing stands, and bring all the residents of the city and the outlying districts to see the open competition.

Essence. A wrestling ring is erected, viewing stands built, residents brought in — a festive cover for assassination. Evil often disguises itself as community spectacle to keep its victims unsuspecting.

BG 36.25

You, elephant-keeper, my good man, should position the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa at the entrance to the wrestling arena and have him kill my two enemies.

Essence. Kaṁsa orders the elephant Kuvalayāpīḍa positioned at the arena entrance to kill the boys. The wicked prepare layered traps; if one fails the next is waiting. So the devotee must trust the Lord meets every layer.

BG 36.26

Commence the bow sacrifice on the Caturdaśī day in accordance with the relevant Vedic injunctions. In ritual slaughter offer the appropriate kinds of animals to the magnanimous Lord Śiva.

Essence. Kaṁsa orders a bow sacrifice on Caturdaśī, slaughtering animals to magnanimous Lord Śiva. Demonic religion uses sacred forms for selfish ends; rituals can be hijacked by ambition.

BG 36.27

Having thus commanded his ministers, Kaṁsa next called for Akrūra, the most eminent of the Yadus. Kaṁsa knew the art of securing personal advantage, and thus he took Akrūra’s hand in his own and spoke to him as follows.

Essence. Kaṁsa next calls Akrūra, eminent among Yadus, taking his hand and speaking sweetly. Manipulators reserve gentle words for those whose cooperation they most need — flattery is often a request in disguise.

BG 36.28

My dear Akrūra, most charitable one, please do me a friendly favor out of respect. Among the Bhojas and Vṛṣṇis, there is no one else as kind to us as you.

Essence. Kaṁsa calls Akrūra 'most charitable one' and the kindest of the Bhojas and Vṛṣṇis — soliciting a friendly favor 'out of respect.' Demons appeal to a devotee's good qualities to enlist him in evil; recognizing this is part of discernment.

BG 36.29

Gentle Akrūra, you always carry out your duties soberly, and therefore I am depending on you, just as powerful Indra took shelter of Lord Viṣṇu to achieve his goals.

Essence. Kaṁsa says he depends on sober Akrūra as Indra depends on Viṣṇu — flattering the devotee with a divine comparison. Manipulative humility imitates real surrender to extract service.

BG 36.30

Please go to Nanda’s village, where the two sons of Ānakadundubhi are living, and without delay bring Them here on this chariot.

Essence. Kaṁsa asks Akrūra to bring the two boys back on a brand-new attractive chariot — counting on innocence and curiosity. The wicked design their bait with care; even Kṛṣṇa's devotees may be tempted by attractive vehicles for the wrong missions.

BG 36.31

The demigods, who are under the protection of Viṣṇu, have sent these two boys as my death. Bring Them here, and also have Nanda and the other cowherd men come with gifts of tribute.

Essence. Kaṁsa says demigods under Viṣṇu's protection have sent these two as his death; bring them with Nanda and tribute. The wicked know who is destroying them but still try to engineer their way out; their clarity does not save them.

BG 36.32

After you bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, I will have Them killed by my elephant, who is as powerful as death itself. And if by chance They escape from him, I will have Them killed by my wrestlers, who are as strong as lightning.

Essence. Kaṁsa plans to have Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma killed first by his death-powerful elephant, then by lightning-strong wrestlers. Backup plans against the Lord are absurd, yet the wicked stack them; the Lord's protection makes every plan equally futile.

BG 36.33

When these two have been killed, I will kill Vasudeva and all Their lamenting relatives — the Vṛṣṇis, Bhojas and Daśārhas.

Essence. Kaṁsa intends to then kill Vasudeva and all the lamenting Vṛṣṇis, Bhojas and Daśārhas. Wickedness doesn't stop at its declared target — it expands to consume everyone connected. Even today such leaders make and execute these plans.

BG 36.34

I will also kill my old father, Ugrasena, who is greedy for my kingdom, and I will kill his brother Devaka and all my other enemies as well.

Essence. Kaṁsa adds his own old father Ugrasena (greedy for the kingdom in his eyes), Devaka, and all other enemies to the list. Tyranny is paranoid; it always finds new enemies, including family.

BG 36.35

Then, my friend, this earth will be free of thorns.

Essence. Then, Kaṁsa says, this earth will be free of thorns. The wicked believe a world cleared of opponents will finally be comfortable — but the thorn is in the heart that lists them.

BG 36.36

My elder relative Jarāsandha and my dear friend Dvivida are solid well-wishers of mine, as are Śambara, Naraka and Bāṇa. I will use them all to kill off those kings who are allied with the demigods, and then I will rule the earth.

Essence. Kaṁsa names allies — Jarāsandha, Dvivida, Śambara, Naraka, Bāṇa — to kill kings allied with the demigods, then rule the earth. Evil counts on its network; demonic alliances mirror but invert the saṅga of devotees.

BG 36.37

Now that you understand my intentions, please go at once and bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to watch the bow sacrifice and see the opulence of the Yadus’ capital.

Essence. Kaṁsa tells Akrūra to bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to watch the bow sacrifice and see Mathurā's opulence. The wicked use spectacle and luxury as bait — assuming everyone responds to grandeur as they themselves do.

BG 36.38

Śrī Akrūra said: O King, you have expertly devised a process to free yourself of misfortune. Still, one should be equal in success and failure, since it is certainly destiny that produces the results of one’s work.

Essence. Akrūra politely says Kaṁsa's plan to escape misfortune is expertly devised, yet one should be equal in success and failure since destiny shapes results. Wise speech can carry hidden dissent — Akrūra speaks correctly even as he knows Kaṁsa is doomed.

BG 36.39

An ordinary person is determined to act on his desires even when fate prevents their fulfillment. Therefore he encounters both happiness and distress. Yet even though such is the case, I will execute your order.

Essence. Akrūra observes that ordinary persons act on their desires even when fate prevents fulfillment, so they encounter happiness and distress alike — yet he will execute the order. As subject he obeys outwardly; as devotee he knows the outcome.

BG 36.40

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Having thus instructed Akrūra, King Kaṁsa dismissed his ministers and retired to his quarters, and Akrūra returned home.

Essence. Having instructed Akrūra, Kaṁsa dismisses his ministers and retires; Akrūra returns home. The scheming day ends in ordinary withdrawal — but Kṛṣṇa's plan is now nested inside Kaṁsa's plan, and only one will survive the night.
