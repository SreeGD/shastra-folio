Chapter 54 · Class prep

BG 54.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Having thus spoken, all those infuriated kings donned their armor and mounted their conveyances. Each king, bow in hand, was surrounded by his own army as he went after Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Infuriated, all the kings don armor, mount their conveyances, and pursue Lord Kṛṣṇa with bows in hand. Defeated egos regroup quickly; opposition reassembles after even decisive blows.

BG 54.2

The commanders of the Yādava army, seeing the enemy racing to attack, turned to face them and stood firm, O King, twanging their bows.

Essence. The Yādava army commanders, seeing the enemy attack, turn to face them and stand firm, twanging their bows. Defenders don't flee — they pivot and twang; readiness is itself signal.

BG 54.3

Mounted on the backs of horses, the shoulders of elephants and the seats of chariots, the enemy kings, expert with weapons, rained down arrows upon the Yadus like clouds pouring rain on mountains.

Essence. Mounted on horses, elephants, and chariots, enemy kings rain down arrows on the Yadus like clouds on mountains. Mass attacks look overwhelming from underneath; the mountain image steadies the picture.

BG 54.4

Slender-waisted Rukmiṇī, seeing her Lord’s army covered by torrents of arrows, shyly looked at His face with fear-stricken eyes.

Essence. Seeing her Lord's army covered by arrow torrents, slender Rukmiṇī shyly looks at Kṛṣṇa's face with fear-stricken eyes. Even the closest devotee experiences fear; vulnerability is honored by glances at His face.

BG 54.5

In response the Lord laughed and assured her, “Do not be afraid, beautiful-eyed one. This enemy force is about to be destroyed by your soldiers.”

Essence. The Lord laughs and assures: do not be afraid; this enemy force is about to be destroyed by your soldiers. He calls the army hers — affectionate language transfers ownership of the protection.

BG 54.6

The heroes of the Lord’s army, headed by Gada and Saṅkarṣaṇa, could not tolerate the aggression of the opposing kings. Thus with iron arrows they began to strike down the enemy’s horses, elephants and chariots.

Essence. The heroes — Gada and Saṅkarṣaṇa leading — cannot tolerate the aggression and strike down the enemy's horses, elephants, chariots with iron arrows. Sometimes 'cannot tolerate' is precisely the right warrior virtue.

BG 54.7

The heads of soldiers fighting on chariots, horses and elephants fell to the ground by the millions; some heads wore earrings and helmets, others turbans.

Essence. Heads of soldiers fall by the millions, some with earrings and helmets, others with turbans. The aftermath of battle is graphic; scripture does not sanitize the human cost.

BG 54.8

Lying all around were thighs, legs and fingerless hands, along with hands clutching swords, clubs and bows, and also the heads of horses, donkeys, elephants, camels, wild asses and humans.

Essence. Thighs, legs, fingerless hands, hands clutching weapons, heads of horses, donkeys, elephants, camels, asses, humans lie around. The full inventory of carnage is named; scripture is not squeamish.

BG 54.9

Seeing their armies being struck down by the Vṛṣṇis, who were eager for victory, the kings headed by Jarāsandha were discouraged and left the battlefield.

Essence. Seeing armies struck down by the victory-eager Vṛṣṇis, the Jarāsandha-led kings are discouraged and leave the battlefield. Demoralization can be enough; not every enemy is destroyed by dying but by losing the will to continue.

BG 54.10

The kings approached Śiśupāla, who was disturbed like a man who has lost his wife. His complexion was drained of color, his enthusiasm was gone, and his face appeared dried up. The kings spoke to him as follows.

Essence. The kings approach Śiśupāla — disturbed like a man who has lost his wife, complexion drained, enthusiasm gone, face dried — and speak. Even an attempted wedding can produce widower-style grief; possessive attachment generates real loss.

BG 54.11

[Jarāsandha said:] Listen, Śiśupāla, O tiger among men, give up your depression. After all, embodied beings’ happiness and unhappiness is never seen to be permanent, O King.

Essence. Listen, Śiśupāla, give up depression; embodied beings' happiness and unhappiness is never permanent. Even an enemy of dharma can offer correct philosophical counsel; truth comes through varied lips.

BG 54.12

Just as a puppet in the form of a woman dances by the desire of the puppeteer, so this world, controlled by the Supreme Lord, struggles in both happiness and misery.

Essence. Like a puppet woman dances at the puppeteer's wish, so this world, controlled by the Lord, struggles in happiness and misery. The puppet image places agency above us; consolation comes from naming the strings.

BG 54.13

In battle with Kṛṣṇa I and my twenty-three armies lost seventeen times; only once did I defeat Him.

Essence. In battle with Kṛṣṇa I and my twenty-three armies lost seventeen times; only once did I defeat Him. Even Jarāsandha admits a mostly losing record; honest accounting belongs even in enemy speech.

BG 54.14

But still I never lament or rejoice, because I know this world is driven by time and fate.

Essence. But I never lament or rejoice, because I know this world is driven by time and fate. Equipoise can be claimed without being true; this is precisely Jarāsandha's mode.

BG 54.15

And now all of us, great commanders of military leaders, have been defeated by the Yadus and their small entourage, who are protected by Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Now all of us, great commanders, have been defeated by the Yadus and their small entourage, protected by Kṛṣṇa. Numbers don't decide outcomes when Kṛṣṇa protects the smaller side.

BG 54.16

Now our enemies have conquered because time favors them, but in the future, when time is auspicious for us, we shall conquer.

Essence. Our enemies have conquered because time favors them; when time is auspicious for us, we shall conquer. Demonic philosophy expects future cycles to vindicate past defeats; revenge waits for a 'right time'.

BG 54.17

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus persuaded by his friends, Śiśupāla took his followers and went back to his capital. The surviving warriors also returned to their respective cities.

Essence. Persuaded by friends, Śiśupāla takes his followers and returns to his capital; survivors return to their cities. Demonic conflicts end with grumbling withdrawals; the day's defeat doesn't end the underlying threat.

BG 54.18

Powerful Rukmī, however, was especially envious of Kṛṣṇa. He could not bear the fact that Kṛṣṇa had carried off his sister to marry her in the Rākṣasa style. Thus he pursued the Lord with an entire military division.

Essence. Rukmī, especially envious, cannot bear that Kṛṣṇa has carried off his sister in Rākṣasa style; he pursues with an entire military division. Some envy continues alone after the group has retreated; private resentment chases longer than public force.

BG 54.19-20

Frustrated and enraged, mighty-armed Rukmī, dressed in armor and wielding his bow, had sworn before all the kings, “I shall not again enter Kuṇḍina if I do not kill Kṛṣṇa in battle and bring Rukmiṇī back with me. I swear this to you.”

Essence. Frustrated and enraged, mighty-armed Rukmī, in armor with bow, swears before all the kings: I will not enter Kuṇḍina until I kill Kṛṣṇa and bring Rukmiṇī back. Public oaths intensify private rage; vows of revenge corner one's future.

BG 54.21

Having said this, he had mounted his chariot and told his charioteer, “Drive the horses quickly to where Kṛṣṇa is. He and I must fight.

Essence. Mounting his chariot, Rukmī tells the charioteer to drive quickly to Kṛṣṇa: he and I must fight. Personal combat ambition isolates the individual; the army follows but the duel is private.

BG 54.22

“This wicked-minded cowherd boy, infatuated with His prowess, has violently abducted my sister. But today I will remove His pride with my sharp arrows.”

Essence. This wicked cowherd boy, infatuated with His prowess, has abducted my sister; today I will remove His pride with sharp arrows. Demonic intelligence flips the truth — He is wicked and proud, you the just avenger.

BG 54.23

Boasting thus, foolish Rukmī, ignorant of the true extent of the Supreme Lord’s power, approached Lord Govinda in his lone chariot and challenged Him, “Just stand and fight!”

Essence. Boasting, foolish Rukmī — ignorant of the Lord's true power — challenges Govinda in his lone chariot: 'just stand and fight!' Solo bravado is the ignorant's reaction to power they cannot measure.

BG 54.24

Rukmī drew his bow with great strength and struck Lord Kṛṣṇa with three arrows. Then he said, “Stand here for a moment, O defiler of the Yadu dynasty!

Essence. Rukmī draws his bow and strikes the Lord with three arrows, calling Him 'defiler of the Yadu dynasty' — fitting words turned into curses. The wicked weaponize ambiguous epithets; words become arrows.

BG 54.25

“Wherever You go, carrying off my sister like a crow stealing sacrificial butter, I will follow. This very day I shall relieve You of Your false pride, You fool, You deceiver, You cheater in battle!

Essence. Wherever You go carrying off my sister like a crow stealing sacrificial butter, I will follow — today I will relieve You of false pride, You fool, deceiver, cheater. Hysterical attack projects one's own qualities onto the opponent; Rukmī sees Rukmī.

BG 54.26

“Release the girl before You are struck dead by my arrows and made to lie down!” In response to this, Lord Kṛṣṇa smiled, and with six arrows He struck Rukmī and broke his bow.

Essence. Release the girl or be struck dead! Kṛṣṇa smiles and with six arrows strikes Rukmī and breaks his bow. The Lord's smile precedes His action; even decisive combat is friendly on His side.

BG 54.27

The Lord struck Rukmī’s four horses with eight arrows, his chariot driver with two, and the chariot’s flag with three. Rukmī grabbed another bow and struck Lord Kṛṣṇa with five arrows.

Essence. The Lord strikes Rukmī's four horses with eight arrows, his charioteer with two, his chariot's flag with three; Rukmī takes another bow and strikes with five. Strategic precision targets equipment, supporters, symbols; Rukmī replies with raw fire.

BG 54.28

Although hit by these many arrows, Lord Acyuta again broke Rukmī’s bow. Rukmī picked up yet another bow, but the infallible Lord broke that one to pieces as well.

Essence. Hit by many arrows, Acyuta again breaks Rukmī's bow; Rukmī picks another and the Lord breaks it too. Persistent rearmament against the Lord is futile; each new bow meets the same end.

BG 54.29

Iron bludgeon, three-pointed spear, sword and shield, pike, javelin — whatever weapon Rukmī picked up, Lord Hari smashed it to bits.

Essence. Iron bludgeon, three-pointed spear, sword and shield, pike, javelin — whatever Rukmī picks up, Hari smashes. The arsenal-by-arsenal exhaustion is poetic; the enemy must eventually stand empty-handed.

BG 54.30

Then Rukmī leaped down from his chariot and, sword in hand, rushed furiously toward Kṛṣṇa to kill Him, like a bird flying into the wind.

Essence. Rukmī leaps down sword in hand, rushing furiously like a bird into the wind. Last-ditch attacks are bird-into-wind; force against the Lord becomes wind against bird.

BG 54.31

As Rukmī attacked Him, the Lord shot arrows that broke Rukmī’s sword and shield into small pieces. Kṛṣṇa then took up His own sharp sword and prepared to kill Rukmī.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa shoots arrows that break Rukmī's sword and shield to pieces; takes up His sharp sword and prepares to kill. Once arsenals are gone, the Lord moves to definitive ending.

BG 54.32

Seeing Lord Kṛṣṇa ready to kill her brother, saintly Rukmiṇī was filled with alarm. She fell at her husband’s feet and piteously spoke as follows.

Essence. Seeing Kṛṣṇa ready to kill her brother, Rukmiṇī is alarmed; she falls at her husband's feet and piteously speaks. Even ideal devotees protect even unworthy relatives; mercy is requested explicitly.

BG 54.33

Śrī Rukmiṇī said: O controller of all mystic power, immeasurable one, Lord of lords, master of the universe! O all auspicious and mighty-armed one, please do not kill my brother!

Essence. O controller of mystic power, immeasurable Lord of lords, master of the universe, all-auspicious, mighty-armed — please do not kill my brother! Stacked titles in petition signal the gravity of the request.

BG 54.34

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Rukmiṇī’s utter fear caused her limbs to tremble and her mouth to dry up, while her throat choked up out of sorrow. And in her agitation her golden necklace scattered. She grasped Kṛṣṇa’s feet, and the Lord, feeling compassionate, desisted.

Essence. Limbs trembling, mouth dry, throat choked, necklace scattered, Rukmiṇī grasps Kṛṣṇa's feet — and the Lord, feeling compassion, agrees not to kill. Bodily distress moves the Lord; full physical surrender is a successful method.

BG 54.35

Lord Kṛṣṇa tied up the evil-doer with a strip of cloth. He then proceeded to disfigure Rukmī by comically shaving him, leaving parts of his mustache and hair. By that time the Yadu heroes had crushed the extraordinary army of their opponents, just as elephants crush a lotus flower.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa ties up the evil-doer with cloth and comically disfigures Rukmī by shaving him — leaving parts of mustache and hair. While Yadu heroes crush the army, the Lord administers humiliation rather than death.

BG 54.36

As the Yadus approached Lord Kṛṣṇa, they saw Rukmī in this sorry condition, practically dying of shame. When the all-powerful Lord Balarāma saw Rukmī, He compassionately released him and spoke the following to Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Yadus see Rukmī in sorry condition, practically dying of shame; Balarāma compassionately releases him and addresses Kṛṣṇa. Even disfigured enemies receive compassion from the elder brother; mercy follows mercy.

BG 54.37

[Lord Balarāma said:] My dear Kṛṣṇa, You have acted improperly! This deed will bring shame on Us, for to disfigure a close relative by shaving off his mustache and hair is as good as killing him.

Essence. Balarāma: My dear Kṛṣṇa, You have acted improperly! Disfiguring a close relative by shaving is as good as killing him. The elder brother critiques the younger publicly; family dharma includes mutual correction.

BG 54.38

Saintly lady, please do not be displeased with Us out of anxiety for your brother’s disfigurement. No one but oneself is responsible for one’s joy and grief, for a man experiences the result of his own deeds.

Essence. Saintly lady, do not be displeased — no one but oneself is responsible for joy and grief; one experiences the result of one's own deeds. Even relatives' suffering tracks back to their own karma; comfort points to that truth.

BG 54.39

[Again addressing Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma said:] A relative should not be killed even if his wrongdoing warrants capital punishment. Rather, he should be thrown out of the family. Since he has already been killed by his own sin, why kill him again?

Essence. A relative should not be killed even if his wrongdoing warrants capital punishment; he should be thrown out of the family. Since he is already killed by his own sin, why kill him again? Multiple levels of consequence; killing isn't required when sin has already done its work.

BG 54.40

[Turning to Rukmiṇī, Balarāma continued:] The code of sacred duty for warriors established by Lord Brahmā enjoins that one may have to kill even his own brother. That is indeed a most dreadful law.

Essence. The warrior's code allows killing even one's own brother — a most dreadful law. Even dharma's sharpest provisions are named dreadful; severity isn't celebrated.

BG 54.41

[Again Balarāma addressed Kṛṣṇa:] Blinded by conceit with their personal opulences, proud men offend others for the sake of such things as kingdom, land, wealth, women, honor and power.

Essence. Blinded by conceit with opulences, proud men offend others for kingdom, land, wealth, women, honor, power. The Lord was meant to marry Rukmiṇī; obstructing the right arrangement is proud offense.

BG 54.42

[To Rukmiṇī Balarāma said:] Your attitude is unfair, for like an ignorant person you wish good to those who are inimical to all living beings and who have done evil to your true well-wishers.

Essence. Your attitude is unfair, like an ignorant person wishing good to those inimical to all beings and evil to your true well-wishers. Inverted loyalty mistakes enemies for kin; honest analysis exposes the misalignment.

BG 54.43

The Supreme Lord’s Māyā makes men forget their real selves, and thus, taking the body for the self, they consider others to be friends, enemies or neutral parties.

Essence. Māyā makes men forget themselves; taking body as self, they consider others friends, enemies, neutral parties. Categories of relationship are products of forgetting the true self.

BG 54.44

Those who are bewildered perceive the one Supreme Soul, who resides in all embodied beings, as many, just as one may perceive the light in the sky, or the sky itself, as many.

Essence. Bewildered persons perceive the one Supreme Soul as many — like seeing light or sky as many. The non-duality of the Soul is masked by perceptual error.

BG 54.45

This material body, which has a beginning and an end, is composed of the physical elements, the senses and the modes of nature. The body, imposed on the self by material ignorance, causes one to experience the cycle of birth and death.

Essence. The material body, composed of elements, senses, and modes, has beginning and end; imposed by ignorance, it causes the birth-death cycle. Recognition of the body's ontology disarms identification.

BG 54.46

O intelligent lady, the soul never undergoes contact with or separation from insubstantial, material objects, because the soul is their very origin and illuminator. Thus the soul resembles the sun, which neither comes in contact with nor separates from the sense of sight and what is seen.

Essence. The soul never undergoes contact with or separation from insubstantial material objects — it is their origin and illuminator, like the sun. Origin doesn't contact what it sources; the soul's transcendence is structural.

BG 54.47

Birth and other transformations are undergone by the body but never by the self, just as change occurs for the moon’s phases but never for the moon, though the new-moon day may be called the moon’s “death.”

Essence. Birth and changes are undergone by the body, not by the self — like phases of the moon don't affect the moon. 'Death' of the new moon is naming convention; the moon endures.

BG 54.48

As a sleeping person perceives himself, the objects of sense enjoyment and the fruits of his acts within the illusion of a dream, so one who is unintelligent undergoes material existence.

Essence. As a sleeper perceives himself, sense objects, and fruits of acts within a dream, so the unintelligent undergoes material existence. Material life is structurally dreamlike; the self is unmoved by what it 'experiences.'

BG 54.49

Therefore, with transcendental knowledge dispel the grief that is weakening and confounding your mind. Please resume your natural mood, O princess of the pristine smile.

Essence. Therefore, with transcendental knowledge dispel the grief weakening and confounding your mind; resume your natural mood, O princess of pristine smile. The remedy is jñāna applied to mood; smile is one's nature.

BG 54.50

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus enlightened by Lord Balarāma, slender Rukmiṇī forgot her depression and steadied her mind by spiritual intelligence.

Essence. Enlightened by Balarāma, slender Rukmiṇī forgets her depression and steadies her mind by spiritual intelligence. Right teaching from the right elder ends specific grief; spiritual intelligence is operational.

BG 54.51

Left with only his life air, cast out by his enemies and deprived of his strength and bodily radiance, Rukmī could not forget how he had been disfigured. In frustration he constructed for his residence a large city, which he called Bhojakaṭa.

Essence. Left with only life air, cast out, deprived of strength and bodily radiance, Rukmī cannot forget the disfigurement; he builds Bhojakaṭa as residence in frustrated rage. Humiliation builds cities — angry people relocate into their grievance.

BG 54.52

Because he had promised “I will not reenter Kuṇḍina until I have killed wicked Kṛṣṇa and brought back my younger sister,” in a mood of angry frustration Rukmī took up residence at that very place.

Essence. Bound by his oath not to enter Kuṇḍina until he killed Kṛṣṇa and brought back his sister, Rukmī takes residence at Bhojakaṭa in frustration. Oaths can corner us into geographies of failure.

BG 54.53

Thus defeating all the opposing kings, the Supreme Personality of Godhead brought the daughter of Bhīṣmaka to His capital and married her according to the Vedic injunctions, O protector of the Kurus.

Essence. Thus defeating opponents, the Lord brings Bhīṣmaka's daughter to His capital and marries her according to Vedic injunctions. Even Rākṣasa-style abduction is followed by proper Vedic marriage; correct form is observed.

BG 54.54

At that time, O King, there was great rejoicing in all the homes of Yadupurī, whose citizens loved only Kṛṣṇa, chief of the Yadus.

Essence. Great rejoicing fills the homes of Yadupurī, whose citizens love only Kṛṣṇa. When a city loves its Lord, His weddings become its festivals; private joy becomes public.

BG 54.55

All the men and women, full of joy and adorned with shining jewels and earrings, brought wedding presents, which they reverently offered to the exquisitely dressed groom and bride.

Essence. Men and women, joyful and adorned with jewels and earrings, bring wedding presents and reverently offer them to the bride and groom. Ordinary citizens offer gifts; a wedding is the community's contribution to the couple.

BG 54.56

The city of the Vṛṣṇis appeared most beautiful: there were tall, festive columns, and also archways decorated with flower garlands, cloth banners and precious gems. Arrangements of auspicious, full waterpots, aguru-scented incense, and lamps graced every doorway.

Essence. The Vṛṣṇi city looked most beautiful with tall festive columns, archways decorated with garlands, banners and gems; full waterpots, aguru incense, and lamps. Detailed ornament makes the public space sacred.

BG 54.57

The city’s streets were cleansed by the intoxicated elephants belonging to the beloved kings who were guests at the wedding, and these elephants further enhanced the beauty of the city by placing trunks of plantain and betel-nut trees in all the doorways.

Essence. The streets are cleansed by intoxicated elephants of guest kings; the elephants beautify the city by placing plantain and betel-nut tree trunks at intersections. Even intoxicated elephants serve weddings; everything available is folded into festival.

BG 54.58

Those who belonged to the royal families of the Kuru, Sṛñjaya, Kaikeya, Vidarbha, Yadu and Kunti clans joyfully met one another in the midst of the crowds of people excitedly running here and there.

Essence. Royal families of Kuru, Sṛñjaya, Kaikeya, Vidarbha, Yadu, and Kunti clans joyfully meet one another amid the running crowds. Major unions become reunions; the broader family meets only at weddings.

BG 54.59

The kings and their daughters were totally astonished to hear the story of Rukmiṇī’s abduction, which was being glorified in song everywhere.

Essence. Kings and their daughters are astonished to hear the story of Rukmiṇī's abduction, glorified in song everywhere. Stories of the Lord's deeds travel in song; the abduction becomes anthology.

BG 54.60

Dvārakā’s citizens were overjoyed to see Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of all opulence, united with Rukmiṇī, the goddess of fortune.

Essence. Dvārakā's citizens are overjoyed to see Kṛṣṇa, Lord of all opulence, united with Rukmiṇī, the goddess of fortune. Public rejoicing at the union of Lord and Lakṣmī completes the chapter; communal joy responds to cosmic alignment.
