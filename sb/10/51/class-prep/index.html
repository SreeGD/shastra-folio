BG 51.1-6

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Kālayavana saw the Lord come out from Mathurā like the rising moon. The Lord was most beautiful to behold, with His dark-blue complexion and yellow silk garment. Upon His chest He bore the mark of Śrīvatsa, and the Kaustubha gem adorned His neck. His four arms were sturdy and long. He displayed His ever-joyful lotuslike face, with eyes pink like lotuses, beautifully effulgent cheeks, a pristine smile and glittering shark-shaped earrings. The barbarian thought, “This person must indeed be Vāsudeva, since He possesses the characteristics Nārada mentioned: He is marked with Śrīvatsa, He has four arms, His eyes are like lotuses, He wears a garland of forest flowers, and He is extremely handsome. He cannot be anyone else. Since He goes on foot and unarmed, I will fight Him without weapons.” Resolving thus, he ran after the Lord, who turned His back and ran away. Kālayavana hoped to catch Lord Kṛṣṇa, though great mystic yogīs cannot attain Him.

Essence. Kālayavana sees the Lord emerge from Mathurā like the rising moon — dark-blue, yellow-silk, Śrīvatsa-marked, Kaustubha-glowing, four-armed in essence, beautifully ornamented. Even seeing Him directly, the wicked cannot adequately appreciate; sight is not yet vision.

BG 51.7

Appearing virtually within reach of Kālayavana’s hands at every moment, Lord Hari led the King of the Yavanas far away to a mountain cave.

Essence. Appearing virtually within reach at every moment, Hari leads the King of the Yavanas far away to a mountain cave. The Lord can lead an enemy precisely where He intends; apparent pursuit is actually being led.

BG 51.8

While chasing the Lord, the Yavana cast insults at Him, saying “You took birth in the Yadu dynasty. It’s not proper for You to run away!” But still Kālayavana could not reach Lord Kṛṣṇa, because his sinful reactions had not been cleansed away.

Essence. Kālayavana insults the Lord while chasing — 'You took birth in the Yadu dynasty; it's not proper for You to run away!' — but cannot reach Him because his sinful reactions are not cleansed. Insult doesn't change distance; karma maintains the gap.

BG 51.9

Although insulted in this way, the Supreme Lord entered the mountain cave. Kālayavana also entered, and there he saw another man lying asleep.

Essence. Insulted, the Lord enters the mountain cave; Kālayavana enters too and sees another man sleeping there. The Lord ignores insult and proceeds with His plan; engagement with insults is not part of His itinerary.

BG 51.11

The man awoke after a long sleep and slowly opened his eyes. Looking all about, he saw Kālayavana standing beside him.

Essence. The man wakes after a long sleep, slowly opens his eyes, and sees Kālayavana standing beside him. Long spiritual sleep can end suddenly; first sight on waking is often of the very enemy whose appearance was the alarm.

BG 51.12

The awakened man was angry and cast his glance at Kālayavana, whose body burst into flames. In a single moment, O King Parīkṣit, Kālayavana was burnt to ashes.

Essence. The awakened Mucukunda casts an angry glance and Kālayavana's body bursts into flame, reduced to ashes. Spiritual potency stored in long sleep can dispatch enemies on a single glance.

BG 51.13

King Parīkṣit said: Who was that person, O brāhmaṇa? To which family did he belong, and what were his powers? Why did that destroyer of the barbarian lie down to sleep in the cave, and whose son was he?

Essence. Parīkṣit asks: who was that person, from what family, what powers, why was he asleep, whose son? Curiosity is appropriate when a single act seems disproportionate; the audience naturally wants the backstory.

BG 51.14

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Mucukunda was the name of this great personality, who was born in the Ikṣvāku dynasty as the son of Māndhātā. He was devoted to brahminical culture and always true to his vow in battle.

Essence. Mucukunda was born in the Ikṣvāku dynasty, son of Māndhātā, devoted to brahminical culture and always true to his vow in battle. The hidden warrior was already exceptional; his pedigree, devotion, and integrity were stable.

BG 51.15

Begged by Indra and the other demigods to help protect them when they were terrorized by the demons, Mucukunda defended them for a long time.

Essence. Begged by Indra and demigods to protect them when demons terrorized them, Mucukunda defended them for a long time. Sustained service to higher authorities is a normal phase of advanced soul's biography.

BG 51.16

When the demigods obtained Kārttikeya as their general, they told Mucukunda, “O King, you may now give up your troublesome duty of guarding us.

Essence. When the demigods obtained Kārttikeya as general, they told Mucukunda: now you may give up your troublesome guardianship. Even essential service can be released when the proper successor arrives.

BG 51.17

“Abandoning an unopposed kingdom in the world of men, O valiant one, you neglected all your personal desires while engaged in protecting us.

Essence. Abandoning an unopposed kingdom, you neglected personal desires while protecting us. Some service costs the worker his own life; the work is named honestly by the served.

BG 51.18

“The children, queens, relatives, ministers, advisers and subjects who were your contemporaries are no longer alive. They have all been swept away by time.

Essence. Your children, queens, relatives, ministers, advisers, and contemporaries are no longer alive; they've all been swept away by time. The cost of a long service can include outliving everyone you knew.

BG 51.19

“Inexhaustible time, stronger than the strong, is the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself. Like a herdsman moving his animals along, He moves mortal creatures as His pastime.

Essence. Inexhaustible time, stronger than the strong, is the Supreme Lord Himself; like a herdsman, He moves mortal creatures as His pastime. Time is not impersonal — it is a face of the Lord at play with creatures.

BG 51.20

“All good fortune to you! Now please choose a benediction from us — anything but liberation, since only the infallible Supreme Lord, Viṣṇu, can bestow that.”

Essence. Choose a benediction — anything but liberation, since only Viṣṇu can bestow that. Demigods give what they can; what is beyond them they openly acknowledge.

BG 51.21

Addressed thus, King Mucukunda took his respectful leave of the demigods and went to a cave, where he lay down to enjoy the sleep they had granted him.

Essence. Mucukunda takes leave of the demigods and goes to a cave to enjoy the granted sleep. After grand service, the boon was the freedom to sleep; rest itself can be the reward.

BG 51.22

After the Yavana was burnt to ashes, the Supreme Lord, chief of the Sātvatas, revealed Himself to the wise Mucukunda.

Essence. After the Yavana is burnt to ashes, the Supreme Lord reveals Himself to wise Mucukunda. Hidden divine presence is revealed precisely when the obstacle is removed; Kṛṣṇa was always there waiting.

BG 51.23-26

As he gazed at the Lord, King Mucukunda saw that He was dark blue like a cloud, had four arms, and wore a yellow silk garment. On His chest He bore the Śrīvatsa mark and on His neck the brilliantly glowing Kaustubha gem. Adorned with a Vaijayantī garland, the Lord displayed His handsome, peaceful face, which attracts the eyes of all mankind with its shark-shaped earrings and affectionately smiling glance. The beauty of His youthful form was unexcelled, and He moved with the nobility of an angry lion. The highly intelligent King was overwhelmed by the Lord’s effulgence, which showed Him to be invincible. Expressing his uncertainty, Mucukunda hesitantly questioned Lord Kṛṣṇa as follows.

Essence. Mucukunda sees the dark-blue, four-armed Lord in yellow silk, Śrīvatsa on chest, Kaustubha gem, Vaijayantī garland — magnificent in all detail. Devotional vision is precise; it sees the Lord's specific features.

BG 51.27

Śrī Mucukunda said: Who are You who have come to this mountain cave in the forest, having walked on the thorny ground with feet as soft as lotus petals?

Essence. Who are You, who have come to this cave with feet soft as lotus petals on thorny ground? Wonder at the Lord's gentleness in a rough setting is the first response of the awakened devotee.

BG 51.28

Perhaps You are the potency of all potent beings. Or maybe You are the powerful god of fire, or the sun-god, the moon-god, the King of heaven or the ruling demigod of some other planet.

Essence. Perhaps You are the potency of all potent beings — fire god, sun god, moon, Indra, or some other ruling demigod? Mucukunda guesses cosmic identities; even high guesses fall short of Kṛṣṇa.

BG 51.29

I think You are the Supreme Personality among the three chief gods, since You drive away the darkness of this cave as a lamp dispels darkness with its light.

Essence. I think You are the Supreme Personality among the three chief gods, since You drive away the cave's darkness like a lamp. Inner darkness is dispelled too; vision of the Lord clarifies internal night.

BG 51.30

O best among men, if You like, please truly describe Your birth, activities and lineage to us, who are eager to hear.

Essence. Please describe Your birth, activities, and lineage; we are eager to hear. The right response to mystery is to ask for the story, not for power.

BG 51.31

As for ourselves, O tiger among men, we belong to a family of fallen kṣatriyas, descendants of King Ikṣvāku. My name is Mucukunda, my Lord, and I am the son of Yauvanāśva.

Essence. We belong to a family of fallen kṣatriyas, descendants of Ikṣvāku — my name is Mucukunda, son of Yauvanāśva. Humble self-introduction names the family's fall along with its lineage; honesty about decline doesn't disqualify devotion.

BG 51.32

I was fatigued after remaining awake for a long time, and my senses were overwhelmed by sleep. Thus I slept comfortably here in this solitary place until, just now, someone woke me.

Essence. I was fatigued after remaining awake for a long time; senses overwhelmed by sleep, I slept comfortably until just now. Long service ends in long sleep; the body's tiredness is acknowledged plainly.

BG 51.33

The man who woke me was burned to ashes by the reaction of his sins. Just then I saw You, possessing a glorious appearance and the power to chastise Your enemies.

Essence. The man who woke me was burnt to ashes by his own sins' reaction; then I saw You, glorious and capable of chastising enemies. Karma can wake one person and burn another; the same alarm has different effects.

BG 51.34

Your unbearably brilliant effulgence overwhelms our strength, and thus we cannot fix our gaze upon You. O exalted one, You are to be honored by all embodied beings.

Essence. Your unbearable effulgence overwhelms our strength; we cannot fix our gaze on You. You are to be honored by all embodied beings. Sometimes vision of the Lord is so bright we must look away; honor compensates for inability to gaze.

BG 51.35

[Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued:] Thus addressed by the King, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, origin of all creation, smiled and then replied to him in a voice as deep as the rumbling of clouds.

Essence. The Supreme Lord smiles and replies in a voice deep as rumbling clouds. The Lord's response to devotee speech is warm and resonant; His voice has texture.

BG 51.36

The Supreme Lord said: My dear friend, I have taken thousands of births, lived thousands of lives and accepted thousands of names. In fact My births, activities and names are limitless, and thus even I cannot count them.

Essence. I have taken thousands of births, lived thousands of lives, accepted thousands of names; My births, activities and names are limitless. Even He cannot count them. The Lord's biography exceeds the Lord's own bookkeeping.

BG 51.37

After many lifetimes someone might count the dust particles on the earth, but no one can ever finish counting My qualities, activities, names and births.

Essence. After many lifetimes one might count earth's dust particles, but never finish counting My qualities, activities, names and births. Infinity is named with a specific comparison; even particle-counting falls short.

BG 51.38

O King, the greatest sages enumerate My births and activities, which take place throughout the three phases of time, but never do they reach the end of them.

Essence. The greatest sages enumerate My births and activities through all three phases of time but never reach the end. Even the sages don't finish — the joy is in the inquiry itself, not in completion.

BG 51.39-40

Nonetheless, O friend, I will tell you about My current birth, name and activities. Kindly hear. Some time ago, Lord Brahmā requested Me to protect religious principles and destroy the demons who were burdening the earth. Thus I descended in the Yadu dynasty, in the home of Ānakadundubhi. Indeed, because I am the son of Vasudeva, people call Me Vāsudeva.

Essence. I will tell you about this current birth: Brahmā asked Me to protect religious principles and destroy demons; I descended in the Yadu dynasty. The current incarnation has a specific commission; particular missions are named clearly.

BG 51.41

I have killed Kālanemi, reborn as Kaṁsa, as well as Pralamba and other enemies of the pious. And now, O King, this barbarian has been burnt to ashes by your piercing glance.

Essence. I have killed Kālanemi reborn as Kaṁsa, Pralamba, and other enemies of the pious; and now this barbarian was burnt by your piercing glance. The Lord credits Mucukunda for the latest deed; collaboration is acknowledged.

BG 51.42

Since in the past you repeatedly prayed to Me, I have personally come to this cave to show you mercy, for I am affectionately inclined to My devotees.

Essence. Since in the past you repeatedly prayed to Me, I have personally come to this cave to show you mercy — I am affectionately inclined to devotees. Past prayers have a long memory in the Lord's reckoning; old asks are eventually answered in person.

BG 51.43

Now choose some benedictions from Me, O saintly King. I will fulfill all your desires. One who has satisfied Me need never again lament.

Essence. Now choose benedictions — I will fulfill all desires; one who has satisfied Me need never lament. Access to the Lord includes invitation to ask; lamentation about the unwanted ends when He is fully pleased.

BG 51.44

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Mucukunda bowed down to the Lord when he heard this. Remembering the words of the sage Garga, he joyfully recognized Kṛṣṇa to be the Supreme Lord, Nārāyaṇa. The King then addressed Him as follows.

Essence. Mucukunda bows, remembers Garga's words, and joyfully recognizes Kṛṣṇa as Nārāyaṇa. Memory of an earlier sage's prophecy enables present recognition; preparation in the past meets revelation in the present.

BG 51.45

Śrī Mucukunda said: O Lord, the people of this world, both men and women, are bewildered by Your illusory energy. Unaware of their real benefit, they do not worship You but instead seek happiness by entangling themselves in family affairs, which are actually sources of misery.

Essence. O Lord, the people of this world, men and women, are bewildered by Your illusory energy; not knowing their real benefit, they don't worship You but seek happiness in family affairs — actually sources of misery. The wise diagnosis precedes the wise request.

BG 51.46

That person has an impure mind who, despite having somehow or other automatically obtained the rare and highly evolved human form of life, does not worship Your lotus feet. Like an animal that has fallen into a blind well, such a person has fallen into the darkness of a material home.

Essence. Impure-minded is one who, having obtained the rare human form, doesn't worship Your feet — like an animal fallen into a blind well. Time will rudely eject us from our material 'homes'; only worship makes that ejection meaningful.

BG 51.47

I have wasted all this time, O unconquerable one, becoming more and more intoxicated by my domain and opulence as an earthly king. Misidentifying the mortal body as the self, becoming attached to children, wives, treasury and land, I suffered endless anxiety.

Essence. I have wasted time, intoxicated by domain and opulence as an earthly king — misidentifying the body as self, attached to children, wives, treasury, land, suffering endless anxiety. Confessing past misuse is part of mature devotion.

BG 51.48

With deep arrogance I took myself to be the body, which is a material object like a pot or a wall. Thinking myself a god among men, I traveled the earth surrounded by my charioteers, elephants, cavalry, foot soldiers and generals, disregarding You in my deluding pride.

Essence. With deep arrogance I took myself to be the body — pot or wall — thinking myself a god, surrounded by charioteers, elephants, cavalry, generals, disregarding You. Pride attaches us to the body's material company.

BG 51.49

A man obsessed with thoughts of what he thinks needs to be done, intensely greedy, and delighting in sense enjoyment is suddenly confronted by You, who are ever alert. Like a hungry snake licking its fangs before a mouse, You appear before him as death.

Essence. A man obsessed with what 'needs to be done,' intensely greedy, delighting in sense enjoyment, is suddenly confronted by You — ever alert — appearing as death like a hungry snake licking its fangs. The vivid image of death-as-snake corrects illusion of safety.

BG 51.50

The body that at first rides high on fierce elephants or chariots adorned with gold and is known by the name “king” is later, by Your invincible power of time, called “feces,” “worms,” or “ashes.”

Essence. The body riding high on elephants and gold chariots, called 'king,' is later called 'feces,' 'worms,' or 'ashes' by Your invincible time. Bodies' final names contradict their flattering ones; honest naming of decay is part of vairāgya.

BG 51.51

Having conquered the entire circle of directions and being thus free of conflict, a man sits on a splendid throne, receiving praise from leaders who were once his equals. But when he enters the women’s chambers, where sex pleasure is found, he is led about like a pet animal, O Lord.

Essence. Free of conflict, sitting on a splendid throne receiving praise — but in the women's chambers led about like an ox by a chain in his nose. Even great rulers are led by base desires; status doesn't free anyone from basic compulsions.

BG 51.52

A king who desires even greater power than he already has strictly performs his duties, carefully practicing austerity and forgoing sense enjoyment. But he whose urges are so rampant, thinking “I am independent and supreme,” cannot attain happiness.

Essence. A king desiring greater power strictly performs duties, practices austerity, forgoes sense enjoyment; but he whose urges are rampant — 'I am independent and supreme' — cannot attain happiness. Discipline serves ambition more reliably than indulgence.

BG 51.53

When the material life of a wandering soul has ceased, O Acyuta, he may attain the association of Your devotees. And when he associates with them, there awakens in him devotion unto You, who are the goal of the devotees and the Lord of all causes and their effects.

Essence. When material life of a wandering soul ceases, he may attain the association of Your devotees; and from that, devotion to You — the goal of devotees, Lord of all causes — awakens. The path is sequential: ceasing material life, sangha, devotion.

BG 51.54

My Lord, I think You have shown me mercy, since my attachment to my kingdom has spontaneously ceased. Such freedom is prayed for by saintly rulers of vast empires who desire to enter the forest for a life of solitude.

Essence. I think You've shown me mercy, since my attachment to my kingdom has spontaneously ceased — the freedom kings pray for to enter forest life. When attachment falls off without effort, recognize it as the Lord's mercy.

BG 51.55

O all-powerful one, I desire no boon other than service to Your lotus feet, the boon most eagerly sought by those free of material desire. O Hari, what enlightened person who worships You, the giver of liberation, would choose a boon that causes his own bondage?

Essence. I desire no boon other than service to Your lotus feet — sought by those free of material desire. What enlightened person would choose a boon causing his own bondage? The mature devotee specifies the ask is service, not items.

BG 51.56

Therefore, O Lord, having put aside all objects of material desire, which are bound to the modes of passion, ignorance and goodness, I am approaching You, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, for shelter. You are not covered by mundane designations; rather, You are the Supreme Absolute Truth, full in pure knowledge and transcendental to the material modes.

Essence. Having put aside material objects bound by the modes, I approach You for shelter — You are not covered by mundane designations; You are pure being beyond all qualities. The Lord's transcendence is the actual shelter.

BG 51.57

For so long I have been pained by troubles in this world and have been burning with lamentation. My six enemies are never satiated, and I can find no peace. Therefore, O giver of shelter, O Supreme Soul, please protect me. O Lord, in the midst of danger I have by good fortune approached Your lotus feet, which are the truth and which thus make one fearless and free of sorrow.

Essence. For so long I have been pained, burning with lamentation; my six enemies — lust, anger, greed, illusion, pride, envy — are never satiated. Protect me, Supreme Soul; in danger I have come to You. Final petition uses both diagnosis and surrender.

BG 51.58

The Supreme Lord said: O emperor, great ruler, your mind is pure and potent. Though I enticed You with benedictions, your mind was not overcome by material desires.

Essence. The Supreme Lord says: your mind is pure and potent; though I enticed you with benedictions, your mind was not overcome by material desires. The Lord tests with offered boons; saints pass by refusing them.

BG 51.59

Understand that I enticed you with benedictions just to prove that you would not be deceived. The intelligence of My unalloyed devotees is never diverted by material blessings.

Essence. Understand I enticed you with benedictions just to prove you would not be deceived; the intelligence of My unalloyed devotees is never diverted by material blessings. Even being tested is itself a testimony from the Lord.

BG 51.60

The minds of nondevotees who engage in such practices as prāṇāyama are not fully cleansed of material desires. Thus, O King, material desires are again seen to arise in their minds.

Essence. The minds of nondevotees doing prāṇāyama are not fully cleansed of material desires; thus desires are seen to arise again. Discipline without devotion leaves the desire-tank refilling.

BG 51.61

Wander this earth at will, with your mind fixed on Me. May you always possess such unfailing devotion for Me.

Essence. Wander this earth at will, with your mind fixed on Me; may you always possess such unfailing devotion. Freedom of movement plus fixed devotion is the prescription for a liberated life.

BG 51.62

Because you followed the principles of a kṣatriya, you killed living beings while hunting and performing other duties. You must vanquish the sins thus incurred by carefully executing penances while remaining surrendered to Me.

Essence. Because you followed kṣatriya principles, you killed beings while hunting and in duties; vanquish those sins by careful penances while remaining surrendered to Me. Past dharmic violence still requires penance; even dharmic karma needs cleansing.

BG 51.63

O King, in your very next life you will become an excellent brāhmaṇa, the greatest well-wisher of all creatures, and certainly come to Me alone.

Essence. In your very next life you will become an excellent brāhmaṇa, well-wisher of all creatures, and certainly come to Me alone. Future lives are real and planned; the Lord gives concrete forecasts to devotees.
