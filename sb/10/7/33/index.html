Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.7.33

द‍ृष्ट्वाद्भ‍ुतानि बहुशो नन्दगोपो बृहद्वने । वसुदेववचो भूयो मानयामास विस्मित: ॥ ३३ ॥

Important Words
  • bahuśaḥ — many times
    Bahu (many) + -śas (suffix forming adverbs of frequency). The Bhāgavatam emphasizes the repetition — many astonishments, not one. Faith for the Vaiṣṇava ripens through accumulated wonders, not single events; the verse names the pattern by which Vraja's elders are gradually trained.
  • bṛhadvane — in Bṛhadvana
    Bṛhat (great) + vana (forest). Bṛhadvana is the first of Vraja's twelve forests, where Kṛṣṇa's earliest pastimes unfold. The Six Gosvāmīs recovered Vraja's geography precisely — Rūpa, Sanātana, and Jīva identified each forest, each kuṇḍa, each līlā-sthāna. Bṛhadvana corresponds to modern-day Mahāvana.
  • vasudeva-vacaḥ — the words of Vasudeva
    Vasudeva (Kṛṣṇa's biological father) + vacas (speech). Vasudeva is here a Vaiṣṇava-vipra to Nanda — a senior, trustworthy speaker. The verse establishes the principle that Vaiṣṇava speech, even when initially obscure, must be remembered and re-honored as events bear it out.
  • mānayām āsa — accepted how true they were, honored
    From man — to think, to honor. The verb means more than 'remembered' — it means 'gave proper weight to.' Nanda finally honors what Vasudeva said. Gauḍīya principle: śravaṇa is incomplete without mānana — turning the heard word over, weighing it, letting events confirm and deepen it.
Having seen all these incidents in Bṛhadvana, Nanda Mahārāja became more and more astonished, and he remembered the words spoken to him by Vasudeva in Mathurā.
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Having seen all these incidents in Bṛhadvana, Nanda Mahārāja became more and more astonished, and he remembered the words spoken to him by Vasudeva in Mathurā.
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

A general description of everyone’s great affection for the Lord has been given. This verse shows Nanda’s increased affection because of worry for his son in trying to determine the cause of the disturbances. Nanda saw the many remarkable incidents at Mahāvana. There were many remarkable incidents in the Lords pastimes here, famous with the people of Mathurā, such as the defeat of the false brāhmaṇa called Śrīdhara, but Śukadeva did not mention them because Parīkṣit had little time left. This place called Mahāvana was a place of great disturbances. The word nanda-gopa suggests that he was the protector of the earth and giver of bliss to Vraja.

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

A general description of everyone’s great affection for the Lord has been given. This verse shows Nanda’s increased affection because of worry for his son in trying to determine the cause of the disturbances. Nanda saw the many remarkable incidents at Mahāvana. Without telling the unlimited events, the conclusion is given. The words of Vasudeva were as follows: karo vai vārṣiko datto rājṣe dṛṣṭā vayaṁ ca vaḥ neha stheyaṁ bahu-tithaṁ santy utpātāś ca gokule Vasudeva said: Since you have paid the annual taxes to Kaṁsa and have also seen me, do not stay in this place for many days. There may be some disturbances in Gokula. SB 10.5.31 He was astonished at Vasudeva’s knowledge. He is called Nanda-gopaḥ because he gave joy to Vraja and protected the earth.

Personal Guidance
When Kṛṣṇa repeatedly shatters our expectations, the wise — like Nanda — return to the trusted vaiṣṇava's words and let astonishment ripen into faith.
Practical Applications
  • Recall one 'impossible' rescue or reversal in your own life today, and name it openly as Kṛṣṇa's hand rather than coincidence.
  • Return to a single instruction from a trusted Vaiṣṇava (your guru, Prabhupāda's books) that you once dismissed, and re-read it in light of what has happened since.
  • Refuse to flatten the wonder — when something astonishing occurs, pause and say 'Kṛṣṇa' before reaching for a mundane explanation.
Analogy

A father who has been told that his son is a hidden prince treats every wonder around the boy with quiet recognition rather than panic — the wonder confirms what he was already told. Nanda, having heard Vasudeva's words in Mathurā, sees in each astonishment of Vraja the unfolding proof of what the trusted Vaiṣṇava said.

Stories
Vasudeva's warning at the meeting in Mathurā

When Nanda came to Mathurā to pay taxes, Vasudeva, secretly Kṛṣṇa's actual father, warned him cryptically: terrible portents are arising; take great care of your child; danger surrounds him (SB 10.5). Nanda heard but did not yet understand.

10.7.33 closes the loop: after Pūtanā, the cart, and Tṛṇāvarta, Nanda remembers Vasudeva's words and accepts them as true. The principle is the Gauḍīya rule of guru-vākya — the trusted Vaiṣṇava's instruction may not make sense at the moment of hearing, but events confirm it, and the disciple returns to it ratified.
