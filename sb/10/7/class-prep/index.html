BG 7.1-2

King Parīkṣit said: My lord, Śukadeva Gosvāmī, all the various activities exhibited by the incarnations of the Supreme Personality of Godhead are certainly pleasing to the ear and to the mind. Simply by one’s hearing of these activities, the dirty things in one’s mind immediately vanish. Generally we are reluctant to hear about the activities of the Lord, but Kṛṣṇa’s childhood activities are so attractive that they are automatically pleasing to the mind and ear. Thus one’s attachment for hearing about material things, which is the root cause of material existence, vanishes, and one gradually develops devotional service to the Supreme Lord, attachment for Him, and friendship with devotees who give us the contribution of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If you think it fit, kindly speak about those activities of the Lord.

Essence. Hearing baby Kṛṣṇa's pastimes from devotees melts the mind's dirt, cuts material craving, and naturally awakens bhakti and sādhu-saṅga.

Practical applications
  • Begin today by hearing or reading one pastime of baby Kṛṣṇa from a Vaiṣṇava source — aloud, slowly, with attention to the ear and heart, not as information.
  • Notice one craving (food, scrolling, validation) that flares up today and, instead of indulging or suppressing it, replace that exact moment with five minutes of śravaṇa about Kṛṣṇa.
  • Reach out to one devotee whose company increases your taste for Kṛṣṇa-kathā, and arrange a real conversation with them this week — guard sādhu-saṅga as concretely as you guard your calendar.

BG 7.3

Please describe other pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality, who appeared on this planet earth, imitating a human child and performing wonderful activities like killing Pūtanā.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa descends as a human child so we can taste His pastimes intimately — fortune the demigods envy; today, hear them with hungry ears.

Practical applications
  • Set aside ten unhurried minutes today to hear or read one of Kṛṣṇa's bāla-līlās aloud, treating the hearing itself as sādhana.
  • When you next meet a small child, remember Kṛṣṇa on Yaśodā's lap killing Pūtanā, and let that memory soften your heart toward the Lord's mādhurya.
  • Refuse the dull thought that this human birth is ordinary — name one concrete way today that you are more fortunate than the demigods because Kṛṣṇa appeared here.

BG 7.4

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: When mother Yaśodā’s baby was slanting His body to attempt to rise and turn around, this attempt was observed by a Vedic ceremony. In such a ceremony, called utthāna, which is performed when a child is due to leave the house for the first time, the child is properly bathed. Just after Kṛṣṇa turned three months old, mother Yaśodā celebrated this ceremony with other women of the neighborhood. On that day, there was a conjunction of the moon with the constellation Rohiṇī. As the brāhmaṇas joined by chanting Vedic hymns and professional musicians also took part, this great ceremony was observed by mother Yaśodā.

Essence. Welcoming a child's every milestone with Vedic ceremony reveals a culture that treats new life as Kṛṣṇa's gift, never a burden — receive your duties the same way.

Practical applications
  • Mark one ordinary milestone in your family today — a child's small step, a parent's recovery, a sibling's effort — with a deliberate offering or prayer to Kṛṣṇa instead of letting it pass unnoticed.
  • Refuse the modern framing that responsibilities (children, dependents, elders, sevā) are 'burdens'; name one such duty aloud today as a cause for jubilation.
  • Invite saṅga into a family moment this week — call a devotee, play kīrtana during a meal, or chant together — so that household life is sanctified by Vaiṣṇava association rather than left secular.

BG 7.5

After completing the bathing ceremony for the child, mother Yaśodā received the brāhmaṇas by worshiping them with proper respect and giving them ample food grains and other eatables, clothing, desirable cows, and garlands. The brāhmaṇas properly chanted Vedic hymns to observe the auspicious ceremony, and when they finished and mother Yaśodā saw that the child felt sleepy, she lay down on the bed with the child until He was peacefully asleep.

Essence. Mother Yaśodā's tender care of baby Kṛṣṇa shows that real bhakti is expressed through attentive, embodied service — not abstract sentiment.

Practical applications
  • Identify one person in your care today (child, parent, guest) and serve them with the same patient attentiveness Yaśodā gave Kṛṣṇa — staying until the task is truly complete.
  • Before a meal or important task, honor a brāhmaṇa or sādhu (in person, by call, or by remembrance) with food, dakṣiṇā, or sincere praṇāma, treating it as a non-negotiable household duty.
  • Refuse to rush through acts of seva today — whether feeding, settling a child, or hosting a guest — and let the work itself become your sādhana.

BG 7.6

The liberal mother Yaśodā, absorbed in celebrating the utthāna ceremony, was busy receiving guests, worshiping them with all respect and offering them clothing, cows, garlands and grains. Thus she could not hear the child crying for His mother. At that time, the child Kṛṣṇa, demanding to drink the milk of His mother’s breast, angrily threw His legs upward.

Essence. Even in the bustle of duty, Kṛṣṇa demands the mother's attention — kick aside the śakaṭa-load of distractions and turn back to Him today.

Practical applications
  • Notice the moment in your day when hospitality, hosting, or 'good work' eclipses direct remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, and pause to chant His name before resuming.
  • Identify one śakaṭa-shaped obstacle parked over your bhakti — a habit, a device, a worry — and decisively kick it aside this evening.
  • Refuse to dismiss the Lord's pastimes as mythology; read this verse aloud with Prabhupāda's purport and let yourself be struck with wonder.

BG 7.7

Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa was lying down underneath the handcart in one corner of the courtyard, and although His little legs were as soft as leaves, when He struck the cart with His legs, it turned over violently and collapsed. The wheels separated from the axle, the hubs and spokes fell apart, and the pole of the handcart broke. On the cart there were many little utensils made of various metals, and all of them scattered hither and thither.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself, dispatches the Śakaṭāsura demon with a touch of His tender foot — His protection requires no exertion.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are straining to engineer outcomes Kṛṣṇa could resolve effortlessly, and offer that strain to Him at your altar or in japa.
  • Refuse the impulse to measure spiritual strength by visible effort — chant one round of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra with full attention rather than many rounds distractedly.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa as Bāla-Gopāla today — small, soft-footed, personally present — not as an abstract force, and ask Him to break whatever 'cart' of accumulated saṁskāras blocks your bhakti.

BG 7.8

When mother Yaśodā and the other ladies who had assembled for the utthāna festival, and all the men, headed by Nanda Mahārāja, saw the wonderful situation, they began to wonder how the handcart had collapsed by itself. They began to wander here and there, trying to find the cause, but were unable to do so.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa's protection looks like coincidence, the worldly eye searches for causes — the devotee recognizes the Lord's quiet hand and bows.

Practical applications
  • Recall one 'lucky' rescue from your past today and re-read it as Kṛṣṇa's unseen intervention rather than chance.
  • When something inexplicably goes right, refuse the reflex to credit yourself or circumstance — speak His name in thanks instead.
  • Notice where you, like the vraja-vāsīs, are still 'wandering here and there' for material explanations, and pause to remember the Lord is the cause of all causes.

BG 7.9

The assembled cowherd men and ladies began to contemplate how this thing had happened. “Is it the work of some demon or evil planet?” they asked. At that time, the small children present asserted that the cart had been kicked apart by the baby Kṛṣṇa. As soon as the crying baby had kicked the cart’s wheel, the cart had collapsed. There was no doubt about it.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's tiny kick dismantles the Śakaṭāsura — what looks accidental in our day is His arrangement; hearing such kathā lifts us beyond māyā.

Practical applications
  • When something in your day suddenly 'breaks' — a plan, a possession, a position — refuse the demon-or-bad-luck story and look for Kṛṣṇa's hand in the dismantling.
  • Read or hear one passage of kṛṣṇa-kathā today aloud, however briefly, and notice the shift from material anxiety to ānanda-cinmaya-rasa.
  • Loosen your grip on one thing you have been clutching as 'mine' — a project, an outcome, an object — and offer it inwardly to the small feet of Kṛṣṇa.

BG 7.10

The assembled gopīs and gopas, unaware that Kṛṣṇa is always unlimited, could not believe that baby Kṛṣṇa had such inconceivable power. They could not believe the statements of the children, and therefore they neglected these statements as being childish talk.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa's aprameya power shows up in plain sight, the worldly mind dismisses it as bāla-bhāṣita — train yourself to take the small signs seriously.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are dismissing a child's, a junior's, or a devotee's witness of Kṛṣṇa as 'just talk' — pause, listen again, and let it land.
  • Refuse the gopas' mistake: when a small mercy or unlikely rescue arrives in your day, name it as Kṛṣṇa's aprameya hand instead of crediting coincidence.
  • Speak one concrete glory of Kṛṣṇa to someone in your household today, even if you fear they will treat it as childish — your job is to say it, not to be believed.

BG 7.11

Thinking that some bad planet had attacked Kṛṣṇa, mother Yaśodā picked up the crying child and allowed Him to suck her breast. Then she called for experienced brāhmaṇas to chant Vedic hymns and perform an auspicious ritualistic ceremony.

Essence. When danger threatens, take shelter in Kṛṣṇa as Yaśodā took shelter in vipra-mantras — practical surrender, not panic, is the Vaiṣṇava response.

Practical applications
  • When alarm strikes today, pause and chant Kṛṣṇa's names before reacting — let the holy sound be your first counter-measure, not your last.
  • Identify one inauspicious situation you have been worrying over and request a qualified devotee or brāhmaṇa to chant or offer prayers on its behalf.
  • Refuse the modern reflex of treating crises as purely material problems — meet them with sādhana (japa, kīrtana, prayer) before logistics.

BG 7.12

After the strong, stout cowherd men assembled the pots and paraphernalia on the handcart and set it up as before, the brāhmaṇas performed a ritualistic ceremony with a fire sacrifice to appease the bad planet, and then, with rice grains, kuśa, water and curd, they worshiped the Supreme Lord.

Essence. When calamity strikes, restore order with steady hands and offer the day's work to Kṛṣṇa — practical action and devotional ritual together protect what we love.

Practical applications
  • After a disturbance today — a spilled task, a broken plan — restore the situation with calm, vigorous effort instead of brooding over the upset.
  • Before resuming normal work after a setback, pause for even one minute of remembrance — offer dadhi-akṣata-style simplicity (a flower, a tulasī leaf, a name) to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse the modern split between 'practical fixing' and 'spiritual response' — do both, in sequence, the way the gopas did.

BG 7.13-15

When brāhmaṇas are free from envy, untruthfulness, unnecessary pride, grudges, disturbance by the opulence of others, and false prestige, their blessings never go in vain. Considering this, Nanda Mahārāja soberly took Kṛṣṇa on his lap and invited such truthful brāhmaṇas to perform a ritualistic ceremony according to the holy hymns of the Sāma Veda, Ṛg Veda and Yajur Veda. Then, while the hymns were being chanted, he bathed the child with water mixed with pure herbs, and after performing a fire ceremony, he sumptuously fed all the brāhmaṇas with first-class grains and other food.

Essence. Nanda Mahārāja, even with Kṛṣṇa on his lap, sought blessings from truthful, envy-free brāhmaṇas — true protection comes through qualified Vaiṣṇava grace, not status.

Practical applications
  • Before any major undertaking today, seek the blessing of one person whose life shows truthfulness and freedom from envy — not someone merely senior in title.
  • Audit your own conduct for the six disqualifications named here (asūyā, anṛta, dambha, īrṣā, hiṁsā, māna) and name aloud the one most active in you this week.
  • Cook or offer one meal today with care as if feeding a Vaiṣṇava — using grains and milk products — and refuse the lazy shortcut of treating food as mere fuel.

BG 7.16

Nanda Mahārāja, for the sake of the affluence of his own son Kṛṣṇa, gave the brāhmaṇas cows fully decorated with garments, flower garlands and gold necklaces. These cows, fully qualified to give ample milk, were given to the brāhmaṇas in charity, and the brāhmaṇas accepted them and bestowed blessings upon the whole family, and especially upon Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Nanda Mahārāja's charity to brāhmaṇas for Kṛṣṇa's welfare shows that wealth is purified when it flows toward the Lord through His devotees.

Practical applications
  • Today, give something tangible — money, food, or time — to a Vaiṣṇava or brāhmaṇa specifically as an offering for the welfare of someone you love.
  • Refuse to hoard your 'best' things; identify one first-class item you've been saving and give it in charity rather than letting aparigraha curdle into possessiveness.
  • Before any act of generosity, name the recipient's spiritual standing and the dependent you're offering it for, so the gift becomes yajña rather than transaction.

BG 7.17

The brāhmaṇas, who were completely expert in chanting the Vedic hymns, were all yogīs fully equipped with mystic powers. Whatever blessings they spoke were certainly never fruitless.

Essence. Words from a qualified brāhmaṇa never fail; in this age, take shelter of saṅkīrtana — the only yajña whose blessings still bear fruit.

Practical applications
  • Chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra audibly today as your personal yajña, trusting it carries the same potency the Vedic brāhmaṇas wielded.
  • Refuse to seek blessings from self-styled gurus and astrologers; instead, seek the saṅga of devotees whose words are anchored in śāstra and Kṛṣṇa.
  • Notice when you speak casually or carelessly, and resolve to make your speech truthful and Kṛṣṇa-centered, since a sādhaka's words gain weight only through tapas and chanting.

BG 7.18

One day, a year after Kṛṣṇa’s appearance, mother Yaśodā was patting her son on her lap. But suddenly she felt the child to be heavier than a mountain peak, and she could no longer bear His weight.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa quietly bears the universe's weight to shield His devotee from grief — He arranges every difficulty before it touches those who love Him.

Practical applications
  • Notice one moment today when a heaviness lifted from your life unexpectedly, and offer silent thanks to Kṛṣṇa for the protection you did not see arranged.
  • Speak to someone you love about a fear you carry for them, and then formally entrust that fear to Kṛṣṇa instead of trying to control the outcome yourself.
  • Refuse to measure Kṛṣṇa's care by visible comfort — when something feels suddenly 'too heavy,' read it as His preparation, not His absence.

BG 7.19

Feeling the child to be as heavy as the entire universe and therefore being anxious, thinking that perhaps the child was being attacked by some other ghost or demon, the astonished mother Yaśodā put the child down on the ground and began to think of Nārāyaṇa. Foreseeing disturbances, she called for the brāhmaṇas to counteract this heaviness, and then she engaged in her other household affairs. She had no alternative than to remember the lotus feet of Nārāyaṇa, for she could not understand that Kṛṣṇa was the original source of everything.

Essence. Yaśodā cannot lift Kṛṣṇa because He carries the universe — when life turns unbearably heavy, the dhīra remembers the Lord rests on no one but Himself.

Practical applications
  • Notice the one burden today that feels heavier than it should, and instead of bracing harder, set it down and turn to Kṛṣṇa as Yaśodā turned to Nārāyaṇa.
  • Refuse the reflex of seeking only material counter-measures (the brāhmaṇas, the fix, the workaround) before first remembering the lotus feet of the Lord who already holds the weight.
  • Speak one honest sentence aloud today acknowledging that you are not the source — Kṛṣṇa is — and let that admission shape your next decision.

BG 7.20

While the child was sitting on the ground, a demon named Tṛṇāvarta, who was a servant of Kaṁsa’s, came there as a whirlwind, at Kaṁsa’s instigation, and very easily carried the child away into the air.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa makes Himself heavy or light by His own sweet will — surrender to His personal līlā, not to a force you can manage.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where Kṛṣṇa feels 'too heavy' to carry — a duty, a relationship, a sādhana — and recognize that weight as His personal arrangement, not your failure.
  • Refuse to reduce Kṛṣṇa to a metaphor or inner force; speak to Him by name in one concrete prayer this morning, asking to be carried as Yaśodā was relieved.
  • When a sudden 'whirlwind' (an unexpected loss, demand, or interruption) sweeps in today, pause and offer that moment to Kṛṣṇa instead of bracing against it.

BG 7.21

Covering the whole land of Gokula with particles of dust, that demon, acting as a strong whirlwind, covered everyone’s vision and began vibrating everywhere with a greatly fearful sound.

Essence. Māyā can blanket Gokula in dust and terror in a moment — only those sheltered in Kṛṣṇa keep their sight when the storm hides everything else.

Practical applications
  • Notice today the first 'dust storm' that obscures your discrimination — a news scroll, a quarrel, a craving — and step back to chant Kṛṣṇa's names before reacting.
  • Refuse to trust your own eyes when fear or noise rises; pause, remember that Kṛṣṇa is present in Gokula even now, and act from that shelter rather than from panic.
  • Identify one 'Tṛṇāvarta' in your week — a force that lifts you off the ground and spins you — and protect your sādhana time from it with a fixed, non-negotiable slot.

BG 7.22

For a moment, the whole pasturing ground was overcast with dense darkness from the dust storm, and mother Yaśodā was unable to find her son where she had placed Him.

Essence. When the dust storm of māyā suddenly blinds you and Kṛṣṇa seems lost from view, do not panic — He is exactly where you placed Him.

Practical applications
  • Notice the moments today when sudden agitation — rajas and tamas as a literal dust storm — obscures your remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, and pause until the air clears rather than acting from that blindness.
  • Refuse to conclude that Kṛṣṇa is absent simply because your senses cannot find Him; return to the place where you last 'set Him down' — your japa, your prasāda offering, your morning sādhana — and resume there.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa with the protective bhāva of Yaśodā today: stand guard over your remembrance of Him as a mother stands guard over her child in a storm.

BG 7.23

Because of the bits of sand thrown about by Tṛṇāvarta, people could not see themselves or anyone else, and thus they were illusioned and disturbed.

Essence. When māyā's dust storm blinds us to self and others, we lose all bearings — only Kṛṣṇa, the eye of the storm, remains the unshaken refuge.

Practical applications
  • Notice today the moments when sensory overload (notifications, noise, news) blinds you to the souls right in front of you, and pause to chant Kṛṣṇa's name once before responding.
  • Refuse to make any consequential decision while the dust of agitation is still in the air — wait until you can see yourself and others clearly again.
  • When you feel disoriented and cannot see your own next step, stop trying to navigate by your own sight and call on Kṛṣṇa, who alone sees through the storm.

BG 7.24

Because of the dust storm stirred up by the strong whirlwind, mother Yaśodā could find no trace of her son, nor could she understand why. Thus she fell down on the ground like a cow who has lost her calf and began to lament very pitifully.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa vanishes behind māyā's dust storm, even mother Yaśodā collapses — our bewilderment in His apparent absence is the call to deeper surrender.

Practical applications
  • Notice today the moments when Kṛṣṇa seems 'lost' to you — in distraction, anxiety, dust-storms of the mind — and call out to Him by name in that exact moment.
  • Refuse to interpret obscurity as absence; when sādhana feels dry, sit and chant anyway, trusting He has not gone anywhere.
  • Speak to someone grieving a loss today not with material consolation but by quietly steadying yourself in the conviction that the soul's relationship with Kṛṣṇa is never severed.

BG 7.25

When the force of the dust storm and the winds subsided, Yaśodā’s friends, the other gopīs, approached mother Yaśodā, hearing her pitiful crying. Not seeing Kṛṣṇa present, they too felt very much aggrieved and joined mother Yaśodā in crying, their eyes full of tears.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa is the center, His absence aches and His presence delights — let your daily emotional weather reveal where your heart truly rests.

Practical applications
  • Notice this evening which absence in your day caused real grief — and ask honestly whether Kṛṣṇa's absence registers with even a fraction of that weight.
  • Speak to one fellow devotee today about Kṛṣṇa specifically, so that your saṅga becomes, like the gopīs', a circle whose center is Him.
  • Refuse to let a day pass in which you do not consciously feel His presence in chanting or His absence in distraction — train the heart to track Him.

BG 7.26

Having assumed the form of a forceful whirlwind, the demon Tṛṇāvarta took Kṛṣṇa very high in the sky, but when Kṛṣṇa became heavier than the demon, the demon had to stop his force and could go no further.

Essence. No yogic feat can outweigh Kṛṣṇa — the demon's whirlwind stalls the moment the Lord chooses; rest your weight in Him, not in your powers.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are competing with Kṛṣṇa — trying to engineer an outcome by personal force — and consciously hand that weight back to Him.
  • Refuse to be impressed by displays of mystic or worldly power (in others or in yourself); test every claim against the simple measure: does it lead to surrender to Kṛṣṇa?
  • Before any task that tempts you to lean on your own siddhi (skill, charm, intellect), pause and pray to act as a servant whose strength is Kṛṣṇa's mercy, not personal vigour.

BG 7.27

Because of Kṛṣṇa’s weight, Tṛṇāvarta considered Him to be like a great mountain or a hunk of iron. But because Kṛṣṇa had caught the demon’s neck, the demon was unable to throw Him off. He therefore thought of the child as wonderful, since he could neither bear the child nor cast aside the burden.

Essence. Even the proudest material force becomes powerless when it tries to lift Kṛṣṇa — what tries to carry Him off ends up carried away by Him.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where some 'whirlwind' — anxiety, ambition, a craving — picked you up promising flight and is now too heavy to set down; sit, chant, and let Kṛṣṇa's weight settle it.
  • Refuse to bargain with the demon you invited in: when a habit you cannot throw off rises, do not negotiate, take shelter in Kṛṣṇa's name and let Him hold its neck.
  • Speak openly with one trusted devotee about the burden you have been pretending to manage alone, and ask for a concrete sādhana you can begin tonight.

BG 7.28

With Kṛṣṇa grasping him by the throat, Tṛṇāvarta choked, unable to make even a sound or even to move his hands and legs. His eyes popping out, the demon lost his life and fell, along with the little boy, down to the ground of Vraja.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa effortlessly slays the whirlwind demon Tṛṇāvarta — surrender to Him and the māyā-storms that try to abduct you cannot keep their grip.

Practical applications
  • Name the 'whirlwind' currently sweeping you off your ground — overwork, anxiety, a craving — and hand it specifically to Kṛṣṇa in your morning prayer before it lifts you again.
  • When a sudden upheaval strikes today, refuse the panic-response and instead chant Kṛṣṇa's names until the demon of agitation loses its breath.
  • Notice one situation where you feel powerless and remember that the same small Kṛṣṇa who choked Tṛṇāvarta is fully present in your sādhana — act from that shelter, not from fear.

BG 7.29

While the gopīs who had gathered were crying for Kṛṣṇa, the demon fell from the sky onto a big slab of stone, his limbs dislocated, as if he had been pierced by the arrow of Lord Śiva like Tripurāsura.

Essence. When devotees lament for Kṛṣṇa, He immediately acts — even calamities become further fuel for transcendental bliss in bhakti.

Practical applications
  • When you feel a wave of separation or distress in your sādhana today, do not suppress it — turn it into a direct call to Kṛṣṇa and watch how He responds.
  • Notice one 'calamity' in your day (a setback, a fear, a loss) and consciously reframe it as Kṛṣṇa's hand removing an obstacle, the way Tṛṇāvarta was struck down.
  • Sit with the gopīs' mood for five minutes today: cry inwardly for Kṛṣṇa's protection over someone you love, then trust that He has already acted.

BG 7.30

The gopīs immediately picked Kṛṣṇa up from the chest of the demon and delivered Him, free from all inauspiciousness, to mother Yaśodā. Because the child, although taken into the sky by the demon, was unhurt and now free from all danger and misfortune, the gopīs and cowherd men, headed by Nanda Mahārāja, were extremely happy.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa rests unhurt on the demon's chest as ānanda-cinmaya-vigraha — when surrendered to Him, even the mouth of death becomes a playground.

Practical applications
  • Name one 'Tṛṇāvarta' in your life — a force that lifted you into anxiety today — and consciously place the outcome at Kṛṣṇa's feet before acting.
  • When fear spikes, remember that Kṛṣṇa is sac-cid-ānanda in every condition; refuse to project your bodily panic onto the soul's situation.
  • Call or message one elder in your circle who has been protecting you (your Yaśodā or Nanda) and thank them concretely for one specific shelter they've given.

BG 7.31

It is most astonishing that although this innocent child was taken away by the Rākṣasa to be eaten, He has returned without having been killed or even injured. Because this demon was envious, cruel and sinful, he has been killed for his own sinful activities. This is the law of nature. An innocent devotee is always protected by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and a sinful person is always vanquished for his sinful life.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa always protects the sādhu who depends on Him; the cruel are undone by their own sin — your only business today is to take shelter.

Practical applications
  • Begin the day by audibly entrusting your safety to Kṛṣṇa with the conviction rakṣiṣyatīti viśvāsaḥ — He will protect me — and refuse to renegotiate that trust when fear arises.
  • Notice one situation today where you are scheming to engineer your own protection, and replace the scheming with śravaṇaṁ kīrtanam — hear or chant about Kṛṣṇa for ten focused minutes instead.
  • Refuse to envy or retaliate against someone who has wronged you; leave their correction to the law of nature and spend that energy serving a devotee instead.

BG 7.32

Nanda Mahārāja and the others said: We must previously have performed austerities for a very long time, worshiped the Supreme Personality of Godhead, performed pious activities for public life, constructing public roads and wells, and also given charity, as a result of which this boy, although faced with death, has returned to give happiness to His relatives.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa preserves what was nearly lost, recognize it as the ripening of past tapas, arcana, and bhūta-sauhṛdam — and keep sowing.

Practical applications
  • Today, perform one concrete act of bhūta-sauhṛdam — a tangible service to the public good (a donation, a repair, a kindness to a stranger) without announcing it.
  • When something dear is spared a near-disaster, refuse the words 'lucky' or 'coincidence' — name it aloud as Kṛṣṇa's mercy ripening from past arcana.
  • Pair every prayer of petition this week with a matching austerity (a skipped meal, an early rising, a held tongue) so that worship and tapas move together as Nanda Mahārāja describes.

BG 7.33

Having seen all these incidents in Bṛhadvana, Nanda Mahārāja became more and more astonished, and he remembered the words spoken to him by Vasudeva in Mathurā.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa repeatedly shatters our expectations, the wise — like Nanda — return to the trusted vaiṣṇava's words and let astonishment ripen into faith.

Practical applications
  • Recall one 'impossible' rescue or reversal in your own life today, and name it openly as Kṛṣṇa's hand rather than coincidence.
  • Return to a single instruction from a trusted Vaiṣṇava (your guru, Prabhupāda's books) that you once dismissed, and re-read it in light of what has happened since.
  • Refuse to flatten the wonder — when something astonishing occurs, pause and say 'Kṛṣṇa' before reaching for a mundane explanation.

BG 7.34

One day mother Yaśodā, having taken Kṛṣṇa up and placed Him on her lap, was feeding Him milk from her breast with maternal affection. The milk was flowing from her breast, and the child was drinking it.

Essence. Mother Yaśodā nurses Kṛṣṇa with sneha-pariplutā — pure parental love is itself a doorway to Him; the Lord accepts the affection of His devotee.

Practical applications
  • Notice one act of caregiving you perform today — feeding, cleaning, comforting — and consciously offer that affection to Kṛṣṇa, not merely to the body in front of you.
  • When you serve someone you love, refuse the impersonalist drift; remember Kṛṣṇa accepts service rendered in vātsalya, sakhya, or dāsya — the relationship is real and personal.
  • Speak to a parent or caretaker in your life today and recognize, in their sneha for you, a faint reflection of Yaśodā's love for Kṛṣṇa.

BG 7.35-36

O King Parīkṣit, when the child Kṛṣṇa was almost finished drinking His mother’s milk and mother Yaśodā was touching Him and looking at His beautiful, brilliantly smiling face, the baby yawned, and mother Yaśodā saw in His mouth the whole sky, the higher planetary system and the earth, the luminaries in all directions, the sun, the moon, fire, air, the seas, islands, mountains, rivers, forests, and all kinds of living entities, moving and nonmoving.

Essence. When baby Kṛṣṇa yawns, Yaśodā sees the entire cosmos in His mouth — the supreme Person holds all worlds, yet permits Himself to be loved as a child.

Practical applications
  • Today, when you eat or sip water, pause and remember that the same Kṛṣṇa who contains all universes accepts your small offering as a mother's milk.
  • Refuse the demigod-confusion: name one 'higher power' you have been treating as ultimate, and re-anchor your surrender in Kṛṣṇa, the source within whom even the sun, moon, and earth abide.
  • Speak to someone you usually 'manage' — a child, a parent, a colleague — and consciously look for Kṛṣṇa within them, the way Yaśodā glimpsed the virāṭ-rūpa within her own son.

BG 7.37

When mother Yaśodā saw the whole universe within the mouth of her child, her heart began to throb, and in astonishment she wanted to close her restless eyes.

Essence. Even when Kṛṣṇa flashes His opulence, Yoga-māyā shields pure vātsalya — relate to Him in your own role rather than chasing displays of grandeur.

Practical applications
  • Choose one fixed relationship with Kṛṣṇa (servant, friend, child, parent) and serve Him in that mood today instead of demanding cosmic visions.
  • Notice when you crave a 'wow' spiritual experience to validate your faith, and refuse it — return to chanting and simple service.
  • When something astonishing happens in sādhana, close the restless eyes of the mind and steady yourself in the duty Kṛṣṇa has placed before you.
