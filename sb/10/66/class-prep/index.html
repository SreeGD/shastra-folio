Chapter 66 · Class prep

A printable handout for teaching this chapter. For each verse with Personal Guidance, you'll see Prabhupāda's translation, the essence, two or three practical applications, and a discussion prompt. Press ⌘P / Ctrl P to print as a handout.

BG 66.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O King, while Lord Balarāma was away visiting Nanda’s village of Vraja, the ruler of Karūṣa, foolishly thinking “I am the Supreme Lord, Vāsudeva,” sent a messenger to Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Pauṇḍraka, ruler of Karūṣa, believed his own delusion of being God and sent a challenging message to Kṛṣṇa. His foolishness shows how the ego, when unchecked, mistakes itself for the Supreme.

BG 66.2

Pauṇḍraka was emboldened by the flattery of childish men, who told him, “You are Vāsudeva, the Supreme Lord and master of the universe, who have now descended to the earth.” Thus he imagined himself to be the infallible Personality of Godhead.

Essence. Pauṇḍraka's delusion was fed by flatterers who told him exactly what he wanted to hear. Surrounding ourselves with sycophants is one of the quickest ways to lose contact with reality.

BG 66.3

Thus slow-witted King Pauṇḍraka sent a messenger to the inscrutable Lord Kṛṣṇa at Dvārakā. Pauṇḍraka was acting just like an unintelligent child whom other children are pretending is a king.

Essence. Even great sages are astonished at the audacity of foolishness — declaring oneself God to God. The verse exposes how cleverness without wisdom only deepens the absurdity of our self-deception.

BG 66.4

Arriving in Dvārakā, the messenger found lotus-eyed Kṛṣṇa in His royal assembly and relayed the King’s message to that almighty Lord.

Essence. The messenger entered the assembly of the actual Lord and delivered a message claiming the Lord was an impostor. The scene foreshadows how spiritual challenges, however brash, are received by Kṛṣṇa with composure.

BG 66.5

[On Pauṇḍraka’s behalf, the messenger said:] I am the one and only Lord Vāsudeva, and there is no other. It is I who have descended to this world to show mercy to the living beings. Therefore give up Your false name.

Essence. Viśvanātha Cakravartī reads the verse against the grain: even Pauṇḍraka's defiance can be heard as a hidden plea for Kṛṣṇa to strip away his false identity. Sometimes our loudest rebellion is a covert prayer for help.

BG 66.6

O Sātvata, give up my personal symbols, which out of foolishness You now carry, and come to me for shelter. If You do not, then You must give me battle.

Essence. Pauṇḍraka carried imitation symbols of the Lord — fake conch, disc, lotus, club. Religious paraphernalia without inner substance becomes a costume, and the Lord eventually has to mercifully strip it away.

BG 66.7

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: King Ugrasena and the other members of the assembly laughed loudly when they heard this vain boasting of unintelligent Pauṇḍraka.

Essence. Ugrasena and the royal court burst into laughter at Pauṇḍraka's pretensions. There is something inherently absurd about a tiny ego claiming to be the Absolute — and devotees can laugh at it kindly, including in themselves.

BG 66.8

The Personality of Godhead, after enjoying the jokes of the assembly, told the messenger [to relay a message to his master:] “You fool, I will indeed let loose the weapons you boast of in this way.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa accepts Pauṇḍraka's challenge with humor: 'Yes, fool, I will release my weapons — on the battlefield.' The Lord meets bluster directly, neither flinching nor pretending it deserves serious negotiation.

BG 66.9

“When you lie dead, O fool, your face covered by vultures, herons and vaṭa birds, you will become the shelter of dogs.”

Essence. Kṛṣṇa reverses Pauṇḍraka's demand for shelter: 'You will become the shelter of dogs.' Whatever shelter we claim outside of surrender to the Lord will not last beyond this body.

BG 66.10

When the Lord had thus spoken, the messenger conveyed His insulting reply to his master in its entirety. Lord Kṛṣṇa then mounted His chariot and went to the vicinity of Kāśī.

Essence. Words eventually give way to action. Kṛṣṇa mounted His chariot and went directly to Kāśī to settle the matter. The Lord answers slowly, but when He moves, He moves decisively.

BG 66.11

Upon observing Lord Kṛṣṇa’s preparations for battle, the mighty warrior Pauṇḍraka quickly went out of the city with two full military divisions.

Essence. Pauṇḍraka came out with full military force, confident in his ability to oppose the Lord. Material strength gives a sense of security that can be quickly humbled when reality arrives.

BG 66.12-14

Pauṇḍraka’s friend, the King of Kāśī, followed behind, O King, leading the rear guard with three akṣauhiṇī divisions. Lord Kṛṣṇa saw that Pauṇḍraka was carrying the Lord’s own insignia, such as the conchshell, disc, sword and club, and also an imitation Śārṅga bow and Śrīvatsa mark. He wore a mock Kaustubha gem, was decorated with a garland of forest flowers and was dressed in upper and lower garments of fine yellow silk. His banner bore the image of Garuḍa, and he wore a valuable crown and gleaming, shark-shaped earrings.

Essence. The verse contrasts the real and the counterfeit: Pauṇḍraka wears imitation Śrīvatsa, Kaustubha, and forest garland — every detail copied without the underlying reality. Spiritual life is not about copying form; it is about realizing substance.

BG 66.15

Lord Hari laughed heartily when He saw how the King had dressed up in exact imitation of His own appearance, just like an actor onstage.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa laughed openly when He saw Pauṇḍraka dressed like Him — an actor onstage pretending to be Vāsudeva. The Lord is not threatened by imitators; He finds them funny, and that laughter is itself a kind of mercy.

BG 66.16

The enemies of Lord Hari attacked Him with tridents, clubs, bludgeons, pikes, ṛṣtis, barbed darts, lances, swords, axes and arrows.

Essence. Multiple enemies attacked the Lord with every weapon imaginable. When we set ourselves against truth, we tend to throw everything we have at it — but truth is unmoved.

BG 66.17

But Lord Kṛṣṇa fiercely struck back at the army of Pauṇḍraka and Kāśirāja, which consisted of elephants, chariots, cavalry and infantry. The Lord tormented His enemies with His club, sword, Sudarśana disc and arrows, just as the fire of annihilation torments the various kinds of creatures at the end of a cosmic age.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's response to overwhelming attack is like the fire of cosmic annihilation — total and effortless. What looks like a battle of opposites is really an opportunity for everything false to dissolve.

BG 66.18

The battlefield, strewn with the dismembered chariots, horses, elephants, humans, mules and camels that had been cut to pieces by the Lord’s disc weapon, shone like the gruesome playground of Lord Bhūtapati, giving pleasure to the wise.

Essence. The battlefield, devastated yet beautiful in a dark way, gave pleasure to the wise. Those who see clearly find a strange satisfaction when illusion is finally cut down, even painfully.

BG 66.19

Lord Kṛṣṇa then addressed Pauṇḍraka: My dear Pauṇḍraka, the very weapons you spoke of through your messenger, I now release unto you.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa keeps His word — 'the weapons you spoke of, I now release.' The Lord is straightforward; what He says He will do, He does. There is no theater in His action, only fidelity to truth.

BG 66.20

O fool, now I shall make you renounce My name, which you have falsely assumed. And I will certainly take shelter of you if I do not wish to fight you.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa playfully echoes Pauṇḍraka's own challenge back to him: 'I will surrender to you if I lose.' The Lord meets us in our own language while gently exposing how empty it really was.

BG 66.21

Having thus derided Pauṇḍraka, Lord Kṛṣṇa destroyed his chariot with His sharp arrows. The Lord then cut off his head with the Sudarśana disc, just as Lord Indra lops off a mountain peak with his thunderbolt weapon.

Essence. With one stroke of the Sudarśana, Pauṇḍraka's head was severed and his pretense ended. The illusion of being Vāsudeva could not survive contact with the actual Vāsudeva.

BG 66.22

With His arrows, Lord Kṛṣṇa similarly severed Kāśirāja’s head from his body, sending it flying into Kāśī city like a lotus flower thrown by the wind.

Essence. Kāśirāja's head flew into his own city like a lotus on the wind. The king who promised to bring back the enemy's head had instead delivered his own — a vivid reminder of how plans against God recoil on the planner.

BG 66.23

Having thus killed envious Pauṇḍraka and his ally, Lord Kṛṣṇa returned to Dvārakā. As He entered the city, the Siddhas of heaven chanted His immortal, nectarean glories.

Essence. After the battle, the Siddhas sang the Lord's nectarean glories as He returned home. Every divine pastime, however violent on the surface, ends in glorification — that is its true center of gravity.

BG 66.24

By constantly meditating upon the Supreme Lord, Pauṇḍraka shattered all his material bonds. Indeed, by imitating Lord Kṛṣṇa’s appearance, O King, he ultimately became Kṛṣṇa conscious.

Essence. Astonishingly, Pauṇḍraka attained sārūpya liberation — his constant absorption in Kṛṣṇa's form, even in imitation, ultimately purified him. Even misdirected attention on Kṛṣṇa can become a hidden path home.

BG 66.25

Seeing a head decorated with earrings lying at the gate of the royal palace, the people present were puzzled. Some of them asked, “What is this?” and others said, “It is a head, but whose is it?”

Essence. A head with earrings appeared at the city gate, leaving citizens puzzled. We often mistake what is happening in front of us until the full picture emerges. Spiritual life requires patience with not-yet-knowing.

BG 66.26

My dear King, when they recognized it as the head of their King — the lord of Kāśi — his queens, sons and other relatives, along with all the citizens of the city, began to cry pitifully: “Alas, we are killed! O my lord, my lord!”

Essence. The queens and citizens of Kāśī cried out at recognizing their fallen king. Material relationships, however dear, end in grief when their basis is mortal. The verse quietly invites a deeper loyalty.

BG 66.27-28

After the King’s son Sudakṣiṇa had performed the obligatory funeral rituals for his father, he resolved within his mind: “Only by killing my father’s murderer can I avenge his death.” Thus the charitable Sudakṣiṇa, together with his priests, began worshiping Lord Maheśvara with great attention.

Essence. Rather than reflecting on why his father had been killed, Sudakṣiṇa vowed revenge and turned to dark worship of Śiva. Grief unprocessed becomes a fuel for further destruction.

BG 66.29

Satisfied by the worship, the powerful Lord Śiva appeared in the sacred precinct of Avimukta and offered Sudakṣiṇa his choice of benedictions. The prince chose as his benediction a means to slay his father’s killer.

Essence. Lord Śiva, satisfied, offered a boon — and Sudakṣiṇa chose the death of his father's killer. Even genuine spiritual access can be misused when the heart is set on vengeance instead of truth.

BG 66.30-31

Lord Śiva told him, “Accompanied by brāhmaṇas, serve the Dakṣiṇāgni fire — the original priest — following the injunctions of the abhicāra ritual. Then the Dakṣiṇāgni fire, together with many Pramathas, will fulfill your desire if you direct it against someone inimical to the brāhmaṇas.” So instructed, Sudakṣiṇa strictly observed the ritualistic vows and invoked the abhicāra against Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Lord Śiva warned that the abhicāra ritual could only succeed against an enemy of brāhmaṇas; directed at Kṛṣṇa, it would consume its own creator. Spiritual weapons aimed at the Lord always backfire.

BG 66.32-33

Thereupon the fire rose up out of the altar pit, assuming the form of an extremely fearsome, naked person. The fiery creature’s beard and tuft of hair were like molten copper, and his eyes emitted blazing hot cinders. His face looked most frightful with its fangs and terrible arched and furrowed brows. As he licked the corners of his mouth with his tongue, the demon shook his flaming trident.

Essence. From the sacrificial fire arose a terrifying figure — fangs, blazing eyes, molten beard. The energies we summon in anger take on a shape, and that shape often becomes ungovernable.

BG 66.34

On legs as tall as palm trees, the monster raced toward Dvārakā in the company of ghostly spirits, shaking the ground and burning the world in all directions.

Essence. The demon raced toward Dvārakā with palm-tall strides, scorching everything in its path. Destructive impulses launched outward never travel cleanly; they harm the world they pass through.

BG 66.35

Seeing the approacḥ of the fiery demon created by the abhicāra ritual, the residents of Dvārakā were all struck with fear, like animals terrified by a forest fire.

Essence. Dvārakā's citizens trembled like animals before a forest fire. Even devotees experience fear when something unprecedented appears — and that fear becomes a doorway to deeper dependence on the Lord.

BG 66.36

Distraught with fear, the people cried out to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who was then playing at dice in the royal court: “Save us! Save us, O Lord of the three worlds, from this fire burning up the city!”

Essence. Distraught, the people cried out, 'Save us, save us!' to the Lord who was casually playing dice. The most direct prayer is often the simplest cry — and the Lord hears it even mid-pastime.

BG 66.37

When Lord Kṛṣṇa heard the people’s agitation and saw that even His own men were disturbed, that most worthy giver of shelter simply laughed and told them, “Do not fear; I shall protect you.”

Essence. Kṛṣṇa simply laughed and said, 'Do not fear; I shall protect you.' Real protection sounds light because it is utterly secure. Anxiety is a sign that we have forgotten who is actually in charge.

BG 66.38

The almighty Lord, the internal and external witness of all, understood that the monster had been produced by Lord Śiva from the sacrificial fire. To defeat the demon, Kṛṣṇa dispatched His disc weapon, who was waiting at His side.

Essence. Without leaving His dice game, Kṛṣṇa dispatched the Sudarśana cakra. The Lord does not need to interrupt His play to handle our crises; one glance, one gesture, is enough.

BG 66.39

That Sudarśana, the disc weapon of Lord Mukunda, blazed forth like millions of suns. His effulgence blazed like the fire of universal annihilation, and with his heat he pained the sky, all the directions, heaven and earth, and also the fiery demon.

Essence. The Sudarśana blazed like millions of suns, scorching every direction. The Lord's protective energy is not small or polite; it is overwhelming, exactly enough for whatever is coming at His devotees.

BG 66.40

Frustrated by the power of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s weapon, O King, the fiery creature produced by black magic turned his face away and retreated. Created for violence, the demon then returned to Vārāṇasī, where he surrounded the city and then burned Sudakṣiṇa and his priests to death, even though Sudakṣiṇa was his creator.

Essence. The fiery demon, defeated by the cakra, turned back and consumed its own creator — Sudakṣiṇa. Black magic, when it fails against its target, must kill someone, and it kills the one who summoned it.

BG 66.41

Lord Viṣṇu’s disc also entered Vārāṇasī, in pursuit of the fiery demon, and proceeded to burn the city to the ground, including all its assembly halls and residential palaces with raised porches, its numerous marketplaces, gateways, watchtowers, warehouses and treasuries, and all the buildings housing elephants, horses, chariots and grains.

Essence. The disc burned Vārāṇasī to the ground — markets, palaces, treasuries, watchtowers. Civilizations built on opposition to the Lord, however grand, do not endure.

BG 66.42

After burning down the entire city of Vārāṇasī, Lord Viṣṇu’s Sudarśana cakra returned to the side of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, whose actions are effortless.

Essence. Having done its work, the Sudarśana returned to Kṛṣṇa's side as if nothing had happened. The Lord's actions are effortless — vast outcomes flow from Him without strain.

BG 66.43

Any mortal who recounts this heroic pastime of Lord Uttamaḥ-śloka’s, or who simply hears it attentively, will become freed from all sins.

Essence. Even hearing this pastime attentively frees one from sin. The point of remembering the story is not entertainment but purification — and the simple act of attentive hearing is itself a sādhana.
