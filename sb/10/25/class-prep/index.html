Chapter 25 · Class prep

BG 25.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: My dear King Parīkṣit, when Indra understood that his sacrifice had been put aside, he became furious with Nanda Mahārāja and the other cowherd men, who were accepting Kṛṣṇa as their Lord.

Essence. Indra, finding his sacrifice ignored, becomes furious at Nanda and the cowherds who accept Kṛṣṇa as Lord. Pride goes before a fall — the demigod who lost worship rages, exposing how shallow his identity was.

BG 25.2

Angry Indra sent forth the clouds of universal destruction, known as Sāṁvartaka. Imagining himself the supreme controller, he spoke as follows.

Essence. Imagining himself the supreme controller, Indra sends the Sāṁvartaka clouds of universal destruction. The 'me generation' is older than modernity — īśa-māna, thinking oneself the Lord, is a perennial trap.

BG 25.3

[Indra said:] Just see how these cowherd men living in the forest have become so greatly intoxicated by their prosperity! They have surrendered to an ordinary human being, Kṛṣṇa, and thus they have offended the gods.

Essence. 'These forest-dwelling cowherds, intoxicated by prosperity, have surrendered to a mortal Kṛṣṇa and offended us gods.' Indra calls Kṛṣṇa 'mortal' — the deepest miscalculation possible. Bigotry against the Supreme begins with category errors.

BG 25.4

Their taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa is just like the foolish attempt of men who abandon transcendental knowledge of the self and instead try to cross over the great ocean of material existence in the false boats of fruitive, ritual sacrifices.

Essence. Indra likens their shelter of Kṛṣṇa to crossing the ocean in 'false boats of fruitive rituals.' Indra inverts truth — ritual is his boat, while Kṛṣṇa is the actual shore. Slander often mirrors the slanderer.

BG 25.5

These cowherd men have acted inimically toward me by taking shelter of this ordinary human being, Kṛṣṇa, who thinks Himself very wise but who is simply a foolish, arrogant, overtalkative child.

Essence. Indra calls Kṛṣṇa 'foolish, arrogant, overtalkative child.' But Sarasvatī, says the ācārya, is using the insults to glorify Him — bāliśa as pretensionless, stabdha as bowing to none because none is higher, ajña as needing no further knowing.

BG 25.6

[To the clouds of destruction King Indra said:] The prosperity of these people has made them mad with pride, and their arrogance is backed up by Kṛṣṇa. Now go and remove their pride and bring their animals to destruction.

Essence. 'Remove their pride and kill their animals.' Indra targets the cows. Attacks on devotional community often hit the cows — the source of subsistence and gentleness — because that's where prosperity flows from.

BG 25.7

I will follow you to Vraja, riding on my elephant Airāvata and taking with me the swift and powerful wind-gods to decimate the cowherd village of Nanda Mahārāja.

Essence. 'I will follow on Airāvata with wind-gods to decimate Nanda's village.' Indra commits personally — anger leads even celestial administrators into petty operations. Pride does not delegate; it shows up.

BG 25.8

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: On Indra’s order the clouds of universal destruction, released untimely from their bonds, went to the cowherd pastures of Nanda Mahārāja. There they began to torment the inhabitants by powerfully pouring down torrents of rain upon them.

Essence. On Indra's order the destructive clouds reach Nanda's pastures and torment the residents with torrents. The Lord's devotees experience real distress when His pastime calls for it — protection is not automatic immunity from weather.

BG 25.9

Propelled by the fearsome wind-gods, the clouds blazed with lightning bolts and roared with thunder as they hurled down hailstones.

Essence. Wind-gods drive clouds blazing with lightning and hurling hail. Cosmic forces gather against the devotees. Sometimes the elements themselves seem coordinated against us.

BG 25.10

As the clouds released torrents of rain as thick as massive columns, the earth was submerged in the flood, and high ground could no longer be distinguished from low.

Essence. Rain falls in column-thick torrents; earth submerges, high and low indistinguishable. When the storm is total, distinctions collapse — the response cannot be situational planning but a single shelter.

BG 25.11

The cows and other animals, shivering from the excessive rain and wind, and the cowherd men and ladies, pained by the cold, all approached Lord Govinda for shelter.

Essence. Shivering cows, pained cowherds, all approach Govinda for shelter. Distress drives every member of the community to one source — and they go together, not separately.

BG 25.12

Trembling from the distress brought about by the severe rainfall, and trying to cover their heads and calves with their own bodies, the cows approached the lotus feet of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

Essence. Cows tremble and use their bodies to cover their calves' heads, then approach the Lord's feet. Even animals model self-sacrificial protection — care for offspring is itself a posture of seeking the Lord.

BG 25.13

[The cowherd men and women addressed the Lord:] Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa, O most fortunate one, please deliver the cows from the wrath of Indra! O Lord, You are so affectionate to Your devotees. Please save us also.

Essence. 'Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa, O fortunate one, deliver the cows from Indra's wrath! Save us too.' Naming the cows first, themselves second — devotional petition often holds others before self.

BG 25.14

Seeing the inhabitants of His Gokula rendered practically unconscious by the onslaught of hail and blasting wind, the Supreme Lord Hari understood that this was the work of angry Indra.

Essence. Seeing the residents nearly unconscious, Hari understands this is Indra's anger. The Lord allows distress to enhance later joy — like hunger sharpening the pleasure of food.

BG 25.15

[Śrī Kṛṣṇa said to Himself:] Because We have stopped his sacrifice, Indra has caused this unusually fierce, unseasonable rain, together with terrible winds and hail.

Essence. 'Because We stopped his sacrifice, Indra has caused unseasonable rain and wind.' The Lord names the cause aloud — no mystery — and accepts the consequence of His own provocation. He owns the chain.

BG 25.16

By My mystic power I will completely counteract this disturbance caused by Indra. Demigods like Indra are proud of their opulence, and out of foolishness they falsely consider themselves the Lord of the universe. I will now destroy such ignorance.

Essence. 'By My mystic power I will counteract this. Demigods proud of opulence think themselves Lord of the universe; I will destroy that ignorance.' The Lord plans to humble not destroy, to enlighten not punish.

BG 25.17

Since the demigods are endowed with the mode of goodness, the false pride of considering oneself the Lord should certainly not affect them. When I break the false prestige of those bereft of goodness, My purpose is to bring them relief.

Essence. 'The demigods are in goodness; false pride shouldn't affect them. I break the pride of those bereft of goodness to bring them relief.' Breaking pride is relief, not punishment.

BG 25.18

I must therefore protect the cowherd community by My transcendental potency, for I am their shelter, I am their master, and indeed they are My own family. After all, I have taken a vow to protect My devotees.

Essence. 'I must protect the cowherd community — they are My shelter, master, family. I have vowed to protect My devotees.' The Lord identifies as Himself sheltered by His devotees, not standing apart from them.

BG 25.19

Having said this, Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is Viṣṇu Himself, picked up Govardhana Hill with one hand and held it aloft just as easily as a child holds up a mushroom.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, Viṣṇu Himself, picks up Govardhana Hill with one hand, easy as a child holding a mushroom. The Lord's strength turns the impossible task into the child's gesture; what crushes us is light to Him.

BG 25.20

The Lord then addressed the cowherd community: O Mother, O Father, O residents of Vraja, if you wish you may now come under this hill with your cows.

Essence. 'O Mother, O Father, O residents of Vraja — come under this hill with your cows.' The Lord opens the shelter explicitly and verbally; nobody has to guess whether they are welcome.

BG 25.21

You should have no fear that this mountain will fall from My hand. And don’t be afraid of the wind and rain, for your deliverance from these afflictions has already been arranged.

Essence. 'Don't fear the hill will fall from My hand. Don't fear wind and rain. Your deliverance is already arranged.' The Lord names the specific fears and addresses each. Reassurance is concrete, not generic.

BG 25.22

Their minds thus pacified by Lord Kṛṣṇa, they all entered beneath the hill, where they found ample room for themselves and all their cows, wagons, servants and priests, and for all other members of the community as well.

Essence. Pacified, they enter beneath the hill, finding ample room for all — cows, wagons, servants, priests, all the community. Real shelter accommodates the whole life, not just the urgent piece.

BG 25.23

Lord Kṛṣṇa, forgetting hunger and thirst and putting aside all considerations of personal pleasure, stood there holding up the hill for seven days as the people of Vraja gazed upon Him.

Essence. Forgetting hunger and thirst and personal pleasure, Kṛṣṇa holds the hill for seven days while everyone gazes at Him. Protection is sustained presence; the Lord doesn't lift and leave but stays for as long as the danger lasts.

BG 25.24

When Indra observed this exhibition of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s mystic power, he became most astonished. Pulled down from his platform of false pride, and his intentions thwarted, he ordered his clouds to desist.

Essence. Indra, astonished and stripped of false pride, orders the clouds to stop. Witnessing real divine power dissolves prideful intent — sometimes the opposition simply runs out of arrogance.

BG 25.25

Seeing that the fierce wind and rain had now ceased, the sky had become clear of rainclouds, and the sun had risen, Lord Kṛṣṇa, the lifter of Govardhana Hill, spoke to the cowherd community as follows.

Essence. Seeing fierce wind and rain ceased, sky clear, sun risen, the Lord speaks. Even calm needs verbal confirmation — He doesn't assume everyone will read the clearing correctly without His word.

BG 25.26

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] My dear cowherd men, please go out with your wives, children and possessions. Give up your fear. The wind and rain have stopped, and the rivers’ high waters have subsided.

Essence. 'Go out with your wives, children, possessions. Give up fear. Wind and rain have stopped; rivers have receded.' Specific permission to resume normal life after danger — the Lord reopens ordinary motion.

BG 25.27

After collecting their respective cows and loading their paraphernalia into their wagons, the cowherd men went out. The women, children and elderly persons gradually followed them.

Essence. The cowherd men gather cows and load wagons; women, children, elders follow gradually. Recovery from crisis is staged and orderly — the strongest move first, others follow at sustainable pace.

BG 25.28

While all living creatures looked on, the Supreme Personality of Godhead put down the hill in its original place, just as it had stood before.

Essence. With all creatures watching, the Lord puts Govardhana back exactly as it was. Service completed is also visibly closed — He returns the borrowed shelter, doesn't leave a mess.

BG 25.29

All the residents of Vṛndāvana were overwhelmed with ecstatic love, and they came forward and greeted Śrī Kṛṣṇa according to their individual relationships with Him — some embracing Him, others bowing down to Him, and so forth. The cowherd women presented water mixed with yogurt and unbroken barleycorns as a token of honor, and they showered auspicious benedictions upon Him.

Essence. Each resident greets Kṛṣṇa according to their relationship — embracing, bowing, offering yogurt-water and barley benedictions. After rescue, different roles express the same love differently; standardization is not required.

BG 25.30

Mother Yaśodā, mother Rohiṇī, Nanda Mahārāja and Balarāma, the greatest of the strong, all embraced Kṛṣṇa. Overwhelmed with affection, they offered Him their blessings.

Essence. Yaśodā, Rohiṇī, Nanda, and Balarāma embrace Kṛṣṇa and offer blessings, overwhelmed with affection. Even the strongest, like Balarāma, embrace — strength does not preclude tenderness.

BG 25.31

In the heavens, O King, all the demigods, including the Siddhas, Sādhyas, Gandharvas and Cāraṇas, sang the praises of Lord Kṛṣṇa and showered down flowers in great satisfaction.

Essence. In heavens, Siddhas, Sādhyas, Gandharvas, Cāraṇas sing Kṛṣṇa's praises and shower flowers in satisfaction. Heaven joins in earthly festivals — celebration is multi-realm when the Lord acts.

BG 25.32

My dear Parīkṣit, the demigods in heaven resoundingly played their conchshells and kettledrums, and the best of the Gandharvas, led by Tumburu, began to sing.

Essence. Conches and kettledrums resound; Tumburu and best Gandharvas sing. Festival sound has its own theology — instruments and voices together mark a victory of the Lord.

BG 25.33

Surrounded by His loving cowherd boyfriends and Lord Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa then went off to the place where He had been tending His cows. The cowherd girls returned to their homes, singing joyfully about the lifting of Govardhana Hill and other glorious deeds performed by Lord Kṛṣṇa, who had so deeply touched their hearts.

Essence. Surrounded by friends and Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa returns to the pasture; the gopīs go home singing joyfully about the hill-lifting. Even the most miraculous pastime ends with everyone going back to ordinary life — singing as they go.
