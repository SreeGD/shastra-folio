Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.4.27

शोकहर्षभयद्वेषलोभमोहमदान्विता: । मिथो घ्नन्तं न पश्यन्ति भावैर्भावं पृथग्द‍ृश: ॥ २७ ॥

Important Words
  • śoka — lamentation
    The first item in the list of six bodily-conception affects. Śoka is grief over what is lost (or feared lost). The Gītā 2.11 begins with Kṛṣṇa noting that the wise do not grieve — the same correction Vasudeva is gently giving.
  • harṣa — jubilation
    Its partner. Jubilation over what is gained. Both śoka and harṣa are children of the bodily ego; the steady devotee, fixed in Kṛṣṇa, is balanced between them (sama-duḥkha-sukha, BG 2.15).
  • bhaya — fear
    Fear of loss or of harm. The verse lists it among the symptoms of pṛthak-dṛk. The Bhāgavatam's elsewhere-stated cure: bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syāt — fear arises from absorption in the second; surrender to the One is fearlessness.
  • lobha — greed
    From √lubh — to crave. The endless reaching for more. Greed is one of Yamarāja's three gates of hell (Gītā 16.21, alongside kāma and krodha). Bhakti redirects lobha — into greed for Kṛṣṇa's name, mood, and service.
  • moha — illusion / bewilderment
    From √muh — to be confused. Moha is the master-symptom — under it, śoka and harṣa and bhaya and lobha all arise. The Gītā 2.52 and 18.73 chart Arjuna's liberation from moha; the same path is offered to Kaṁsa here in compressed form.
  • pṛthak-dṛśaḥ — of those with separate-vision
    Compound: 'separate-seeing'. Genitive plural. Those who see Kṛṣṇa's energies as separate from Him fall into all the above affects. The Gauḍīya acintya-bhedābheda corrects this: real bheda is held within real abheda, and seeing both at once is the vision of the surrendered soul.
Persons with the vision of differentiation are imbued with the material qualities lamentation, jubilation, fear, envy, greed, illusion and madness. They are influenced by the immediate cause, which they are busy counteracting, because they have no knowledge of the remote, supreme cause, the Personality of Godhead.
Purport

Kṛṣṇa is the cause of all causes ( sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam ), but one who has no connection with Kṛṣṇa is disturbed by immediate causes and cannot restrain his vision of separation or differences. When an expert physician treats a patient, he tries to find the original cause of the disease and is not diverted by the symptoms of that original cause. Similarly, a devotee is never disturbed by reverses in life. Tat te ’nukampāṁ susamīkṣamāṇaḥ ( Bhāg. 10.14.8 ). A devotee understands that when he is in distress, this is due to his own past misdeeds, which are now accruing reactions, although by the grace of the Supreme Personality of Godhead these are only very slight. Karmāṇi nirdahati kintu ca bhakti-bhājām ( Brahma-saṁhitā 5.54 ). When a devotee under the protection of the Supreme Personality of Godhead is to suffer because of faults in his past deeds, he passes through only a little misery by the grace of the Lord. Although the disease of a devotee is due to mistakes committed sometime in the past, he agrees to suffer and tolerate such miseries, and he depends fully on the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Thus he is never affected by material conditions of lamentation, jubilation, fear and so on. A devotee never sees anything to be unconnected with the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Śrīla Madhvācārya, quoting from the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, says: bhagavad-darśanād yasya virodhād darśanaṁ pṛthak pṛthag-dṛṣṭiḥ sa vijñeyo na tu sad-bheda-darśanaḥ

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Persons with the vision of differentiation are imbued with the material qualities lamentation, jubilation, fear, envy, greed, illusion and madness. They are influenced by the immediate cause, which they are busy counteracting, because they have no knowledge of the remote, supreme cause, the Personality of Godhead.
Commentary

Those jivas with external material vision cannot see that the Lord arranges that some entities and forces, such as kings, tigers, and disease are killing others such as men and cows.

Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Those with material vision (pṛthag-dṛśaḥ) do not see that the truth about one entity being killed by others (bhāvaiḥ). Actually the Lord is the killer, not the body or the ātmā. The body dies, but not the ātmā. They mutually kill each other, one being the cause of killing the other. Another meaning is “Such ignorant people do not see that they are born in order to kill others like them (mithaḥ).”

Śrīdhara Svāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

Those with material vision (pṛthag-dṛśaḥ) do not see that the truth about one entity being killed by others (bhāvaiḥ). In the list of material qualities the order is with increasing ability to produce illusion or inability to recognize the Lord. Happiness from material objects (harṣa) creates more illusion than lamentation. Fear of death creates more illusion than happiness because sometimes by suffering one gains knowledge of the Lord. By fear, the heart becomes agitated. Ignorant persons do not see the Lord who has appeared directly to kill Duryodhana through the Pāṇḍavas and to kill the troops of Jarāsandha through the Yadus (mitho ghnāntam).

Personal Guidance
When you forget Kṛṣṇa as the cause of all causes, you exhaust yourself fighting symptoms — śoka, harṣa, bhaya — instead of taking shelter of the source.
Practical Applications
  • Notice the next time you feel śoka, bhaya, or lobha rising, and instead of strategizing against the immediate trigger, name Kṛṣṇa as the remote cause and offer the disturbance to Him.
  • When something goes wrong today, refuse the reflex to blame the proximate person or circumstance — read it as tat te 'nukampām, a slight reaction softened by the Lord's mercy, and tolerate it.
  • Speak to one situation today that you have been treating as 'separate' from Kṛṣṇa — your work, a difficult relative, your health — and consciously reconnect it to Him in plain words before acting.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I exhausting myself counteracting an immediate cause — a person, a circumstance, a setback — while ignoring that Kṛṣṇa, the sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam, has arranged this for my purification?
  • Which recent reversal did I treat as an injustice to be fought rather than as anukampā — slight reaction to past karma, softened by the Lord's grace — and what would change in my response if I truly saw it that way?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When an exam result, a breakup, or a friend's betrayal hits, write down the immediate cause you are blaming (the professor, the ex, the friend) and then write underneath: 'And Kṛṣṇa is the sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam behind this.' Do not skip the first line — name the immediate cause honestly — but do not stop there.
  • Audit your last week of social-media reactions. How many were śoka (someone's highlight reel made you sad), harṣa (a like-spike made you elated), or dveṣa (a rival's win stung)? Each of those is the verse's seven-fold list operating in real time, on a screen, on pṛthag-dṛṣṭi.
  • Next time peer pressure pushes you toward lobha or mada — the party, the cheating shortcut, the 'everyone's doing it' purchase — pause and ask: am I treating my friend group as the cause? They are an immediate cause. The remote cause is what I am avoiding.
householder
  • When a family conflict erupts — spouse, in-laws, a child's school crisis — resist the householder reflex of 'who is to blame and how do I fix them.' Treat the person in front of you as the immediate cause and Kṛṣṇa as the remote cause; then act on your kartavya without dveṣa or moha contaminating the action.
  • Make a money decision this week — a job offer, an investment, a major purchase — and notice how lobha (greed for upside) and bhaya (fear of loss) drive the deliberation. Both are pṛthag-dṛṣṭi. Offer the decision to Kṛṣṇa first, then choose with sober judgment, not with the seven disturbances in the driver's seat.
  • When your child suffers — a failed exam, a social wound, an illness — your first instinct is to eliminate the immediate cause. Do that, as a parent must. But do not let the elimination of the symptom become your whole spiritual life. Teach the child, by your own steadiness, that there is a remote cause behind the immediate one.
senior
  • Aging bodies bring a daily catalog of immediate causes — this joint, that diagnosis, this lost capacity. Each morning, name one bodily reverse and consciously refuse to make it the whole story. The remote cause is Kṛṣṇa; the body's decline is the symptom your karma is working out under His protection.
  • When you watch children and grandchildren make choices you would not have made, the seven disturbances arise — śoka, bhaya, even mada (the pride of 'I knew better'). Mentor without trying to be the immediate cause of their correction. Point them — gently, once — to the remote cause, and then release.
  • Begin the deliberate loosening of attachments to possessions, roles, and reputations by asking of each: am I clinging to this as if it were a cause of my happiness? Each renunciation is a small correction of pṛthag-dṛṣṭi before the final correction at the moment of death.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using 'Kṛṣṇa is the cause of all causes' as fatalism — refusing to act responsibly on the immediate cause (the symptom) when the verse only warns against being bewildered by it, not against treating it.
  • Mistaking emotional flatness for the devotee's equipoise — suppressing śoka, harṣa, bhaya in public while inwardly still gripped by pṛthag-dṛśi, rather than actually re-anchoring vision in Kṛṣṇa.
  • Weaponizing this verse against others — diagnosing friends and family as 'pṛthag-dṛśaḥ driven by lobha and moha' instead of using it as a mirror for one's own divided vision.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a recent reverse in your life — illness, loss, betrayal, financial setback. Did you instinctively reach for an immediate cause to fight (a person, a circumstance, your own failure), or did you see Kṛṣṇa as the sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam behind it? Be honest about the gap between the doctrine and your gut response.
  • The verse names seven disturbances — śoka, harṣa, bhaya, dveṣa, lobha, moha, mada. Pick the one you most often mistake for a 'normal human reaction' and defend or surrender it to the group. Where is the line between healthy emotion and pṛthag-dṛṣṭi?
  • Tat te 'nukampāṁ susamīkṣamāṇaḥ says distress is the Lord's mercy minimizing past karma. Does this risk becoming a spiritual bypass that silences real grievances — abuse, injustice, exploitation — or is it the only frame that keeps a devotee steady? Argue both sides.
Role Play

Two students are paired. Student A has just received devastating news — a parent's terminal diagnosis, a job loss, a betrayal by a close friend (teacher assigns one). Student A is in the grip of śoka and bhaya and is scrambling to fix the immediate cause: switch hospitals, blame the boss, confront the friend. Student B is a Gauḍīya senior who must neither dismiss A's pain with platitudes ('it's all māyā') nor collude with the pṛthag-dṛṣṭi by helping A strategize against the symptom. B must lead A — through honest acknowledgment — to see the sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam behind the immediate cause, while still honoring A's duty to act sensibly. After 7 minutes, swap roles with a new scenario. Debrief: where did B slip into either bypass or worldly counseling?

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like a recipe for passivity and victim-blaming. If every misfortune is 'my own past karma being mercifully reduced,' then we never confront real injustice — we just tolerate it and call it sādhana.
    The verse does not forbid action; it forbids being bewildered by immediate causes. Arjuna was told to fight, not to retreat into 'it's all karma.' A devotee acts — treats the disease, leaves the abusive situation, fixes the broken system — but acts without lamentation, fear, envy, or madness, because he sees Kṛṣṇa as the remote cause. The pṛthag-dṛṣṭi person is not more active; he is more frantic. Equipoise is not passivity. Prabhupāda's physician analogy is precise: the doctor still treats symptoms, but he is not deceived by them. Tolerance of one's own karma is not tolerance of others' wrongdoing.
  • The 'vision of differentiation' framing sounds suspiciously like Advaita — see past the differences to the One. Why is a Gauḍīya text using language that sounds impersonalist?
    Madhvācārya, quoted in the purport, makes the distinction explicit: pṛthag-dṛṣṭi here means seeing things as disconnected from Bhagavān, not seeing things as differentiated entities. The Gauḍīya view affirms eternal variety — souls, the Lord, and the world are eternally distinct in acintya-bhedābheda. What the verse condemns is the failure to see Kṛṣṇa as the underlying cause connecting all those distinct things. The mistake is not perceiving difference; it is perceiving difference unconnected to Him. A devotee sees a child and an elder as distinct persons and sees Kṛṣṇa as the cause behind both. That is precisely the opposite of impersonalist erasure.
Analogy

A man flailing at one mosquito after another forgets that the lamp is what draws them. Vasudeva tells Kaṁsa: while you battle every immediate cause — this child, that prophecy, those enemies — you fail to see the supreme cause of all, who is Bhagavān. The wise devotee turns the lamp off the wrong way — he offers it to Kṛṣṇa.

Stories
Brahmā's bewilderment in Vraja

When Lord Brahmā stole the calves and cowherd boys of Vraja to test Kṛṣṇa (SB 10.13), Kṛṣṇa Himself expanded into every calf, every boy, every staff, every flute. For a full year, no one in Vraja noticed; Brahmā, returning, was stunned. He saw before him a forest of Viṣṇu-forms and understood: he had been fighting the immediate when the supreme was everywhere.

Brahmā's prayer of repentance (SB 10.14) is the cure for Kaṁsa's disease, sketched in this verse. Brahmā says: I forgot You as the supreme cause, took myself as a doer, and acted from pṛthak-dṛk. Now I see. Vasudeva is offering Kaṁsa the same medicine; Kaṁsa, lacking Brahmā's saṅga and humility, cannot drink it.
