Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.4.32

The demigods always fear the sound of your bowstring. They are constantly in anxiety, afraid of fighting. Therefore, what can they do by their endeavors to harm you?
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
The demigods always fear the sound of your bowstring. They are constantly in anxiety, afraid of fighting. Therefore, what can they do by their endeavors to harm you?
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

“But the devatās will be eager to fight.” What can they do? When you twang your bow before fighting, or just for sport, they become frightened.

Concepts
Jiva
Personal Guidance
Material power frightens even the demigods — but for the surrendered devotee, Kṛṣṇa's protection makes such threats irrelevant.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today where you're posturing in fear of someone's 'bowstring' — a boss, a critic, a rival — and consciously reposition yourself behind Kṛṣṇa's shelter instead.
  • Refuse to mistake material strength (power, wealth, intimidation) as the real source of safety; reaffirm in japa that śaraṇāgati to Kṛṣṇa is the only true refuge.
  • Speak one honest sentence to Kṛṣṇa today admitting an anxiety you've been carrying, and hand it over with the words 'I cannot fight this — I take shelter of You.'
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I, like these demigods, expending exhausting udyama against a force I have already, in my heart, accepted as superior — and what would it cost me to simply lay down the bow?
  • Whose 'jyā-ghoṣa' — whose mere presence or reputation — keeps my mind in nityam udvigna-manasaḥ, and what does my fear of that person reveal about where I have placed my refuge instead of in Kṛṣṇa?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the people in your friend group or DMs who only ever tell you what you want to hear — the Kaṁsa-style flatterers. Today, name them honestly to yourself, and seek out one friend who will tell you the uncomfortable truth instead.
  • Before posting or scrolling today, ask: am I performing strength online to mask udvigna-manasaḥ — an anxious mind? Replace fifteen minutes of feed-time with a chapter of the Gītā or one round of japa, and watch what shifts.
  • In exam season or competition, you will be tempted to think effort alone (udyama) guarantees the result. Do the work fully — and then formally hand the outcome to Kṛṣṇa before sleep. Refuse the Kaṁsa-illusion that your hustle alone secures your future.
householder
  • Audit the 'security structures' you have built around your family — savings, insurance, status, network. Keep them, but stop pretending they are ultimate. Each morning, before opening email, formally place wife, husband, children, and finances under Kṛṣṇa's shelter.
  • Watch for the moment in a work meeting or family argument when you reach for intimidation — raised voice, status, money — to make others 'fear your bowstring.' Catch it, soften, and choose the harder path of sva-dharma performed without coercion.
  • Teach your children, by example, that udyama is required but never sufficient. When a deal closes or a child does well on a test, say aloud at the dinner table: 'Kṛṣṇa arranged this; we only worked.' Let them grow up knowing whose protection actually holds.
senior
  • At this stage, the body itself is the bowstring whose twang you once thought would frighten time away. It no longer does. Sit with that honestly today, and let vairāgya soften — not harden — your heart toward those still caught in the illusion.
  • Identify one younger person — a child, grandchild, or junior — who treats you as a Kaṁsa-figure of authority, and quietly redirect their respect upward toward Kṛṣṇa and the paramparā. Your legacy is not your fearsomeness; it is the shelter you point them toward.
  • Each evening, loosen one attachment — a possession, a grudge, a plan for how things 'should' go after you. Practice ishvara-praṇidhāna concretely: hand it over by name, and notice the santoṣa that arrives where the anxiety used to live.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as a celebration of intimidation and concluding that spiritual strength means making others afraid of you — missing that the demigods' fear is precisely the diseased condition the Gītā wants to cure, not the goal.
  • Using 'what can endeavor accomplish against the Lord's arrangement?' as a pretext for fatalistic inaction, abandoning sva-dharma and legitimate udyama under the guise of vairāgya — when the verse critiques misdirected effort, not effort itself.
  • Identifying with Kaṁsa's confidence rather than examining one's own demigod-like anxiety — flattering oneself as the unshakeable one while quietly living in udvigna-manasaḥ over health, money, reputation, and outcomes that Kṛṣṇa alone governs.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kaṁsa's ministers tell him the demigods tremble at the twang of his bow — yet within a few chapters he is dead. Share with your partner: where in your own life are you mistaking the fear others show you for actual security? What is the difference between being feared and being safe?
  • The verse assumes that udyama (endeavor) is futile against a sufficiently powerful enemy. A modern reader will say: effort always matters, you just need the right strategy. A Gauḍīya reading says: effort divorced from Kṛṣṇa's sanction is finally powerless. Argue both sides honestly in your group — where does each view break down?
  • The demigods here are described as samara-bhīravaḥ — afraid of fighting — and udvigna-manasaḥ — minds disturbed. Yet they are the demigods, not the demons. Discuss: when have you been on the 'right side' of a conflict and still found yourself anxious and timid? What does that anxiety tell you about where your shelter actually is?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A plays a flatterer — a colleague, a junior employee, or a sibling — who has come to praise Student B extravagantly: 'Everyone is afraid of you. Nobody can touch you. You've already won.' Student B must receive this praise and then respond — first as Kaṁsa would (drinking it in, becoming more bound by it), and then a second time as a sādhaka who recognizes the flattery as a trap. Afterward, debrief: what did the flatterer's words do to your body and mind in each round? Where in real life are you a Kaṁsa, surrounded by ministers reinforcing your false security?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole verse is just demon-propaganda — Kaṁsa's ministers boasting. Why are we extracting spiritual lessons from the speech of the wicked?
    Precisely because Vyāsadeva preserves their speech, we are meant to study it. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is not naive about evil; it shows us, in their own words, exactly how the asuric mentality reasons — overestimating its strength, mistaking the temporary terror of others for permanence, dismissing the very devas it should fear. We read these verses the way a physician studies a disease: to recognize the symptoms in ourselves. When you next hear yourself or others say 'they cannot touch us,' you will know whose script you are reading from.
  • The demigods being afraid sounds like cosmic cowardice. Aren't they supposed to be the good guys? Why should I take refuge in beings who themselves run from a fight?
    This is exactly why the personalist Gauḍīya line insists that the demigods are not the supreme shelter. They are empowered administrators within Kṛṣṇa's universe, subject themselves to fear and disturbance when separated from His direct protection. Their anxiety in this verse is not a scandal — it is a teaching. Even Indra and Brahmā take shelter of Kṛṣṇa when truly pressed. If they need Him, what does that say about the wisdom of trying to make our own little kingdom of demigod-like security? Surrender is to Kṛṣṇa personally, not to any intermediary, however exalted.
