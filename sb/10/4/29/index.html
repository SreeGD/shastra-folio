Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.4.29

तस्यां रात्र्यां व्यतीतायां कंस आहूय मन्त्रिण: । तेभ्य आचष्ट तत् सर्वं यदुक्तं योगनिद्रया ॥ २९ ॥

Important Words
  • rātryām-vyatītāyām — the night having passed
    Locative absolute — 'on the passing of that night'. The Bhāgavatam marks the exact hinge: the apology was last night, the plotting is this morning. Repentance unguarded by sādhu-saṅga rarely survives a single sunrise.
  • mantriṇaḥ — ministers
    From √mantr — those who counsel. The asura's mantrins (counselors) are mentioned across the chapter as the agents of his return to evil. The Gauḍīya reading: counsel from the wrong saṅga can dissolve the best impulse overnight.
  • ācaṣṭa — he related / told
    Imperfect of ā-√cakṣ — 'spoke in detail'. Kaṁsa tells them everything — Yoga-māyā's words, his own apology, the release of his sister. The narrative remains, but the bhāva has already evaporated.
  • yat uktam — what was said
    Relative clause: 'what was spoken'. The content of yoga-nidrā's speech is now retold to the ministers — and they will hear it through asuric ears, drawing exactly the wrong conclusion.
  • yoga-nidrayā — by Yoga-nidrā (mystic sleep)
    Instrumental — 'by the mystic-sleep one'. Another name for Yoga-māyā: she who lies in mystic sleep upon the milk-ocean, and who covers the eyes of the conditioned. The Gauḍīya distinction holds: she covers asuras and reveals herself to devotees.
After that night passed, Kaṁsa summoned his ministers and informed them of all that had been spoken by Yoga-māyā [who had revealed that He who was to slay Kaṁsa had already been born somewhere else].
Purport

The Vedic scripture Caṇḍī describes māyā, the energy of the Supreme Lord, as nidrā: durgā devī sarva-bhūteṣu nidrā-rūpeṇa samāsthitaḥ . The energy of Yoga-māyā and Mahā-māyā keeps the living entities sleeping in this material world in the great darkness of ignorance. Yoga-māyā, the goddess Durgā, kept Kaṁsa in darkness about Kṛṣṇa’s birth and misled him to believe that his enemy Kṛṣṇa had been born elsewhere. Kṛṣṇa was born the son of Devakī, but according to the Lord’s original plan, as prophesied to Brahmā, He went to Vṛndāvana to give pleasure to mother Yaśodā and Nanda Mahārāja and other intimate friends and devotees for eleven years. Then He would return to kill Kaṁsa. Because Kaṁsa did not know this, he believed Yoga-māyā’s statement that Kṛṣṇa was born elsewhere, not of Devakī.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
After that night passed, Kaṁsa summoned his ministers and informed them of all that had been spoken by Yogamāyā [who had revealed that He who was to slay Kaṁsa had already been born somewhere else].
Jīva Gosvāmī Gauḍīya
Commentary

This verse shows that knowledge will not remain steady in the hearts of those who hate the Lord. Kaṁsa acts opposite to the knowledge that he spoke. The description lasts till the end of the chapter. His ministers included Pralamba, Keśī and Cānūra.

Personal Guidance
Yoga-māyā veils even the mighty — Kaṁsa plots while Kṛṣṇa grows in Vṛndāvana; without surrender, our cleverest plans serve only our own bewilderment.
Practical Applications
  • Notice one situation today where you are confidently strategizing in the dark, and pause to ask whether Kṛṣṇa's plan might already be unfolding outside your view.
  • Refuse to act from Kaṁsa-bhāva — fear-driven scheming against imagined enemies — and instead chant Kṛṣṇa's name once before any consequential decision today.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa directly in your morning sādhana and admit one area where you have been trusting your own intelligence over His arrangement.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I, like Kaṁsa, taking counsel from my 'ministers' — the inner advisors of fear, calculation, and self-preservation — when Yoga-māyā has already redirected the very thing I am chasing?
  • What 'birth elsewhere' have I been told by the world that keeps me sleeping in nidrā — convinced the threat (or the treasure) is somewhere other than where Kṛṣṇa has actually placed it?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice one belief you hold about yourself right now — 'I'm not smart enough for this course,' 'that friend group will never accept me,' 'I have to post to stay relevant' — and ask: who told me this, and what if Yoga-māyā is keeping me asleep on it the way she kept Kaṁsa asleep? Write the belief down and one śāstra-grounded counter-statement next to it.
  • Before the next exam or major submission, do one round of japa or read one verse with full attention — not as a lucky charm, but to puncture the māyā-story that your worth equals your result. Then sit the exam as nishkāma karma: full effort, fruit offered.
  • Pick one social media account whose feed leaves you feeling subtly worse about your life and unfollow it today. That algorithm is a literal nidrā-machine — it is paid to keep you sleeping in a particular version of reality. Replace the time with ten minutes of svādhyāya.
householder
  • In the next family or work decision you face, name out loud (to your spouse, to yourself in writing) the 'Kaṁsa-assumption' you are operating on — the unexamined story about money, the children's future, your career trajectory — and test it against śāstra and a senior devotee's counsel before acting.
  • Kṛṣṇa delayed killing Kaṁsa for eleven years to give pleasure to His Vraja devotees first. Apply this to your home: the 'big problem' you are urgently trying to solve (the promotion, the move, the renovation) may be Kṛṣṇa's chosen delay so that bhakti and family relationships ripen first. This week, postpone one 'urgent' fix and instead spend that energy on ārati, kīrtana, or reading with your children.
  • Audit one recurring household expense or commitment that is built on a fear-story ('we must, or else…'). Sit with your spouse and ask whether it is actually serving the family's real welfare (the souls in your care) or only its material comfort. Cut or restructure one thing accordingly.
senior
  • Kaṁsa surrounded himself with ministers who confirmed his version of reality. In this stage of life, identify one area — health, money, the children's choices, your own legacy — where you have been quietly gathering only confirming voices. Seek out one person, ideally a senior Vaiṣṇava, who will tell you what you have not wanted to hear.
  • Make a written list of three attachments you are still holding as if they were yourself — a property, a role, a grievance, a self-image. Next to each, write the verse number of one BG or SB śloka that addresses it. Read those verses aloud each morning for the next month as a deliberate loosening.
  • Mentor one younger devotee or family member specifically on the difference between real compassion (serving the soul) and material compassion (smoothing the body's path). Share a concrete instance where, earlier in your life, you mistook one for the other — and what it cost. Your honesty here is itself a gift Kaṁsa never received.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as merely Kaṁsa's misfortune and missing that the same Yoga-māyā keeps us, too, in nidrā-rūpa — assuming we see clearly while the demon was the only one deluded.
  • Treating Yoga-māyā as an impersonal cosmic force or 'the universe's mystery' instead of recognizing her as Kṛṣṇa's personal energy (Durgā Devī) acting precisely under His direction.
  • Drawing the lesson 'plans get foiled, stay humble' — a generic moral — rather than seeing the specific teaching: the Lord's original plan (prophesied to Brahmā, eleven years in Vṛndāvana) is being protected, and our task is to align with that plan, not merely to be wary of being outwitted.
Discussion Prompts
  • Kaṁsa receives accurate information from Yoga-māyā but draws the wrong conclusion because his ahaṅkāra distorts what he hears. Share with your partner: when has someone given you correct guidance recently — a teacher, a parent, a śāstra — that you mis-heard because of what you wanted to be true? Push back if your partner only describes the past comfortably; ask where they are still doing it now.
  • The purport says māyā keeps living entities 'sleeping in the great darkness of ignorance.' Some in the group will say this sounds harsh — that most people are doing their best, not 'asleep.' Others will say it is the plain truth and softening it is itself māyā's work. Take the side you do NOT instinctively hold and argue it for five minutes.
  • Kṛṣṇa's plan was to delay killing Kaṁsa for eleven years so He could give pleasure to Yaśodā, Nanda, and the Vraja-vāsīs first. What does this say about Kṛṣṇa's priorities — relationship with devotees over the swift execution of justice? Where in your own life are you demanding that Kṛṣṇa 'fix the Kaṁsa' before you will trust the timing?
Role Play

Two students. Student A plays a senior Kaṁsa-minister who has just been told by the king that 'the enemy was born elsewhere — relax security at the prison, redirect resources outward.' Student A privately suspects this is wrong but the king is certain and well-informed. Student B plays a junior minister who is convinced the king is being deceived by a higher power and must be told. Role-play the conversation: B tries to wake A up; A defends the comfortable, official version. After five minutes, swap. Debrief: where in your own life are you the senior minister, smoothing over a gut-suspicion that the 'official story' you have been told about your situation is exactly what māyā wants you to believe?

Objections & Responses
  • If Yoga-māyā deliberately deceived Kaṁsa, isn't Kṛṣṇa's energy responsible for his evil acts that followed? This sounds like God setting people up to fail.
    Kaṁsa was not made evil by Yoga-māyā — he was already a demon who had imprisoned his sister and murdered six infants. Yoga-māyā did not plant cruelty in him; she simply withheld a particular piece of information so that Kṛṣṇa's Vṛndāvana-līlā could unfold as the Lord had promised Brahmā. Māyā never forces a soul against its nature; she covers and uncovers in proportion to what the soul has chosen. The Gauḍīya teaching is precise here: Kṛṣṇa's energies serve His pleasure and His devotees' pleasure, and they leave the envious exactly as envious as they have made themselves.
  • This is a story about a tyrant 5,000 years ago being fooled by a goddess. What does it have to do with my actual life as a modern person?
    Read the purport again — durgā devī sarva-bhūteṣu nidrā-rūpeṇa samāsthitaḥ. The same Yoga-māyā that kept Kaṁsa convinced his enemy was 'somewhere else' is keeping you convinced that your real problem is somewhere else: the next job, the next relationship, the next political outcome. Kaṁsa is not a historical curiosity; Kaṁsa is the template for every conditioned soul who is certain the threat to his happiness is external. The verse is a mirror. The question is not whether you believe the story — the question is whether you can see yourself as the king in his council chamber, confidently briefing his ministers on a situation he has completely misread.
Analogy

The wind has stopped for one hour and the sailor mistakes the calm for journey's end — yet the storm is reorganizing on the other side of the headland. Kaṁsa's call to his ministers, on the morning after his apology, is exactly this re-organization. Yoga-māyā has covered Kṛṣṇa's location; the demon, deceived by his own deceiver, drifts back toward his old course.

Stories
Brahmā lifted into yoga-māyā's cover

When Lord Brahmā, after stealing the cowherd boys, returned to look for Kṛṣṇa, he could not find Him — yoga-māyā covered his vision until Kṛṣṇa Himself chose to reveal the līlā (SB 10.13). Even the four-headed creator can be covered by Bhagavān's potency.

Kaṁsa here is covered in the same way. Yoga-māyā has already conveyed her message and departed; the demon, unable to act on it, summons his ministers as though nothing of his apology had happened. The verse names her by a beautiful epithet — yoga-nidrā, the mystic sleep. Under her sleep, the asura plans against the very Lord he had just begged forgiveness from.
