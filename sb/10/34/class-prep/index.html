Chapter 34 · Class prep

BG 34.1

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: One day the cowherd men, eager to take a trip to worship Lord Śiva, traveled by bullock carts to the Ambikā forest.

Essence. The cowherd men travel to Ambikā forest to worship Lord Śiva on Śiva-rātri. Even Kṛṣṇa's intimate devotees honor the demigods properly — devotion to the Lord doesn't make us dismissive of legitimate worship.

BG 34.2

O King, after arriving there, they bathed in the Sarasvatī and then devotedly worshiped with various paraphernalia the powerful Lord Paśupati and his consort, goddess Ambikā.

Essence. After bathing in the Sarasvatī, the cowherds devotedly worship Lord Paśupati and goddess Ambikā with various offerings. Real worship is embodied and prepared — bathing, paraphernalia, attention — not casual.

BG 34.3

The cowherd men gave the brāhmaṇas gifts of cows, gold, clothing and cooked grains mixed with honey. Then the cowherds prayed, “May the lord be pleased with us.”

Essence. The cowherds give brāhmaṇas cows, gold, clothing and honeyed grains, then pray that the lord be pleased. Generosity to holy persons is a natural part of worshipful living, not an optional add-on.

BG 34.4

Nanda, Sunanda and the other greatly fortunate cowherds spent that night on the bank of the Sarasvatī, strictly observing their vows. They fasted, taking only water.

Essence. Nanda, Sunanda and the cowherds spend the night on the Sarasvatī's bank fasting, taking only water, strictly observing vows. Devotional vows are kept best in community — collective austerity holds when solitary resolve weakens.

BG 34.5

During the night a huge and extremely hungry snake appeared in that thicket. Slithering on his belly up to the sleeping Nanda Mahārāja, the snake began swallowing him.

Essence. A huge, ravenous serpent slithers up to the sleeping Nanda Mahārāja and begins to swallow him. Even faithfully kept vows do not exempt devotees from sudden, terrifying tests — danger comes precisely on holy nights.

BG 34.6

In the clutches of the snake, Nanda Mahārāja cried out, “Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa, my dear boy! This huge serpent is swallowing me! Please save me, who am surrendered to You!”

Essence. Nanda cries out, 'Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa, my dear boy! Save me, I am surrendered to You!' The cry of surrender is short, particular, and uses the name — emergency prayer is rarely eloquent and never needs to be.

BG 34.7

When the cowherd men heard the cries of Nanda, they immediately rose up and saw that he was being swallowed. Distraught, they beat the serpent with blazing torches.

Essence. The cowherds beat the serpent with torches, but it doesn't release Nanda — until Kṛṣṇa arrives and touches it with His foot. Friends' best efforts may not be enough; some snakes only let go at the Lord's touch.

BG 34.8

But even though the firebrands were burning him, the serpent would not release Nanda Mahārāja. Then the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa, master of His devotees, came to the spot and touched the snake with His foot.

Essence. By the touch of Kṛṣṇa's foot, all the snake's sinful reactions are destroyed, and he gives up his serpent body to appear as a worshipable Vidyādhara. The Lord's touch doesn't merely relieve — it transforms creature into demigod.

BG 34.9

The snake had all his sinful reactions destroyed by the touch of the Supreme Lord’s divine foot, and thus he gave up his serpent body and appeared in the form of a worshipable Vidyādhara.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa questions the brilliantly effulgent personality, head bowed, body bedecked with golden necklaces. The Lord draws out the testimony of His delivered devotees so others can hear and learn.

BG 34.10

The Supreme Lord Hṛṣīkeśa then questioned this personality, who was standing before Him with his head bowed, his brilliantly effulgent body bedecked with golden necklaces.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa asks: who are you, and who forced you to assume this terrible serpent body? The question implies that ugliness is acquired, not original — every snake has a backstory of who he really is.

BG 34.11

[Lord Kṛṣṇa said:] My dear sir, you appear so wonderful, glowing with such great beauty. Who are you? And who forced you to assume this terrible body of a snake?

Essence. Kṛṣṇa asks the radiant figure: who are you, and who forced you to assume that terrible serpent body? The Lord's first question to a delivered soul is identity — He invites us to remember who we really are beneath our forms.

BG 34.12-13

The serpent replied: I am the well-known Vidyādhara named Sudarśana. I was very opulent and beautiful, and I used to wander freely in all directions in my airplane. Once I saw some homely sages of the lineage of Aṅgirā Muni. Proud of my beauty, I ridiculed them, and because of my sin they made me assume this lowly form.

Essence. Sudarśana confesses he was a proud, opulent Vidyādhara who mocked some homely sages of Aṅgirā's lineage and was cursed into snake form. Pride that ridicules outwardly humble holy persons re-shapes us into the lowness we despised.

BG 34.14

It was actually for my benefit that those merciful sages cursed me, since now I have been touched by the foot of the supreme spiritual master of all the worlds and have thus been relieved of all inauspiciousness.

Essence. Sudarśana sees the sages' curse as actually merciful — it brought him to the foot of the supreme spiritual master and removed all inauspiciousness. Apparent punishments are often the road by which the Lord reaches us.

BG 34.15

My Lord, You destroy all fear for those who, fearing this material world, take shelter of You. By the touch of Your feet I am now freed from the curse of the sages. O destroyer of distress, please let me return to my planet.

Essence. Sudarśana addresses the Lord as the destroyer of fear for those who take shelter, and asks permission to return to his planet to resume duty. Deliverance ends not in passivity but in returning to one's post, chastened and grateful.

BG 34.16

O master of mystic power, O great personality, O Lord of the devotees, I surrender to You. Please command me as You will, O supreme God, Lord of all lords of the universe.

Essence. Sudarśana surrenders as a servant: command me as You will, O Lord of all lords. Real liberation expresses itself as availability — 'tell me what to do' rather than 'leave me alone now'.

BG 34.17

O infallible one, I was immediately freed from the brāhmaṇas’ punishment simply by seeing You. Anyone who chants Your name purifies all who hear his chanting, as well as himself. How much more beneficial, then, is the touch of Your lotus feet?

Essence. Sudarśana says merely seeing Kṛṣṇa freed him from the brāhmaṇas' punishment; chanting His name purifies all who hear; how much more the touch of His feet. Each level of contact with Kṛṣṇa is exponentially purifying.

BG 34.18

Thus receiving the permission of Lord Kṛṣṇa, the demigod Sudarśana circumambulated Him, bowed down to offer Him homage and then returned to his heavenly planet. Nanda Mahārāja was thus delivered from peril.

Essence. Sudarśana circumambulates the Lord, bows, and returns to his heavenly planet; Nanda is delivered from peril. Grace passes through the saved person and back into circulation — others benefit when one of us is freed.

BG 34.19

The inhabitants of Vraja were astonished to see the mighty power of Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Dear King, they then completed their worship of Lord Śiva and returned to Vraja, along the way respectfully describing Kṛṣṇa’s powerful acts.

Essence. Astonished, Vraja's inhabitants finish their Śiva worship and return home telling Kṛṣṇa's powerful deeds along the way. Pilgrimage doesn't end at the shrine — the journey home is the time to share what the Lord has done.

BG 34.20

Once Lord Govinda and Lord Rāma, the performers of wonderful feats, were playing in the forest at night with the young girls of Vraja.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, performers of wonderful feats, play in the forest at night with the young girls of Vraja. The Lord's amazing deeds and intimate pastimes are part of the same life — the same hands that lift mountains hold the gopīs.

BG 34.21

Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma wore flower garlands and spotless garments, and Their limbs were beautifully decorated and anointed. The women sang Their glories in a charming way, bound to Them by affection.

Essence. Garlanded, in spotless garments, beautifully anointed, the brothers receive the women's affectionate songs of praise. The Lord arrives well-prepared, the devotee receives Him well-decorated — both sides of love put effort into the meeting.

BG 34.22

The two Lords praised the nightfall, signaled by the rising of the moon and the appearance of stars, a lotus-scented breeze and bees intoxicated by the fragrance of jasmine flowers.

Essence. The Lords praise the night — rising moon, stars, lotus-scented breeze, jasmine-intoxicated bees. The Lord blesses the present moment by praising it; devotees can learn to bless what is here rather than long for elsewhere.

BG 34.23

Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma sang, producing the entire range of musical sounds simultaneously. Their singing brought happiness to the ears and minds of all living beings.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma sing, producing the entire range of musical sounds simultaneously, bringing happiness to ears and minds of all living beings. The Lord's voice is universally consoling — every creature finds its own joy in it.

BG 34.24

The gopīs became stunned when they heard that song. Forgetting themselves, O King, they did not notice that their fine garments were becoming loose and their hair and garlands disheveled.

Essence. The gopīs are stunned by that song, forgetting themselves so completely that loosening garments and disheveled hair go unnoticed. Hearing the Lord's voice fully sometimes means losing track of self-presentation entirely.

BG 34.25

While Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Balarāma thus played according to Their own sweet will and sang to the point of apparent intoxication, a servant of Kuvera named Śaṅkhacūḍa came upon the scene.

Essence. While Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma play and sing intoxicatedly, Śaṅkhacūḍa — a servant of Kuvera — comes on the scene. Even in the most blissful divine pastime, an adversary may arrive; ecstasy doesn't suspend the need for protection.

BG 34.26

O King, even as the two Lords looked on, Śaṅkhacūḍa brazenly began driving the women off toward the north. The women, who had accepted Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma as their Lords, began to cry out to Them.

Essence. Even before the Lords' eyes, Śaṅkhacūḍa brazenly herds the gopīs north; they cry out to Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma. Demons act even within sight of the Lord — but devotees who cry out are never left in their grasp for long.

BG 34.27

Hearing Their devotees crying out “Kṛṣṇa! Rāma!” and seeing that they were just like cows being stolen by a thief, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma began to run after the demon.

Essence. Hearing the cries 'Kṛṣṇa! Rāma!' and seeing the gopīs taken like cows by a thief, the Lords run after the demon. The cry of the devotee mobilizes the Lord — His mercy is not abstract distance but pursuit.

BG 34.28

The Lords called out in reply, “Do not fear!” Then They picked up logs of the śala tree and quickly pursued that lowest of Guhyakas, who swiftly ran away.

Essence. 'Do not fear!' the Lords call out, picking up śala-tree logs to chase the swiftly fleeing demon. Reassurance and decisive action arrive together; the Lord both calms us and goes to deal with the threat.

BG 34.29

When Śaṅkhacūḍa saw the two of Them coming toward him like the personified forces of Time and Death, he was filled with anxiety. Confused, he abandoned the women and fled for his life.

Essence. Seeing the brothers like personified Time and Death, Śaṅkhacūḍa is filled with anxiety, abandons the women, and flees for his life. When the Lord enters the chase, the demon's appetite quickly becomes self-preservation.

BG 34.30

Lord Govinda chased the demon wherever he ran, eager to take his crest jewel. Meanwhile Lord Balarāma stayed with the women to protect them.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa chases Śaṅkhacūḍa to get his crest jewel, while Balarāma stays with the women to protect them. Different aspects of the Lord serve different needs at once — pursuit and protection are both His.

BG 34.31

The mighty Lord overtook Śaṅkhacūḍa from a great distance as if from nearby, my dear King, and then with His fist the Lord removed the wicked demon’s head, together with his crest jewel.

Essence. From a distance Kṛṣṇa overtakes the demon and with His fist removes his head and crest jewel together. The Lord's reach is not constrained by space; what looks far from us is not far from Him.

BG 34.32

Having thus killed the demon Śaṅkhacūḍa and taken away his shining jewel, Lord Kṛṣṇa gave it to His elder brother with great satisfaction as the gopīs watched.

Essence. Having killed Śaṅkhacūḍa, Kṛṣṇa gives the shining jewel to elder brother Balarāma, preventing rivalry among the gopīs. The Lord avoids inflaming jealousy even when honoring family — He thinks about devotee harmony.
