BG 1.1

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, accompanied by Balarāma and surrounded by the Yadu dynasty, executed the killing of many demons. Then, further to remove the burden of the earth, the Lord arranged for the great Battle of Kurukṣetra, which suddenly erupted in violence between the Kurus and the Pāṇḍavas.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa removes the earth's burden by slaying demons and orchestrating Kurukṣetra — even those near Him fall if they forget He is supreme.

Practical applications
  • Name one 'demon' in your own life today — pride, lust, anger, contempt — and stand against it as service to Kṛṣṇa, not self-improvement.
  • Examine where familiarity with Kṛṣṇa, guru, or devotees has bred quiet contempt in you, and re-establish reverence in your next act of sādhana.
  • Read or hear today's verse aloud and consciously side with the Vedic injunctions over your own concocted whims in one concrete decision.
Discussion

Prabhupāda's purport draws a sharp line: those who obey revealed scripture are 'demigods,' those who defy it are 'asuras.' Where in your own working life — your office, your family, your peer group — does that line cut through people you respect or love, and how do you actually relate to them tomorrow morning without either flattering them or condemning them?

BG 1.2

Because the sons of Pāṇḍu were enraged by the numerous offenses of their enemies, such as duplicitous gambling, verbal insults, the seizing of Draupadī’s hair, and many other cruel transgressions, the Supreme Lord engaged those Pāṇḍavas as the immediate cause to execute His will. On the pretext of the Battle of Kurukṣetra, Lord Kṛṣṇa arranged for all the kings who were burdening the earth to assemble with their armies on opposite sides of the battlefield, and when the Lord killed them through the agency of war, the earth was relieved of its burden.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa engages His surrendered devotees as the nimitta to remove the world's burden — our outrage and effort, offered to Him, become His instrument.

Practical applications
  • Identify one injustice that genuinely angers you and act on it today as Kṛṣṇa's instrument, not as the doer.
  • Before any difficult task, audibly say 'kṛtvā nimittam' — I am only the pretext — and proceed with full effort.
  • When wronged, refuse private revenge; bring the grievance into your sādhana and let Kṛṣṇa arrange the outcome.
Discussion

The verse says Kṛṣṇa made the Pāṇḍavas nimitta — the immediate cause — for killing kings who were burdening the earth. Share a time you were certain you were 'doing what had to be done.' Looking back, were you the doer, or were you being used? How would you tell the difference in the moment, not afterward?

BG 1.3

The Supreme Personality of Godhead used the Yadu dynasty, which was protected by His own arms, to eliminate the kings who with their armies had been the burden of this earth. Then the unfathomable Lord thought to Himself, “Although some may say that the earth’s burden is now gone, in My opinion it is not yet gone, because there still remains the Yādava dynasty itself, whose strength is unbearable for the earth.”

Essence. Even the Lord's own associates cannot stand apart from His will — what looks impressive in this world remains a burden until it is surrendered to Kṛṣṇa.

Practical applications
  • Audit one possession, position, or relationship you secretly count as 'mine' and re-offer it to Kṛṣṇa before you use it today.
  • Notice where proximity to bhakti — a guru, a temple, a saṅga — has quietly bred entitlement, and humble yourself in that exact spot.
  • Refuse to read sudden losses as setbacks; take each as Kṛṣṇa lightening a burden you were too attached to drop yourself.
Discussion

The verse shows Kṛṣṇa judging His own dynasty more strictly than His enemies — the Yādavas had become 'unbearable for the earth.' In your own life, who is the 'inner Yādava' — the part of you that, because of close association with bhakti or sādhana, has quietly started acting whimsically as if the rules don't apply? Share one concrete example, not an abstract one.

BG 1.4

Lord Kṛṣṇa thought, “No outside force could ever bring about the defeat of this family, the Yadu dynasty, whose members have always been fully surrendered to Me and are unrestricted in their opulence. But if I inspire a quarrel within the dynasty, that quarrel will act just like a fire created from the friction of bamboo in a grove, and then I shall achieve My real purpose and return to My eternal abode.”

Essence. Those who take full shelter of Kṛṣṇa (mat-saṁśraya) cannot be defeated by any outside force — protection comes from surrender, not strength.

Practical applications
  • Today, name one threat you fear — financial, social, health — and explicitly place it under Kṛṣṇa's shelter rather than rehearsing defenses.
  • Notice where your sense of security rests on your own opulence or capability, and consciously transfer that reliance to mat-saṁśraya through japa or prayer.
  • Refuse to envy or imitate powerful families and personalities; remember that even the Yadus' invincibility flowed from surrender, not from their own greatness.
Discussion

Kṛṣṇa says no outside force could defeat the Yadus because they were mat-saṁśrayasya — fully surrendered to Him. Share with your partner: where in your own life do you experience this protection, and where do you secretly still rely on your own competence, family name, money, or network as the 'real' shield? Push each other past the polite answer.

BG 1.5

My dear King Parīkṣit, when the supreme almighty Lord, whose desire always comes to pass, had thus made up His mind, He withdrew His own family on the pretext of a curse spoken by an assembly of brāhmaṇas.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's will is always perfect: He withdrew His own dynasty to protect future souls from worshiping seminal birth over surrendered devotion.

Practical applications
  • Honor your spiritual teachers today by their bhakti and character, never by their family name, caste, or institutional pedigree.
  • Refuse the pride of any inherited identity — biological, cultural, or sectarian — and re-stake your worth in surrender to Kṛṣṇa alone.
  • When something cherished is taken from you, trust satya-saṅkalpa: name one recent loss and ask what Kṛṣṇa is protecting you, or others, from.
Discussion

Share a time when you assumed authority — spiritual, professional, or familial — was yours by birth, lineage, or association rather than by qualification. What was the cost when reality caught up? Where in your life are you still doing this?
