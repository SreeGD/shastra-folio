Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.1.1

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, accompanied by Balarāma and surrounded by the Yadu dynasty, executed the killing of many demons. Then, further to remove the burden of the earth, the Lord arranged for the great Battle of Kurukṣetra, which suddenly erupted in violence between the Kurus and the Pāṇḍavas.
Purport

The Eleventh Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam begins with a reference to the pastimes executed by Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa in the Tenth Canto. The beginning of the Tenth Canto describes that when the earth was overburdened by demoniac rulers, the personified earth, Bhūmi, approached Lord Brahmā with tears in her eyes, begging for relief, and Brahmā immediately went with the demigods to approach the Supreme Lord in His form of Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu. As the demigods waited respectfully on the shore of the Milk Ocean, the Supreme Lord announced through Brahmā that He would soon incarnate on earth and that the demigods should also descend to assist in His pastimes. Thus from the very beginning of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s appearance it was understood that He would descend to the earth to remove the demons.

As Śrīla Prabhupāda states in his commentary to Bhagavad-gītā (16.6) , those who agree to obey the injunctions of revealed scriptures are known as demigods, whereas those who defy the orders of Vedic scriptures are known as asuras, or demons. The Vedic literatures are presented within the universe for the guidance of the conditioned souls, who are trapped under the three modes of material nature and who are therefore rotating in a continuous cycle of birth and death. By strictly adhering to the Vedic injunctions, we can easily satisfy our material needs and at the same time make tangible progress on the path back home, back to Godhead. Thus we can achieve an eternal life of bliss and knowledge in the Lord’s own abode simply by obeying the Lord’s instructions as they are presented in Vedic literatures such as Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. The demons, however, minimize or even mock the absolute authority of the Supreme Lord and His teachings. Because these asuras envy the sovereign status of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, they minimize the importance of the Vedic scriptures, which emanate directly from the breathing of the Lord. The demons establish a society governed by their own concocted whims and inevitably create chaos and misery, especially for pious living entities who sincerely desire to follow the will of God.

Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa states in Bhagavad-gītā that when there is a predominance of such chaotic, irreligious societies on the earth, He personally descends to rectify the imbalance. Thus from the very beginning of His transcendental infancy, Kṛṣṇa systematically killed the powerful asuras, or demons, who were an intolerable burden for the earth. Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa was assisted by His brother, Balarāma, who is also the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Although God is one, He can expand Himself to enjoy in many forms at once. That is His omnipotence. And the first such expansion is Balarāma, or Baladeva. Balarāma killed many noteworthy demons, including Dhenukāsura, Dvivida and the envious Rukmī. Kṛṣṇa was also accompanied by the members of the Yadu dynasty, many of whom were demigods who had descended to assist the Lord.

Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, however, has revealed that although many demigods had taken birth in the Yadu dynasty to assist the Lord, some members of the Yadu dynasty were actually inimical toward Kṛṣṇa. Because of their mundane vision of the Lord, they considered themselves to be on the same level as Kṛṣṇa. Having taken birth in the family of the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself, they had inconceivable strength, and thus they misunderstood Kṛṣṇa’s supreme position. Having forgotten that Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, they would constitute a great burden, and consequently it was necessary for Kṛṣṇa to remove them from the earth. There is a popular saying that familiarity breeds contempt. To destroy the contemptuous members of His own dynasty, the Lord caused a quarrel among them. For this purpose, He arranged for Nārada and other sages to display anger against the Kārṣṇas, the members of His family. Although many Yadus who were devoted to Kṛṣṇa were apparently killed in this fratricidal war, Lord Kṛṣṇa actually returned them to their original positions as universal directors, or demigods. It is the Lord’s promise in Bhagavad-gītā that He will always protect those who are favorable to His service.

Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, in his commentary on this verse, has given a summary of the entire Eleventh Canto as follows. Chapter One describes the beginning of the mauṣala-līlā, or the prelude to the destruction of the Yadu dynasty. Chapters Two through Five describe the conversations between the nine Yogendras and King Nimi. Chapter Six describes the prayers of Brahmā, Śiva and other residents of heaven. Chapters Seven through Twenty-nine present the conversation between Kṛṣṇa and Uddhava that is known as the Uddhava-gītā. Chapter Thirty describes the withdrawal of the Yadu dynasty from the earth. The final chapter describes the disappearance of Lord Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Kṛṣṇa, accompanied by Balarāma and surrounded by the Yadu dynasty, killed many demons. Then, producing a quarrel, he quickly relieved the earth of its burden. I offer respects to Śrī Kṛṣṇa-caitanya. I take shelter of the Lord of Govardhana, the supporter of the earth which nourishes the cows, and to the Govardhana Mountain. Offering respects to guru and Kṛṣṇa, the ocean of mercy, I take shelter of Śukadeva, master of the world, eye of the universe. I offer myself and everything I possess to Kṛṣṇa who is the life of the gopīs and the controller, for service to his dear devotees. One chapter begins to describe Kṛṣṇa’s disappearance. Four chapters deal with the Navayogendras. One chapter describes the curse of the sages and the prayers of the devatās. Twenty-three chapters are dedicated to Kṛṣṇa’s teachings to Uddhava. One chapter describes the destruction of the Yadu dynasty, and one chapter describes Kṛṣṇa’s disappearance. Thus, in thirty-one chapters of the Eleventh Canto, the subject of liberation, previously mentioned, is described. In the First Chapter, thinking of how to destroy the Yadu dynasty the Lord arranged for brāhmaṇas to curse them and the iron ball became reeds. In the Tenth Canto Kṛṣṇa, the tenth subject āśraya, Svayam Bhagavān, who satisfies and astonishes his devotees with his variegated, sweet pastimes, was described. In the Eleventh Canto, liberation, which takes shelter of his lotus feet, is described, and to relate a little of the remaining pastimes, the previous pastimes are again mentioned in this verse. The augment on the verse avatārayat is absent as poetic license. Javiṣṭham means “most quickly.” The quarrel refers to the dissension between the Kurus and Pāṇḍavas.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa removes the earth's burden by slaying demons and orchestrating Kurukṣetra — even those near Him fall if they forget He is supreme.
Practical Applications
  • Name one 'demon' in your own life today — pride, lust, anger, contempt — and stand against it as service to Kṛṣṇa, not self-improvement.
  • Examine where familiarity with Kṛṣṇa, guru, or devotees has bred quiet contempt in you, and re-establish reverence in your next act of sādhana.
  • Read or hear today's verse aloud and consciously side with the Vedic injunctions over your own concocted whims in one concrete decision.
Reflection Questions
  • Kṛṣṇa removes the earth's burden by killing demons and even by arranging the destruction of inimical members of His own dynasty — where in my own life have I mistaken familiarity with sacred things (a guru, a deity, a scriptural community) for being on the same level as them, and how has that bred quiet contempt?
  • Bhūmi wept under the weight of demoniac rulers before Kṛṣṇa descended. What 'burden on the earth' am I personally adding through my defiance of Vedic injunction — in speech, work, or appetite — that I have been calling normal life?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • The purport warns that 'familiarity breeds contempt' — even within Kṛṣṇa's own family. This week, watch for the moment you roll your eyes at a parent's instruction, a professor's correction, or a temple senior's reminder about japa. Pause there. That eye-roll is the seed of the Yadu disease. Replace it with one sincere question instead.
  • Your generation lives inside a feed engineered by people who fit the purport's definition of asura — those who 'establish a society governed by their own concocted whims.' Pick the one app or account that most consistently leaves you feeling contemptuous of scripture, parents, or sādhana, and delete it before your next exam cycle. Not 'limit screen time' — delete.
  • When your friend group laughs at someone for being 'too religious,' 'too traditional,' or 'too into their culture,' notice that you are watching asuric speech in real time, and notice the pull to join in. Stay quiet that once. The next day, message that person privately and ask them one real question about their practice.
householder
  • The verse shows Kṛṣṇa using Balarāma, the Yadus, Nārada, and even an apparent fratricidal war as His instruments. In your own household this week, identify one situation — a teenager's defiance, a spouse's complaint, a financial setback — that you have been treating as obstacle, and re-read it as Kṛṣṇa's arrangement to remove some bhāra from your life. Then act accordingly: do not fight the arrangement, cooperate with it.
  • Raising children inside a culture Prabhupāda would call asuric requires deliberate counter-formation. Pick one Vedic injunction your family has quietly let slide — morning maṅgala-ārati, no meat in the house, scripture reading before screens — and reinstate it this month, not as rule but as the line that separates your home from the 'society governed by concocted whims.'
  • Money and career decisions are where householders most often slip into asuric reasoning — 'whatever works,' 'everyone does it.' Before your next significant work or financial decision, write down the choice and ask: would I be comfortable presenting this to my dīkṣā-guru, or to Prabhupāda's books, exactly as I am about to do it? If not, the decision is already made — change the action, not the rationalization.
senior
  • At 60+ you have watched a full generation of familiarity breed contempt — in your own children, in fellow devotees, perhaps in yourself toward the deity or the guru. Name, privately and honestly, the one relationship in your spiritual life where contempt has crept in, and spend one week reading aloud from Prabhupāda's books on that exact topic before your morning japa. Do not discuss it with anyone yet.
  • The purport says Kṛṣṇa returned the favorable Yadus to their 'original positions as universal directors.' Your task at this stage is to prepare to be withdrawn well — not to cling, but also not to disappear. Identify two younger devotees and commit to one hour a week, in person, transmitting something specific you learned the hard way — not generic advice, the particular mistake and its particular correction.
  • Loosen one material attachment this month in a visible, irreversible way — give away a possession you have been 'saving for someone,' transfer a financial responsibility, hand over a service you have controlled for years to a younger devotee even if they will do it imperfectly. The mauṣala-līlā teaches that even Kṛṣṇa's own dynasty had to be withdrawn. Practice the withdrawal while you can still do it with grace.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the killing of demons as license for material aggression against people one dislikes, rather than as Kṛṣṇa's sovereign act — and so importing a 'demon-slaying' mood into ordinary disagreements where one's own ahaṅkāra is the actual asura.
  • Concluding from the Yadus' fall that proximity to Kṛṣṇa is dangerous, and using that as an excuse to keep a 'safe' distance from serious sādhana, guru and Vaiṣṇava association — when the verse's warning is against contempt born of familiarity, not against intimacy itself.
  • Treating the Kurukṣetra war as evidence that Kṛṣṇa is partial, violent, or merely one regional deity managing political affairs, instead of recognizing Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead arranging even apparent destruction as protection for those favorable to His service.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda's purport draws a sharp line: those who obey revealed scripture are 'demigods,' those who defy it are 'asuras.' Where in your own working life — your office, your family, your peer group — does that line cut through people you respect or love, and how do you actually relate to them tomorrow morning without either flattering them or condemning them?
  • Viśvanātha Cakravartī tells us Kṛṣṇa Himself engineered the quarrel that destroyed His own dynasty, because familiarity had bred contempt. Share with your partner one relationship — guru, deity worship, a senior devotee, even your own japa — where familiarity has quietly bred contempt in you. What would 'Kṛṣṇa engineering a quarrel' inside that relationship look like, and would you welcome it?
  • The verse says Kṛṣṇa removed the earth's burden by arranging Kurukṣetra — mass violence as the Lord's compassion. Pair up and argue opposite sides: (a) this proves real compassion sometimes looks nothing like material kindness, versus (b) this is exactly the kind of passage a thoughtful outsider points to when they call our tradition violent. Don't resolve it cheaply — stay with the disagreement for the full ten minutes.
Role Play

You are leading a Bhāgavatam class of twelve regulars. Halfway through, a newcomer — a hospital social worker, clearly intelligent, visibly disturbed — interrupts: 'So Kṛṣṇa orchestrated a war that killed millions, including His own family, and you're calling this scripture? I came here for peace.' One student is the teacher and must respond from the verse and purport — not by softening Kṛṣṇa's act, but by distinguishing material compassion (preventing bodily pain) from real compassion (relieving the bhāra, the burden, of adharma on the soul of the earth). A second student plays the social worker, pushing back hard for at least three exchanges. A third student plays a long-time devotee who jumps in 'to help' with a clumsy answer the teacher must gently correct. Run it twice with roles rotated. Debrief: where did the teacher slip into apologetics, and where did real conviction come through?

Objections & Responses
  • This whole framing — 'demons burdening the earth,' the Lord descending to slaughter them — is mythological language we can't take seriously in 2026. Isn't this just an ancient society dressing up its wars as divine sanction?
    Notice what the purport actually claims. Prabhupāda defines asura functionally, not racially or tribally: an asura is one who defies the injunctions of revealed scripture and 'establishes a society governed by their own concocted whims.' That is not a mythological category, that is a sociological one, and it describes much of modernity with uncomfortable precision. The 'burden on the earth' is not metaphor for population — Bhūmi is a person, and her suffering is the suffering of a world organized against its own dharma. Kṛṣṇa's response is not divine sanction for human violence; it is the opposite — it is the Supreme Person taking the karmic weight of that violence onto Himself so that the cosmic order can be restored. The Gauḍīya answer is not to demythologize the verse but to let the verse demythologize our confidence that our era is fundamentally different.
  • If Kṛṣṇa deliberately arranged Kurukṣetra and even engineered His own dynasty's destruction through the sages' curse, then human moral agency is a fiction. Why do sādhana at all?
    This is the same confusion Arjuna had at the start of the Gītā, and Kṛṣṇa's answer there is the answer here. The Lord's arranging does not abolish your choice; it sets the field on which your choice becomes meaningful. The Yadus who were favorable to Kṛṣṇa were returned to their 'original positions as universal directors' — the purport says this explicitly. The Yadus who had grown contemptuous through familiarity were removed. Same family, same Lord, same arrangement — different orientations of the heart, radically different outcomes. Sādhana is precisely how you cultivate the orientation that, when Kṛṣṇa's arrangement comes for you — and it will, in the form of loss, illness, a quarrel you did not seek — places you among the favorable, not the contemptuous. The arrangement is His. Which side of it you stand on is yours.
