Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.1.13-15

To that holy place, the young boys of the Yadu dynasty had brought Sāmba, son of Jāmbavatī, dressed in woman’s garb. Playfully approaching the great sages gathered there, the boys grabbed hold of the sages’ feet and impudently asked them with feigned humility, “O learned brāhmaṇas, this black-eyed pregnant woman has something to ask you. She is too embarrassed to inquire for herself. She is just about to give birth and is very desirous of having a son. Since all of you are great sages with infallible vision, please tell us whether her child will be a boy or a girl.”
Purport

Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī has made the following comment: “The impudent behavior of the young Yadus toward the sages headed by Nārada, who were all brāhmaṇas and devotees of the Lord, was a display of deviation from the path of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Similarly, although the prākṛta-sahajiyās think of themselves as intimate associates of Kṛṣṇa, the supremely merciful Lord’s determination is perfectly correct in working to finish such false devotees. Such impostors actually never accept real service to Kṛṣṇa. The yadu-kumāras’ deception is termed ‘seemingly humble,’ meaning that in fact they were anything but humble. Therefore, the ridiculing of Vaiṣṇavas by the Lord’s family resulted in a great offense against the devotees of the Lord.”

A similar incident occurred during the pastimes of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu when His own mother offended Śrī Advaita Ācārya. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu personally rectified this offense against a great Vaiṣṇava, and thus the Lord showed His magnanimity. Lord Kṛṣṇa’s pastime of destroying the Yadu dynasty is also a demonstration of His mercy to His devotees.

Believing the brāhmaṇas, Vaiṣṇavas and ṛṣis to be foolishly lacking knowledge in material affairs of sense gratification, the yadu-kumāras dressed Sāmba, the son of Jāmbavatī, as a woman and tried to mock the saintly assembly. Lord Kṛṣṇa wanted to teach that such an offense committed against great devotees by His associate Sāmba would be the cause of the Yadu dynasty’s destruction, all as part of His līlā.

In modern times such misbehavior has also manifested itself within the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava community. Unauthorized persons have initiated the process of deceitfully bestowing a woman’s dress on their followers. This process is to be counted as a variety of aparādha, or offense against Kṛṣṇa. Such an attempt to cheapen and ridicule devotional service to Kṛṣṇa is certainly caused by envy toward the real Vaiṣṇavas, who are faithfully engaged in devotional service according to the rules and regulations of the Vedic literature. Thus Rūpa Gosvāmī has said:

śruti-smṛti-purāṇādi- paṣcarātra-vidhiṁ vinā aikāntikī harer bhaktir utpātāyaiva kalpate

“If one wants to demonstrate his great devotion to the Supreme Lord but his process of devotional service violates the standard rules of revealed scriptures such as śruti, smṛti, Purāṇas and Nārada-paṣcarātra, then his alleged love of Godhead will simply disturb society by misleading people from the auspicious path of spiritual advancement.” ( Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.101) The taking of a woman’s clothing by a man in kṛṣṇa-līlā was intended to point out this fact. Such an act amounts to cheating and ridicule of the devotees of Kṛṣṇa. Sāmba is a personal associate of the Lord, but acting as a harbinger of the future misfortune to be created in Kali-yuga by bogus followers of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Sāmba displayed this didactic pastime to help the living entities be blessed on the correct path of devotional service.

The boys said to the sages, “O ṛṣis, O brāhmaṇas, O Nārada and other great personalities, can you tell us whether it will be a son or a daughter that will be born from this pregnant woman’s womb?” By addressing pure Vaiṣṇavas in this way, they anticipated the fraudulent sampradāyas of the modern age in their practice of sakhī-bheka, or dressing men as female associates of the gopīs. This unauthorized activity constitutes contempt and mockery of the pure devotees of the Lord.

Many false yogīs, imagining they are distributing first-class devotion on the liberated platform, attempt to award the status of “pure devotee” to candidates totally ignorant of the transcendental tastes of madhura-rati, or the Lord’s conjugal love in the spiritual world. Even though they know that the general populace is unfit to imitate the liberated associates of the Lord, they artificially decorate ordinary persons with the ornaments of spiritual perfection, such as tears, a melted heart, and the standing on end of the bodily hairs. Thus these bogus yogīs introduce a process that misleads the world. Because Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu understood that the great misfortune caused by such false yogīs, or kuyogīs, was impossible to forestall in the Kali-yuga, He infected them with insane desires for material objects of lust so that ordinary persons can easily identify such false yogīs as deviated from the path of pure devotional service.

The mockery of the brāhmaṇas and Vaiṣṇavas by the young boys of the Yadu dynasty who dressed Sāmba in woman’s garb, and the resultant destruction of the Yadu dynasty, conclusively demonstrate the uselessness of the sahajiyāsampradāyas.

Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī has confirmed that the lack of humility shown by the sons of the Yadu dynasty was an arrangement by the Lord Himself. In other words, the members of the Yadu dynasty are ultimately associates of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and to facilitate the instructive pastimes of the Lord they acted in apparently unethical ways.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas

To that holy place, the young boys of the Yadu dynasty had brought Sāmba, son of Jāmbavatī, dressed in woman's garb. Playfully approaching the great sages gathered there, the boys grabbed hold of the sages' feet and impudently asked them with feigned humility, "O learned brāhmaṇas, this black-eyed pregnant woman has something to ask you. Upasaṅgṛhya means “they touched the sages’ feet.” Kiṁ svit means “Will she bear a son or not?”

Personal Guidance
Mocking pure Vaiṣṇavas under feigned humility — even by the Lord's own associates — invites destruction; guard speech around devotees.
Practical Applications
  • Refuse every joke, meme, or imitation that cheapens a Vaiṣṇava, a brāhmaṇa, or the dress of devotional service — even among friends.
  • Before approaching a sādhu or senior devotee today, drop any clever question meant to test them and ask one sincere question about your own sādhana.
  • Examine where you wear 'feigned humility' (vinīta-vat) — flattering words masking pride — and replace one such exchange today with plain, honest speech.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my own devotional life do I, like the yadu-kumāras, approach senior Vaiṣṇavas with outward humility while inwardly testing or ridiculing them?
  • Which 'shortcut' to advanced bhakti am I quietly attracted to that bypasses the śruti-smṛti-purāṇādi standard Rūpa Gosvāmī warns against?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • In your friend group or online, notice the next time someone mocks a 'cringe' devotee, a strict teacher, or a religious peer who is actually trying to follow the rules. Refuse to laugh along, even silently — that quiet laugh is the yadu-kumāras' bow.
  • On social media you can curate a persona of advanced spirituality — quotes, aesthetic, melted-heart captions — without doing the underlying japa, study, or service. Audit your last week's posts and DMs: where are you performing bhakti symptoms (artificial 'tears, melted heart, hair standing on end') instead of building them through sādhana?
  • When an older devotee, parent, or teacher gives correction, watch for the avinītā vinīta-vat reflex — outward 'yes uncle, yes aunty' while inwardly rolling your eyes. Once today, instead of fake-humble compliance, ask a real follow-up question that shows you actually want to learn.
householder
  • In raising your children, do not let them grow up watching you speak respectfully to senior Vaiṣṇavas in person and criticize them at the dinner table. Decide as a couple this week: which devotees do we never allow to be ridiculed in our home, even jokingly?
  • Examine the spiritual processes you have adopted for the family — japa, deity worship, festival observance, association. Are they authorized by śruti, smṛti, Purāṇa, Pañcarātra and your guru's instructions, or have you quietly substituted shortcuts that feel more 'authentic' to your modern sensibility? Bring one such shortcut back into alignment this week.
  • When career or money decisions tempt you to cut a corner on a regulative principle — 'just this once,' 'everyone in our position does it' — recognize the yadu-kumāra logic: that the rules are for less sophisticated people. Hold the line precisely where it costs you something.
senior
  • You have decades of association with devotees. Make a written list of any Vaiṣṇava you have offended — in speech, in writing, in private criticism — and before this body is gone, seek out each one (or their family) to clear it. Vaiṣṇava-aparādha does not age out.
  • In mentoring younger devotees, refuse to dispense confidential or advanced 'rasa-talk' to those still building basic sādhana — this is precisely the kuyogī error the purport warns against. Teach them japa, scriptural reading, and the regulative principles first; let Kṛṣṇa award the higher tastes in His time.
  • As physical capacity loosens, the temptation grows to imagine that your decades of practice have earned you a private, internal status above the regulations followed by 'beginners.' Recommit publicly — in front of your family or congregation — to the same śruti-smṛti-pañcarātra standards a new devotee follows. Model that there is no graduation from the rules.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as a remote historical curiosity about the Yadu dynasty rather than a direct warning against vaiṣṇava-aparādha in one's own dealings today.
  • Assuming that closeness to Kṛṣṇa — by family, institution, or sampradāya affiliation — exempts one from the standard rules of sādhana, when in fact intimacy raises, not lowers, the cost of offense.
  • Mistaking imitation of the symptoms of advanced bhakti (tears, melted heart, conjugal moods) for the substance, and so drifting toward the prākṛta-sahajiyā error this purport explicitly condemns.
Discussion Prompts
  • The yadu-kumāras approached Nārada and the sages with 'feigned humility' — outwardly bowing and grabbing their feet while inwardly mocking them. Share a recent instance when you offered seemingly respectful behavior to a devotee, teacher, or elder while inwardly judging or testing them. What was the cost — to you, and to the relationship?
  • Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī calls sakhī-bheka — dressing men as female associates of the gopīs — an aparādha and a 'cheapening' of devotional service. Where in your own sādhana have you been tempted to artificially imitate the symptoms of advanced bhakti (tears, melted heart, intimate moods) rather than do the slower work prescribed by śāstra and guru? Disagree with your partner if you must — is this caution puritanical, or protective?
  • Lord Kṛṣṇa engineered the destruction of His own Yadu dynasty in response to this offense against Vaiṣṇavas. What does this tell you about how seriously Kṛṣṇa takes vaiṣṇava-aparādha — even from His own family members and associates? Where in your own life are you treating an offense against a devotee as a small thing because the offender is 'one of us'?
Role Play

Two students play yadu-kumāras: enthusiastic, charismatic, popular in the bhakti community. A third plays a newer devotee they have befriended. The 'kumāras' have decided that the local temple's emphasis on śruti-smṛti-purāṇādi-pañcarātra-vidhi — early rising, japa quotas, dress codes, regulative principles — is 'external' and that they themselves have access to a higher, more confidential rasa. They invite the newer devotee to a private gathering where they will 'reveal' the inner moods of the gopīs and bestow on them a confidential identity, bypassing the standard sādhana. The newer devotee must decide whether to go. The rest of the class observes, then a fourth student plays a senior Vaiṣṇava the newer devotee consults afterward. Debrief: where exactly did the kumāras' pitch sound most appealing? What language did they use that mirrored 'avinītā vinīta-vat' — unhumble behavior dressed as humility? How did the senior Vaiṣṇava distinguish real intimacy with Kṛṣṇa from its counterfeit?

Objections & Responses
  • Isn't this purport — and your reading of it — just a sectarian attack on other Vaiṣṇava lineages who happen to emphasize the gopī-bhāva more directly? It feels like in-group policing dressed up as scripture.
    The objection assumes the line being drawn is between groups. The line Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī and Rūpa Gosvāmī draw is between any process that follows śruti, smṛti, Purāṇa and Nārada-pañcarātra and any process that bypasses them — regardless of which sampradāya the practitioner claims. Rūpa Gosvāmī's verse (Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.2.101) is explicit: bhakti without the regulative framework of revealed scripture becomes utpāta, a social disturbance, however sincere the practitioner feels. The test is not 'which lineage' but 'is the process authorized by śāstra and a bona fide guru in disciplic succession.' A Gauḍīya practitioner can fail this test as easily as anyone else — and the purport names sahajiyā precisely as an internal Gauḍīya deviation, not an external rival. Far from sectarian, it is the tradition policing itself.
  • The boys were just being boys — playful, mischievous, sixteen-year-olds with the most exalted Vaiṣṇavas in the world. Why does Kṛṣṇa respond by destroying the entire Yadu dynasty? Isn't that wildly disproportionate, even cruel?
    Three things to hold together. First, Jīva Gosvāmī clarifies that the Yadus are eternal associates of Kṛṣṇa; the apparent offense is itself arranged by the Lord to instruct future generations. The 'destruction' is the Lord winding up His manifest pastimes, not a punitive massacre of strangers. Second, the disproportion is the teaching: vaiṣṇava-aparādha is not measured by the offender's age or intent but by the position of the offended. If Kṛṣṇa's own associates, in apparent play, can trigger this — what about us, in deliberate criticism? The severity of the response calibrates our sense of the gravity of the offense. Third, Kṛṣṇa's response is also His mercy to His devotees: the sages are honored, the false process of sakhī-bheka is preemptively discredited, and Kali-yuga is given a clear case study. What looks like cruelty in the frame of bodily welfare is precise compassion in the frame of the soul.
