Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.12.11

तास्ता: क्षपा: प्रेष्ठतमेन नीता मयैव वृन्दावनगोचरेण । क्षणार्धवत्ता: पुनरङ्ग तासां हीना मया कल्पसमा बभूवु: ॥ ११ ॥
tās tāḥ kṣapāḥ preṣṭhatamena nītā
[taas taah kshapaah preshthatamena neetaa]

[mayaiva vrindaavana-gocharena]

hīnā mayā kalpa-samā babhūvuḥ
[heenaa mayaa kalpa-samaa babhoovuh]

Dear Uddhava, all of those nights that the gopīs spent with Me, their most dearly beloved, in the land of Vṛndāvana seemed to them to pass in less than a moment. Bereft of My association, however, the gopīs felt that those same nights dragged on forever, as if each night were equal to a day of Brahmā.
Purport

Śrīla Śrīdhara Svāmī comments as follows. “The gopīs suffered extreme anxiety in the absence of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and though outwardly appearing bewildered, they actually achieved the highest perfectional stage of samādhi. Their consciousness was intensely and intimately attached to Lord Kṛṣṇa, and by such Kṛṣṇa consciousness their own bodies seemed very far away from them, even though people normally consider their body to be their closest possession. In fact, the gopīs did not think about their own existence. Although a young woman normally considers her husband and children to be her dearmost possessions, the gopīs did not even consider the existence of their so-called families. Nor could they think of this world or life after death. Indeed, they were not at all aware of these things. Just like great sages who become detached from the names and forms of the material world, the gopīs could not think of anything, because they were rapt in loving remembrance of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Just as rivers enter the ocean, similarly, the gopīs completely merged into consciousness of Lord Kṛṣṇa through intense love.”

Thus a day of Brahmā seemed like a single moment for the gopīs when Lord Kṛṣṇa was present with them, and a single moment seemed like a day of Brahmā when Lord Kṛṣṇa was absent. The Kṛṣṇa consciousness of the gopīs is the perfection of spiritual life, and the symptoms of such perfection are described here.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
All of those nights that the gopīs spent with me, their most dearly beloved, in the land of Vṛndāvana while I herded the cows, seemed to them to pass in less than a moment. Bereft of my association, however, the gopīs felt that those same nights were equal to a day of Brahmā. This verse shows the outstanding quality of rūḍha-bhāva which is a type of mahā-bhāva. The seventh state of prema, mahā-bhāva is defined as kalpasya kṣaṇatā yoge viyoge tad-viparyayaḥ: in meeting the Lord a kalpa seems to be a moment, and in separation a moment seems to be a kalpa. (Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi 14.168) The nights of the rāsa dance with me, which lasted for a night of Brahmā, while I was situated in Vṛndāvana (vṛndāvana-gocareṇa) or while I was tending cows in Vṛndāvana, passed like half a moment. Nights of four praharas (twelve hours) became like many days of Brahmā because they could not tolerate that time in separation.
Personal Guidance
When the heart is rapt in Kṛṣṇa, time with Him flies and time without Him drags — love, not the clock, measures life.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today which activities make time vanish and which make it crawl, and honestly ask which direction your heart is leaning.
  • Spend even fifteen unhurried minutes chanting or hearing about Kṛṣṇa and refuse to glance at the clock during it.
  • When separation from sādhana drags the day, treat the ache itself as a doorway, not a problem to numb with distraction.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life does a moment without Kṛṣṇa already feel like a day of Brahmā, and where does it not yet register at all?
  • What 'closest possessions' — body, family, reputation — would have to recede for Kṛṣṇa to occupy the foreground the gopīs gave Him?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Track an honest hour of scrolling versus an honest hour of chanting and notice which one leaves you feeling like time was stolen.
  • When a friendship, crush, or exam result makes a single evening feel endless, use that intensity as a small clue to what gopī-bhāva is pointing at.
  • Refuse to outsource your sense of time to notifications — keep one daily window where Kṛṣṇa, not the feed, sets the pace.
householder
  • Protect one fixed slot of sādhana in the family schedule and guard it as carefully as you guard work deadlines or your children's routines.
  • When work travel or duties pull you from temple and association, name the dullness honestly to yourself instead of filling it with entertainment.
  • Speak with your spouse about which 'dearmost possessions' — house, status, plans — quietly occupy the seat that Kṛṣṇa should hold.
senior
  • Treat each long, slow day as a gift of time that younger years did not afford, and fill it with hearing, chanting, and remembrance.
  • Where the body's complaints make hours drag, practice turning that very heaviness into a prayer for Kṛṣṇa's shelter.
  • Mentor a younger devotee by sharing, plainly, which attachments you once thought essential and how remembrance of Kṛṣṇa has slowly relocated them.
Common Pitfalls
  • Romanticizing the gopīs' viraha as a mood to perform rather than a perfection achieved by surrendered souls.
  • Mistaking ordinary boredom or restlessness for spiritual separation from Kṛṣṇa.
  • Treating absorption as a feeling to manufacture instead of the natural fruit of steady hearing, chanting, and service.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share an experience where time collapsed or stretched, and discuss whether the cause was material absorption or something closer to remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
  • The purport says the gopīs did not even register their own bodies — is such forgetfulness of the body desirable for us, or dangerous, and why?
  • If a day of Brahmā can pass as a moment in Kṛṣṇa's company, what does that say about the value of one sincere hour of sādhana today?
Role Play

One student plays a busy professional who insists they 'don't have time' for chanting; the other plays a friend who gently points out where their hours actually go and how the verse's measure of time — by absorption, not by minutes — might reframe their schedule.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like a love story dressed up as philosophy — how is gopī longing relevant to a serious modern student?
    Prabhupāda, following Śrīdhara Svāmī, explicitly calls the gopīs' state the highest samādhi and the perfection of spiritual life; their longing is not sentiment but the topmost example of consciousness fully fixed on Kṛṣṇa, which every sādhaka is meant to aspire toward.
  • Forgetting body, family, and the world sounds like dissociation, not health.
    The gopīs do not forget through avoidance or numbness; they forget because something infinitely more real has filled their consciousness. The test is not detachment from duty but whether remembrance of Kṛṣṇa is strong enough that lesser attachments naturally loosen.
