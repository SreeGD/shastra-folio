In every yuga many living entities entangled in the modes of passion and ignorance gained the association of My devotees. Thus, such living entities as the Daityas, Rākṣasas, birds, beasts, Gandharvas, Apsarās, Nāgas, Siddhas, Cāraṇas, Guhyakas and Vidyādharas, as well as such lower-class human beings as the vaiśyas, śūdras, women and others, were able to achieve My supreme abode. Vṛtrāsura, Prahlāda Mahārāja and others like them also achieved My abode by association with My devotees, as did personalities such as Vṛṣaparvā, Bali Mahārāja, Bāṇāsura, Maya, Vibhīṣaṇa, Sugrīva, Hanumān, Jāmbavān, Gajendra, Jaṭāyu, Tulādhāra, Dharma-vyādha, Kubjā, the gopīs in Vṛndāvana and the wives of the brāhmaṇas who were performing sacrifice.
Purport

The Lord has mentioned devotees such as the gopīs in Vṛndāvana and also demons like Bāṇāsura to illustrate how He comes under the control of those who surrender to Him. It is understood that devotees like the gopīs and others mentioned here obtained pure love of Kṛṣṇa, whereas the demons generally obtained only salvation. Many demons were purified by association with devotees and came to accept devotional service to the Lord as the most important among the various activities in their lives, but the exalted devotees like Prahlāda and Bali Mahārāja know nothing except devotional service, which they accept as their very life. Still, the reformed demons are also mentioned so that readers of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam will understand the enormous benefits one may achieve by associating with devotees of the Lord.

The demon Vṛtrāsura was the pious King Citraketu in his previous life, during which he associated with Śrī Nārada Muni, Śrī Aṅgirā Muni and Lord Saṅkarṣaṇa. Prahlāda Mahārāja, being the son of Hiraṇyakaśipu, is considered a Daitya, or demon. Yet while still in the womb of his mother, Kayādhū, he associated with Nārada Muni by sound vibration. The demon Vṛṣaparvā was abandoned by his mother at birth, but he was raised by a muni and became a devotee of Lord Viṣṇu. Bali Mahārāja associated with his grandfather Prahlāda and also with Lord Vāmanadeva. Bali Mahārāja’s son, Bāṇāsura, was saved by association with his father and Lord Śiva. He also associated with Lord Kṛṣṇa personally when the Lord cut off all but two of his one thousand arms, which had been awarded as a benediction by Lord Śiva. Understanding the glories of Lord Kṛṣṇa, Bāṇāsura also became a great devotee. The demon Maya Dānava constructed an assembly house for the Pāṇḍavas and also associated with Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself, eventually achieving the shelter of the Lord. Vibhīṣaṇa was a pious-natured demon, the brother of Rāvaṇa, and he associated with Hanumān and Rāmacandra.

Sugrīva, Hanumān, Jāmbavān and Gajendra are examples of animals who achieved the mercy of the Lord. Jāmbavān, or Ṛkṣarāja, was a member of a race of bears. He personally associated with Lord Kṛṣṇa, fighting with Him over the Syamantaka jewel. The elephant Gajendra in a previous life had association with devotees, and at the end of his life as Gajendra he was personally saved by the Lord. Jaṭāyu, the bird who at the cost of his own life assisted Lord Rāmacandra, associated with Śrī Garuḍa and Mahārāja Daśaratha as well as other devotees in rāma-līlā. He also personally met with Sītā and Lord Rāma. According to Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, the association that the Gandharvas, Apsarās, Nāgas, Siddhas, Cāraṇas, Guhyakas and Vidyādharas had with the devotees is not very prominent and does not need to be mentioned. Vaṇikpatha is a vaiśya, and his story is mentioned in the Mahābhārata in connection with the pride of Jājali Muni.

The importance of association with devotees is illustrated in the story of Dharma-vyādha, the nonviolent hunter, as described in the Varāha Purāṇa. In a previous life he somehow became a brahma-rākṣasa, or brāhmaṇa ghost, but was eventually saved. In a previous Kali-yuga he had the association of a Vaiṣṇava king named Vāsu. The lady Kubjā associated directly with Lord Kṛṣṇa, and in her previous birth she had associated with Śrī Nārada Muni. The gopīs of Vṛndāvana rendered service to saintly persons in their previous births. Having had ample association with devotees, they became gopīs in Vṛndāvana in their next lives and associated with the eternally liberated gopīs who had descended there. They also had association with Tulasī-devī, or Vṛndādevī. The wives of the brāhmaṇas performing sacrifice had association with women sent by Lord Kṛṣṇa to sell flower garlands and betel nuts and heard about the Lord from them.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In every yuga many living entities entangled in the modes of passion and ignorance gained the association of my devotees. Thus, such living entities as the Daityas, Rākṣasas, birds, beasts, Gandharvas, Apsarās, Nāgas, Siddhas, Cāraṇas, Guhyakas and Vidyādharas, as well as such lower-class human beings as the vaiśyas, śūdras, women and others, were able to achieve my supreme abode. Vṛtrāsura, Prahlāda Mahārāja and others like them also achieved my abode by association with my devotees, as did personalities such as Vṛṣaparvā, Bali Mahārāja, Bāṇāsura, Maya, Vibhīṣaṇa, Sugrīva, Hanumān, Jāmbavān, Gajendra, Jaṭāyu, Tulādhāra, Dharma-vyādha, Kubjā, the gopīs in Vṛndāvana and the wives of the brāhmaṇas who were performing sacrifice. Four verses show that association with devotees brings the Lord under control, either secondarily or significantly, giving examples like Bāṇa and the gopīs. There are persons with mixed bhakti and pure bhakti. By association with persons with mixed bhakti some persons brought the Lord under control in a secondary way. By association with persons with pure bhakti, some persons brought the Lord under control significantly. Yātudhānāḥ means Rākṣasas. Tvāṣṭraḥ means Vṛtrāsura. Kāyādhavaḥ is Prahlāda. Before their birth Vṛtra and Prahlāda had the association of Nārada. Vṛṣa-parvā is well known in the Purāṇas as a devotee of Viṣṇu who gave up his mother as soon as he was born and was raised by sages. Bali had association of Prahlāda. When Bāṇa’s arms were cut off, he got the association of compassionate Śiva. Maya, in building the assembly hall, got the association of the Pāṇḍavas. Vibhīṣaṇa got the association of Hanumān. Sugrīva, Hanumān and Jambavān got the association of Lakṣmaṇa. Gajendra in his previous life had the association of Nārada. Jaṭāyu had the association of Garuḍa and Daśartha. The merchant or tulādhāra is famous in Mahābhārata. His association is unclear. Dharmavyādha was a hunter. In his previous birth as a brahma-rākṣasa, he got the association of a devotee king according to Varāha Purāṇa. Hari-vaṁśa tells how Kubja and others got the association of Nārada in a previous birth. The gopīs who were previously sages of Daṇḍakāraṇya forest had plentiful association with devotees in that life. In their life as gopīs they got the association of nitya-siddha gopīs. The wives of the brāhmaṇas got the association of messengers of Kṛṣṇa--the garland makers and betel nut sellers who came from Vraja to Mathurā to sell their products.
Personal Guidance
Sat-saṅga lifts even demons, animals, and the lowest-born to Kṛṣṇa's abode — your birth does not bar you; your association decides everything.
Practical Applications
  • Audit today's company: name the three people whose words shape you most, and ask whether any of them speak of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Seek one concrete contact with a devotee today — a kīrtana, a class, a phone call — and treat it as the most important appointment on the calendar.
  • Refuse the inner verdict that says 'I am too fallen, too modern, too far gone' — the verse names birds, beasts, and Bāṇāsura before it names you.
Reflection Questions
  • Which 'disqualification' — caste, gender, past sins, modern conditioning — have I been quietly using as proof that pure bhakti is not really meant for me?
  • When I look at my week, whose association is actually feeding my consciousness, and whose am I rationalizing because it is convenient?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before the next exam week, pick one friend group whose conversations leave you emptier and one whose presence sharpens your sādhana, and shift one evening a week from the first to the second.
  • Treat your feed like a guest list: unfollow three accounts whose worldview you would not want Kṛṣṇa-conscious children of yours raised on, and follow one devotee who actually challenges you.
  • When peers say 'you're too young to be serious about this,' remember Prahlāda heard Nārada in the womb — your stage of life is not the disqualification you think it is.
householder
  • Look at the people your children watch you laugh with, eat with, take advice from — and ask whether that roster would, over fifteen years, produce a devotee or a careerist.
  • Build one non-negotiable weekly slot for devotee association as a family — a program, a home kīrtana, a meal with a senior Vaiṣṇava — and defend it like a school run.
  • When a career or money decision pulls you toward company that mocks your practice, recall Bāṇāsura: even great power did not save him until association did — and even great earnings will not save you alone.
senior
  • Inventory the last decade of your association and name, without flinching, which relationships you continued out of habit or status rather than spiritual benefit — and quietly thin the calendar.
  • Position yourself as Nārada was for Prahlāda: make yourself a steady devotee-presence for one younger person — a grandchild, a neighbor, a junior at the temple — whose later life will date its turn to your company.
  • As the body declines, stop measuring your standing by past services and start measuring it by present saṅga — whose voice is in your ear in this season, when illness or solitude finally strips away everything but what you listened to.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the list of demons saved and concluding that conduct does not matter — missing that each of them had to actually associate with devotees, not merely hear about them.
  • Treating sat-saṅga as a feeling of being uplifted in a crowd of devotees rather than a costly reordering of whom one listens to, eats with, and confides in.
  • Using the verse's mention of 'lower-class' beings as license for false humility or false egalitarianism, instead of as Kṛṣṇa's own dismantling of the excuse that one is unfit.
Discussion Prompts
  • The verse lists Bāṇāsura beside the gopīs. Where does each of you draw the line between 'understandable' and 'embarrassing' on that list, and what does that line reveal about your own theology?
  • If sat-saṅga is the operative cause here, what specific association in your present life would you have to end for the next association to actually take hold?
  • Prabhupāda notes the demons got mukti while the gopīs got prema. In your own aspiration, are you secretly aiming at the demon's reward and calling it the gopī's?
Role Play

One student plays a sincere seeker who insists, 'I was not born in this tradition, I eat meat, I work in finance — I cannot really be a candidate for pure bhakti.' The other student, drawing only on this verse and its purport, must answer them — naming specific figures from the list and what their association consisted of — without flattering the seeker and without dismissing the obstacle.

Objections & Responses
  • This is a convenient list assembled to flatter every group. Surely a real spiritual path has standards, not a roster of demons and animals who got in anyway.
    The standard is precisely what the verse names: sustained association with a pure devotee. Vṛtrāsura was Citraketu with Nārada and Aṅgirā; Prahlāda heard Nārada in the womb; Gajendra cried out after lifetimes of devotee contact. The bar is not lowered — it is relocated from birth to saṅga, and that bar is higher, not lower, than respectability.
  • I have devotee friends online, I listen to lectures, I chant when I remember — I already have sat-saṅga.
    Notice that in the purport every figure named had embodied, often inconvenient contact: Bāṇāsura before Kṛṣṇa with severed arms, Jāmbavān wrestling Him for a jewel, the brāhmaṇa wives risking their husbands' anger to hear from flower-sellers. Curated, comfortable, on-demand association is not yet the sat-saṅga of this verse; the test is whether it costs you something and reorders your day.
