Chapter 4 · Class prep

BG 4.1

King Nimi said: The Supreme Personality of Godhead descends into the material world by His internal potency and according to His own desire. Therefore, please tell us about the various pastimes Lord Hari has performed in the past, is performing now and will perform in this world in the future in His various incarnations.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa descends by His own will in eternal forms; hearing of His pastimes from authority is how a devotee finds the mūrti that draws love.

Practical applications
  • Today, hear or read one description of Hari's incarnations from a bona fide source rather than scrolling for entertainment.
  • Notice which eternal form of the Lord most naturally draws your heart, and resolve to learn more about Him through scripture and the spiritual master.
  • Refuse to concoct or imagine a form of God on your own whim; instead, submissively receive the forms already revealed in the Bhāgavatam.
Discussion

King Nimi already knows Hari is supreme — why does he still need to hear about the specific incarnations? What does that say about our own hearing?

BG 4.2

Śrī Drumila said: Anyone trying to enumerate or describe fully the unlimited qualities of the unlimited Supreme Lord has the intelligence of a foolish child. Even if a great genius could somehow or other, after a time-consuming endeavor, count all the particles of dust on the surface of the earth, such a genius could never count the attractive qualities of the Personality of Godhead, who is the reservoir of all potencies.

Essence. The Lord's qualities are unlimited; abandon the conceit of mastering Him through study and instead submissively hear, tasting a drop of the ocean.

Practical applications
  • Before opening a śāstric book today, pause and admit aloud that you approach as a bāla-buddhi, not a conqueror of the text.
  • Refuse the urge to summarize Kṛṣṇa in a clever sentence at the end of your reading; instead, sit with one quality and let it work on you.
  • Spend ten minutes hearing Bhāgavatam from a realized source today rather than reading commentary about it, honoring the submissive-hearing order Prabhupāda gives.
Discussion

Share one moment when your study of Kṛṣṇa shifted from collecting information to actually hearing — and one moment when it slid back.

BG 4.3

When the primeval Lord Nārāyaṇa created His universal body out of the five elements produced from Himself and then entered within that universal body by His own plenary portion, He thus became known as the Puruṣa.

Essence. Nārāyaṇa enters the universal body He Himself forms from the five elements; the elements are His, never the false ego's, to enjoy.

Practical applications
  • Before eating today, pause and remember the food's earth, water, fire, air and sky belong to Nārāyaṇa, then offer it to Him.
  • Notice one moment when ahaṅkāra whispers 'I am the enjoyer of this body' and answer back that the Puruṣa within is the true proprietor.
  • Refuse to use any of the five elements today purely for sense gratification; route at least one act of eating, working, or speaking into yajña.
Discussion

Share a concrete area of life — food, money, body, home — where you still operate as the enjoyer of the five elements. What would shift if you treated Nārāyaṇa as the actual Puruṣa within them?

BG 4.4

Within His body are elaborately arranged the three planetary systems of this universe. His transcendental senses generate the knowledge-acquiring and active senses of all embodied beings. His consciousness generates conditioned knowledge, and His powerful breathing produces the bodily strength, sensory power and conditioned activities of the embodied souls. He is the prime mover, through the agency of the material modes of goodness, passion and ignorance. And thus the universe is created, maintained and annihilated.

Essence. The Lord is the prime mover whose body holds the three worlds and whose breath powers your senses — surrender to Him is the only sane response.

Practical applications
  • Before any action today, pause and acknowledge that your senses, strength, and knowledge are on loan from Kṛṣṇa, not your own equipment.
  • When sickness, fatigue, or fear strips your capacity, refuse the panic-response and instead chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, recognizing the Supersoul within your heart.
  • End the day by offering whatever knowledge or work you produced back to the Personality of Godhead who supplied the breath behind it.
Discussion

Share a moment when disease, exhaustion, or fear suddenly stripped your sense of competence — what did that reveal about who was actually running your senses?

BG 4.5

In the beginning, the original Supreme Personality manifested the form of Brahmā through the material mode of passion in order to create this universe. The Lord manifested His form as Viṣṇu, the Lord of sacrifice and protector of the twice-born brāhmaṇas and their religious duties, to maintain the universe. And when the universe is to be annihilated the same Supreme Lord employs the material mode of ignorance and manifests the form of Rudra. The created living beings are thus always subject to the forces of creation, maintenance and destruction.

Essence. Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra preside over creation, maintenance and dissolution — but Viṣṇu alone is viśuddha-sattva, the Lord Himself, the only safe shelter.

Practical applications
  • Notice today which guṇa — rajas, sattva or tamas — is driving each major decision, and refuse to act from rajas or tamas without first pausing.
  • When you feel proud of building something or devastated when something ends, remind yourself you are watching sṛṣṭi-sthiti-laya pass through you, not happening to you.
  • Direct your worship and shelter-taking specifically to Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa today, not to a generic "higher power" — speak His name aloud at least once.
Discussion

Share one project, relationship or phase of life you are currently in, and identify which of the three — sṛṣṭi, sthiti or laya — it is in; do others see it the same way you do?

BG 4.6

Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi, who is perfectly peaceful and is the best of sages, was born as the son of Dharma and his wife Mūrti, the daughter of Dakṣa. Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi taught the devotional service of the Lord, by which material work ceases, and He Himself perfectly practiced this knowledge. He is living even today, His lotus feet served by the greatest of saintly persons.

Essence. Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi shows that naiṣkarmya is not idle quietism but action fully offered to Kṛṣṇa — He still serves; so must we.

Practical applications
  • Pick one task today that you usually do for results, and consciously offer the doing and the fruit to Kṛṣṇa before you begin.
  • Refuse the dichotomy of work-or-renunciation: stay at your post and let bhakti, not withdrawal, convert your karma into naiṣkarmya.
  • Choose one sober, peaceful action over a reactive one today, taking Nara-Nārāyaṇa's praśānta bearing as your model.
Discussion

Share a current responsibility you secretly wish you could renounce — and test together whether Nara-Nārāyaṇa's example calls you to leave it or to re-offer it.

BG 4.7

King Indra became fearful, thinking that Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi would become very powerful by His severe penances and seize Indra’s heavenly kingdom. Thus Indra, not knowing the transcendental glories of the incarnation of the Lord, sent Cupid and his associates to the Lord’s residence in Badarikāśrama. As the charming breezes of spring created a most sensuous atmosphere, Cupid himself attacked the Lord with arrows in the form of the irresistible glances of beautiful women.

Essence. Indra projects his own enjoyer-mentality onto the Lord; the soul who mistakes Kṛṣṇa for a rival enjoyer reveals only his own atan-mahi-jṣaḥ blindness.

Practical applications
  • Notice today the precise moment you assume someone holier than you is secretly after the same things you want, and check that projection.
  • When envy of another's sādhana arises, refuse the Indra-impulse to test or undermine them, and instead inquire what they are actually pursuing.
  • Before any act of worship, remind yourself that Kṛṣṇa is not an enjoyer of mundane fruits, so do not bargain with Him as if He were.
Discussion

Indra sends spring breezes, apsarās, and Cupid's arrows — which of these three weapons does the modern environment deploy most heavily against you, and do the others even register anymore?

BG 4.8

The primeval Lord, understanding the offense committed by Indra, did not become proud. Instead He spoke laughingly as follows to Cupid and his followers, who were trembling before Him: “Do not fear, O mighty Madana, O wind-god and wives of the demigods. Rather, please accept these gifts I am offering you and kindly sanctify My āśrama by your presence.”

Essence. True greatness is gata-vismaya — free of pride; meet provocation with hospitality, not retaliation, and make your space useful to Kṛṣṇa's servants.

Practical applications
  • When someone tests or insults you today, refuse the satisfaction of a curse-like reply and instead offer them something concrete — tea, food, a kind word.
  • Audit one austerity or discipline you secretly take pride in and offer its fruit aloud to Kṛṣṇa before the day ends.
  • Invite a guest into your home or room this week specifically so the space does not become 'śūnya' — empty of service to devotees and seekers.
Discussion

Share a time you had every right to retaliate and chose hospitality instead — what did it cost you, and was the cost worth it?

BG 4.9

My dear King Nimi, when Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi thus spoke, eradicating the fear of the demigods, they bowed their heads with shame and addressed the Lord as follows, to invoke His compassion: Our dear Lord, You are always transcendental, beyond the reach of illusion, and therefore You are forever changeless. Your causeless compassion toward us, despite our great offense, is not at all unusual in You, since innumerable great sages who are self-satisfied and free from anger and false pride bow down humbly at Your lotus feet.

Essence. Krishna remains forever changeless and causelessly compassionate; the self-satisfied dhīra sages bow at His lotus feet — so can we, despite our offenses.

Practical applications
  • Bow your head — literally — before Krishna's image today and name one specific offense you carry, trusting His changeless compassion.
  • Notice the next moment material pride or anger rises in you, and contrast it with the avikṛta (unchanging) nature of the Lord you are trying to serve.
  • Refuse the impulse to hide your fault from your guru, deity, or sādhu-saṅga; approach with bowed head, as the demigods did, rather than performing composure.
Discussion

Share a time you, like the demigods, only recognized your offense after the fact — what made approaching with a bowed head harder or easier than staying away?

BG 4.10

The demigods place many obstacles on the path of those who worship You to transcend the temporary abodes of the demigods and reach Your supreme abode. Those who offer the demigods their assigned shares in sacrificial performances encounter no such obstacles. But because You are the direct protector of Your devotee, he is able to step over the head of whatever obstacle the demigods place before him.

Essence. Demigods may obstruct the exclusive devotee, but Kṛṣṇa Himself steps in as protector — so every obstacle becomes a rung on the ladder home.

Practical applications
  • Notice today which obstacle you have been reading as punishment, and re-read it as Kṛṣṇa's personal training of a devotee He is protecting.
  • Refuse the bargain of worshiping lesser powers (status, position, reputation) for a smoother path, and offer that worship directly to Kṛṣṇa instead.
  • When a sura-kṛta obstacle lands — a sudden setback in work, health, or relationships — pause, take shelter in Kṛṣṇa by name, and step forward in sādhana rather than retreat.
Discussion

Share an obstacle on your devotional path that, in hindsight, functioned as a ladder — and one you still cannot read that way. What is different about the two?

BG 4.11

Some men practice severe penances to cross beyond our influence, which is like an immeasurable ocean with endless waves of hunger, thirst, heat, cold and the other conditions brought about by the passing of time, such as the sensuous wind and the urges of the tongue and sex organs. Nevertheless, although crossing this ocean of sense gratification through severe penances, such persons foolishly drown in a cow’s hoofprint when conquered by useless anger. Thus they exhaust the benefit of their difficult austerities in vain.

Essence. Austerity without devotion to Kṛṣṇa breeds krodha that burns up all its fruit — the yogī crosses the ocean of senses only to drown in a hoofprint of anger.

Practical applications
  • Notice the next moment you feel righteous anger after disciplining yourself, and recognize it as krodha draining your sādhana.
  • Offer every act of restraint — fasting, early rising, sense-control — to Kṛṣṇa explicitly, so the tapas does not feed your false pride.
  • Refuse to curse, insult, or sharply criticize anyone today, especially those who have disturbed your routine of discipline.
Discussion

Share a time your discipline made you more irritable, not more peaceful — what was actually being purified, and what was not?

BG 4.12

While the demigods were thus praising the Supreme Lord, the all-powerful Lord suddenly manifested before their eyes many women, who were astonishingly gorgeous, decorated with fine clothes and ornaments, and all faithfully engaging in the Lord’s service.

Essence. The Lord cures the demigods' pride in beauty and companionship by revealing that He is already served by countless beings infinitely more glorious.

Practical applications
  • Notice today the one possession, relationship, or talent you secretly use to feel superior, and silently offer it to Kṛṣṇa as already His.
  • When you catch yourself admiring your own attractiveness or social standing, recall that the Lord's least servant outshines it, and let that thought soften your chest.
  • Refuse to compete with another devotee over external assets; instead ask how you can serve alongside them in śuśrūṣā (faithful service).
Discussion

Share one area where you, like the demigods, take quiet pride in something the Lord could replicate a million-fold — and discuss whether you would welcome or resent His exposing it.

BG 4.13

When the followers of the demigods gazed upon the fascinating mystic beauty of the women created by Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi and smelled the fragrance of their bodies, the minds of these followers became bewildered. Indeed, upon seeing the beauty and magnificence of such women, the representatives of the demigods were completely diminished in their own opulence.

Essence. Even the demigods' followers are unmade by beauty crafted by a ṛṣi — viṣaya enters through the senses; vairāgya begins by knowing this.

Practical applications
  • Notice the first sense-impression today that quietly diminishes your steadiness, and name it as viṣaya before it names you.
  • Refuse the modern story that you are too sophisticated to be bewildered — guard the eyes and nose the way the demigods' followers failed to.
  • Each evening, identify one moment your own opulence (skill, calm, focus) was hata-śriyaḥ — diminished — by an attraction, and offer that exposure to Kṛṣṇa.
Discussion

Share one category of sense-object that diminishes your steadiness more than you publicly admit — and what you actually do about it.

BG 4.14

The Supreme Lord of lords then smiled slightly and told the representatives of heaven, who were bowing down before Him, “Please choose one of these women, whomever you find suitable for you. She will become the ornament of the heavenly planets.”

Essence. Even when bewildered by beauty, the soul's safety lies in Kṛṣṇa's grave smile — He offers what is truly suitable, not what dazzles.

Practical applications
  • Notice one allurement today that makes you feel "low-class" and small, and bring it before Kṛṣṇa instead of chasing or fleeing it.
  • Before any major choice, pause and ask whether you are picking what dazzles or what is sa-varṇa — genuinely suited to your character and sva-dharma.
  • When you sense the Lord's gentle, almost-laughing correction in your life, bow your head as the demigods did rather than defending your ego.
Discussion

Share a time you were quietly defeated by something beautiful — money, a relationship, recognition — and how you wish you had bowed instead of bargained.

BG 4.15

Vibrating the sacred syllable om, the servants of the demigods selected Urvaśī, the best of the Apsarās. Placing her in front of them out of respect, they returned to the heavenly planets.

Essence. When a deceptive plan succeeds, the worldly celebrate their prize and return home triumphant — but Urvaśī is the very lure that will undo them.

Practical applications
  • Notice today the moment a 'win' makes you carry it home with pride — and ask whose pleasure that prize will actually serve.
  • Refuse to chant sacred sounds like om as a polite frame around a desire-driven errand; speak Kṛṣṇa's names with the intent to be transformed, not endorsed.
  • Before accepting any reward offered to you 'with respect,' ask what the giver wants from you in return, and whether your sva-dharma can survive the trade.
Discussion

Share a time you returned home with a 'prize' — a job, a relationship, a possession — and only later saw what it cost your inner life; what would you tell your earlier self?

BG 4.16

The servants of the demigods reached the assembly of Indra, and thus, while all the residents of the three heavens listened, they explained to Indra the supreme power of Nārāyaṇa. When Indra heard of Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi and became aware of his offense, he was both frightened and astonished.

Essence. When Indra hears of Nara-Nārāyaṇa's supreme power, even the king of heaven is struck with fear and wonder — true power belongs to Kṛṣṇa alone.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you secretly identify with your position or status, and remember that even Indra trembled before Nārāyaṇa's power.
  • When you receive a report about someone whose spiritual stature exceeds yours, refuse the urge to compete and instead offer respect.
  • Before any meeting where you fear losing face, take one minute to remember that Nārāyaṇa is the actual source of every strength in the room.
Discussion

Share a time you discovered, after the fact, that someone you had dismissed or opposed was actually a serious devotee — what shifted in you?

BG 4.17

The infallible Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, has descended into this world by His various partial incarnations such as Lord Haṁsa [the swan], Dattātreya, the four Kumāras and our own father, the mighty Ṛṣabhadeva. By such incarnations, the Lord teaches the science of self-realization for the benefit of the whole universe. In His appearance as Hayagrīva He killed the demon Madhu and thus brought the Vedas back from the hellish planet Pātālaloka.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa descends in forms like Haṁsa, Dattātreya, the Kumāras, Ṛṣabha and Hayagrīva to personally deliver the science of self-realization — receive it as His direct mercy.

Practical applications
  • Open your daily reading by remembering that the words before you reach you because Hayagrīva personally rescued the Vedas — treat scripture as His direct gift, not generic philosophy.
  • Identify one teacher in your life whose instruction carries this paramparā current, and act on a single instruction of theirs today rather than only admiring it.
  • When tempted to manufacture your own spiritual technique, refuse it and instead study how the avatāras themselves taught — let Kṛṣṇa define ātma-yoga, not your preferences.
Discussion

Share one teaching from a recognized teacher in your line that you have heard many times but quietly not applied — what is the real reason it has not landed?

BG 4.18

In His appearance as a fish, the Lord protected Satyavrata Manu, the earth and her valuable herbs. He protected them from the waters of annihilation. As a boar, the Lord killed Hiraṇyākṣa, the son of Diti, while delivering the earth from the universal waters. And as a tortoise, He lifted Mandara Mountain on His back so that nectar could be churned from the ocean. The Lord saved the surrendered king of the elephants, Gajendra, who was suffering terrible distress from the grips of a crocodile.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa descends as Matsya, Varāha, Kūrma, and rescuer of Gajendra — He personally answers those who surrender in distress.

Practical applications
  • Name one situation today where you are caught like Gajendra, and verbally call out to Kṛṣṇa by name instead of strategizing alone.
  • Refuse the modern myth that you must self-rescue — pick one task and begin it by acknowledging that Kṛṣṇa, not your effort, is the deliverer.
  • Speak to someone drowning in difficulty today and remind them that surrender is not weakness but the doorway Kṛṣṇa Himself has promised to open.
Discussion

Share a time you tried to self-rescue from a situation that only worsened — what would calling out to Kṛṣṇa have looked like at that moment?

BG 4.19

The Lord also delivered the tiny ascetic sages called the Vālakhilyas when they fell into the water in a cow’s hoofprint and Indra was laughing at them. The Lord then saved Indra when Indra was covered by darkness due to the sinful reaction for killing Vṛtrāsura. When the wives of the demigods were trapped in the palace of the demons without any shelter, the Lord saved them. In His incarnation as Nṛsiṁha, the Lord killed Hiraṇyakaśipu, the king of demons, to free the saintly devotees from fear.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa rescues the fallen, the darkened, the trapped, and the fearful — surrender is the doorway through which His protection reaches every kind of helplessness.

Practical applications
  • Name one situation today where you feel small, fallen, or laughed at, and explicitly hand it to Kṛṣṇa instead of trying to claw your way out alone.
  • When darkness of regret over a past sinful act covers you, do not hide — chant the holy name and remember that even Indra was retrieved from tamas by the Lord.
  • Refuse the cynicism that says 'no one is coming to save me'; structure your day around remembering that the Lord has a track record of intervening for the helpless.
Discussion

Share a time you felt like one of the Vālakhilyas — tiny, dismissed, drowning in something others found trivial. What would 'Kṛṣṇa's hand' have looked like then, and did you recognize it?

BG 4.20

The Supreme Lord regularly takes advantage of the wars between the demons and demigods to kill the leaders of the demons. The Lord thus encourages the demigods by protecting the universe through His various incarnations during the reigns of each Manu. The Lord also appeared as Vāmana and took the earth away from Bali Mahārāja on the plea of begging three steps of land. The Lord then returned the entire world to the sons of Aditi.

Essence. Across every age, Kṛṣṇa Himself descends to dismantle adharma and restore what belongs to His devotees — never doubt that He personally intervenes.

Practical applications
  • Today, name one situation where you are waiting for impersonal fate to resolve things, and instead pray to Kṛṣṇa by name to act.
  • When you see injustice favoring the powerful, refuse cynicism — remember that the Lord uses even the cleverness of the strong to undo them.
  • Offer one small act of service explicitly to Kṛṣṇa as the protector who has already arranged your welfare across many lifetimes.
Discussion

Share a time you felt the Lord 'took something away' from you under a small pretext — and what He gave in its place.

BG 4.21

Lord Paraśurāma appeared in the family of Bhṛgu as a fire that burned to ashes the dynasty of Haihaya. Thus Lord Paraśurāma rid the earth of all kṣatriyas twenty-one times. The same Lord appeared as Rāmacandra, the husband of Sītādevī, and thus He killed the ten-headed Rāvaṇa, along with all the soldiers of Laṅkā. May that Śrī Rāma, whose glories destroy the contamination of the world, be always victorious.

Essence. The same Lord who annihilates adharma as Paraśurāma and Rāma is the shelter whose glories alone wash away the world's contamination.

Practical applications
  • Chant or recite one name of Rāma today as a deliberate act of taking shelter, not as background sound.
  • Notice one place where you tolerate adharma in yourself and resolve to cut it cleanly, the way Paraśurāma cuts kṣatriya pride.
  • Refuse to treat Kṛṣṇa's avatāras as separate gods — hold Paraśurāma, Rāma, and Kṛṣṇa as one Person in your remembrance.
Discussion

Where in modern life does 'real compassion' require the Paraśurāma-stroke rather than the soothing word, and who decides?

BG 4.22

To diminish the burden of the earth, the unborn Lord will take birth in the Yadu dynasty and perform feats impossible even for the demigods. Propounding speculative philosophy, the Lord, as Buddha, will bewilder the unworthy performers of Vedic sacrifices. And as Kalki the Lord will kill all the low-class men posing as rulers at the end of the Age of Kali.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa Himself descends in every age — as Yadu-prince, as Buddha, as Kalki — to lift the earth's burden; trust His timing and align with His mission.

Practical applications
  • Notice one place today where you are quietly waiting for some other rescuer, and consciously re-anchor that hope in Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse to dress up sense gratification in the language of dharma — name one ritual or habit where you have been doing exactly that.
  • Speak up once today, kindly and concretely, against a 'leader' or influencer whose conduct you have been excusing out of convenience.
Discussion

Share one area where you have personally seen 'Vedic-sounding' language used to justify gross sense gratification — and what made it hard to name.

BG 4.23

O mighty-armed King, there are innumerable appearances and activities of the Supreme Lord of the universe similar to those I have already mentioned. In fact, the glories of the Supreme Lord are unlimited.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's appearances and activities are unlimited; what we have heard is only a doorway into His ever-expanding glories.

Practical applications
  • Today, when you sit for reading, remind yourself you are entering an unlimited ocean, not finishing a book.
  • Refuse the urge to 'sum up' Kṛṣṇa in a tidy concept — keep one question about His līlā open as you go through the day.
  • Speak one specific glory of the Lord you have already heard to someone in your life, instead of waiting until you 'know more.'
Discussion

Share one pastime of Kṛṣṇa that has shaped you, and one you have quietly skipped over — what does the contrast reveal?
