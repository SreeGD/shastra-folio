Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.4.11

क्षुत्तृट्‌त्रिकालगुणमारुतजैह्वशैष्णा- नस्मानपारजलधीनतितीर्य केचित् । क्रोधस्य यान्ति विफलस्य वशं पदे गो- र्मज्जन्ति दुश्चरतपश्च वृथोत्सृजन्ति ॥ ११ ॥

Some men practice severe penances to cross beyond our influence, which is like an immeasurable ocean with endless waves of hunger, thirst, heat, cold and the other conditions brought about by the passing of time, such as the sensuous wind and the urges of the tongue and sex organs. Nevertheless, although crossing this ocean of sense gratification through severe penances, such persons foolishly drown in a cow’s hoofprint when conquered by useless anger. Thus they exhaust the benefit of their difficult austerities in vain.
Purport

Those who do not accept the devotional service of the Supreme Lord can be considered in two categories. Those engaged in sense gratification are easily conquered by the demigods through various weapons such as hunger, thirst, sexual desire, lamentation for the past and vain hoping for the future. Such materialistic fools, infatuated with the material world, are easily controlled by the demigods, who are the ultimate supplying agents of sense gratification. But according to Śrīdhara Svāmī, persons who attempt to subdue the desires of the material senses and thus avoid the control of the demigods without surrendering to the Supreme Lord are even more foolish than the sense gratifiers. Although crossing the ocean of sense gratification, those who perform severe penances without service to the Lord eventually drown in tiny puddles of anger. One who simply performs material penances does not actually purify his heart. By one’s material determination one may restrict the activities of the senses although one’s heart is still filled with material desires. The practical result of this is krodha, or anger. We have seen artificial performers of penance who have become very bitter and angry through denial of the senses. Being indifferent to the Supreme Lord, such persons do not achieve ultimate liberation, nor can they enjoy material sense gratification; rather, they become angry, and through cursing others or enjoying false pride they uselessly exhaust the results of their painful austerities. It is understood that when a yogī curses he diminishes the mystic power he has accumulated. Thus, anger gives neither liberation nor material sense gratification but merely burns up all the results of material penances and austerities. Being useless, such anger is compared to a useless puddle found in a cow’s hoofprint. Thus after crossing over the ocean of sense gratification the great yogīs who are indifferent to the Supreme Lord drown in puddles of anger. Although the demigods admit that the devotees of the Lord actually conquer the miseries of material life, it is understood here that a similar result is not to be obtained by so-called yogīs who are not interested in devotional service to the Supreme Lord.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Some men practice severe penances to cross beyond our influence, which is like an immeasurable ocean with hunger, thirst, heat, cold rain brought about by the passing of time, sensuous wind and the urges of the tongue and sex organs. Nevertheless such persons fall under the control of useless anger and drown in the water of a cow’s hoof print. Thus they destroy the benefit of their difficult austerities and can neither enjoy nor attain liberation. Those how perform austerities but do not worship you have two destinations. They become controlled by us, or by anger. Under our control, they pursue enjoyment of their desires. That is stated in this verse. Some persons surpass us, who are like a limitless ocean, bestowing hunger, thirst, heat, cold and rain arising from the qualities of past, present and future, and as well the wind of Malaya Hills which gives pleasure to the skin, and bestowing pleasure of the tongue and genital. But they drown in the water of a cow’s hoof print. Just as a person who is drowning and out of control gives up a treasure he has placed on his head, they destroy their difficult austerities by cursing in anger, and thus that austerity becomes useless for liberation or enjoyment.

Personal Guidance
Austerity without devotion to Kṛṣṇa breeds krodha that burns up all its fruit — the yogī crosses the ocean of senses only to drown in a hoofprint of anger.
Practical Applications
  • Notice the next moment you feel righteous anger after disciplining yourself, and recognize it as krodha draining your sādhana.
  • Offer every act of restraint — fasting, early rising, sense-control — to Kṛṣṇa explicitly, so the tapas does not feed your false pride.
  • Refuse to curse, insult, or sharply criticize anyone today, especially those who have disturbed your routine of discipline.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I conquering a large appetite only to be conquered by a small irritation, and what does that reveal about the purity of my motive?
  • Whose austerity am I performing — Kṛṣṇa's servant's, or an independent yogī's accumulating power to spend on anger?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When you finally resist scrolling or junk food and a friend teases you for being uptight, watch for the flash of contempt — that is the hoofprint puddle.
  • Before an exam fast or a study sprint, silently offer the effort to Kṛṣṇa so the discipline does not turn into superiority over classmates who indulge.
  • Refuse to post or DM anything sarcastic about peers who lack your discipline, even when you are clearly right.
householder
  • When your sādhana is interrupted by a child, spouse, or work crisis, treat the interruption itself as the test the tapas was preparing you for, not an enemy of it.
  • Audit your speech this week for the cutting tone you use with family members whose habits are less disciplined than yours.
  • Offer the fruits of your fasting, ekādaśī, and early rising to Kṛṣṇa at the household altar so they do not become ammunition in domestic arguments.
senior
  • Watch for the elder's particular krodha — disgust at how the young live — and recognize that it can drown decades of restraint in a single afternoon.
  • When mentoring, withhold the sharp correction you have earned the right to deliver, and offer the restraint itself to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Convert the energy of your remaining austerities from self-mastery into hearing, chanting, and serving devotees, so the tapas ends in bhakti rather than in cursing.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating sense-restraint as the goal rather than as a support for devotional service, so the heart stays full of material desire.
  • Mistaking the bitterness that comes from forced denial for spiritual seriousness or advancement.
  • Spending stored tapas on cursing, sarcasm, or contempt for less disciplined people, and calling it discrimination.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a time your discipline made you more irritable, not more peaceful — what was actually being purified, and what was not?
  • Is it possible to conquer lust and greed and still be spiritually ruined? Argue both sides from this verse.
  • What is the practical difference between a sādhaka restraining the senses for Kṛṣṇa and a yogī restraining them for self-mastery?
Role Play

Two roommates keep a strict morning sādhana. One skips his japa one day and apologizes; the other, who has not missed a day in months, erupts at him for ruining the household standard. Play out the confrontation, then replay it with the strict roommate aware that his anger is the cow's-hoofprint puddle threatening to drown his austerity.

Objections & Responses
  • Anger is just a human emotion — calling it the destroyer of austerity sounds extreme and shaming.
    The verse is not shaming feeling; it is naming a mechanism. Tapas concentrates a real energy, and krodha is the channel that spends it in seconds — cursing, contempt, false pride. Gauḍīya teachers point this out so the student protects what he has earned, not so he hates himself for having a temper.
  • Plenty of disciplined people without bhakti seem calm and accomplished, so devotion can't be that essential.
    Outward calm is cheap when conditions are favorable; this verse describes what happens under pressure, when restraint meets provocation. Without surrender to Kṛṣṇa the heart still holds material desire, and that desire eventually exits as anger or pride. Devotion is what actually purifies the heart that mere restraint only muzzles.
