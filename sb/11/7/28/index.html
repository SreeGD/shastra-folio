Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.28

You, however, although capable, learned, expert, handsome and most eloquent, are not engaged in doing anything, nor do you desire anything; rather, you appear stupefied and maddened as if you were a ghostly creature.
Purport

Ignorant persons often think that renounced spiritual life is meant for those who are impotent or homely or incompetent in practical worldly affairs. Sometimes foolish people say that religious life is a crutch for those who are not expert enough to achieve a high status in society. Therefore King Yadu has described the qualities of the mendicant brāhmaṇa in order to show that the brāhmaṇa has taken to renounced spiritual life in spite of great potential for worldly success. The avadhūta brāhmaṇa is described as being expert, learned, good-looking, eloquent and in every sense qualified to be a great material success. Still, the avadhūta has renounced material life and taken to Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Going back home, back to Godhead, for an eternal life of bliss and knowledge is the real work of a human being.

The followers of Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu simultaneously cultivate their own Kṛṣṇa consciousness and strenuously endeavor in missionary work to help others become Kṛṣṇa conscious. Often foolish persons deride the devotees by saying, “Why don’t you get a job?” They think that one who is sincerely endeavoring for spiritual enlightenment and who is also enlightening others is not doing anything practical. Foolish materialists will pay millions of dollars to extend their lives by a few weeks or months in a hospital, but they do not appreciate someone endeavoring for eternal life. There is no actual logic in material life. The act of trying to enjoy without Kṛṣṇa is in itself the culmination of irrationality, and thus we cannot expect to find anything ultimately rational or logical in a materialistic life devoid of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Many devotees of Kṛṣṇa come from wealthy, learned and influential families, and they take to Kṛṣṇa consciousness in order to perfect their lives, and certainly not due to lack of opportunity for material advancement. Although sometimes persons in material distress approach the Supreme Lord for help in material life, a real devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa voluntarily gives up all types of material enjoyment, knowing that nothing but love of Kṛṣṇa and service to His lotus feet are the actual perfection of life.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
You, however, although capable, learned, expert, handsome and most eloquent, are not engaged in doing anything, nor do you desire anything; rather, you appear stupefied and maddened as if haunted. You do not perform any actions. You do not desire anything. You are capable of doing things, and you have knowledge, skill and a good body. You can desire a beautiful wife. You can speak to anyone because you speak nicely. But you remain like a dumb person, not doing anything.

Personal Guidance
Renunciation is not impotence — the avadhūta is capable, learned, handsome and eloquent, yet acts and desires nothing because he lives for Kṛṣṇa alone.
Practical Applications
  • Today, refuse to measure your spiritual seriousness by how much worldly success you have given up versus how much you still could.
  • Identify one talent or qualification you secretly use to justify staying in material ambition, and offer that very capacity to Kṛṣṇa's service this week.
  • When someone asks "why don't you get a real job?" about your sādhana, answer plainly without defensiveness — your work is going back to Godhead.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I quietly assuming that renunciation is for those who could not make it materially, and how does that assumption protect my own attachments?
  • If I am capable, learned, and presentable, what exactly am I still trying to achieve with those gifts that the avadhūta has already laid down?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When classmates rank each other by placements, grades, or follower counts, refuse internally to let that scoreboard define what a successful life looks like for you.
  • Pick one capability you are proud of — debating, coding, looks, athletics — and consciously use it this week in some seva rather than only for self-promotion.
  • When friends mock a devotee peer as "wasting their potential," do not laugh along; quietly note what assumption about life that joke depends on.
householder
  • In the next big family decision about job, house, or schooling, name out loud whether the goal is material advancement or supporting everyone's path back to Kṛṣṇa, and let that naming shape the choice.
  • Stop apologizing to relatives for the time, money, and energy you give to sādhana and devotional service — present it as the most rational use of a capable adult's life, not as an indulgence.
  • Teach your children, by example, that your professional competence exists to serve Kṛṣṇa and His devotees, not as the family's ultimate identity.
senior
  • Review the resume of your life — degrees, positions, accomplishments — and consciously hand each item to Kṛṣṇa, refusing to let it remain a private trophy.
  • When younger people seek your mentorship, do not only coach them toward worldly success; share frankly that eternal life is the real work of a human being.
  • Resist the urge in this stage to prove you are still relevant by material standards; let your visible "doing less" be filled inwardly with japa, hearing, and remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking outward inactivity or eccentricity for the avadhūta's inner state, and cultivating the look of detachment without its substance.
  • Treating renunciation as a consolation prize for worldly failure, so that bhakti becomes a fallback rather than the deliberate choice of someone with options.
  • Using "I am not the doer" as cover for laziness or neglect of duty, instead of the avadhūta's fully Kṛṣṇa-centered non-doership.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a moment when you or someone close to you dismissed a devotee as escapist — what assumption about success was driving that judgment, and was it ever challenged?
  • The verse praises the brāhmaṇa as kalpa, kavi, dakṣa, su-bhaga, amṛta-bhāṣaṇa — which of these five would be hardest for you personally to lay at Kṛṣṇa's feet, and why?
  • Where do we as a group risk romanticizing the "ghostly, maddened" outward appearance of the avadhūta while avoiding the inner discipline it actually requires?
Role Play

A college student devotee is home for the holidays. An accomplished uncle, seeing the student's grades and abilities, corners them at dinner and says, "You are clearly capable — this Kṛṣṇa thing is fine as a hobby, but don't throw your career away like some confused dropout." One student plays the uncle pressing hard on practical concerns (money, marriage, parents' old age), another plays the devotee responding from the spirit of SB 11.7.28 — neither dismissive of the uncle nor apologetic about bhakti.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like spiritualized laziness — glorifying someone who is capable but chooses to do nothing and want nothing is a recipe for parasitism on society.
    Prabhupāda directly addresses this: the avadhūta has not stopped acting, he has stopped acting for material enjoyment. The followers of Caitanya Mahāprabhu cultivate Kṛṣṇa consciousness and strenuously work to share it — that is real action for the eternal welfare of others, not idleness.
  • Why valorize renunciation at all? Surely a capable person serves the world better by using their gifts in a career, family, and civic life.
    The verse does not condemn capable engagement; it exposes the assumption that material success is the highest use of capability. Foolish materialists pay millions to extend life by weeks, while ridiculing one who endeavors for eternal life. The avadhūta has simply seen which investment actually matures.
