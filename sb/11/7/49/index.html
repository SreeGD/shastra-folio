Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.49

कालेन ह्योघवेगेन भूतानां प्रभवाप्ययौ । नित्यावपि न द‍ृश्येते आत्मनोऽग्नेर्यथार्चिषाम् ॥ ४९ ॥

The flames of a fire appear and disappear at every moment, and yet this creation and destruction is not noticed by the ordinary observer. Similarly, the mighty waves of time flow constantly, like the powerful currents of a river, and imperceptibly cause the birth, growth and death of innumerable material bodies. And yet the soul, who is thus constantly forced to change his position, cannot perceive the actions of time.
Purport

The brāhmaṇa avadhūta instructing King Yadu again gives the example of fire after having already proceeded to the example of the moon. This analytic method is called siṁhāvalokana, or “the lion’s glance,” by which one simultaneously proceeds forward and casts backward glances to see if anything has been overlooked. Thus the sage proceeds with his analysis but returns to the example of fire to illustrate the need for renunciation. The material body is certainly an ephemeral and phantasmagorical manifestation of the Lord’s external potency. The flames of a fire constantly take birth and disappear, yet we perceive the fire as a continuous reality. Similarly, the soul is a continuous reality, although his material bodies appear and disappear constantly, by the influence of time. It is said that the most astonishing thing is that no one thinks that they will die. Because the soul is eternal, the living entity is prone to accept any fleeting situation as permanent, forgetting that his eternal nature can be truly experienced only in the eternal atmosphere of the spiritual sky. If one is convinced of this fact, he develops the quality of vairāgya, or detachment from material illusion.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Because of time, which has the speed of the wind, the constant birth and death of living entities related to oneself are not seen, just as the appearance and disappearance of flames in fire are not seen. He learns renunciation from fire. The order is reversed, and again fire is explained, in the manner of siṁhāvalokana (a lion glancing back). One does not see the birth and death of beings related to oneself (ātmanaḥ). Arciṣām means “of flames.”

Personal Guidance
Time silently swaps your body moment by moment like flickering flames; the soul endures, so cultivate vairāgya before the next change arrives.
Practical Applications
  • Watch a candle flame for one minute today and notice how each flicker is a new flame — then apply the same lens to your aging body.
  • Refuse to call any current arrangement of health, looks, or relationships 'permanent' — name one such illusion you have been quietly banking on.
  • Before sleep, formally acknowledge that the body that woke up this morning is not the body lying down tonight, and offer the day to Kṛṣṇa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I treating a phantasmagorical, ephemeral situation as if it were the eternal atmosphere my soul actually belongs to?
  • What would change in how I plan my next five years if I truly accepted that time is already eroding this body unnoticed?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Scroll your camera roll back five years and notice that the person in those photos no longer biologically exists — let that puncture the illusion that your current body is 'you'.
  • Refuse the social-media bargain that says your worth is locked to how this particular face and physique look this particular year.
  • Pick one exam result, breakup, or follower count you are treating as life-defining, and ask honestly whether the soul who sat in this seat ten years ago would even recognize the stakes.
householder
  • Look at your children today and consciously register that the toddler version is already gone — let that grief become attentiveness rather than clinging.
  • Review one financial or career plan built on the assumption that your body will keep performing at this level for decades, and rebuild it with mortality factored in.
  • Speak with your spouse this week about the fact that the bodies you married are not the bodies you now share — and recommit to the souls instead.
senior
  • When you catch yourself mourning a capacity you have lost, name it as a flame that has flickered out and thank Kṛṣṇa that the witness remains.
  • Choose one possession, role, or identity you have been gripping past its season, and deliberately begin loosening your hand this month.
  • Sit with a younger family member and tell them, without melodrama, what it actually feels like to watch the body change — give them the siṁhāvalokana glance you wish someone had given you.
Common Pitfalls
  • Turning the teaching into morbid fatalism — sulking about death instead of letting it sharpen detachment and devotion.
  • Using 'the body is temporary' as an excuse to neglect duties, health, or dependents whom Kṛṣṇa has placed in your care.
  • Intellectually agreeing the body changes while emotionally still negotiating with the mirror, the scale, and the calendar as if they were permanent.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one area of life where you have noticed time's current actively flowing — and one area where you are still pretending the river is frozen.
  • Is vairāgya something you cultivate by withdrawing from the world, or by staying in it with clearer eyes? Defend your position to someone who disagrees.
  • The verse says the soul itself cannot perceive time's action on it — what practices in our tradition are designed precisely to break that blindness?
Role Play

A student has just been told by a doctor that a parent has six months to live. A classmate, trying to comfort them, says 'don't think about it, stay positive.' One student plays the grieving child, another plays a fellow devotee who must respond from SB 11.7.49 — not with platitudes, but by helping them see the flame-like nature of the body without minimizing the love.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like denial dressed up as philosophy — telling people their bodies don't really matter is how religions have always justified ignoring suffering.
    The verse is not denying the body's reality; it is locating it correctly. The flame is real while it burns — but mistaking one flame for a permanent object is what causes suffering. Real compassion serves the soul precisely because we have stopped lying to it about the body.
  • Modern science says I am my brain and body — when they end, I end. The 'eternal soul' is just a story to soften that fact.
    Notice that even the materialist position depends on a continuous 'I' that remembers being a child, a teenager, an adult — yet the cells, the brain, the body of each stage are gone. The verse points exactly at this: something witnesses the flames without being any one flame. That witness is what Kṛṣṇa calls the ātmā.
