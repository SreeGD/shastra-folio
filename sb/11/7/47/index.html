Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.47

स्वमायया सृष्टमिदं सदसल्ल‍क्षणं विभु: । प्रविष्ट ईयते तत्तत्स्वरूपोऽग्निरिवैधसि ॥ ४७ ॥

Just as fire manifests differently in pieces of wood of different sizes and qualities, the omnipotent Supreme Soul, having entered the bodies of higher and lower life forms created by His own potency, appears to assume the identity of each.
Purport

Although the Supreme Lord is within everything, everything is not the Lord. By the mode of goodness the Lord creates the exalted material bodies of demigods and brāhmaṇas, and by expanding the mode of ignorance He similarly creates the bodies of animals, śūdras and other lower forms of life. The Lord enters all of these superior and inferior creations, but He remains vibhu, the all-powerful Personality of Godhead. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura explains that although fire is present within smoldering wood, it blazes forth when we stir the wood around. Similarly, although the Personality of Godhead is indirectly present everywhere, when we chant and hear His glories with love and devotion the Lord is stirred into manifestation and directly appears before His devotees.

The foolish conditioned souls ignore the spectacular presence of the Lord within everything and instead absorb their mediocre consciousness in their own temporary material coverings, thinking, “I am a strong man,” “I am a beautiful woman,” “I am the richest man in this city,” “I am a Ph.D.,” and so on. One should cut off such useless entanglement and accept the fact that one is pure spirit soul, the eternal, blissful servant of Lord Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Just as fire enters and manifests in pieces of wood of different sizes and qualities, the Supreme Soul, having entered the universe with its higher and lower life forms created by his own potency, manifests his form there. Just as fire enters wood and manifests itself by rubbing, the Lord enters the universe and manifests himself by repeated hearing and chanting.
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa enters every body like fire in wood of different grades — He appears varied, yet remains vibhu; stir Him out by hearing and chanting.
Practical Applications
  • Notice each time today you identify with a body-label — "smart," "tired," "attractive," "older" — and silently re-anchor as eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Stir the smoldering fire: chant Hare Kṛṣṇa aloud and hear it back, treating japa as the act that makes the Lord blaze forth from within.
  • Refuse to rank the people you meet today by the "wood" of their bodies — speak to each one as a covering of the same Supreme Soul.
Reflection Questions
  • Which material covering — strength, beauty, income, credential — am I most quietly mistaking for my actual self, and what does that cost my bhakti?
  • Where in my day is Kṛṣṇa present but dormant in me like fire in unstirred wood, waiting for hearing and chanting to call Him forth?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When your feed tempts you to rate yourself by looks, grades, or follower count, name that as "wood" and chant one round of japa before scrolling further.
  • In your friend group today, catch the moment you size someone up by their college, body, or family income, and silently greet the soul inside that covering instead.
  • Refuse the campus story that you are your résumé — write tomorrow's to-do list with one item that serves Kṛṣṇa directly, not your future profile.
householder
  • When you introduce yourself at work or at a family gathering, notice how quickly you reach for job title, salary, or your children's achievements, and inwardly re-introduce yourself as a servant of Kṛṣṇa.
  • In a disagreement with your spouse or child tonight, pause before reacting and remember the same Lord has entered their body too — let that shape your tone, not just your words.
  • Stir the fire at home: read one verse aloud with the family and hear it together, so the Lord's presence in the household becomes direct, not merely indirect.
senior
  • As the body weakens, treat each new limitation as proof that you were never the wood — and use the freed attention for longer, more attentive japa.
  • When younger relatives defer to you for your status or wealth, redirect: speak about the Lord within, not about your achievements, so your legacy becomes hearing and chanting rather than résumé.
  • Loosen your grip on one possession or role this week — give it away or hand it off — as a practical admission that the vibhu, not the body's holdings, is your real shelter.
Common Pitfalls
  • Sliding from "He is within everything" into "everything is Him" — collapsing the personal Lord into an impersonal pervasion.
  • Using the fire-in-wood image to flatten distinctions, ignoring that sattva and tamas really do shape what kind of body and life one receives.
  • Treating the Lord's indirect presence as enough, and never doing the stirring — the chanting and hearing — that makes Him manifest directly.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one body-based identity you each still cling to — and test whether the group hears it as your real self or as the wood around the fire.
  • Fire blazes when wood is stirred; what specifically stirs Kṛṣṇa into manifestation for you, and where does the group disagree on what counts as real stirring?
  • If Kṛṣṇa equally enters the demigod's body and the animal's body, what does that do to how we rank people — and where do we still secretly rank them?
Role Play

One student plays a successful professional introducing themselves entirely through credentials, salary, and titles. A second student, as a Gauḍīya friend, must respond using the fire-in-wood image — without insulting the wood — to point to the vibhu within, and steer the conversation toward hearing and chanting. The group then debates whether the second student honored the person or only their covering.

Objections & Responses
  • If the Supreme Soul is equally in everyone, then all paths and identities are equal and your tradition's exclusivity is arrogant.
    Fire is in every piece of wood, but only stirred wood blazes — the Lord is indirectly in all, yet directly manifest only where He is heard and chanted with love. The tradition is not ranking souls; it is pointing to the specific stirring that lets the same Lord, present in everyone, actually appear.
  • This sounds like a pious way to ignore real bodily suffering — "you are not the body, so your pain doesn't matter."
    The verse does not deny the body; it denies that the body is you. Real compassion means serving the soul inside the suffering body — feeding it kṛṣṇa-prasāda, giving it the holy name, treating it as an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa — not merely soothing the wood while the fire within stays unstirred.
