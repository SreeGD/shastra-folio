Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.7.21

पुरुषत्वे च मां धीरा: साङ्ख्ययोगविशारदा: । आविस्तरां प्रपश्यन्ति सर्वशक्त्युपबृंहितम् ॥ २१ ॥

In the human form of life, those who are self-controlled and expert in the spiritual science of Sāṅkhya can directly see Me along with all of My potencies.
Purport

We find the following statement in the Vedas: puruṣatve cāvistarām ātmā sahita-prajṣānena sampanna-tamo vijṣātaṁ vadati vijṣātaṁ paśyati veda śvastanaṁ veda lokālokau martyenāmṛtam īpsaty evaṁ sampanno ’thetareṣāṁ paśūnām āsanā-pipāse evābhijṣānam. “In the human form of life, the soul is fully endowed with intelligence to understand spiritual knowledge. Thus, in human life the soul may speak realized knowledge, see the truth, know the future and also understand the reality both of this world and of the next. Taking advantage of the experience of mortal life, the soul in human form may endeavor for immortality, and the human body is fully equipped to achieve this end. In such an elevated state the soul is certainly well acquainted with the ordinary activities of animals, such as eating and drinking.”

The human form of life ( puruṣatve ) is very significant because it affords us the opportunity to perfect our existence. The Sāṅkhya system mentioned here is best illustrated by the instructions of Lord Kapila to His mother, Devahūti. Lord Kapila is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and His mother approached Him, saying:

nirviṇṇā nitarāṁ bhūmann asad-indriya-tarṣaṇāt yena sambhāvyamānena prapannāndhaṁ tamaḥ prabho

“I am very sick of the disturbance caused by my material senses, for because of this sense disturbance, my Lord, I have fallen into the abyss of ignorance.” ( Bhāg. 3.25.7 ) Lord Kapila delivered to His mother a highly analytical summary of all material and spiritual reality. It is significant that Lord Kapila did not discriminate against His mother because she was a woman, nor did He consider her incapable of understanding the most elaborate spiritual knowledge. Thus, in a Kṛṣṇa conscious society of liberated souls, any man, woman or child may become a great devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa without discrimination. It is also significant that in the highly intellectual Sāṅkhya system enunciated by Lord Kapila, the solid conclusion is surrender to the lotus feet of the pure devotees and love of Godhead. In Lord Kapila’s instructions in the Third Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, He emphasizes the need to take shelter of the pure devotees of the Lord. In the present verse Lord Kṛṣṇa says, sāṅkhya-yoga-viśāradāḥ: those who are expert in taking shelter of the pure devotees and who can thus understand the actual situation of this world are able to see Kṛṣṇa in His personal form, along with His internal and external potencies.

A spiritual master becomes bona fide by his full surrender to his spiritual master; but as explained in this chapter, one may also act as his own guru. This means that an intelligent and perceptive person can understand the nature of this world and his own limitations. Such a person then becomes very much inclined to associate with the pure devotees of the Lord and to receive the mercy of those who are advanced in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. According to Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, sāṅkhya-yoga, as described here, refers to the rigorous intellectual progress of the jṣāna-yoga system, along with the devotion of bhakti-yoga, which emphasizes the mercy of the lotus feet of the pure devotees.

Actually, jṣāna-yoga is a factor in the bhakti-yoga system, since Kṛṣṇa is jṣāna-gamya, or the goal of all knowledge. The Lord also says in Bhagavad-gītā (10.10) that He personally enlightens a sincere devotee with all knowledge. In this chapter, Lord Kṛṣṇa is training Uddhava to see the Lord’s personal form in the course of his daily experiences in the material world. The Lord has already indicated to Uddhava that he will travel throughout the world in samādhi, or trance, and now Lord Kṛṣṇa is preparing Uddhava to travel as a true sannyāsī seeing the Personality of Godhead everywhere.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In the human form of life, those who are without envy and are expert in jṣāna and bhakti can directly see me endowed with all of my energies. This verse indicates that those who have attained human life can generally know me. Those who are without envy (dhīrāḥ), who are expert in jṣāna and bhakti (sāṅkhyam), see me. Śruti says puruṣatve cāvistarām ātmā sa hi prajṣānena sampannatamo vijṣātaṁ paśyati, veda śvastanaṁ veda lokālokau martyenāmṛtam īpsaty evaṁ sampanno’thetareṣāṁ paśūnām aśanāpipāse evābhivijṣānam A person develops knowledge of ātmā in the human form. Endowed with intelligence he sees what he has known. He knows the future, he knows heaven and hell, and he desires immorality through his mortal body. In this way he is endowed. Others, animals, know only eating and drinking. Aitareya-āraṇyaka 2.3.2.4 -5

Personal Guidance
The human form is the rare chance to actually see Kṛṣṇa with all His potencies — use it for Sāṅkhya-bhakti under pure devotees, not for animal concerns.
Practical Applications
  • List the activities of your day that an animal also performs (eating, sleeping, mating, defending) and identify the one slot you will reclaim today for hearing about Kṛṣṇa.
  • Approach one advanced devotee this week with a specific question about your material entanglement, in the mood of Devahūti approaching Lord Kapila.
  • Refuse the impersonalist shortcut in your study — wherever you read 'the Self' or 'consciousness,' deliberately substitute the personal name and form of Kṛṣṇa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I using my human intelligence merely to refine animal pursuits rather than to see Kṛṣṇa along with His potencies?
  • Whose shelter am I actually taking when difficulty comes — my own analysis, or a pure devotee in the line of Kapila?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit your phone's screen-time report this week and name which categories are simply digitized versions of eating, sleeping, mating, and defending — then cap the largest one.
  • Pick one professor, senior, or devotee you respect and ask them a question you would never type into a search engine, in the spirit of Devahūti approaching Kapila.
  • When friends frame ambition purely as career and relationships, refuse the script and say plainly that you want a human life that ends in seeing Kṛṣṇa, not in a better job title.
householder
  • In the next family decision about money, schooling, or career, openly weigh it against this question: does it protect or erode our chance to use this human form for Kṛṣṇa?
  • Teach your children the personal name and form of Kṛṣṇa before any abstract 'God' or 'universe' language colonizes their vocabulary, as Kapila taught Devahūti directly.
  • Identify one senior devotee couple or elder and commit to a regular association with them, so that your household has a living link to the paramparā, not only books.
senior
  • Use the loosening grip of bodily strength as evidence that puruṣatve is finite — and reorganize your week so hearing and chanting about Kṛṣṇa get the prime hours, not the leftovers.
  • Identify one younger seeker and offer them what Kapila offered Devahūti: serious, undiluted instruction, without assuming they cannot handle it because of age, gender, or background.
  • Begin loosening attachments to property, position, and even your own opinions, so that when the body falls, the soul departs already practiced in taking shelter of pure devotees.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating Sāṅkhya as a dry intellectual exercise of counting tattvas while skipping its conclusion: surrender at the lotus feet of pure devotees.
  • Imagining that 'seeing Kṛṣṇa everywhere' means a vague pantheistic feeling rather than perceiving the personal Lord with His internal and external potencies.
  • Claiming the verse's permission to act as one's own guru as license to bypass association with advanced Vaiṣṇavas.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one concrete way your daily schedule still looks more like an animal's than a dhīra's — and what would have to change for it to shift.
  • Lord Kapila taught His own mother the full Sāṅkhya. Where in your life have you withheld the highest teaching from someone close, assuming they couldn't receive it?
  • Debate honestly: can a sincere person today truly 'act as his own guru' as the purport suggests, or does that almost always become self-deception?
Role Play

A college-educated friend tells you he has decided to pursue Vedānta purely through books and reasoning — no guru, no devotees, no temple — because his intelligence is sufficient. Drawing on this verse and Devahūti's approach to Kapila, show him in conversation why sāṅkhya-yoga-viśāradāḥ ends at the lotus feet of pure devotees, without dismissing his intellectual seriousness.

Objections & Responses
  • Animals also seem aware and intelligent in their own way — calling the human form uniquely capable of seeing God sounds like speciesist religious flattery.
    The verse and its Vedic citation do not deny animal awareness; they specify that only in puruṣatve is the soul equipped to speak realized knowledge, know past and future, and endeavor for immortality. Animals share eating and drinking with us; what they cannot do is inquire into Kṛṣṇa with all His potencies. Honoring this difference is not contempt for animals but refusal to waste our one chance.
  • If Kṛṣṇa can be directly seen by the self-controlled, why do so many lifelong practitioners report no such vision?
    The verse pairs dhīrāḥ with sāṅkhya-yoga-viśāradāḥ — self-control plus expertise in taking shelter of pure devotees. Where vision is absent, usually one leg is missing: either the senses still run the show, or there is no real submission to an advanced Vaiṣṇava. Kṛṣṇa is sat — the lack is on our side, and the remedy is named in the purport, not in our complaint.
