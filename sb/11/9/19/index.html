Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.9.19

केवलात्मानुभावेन स्वमायां त्रिगुणात्मिकाम् । सङ्क्षोभयन् सृजत्यादौ तया सूत्रमरिन्दम ॥ १९ ॥

arindama
O subduer of the enemies.
O subduer of the enemies, at the time of creation the Personality of Godhead expands His own transcendental potency in the form of time, and agitating His material energy, māyā, composed of the three modes of material nature, He creates the mahat-tattva.
Purport

The word kevala means “pure” and indicates that the Lord’s kālaśakti, or time potency, is a transcendental energy nondifferent from His personal body. The brāhmaṇa addresses King Yadu here as arindama, subduer of the enemies. This indicates that although the topic of māyā, or illusory creation, is being discussed, the King need not worry, because as a staunch devotee of the Lord, he is able to subdue the real enemies of life, namely lust, anger and greed, which make one a prisoner in māyā’s kingdom. The word sūtram indicates the mahat-tattva, on which many material creations rest, just like jewels rest on a thread. In the state of pradhāna, or material equilibrium, the modes of nature do not interact. In the Third Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Lord Kapila explains in His Sāṅkhya teachings that the Supreme Personality of Godhead agitates the neutral state of nature and thus creation takes place. The created manifest form of nature in which fruitive activities are stimulated is called mahat-tattva, as indicated in this verse.

If one tries to renounce the illusory creation of the Lord by taking shelter of impersonal Vedānta philosophy, thus artificially equating the infinite consciousness of the Lord and the infinitesimal consciousness of the conditioned soul, one’s analysis will fall far short of reality. The word sva-māyām in this verse indicates that the illusory potency that covers the conditioned souls is always subordinate to the Lord, whose consciousness is infallible and infinite and who is always a person.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O subduer of the enemies! At the time of creation the Lord by the power of his cit-śakti agitates prakṛti made of the guṇas and creates mahat-tattva (sūtra). Having shown destruction, the brāhmaṇa now shows creation. By the power of his cit-śakti he awakes pradhāna (sva-māyām), agitating it by his glance, and creates the mahat-tattva (sūtram) predominated by kriyā-śakti.

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa alone, by His pure presence through time, agitates His own māyā of three guṇas to create — so the cure for illusion is taking shelter of Him personally.
Practical Applications
  • Name today the three guṇa-currents pulling you — a tamas drift, a rajas chase, a sattva pretension — and offer each one to Kṛṣṇa instead of fighting it alone.
  • When you feel trapped by circumstances, remember the brāhmaṇa called the King arindama and act as a subduer of the inner enemies — lust, anger, greed — by chanting and reading even five minutes more than yesterday.
  • Refuse the impersonalist temptation today: when your mind says 'I am consciousness,' correct it to 'I am Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant,' and speak to Him as a person in your prayers.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I still treating māyā as an autonomous force I must defeat by my own will, rather than as Kṛṣṇa's energy that yields only to His shelter?
  • Which of the three guṇas is currently dressing itself up as my 'spiritual life,' and what would it cost me to see through that costume?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before opening social media today, name aloud which guṇa you are about to feed — restless rajas, numbing tamas, or self-image sattva — and decide consciously whether to proceed.
  • When peer pressure or exam stress makes you feel powerless, recall arindama: spend ten minutes chanting or reading Bhagavad-gītā as your actual answer to anxiety, not as an add-on after.
  • When a professor or friend insists 'all paths lead to the same impersonal truth,' politely hold your ground that the source of creation is a Person, and study one verse this week that helps you articulate why.
householder
  • Audit one family decision this week — a purchase, a school choice, a vacation — and ask which guṇa is really driving it, then bring the decision into your morning prayer before finalizing.
  • When marriage or parenting friction rises, stop treating your spouse or child as the enemy and remember the real enemies named in the purport — lust, anger, greed in yourself — and subdue one of them today.
  • Lead a short family reading after dinner this week on Kṛṣṇa as the personal source of creation, so your children inherit a personalist frame before the impersonalist culture installs its own.
senior
  • Look back on the bodies and roles you have already passed through and recognize how each was a configuration of the three guṇas — then sit with the relief that the Person behind them all is still available to you now.
  • Loosen one attachment this month — a possession, a grievance, a role — by formally offering it to Kṛṣṇa, treating the offering as more real than the holding.
  • Mentor a younger devotee or family member specifically on the personal nature of Kṛṣṇa, so that your legacy is not vague spirituality but a clear handoff of the personalist line.
Common Pitfalls
  • Hearing that māyā is Kṛṣṇa's energy and concluding that illusion is therefore harmless or even desirable, instead of recognizing it as a prison one must be rescued from.
  • Reading kevala-ātmā-anubhāva as proof of an impersonal Absolute and quietly demoting Kṛṣṇa to a symbol of 'pure consciousness' rather than the supreme Person.
  • Using the language of guṇas to diagnose everyone else's conditioning while never auditing one's own dominant mode in food, speech, work, and entertainment.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one area of your life where you have been trying to subdue māyā by willpower alone — what would it actually look like to hand that struggle to Kṛṣṇa instead?
  • The brāhmaṇa addresses the King as arindama precisely while teaching about illusion — discuss whether you experience yourself more as māyā's prisoner or as a subduer of inner enemies, and why.
  • Where in contemporary spirituality have you seen impersonal Vedānta framing creep in, and how would you lovingly correct it in yourself or a friend without becoming sectarian?
Role Play

One student plays a sincere seeker who has just returned from a silent retreat convinced that 'pure awareness' is the highest truth and that Kṛṣṇa is a useful but optional symbol; another plays a Gauḍīya friend who must, over a cup of tea, gently use SB 11.9.19 to show that the very potency creating the three guṇas is subordinate to a Person, without sounding dismissive of the seeker's experience.

Objections & Responses
  • If everything is Kṛṣṇa's energy, including māyā, then surrendering or not surrendering is also just His energy moving — why bother with sādhana?
    The verse says He agitates māyā by His own potency; it does not say He removes your tiny independence as a soul. That spark of choice is precisely where sādhana lives — you cannot create or destroy māyā, but you can turn toward the Person who governs it, and that turning is the one move that is genuinely yours to offer.
  • Modern cosmology explains creation through physics — invoking a personal God agitating three modes feels like pre-scientific mythology.
    The verse is not competing with mechanism; it is naming the agent. Physics describes how the threads of the mahat-tattva weave, but cannot say why there is weaving at all or why consciousness witnesses it. The Bhāgavatam answers that prior question — that a conscious Person, through His time potency, is the upstream cause — and a serious student can hold both without confusion.
