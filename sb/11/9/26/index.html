Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.9.26

जायात्मजार्थपशुभृत्यगृहाप्तवर्गान् पुष्णाति यत्प्रियचिकीर्षया वितन्वन् । स्वान्ते सकृच्छ्रमवरुद्धधन: स देह: सृष्ट्वास्य बीजमवसीदति वृक्षधर्म: ॥ २६ ॥

A man attached to the body accumulates money with great struggle to expand and protect the position of his wife, children, property, domestic animals, servants, homes, relatives, friends, and so on. He does all this for the gratification of his own body. As a tree before dying produces the seed of a future tree, the dying body manifests the seed of one’s next material body in the form of one’s accumulated karma. Thus assuring the continuation of material existence, the material body sinks down and dies.
Purport

One might argue, “Among all the gurus mentioned thus far, the material body is certainly the best, since it awards the detachment and fine intelligence that enable one to engage in the devotional service of the Lord. Thus, we should serve the body, although it is temporary, with great attachment, or risk the offense of ungratefulness. How can detachment from the body be recommended when the body is endowed with so many wonderful qualities?” The answer is given in this verse. The body does not award detachment and knowledge in the manner of some benevolent teacher; rather, it causes so much pain and misery that any commonsense person cannot help being convinced of the uselessness of material life. Just as a tree produces the seeds of the next tree and then dies, the body’s lusty desires induce the conditioned soul to create a further chain of karma. Finally the body, having paved the way for unlimited suffering in material existence, drops dead.

According to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, deha indicates both the gross body and the subtle, mental body. Those who do not clearly understand the difference between body and soul falsely think that body and soul are identical and that one can find perfect happiness in bodily sense gratification. But those who foolishly accept the temporary body as all-important cannot be compared with self-realized souls who intelligently understand the superiority of the eternal soul.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
A person with a desire to please the body expands himself and nourishes wife, children, money, animals, servants, house and relatives. In the end, the body, with its accumulating wealth, then dies with difficulty after creating karma for another body, like a tree which produces seeds and then dies. “The body, the best among all the gurus, giving bhakti-yoga, detachment and discrimination, though temporary, should be engaged in service with the greatest attachment. To do otherwise would be sign of ingratitude. Then why do you say you are unattached to the body?” That is true. But the body is an amazing guru since, served with great attachment, it does not at all teach discrimination and detachment. Rather the body then throws a person into the huge blind well of saṁsāra. This is expressed in two verses. By wanting to please his body, a person nourishes a wife, children, money etc. That body, with its accumulated money, becomes devoid of the wealth of discrimination. At the end of life, the body is destroyed while enduring great difficulties. It creates karma, the seed of the next body, by which the current of repeated birth takes place. It is like a tree which produces seeds and dies.

Personal Guidance
The body endlessly drives you to acquire and protect family, wealth, and possessions, sowing the seed of the next birth before it dies — see this pattern and step out of it.
Practical Applications
  • List the people and possessions you are currently straining to maintain, and ask which of these struggles are truly for Kṛṣṇa and which are bodily self-extension.
  • Refuse one act of needless accumulation today — a purchase, a status move, an extra commitment — and offer the freed energy to sādhana.
  • When you exhaust yourself "providing" for family or work, pause and rename the activity honestly: is this seva, or is this the body sowing its next seed?
Reflection Questions
  • Which struggles in my life look like duty but are actually the body planting the bīja of its next chain of karma?
  • If this body dropped tonight, what "seed" of attachments would it leave behind to grow into my next entanglement?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice when you are curating your social media, grades, or friend group mainly to expand and protect a self-image — and cut one such effort this week.
  • Before spending on the next gadget, outfit, or trip "everyone is doing," ask plainly: am I serving the soul or feeding the body's seed?
  • Pick one relationship — a friend group, a romance, a rivalry — and honestly check whether you are maintaining it for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure or for bodily belonging.
householder
  • Review your last three big financial or career decisions and mark which were genuine maintenance of dependents and which were bodily expansion dressed as duty.
  • Set a clear ceiling on house, lifestyle, and schooling upgrades this year, and direct surplus income and energy into sādhana, service, and giving.
  • When you feel the familiar "I must provide more" anxiety, stop and offer that exact moment to Kṛṣṇa before acting, so the work is done as seva, not as seed-sowing.
senior
  • Walk through your home and holdings and identify what you are still anxiously "protecting" — and begin handing over, simplifying, or giving away while you can.
  • Speak honestly with your children and grandchildren about death and the soul, instead of pouring your remaining strength only into securing their bodily future.
  • Each evening, take stock of the bījas you are still planting — grudges, ambitions, possessions — and consciously offer them at Kṛṣṇa's feet before sleep.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using this verse to neglect genuine responsibilities to dependents, mistaking irresponsibility for vairāgya.
  • Despising the body as an enemy, instead of seeing it soberly as a temporary instrument that must be engaged in bhakti.
  • Treating wife, children, and home as inherently bad, rather than examining the attached, body-centered motive with which one maintains them.
Discussion Prompts
  • Where in your own life do you most clearly see yourself "expanding and protecting" a circle around the body — and what would change if you saw that circle as a seed of future bondage?
  • Is it possible to maintain a family, career, and home without sowing the bīja described here? What concretely distinguishes such a householder from the one in this verse?
  • The purport says the body teaches by causing misery, not by benevolence. Share an experience where bodily suffering actually pushed you toward Kṛṣṇa — and one where it only pushed you toward more arrangement.
Role Play

A working householder has just received a large bonus. One classmate plays the householder, excitedly planning a bigger house, a second car, and a private school upgrade "for the family." Another plays a Gauḍīya friend who has read SB 11.9.26 that morning. Without moralizing, the friend must help the householder examine which of these plans are genuine duty and which are the body sowing its next seed — and propose at least one concrete adjustment to the use of the bonus.

Objections & Responses
  • Providing well for my wife, children, and parents is my dharma — calling it the body's seed-sowing sounds like an excuse to abandon them.
    The verse does not forbid maintenance; it exposes the motive. A Gauḍīya householder maintains dependents as Kṛṣṇa's service, with regulated effort and detachment from fruits. What this verse condemns is the endless, anxious expansion "for the gratification of his own body" — accumulating far beyond need, identifying with the circle as "mine," and calling that dharma. Do your duty; refuse the bodily inflation around it.
  • This sounds like a depressing, world-denying view — work hard, love your family, and you're just growing a karmic tree. Why bother living?
    The verse is not anti-life; it is anti-illusion. It diagnoses why an honest, hardworking person still ends up exhausted and reborn: the whole project was centered on the body. Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism offers the alternative — the same work, the same family, the same effort, but reoriented as offering to Kṛṣṇa. Then the tree that grows from this body is not another cage but a creeper of bhakti.
