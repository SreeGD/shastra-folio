Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.9.22

यत्र यत्र मनो देही धारयेत् सकलं धिया । स्‍नेहाद् द्वेषाद् भयाद् वापि याति तत्तत्स्वरूपताम् ॥ २२ ॥

If out of love, hate or fear an embodied soul fixes his mind with intelligence and complete concentration upon a particular bodily form, he will certainly attain the form that he is meditating upon.
Purport

From this verse it is not hard to understand that if one constantly meditates upon the Supreme Personality of Godhead, one will achieve a spiritual body just like that of the Lord. The word dhiyā, “with intelligence,” indicates complete intellectual conviction in a particular understanding, and similarly the word sakalam indicates one-pointed attention of the mind. With such complete absorption of consciousness, surely one will attain in the next life a form exactly like that upon which one was meditating. This is another example learned from the insect kingdom, as explained in the following verse.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
If, out of love, hate or fear, an embodied soul fixes his mind with intelligence and complete concentration upon a particular form, he will certainly attain the form. It is not surprising that those who constantly meditate on the Lord attain forms similar to his. This the brāhmaṇa learned from the wasp. This is explained in two verses. When the action of the mind does not go elsewhere, the jīva attains a form similar to that of his meditation.
Personal Guidance
Whatever form the mind dwells on with conviction — through love, hate, or fear — becomes your next body; so fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa.
Practical Applications
  • Audit what your mind returns to in idle moments today — that recurring object is shaping your next form, so consciously redirect it to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Notice when love, resentment, or fear locks your attention on a person or possession, and immediately offer that intensity back to Kṛṣṇa instead.
  • Before sleep, deliberately fix your mind on Kṛṣṇa's form with the same one-pointed absorption (sakalaṁ dhiyā) you give to your worries.
Reflection Questions
  • Which form — a person, an enemy, a fear — am I unknowingly meditating on with the very absorption this verse warns will shape my next body?
  • If hate and fear can fix the mind as powerfully as love, where am I being delivered into bondage by an aversion I have not yet recognized as meditation?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Track for one day how many minutes your mind spends on a crush, a rival, or social media — that absorption is sculpting who you become, so reclaim some of it for Kṛṣṇa.
  • Before an exam or interview, instead of mentally rehearsing failure (fear-meditation), fix your mind on Kṛṣṇa with the same intensity and let the action follow.
  • When a peer humiliates you, refuse to spend the next week mentally re-litigating it — that is meditation on them, and this verse warns where it leads.
householder
  • Notice how much of your mental life is absorbed in your spouse's faults, your child's future, or your finances, and consciously offer each of those attachments back to Kṛṣṇa as His property.
  • When a work conflict or money worry hijacks your mind at home, treat it as a meditation alarm — pause, chant a round, and re-fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa before resuming.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's names and pastimes aloud with your children at meals, so the family's collective mental absorption is being shaped together, not just yours.
senior
  • Use the loosening grip on career and reputation to redirect that freed mental bandwidth into one-pointed absorption on Kṛṣṇa, not into nostalgia or grievance.
  • Recognize that fear of death is itself a powerful meditation — if it fixes the mind on the body, it binds you to another body; turn that same intensity toward Kṛṣṇa's form.
  • Mentor a younger devotee by describing Kṛṣṇa's form, qualities, and names in concrete detail, so that teaching becomes your own deepening sakalaṁ dhiyā.
Common Pitfalls
  • Assuming only loving meditation transfers the soul into a form, and missing that hatred and fear of someone bind us to their nature just as surely.
  • Treating remembrance of Kṛṣṇa as a five-minute morning ritual while the bulk of the day's mental absorption is poured into work, enemies, or anxieties.
  • Reducing this verse to a self-help slogan about positive thinking, instead of taking seriously that the next birth is literally being formed right now.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one form — person, fear, ambition — your mind returns to most often, and discuss honestly whether you are willing to let it shape your next body.
  • Is fear-based or hate-based absorption on Kṛṣṇa (as some demons displayed) really equivalent to loving absorption — or does the Gauḍīya line distinguish them, and how?
  • If sakalaṁ dhiyā — full intellectual conviction plus one-pointed mind — is the standard, can casual chanting actually transfer us, and what would close the gap?
Role Play

A devotee confides that they cannot stop mentally rehearsing arguments with a coworker who wronged them; they chant rounds but the mind keeps returning to that person. Pair up: one plays the devotee, one plays a senior practitioner who must, using this verse, help them see that this rehearsal is itself a form of meditation shaping their next body, and offer a concrete redirection toward Kṛṣṇa.

Objections & Responses
  • This is just the law of attraction dressed up in Sanskrit — whatever you focus on, you become. Why do I need Kṛṣṇa specifically?
    The verse is not promising worldly manifestation; it is describing the mechanics of transmigration. You will get a body matching your absorption — insect, animal, human, or like the Lord's. Without Kṛṣṇa as the object, the mechanism still operates, but it delivers you into another mortal form. He is the only object whose attainment ends the cycle.
  • I cannot control my mind that completely. Telling me my next body depends on where my mind wanders is terrifying, not helpful.
    The fear is appropriate — it is meant to wake us up. But Kṛṣṇa has not left us alone with an impossible task: He gives the holy name, which by its own power gradually pulls the mind toward Him. Begin where you are — even a fearful mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa is being delivered, because His form is non-different from Him.
