Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.6.33

अथ तस्यां महोत्पातान् द्वारवत्यां समुत्थितान् । विलोक्य भगवानाह यदुवृद्धान् समागतान् ॥ ३३ ॥

Thereafter, the Personality of Godhead observed that tremendous disturbances were taking place in the holy city of Dvārakā. Thus the Lord spoke to the assembled senior members of the Yadu dynasty as follows.
Purport

Muni-vāsa-nivāse kiṁ ghaṭetāriṣṭa-darśanam: there is no possibility of actual disturbances or inauspicious events in holy places inhabited by saintly persons. Thus the so-called disturbances in Dvārakā were directly enacted by the Personality of Godhead for His own auspicious purpose.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Thereafter, observing that tremendous disturbances were taking place in the city of Dvārakā, the Lord spoke to the assembled senior members of the Yadu dynasty. Disturbances took place by the desire of the Lord. It is said: ity aṅgopadiśanty eke vismṛtya prāg udāhṛtam muni-vāsa-nivāse kiṁ ghaṭetāriṣṭa-darśanam Some men proposed that the troubles were due to Akrūra’s absence, but they had forgotten the glories of the Supreme Lord, which they themselves had so often described. Indeed, how can calamities occur in a place where Lord, the residence of all the sages, resides? SB 1.57.31

Personal Guidance
When Kṛṣṇa Himself stages the omens, even apparent calamity in Dvārakā is His arrangement — read disturbance as His call to gather and listen.
Practical Applications
  • When something disturbing erupts in your life today, pause before reacting and ask what Kṛṣṇa may be arranging through it.
  • Call together the senior, sober voices in your circle — family elder, guru, trusted devotee — before deciding what a disturbance means.
  • Refuse the reflex that says holy or settled environments should be free of upheaval; receive the upheaval as a summons, not a failure.
Reflection Questions
  • Which recent 'disturbance' in my life have I been treating as random misfortune rather than as Kṛṣṇa's deliberate signal to me?
  • When the Lord speaks through unsettling omens, whom do I gather around me — the indulgent and agreeable, or the yadu-vṛddhān, the senior and sobering?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When an exam result, breakup, or friend-group fallout suddenly upends your plans, treat it as Kṛṣṇa staging a moment to speak to you, not as proof you're behind in life.
  • Before posting or venting online about a personal upheaval, first sit with one senior devotee or elder and let them help you read what just happened.
  • Refuse the campus story that a 'blessed' life means a frictionless one; receive your disturbances as invitations to grow up faster than your peers.
householder
  • When a job loss, a child's crisis, or a sudden financial reversal hits the home, call a family council with the most sober elders before you make any large decision.
  • When your household feels like it should be a settled, dharmic Dvārakā and instead feels like one with omens, resist the urge to blame your spouse or yourself and ask together what Kṛṣṇa is arranging.
  • Speak to your children about a current family disturbance in terms of the Lord's hand in it, so they learn early to read events as messages rather than as mere misfortune.
senior
  • Read the unmistakable utpātas of late life — a diagnosis, a friend's death, fading strength — as Kṛṣṇa convening you, the way He convened the yadu-vṛddhān, to speak and to listen.
  • Make yourself available as one of the senior, sober voices the younger generation can gather when their own Dvārakā shakes, instead of withdrawing into private worry.
  • Loosen your grip on the image of a peaceful, undisturbed final chapter, and let each disturbance now teach you to depend on Kṛṣṇa personally rather than on stable circumstances.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading every inconvenience as a personal cosmic message, instead of the specific, gathered, witness-confirmed kind of omen the verse describes.
  • Assuming a holy or devotional setting should be permanently disturbance-free, and losing faith the moment it isn't.
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's arranged disturbances as problems to fix or escape, rather than as occasions to assemble and hear what He wants to say.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a disturbance in your own life that, in hindsight, was clearly Kṛṣṇa's arrangement — and one you still cannot read that way. What is the difference?
  • If even Dvārakā receives mahā-utpātas by the Lord's design, what does that say about our expectation that a 'good spiritual life' should look outwardly stable?
  • Who are the yadu-vṛddhān in your life — the seniors you would actually gather when something inauspicious appears — and who do you gather instead?
Role Play

A devotee's long-running service project in a temple community suddenly collapses — funding withdrawn, key volunteers leaving, public criticism online. They come to you convinced this proves they were never qualified to serve. Sitting with them, walk through this verse: that even in Dvārakā the Lord Himself staged the disturbances, and that the right response is not self-recrimination but gathering the seniors and asking what He is saying.

Objections & Responses
  • Calling calamities 'arranged by God' is just a coping story — it lets believers avoid the real causes of their problems and the responsibility to fix them.
    The verse doesn't tell the Yadus to do nothing; it shows Kṛṣṇa Himself convening the elders to speak and act. Recognizing His hand behind events doesn't suspend responsibility — it changes who we consult, what we're willing to hear, and which outcome we're actually working toward. Material problem-solving without that reading just rearranges the disturbance; it doesn't read the message.
  • If Dvārakā is a holy city full of saintly persons, the very idea of inauspicious omens there is a contradiction — either the place wasn't really holy, or the omens weren't really inauspicious.
    Prabhupāda resolves precisely this in the purport: in a place inhabited by saintly persons, real inauspiciousness cannot arise, so what looked like ariṣṭa-darśana was directly enacted by the Personality of Godhead for His own auspicious purpose. The 'contradiction' dissolves once we accept that He is the doer; the form is fearsome, the substance is His will.
