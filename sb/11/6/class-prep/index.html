BG 6.1

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Lord Brahmā then set off for Dvārakā, accompanied by his own sons as well as by the demigods and the great Prajāpatis. Lord Śiva, the bestower of auspiciousness to all living beings, also went, surrounded by many ghostly creatures.

Essence. Even Brahmā and Śiva, with all demigods and Prajāpatis, travel together to wait upon Kṛṣṇa at Dvārakā — the highest powers bow before the Person.

Practical applications
  • Identify one authority figure or capability you secretly rank above Kṛṣṇa today, and consciously place it under Him in your morning offering.
  • Approach your sādhana the way Brahmā approached Dvārakā — go to Kṛṣṇa with company, not alone; sit with devotees today instead of practicing in isolation.
  • Refuse the modern reflex that says 'I have outgrown needing to bow'; physically offer praṇāma before the Deity or your guru-paramparā today.
Discussion

Share a moment when you treated a capable person or institution as a final authority. What would it have looked like to relate to them as Brahmā relates to Kṛṣṇa here?

BG 6.2-4

The powerful Lord Indra, along with the Maruts, Ādityas, Vasus, Aśvinīs, Ṛbhus, Aṅgirās, Rudras, Viśvedevas, Sādhyas, Gandharvas, Apsarās, Nāgas, Siddhas, Cāraṇas, Guhyakas, the great sages and forefathers and the Vidyādharas and Kinnaras, arrived at the city of Dvārakā, hoping to see Lord Kṛṣṇa. By His transcendental form, Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Lord, enchanted all human beings and spread His own fame throughout the worlds. The Lord’s glories destroy all contamination within the universe.

Essence. Even the demigods, who routinely see Viṣṇu, travel to Dvārakā hungry for Kṛṣṇa's most beautiful form — His personal beauty outranks every cosmic post.

Practical applications
  • Today, choose a darśana of Kṛṣṇa's personal form (deity, picture, described līlā) over an abstract idea of God, and let your eyes rest on Him.
  • Notice when you treat spiritual life as a function (problem-solving, anxiety-management) and re-aim one act of sādhana at simply seeing Him.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's name and glories aloud once today, trusting the verse's claim that they destroy contamination in the one who hears.
Discussion

The demigods can see Upendra at will, yet they come for Kṛṣṇa — what does this reveal about the difference between access and attraction in our own sādhana?

BG 6.5

In that resplendent city of Dvārakā, rich with all superior opulences, the demigods beheld with unsatiated eyes the wonderful form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Even the demigods could not satisfy their eyes drinking in Kṛṣṇa's wondrous form — His beauty is the one viṣaya that never breeds satiety.

Practical applications
  • Begin the day by looking at Kṛṣṇa's deity or picture for one unhurried minute before reaching for the phone.
  • Notice when your eyes feel restless and redirect them to a tulasī leaf, an image of Kṛṣṇa, or the words of a verse.
  • Refuse the next piece of visual content that promises stimulation but leaves you emptier, and read one line of Bhāgavatam instead.
Discussion

Share one kind of looking in your life that produces avitṛpta — unsatiated craving — and contrast it with the unsatiated gazing this verse describes.

BG 6.6

The demigods covered the Supreme Lord of the universe with flower garlands brought from the gardens of heaven. Then they praised Him, the best of the Yadu dynasty, with statements containing charming words and ideas.

Essence. Even the demigods garland and praise Kṛṣṇa, the jagad-īśvara — true worship is offering our finest words and works to Him, not to ourselves.

Practical applications
  • Begin today by offering your most cherished possession or skill mentally at Kṛṣṇa's feet before using it for any other purpose.
  • Speak your praises of Kṛṣṇa aloud once today using your own carefully chosen words, not borrowed phrases recited by rote.
  • Refuse to claim credit for any success you receive this week — redirect each compliment inwardly to the jagad-īśvara who supplied the result.
Discussion

Share a moment when you offered praise to Kṛṣṇa and it felt hollow — what was missing, and what would have made it real?

BG 6.7

The demigods began to speak: Our dear Lord, advanced mystic yogīs, striving for liberation from the severe bondage of material work, meditate with great devotion upon Your lotus feet within their hearts. Dedicating our intelligence, senses, vital air, mind and power of speech to Your Lordship, we demigods bow down at Your lotus feet.

Essence. The currents of māyā are violent; cling tightly to Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet today with intelligence, senses, prāṇa, mind, and speech.

Practical applications
  • Offer daṇḍavat praṇāma before your altar today, consciously dedicating arms, legs, knees, chest, head, eyes, mind, and speech to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Notice the first moment a sensory current pulls you toward viṣaya and immediately call to mind Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet instead of speculating your way out.
  • Refuse to coast through japa today — engage buddhi, indriya, prāṇa, manas, and vacas together rather than chanting with only the tongue.
Discussion

The demigods say they are astonished that yogīs only contemplate internally what they see externally — does easy access to Kṛṣṇa's deity, name, and verse make us more grateful or more casual, and why?

BG 6.8

O unconquerable Lord, You engage Your illusory energy, composed of three modes, to unleash, maintain and devastate the inconceivable manifest cosmos, all within Your own self. As the supreme superintendent of māyā, You appear to be situated in the interaction of the modes of nature; however, You are never affected by material activities. In fact, You are directly engaged in Your own eternal, spiritual bliss, and thus You cannot be accused of any material infection.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa wields the three-mode māyā to create, sustain and dissolve cosmos yet remains untouched, ever absorbed in His own faultless spiritual bliss.

Practical applications
  • Notice today when you assume the world's chaos must drag you down, and remember the Lord directs all three guṇas without being stained by any.
  • Before any task, mentally place Kṛṣṇa as the unaffected superintendent, and act as His instrument rather than as the proprietor of outcomes.
  • Refuse to evaluate Kṛṣṇa's instructions or activities by mundane moral arithmetic, recalling that anavadya means no fault can be found in Him.
Discussion

Share one concrete area of life where you instinctively credit māyā with power independent of Kṛṣṇa, and let the group push back.

BG 6.9

O greatest of all, those whose consciousness is polluted by illusion cannot purify themselves merely by ordinary worship, study of the Vedas, charity, austerity and ritual activities. Our Lord, those pure souls who have developed a powerful transcendental faith in Your glories achieve a purified state of existence that can never be attained by those lacking such faith.

Essence. Vedic study, charity, austerity, and ritual cannot purify a heart polluted by illusion; only transcendental faith born of hearing Kṛṣṇa's glories purifies.

Practical applications
  • Begin today by hearing or reading about Kṛṣṇa's glories before turning to any other study or duty.
  • Notice when you lean on your scholarship, charity, or piety as proof of advancement, and consciously redirect that pride into śravaṇa of His name.
  • Refuse to evaluate a fellow devotee by external qualifications, and instead look for the faith that has come through hearing.
Discussion

Share one religious activity you have invested heavily in, and honestly examine whether it has produced faith in Kṛṣṇa or only self-satisfaction.

BG 6.10

Great sages, desiring the highest benefit in life, always cherish Your lotus feet within their hearts, which are melted by love for You. Similarly, Your self-controlled devotees, desiring to cross beyond the material kingdom of heaven to achieve opulence equal to Yours, worship Your lotus feet in the morning, at noon and in the evening. Thus, they meditate upon Your Lordship in Your quadruple expansion. Your lotus feet are just like a blazing fire that burns to ashes all the inauspicious desires for material sense gratification.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, worshipped morning, noon, and evening, are a blazing fire that burns inauspicious desires for sense gratification to ashes.

Practical applications
  • Anchor three explicit remembrances of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet today — morning, noon, and evening — even if only sixty seconds each.
  • Name one specific aśubha desire you have been negotiating with, and hold it in the heat of His lotus feet rather than rehearsing it again.
  • Refuse the bargain of crossing to material heavens (promotion, status, comfort) and reset your aim to sama-vibhūti — serving Him in His own abode.
Discussion

Compare what each of you actually does at noon — does Kṛṣṇa enter that hour, or is noon the most secular part of your day?

BG 6.11

Those about to offer oblations into the fire of sacrifice in accordance with the Ṛg, Yajur and Sāma Vedas meditate on Your lotus feet. Similarly, the practitioners of transcendental yoga meditate upon Your lotus feet, hoping for knowledge about Your divine mystic potency, and the most elevated pure devotees perfectly worship Your lotus feet, desiring to cross beyond Your illusory potency.

Essence. All seekers — ritualists, yogīs, pure devotees — converge on Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet; the parama-bhāgavata aims simply to cross māyā and serve Him.

Practical applications
  • Begin today's sādhana by mentally placing your forehead at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet before any other duty.
  • Notice when you are seeking mystic powers or results from spiritual practice, and re-aim toward crossing māyā to serve Him.
  • Offer the work of your hands today as a havis — an oblation — rather than as a transaction for personal gain.
Discussion

Compare what the Vedic ritualist, the mystic yogī, and the parama-bhāgavata each want from the Lord — where do you most honestly locate yourself, and why?

BG 6.12

O almighty Lord, You are so kind to Your servants that You have accepted the withered flower garland that we have placed on Your chest. Since the goddess of fortune makes her abode on Your transcendental chest, she will undoubtedly become agitated, like a jealous co-wife, upon seeing our offering also dwelling there. Yet You are so merciful that You neglect Your eternal consort Lakṣmī and accept our offering as most excellent worship. O merciful Lord, may Your lotus feet always act as a blazing fire to consume the inauspicious desires within our hearts.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa prefers a withered garland from a loving devotee over Lakṣmī's embrace — bring Him your humble offering today and let His feet burn out inauspicious desire.

Practical applications
  • Make one small, imperfect offering to Kṛṣṇa today — a flower, a verse, a chore — without apologizing for its smallness.
  • Name one aśubha-āśaya (inauspicious desire) you are nursing and consciously place it at His lotus feet to be burnt up.
  • Refuse the calculation that says 'I'll serve Him properly once my life is presentable' — offer now, in this withered state.
Discussion

Share an offering you have actually made to Kṛṣṇa that felt 'withered' — and whether you believed He accepted it. Where do the group's stories agree or differ?

BG 6.13

O omnipotent Lord, in Your incarnation as Trivikrama, You raised Your leg like a flagpole to break the shell of the universe, allowing the holy Ganges to flow down, like a banner of victory, in three branches throughout the three planetary systems. By three mighty steps of Your lotus feet, Your Lordship captured Bali Mahārāja, along with his universal kingdom. Your lotus feet inspire fear in the demons by driving them down to hell and fearlessness among Your devotees by elevating them to the perfection of heavenly life. We are sincerely trying to worship You, our Lord; therefore may Your lotus feet kindly free us from all of our sinful reactions.

Essence. Trivikrama's three steps split fear from fearlessness; take shelter of His lotus feet and your sinful reactions are washed away like the Gaṅgā.

Practical applications
  • Begin today's worship by mentally bowing at the lotus feet of Trivikrama and asking, plainly, to be freed from a specific sinful reaction you keep repeating.
  • Notice one place where you have, like Bali, claimed kingdom over what is not yours — money, time, a relationship — and offer that territory back at His feet.
  • When fear rises in you today, refuse to manage it horizontally and instead place it directly under the same feet that terrify the asuras and reassure the devotees.
Discussion

Share one concrete kingdom in your life that you suspect Kṛṣṇa is asking you to measure out and return — and one you are still defending.

BG 6.14

You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the transcendental entity who is superior to both material nature and the enjoyer of nature. May Your lotus feet bestow transcendental pleasure upon us. All of the great demigods, beginning with Brahmā, are embodied living entities. Struggling painfully with one another under the strict control of Your time factor, they are just like bulls dragged by ropes tied through their pierced noses.

Essence. Even Brahmā and the demigods are bulls led by Kṛṣṇa's time-rope; surrender at His lotus feet, not at the feet of the powerful.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are quietly bowing to a powerful person, institution, or trend, and consciously redirect that reverence to Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet.
  • When you feel painfully pulled by deadlines, pressures, or rivalries, remember the bull's nose-rope and offer that very tug as a reminder to take shelter of puruṣottama.
  • Refuse to envy the materially successful and refuse to despise the materially defeated — both are being moved by kāla under Kṛṣṇa's hand.
Discussion

Share one 'demigod' in your own life — a boss, system, ideology, or capacity — that you have been treating as more powerful than it really is. What changes if you see it as nose-roped by kāla?

BG 6.15

You are the cause of the creation, maintenance and destruction of this universe. As time, You regulate the subtle and manifest states of material nature and control every living being. As the threefold wheel of time You diminish all things by Your imperceptible actions, and thus You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa as time moves with imperceptible speed, silently dismantling all you cling to — surrender to Him, the uttama-pūruṣa behind the wheel.

Practical applications
  • Notice one thing today that has quietly aged or deteriorated without your perceiving it — your body, a relationship, a possession — and offer that recognition to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse the illusion of permanence: before committing to a worry about the future, remember that kāla is already at work and the outcome is not in your hands.
  • Speak Kṛṣṇa's name once at the start and end of your day, acknowledging Him as the uttama-pūruṣa who governs the unseen wheel of time.
Discussion

Share one concrete attachment in your life where you can feel kāla quietly diminishing it — and discuss what surrender to the uttama-pūruṣa would look like there.

BG 6.16

My dear Lord, the original puruṣa-avatāra, Mahā-Viṣṇu, acquires His creative potency from You. Thus with infallible energy He impregnates material nature, producing the mahat-tattva. Then the mahat-tattva, the amalgamated material energy, endowed with the potency of the Lord, produces from itself the primeval golden egg of the universe, which is covered by various layers of material elements.

Essence. Even Mahā-Viṣṇu, who fathers every universe, draws His creative potency from Kṛṣṇa — so trace every power you wield back to its true source.

Practical applications
  • Before starting any creative work today, pause and explicitly acknowledge Kṛṣṇa as the source of the potency you are about to deploy.
  • When you feel proud of something you produced — a project, an idea, even a child raised well — name the borrowed energy behind it and offer the credit upward.
  • Refuse the modern reflex that says "I made this" and replace it with "this was made through me" at least once today, in writing or aloud.
Discussion

Share one ability you have privately considered "yours" — and discuss what changes if you take seriously that even Mahā-Viṣṇu's potency is borrowed.

BG 6.17

O Lord, You are the supreme creator of this universe and the ultimate controller of all moving and nonmoving living entities. You are Hṛṣīkeśa, the supreme controller of all sensory activity, and thus You never become contaminated or entangled in the course of Your supervision of the infinite sensory activities within the material creation. On the other hand, other living entities, even yogīs and philosophers, are disturbed and frightened simply by remembering the material objects that they have supposedly renounced in their pursuit of enlightenment.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa as Hṛṣīkeśa engages with all sense objects yet remains untouched, while we tremble even at what we have supposedly renounced.

Practical applications
  • Notice today one object you have outwardly given up but still rehearse in your mind, and admit the renunciation is incomplete.
  • Before any sense engagement — eating, scrolling, conversing — offer it inwardly to Hṛṣīkeśa as the true owner of the senses.
  • Refuse the self-image of the detached yogī; instead, take shelter of the only One who is truly unentangled.
Discussion

Share one thing you have given up that still 'follows' you in memory — what does this verse say about that haunting?

BG 6.18

My Lord, You are living with sixteen thousand exquisitely beautiful, aristocratic wives. By their irresistible coy and smiling glances and by their lovely arching eyebrows, they send You messages of eager conjugal love. But they are completely unable to disturb the mind and senses of Your Lordship.

Essence. Even Kṛṣṇa's sixteen thousand queens cannot disturb His senses — He is ātmārāma, conquered only by pure love, never by enjoying spirit.

Practical applications
  • Notice one moment today when an alluring sense object pulls you, and consciously offer that attraction to Kṛṣṇa instead of consuming it.
  • Refuse the assumption that more beauty, more partners, or more pleasure would finally satisfy you — the fullest enjoyment imaginable did not move Kṛṣṇa.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa today as a person who is already complete, asking to serve His pleasure rather than to extract pleasure from Him.
Discussion

If Kṛṣṇa cannot be disturbed even by sixteen thousand queens, what does that say about the kind of 'love' we usually offer Him in our sādhana?

BG 6.19

The nectar-bearing rivers of discussions about You, and also the holy rivers generated from the bathing of Your lotus feet, are able to destroy all contamination within the three worlds. Those who are striving for purification associate with the holy narrations of Your glories by hearing them with their ears, and they associate with the holy rivers flowing from Your lotus feet by physically bathing in them.

Essence. Two nectarean rivers purify the heart: hearing Kṛṣṇa's glories from the bona fide guru, and bathing in the waters that wash His lotus feet.

Practical applications
  • Each morning, hear at least one verse and purport about Kṛṣṇa from the paramparā before opening any other input stream.
  • Visit the temple to receive three drops of caraṇāmṛta from the Deity's bathing, and accept it as actual purification, not symbol.
  • Refuse to substitute speculative reading about Kṛṣṇa for śravaṇam from a bona fide spiritual master.
Discussion

Share one contamination of the heart that you have noticed only hari-kathā softens — and one that even hari-kathā has not yet touched. Why the difference?

BG 6.20

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī continued: After Brahmā, along with Lord Śiva and the other demigods, thus offered prayers to the Supreme Lord, Govinda, Lord Brahmā situated himself in the sky and addressed the Lord as follows.

Essence. When the highest demigods themselves bow and offer prayers to Govinda, take it as a settled signal: surrender belongs to Kṛṣṇa alone.

Practical applications
  • Before any prayer today, physically bow or pause and address Kṛṣṇa by name as Govinda, not as a vague higher power.
  • Notice when you treat any boss, guru, or institution as ultimate, and consciously re-route that final reliance to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Refuse to flatten Kṛṣṇa into one option among many; speak of Him today as the Lord whom even Brahmā and Śiva petition.
Discussion

If Brahmā, the creator of this universe, still situates himself in the sky to address Kṛṣṇa, what does that say about every authority figure we are tempted to absolutize?

BG 6.21

Lord Brahmā said: My dear Lord, previously we requested You to remove the burden of the earth. O unlimited Personality of Godhead, that request has certainly been fulfilled.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa is the unlimited Personality of Godhead from whom all Viṣṇu expansions emanate — every prayer offered to His expansions reaches Him directly.

Practical applications
  • Address Kṛṣṇa in your morning prayer as aśeṣātmā — the source of every form of the Lord you have ever turned to.
  • Notice when you treat a particular form or name of the Lord as separate from Kṛṣṇa, and consciously reconnect it to Him.
  • When a long-standing prayer of yours is answered today, name Kṛṣṇa as the one who fulfilled it, not chance or your own effort.
Discussion

Share a request you once made in prayer that was eventually fulfilled — who did you address, and looking back, who do you now believe actually fulfilled it?

BG 6.22

My Lord, You have reestablished the principles of religion among pious men who are always firmly bound to the truth. You have also distributed Your glories all over the world, and thus the whole world can be purified by hearing about You.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa reestablishes dharma among the truthful and spreads His glories worldwide — our share is to live truthfully and pass on what purifies.

Practical applications
  • Speak one difficult truth today that you have been softening to keep social peace, and notice what stabilizes when you do.
  • Choose one of Kṛṣṇa's names, qualities, or activities and tell it to at least one person in your day — let your speech carry kīrti, not just opinion.
  • Audit one area where your private conduct does not match your stated principles, and bring it back into alignment before the day ends.
Discussion

Share a recent moment when telling the truth would have cost you something real — what did you actually do, and what does this verse say to that choice?

BG 6.23

Descending into the dynasty of King Yadu, You have manifested Your unique transcendental form, and for the benefit of the entire universe You have executed magnanimous transcendental activities.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa descended into the Yadu dynasty in His unique form and performed magnanimous activities for the benefit of the entire universe.

Practical applications
  • Begin the day by remembering that Kṛṣṇa's descent and activities are for your benefit personally, not abstract cosmic events.
  • Refuse to reduce Kṛṣṇa to a symbol or principle; address Him today as the personal Lord who entered a specific lineage in a specific form.
  • Take up one concrete act of service whose only justification is the welfare of others, modeled on the magnanimous spirit of His activities.
Discussion

Share a moment when Kṛṣṇa's descent stopped being a story for you and became personally relevant; what shifted, and what resisted shifting?

BG 6.24

My dear Lord, those pious and saintly persons who in the Age of Kali hear about Your transcendental activities and also glorify them will easily cross over the darkness of the age.

Essence. In Kali-yuga, hearing and glorifying Kṛṣṇa's transcendental activities is the easy boat that carries saintly persons across the age's darkness.

Practical applications
  • Replace one stretch of news, social media, or background entertainment today with audio of Kṛṣṇa's activities from a bona fide source.
  • Speak about something you have heard of Kṛṣṇa to at least one person today, however briefly, so śravaṇa becomes kīrtana on your lips.
  • Notice when you are about to offer your own opinion on everything, and instead ask, 'What have I actually heard from guru and śāstra on this?'
Discussion

Share one 'whimsical vibration' you have quietly justified in your daily intake, and let the group test whether it actually serves your bhakti or merely soothes you.

BG 6.25

O Supreme Personality of Godhead, O my Lord, You have descended into the Yadu dynasty, and thus You have spent one hundred twenty-five autumns with Your devotees.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, descended into the Yadu dynasty and spent 125 autumns with His devotees — His presence is the gift, not a metaphor.

Practical applications
  • Today, read or hear one passage about Kṛṣṇa's personal descent into the Yadu line and refuse to abstract Him into a symbol.
  • Mark on the calendar that you, like the Yadus, have a finite span with Kṛṣṇa's saṅga in this life — and spend one block of time accordingly.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa directly, by name, once today, the way the Yadus addressed Him as Puruṣottama and Prabho.
Discussion

What does it change for you to address Kṛṣṇa as Puruṣottama — the Supreme Person — rather than as the Self or consciousness?

BG 6.26-27

My dear Lord, there is nothing remaining at this time for Your Lordship to do on behalf of the demigods. You have already withdrawn Your dynasty by the curse of the brāhmaṇas. O Lord, You are the basis of everything, and if You so desire, kindly return now to Your own abode in the spiritual world. At the same time, we humbly beg that You always protect us. We are Your humble servants, and on Your behalf we are managing the universal situation. We, along with our planets and followers, require Your constant protection.

Essence. When your assigned work for Kṛṣṇa is finished, ask Him to return to His abode while begging His continued protection over the duties still entrusted to you.

Practical applications
  • Name one service Kṛṣṇa has clearly already completed through you, and stop clinging to it as though it still defines your usefulness.
  • Before any task today, pray as the demigods do: "I am Your servant managing this small situation on Your behalf — please protect me."
  • Notice when you are postponing a natural ending (a project, a role, a season) and offer that ending to Kṛṣṇa instead of resisting it.
Discussion

The demigods say there is "nothing remaining" for Kṛṣṇa to do — share an area where you sense Kṛṣṇa's work through you is finished, and one where it clearly is not, and how you tell the difference.

BG 6.28

The Supreme Lord said: O lord of the demigods, Brahmā, I understand your prayers and request. Having removed the burden of the earth, I have executed everything that was required on your behalf.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa Himself declares the mission complete — the work He came to do is finished, and He receives Brahmā's request with full awareness.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you are still 'fighting battles' that Kṛṣṇa has already concluded, and consciously lay one of them down.
  • Before beginning any task, remember that Kṛṣṇa, not you, is the actual remover of burdens — and act as His instrument, not as the proprietor of the outcome.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa today as one who hears you fully, the way He heard Brahmā — bring Him a real request, not a polite formality.
Discussion

Share one situation in your life where you secretly believe the burden is yours alone to remove — and let the group press you on whether that is actually true.

BG 6.29

That very Yādava dynasty in which I appeared became greatly magnified in opulence, especially in their physical strength and courage, to the extent that they threatened to devour the whole world. Therefore I have stopped them, just as the shore holds back the great ocean.

Essence. Even God-given strength becomes destructive when unchecked; Kṛṣṇa Himself draws back what He has empowered when it threatens to devour the world.

Practical applications
  • Notice one area today where your strength, talent, or success has begun to crowd out humility, and consciously pull it back like a shore holds the ocean.
  • Refuse to interpret an unexpected setback as mere misfortune; ask first whether Kṛṣṇa is checking an unchecked momentum in your life.
  • Speak to someone you have been overpowering by force of personality, position, or argument, and deliberately yield ground today.
Discussion

Share an instance when a strength of yours had to be checked — by circumstance, by another person, or by the Lord — and what you only understood in hindsight.

BG 6.30

If I were to leave this world without withdrawing the overly proud members of the Yadu dynasty, the whole world would be destroyed by the deluge of their unlimited expansion.

Essence. Even strength rooted in nearness to Kṛṣṇa can swell into dṛpta-pride; the Lord lovingly withdraws what would otherwise crush the world.

Practical applications
  • Name one strength of yours that has quietly turned into dṛpta-pride because of your devotional standing, and confess it before your morning prayer today.
  • Refuse to use your closeness to Kṛṣṇa, guru, or saṅga as social leverage in even one conversation today — speak as a servant, not as an insider.
  • When Kṛṣṇa withdraws something you assumed was permanent — a position, a relationship, a role — receive it as the husband gently removing the wife's heavy ornaments, not as loss.
Discussion

Share an area where you have noticed dṛpta-pride creeping into your devotional life — and one where the rest of the group sees it more clearly than you do.

BG 6.31

Now due to the brāhmaṇas’ curse, the annihilation of My family has already begun. O sinless Brahmā, when this annihilation is finished and I am enroute to Vaikuṇṭha, I will pay a small visit to your abode.

Essence. What looks like the annihilation of Kṛṣṇa's dynasty is really their entry into eternal Dvārakā — His associates are never destroyed, only veiled from our conditioned sight.

Practical applications
  • When you read of the Yadus' end, refuse the materialist reading — remind yourself the destruction was only of our visible access, not of them.
  • Today, name one loss in your life (a person, a phase, a place) and ask whether what ended was the thing itself or only your conditioned view of it.
  • Before sleep, offer obeisances to the Lord's eternal associates and pray for the vision to see continuity where the senses report disappearance.
Discussion

Jīva Gosvāmī reads nāśaḥ as entry into the hidden Dvārakā — share with your partner one passage of scripture where you have been reading destruction where you should have been reading concealment.

BG 6.32

Śrī Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: Thus addressed by the Lord of the universe, the self-born Brahmā fell down in obeisances at the lotus feet of the Lord. Surrounded by all the demigods, the great Brahmā then returned to his personal abode.

Essence. Having received Kṛṣṇa's instruction, even Brahmā bows and withdraws — the highest response to the Lord's word is obeisance, then quiet return to one's post.

Practical applications
  • After hearing or reading Kṛṣṇa's instruction today, physically offer praṇāma before standing up and moving on to anything else.
  • Notice when you receive guidance and immediately argue or analyze instead of bowing first — pause, offer obeisance, then think.
  • Return to your sva-dharma post today the way Brahmā returned to his abode — not seeking more revelation, but executing what was already given.
Discussion

Brahmā, the creator of the universe, falls down before Kṛṣṇa — share a moment when you encountered something that made your usual self-importance feel absurd, and what you did next.

BG 6.33

Thereafter, the Personality of Godhead observed that tremendous disturbances were taking place in the holy city of Dvārakā. Thus the Lord spoke to the assembled senior members of the Yadu dynasty as follows.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa Himself stages the omens, even apparent calamity in Dvārakā is His arrangement — read disturbance as His call to gather and listen.

Practical applications
  • When something disturbing erupts in your life today, pause before reacting and ask what Kṛṣṇa may be arranging through it.
  • Call together the senior, sober voices in your circle — family elder, guru, trusted devotee — before deciding what a disturbance means.
  • Refuse the reflex that says holy or settled environments should be free of upheaval; receive the upheaval as a summons, not a failure.
Discussion

Share a disturbance in your own life that, in hindsight, was clearly Kṛṣṇa's arrangement — and one you still cannot read that way. What is the difference?

BG 6.34

The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Our dynasty has been cursed by the brāhmaṇas. Such a curse is impossible to counteract, and thus great disturbances are appearing everywhere around us.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa Himself acknowledges the brāhmaṇas' curse on His dynasty as uncounteractable — even the Lord honors the weight of offense to devotees.

Practical applications
  • Notice today where you have offended a devotee or sādhu and seek their forgiveness directly, not in your head.
  • When omens of disturbance gather in your life, read them as Kṛṣṇa's arrangement rather than random misfortune.
  • Refuse to console yourself that an offense to a brāhmaṇa is small — treat vaiṣṇava-aparādha as the most serious leak in your sādhana.
Discussion

If Kṛṣṇa's own kula could not escape a brāhmaṇa's curse, what does that tell us about the relative weight we give to social status versus devotee-respect in our own communities?

BG 6.35

My dear respected elders, we must not remain any longer in this place if we wish to keep our lives intact. Let us go this very day to the most pious place Prabhāsa. We have no time to delay.

Essence. When Kṛṣṇa Himself signals it is time to move, do not linger — recognize His arrangement and act today, not later.

Practical applications
  • Identify one situation where Kṛṣṇa is clearly signaling you to leave or change course, and act on it today rather than negotiating delay.
  • Stop treating "mā ciram" — no delay — as optional; when sādhana or duty calls, refuse the inner voice that says "tomorrow."
  • Speak respectfully to the elders in your life ("āryakāḥ") even when urging hard change, modeling Kṛṣṇa's tone in this verse.
Discussion

Share a time when an outwardly inauspicious event in your life turned out to be Kṛṣṇa's auspicious arrangement — and one where you are still unsure.

BG 6.36

Once, the moon was afflicted with consumption because of the curse of Dakṣa, but just by taking bath at Prabhāsa-kṣetra, the moon was immediately freed from his sinful reaction and again resumed the waxing of his phases.

Essence. Even the moon, cursed and wasting away, was restored by bathing at Prabhāsa — sacred place and surrender can dissolve reactions no self-effort can.

Practical applications
  • Identify one "wasting" pattern in your life — a reaction that keeps depleting you — and stop trying to fix it by willpower alone; place it before Kṛṣṇa in your sādhana today.
  • Make at least one act of bathing, prasāda, or chanting today a deliberate refuge rather than a routine, the way the moon entered Prabhāsa as a desperate appeal, not a habit.
  • When you feel the slow drain of a past mistake, refuse the modern script of "just work harder on yourself" and instead approach a holy place, name, or association with the humility of one already cursed.
Discussion

The moon was cursed by Dakṣa for a real offense, not a misunderstanding — discuss whether we tend to minimize our own offenses (to Vaiṣṇavas, to guru, to spouse) and how that minimization blocks the kind of bath the moon took.

BG 6.37-38

By bathing at Prabhāsa-kṣetra, by offering sacrifice there to placate the forefathers and demigods, by feeding the worshipable brāhmaṇas with various delicious foodstuffs and by bestowing opulent gifts upon them as the most suitable candidates for charity, we will certainly cross over these terrible dangers through such acts of charity, just as one can cross over a great ocean in a suitable boat.

Essence. Cross life's terrible dangers as one crosses an ocean by boat — through pilgrimage, offering to forefathers, and feeding worthy brāhmaṇas with śraddhā.

Practical applications
  • Identify one 'worshipable' recipient today — a sincere devotee or brāhmaṇa — and feed them or serve them with care, not as a transaction but as offering.
  • Set aside a fixed portion of this month's income for dāna placed in a suitable pātra, refusing the impulse to scatter it on unworthy causes that feel good.
  • Before any act of charity today, pause and place śraddhā in the heart first — give nothing mechanically, give nothing without faith in Kṛṣṇa behind the act.
Discussion

The verse names specific recipients — forefathers, demigods, brāhmaṇas. How do we honor this specificity today without collapsing it into a generic 'give to whoever is in need'?

BG 6.39

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O favorite son of the Kurus, thus advised by the Personality of Godhead, the Yādavas made up their minds to go to that holy place, Prabhāsa-kṣetra, and thus yoked their horses to their chariots.

Essence. When the Lord instructs, the dhīra acts at once — the Yādavas yoke their chariots without delay, modeling immediate, embodied obedience to Kṛṣṇa.

Practical applications
  • Identify one clear instruction from guru, śāstra, or sādhus you have been postponing, and take the first physical step toward it today.
  • Close the gap between hearing and doing: when you finish reading or class, write down one concrete action and execute it before nightfall.
  • Refuse the modern habit of endless deliberation — once the direction is clear from Kṛṣṇa's words, yoke your time, schedule, and resources to it now.
Discussion

Share an instruction from śāstra or your guru that you executed quickly, and one you delayed — what was different about the inner state in each case?

BG 6.40-41

My dear King, Uddhava was a constantly faithful follower of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Upon seeing the imminent departure of the Yādavas, hearing from them of the Lord’s instructions and taking note of the fearful omens, he approached the Personality of Godhead in a private place. He bowed down with his head at the lotus feet of the supreme controller of the universe and with folded hands addressed Him as follows.

Essence. When the signs of dissolution gather, the faithful devotee withdraws to a private place, bows at Kṛṣṇa's feet, and speaks to Him directly.

Practical applications
  • Notice the ominous signs in your own life today — a failing body, a fracturing relationship, a collapsing plan — and take them as Kṛṣṇa's summons to approach Him personally.
  • Carve out one vivikta — a private, undistracted place — and offer praṇāma with head bowed before speaking your real question to Him.
  • Refuse to philosophize about Kṛṣṇa's pastimes through material logic; hear them only from the recognized ācāryas as inconceivable acintya-śakti.
Discussion

Share an 'ariṣṭa' — a fearful omen — you have personally seen recently: did it pull you closer to Kṛṣṇa or push you into denial, and why?

BG 6.42

Śrī Uddhava said: O my Lord, O supreme God among all the demigods, real piety is invoked simply by hearing and chanting Your transcendental glories. My Lord, it appears that You will now withdraw Your dynasty, and thus You Yourself will finally give up Your pastimes within this universe. You are the supreme controller and the master of all mystic power. But although You are fully capable of counteracting the brāhmaṇas’ curse against Your dynasty, You are not doing so, and Your disappearance is imminent.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's departure is not loss but withdrawal — He stages even His disappearance so future hearers can return to Him through śravaṇa-kīrtana.

Practical applications
  • Begin your day by hearing or chanting one episode of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, treating it as the very puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtana Uddhava praises.
  • When a loss feels final, pause and ask whether the Lord is withdrawing something rather than destroying it, and adjust your grief accordingly.
  • Refuse to measure Kṛṣṇa's actions by what looks efficient to ordinary eyes — trust that He has a purpose even when He declines to intervene.
Discussion

Uddhava says Kṛṣṇa could simply neutralize the brāhmaṇas' curse but is not doing so — where in your own life have you seen the Lord decline to fix something for a deeper reason?

BG 6.43

O Lord Keśava, my dear master, I cannot tolerate giving up Your lotus feet even for a fraction of a moment. I urge You to take me along with You to Your own abode.

Essence. Uddhava begs Kṛṣṇa: I cannot bear to leave Your lotus feet for even half a moment — take me with You to Your own abode.

Practical applications
  • Today, name the one attachment you would refuse to drop even for a moment, and honestly compare it with Uddhava's attachment to Kṛṣṇa's feet.
  • Before sleep, pray in your own words to be taken closer to Kṛṣṇa's personal service, not to be merged into anything impersonal.
  • When a task pulls you away from your sādhana, mark the interruption and return to remembrance — refuse to treat even brief separation as acceptable.
Discussion

Uddhava asks to be taken along — share with your partner one area where you are quietly hoping Kṛṣṇa will leave you behind a little longer, and why.

BG 6.44

O my dear Kṛṣṇa, Your pastimes are supremely auspicious for mankind and are an intoxicating beverage for the ears. Tasting such pastimes, people forget their desires for other things.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are nectar for the ears — drink them daily, and the thirst for everything other than Him quietly falls away.

Practical applications
  • Begin the day by hearing or reading one of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes before opening any news, feed, or message.
  • Notice when you reach for entertainment to soothe restlessness, and replace one such session today with hearing about Kṛṣṇa.
  • Speak about a pastime of Kṛṣṇa with one person today — let your tongue and ears taste what the verse calls karṇa-pīyūṣam.
Discussion

Share one material desire that has genuinely weakened in you through hearing about Kṛṣṇa — and one that has stubbornly remained. What is the difference?

BG 6.45

My dear Lord, You are the Supreme Soul, and thus You are most dear to us. We are Your devotees, and how can we possibly reject You or live without You even for a moment? Whether we are lying down, sitting, walking, standing, bathing, enjoying recreation, eating or doing anything else, we are constantly engaged in Your service.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Soul most dear to us; lying, sitting, walking, eating — every act of the devotee is service to Him.

Practical applications
  • Before lying down tonight, offer the act of sleep itself to Kṛṣṇa as service rather than as private retreat.
  • Pick one mundane activity tomorrow — bathing, eating, or walking — and consciously remember it is occurring in Kṛṣṇa's presence and for His pleasure.
  • Refuse the split between sādhana time and ordinary time; speak Kṛṣṇa's name once aloud during a recreation or meal today to dissolve that boundary.
Discussion

Share one activity from yesterday — sleeping, bathing, eating, recreation — and discuss honestly whether Kṛṣṇa had any place in it, and what stopped you if He did not.

BG 6.46

Simply by decorating ourselves with the garlands, fragrant oils, clothes and ornaments that You have already enjoyed, and by eating the remnants of Your meals, we, Your servants, will indeed conquer Your illusory energy.

Essence. Uddhava's prayer: we conquer māyā not by speculation but by honoring Kṛṣṇa's remnants — His prasāda, garlands, and used ornaments.

Practical applications
  • Eat nothing today that has not first been offered to Kṛṣṇa — take only His remnants as your meal.
  • Wear or keep near you one item (a tulasī bead, a flower, a piece of cloth) connected to the Lord's service rather than your own decoration.
  • Refuse the urge to defeat māyā by clever thinking today; instead, take a small portion of prasāda and trust that as your weapon.
Discussion

Uddhava, already a liberated associate, still prays this way — what does that tell us about why we honor prasāda, and where does our usual reasoning fall short?

BG 6.47

Naked sages who seriously endeavor in spiritual practice, who have raised their semen upward, who are peaceful and sinless members of the renounced order, attain the spiritual abode called Brahman.

Essence. Even naked, celibate, peaceful sannyāsīs reach only impersonal Brahman through harsh struggle — Kṛṣṇa's causeless mercy through nāma is incomparably easier and higher.

Practical applications
  • Today, chant a fixed number of rounds of the holy name attentively, trusting that this single act outweighs years of austerity aimed at impersonal liberation.
  • When tempted to admire a feat of severe self-denial, remind yourself that Uddhava preferred Kṛṣṇa's smiling face to the brahmajyoti — and choose connection over conquest.
  • Refuse the self-made-man narrative for one full day: notice each breath, meal, and faculty as borrowed from Kṛṣṇa, and audibly thank Him for it.
Discussion

Share an area where you have been investing real effort in self-improvement — is it bringing you closer to Kṛṣṇa personally, or only to a more impressive version of yourself?

BG 6.48-49

O greatest of mystics, although we are conditioned souls wandering on the path of fruitive work, we will certainly cross beyond the darkness of this material world simply by hearing about Your Lordship in the association of Your devotees. Thus we are always remembering and glorifying the wonderful things You do and the wonderful things You say. We ecstatically recall Your amorous pastimes with Your confidential conjugal devotees and how You boldly smile and move about while engaged in such youthful pastimes. My dear Lord, Your loving pastimes are bewilderingly similar to the activities of ordinary people within this material world.

Essence. Though still wandering on karma's path, we cross māyā by remembering and glorifying Kṛṣṇa's pastimes and words in devotee association.

Practical applications
  • Before starting work today, audibly admit you are still bhramantaḥ karma-vartmasu, then immediately chant or recite one pastime of Kṛṣṇa to begin from honesty rather than pretense.
  • Carry one specific līlā or saying of Kṛṣṇa in your mind through the day and return to it during transitions — commutes, between meetings, before meals — instead of scrolling.
  • Seek out one devotee today and speak with them about something Kṛṣṇa did or said, refusing the temptation to keep your remembrance private and self-managed.
Discussion

Uddhava calls himself one of the karma-wanderers even while standing before Kṛṣṇa — where in your life are you reluctant to admit the same about yourself, and what changes when you do?

BG 6.50

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said: O King Parīkṣit, thus addressed, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, the son of Devakī, began to reply confidentially to His dear, unalloyed servant Uddhava.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa speaks the supreme path of liberation confidentially to Uddhava — His unalloyed servant — because intimate truth flows to the surrendered heart.

Practical applications
  • Identify one teaching you have heard publicly but never received as if Kṛṣṇa were speaking it personally to you, and sit with it today as His private instruction.
  • Before your sādhana, place yourself in Uddhava's posture — an ekāntin, hearing from Kṛṣṇa alone — rather than as a curious bystander collecting ideas.
  • Refuse to gossip or laugh away the day; instead spend one slot hearing or chanting about Kṛṣṇa, which Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī says cuts the bondage of birth and death.
Discussion

Share a teaching you have heard a hundred times — what would change if you received it as Kṛṣṇa speaking directly to you, the way He speaks to Uddhava here?
