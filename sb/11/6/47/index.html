Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.6.47

वातवसना य ऋषय: श्रमणा ऊर्ध्वमन्थिन: । ब्रह्माख्यं धाम ते यान्ति शान्ता: सन्न्यासीनोऽमला: ॥ ४७ ॥

Naked sages who seriously endeavor in spiritual practice, who have raised their semen upward, who are peaceful and sinless members of the renounced order, attain the spiritual abode called Brahman.
Purport

In Chapter Twelve of Bhagavad-gītā it is stated, kleśo ’dhikataras teṣām avyaktāsakta-cetasām: those who are attached to the impersonal feature of the Personality of Godhead must endure grueling penances in order to achieve impersonal liberation in the realm of Brahman. Also it is stated in the Bhāgavatam, āruhya kṛcchreṇa paraṁ padaṁ tataḥ/ patanty adho ’nādṛta-yuṣmad-aṅghrayaḥ. Kṛcchreṇa: with great struggle and trouble the yogīs climb their way up into the impersonal effulgence called brahmajyoti, but again they slip out of the jyoti and fall back into the material world because they do not take shelter of the Personality of Godhead.

Envious fools object to the “paternalism” of the Personality of Godhead, but these fools cannot take credit for the creation of their own body, brain or energy, nor can they claim credit for the air, rain, vegetables, fruits, sun, moon, and so on. In other words, they are totally dependent on the mercy of God at every second, and yet they arrogantly state that they do not want to accept the shelter of the Lord, because they are self-sufficient. In fact, some bewildered living entities even think that they themselves are God, although they cannot explain why “God” has to struggle and toil to achieve meager success in the yoga system. Therefore Uddhava is pointing out that, unlike the impersonalists and meditators, the pure devotees very easily cross over the illusory energy because they are totally attached to the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa. Lord Kṛṣṇa is always transcendental, and if one is firmly attached to the Lord’s lotus feet, then he is also transcendental. The causeless mercy of Kṛṣṇa is more valuable than millions and billions of years of one’s own struggling and straining. One should try to get the Lord’s mercy, and then everything will become very easy on the path of spiritual realization. In this age one can achieve the mercy of Lord Kṛṣṇa by constantly chanting His holy name, as recommended in the śāstra:

harer nāma harer nāma harer nāmaiva kevalam kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā

( Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa )

If one constantly chants Lord Kṛṣṇa’s holy names, avoiding offenses against the names, then surely one will achieve the same result as Uddhava. Uddhava was not interested in so-called Brahman realization, but rather he wanted to go on drinking the maddening nectar of the beautiful smiling of the Lord’s moonlike face.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Naked sages who seriously endeavor in spiritual practice, who have raised their semen upward, who are peaceful and sinless members of the renounced order, attain the spiritual abode called Brahman. Naked sages attain your abode called Brahman by jṣāna and vairāgya. Kṛṣṇa said to Arjuna: tat-paraṁ paramaṁ brahma sarvaṁ vibhajate jagat | mamaiva tat ghanaṁ tejo jṣātum arhasi bhārata || This supreme Brahman illuminats the whole universe. O Arjuna! Understand that it is my condensed light. Hari-vaṁśa Such sages go to that light. Let them go there. But we do not want to go there. We remain intoxicated with drinking the nectar of the sweet smile on your moon-like face.

Personal Guidance
Even naked, celibate, peaceful sannyāsīs reach only impersonal Brahman through harsh struggle — Kṛṣṇa's causeless mercy through nāma is incomparably easier and higher.
Practical Applications
  • Today, chant a fixed number of rounds of the holy name attentively, trusting that this single act outweighs years of austerity aimed at impersonal liberation.
  • When tempted to admire a feat of severe self-denial, remind yourself that Uddhava preferred Kṛṣṇa's smiling face to the brahmajyoti — and choose connection over conquest.
  • Refuse the self-made-man narrative for one full day: notice each breath, meal, and faculty as borrowed from Kṛṣṇa, and audibly thank Him for it.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my sādhana am I still secretly chasing the prestige of struggle rather than the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's mercy?
  • Which areas of my life do I quietly credit to my own self-sufficiency, when in truth I am dependent on Kṛṣṇa at every second?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When classmates flex their discipline — early gym, extreme diets, no-phone challenges — admire the effort but ask yourself whether it is bringing you closer to Kṛṣṇa or just to a more polished self-image.
  • Before an exam or interview, instead of relying only on grind-culture self-talk, chant Kṛṣṇa's names for a few minutes and consciously place the outcome at His feet.
  • On social media, refuse to post your sādhana as proof of how spiritual you are; let chanting and study be quiet acts of taking shelter, not performance.
householder
  • When a big career or family decision tempts you to lean on your own competence, pause, chant a round, and ask Kṛṣṇa for guidance before acting — treat His mercy as your real working capital.
  • Teach your children, by your example, that the air they breathe, the food on the table, and the parents who love them are all Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, not mere products of your hard work.
  • Resist the household cult of self-sufficiency: openly thank Kṛṣṇa at meals and at the end of the workday, so the family sees dependence on Him as strength, not weakness.
senior
  • As the body weakens, let go of any lingering pride in past austerities or achievements and invest your remaining energy in offenseless chanting of the holy name.
  • When you sit with grandchildren or younger seekers, point them away from impersonal "spiritual but not religious" framings and toward personal relationship with Kṛṣṇa, as Uddhava preferred His smiling face to Brahman.
  • Use the approach of death as a daily reminder that nothing — body, savings, reputation — was ever yours, and rehearse handing it all back to Kṛṣṇa with the holy name on your tongue.
Common Pitfalls
  • Romanticizing renunciation — treating nakedness, fasting, or celibacy as the prize itself rather than as servants of devotion to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Reading this verse as praise of impersonal Brahman, when the purport explicitly contrasts that grueling path with the easy path of nāma and shelter at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet.
  • Using "mercy is easier" as cover for laziness in chanting, hearing, and following regulative principles, ignoring that mercy flows through offenseless practice.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share an area where you have been investing real effort in self-improvement — is it bringing you closer to Kṛṣṇa personally, or only to a more impressive version of yourself?
  • The verse honors these naked sages yet the purport says they often fall back down — how do you reconcile genuine respect for their austerity with the warning that it is insufficient?
  • Where in your life do you behave like the "envious fools" of the purport, claiming credit for body, brain, or energy that you did not create?
Role Play

Two students pair up. One plays a sincere modern seeker who has just returned from a silent meditation retreat convinced they touched "pure consciousness" and no longer need a personal God. The other plays a Gauḍīya friend who must honor the seeker's sincerity, acknowledge the real austerity involved, and yet gently show — using this verse and its purport — why Uddhava chose Kṛṣṇa's moonlike face over the brahmajyoti. Switch roles after five minutes.

Objections & Responses
  • Isn't it arrogant to say chanting a name is superior to the lifelong tapasyā of these celibate sages? That sounds like spiritual entitlement.
    The verse itself salutes those sages — Uddhava is not mocking them. The point is not that we are greater, but that Kṛṣṇa's mercy is greater. A child carried by his father covers more ground than an athlete walking alone; this is humility about our own capacity, not arrogance about our practice.
  • Calling dependence on God "shelter" sounds like infantilizing paternalism — adults should stand on their own.
    The purport answers directly: we did not manufacture our body, brain, air, rain, sun, or moon. Independence is the illusion; the only real choice is between conscious dependence on Kṛṣṇa and unconscious dependence on māyā. To accept the Lord's shelter is not regression — it is finally seeing the situation as it is.
