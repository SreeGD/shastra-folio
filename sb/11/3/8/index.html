Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.8

धातूपप्लव आसन्ने व्यक्तं द्रव्यगुणात्मकम् । अनादिनिधन: कालो ह्यव्यक्तायापकर्षति ॥ ८ ॥

When the annihilation of the material elements is imminent, the Supreme Personality of Godhead in His form of eternal time withdraws the manifest cosmos, consisting of gross and subtle features, and the entire universe vanishes into nonmanifestation.
Purport

In the Third Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam Lord Kapiladeva teaches that the material nature originally exists in an inert state of equilibrium called pradhāna. When Lord Viṣṇu casts His potent glance in the form of kāla, or time, material interactions take place, culminating in the variegated creation of the material cosmos. In this verse it is stated that at the end of universal time the same kāla that originally incited the female nature into manifestation again withdraws the cosmos into its original state of inert nonmanifestation. According to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, the time potency itself, kāla, is then withdrawn, and it merges into the Supreme Soul, who manifests Himself as the original cause of material nature ( anādir ādir govindaḥ sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam ).

Such technical arrangements for creation and annihilation, birth and death, do not exist in the eternal spiritual kingdom of God. In the spiritual sky the variegated spiritual enjoyment of the Lord and His devotees is not hampered by the inferior cycles of birth, maintenance and destruction found in the material world.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
When the annihilation of the material elements is imminent, time without beginning or end withdraws the manifest cosmos, consisting of gross and subtle features, into prakṛti. The destruction is described in eight and a half verses. When destruction of the material elements takes place (dhātūpaplave), time pulls the products composed of gross and subtle matter (dravya-guṇa) into the cause, prakrṭi (avyaktāya).

Personal Guidance
At cosmic dissolution Kṛṣṇa as kāla withdraws the manifest universe into nonmanifestation — what time built, time will unbuild; only He is the eternal shelter.
Practical Applications
  • Name one possession or position you treat as permanent, and acknowledge today that kāla is already withdrawing it.
  • Begin or end your sādhana by remembering that Kṛṣṇa, not your calendar, is the master of time.
  • Refuse to stake your security on any feature of the manifest world — gross or subtle — and re-anchor in service to Him.
Reflection Questions
  • Which gross or subtle attachment am I quietly betting will outlast kāla's withdrawal?
  • If even the cosmos returns to nonmanifestation, what in my life am I still building as though it were exempt?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick one metric — grades, followers, a friend group's approval — and notice today how much of your mood it controls, knowing kāla will dissolve it.
  • When peer pressure pushes you to chase a trend, ask whether you are building on something the manifest cosmos itself will not keep.
  • Spend ten minutes off your phone in direct remembrance of Kṛṣṇa as the one who outlasts every feed and platform.
householder
  • Review one family or career plan this week and re-make the decision with the honest premise that kāla is already at work on it.
  • When anxiety about money, children, or status spikes, name it as fear of the manifest being withdrawn, and offer the worry to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Choose one household practice — meal offering, evening kīrtana, reading — to anchor your family in what kāla cannot withdraw.
senior
  • Loosen your grip on one specific holding — a role, a property, a grievance — and let the verse name it as already being withdrawn.
  • Use the reality of approaching personal annihilation of the body as a daily prompt to deepen remembrance of Kṛṣṇa rather than to despair.
  • Mentor someone younger by translating this verse into your own life: what you once thought permanent, and what alone remained.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using cosmic annihilation as an excuse for fatalism or neglect of present duties.
  • Treating kāla as an impersonal force rather than Kṛṣṇa's own potency.
  • Romanticizing dissolution intellectually while remaining gripped by ordinary fears of personal loss.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one area of life where you behave as if kāla does not apply to you — and let the group press on it.
  • Where does the difference between the temporary material cosmos and the eternal spiritual kingdom actually change a decision you made this week?
  • Is remembering universal annihilation a comfort or a threat for you right now, and why?
Role Play

A student has just received news that a long-planned project, into which they poured years, is being shut down. One partner plays the grieving student; the other plays a senior devotee who must console them using this verse — without minimizing the loss and without slipping into impersonal 'everything is temporary' platitudes.

Objections & Responses
  • Talk of cosmic dissolution feels like ancient mythology — why should a modern, scientific person take it seriously?
    The verse is not asking you to picture a scene; it is naming a structural fact you already accept — that every manifest configuration of matter is on a timeline. Gauḍīya teaching only adds that the timeline has an author, Kṛṣṇa as kāla, and that this author is personal and approachable.
  • If everything is going to be withdrawn anyway, why bother working, planning, or caring?
    Kāla withdraws the manifest, not the soul or its relationship with Kṛṣṇa. Knowing the cosmos is temporary frees you to act here without staking your identity on outcomes — sva-dharma is performed more carefully, not less, when you stop pretending it is eternal.
