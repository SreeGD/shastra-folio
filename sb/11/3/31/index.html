Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.31

स्मरन्त: स्मारयन्तश्च मिथोऽघौघहरं हरिम् । भक्त्या सञ्जातया भक्त्या बिभ्रत्युत्पुलकां तनुम् ॥ ३१ ॥
smarantaḥ smārayantaś ca
[smarantah smaarayantash cha]
mitho ’ghaugha-haraṁ harim
[mitho ’ghaugha-haram harim]
bhaktyā saṣjātayā bhaktyā
[bhaktyaa sashjaatayaa bhaktyaa]

[bibhraty utpulakaam tanum]

The devotees of the Lord constantly discuss the glories of the Personality of Godhead among themselves. Thus they constantly remember the Lord and remind one another of His qualities and pastimes. In this way, by their devotion to the principles of bhakti-yoga, the devotees please the Personality of Godhead, who takes away from them everything inauspicious. Being purified of all impediments, the devotees awaken to pure love of Godhead, and thus, even within this world, their spiritualized bodies exhibit symptoms of transcendental ecstasy, such as standing of the bodily hairs on end.
Purport

The word aghaugha-haram is very significant in this verse. Agha refers to that which is inauspicious or sinful. The living entity is actually sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha, or eternal and full of bliss and knowledge, but by neglecting his eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, he commits sinful activities and undergoes the inauspicious result in the form of material suffering. The chain of sinful reactions is called ogha, or a relentless wave of suffering. Kṛṣṇa is aghaugha-haraṁ harim; He takes away the sinful reactions of His devotees, who are thus entitled to experience the inconceivable bliss of the kingdom of God even while remaining in this world.

The words bhaktyā saṣjātayā bhaktyā indicate that there are two divisions of bhakti-yoga: sādhana-bhakti and rāgānuga-bhakti. Śrīla Prabhupāda has elaborately explained in his book The Nectar of Devotion the progress of the devotee from sādhana-bhakti, or the execution of regulative principles, to rāgānuga-bhakti, or service executed in love of Godhead. According to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, a liberated soul is always enthusiastic due to the appearance of transcendental ecstasy within his body. Thus he always aspires to remain overwhelmed in chanting the glories of the Personality of Godhead, Hari.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Remembering and inspiring other devotees to remember the Lord who destroys all sins, the devotees will develop hairs standing on end in ecstasy by prema-bhakti produced from sādhana-bhakti. In this way one will develop prema-bhakti by sādhana-bhakti. By prema-bhakti developed by sādhana-bhakti one will have a body with hairs standing on end.

Personal Guidance
Devotees who remember Hari together and remind one another of His glories become free of all inauspiciousness, and bhakti awakens bhakti — visible even in the body.
Practical Applications
  • Call one devotee today and speak only of Kṛṣṇa's qualities for ten minutes — no news, no complaints, no small talk.
  • When something inauspicious surfaces in your mind, chant Hari's names aloud and trust the aghaugha-haram promise rather than analyzing the disturbance.
  • Keep your sādhana steady today even if no rāga has appeared — the verse says bhakti is born from bhakti, not from waiting for feeling first.
Reflection Questions
  • Whom do I currently remind of Kṛṣṇa, and who reminds me — and where in my circle has this mutual smaraṇa quietly collapsed into mundane talk?
  • What inauspicious reaction (agha) am I still trying to manage by my own effort instead of letting Hari take it as I serve in association?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Replace one scroll session today with a voice note to a devotee friend about something you read in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — make Hari, not the algorithm, the source of your next emotional charge.
  • In your friend group, become the one person who can mention Kṛṣṇa without it being weird; start by asking a friend what they actually believe about God rather than performing your own certainty.
  • When exam stress or a breakup floods you, test the aghaugha-haram promise: chant for twenty minutes before opening any app, and notice what gets carried away.
householder
  • Build one fixed weekly slot — a meal, a walk, a call — with a devotee couple or friend where the explicit purpose is mutual remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, not parenting tips or career talk.
  • At the dinner table, model smārayantaś ca for your children by mentioning one quality or pastime of Hari each evening, without making it a lecture they have to endure.
  • When financial or marital anxiety rises, name it as agha and bring it into a conversation with one trusted devotee rather than ruminating alone or only with your spouse.
senior
  • Identify one younger devotee and commit to a regular call whose only agenda is reminding each other of Kṛṣṇa — your legacy is smārayantaś ca, not advice about life.
  • As the body loses capacities, lean into the verse's promise that bhakti can spiritualize even this aging tanu; let your visible steadiness in chanting become the teaching.
  • Loosen your grip on grievances accumulated over decades by treating them as aghaugha you no longer have to carry — bring them into kīrtana and association rather than into your will.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating ecstatic bodily symptoms as the goal to manufacture, rather than as a spontaneous fruit of sādhana-bhakti maturing into rāgānuga-bhakti.
  • Waiting in isolation for rāga to arise before doing regulated sādhana, when the verse says bhakti generates bhakti through mutual remembrance.
  • Reducing 'discussing Kṛṣṇa' to gossip about devotees or institutional politics, which is the opposite of the mitho 'gha-augha-haram exchange described here.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a recent conversation where smaraṇa of Kṛṣṇa actually happened between you and another devotee — and one where it should have but did not. What made the difference?
  • The verse promises Hari removes aghaugha from those who do this together. Where in your life do you secretly doubt this promise, and rely on therapy, planning, or willpower instead?
  • Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura says the liberated soul is always enthusiastic. Where does forced enthusiasm in our communities look like the symptom described here, and where is it real?
Role Play

Two devotees meet for coffee. One has just received bad news at work and starts venting about a colleague. The other knows the verse SB 11.3.31. Without lecturing or shutting the first person down, the second must redirect the conversation into mutual remembrance of Hari's qualities and pastimes within five minutes — staying warm, not preachy. Run it twice and switch roles.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like an echo chamber — a group of believers reinforcing each other's emotions and calling the resulting goosebumps 'transcendental.'
    The verse is precise: the symptom arises from bhaktyā saṣjātayā bhaktyā, devotion born of devotion under regulative sādhana, not from emotional reinforcement. Prabhupāda traces the progression in The Nectar of Devotion; the test is whether aghaugha — the chain of sinful reactions and material suffering — actually lifts over time. An echo chamber intensifies anxiety and ego; this exchange dissolves them.
  • I prefer to keep my spiritual life private. Why do I need to discuss Kṛṣṇa with others at all?
    The verse pairs smarantaḥ with smārayantaś ca — remembering and causing to remember — as a single act. Private remembrance alone tends to fade because the mind is fickle and conditioned; mutual reminding is Hari's mercy structure for keeping smaraṇa alive. Privacy is often ahaṅkāra protecting itself from being seen; sādhu-saṅga gently dismantles that, which is exactly why the soul resists it.
