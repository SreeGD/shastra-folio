Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.48

लब्ध्वानुग्रह आचार्यात् तेन सन्दर्शितागम: । महापुरुषमभ्यर्चेन्मूर्त्याभिमतयात्मन: ॥ ४८ ॥

Having obtained the mercy of his spiritual master, who reveals to the disciple the injunctions of Vedic scriptures, the devotee should worship the Supreme Personality of Godhead in the particular personal form of the Lord the devotee finds most attractive.
Purport

According to Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, the word labdhvānugrahaḥ indicates formal initiation by a bona fide spiritual master. It is stated in the Padma Purāṇa:

ṣaṭ-karma-nipuṇo vipro mantra-tantra-viśāradaḥ avaiṣṇavo gurur na syād vaiṣṇavaḥ śvapaco guruḥ

A bona fide spiritual master must be a soul surrendered at the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord. In the Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa it is stated:

bodhaḥ kaluṣitas tena daurātmyaṁ prakaṭī-kṛtam gurur yena parityaktas tena tyaktaḥ purā hariḥ

“One pollutes his own intelligence and exhibits severe weakness of character when he rejects his own spiritual master. Indeed, such a person has already rejected the Supreme Lord, Hari.” The bona fide disciple should always remember that his entire understanding of Vedic knowledge is coming through the mercy of his bona fide spiritual master. If one superficially or whimsically accepts and rejects a bona fide Vaiṣṇava spiritual master, sometimes becoming attracted to another spiritual master, one commits a vaiṣṇava-aparādha, a great offense against the devotees of the Lord. Sometimes a foolish neophyte mistakenly thinks that the relationship with the spiritual master is meant for the sense gratification of the disciple, and therefore in the name of spiritual aspirations such a fool gives up a bona fide Vaiṣṇava guru. One should understand oneself to be the eternal servant of the guru. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, however, has quoted this verse from the Nārada-paṣcarātra:

avaiṣṇavopadiṣṭena mantreṇa nirayaṁ vrajet punaś ca vidhinā samyag grāhayed vaiṣṇavād guroḥ

“One who is initiated into a mantra by a non-Vaiṣṇava must go to hell. Therefore he should again be initiated properly, according to the prescribed method, by a Vaiṣṇava guru. ” It is the duty of the bona fide spiritual master to examine carefully the qualification of the disciple, and the disciple should similarly approach a bona fide spiritual master. Otherwise, the foolish disciple and the indiscriminate guru may both be punished by the laws of nature.

One should not artificially try to assimilate all of the apparently conflicting branches of Vedic knowledge. Vedaiś ca sarvair aham eva vedyaḥ. Conditioned souls have various conflicting natures, which are engaged by apparently conflicting Vedic injunctions called pravṛtti and nivṛtti-mārga. But the easiest path is simply to learn the process of regularly worshiping advaya-jṣāna, Lord Viṣṇu. All the demigods mentioned in the Vedas are paraphernalia for the service of the Supreme Lord, Viṣṇu. Whatever exists in the visible material world is also meant to be engaged in the Lord’s service; otherwise, it has no value. If one artificially renounces material things useful in the service of the Supreme Lord, he loses his spiritual qualification of seeing everything as meant for Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure and will be forced to think of material objects as meant for his own sense enjoyment. In other words, material things should be accepted or rejected according to the pleasure of the Supreme Lord. Otherwise, one will fall down from the standard of pure devotional service. As stated in this verse, labdhvānugraha ācāryāt: such discrimination can be learned when one receives the mercy of a bona fide spiritual master, who reveals to the sincere disciple the practical application of Vedic knowledge.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Having obtained the mercy of his guru, who reveals to the disciple the injunctions of Vedic scriptures, the devotee should worship the Supreme Lord in the particular personal form of the Lord the devotee finds most attractive. The method of worship is described. Having been shown the method of worshiping the deity (sandarśitāgamaḥ), one should worship the Lord.

Personal Guidance
Having received the ācārya's mercy, which unlocks the scriptures, worship Kṛṣṇa in the personal form of Him that most attracts your heart.
Practical Applications
  • Identify the specific personal form of Kṛṣṇa that most attracts you, and worship that form today rather than drifting between many.
  • Open śāstra only after offering pranāms to your guru, treating each insight that arises as his mercy made tangible.
  • Refuse the temptation to shop for a new guru when your sādhana feels dry — recognize that whim as the offense the verse warns against.
Reflection Questions
  • Which form of Kṛṣṇa actually draws my heart, and am I worshiping that form, or one I think I am supposed to prefer?
  • Where in my study of scripture am I trusting my own intelligence rather than the mercy that comes through my spiritual master?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before scrolling through more spiritual content online, sit with one verse your guru or his books have already given you and let that be today's input.
  • Notice the impulse to switch teachers, courses, or communities the moment things feel boring — treat that impulse itself as something to examine, not obey.
  • Pick one form of Kṛṣṇa from Prabhupāda's books that genuinely moves you, and keep a small picture of that form where you study for exams.
householder
  • Establish one deity or picture at the family altar that corresponds to the form of the Lord you actually feel drawn to, and worship there daily rather than rotating to please everyone.
  • When scripture conflicts with what feels convenient for the family budget or schedule, defer to how your guru has applied the verse rather than to your own reading.
  • Refuse to badmouth your spiritual master in front of your spouse or children even when sādhana is hard — the verse warns this is already rejection of Hari Himself.
senior
  • Use this stage to deepen worship of the one form of the Lord that has accompanied you longest, rather than experimenting with new practices for novelty.
  • When mentoring younger devotees, point them back to their own ācārya's instructions rather than to your personal opinions.
  • Make peace with any past offense toward your guru — verbally, in writing, or in prayer — before the body's final transition, since the verse names this as the gravest weakness of character.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating initiation as a one-time ceremony rather than a permanent relationship that governs how scripture is received.
  • Choosing an iṣṭa-deva based on aesthetics or fashion rather than the natural attraction the ācārya helps uncover.
  • Using the freedom to choose a personal form as license to mix and match deities outside the Vaiṣṇava line, blurring Kṛṣṇa with demigods.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share with your partner which form of the Lord most attracts you and why — then examine together whether that attraction is cultivated or merely inherited.
  • Discuss what changes about your reading of Bhāgavatam when you read it as the ācārya's mercy rather than as information you are acquiring.
  • Debate honestly: is shifting from one guru to another ever justified, and how would you tell a real reason from a self-serving one?
Role Play

A new devotee tells you they were initiated two years ago but now feel more drawn to a different teacher whose classes are more engaging, and they ask whether they should re-initiate. Walk them through the verse and the Padma Purāṇa and Brahma-vaivarta references in Prabhupāda's purport without either flattering their restlessness or shaming them.

Objections & Responses
  • Why do I need a guru at all? I can read the books directly and form my own understanding of Kṛṣṇa.
    The verse says ācāryāt — through the ācārya — because Vedic knowledge has pravṛtti and nivṛtti currents that look contradictory on the page; without one who has lived the synthesis, the reader inevitably picks the verses that flatter his current conditioning and calls that 'his own understanding.'
  • Letting me pick the form of God I like most sounds like spiritual consumerism — choose-your-own-deity.
    It is the opposite: the ācārya first qualifies you so that what attracts you is your actual eternal relationship surfacing, not your present taste; the freedom is real, but it is freedom within a tradition that has already filtered out the false candidates.
