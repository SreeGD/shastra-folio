Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.8.5

मुनि: प्रसन्नगम्भीरो दुर्विगाह्यो दुरत्यय: । अनन्तपारो ह्यक्षोभ्य: स्तिमितोद इवार्णव: ॥ ५ ॥

A saintly sage is happy and pleasing in his external behavior, whereas internally he is most grave and thoughtful. Because his knowledge is immeasurable and unlimited he is never disturbed, and thus in all respects he is like the tranquil waters of the unfathomable and unsurpassable ocean.
Purport

Even in the midst of great distress, a self-realized sage does not lose control of himself, nor is his spiritual knowledge lost. Thus he is akṣobhya, or undisturbed. His mind is fixed on the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is the reservoir of unlimited spiritual bliss, and having linked his consciousness with the supreme consciousness, his knowledge cannot be measured. A pure devotee, having taken shelter of the Lord’s lotus feet, possesses great spiritual power, and therefore he cannot be surpassed or overwhelmed. In fact, having developed his spiritual body, he is not affected by the deteriorating actions of time. Although outwardly he is friendly and pleasing to everyone, internally his mind is fixed in the Absolute Truth, and no one can understand his actual purpose or plan. Even the most intelligent human being cannot understand the mental activities of a self-realized devotee who has given up material life based on lust and greed and taken shelter at the lotus feet of the Lord. Such a great soul can be compared to the mighty ocean. Innumerable powerful rivers plunge into the ocean, but the ocean remains calm and peaceful. Thus, a saintly person is understood to be, like the ocean, pleasing, unfathomable, grave, unsurpassable, unlimited and unshakable.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
A sage is happy but inscrutable, impossible to understand and impossible to surpass. He does not reveal his inner nature and is without agitation, like the calm ocean. He explains what he learned from the ocean in two verses. He is inscrutable. Even the intelligent person cannot understand him. The yogī’s mind cannot be understood by anyone (durvigāhyaḥ). He cannot be surpassed because of his great power. Though sick or in difficult circumstances, he does not lose control of himself and reveal his inner nature (ananta-paraḥ). He is unagitated because he has conquered lust.
Personal Guidance
Be ocean-like: pleasant outside, grave within — undisturbed because consciousness is linked to Kṛṣṇa, the unlimited reservoir of bliss.
Practical Applications
  • Notice the next moment of inner agitation today, and instead of venting it, silently fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa before responding outwardly.
  • Greet every person you meet today with a pleasant, friendly demeanor, while keeping your actual purpose and sādhana private and undisclosed.
  • When a 'river' of praise or criticism rushes at you today, refuse to let your surface rise or fall — receive it and return to japa.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life is my external behavior grave and severe while my inner mind is shallow and easily disturbed — the exact inversion of the sage described here?
  • Which 'rivers' — opinions, losses, praise, fears — am I still letting alter the level of my consciousness, and what would akṣobhya look like in that specific situation?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When grades, a breakup, or a friend group's drama hits today, practice one cycle of the ocean: feel it, return to Kṛṣṇa internally, then respond outwardly with a normal pleasant face.
  • Stop narrating your inner life on social media for one week — keep your sādhana, your struggles, and your aspirations gambhīra, shared only with a trusted devotee or guide.
  • Notice the peer who is loudest about being 'unbothered' and the peer who is quietly steady under pressure — study the difference, and choose which ocean you want to become.
householder
  • When you walk through the door after work today, leave the office's rivers at the threshold and greet your spouse and children with a prasanna face before processing your own day.
  • Make one financial or family decision this week from gravity rather than reactivity — sit with it overnight, consult Kṛṣṇa in your morning japa, and only then speak.
  • Refuse to let your children see every fluctuation of your moods; let them grow up knowing a parent who is friendly on the surface and anchored underneath, so they learn what steadiness looks like.
senior
  • As the body's 'rivers' — aches, diagnoses, losses of peers — flow in more frequently, practice receiving each one into the ocean of remembrance of Kṛṣṇa rather than letting it churn the surface.
  • Mentor a younger devotee this month by being prasanna and available, while keeping the deepest portions of your bhajana private — let them sense depth without being shown it.
  • Loosen one more visible attachment this season — a possession, a role, an old grievance — and notice how the ocean of the self becomes a little more unfathomable as the surface clutter recedes.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking a cold, aloof, or unfriendly demeanor for gravity — the verse insists the sage is prasanna, pleasing and happy externally, not stern.
  • Cultivating a pleasant outer face as social performance while the inner mind remains agitated by lust and greed, instead of actually fixing it on Kṛṣṇa.
  • Treating 'undisturbed' as numbness or emotional suppression rather than the natural steadiness of a mind sheltered at the Lord's lotus feet.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a recent moment when you were visibly disturbed — what was the 'river' that entered, and what would it have taken for your ocean to remain stimita?
  • The verse says no one can understand the sage's actual purpose or plan — is this secrecy compatible with the openness and transparency our modern relationships demand, or are they in real tension?
  • Prasanna outside, gambhīra inside — most of us live the opposite. Discuss with your partner: which inversion is more dangerous in your own life right now, and why?
Role Play

Two students pair up. One plays a long-time devotee who has just received simultaneously bad news (a close family member's serious illness) and unexpected public praise (recognition from a senior Vaiṣṇava). A guest arrives at the door needing hospitality and conversation. The devotee must host the guest pleasantly and attentively for five minutes without revealing either piece of news or letting either disturb the exchange. The other student plays the guest and afterwards reports what they sensed beneath the surface.

Objections & Responses
  • Hiding your inner state from people is dishonest — modern psychology says authenticity and emotional transparency are healthier than this 'ocean' performance.
    The verse is not asking you to fake pleasantness over a roiling interior; it is describing one whose interior is genuinely stimita because it rests in Kṛṣṇa. The pleasant exterior is not a mask hiding turmoil — it is the natural overflow of a mind that no longer needs to broadcast its agitations onto others. Authenticity to the soul outranks authenticity to a temporary mental weather pattern.
  • An ocean that absorbs everything without reacting sounds passive — shouldn't a devotee speak up against injustice and feel real grief at suffering?
    Akṣobhya does not mean inert. The ocean still moves ships, feeds lands, and receives every river — but its level does not rise and fall with each one. A self-realized devotee acts decisively and feels real compassion for the conditioned soul, but his consciousness is not destabilized by the very disturbances he is working to relieve. That steadiness is precisely what makes sustained, effective service possible.
