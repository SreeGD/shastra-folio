Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.11.28

त्वं ब्रह्म परमं व्योम पुरुष: प्रकृते: पर: । अवतीर्णोऽसि भगवन् स्वेच्छोपात्तपृथग्वपु: ॥ २८ ॥

My dear Lord, as the Absolute Truth You are transcendental to material nature, and like the sky You are never entangled in any way. Still, being controlled by Your devotees’ love, You accept many different forms, incarnating according to Your devotees’ desires.
Purport

The pure devotees of the Lord propagate devotional service all over the world, and therefore, although separate from the personal form of the Lord, they are considered to be manifestations of God’s mercy and potency. As stated in Caitanya-caritāmṛta ( Antya 7.11) : kṛṣṇa-śakti vinā nahe tāra pravartana.

The Lord is like the sky ( vyoma ) because although expanded everywhere, He is not entangled in anything. He is prakṛteḥ paraḥ, or completely transcendental to material nature. The Lord is fully self-satisfied and is therefore indifferent to the affairs of the material world. Still, because of His causeless mercy, the Lord desires to expand pure devotional service, and for this reason He incarnates within the material world to uplift the fallen conditioned souls.

The Lord descends in selected spiritual bodies to please His loving devotees. Sometimes He appears in His original form as Kṛṣṇa. And even Kṛṣṇa Himself appears in different forms to special devotees so that they may fully develop their loving sentiments for Him. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī gives several examples of the Lord’s special mercy upon His devotees. Lord Kṛṣṇa personally went to the home of Jāmbavān and there displayed a form with slightly angry glances. In that form, the Lord enjoyed a fight with His devotee. The Lord displayed His form as Dattātreya to Atri Muni and similarly bestowed special mercy upon Lord Brahmā, the demigods, Akrūra and innumerable other devotees. And in Vṛndāvana the Lord displayed His most beautiful form as Govinda to the fortunate inhabitants.

Śrīla Madhvācārya has quoted from the Prakāśa-saṁhitā as follows. “The Lord accepts different spiritual bodies according to the desire of His devotees. For example, the Lord agreed to become the son of Vasudeva and Devakī. Thus, although Lord Kṛṣṇa has an eternal, blissful spiritual form, He appears to enter within the body of His devotee who becomes His mother. Although we speak of the Lord’s ‘taking on a body,’ the Lord does not change His form, as do the conditioned souls, who must change their material bodies. The Lord appears in His own eternally unchangeable forms. Lord Hari always appears in the forms that are especially desired by His loving devotees, and never in other forms. However, if one thinks that the Lord, in the manner of an ordinary person taking birth, becomes the physical son of Vasudeva or other devotees, then one is victimized by illusion. The Lord merely expands His spiritual potency, causing His pure devotees to think, ‘Kṛṣṇa is now my son.’ One should understand that the Supreme Personality of Godhead never accepts or rejects a material body, nor does He ever give up His eternal spiritual forms; rather, the Lord eternally manifests His blissful bodies according to the loving sentiments of His eternal pure devotees.”

Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī mentions that the word vyoma also indicates the Lord’s name of Paravyoma, or the Lord of the spiritual sky. One should not misinterpret this verse to mean that Lord Kṛṣṇa is impersonal, like the material sky, or that the form of Kṛṣṇa is merely another selected incarnation equal to any other. Such casual and whimsical speculations cannot be accepted as actual spiritual knowledge. Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead ( kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam ), and the Lord has explained elaborately in Bhagavad-gītā that He is the original source of everything. Therefore, the pure devotees of the Lord are eternally engaged, in full knowledge and bliss, in loving service to the Lord’s original form as Kṛṣṇa. The whole purpose of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is to arouse our love for Lord Kṛṣṇa, and one should not foolishly misunderstand this great purpose.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
As the Supreme Brahman, you are unattached like the sky, since you are superior to material nature. Still, by the desire of your devotees, you produce many forms of devotees to spread bhakti. Those devotees who teach bhakti are different from your svarūpa, but arise from your svarūpa. You are the Supreme Brahman, without material association like the sky, since you are superior to prakṛti. Yet, you appear by your mercy in this world to deliver the jīvas. You manifest many forms according to the desires of your devotees. This means that you give forms which arise from your svarūpa to your devotees so that bhakti can be spread. Nārada has said: prayujyamāne mayi tāṁ śuddhāṁ bhāgavatīṁ tanum ārabdha-karma-nirvāṇo nyapatat pāṣca-bhautikaḥ Having been awarded a transcendental body befitting an associate of the Lord, I quit the body made of five material elements, and thus all acquired fruitive results of work stopped. SB 1.6.29 Thus people meditate upon guru, your devotee, as your svarūpa. Another meaning is “You appear in this world in order to spread your bhakti, even though you are indifferent to the world since you enjoy in yourself.” By your will, you give rise to many forms like Kapila, Dattātreya and Nārada. It is said: anye ca saṁskṛtātmāno vidhinābhihitena te yajanti tvan-mayās tvāṁ vai bahu-mūrty-eka-mūrtikam And yet others—those whose intelligence is pure—follow the injunctions of Vaiṣṇava scriptures promulgated by you. Absorbing their minds in you, they worship you as the one Supreme Lord manifesting in multiple forms. SB 10.40.7

Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa is transcendental like the sky, yet bound by His devotees' love He descends in the very forms their bhakti desires.
Practical Applications
  • Begin your sādhana today addressing Kṛṣṇa personally by name, not as "the Absolute" or "the Self" — let the personalist framing reshape your prayer.
  • Notice every time you reduce Kṛṣṇa to a concept or symbol, and replace that thought with a concrete recollection of Him as a Person who responds to love.
  • Serve one devotee today as if serving the Lord directly — since pure devotees are described here as manifestations of His mercy and potency.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I quietly preferring an impersonal, manageable Absolute over a Person who can be loved, displeased, and pleased?
  • If Kṛṣṇa descends in forms shaped by His devotees' love, what form of relationship am I, by the quality of my bhakti, actually inviting?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • On social media and in class discussions, refuse the easy move of calling Kṛṣṇa "just a symbol" to sound sophisticated — defend Him as a Person, even quietly, even to yourself.
  • When peers frame spirituality as "finding the divine within," notice the impersonalist drift and translate it back into personalist terms in your own thinking before you absorb it.
  • Pick one devotee-friend or teacher this week and serve them concretely — a ride, a meal, careful listening — remembering this verse's teaching that devotees are manifestations of His mercy.
householder
  • In family decisions about money, schooling, and work, pause to ask whom you are actually surrendering the outcome to — an impersonal "universe" or Kṛṣṇa as a Person — and adjust your prayer accordingly.
  • Teach your children Kṛṣṇa's name and form before abstractions about "God" — let their first religious vocabulary be personal, since this verse insists the Lord's forms are real, not metaphorical.
  • When your spouse or relatives drift toward impersonalist or syncretic language at the dinner table, respond with patience but do not nod along — gently hold the personalist line this verse defends.
senior
  • As you loosen attachments to body and roles, deliberately deepen attachment to Kṛṣṇa's specific form — the form your bhakti has been quietly shaping toward all these years.
  • Mentor a younger devotee in personalist theology, using this verse to inoculate them against the fashionable impersonalism they will meet outside.
  • Use the nearness of mortality to ask honestly: which form of the Lord do I most long to meet, and is my daily sādhana actually inviting that meeting, as this verse promises it will?
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading "like the sky" as proof of impersonalism and quietly dropping the personal Kṛṣṇa, when the verse explicitly affirms Him as puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ.
  • Treating Kṛṣṇa's incarnations as interchangeable options on a spiritual menu, instead of recognizing kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam as the original.
  • Imagining the Lord "takes on" a material body the way a conditioned soul does, instead of understanding that He manifests His own eternal spiritual forms by His own sweet will.
Discussion Prompts
  • Prabhupāda says the Lord is indifferent to the material world yet descends out of causeless mercy — how do you reconcile transcendence and intimate involvement without collapsing one into the other?
  • Share a moment when your conception of Kṛṣṇa shifted from idea to Person — what changed in your sādhana, and where do you still slip back?
  • If the Lord accepts forms according to His devotees' desires, what does that say about the responsibility of our own bhāva — and where in this group do you sense honest disagreement about that?
Role Play

One student plays a sincere seeker who has been reading Advaita literature and insists Kṛṣṇa's likeness to the sky proves He is ultimately impersonal; the other plays a Gauḍīya friend who must, using only this verse and its purport, help the seeker see that vyoma here points to Paravyoma and that svecchopātta-pṛthag-vapuḥ affirms a personal Lord who freely accepts forms for His devotees.

Objections & Responses
  • If Kṛṣṇa is truly transcendental and self-satisfied, the idea that He is "controlled by devotees' love" sounds like sentimental projection — why would the Absolute need anyone?
    He does not need anyone; the verse and purport are clear that He is fully self-satisfied. "Controlled by love" is not lack but causeless mercy — He freely allows Himself to be moved by bhakti, the way a father freely lets a small child lead him by the finger. Transcendence is not aloofness; it is the freedom to descend without being entangled.
  • Saying the Lord appears in different forms for different devotees sounds like every tradition is just projecting its preferred deity — isn't this religious relativism?
    The purport guards against exactly that misreading. Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead, kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam, and the various forms mentioned — Dattātreya to Atri, the angry-glanced fighter to Jāmbavān, Govinda in Vṛndāvana — are His own eternal spiritual forms, not subjective projections. Devotees' love calls forth which form He shows, not whether He exists.
