Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.11.21

Coming to this conclusion of all knowledge, one should give up the false conception of material variety that one imposes upon the soul and thus cease one’s material existence. The mind should be fixed on Me, since I am all-pervading.
Purport

Although in previous verses Lord Kṛṣṇa has described the lifestyle and approach of the impersonal philosophers who meditate on the distinction between matter and spirit, the Lord here rejects the path of jṣāna, or speculation, and comes to the final conclusion, bhakti-yoga. The path of jṣāna is interesting only to one who does not know that Lord Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, as stated in Bhagavad-gītā (7.19) :

bahūnāṁ janmanām ante jṣānavān mām prapadyate vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā su-durlabhaḥ

The words vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti, or “Vāsudeva is everything,” are similar to the words sarva-ge found in this verse. One should know why the Personality of Godhead is all-pervading. The first verse of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam states, janmādy asya yataḥ: the Supreme Lord is the source of everything. And as stated in the previous verse of this chapter, He creates, maintains and annihilates everything. Thus the Lord is not all-pervading in the manner of air or sunlight; rather, the Lord is all-pervading as the absolute controller who holds in His hands the destiny of every living entity.

Everything is ultimately an expansion of Kṛṣṇa, and therefore there is really no other object of meditation besides Kṛṣṇa. Meditation upon any other object is also meditation on Kṛṣṇa but is imperfectly performed, as confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā by the word avidhi-pūrvakam. The Lord also states in the Gītā that all living entities are on the path back home, back to Godhead. Because of ignorance, however, some of them go backward or stop along the way, foolishly thinking that their journey is finished, when in fact they are suspended in one of the minor potencies of the Supreme Lord. If one wants to intimately understand the nature of the Absolute Truth, one must take to the path of love of Godhead. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (18.55) :

bhaktyā mām abhijānāti yāvān yaś cāsmi tattvataḥ tato māṁ tattvato jṣātvā viśate tad-anantaram

“One can understand the Supreme Personality as He is only by devotional service. And when one is in full consciousness of the Supreme Lord by such devotion, he can enter into the kingdom of God.”

The words nānātva-bhramam in this verse indicate false identification with the gross and subtle material bodies. The word bhramam indicates a mistake; it can also mean “wandering” or “rotating.” The conditioned living entity, because of his mistake of falling into illusion, is wandering through a succession of material bodies, sometimes appearing as a demigod and sometimes as a worm in stool. The word upārameta means that one should stop such fruitless wandering and fix one’s mind on the Absolute Truth, the Supreme Lord, who is the true object of everyone’s love. Such a conclusion is not sentimental but is the result of keen analytic intelligence ( jijṣāsayā ). Thus after elaborately explaining to Uddhava many aspects of analytic knowledge, the Lord now comes to the ultimate conclusion, Kṛṣṇa consciousness, pure love of Godhead. Without such love there is no question of eternally fixing one’s mind on the Lord.

Quoting from the Viveka, Śrīla Madhvācārya states that nānātva-bhramam indicates the following illusions: considering the living entity to be the Supreme; considering all living entities to be ultimately one entity without separate individuality; considering that there are many Gods; thinking that Kṛṣṇa is not God; and considering that the material universe is the ultimate reality. All of these illusions are called bhrama, or mistakes, but such ignorance can be eliminated at once by the chanting of the holy names of Kṛṣṇa: Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Rejecting the misconception of variety concerning ātmā by deliberation, fixing the mind devoid of māyā upon me, the Lord who pervades everywhere, a person will attain sāyujya. Coming to the conclusion of all knowledge, one should give up the false conception of material variety that one imposes upon the soul and thus cease one’s material existence. The mind should be fixed on me, since I am all-pervading. The Lord summarizes the path of jṣāna. Discarding false concepts of variety concerning the self—the gross and subtle bodies, bodies of devatā and human--by deliberation (jijṣāsayā) as described above, fixing the mind devoid of contamination of māyā (virajam) on me by bhakti, one attains sāyujya with me (upārameta) by vijṣāna arising from that bhakti. It is said: bhaktyā tv ananyayā śakya aham evaṁ-vidho ’rjuna | jṣātuṁ draṣṭuṁ ca tattvena praveṣṭuṁ ca paraṅtapa || Only by ananyā bhakti it is possible to know me, see me, or even merge with me, O Arjuna. Personal Guidance
Drop the illusion of material variety imposed on the soul, and rest the mind on Kṛṣṇa — the all-pervading absolute controller, not an impersonal void.
Practical Applications
  • Each time you catch yourself identifying with body, role, or status today, name it as nānātva-bhramam and bring the mind back to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra attentively this morning as the direct antidote to the five bhramas Madhvācārya names — especially 'I am the Supreme' and 'Kṛṣṇa is not God.'
  • Refuse to treat any object of your attention today as ultimately separate from Kṛṣṇa — meet it knowing He is sarva-ga, all-pervading as the controller behind it.
Reflection Questions
  • Which specific 'variety' in my life — a body, a role, a possession, a relationship — am I still treating as a self-existing reality apart from Kṛṣṇa?
  • Where am I letting jñāna-style analysis become a substitute for the bhakti-yoga this verse ultimately points to?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • On social media today, notice each time you measure yourself by a body, a follower count, or a peer group — label that comparison nānātva-bhramam and chant one round before responding.
  • Before an exam or interview, offer the effort to Kṛṣṇa as sarva-ga rather than to your future self-image, and refuse the story that the result defines who you are.
  • When friends frame spirituality as 'all paths lead to the same One,' gently test that against this verse — Kṛṣṇa here is a specific Person to whom the mind is offered, not a generic Absolute.
householder
  • In one family decision today — money, children's schooling, a work choice — consciously fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa first, then act, so the decision is mayy arpya rather than ego-driven.
  • Speak to your spouse or child this evening seeing them as souls held by the sarva-ga Lord, not as extensions of your identity or your plans for them.
  • Refuse the householder's special bhrama — that career, house, and reputation are the 'real' world while sādhana is decorative; treat your morning chanting as the load-bearing pillar of the day.
senior
  • As bodily strength loosens, practice upārameta deliberately — withdraw the mind from one long-held material identification today and re-fix it on Kṛṣṇa.
  • When mentoring a younger person, point them past your own personality toward Kṛṣṇa as sarva-ga, so what you leave behind is direction, not dependence.
  • Facing mortality, reject the two extreme bhramas at once — 'I will simply merge into the One' and 'this body and its legacy are what remain' — and rest the mind, daily, on Kṛṣṇa as a Person.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading 'give up material variety' as license for impersonalism — collapsing Kṛṣṇa into 'the One' rather than fixing the mind on Him as a Person.
  • Treating cessation of material existence (upārameta) as inner withdrawal or quietism, while the verse asks for the mind arpita — offered — to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Mistaking keen analytic intelligence (jijñāsā) for the goal, when the Lord here uses analysis only to deliver the student into pure devotion.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a concrete 'variety' you have been mistaking for the self this week — and test together whether your group hears it as nānātva-bhramam or as legitimate sva-dharma.
  • Madhvācārya lists five bhramas, including 'all living entities are ultimately one.' Where in contemporary spiritual talk — even inside our own saṅga — do you hear these creeping in?
  • If Kṛṣṇa is sarva-ga, all-pervading as controller, what changes in how you treat the next difficult person or situation you meet — and where does that framing feel forced rather than real?
Role Play

One student plays a sincere seeker who has spent years on impersonal meditation — 'I am That, all is One' — and is now curious about Kṛṣṇa. The other plays a Gauḍīya friend who must, in five minutes, use only this verse and its purport to show why fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa as a Person is the actual conclusion of the analysis the seeker already loves.

Objections & Responses
  • Telling me to drop 'material variety' sounds like spiritual bypassing — my job, my children, my body are real and need attention.
    The verse does not deny that these exist; it denies that they exist apart from Kṛṣṇa. You still work, parent, and care for the body — but as offerings to the sarva-ga Lord, not as self-standing realities that define who you are. That is precisely the difference between impersonal withdrawal and bhakti-yoga.
  • If Kṛṣṇa is all-pervading, why insist on Him as a specific Person? Isn't 'the Absolute' enough?
    Prabhupāda is explicit here: the Lord is not all-pervading like air or sunlight, but as the absolute controller holding every living entity's destiny. An impersonal Absolute cannot be loved, surrendered to, or served — and this verse, with sarva-ge and mayy arpya, asks for exactly that personal offering of the mind.
