Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.11.34-41

My dear Uddhava, one can give up false pride and prestige by engaging in the following devotional activities. One may purify oneself by seeing, touching, worshiping, serving, and offering prayers of glorification and obeisances to My form as the Deity and to My pure devotees. One should also glorify My transcendental qualities and activities, hear with love and faith the narrations of My glories and constantly meditate on Me. One should offer to Me whatever one acquires, and accepting oneself as My eternal servant, one should give oneself completely to Me. One should always discuss My birth and activities and enjoy life by participating in festivals, such as Janmāṣṭamī, which glorify My pastimes. In My temple, one should also participate in festivals and ceremonies by singing, dancing, playing musical instruments and discussing Me with other Vaiṣṇavas. One should observe all the regularly celebrated annual festivals by attending ceremonies, pilgrimages and making offerings. One should also observe religious vows such as Ekādaśī and take initiation by the procedures mentioned in the Vedas, Paṣcarātra and other, similar literatures. One should faithfully and lovingly support the installation of My Deity, and individually or in cooperation with others one should work for the construction of Kṛṣṇa conscious temples and cities as well as flower gardens, fruit gardens and special areas to celebrate My pastimes. One should consider oneself to be My humble servant, without duplicity, and thus should help to clean the temple, which is My home. First one should sweep and dust thoroughly, and then one should further cleanse with water and cow dung. Having dried the temple, one should sprinkle scented water and decorate the temple with maṇḍalas. One should thus act just like My servant. A devotee should never advertise his devotional activities; therefore his service will not be the cause of false pride. One should never use lamps that are offered to Me for other purposes simply because there is need of illumination, and similarly, one should never offer to Me anything that has been offered to or used by others. Whatever is most desired by one within this material world, and whatever is most dear to oneself — one should offer that very thing to Me. Such an offering qualifies one for eternal life.
Purport

In these eight verses Lord Kṛṣṇa ends His discussion of saintly qualities in general and describes the specific characteristics of the devotees of the Lord. Lord Kṛṣṇa has clearly described both here and in Bhagavad-gītā that the ultimate goal of life is to surrender fully to Him and become His pure devotee. Herein the Lord elaborately describes the process of devotional service. One should offer everything that one acquires to the Lord, thinking, “Lord Kṛṣṇa has sent these things so that I may serve Him nicely.” One ultimately should understand that the minute spirit soul is part and parcel of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and thus one should surrender one’s very self to the Lord. Just as an ordinary servant is meek and submissive to his master, similarly, a devotee should always be submissive to his spiritual master, who is a representative of Lord Kṛṣṇa. He should realize how his body and mind are purified simply by his seeing the spiritual master or by accepting on his head the water offered to the spiritual master. It is emphasized in these verses that one should attend Vaiṣṇava festivals. As far as possible, large festivals should be held all over the world so that people can gradually learn how to perfect human life. The words mamārcā-sthāpane śraddhā are significant. Here Lord Kṛṣṇa states that one should have faith in His Deity worship, since the Lord is personally present as the Deity. The words udyānopavanākrīḍa-pura-mandira-karmaṇi indicate that there should be a serious endeavor to construct beautiful temples and Vaiṣṇava cities with ample parks, orchards and flower gardens. An outstanding example of such endeavors can be presently seen in India at the Māyāpur Candrodaya Mandira.

The words dīpāvalokaṁ me nopayuṣjyān niveditam indicate that one may never use the Deity’s paraphernalia for sense gratification. If there is a shortage of electricity or lights, one may not use the Deity’s lamps, nor should one ever offer to Lord Kṛṣṇa paraphernalia previously offered to or used by others. In these verses, the importance of Deity worship and Vaiṣṇava festivals is emphasized in many ways. Lord Kṛṣṇa promises that whoever sincerely performs these activities will certainly go back home, back to Godhead ( tad ānantyāya kalpate ). One should offer his most dear possession to Lord Kṛṣṇa, not that which is superfluous or unwanted. If one is most attached to his family, one should see that his family is engaged in Lord Kṛṣṇa’s service. If one is most attached to money, that should be given for propagating Kṛṣṇa consciousness. And if one considers one’s intelligence to be most valuable, he should preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness with great logic and reason. If we offer our most valuable possessions to Lord Kṛṣṇa, we will automatically become dear to the Lord and go back to Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda

Translation
One should see, touch, worship serve, praise, offer respects and glorify the qualities and activities of my deity form and my devotees. You have asked how to perform bhakti (verse 26). I am giving the answer now. The first line should modify all the following words: One should see, touch, worship serve, praise offer respects and glorify the qualities and activities of my deity form and my devotees. Mal-liṅga-mad-bhakta-jana should properly be in the genitive case. Prahva means offering respects.

Personal Guidance
Surrender false pride by seeing, serving, and worshiping Krsna's Deity and devotees, and offering Him whatever is dearest to you.
Practical Applications
  • Offer your single most cherished possession or skill today to Krsna's service rather than something leftover or superfluous.
  • Take up one humble, unseen temple task such as sweeping, dusting, or cleaning, and tell no one you did it.
  • Set a recurring weekly time to hear and discuss Krsna's birth, activities, and qualities with other Vaisnavas instead of consuming entertainment.
Reflection Questions
  • What is most dear to me in this world right now, and what stops me from offering that very thing to Krsna?
  • Where am I advertising my devotional service to be seen by others, and how is that pride quietly replacing the offering itself?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Offer the activity you are most proud of, whether sport, music, or academics, by dedicating one performance or exam result explicitly to Krsna before you begin.
  • Volunteer for the unglamorous setup or cleanup at your campus or temple program and refuse to post about it on social media.
  • Identify the one app, show, or scroll-habit that is most dear to you and replace that exact slot with hearing Krsna's pastimes or chanting.
householder
  • Bring your family into one major financial decision this month by asking how a portion of it can be offered to Krsna's service rather than only optimizing for the household.
  • Establish a small home altar or improve the existing one with real faith in mama arca-sthapane sraddha, and involve your children in daily cleaning and offering.
  • Plan your next family vacation around a pilgrimage or Vaisnava festival instead of a purely recreational trip, and let the children see you participate as a servant, not a tourist.
senior
  • Identify the possession, role, or recognition you are most reluctant to release at this stage and consciously offer it to Krsna through a concrete act of giving or seva.
  • Take on a quiet, regular temple service such as cleaning, gardening, or Deity dressing, and decline any public acknowledgement for it.
  • Mentor a younger devotee in observing Ekadasi, Janmastami, and other annual festivals, transmitting the practical details so the tradition continues after you.
Common Pitfalls
  • Offering Krsna only what we no longer want, treating Him as a recipient of leftovers rather than as the object of our deepest love.
  • Using Deity paraphernalia such as lamps, flowers, or food for personal convenience when something runs short at home.
  • Treating cleaning, decorating, or festival work as menial labor instead of recognizing it as direct, intimate service to the Lord's home.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share with your partner the single thing you are most attached to in this world, and discuss honestly whether you could offer it to Krsna and what would change if you did.
  • Compare a festival or temple service you performed loudly versus one you performed quietly: which one actually loosened your false pride, and why?
  • Debate whether elaborate temple construction, gardens, and festivals are essential bhakti or a distraction from simpler internal devotion, using this verse as the anchor.
Role Play

A devotee has just received a long-awaited bonus at work and is thrilled. A senior Vaisnava friend visits and gently asks what they plan to do with it. The devotee admits they want to upgrade their car, but knows the verse says to offer what is most dear. Two students enact the conversation that follows, with the senior friend helping the devotee think through what honest offering would look like without forcing or guilt-tripping.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like medieval temple ritual that has nothing to do with my inner spiritual life in the modern world.
    Krsna Himself prescribes these practices in this verse precisely because the mind needs concrete, embodied engagement; seeing, touching, sweeping, and offering train the heart in a way that abstract inner reflection alone cannot, and Prabhupada points to Mayapur as a living modern example.
  • Why would Krsna, who owns everything, want my dearest possession? This feels like spiritual extortion.
    Krsna does not need the object; you need to give it. The offering is medicine for the false proprietorship that keeps you bound, and the verse promises tad anantyaya kalpate, that such offering qualifies one for eternal life, which is the real return on the exchange.
