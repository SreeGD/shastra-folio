Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.11.19

गां दुग्धदोहामसतीं च भार्यां देहं पराधीनमसत्प्रजां च । वित्तं त्वतीर्थीकृतमङ्ग वाचं हीनां मया रक्षति दु:खदु:खी ॥ १९ ॥

My dear Uddhava, that man is certainly most miserable who takes care of a cow that gives no milk, an unchaste wife, a body totally dependent on others, useless children or wealth not utilized for the right purpose. Similarly, one who studies Vedic knowledge devoid of My glories is also most miserable.
Purport

A human being is actually learned or expert when he understands that all material objects perceived through the various senses are expansions of the Supreme Personality of Godhead and that nothing exists without the support of the Supreme Lord. In this verse, through various examples, it is concluded that the power of speech is useless if not engaged for the Supreme Lord. According to Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, this verse implies that all of the functions of the various senses are useless if they are not engaged in the glorification of God. Indeed, the avadhūta brāhmaṇa previously stated to King Yadu that if the tongue is not controlled, one’s entire program of sense control is a failure. One cannot control the tongue unless he vibrates the glories of the Lord.

The example of the milkless cow is significant. A gentleman never kills a cow, and therefore when a cow becomes sterile and no longer gives milk, one must engage in the laborious task of protecting her, since no one will purchase a useless cow. For some time, the greedy owner of a sterile cow may continue thinking, “I have already invested so much money in taking care of this cow, and certainly in the near future she will again become pregnant and give milk.” But when this hope is proven futile, he becomes neglectful and indifferent to the health and safety of the animal. Because of such sinful neglect, he must suffer in the next life, after having already suffered because of the sterile cow in the present life.

Similarly, although a man may discover that his wife is neither chaste nor affectionate, he may be so eager to get children that he goes on taking care of such a useless wife, thinking, “I will teach my wife the religious duties of a chaste woman. By hearing historical examples of great women surely her heart will change, and she will become a wonderful wife to me.” Unfortunately, the unchaste wife in many cases does not change and also gives a man many useless children who are just as foolish and irreligious as she. Such children never give any happiness to the father, yet the father tediously labors to take care of them.

Also, one who has accumulated wealth by the mercy of God must be vigilant to give in charity to the right person and for the right cause. If such a right person or cause appears and one hesitates and selfishly does not give in charity, one loses his reputation, and in the next life he will be poverty-stricken. One who fails to give properly in charity spends his life anxiously protecting his wealth, which ultimately brings him no fame or happiness.

The previous examples are given to illustrate the uselessness of laboriously studying Vedic knowledge that does not glorify the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī comments that the spiritual vibration of the Vedas is meant to bring one to the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord, Kṛṣṇa. Many processes for achieving the Supreme Truth are recommended in the Upaniṣads and other Vedic literatures, but because of their innumerable and seemingly contradictory explanations, commentaries and injunctions, one cannot achieve the Absolute Truth, the Personality of Godhead, merely by reading such literature. If, however, one understands Śrī Kṛṣṇa to be the ultimate cause of all causes and reads the Upaniṣads and other Vedic literature as glorification of the Supreme Lord, then one can actually become fixed at the Lord’s lotus feet. For example, His Divine Grace Śrīla Prabhupāda translated and commented upon Śrī Īśopaniṣad in such a way that it brings the reader closer to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Undoubtedly, the lotus feet of Lord Kṛṣṇa are the only reliable boat by which to cross the turbulent ocean of material existence. Even Lord Brahmā has stated in the Tenth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam that if one gives up the auspicious path of bhakti and takes to the fruitless labor of Vedic speculation, one is just like a fool who beats empty husks in hopes of getting rice. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī recommends that one completely ignore dry Vedic speculation because it does not bring one to the point of devotional service to the Absolute Truth, Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
O Uddhava! That man is certainly most miserable who takes care of a barren cow, an unchaste wife, a body totally dependent on others, useless children or wealth not utilized for the right purpose. Similarly, one who studies Vedic knowledge devoid of my glories is also most miserable. Expert service to me means accepting things seen and heard which are related to me and rejecting things which are without relation to me. To illustrate this, the Lord uses many examples to show how one sense, the voice, should be used to glorify the Lord. This of course represents all the senses. One receives a free cow from someone, but that cow has been milked of its milk and cannot give any more milk. The person who thinks that by feeding the cow plenty of grass it will again give milk is greedy for milk. Suffering daily because of expecting milk from the cow his has obtained, he eventually gives up hope and in disappointment curses the donor. Thus he suffers further in the next life (duḥkha-duḥkhī). A man thinking that his unchaste wife will become chaste by instructions on morality from righteous persons continues to maintain her with the desire of having children. A foolish person also maintains unworthy children with the same mentality. Asat-prajām means sons who have no pious credits in this and next life. A body dependent on others is the cause of distress at every moment. If one does not give wealth to a qualified person who comes, it causes infamy and sin. O Uddhava! The person who maintains these persons suffers misery after misery.

Personal Guidance
Speech, wealth, body and study become a burden when not engaged for Kṛṣṇa — the miserable life is the one whose efforts do not glorify Him.
Practical Applications
  • Audit one daily activity — your work, your reading, your speech — and ask whether it is being engaged for Kṛṣṇa or merely consuming your energy.
  • Before opening any Vedic or spiritual text today, set the intention to read it as glorification of Kṛṣṇa rather than as abstract speculation.
  • Use your tongue at least once today to vibrate the Lord's names or glories aloud, recognizing that an uncontrolled tongue collapses all other sense control.
Reflection Questions
  • Which 'sterile cow' am I still laboriously maintaining in my life — a project, relationship, or study that yields no spiritual milk yet drains me daily?
  • When I study scripture or philosophy, am I reading toward Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, or am I beating empty husks in hope of rice?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice which study habits — endless notes, podcasts, debate — are accumulating information without changing your relationship with Kṛṣṇa, and reorient one of them this week.
  • Refuse to keep pouring effort into a friendship or online identity that has gone 'milkless' — drains your energy while yielding no real care or growth.
  • Use your tongue on campus today for at least one honest mention of Kṛṣṇa or His teachings, instead of letting it run only on gossip, memes, and exam stress.
householder
  • Examine one major financial commitment this week and ask whether it is genuinely supporting your family's spiritual life or simply protecting a sunk cost.
  • Identify a charity — a person or cause Kṛṣṇa has clearly placed before you — and act on it now, rather than waiting until your wealth feels 'enough.'
  • When teaching your children or guiding your spouse, frame Vedic stories and values explicitly around Kṛṣṇa rather than as generic ethics, so the household reading does not become a 'milkless cow.'
senior
  • Survey your library and lifelong learning — which volumes have actually brought you closer to Kṛṣṇa, and which were intellectual collection? Reread the first set; release the second.
  • Before any further accumulation, distribute wealth and possessions now to the right persons and causes, so charity is not left undone when the body becomes 'totally dependent on others.'
  • Mentor younger devotees by speaking from your hard-won realization that a life of effort uncoupled from Kṛṣṇa is the very 'duḥkha-duḥkhī' this verse describes.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating this verse as license to abandon dependents or responsibilities, rather than as a call to reorient every engagement toward Kṛṣṇa.
  • Accumulating Vedic and philosophical knowledge as intellectual capital while never letting it culminate in devotion to the Supreme Person.
  • Hoarding wealth under the rationalization of 'security' while postponing the right charity to the right person and cause.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one 'milkless cow' in your life — something you keep investing in out of past commitment rather than present fruit. What would honest reassessment look like?
  • Where in your scriptural study do you feel you are accumulating information without moving closer to Kṛṣṇa? What would change if you read every verse as His glorification?
  • Discuss the tension between this verse and the duty to care for dependents — how does a Gauḍīya practitioner hold both without using detachment as an exit?
Role Play

A friend tells you he has spent fifteen years studying the Upaniṣads and comparative philosophy, can quote dozens of ślokas, but admits he feels spiritually dry and has never chanted a round of japa. He asks whether his learning was wasted. Respond as a fellow practitioner using this verse.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds harsh — calling a wife 'unchaste' or children 'useless' is offensive and judgmental by modern standards.
    The verse is not licensing cruelty toward anyone; it is exposing the misery of a life whose every relationship and possession is severed from Kṛṣṇa. The point is diagnostic, not punitive — the same wife, children, wealth and study become sources of joy when reconnected to the Supreme Lord, and sources of suffering when they are not.
  • Studying Vedic literature is valuable in itself — why insist it must glorify Kṛṣṇa to count?
    Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī explains that the spiritual vibration of the Vedas is meant to deliver one to the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa; dry speculation without that aim is, as Lord Brahmā says in the Tenth Canto, like beating empty husks hoping for rice. The literature is not devalued — its actual purpose is restored.
