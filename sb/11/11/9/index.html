Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.11.9

इन्द्रियैरिन्द्रियार्थेषु गुणैरपि गुणेषु च । गृह्यमाणेष्वहं कुर्यान्न विद्वान् यस्त्वविक्रिय: ॥ ९ ॥

An enlightened person who is free from the contamination of material desire does not consider himself to be the performer of bodily activities; rather, he knows that in all such activities it is only the senses, born of the modes of nature, that are contacting sense objects born of the same modes of nature.
Purport

Lord Kṛṣṇa makes a similar statement in Bhagavad-gītā (3.28) :

tattva-vit tu mahā-bāho guṇa-karma-vibhāgayoḥ guṇā guṇeṣu vartanta iti matvā na sajjate

“One who is in knowledge of the Absolute Truth, O mighty-armed, does not engage himself in the senses and sense gratification, knowing well the difference between work in devotion and work for fruitive results.”

The material body always interacts with the sense objects, for in order to survive the body must eat, drink, speak, sleep, and so on, but an enlightened person who knows the science of Kṛṣṇa consciousness never thinks, “I am accepting these sense objects as my property. They are meant for my pleasure.” Similarly, if the body performs a wonderful activity, a Kṛṣṇa conscious person does not become proud, nor is he depressed by the failure of the body to function in a particular way. In other words, Kṛṣṇa consciousness means giving up identification with the gross and subtle material bodies. One should see them as the external energy of the Lord, working under the direction of the Lord’s empowered representative māyā. One absorbed in fruitive activities works under the jurisdiction of mahā-māyā, or the external illusory potency, and experiences the miseries of material existence. On the other hand, a devotee works under the internal potency, called yoga-māyā, and remains satisfied by offering his loving service to the Lord. In either case, the Lord Himself, by His multifarious potencies, is the ultimate performer of action.

According to Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, one who claims to be transcendental to the bodily concept of life, but at the same time remains under the influence of material desire and mental transformation, is understood to be a cheater and the lowest type of conditioned soul.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
When the sense objects are received by the senses, the liberated person, who has no transformations arising from ahaṅkāra, does not think “I am experiencing this,” since it is only material guṇas received by other material guṇas. When the sense objects are received by the senses, the liberated person does not think “I am experiencing this,” since it is only material guṇas received by other material guṇas. The sign of the person without ahaṅkāra is that he is devoid of transformations arising from ahaṅkāra. But a person who undergoes transformations and still says “I am doing nothing at all” is a fraud, deeply bound up in māyā.

Personal Guidance
The enlightened soul sees the senses, born of the guṇas, interacting with sense objects also born of the guṇas — he is not the doer.
Practical Applications
  • Notice one moment today when the body eats, speaks, or scrolls, and quietly recognize: "the guṇas are moving among the guṇas — I am the witness."
  • When a task succeeds or fails today, refuse both pride and depression — offer the action and its outcome to Kṛṣṇa as the real performer.
  • Speak about your work today without using "I did," "I achieved," or "I failed" — practice locating yourself as the soul, not the body.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I still claiming bodily activities — eating, speaking, working — as "my" doing, and what does that claim cost me?
  • Viśvanātha Cakravartī warns of the "cheater" who speaks of transcendence while clinging to desire — where in my own life does that pattern hide?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • After your next exam result or social media post, catch the spike of "I crushed it" or "I'm a failure" and replace it with: "the senses and guṇas met their objects — Kṛṣṇa is the doer."
  • Pick one peer-pressure moment this week — a party, a comparison, a trend — and notice the guṇas pulling the senses toward objects, then choose your response from the soul, not the body.
  • Refuse to build your identity on a single grade, follower count, or rejection; rehearse seeing them as guṇas interacting with guṇas, while you remain the unchanged witness.
householder
  • Before a big family decision — a job change, a school for the kids, a purchase — pause and ask whether "I" am deciding or the modes of nature are pushing the senses; then offer the choice to Kṛṣṇa.
  • When your spouse or child praises or blames you today, receive it without inflating or collapsing — the body acted, the guṇas moved, and you can return to your sva-dharma steadily.
  • Track one full workday with the inner note "the senses are contacting sense objects under Kṛṣṇa's potency," and see how it changes your tone at dinner with the family.
senior
  • As the body ages and capacities shift, practice naming each loss as "the guṇas withdrawing from the guṇas" — not the diminishment of you, the soul.
  • When you mentor a younger person, refuse to claim authorship of their progress; show them by example that the vidvān credits Kṛṣṇa and the guṇas, not himself.
  • Loosen one long-held attachment this month — a possession, a role, a grievance — by recognizing it as the body's history, not the soul's identity, and gently offer it to Kṛṣṇa.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using "the guṇas are acting, not me" as an excuse for careless work, indulgence, or moral evasion.
  • Mistaking this verse's witnessing stance for impersonal detachment, instead of active service to Kṛṣṇa under yoga-māyā.
  • Imitating the language of the vidvān outwardly while inwardly still hungry for sense pleasure — the very cheating Viśvanātha names.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a recent situation where you took personal credit or personal blame for what was really the body and senses doing their work — what would the vidvān of this verse have done instead?
  • Prabhupāda contrasts working under mahā-māyā with working under yoga-māyā — debate with a partner: in your daily routine, which potency is actually directing most of your activity?
  • Discuss the line between healthy responsibility and false ego-identification with action — where do you and your partner draw it differently?
Role Play

Two students pair up. Student A has just been praised at work for a major success and feels the surge of "I did this." Student B, playing a senior Vaiṣṇava, must respond using this verse — without deflating A's joy, redirect the credit to Kṛṣṇa and the guṇas, and help A see the danger of claiming doership. Then switch: A is criticized harshly for a failure and feels crushed; B applies the same verse to lift A out of bodily identification.

Objections & Responses
  • If the senses act on sense objects and I am not the doer, doesn't this dissolve all moral responsibility? Why not just indulge?
    The verse describes the vision of the vidvān who is already free of material desire — it is the fruit of purification, not a license preceding it. Viśvanātha Cakravartī explicitly calls one who claims this status while still ruled by desire a cheater. Responsibility deepens, not dissolves: you stop claiming credit, but you also stop directing the senses toward your own gratification and instead engage them in Kṛṣṇa's service.
  • This sounds like the impersonal Advaita position — the Self is mere witness, action is just nature unfolding. How is this different?
    Prabhupāda's purport makes the distinction sharp: the activities are not unfolding in an impersonal void but under Kṛṣṇa's own potencies — mahā-māyā for the conditioned, yoga-māyā for the devotee. The Lord Himself, by His multifarious potencies, is the ultimate performer. The witnessing here is not retreat into a featureless Self but recognition of Kṛṣṇa as the actual agent, freeing the devotee to serve Him personally.
