obeisances.
“Obeisances to You, O Supreme Lord Vāsudeva, and to Your forms of Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha. O Supreme Personality of Godhead, all obeisances unto You. O Lord Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi, O creator of the universe, best of personalities, master of this cosmos and original form of the universe, O Supersoul of all created entities, all homage unto You.”
Purport

Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī explains that although Lord Kṛṣṇa appeared at the end of Dvāpara-yuga, great sages chanted this verse from the beginning of that age in expectation of His appearance.

Ordinary living entities, although eternally subordinate to the Lord, become absorbed in attempting to dominate the material creation yet ultimately remain under the control of the Supreme Lord. The constitutional position of the living entity is engagement in the Lord’s service. It is further the constitutional position of the material nature to be engaged by the living entity in satisfying the transcendental desires of the Lord. Thus these prayers, as mentioned in this verse, are offered to the Supreme Lord in accordance with the Paṣcarātra and the Vedic mantras so that one may become fixed in remembering his eternal subordination to the Absolute Truth.

The supreme living entity, Kṛṣṇa, eternally manifests Himself as the catur-vyūha, or quadruple plenary expansion. The purport of this prayer is that one should give up his false ego and pray to this catur-vyūha by offering Them obeisances. Although the Absolute Truth is one without a second, the Absolute Truth displays His unlimited opulences and potencies by expanding Himself in innumerable plenary forms, of which the catur-vyūha is a principal expansion. The original being is Vāsudeva, the Personality of Godhead. When the Godhead manifests His primeval energies and opulences, He is called Saṅkarṣaṇa. Pradyumna is the basis of the Viṣṇu expansion who is the soul of the entire universe, and Aniruddha is the basis of the personal manifestation of Viṣṇu as the Supersoul of every individual entity within the universe. Among the four plenary expansions mentioned here, the original expansion is Vāsudeva, and the other three are considered to be particular manifestations of Him.

When the living entity forgets that both he himself as well as the material nature are meant for the Lord’s service, the quality of ignorance becomes prominent, and the conditioned entity desires to become himself the master. Thus the conditioned soul imagines that he is a very important person within society or that he is a great philosopher. The Vedic mantras and Paṣcarātra give mankind instructions about devotional service to the Personality of Godhead, which free one from the contamination of considering oneself a prestigious member of society or a great philosopher. One in knowledge should recognize himself as a tiny servant of the Supreme Absolute Truth.

In Dvāpara-yuga, Deity worship of the Lord is prominent. Such Deity worship is ultimately aimed at the process of śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ. Without hearing and chanting the glories of the Lord one cannot perform Deity worship. In Deity worship it is required that the worshiper glorify the names, forms, qualities, paraphernalia, entourage and pastimes of the Supreme Lord. When such glorification is complete, the worshiper becomes eligible to realize transcendental knowledge through hearing about the Lord.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Obeisances to you, Vāsudeva, to your forms of Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha, to Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi, to the puruṣa, best of personalities, the master of this universe, the form of the universe, the soul within all created entities. He offers respects.

Personal Guidance
Offer obeisances to the catur-vyūha — Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha — and remember today you are His tiny eternal servant, not the master.
Practical Applications
  • Before starting work today, bow inwardly to Vāsudeva and name yourself out loud as His servant, not the doer.
  • When you catch yourself posturing as an important person or clever philosopher, stop mid-sentence and offer that pride to Aniruddha within your heart.
  • Spend five minutes before your Deity or picture of Kṛṣṇa hearing or chanting His names before any other request — śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ first, then everything else.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life am I still trying to be the master of material nature instead of letting it be engaged in Kṛṣṇa's service through me?
  • Which expansion of the catur-vyūha do I most need to remember today — the original Vāsudeva, the energetic Saṅkarṣaṇa, the universal soul Pradyumna, or the Supersoul Aniruddha within me?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before an exam or interview, name yourself silently as Kṛṣṇa's servant and offer the result to Him — then go in and do your best work without bargaining for the outcome.
  • When a post or grade tempts you to feel like the "important person" the purport warns about, pause and offer that flash of pride to Aniruddha, the Supersoul seated in your heart.
  • Replace five minutes of scrolling today with hearing or chanting Kṛṣṇa's names — actual śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ, not just the idea of it.
householder
  • Begin the workday by offering obeisances to Vāsudeva at your desk or altar, explicitly placing the day's earnings and decisions at His feet rather than your own.
  • When tempted to brand yourself as a great professional, parent, or philosopher in front of family or colleagues, quietly remember you are managing Kṛṣṇa's resources, not your own.
  • Establish one short daily Deity or picture worship with your spouse or children where you chant the names before any family request or planning conversation.
senior
  • Make this verse part of your morning prayer so that, as the body weakens, the identity of eternal subordinate servant — not retired professional or family head — becomes your firmest ground.
  • When younger people seek your counsel, redirect quietly from your own accomplishments to the catur-vyūha; let them see a tiny servant, not a great philosopher.
  • Loosen one attachment this week — a possession, a title, a grievance — by formally offering it in obeisance to Vāsudeva, acknowledging it was never yours to master.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reciting these names as a mantra-formula while quietly holding on to the conviction that you are the controller of your day.
  • Collapsing the four forms into a vague "divine consciousness" instead of honoring them as distinct personal expansions of the one Lord.
  • Treating offering obeisances as a private feeling rather than the Pañcarātra-prescribed act of body, mind, and words actually bowing.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a recent moment when you acted as if you were the master rather than the servant — what tipped you off, and what would offering obeisances in that moment have changed?
  • The purport says one in knowledge recognizes himself as a tiny servant; where does this clash with how your workplace or family expects you to present yourself, and how do you hold both?
  • Why do you think the sages began chanting this verse at the start of Dvāpara-yuga, before Kṛṣṇa appeared — what does that say about how we should pray when we do not yet "see" Him?
Role Play

One student plays a successful professional who has just been publicly praised as a "thought leader" at a conference; another plays a Gauḍīya friend who meets them that evening. The friend's task is not to deflate them but to help them, in plain conversation, locate where the false ego has crept in and how the catur-vyūha prayer could re-orient tomorrow morning's work.

Objections & Responses
  • Why four names? If God is one, this looks like polytheism dressed up in Sanskrit.
    The Absolute Truth is one without a second, but a person of unlimited opulence naturally manifests Himself in distinct forms. Vāsudeva is the original; Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha are His own plenary expansions for specific functions — energy, universal soul, Supersoul. Bowing to the catur-vyūha is bowing to the one Lord in the fullness of how He actually exists, not to four separate gods.
  • Calling myself a "tiny servant" sounds like self-erasure — modern life requires confidence and agency.
    Recognizing yourself as Kṛṣṇa's servant is not erasure; it is the end of the exhausting pretense that you are the master of nature. Real agency begins when action is done as offering — you still work, decide, and lead, but without the contamination of considering yourself the cause. That is freedom, not diminishment.
