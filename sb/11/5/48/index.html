Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.48

वैरेण यं नृपतय: शिशुपालपौण्ड्र- शाल्वादयो गतिविलासविलोकनाद्यै: । ध्यायन्त आकृतधिय: शयनासनादौ तत्साम्यमापुरनुरक्तधियां पुन: किम् ॥ ४८ ॥
vaireṇa yaṁ nṛpatayaḥ śiśupāla-pauṇḍra-
[vairena yam nripatayah shishupaala-paundra-]

[tat-saamyam aapur anurakta-dhiyaam punah kim]

Inimical kings like Śiśupāla, Pauṇḍraka and Śālva were always thinking about Lord Kṛṣṇa. Even while they were lying down, sitting or engaging in other activities, they enviously meditated upon the bodily movements of the Lord, His sporting pastimes, His loving glances upon His devotees, and other attractive features displayed by the Lord. Being thus always absorbed in Kṛṣṇa, they achieved spiritual liberation in the Lord’s own abode. What then can be said of the benedictions offered to those who constantly fix their minds on Lord Kṛṣṇa in a favorable, loving mood?
Purport

On the eve of the disappearance of Lord Kṛṣṇa from this world, Vasudeva became filled with lamentation thinking that he had not properly utilized the opportunity of the Lord’s personal presence to become perfectly Kṛṣṇa conscious. However, Nārada Muni assured Śrī Vasudeva that the glories of Vasudeva and his good wife Devakī were chanted throughout the universe because even the demigods worshiped the exalted status of the Lord’s own parents. Vasudeva was not only concerned about his own spiritual position, but he also lamented for the Yadu dynasty, which had left the world in an apparently inauspicious way, being cursed by great brāhmaṇas such as Nārada and dying in a fratricidal war. Although the members of the Yadu dynasty were personal associates of the Lord, their disappearance from the earth was apparently inauspicious, and therefore Vasudeva was concerned about their eventual destination. So Nārada here assures Vasudeva that even the demons who opposed Kṛṣṇa, such as Śiśupāla, Pauṇḍraka and Śālva, achieved promotion to the Lord’s own abode because of their constant absorption in thinking of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore what to speak of the exalted members of the Yadu dynasty who actually loved Kṛṣṇa more than anything ( anurakta-dhiyāṁ punaḥ kim )? Similarly, it is stated in the Garuḍa Purāṇa:

ajṣāninaḥ sura-varaṁ samadhikṣipanto yaṁ pāpino ’pi śiśupāla-suyodhanādyāḥ muktiṁ gatāḥ smaraṇa-mātra-vidhūta-pāpāḥ kaḥ saṁśayaḥ parama-bhaktimatāṁ janānām

“Even foolish sinners like Śiśupāla and Duryodhana who showered abuse upon the Lord were cleansed of all sins simply by remembering Lord Kṛṣṇa. Somehow or other their minds were absorbed in the Lord, and thus they attained liberation. What doubt then is there about the destination of those who are overwhelmed with devotional feelings for the Lord?”

Vasudeva was also feeling anxiety because on the one hand he was aware that Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, but at the same time he treated the Lord as his beloved son. In the relationship between the father and son, sometimes the father must chastise the son and restrict him in various ways. In this way Vasudeva was feeling that undoubtedly he had offended the Lord in his attempt to train Lord Kṛṣṇa as his son. However, Lord Kṛṣṇa is actually pleased when a pure devotee becomes absorbed in parental love for Him and thus devotedly tries to care for Him just as loving parents care for a small child. Kṛṣṇa reciprocates the intense devotional feelings of such devotees by actually appearing before them as a young boy and acting just like their son.

As mentioned in this verse, the demons actually chastised Kṛṣṇa with feelings of enmity. Nevertheless, such demons attained liberation due to their absorption in Kṛṣṇa. Therefore, what to speak of the destination of Vasudeva, who chastised Kṛṣṇa due to his overwhelming parental love for Him? The conclusion is that the devotees of the Lord should never consider Vasudeva and Devakī to be ordinary, conditioned souls. Their relationship with Lord Kṛṣṇa is completely on the transcendental platform of vātsalya-rasa, or parental love for the Personality of Godhead. It has nothing to do with the parental love of the material world, which is based on a mood of sense gratification, since the parents see their children as objects of material enjoyment.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Inimical kings like Śiśupāla, Pauṇḍraka and Śālva, while they were lying down, sitting or engaging in other activities, enviously meditated upon the bodily movements of the Lord, his sporting pastimes, and his loving glances. Being thus always absorbed in Kṛṣṇa, they achieved positions in the spiritual world. What then can be said of the benedictions offered to those who constantly fix their minds on Lord Kṛṣṇa in a favorable, loving mood? “But thinking of the Lord as one’s son gives rise to obstacles, since we see that as parents we offended the Lord.” No, that is not so. Even those who become absorbed in Kṛṣna with hatred become successful. What to speak of those who serve him with devotion! Those who thought of Kṛṣṇa’s form with its movement and glances, but not its sweetness, while lying down or sitting, attained sāyujya or sārūpya (sāmyam). How much more the devotees will attain! There is no possibility of offense for those devotees.

Personal Guidance
Even enemies who obsessed over Kṛṣṇa in hatred attained His abode — so absorption itself is liberating; how much more for those who think of Him in love.
Practical Applications
  • Audit your mental rehearsals today: notice whom you replay in anger or envy, and consciously redirect that same intensity toward remembering Kṛṣṇa instead.
  • When lying down, sitting, or between tasks, deliberately bring Kṛṣṇa's form, name, or qualities to mind — claim those in-between moments the demons used for enmity.
  • Refuse the excuse that you are too distracted or impure to remember Kṛṣṇa; if Śiśupāla's hostile absorption purified him, your imperfect favorable attempt is not wasted.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my day does my mind actually go when it is unoccupied — and what does that absorption reveal about what I am truly worshipping?
  • If even hateful absorption in Kṛṣṇa yielded liberation, why am I still rationing my favorable remembrance of Him?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Track for one day where your mind drifts during lectures, scrolling, or walking between classes — that drift is your real worship, and this verse asks you to point it toward Kṛṣṇa.
  • When a classmate or rival lives rent-free in your head with envy or resentment, recognize you have already mastered absorption — now redirect that same capacity to Kṛṣṇa's name and qualities.
  • Stop waiting until exams end or you 'have time' for spiritual life; favorable remembrance fits into sitting in lectures and lying in bed exactly where the demons fit their hostile remembrance.
householder
  • While commuting, cooking, or putting children to bed, audibly or mentally chant Kṛṣṇa's name — claim the lying-down, sitting, and routine moments the verse explicitly names.
  • When you find yourself rehearsing arguments with a spouse, boss, or relative for the hundredth time, treat that as evidence your mind can in fact stay absorbed — and turn that same circuit toward Kṛṣṇa.
  • Take real consolation from this verse about family members or ancestors who died without obvious devotion; let it loosen your anxiety so you can serve the living without that weight.
senior
  • As physical activity narrows to more sitting and lying down, treat those very postures — named in this verse — as the prime field of remembrance, not as decline.
  • Mentor a younger devotee by sharing how your own mind has been absorbed across decades — in work, in worry, in family — and what it has cost or gained you, so they choose the favorable mood earlier.
  • Release the lifelong grudges you have been quietly replaying; at this stage their only function is to crowd out the remembrance of Kṛṣṇa that this verse says decides your destination.
Common Pitfalls
  • Concluding that envy of Kṛṣṇa is a valid path — the verse honors anurakta-dhī (loving absorption), and uses Śiśupāla only as a 'how much more so' argument, never as a model to imitate.
  • Treating constant remembrance as a feeling to wait for rather than a discipline of mind to be practiced in lying down, sitting, and ordinary acts.
  • Using this verse to be lenient with one's own offenses — 'even demons were liberated' — instead of being sobered by how rare and costly such absorption actually was for them.
Discussion Prompts
  • Śiśupāla cursed Kṛṣṇa for a lifetime and was liberated; many lifelong temple-goers seem not to be. What is the actual mechanism this verse is pointing to, and where does that leave our daily sādhana?
  • Nārada offers this teaching to console Vasudeva's anxiety about the Yadus' fratricidal end. When have you needed this same consolation about someone you love who died in 'inauspicious' circumstances, and did it actually settle you?
  • Is enmity-absorption a real spiritual path, a rhetorical device, or a dangerous misreading? Defend your view against the other two.
Role Play

One student plays a grieving family member who has just lost a relative that died angry, estranged, and openly hostile to Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The other student plays a Gauḍīya friend asked at the funeral, 'Is there any hope for him?' Use SB 11.5.48 — without weaponizing it, without cheap consolation, and without inventing pastimes — to respond honestly.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like a loophole — hate Kṛṣṇa enough and you still get liberated. That makes the whole moral framework of bhakti collapse.
    The verse is not prescribing enmity; it is showing the staggering potency of absorption in Kṛṣṇa's person. Śiśupāla paid with a lifetime of torment, repeated births of enmity, and death at the Lord's hand — favorable devotees reach the same destination in love, with far less cost. The argument is a fortiori, not a loophole: if even the worst route led there, why not take the loving one?
  • I can't relate to thinking about a deity all day. My mind is full of work, kids, deadlines — this standard seems impossible for a modern householder.
    Notice that the demons did not interrupt their lives to meditate — they thought of Kṛṣṇa while lying down, sitting, eating, ruling kingdoms, fighting wars. The verse explicitly names ordinary postures. The instruction is not to add a new activity but to change what occupies the mind during the activities already underway. Begin with the moments between tasks; favorable absorption grows there.
