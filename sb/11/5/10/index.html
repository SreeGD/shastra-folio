Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.10

सर्वेषु शश्वत्तनुभृत्स्ववस्थितं यथा खमात्मानमभीष्टमीश्वरम् । वेदोपगीतं च न श‍ृण्वतेऽबुधा मनोरथानां प्रवदन्ति वार्तया ॥ १० ॥

The Personality of Godhead is eternally situated within the heart of every embodied being; still the Lord remains situated apart, just as the sky, which is all-pervading, does not mix with any material object. Thus the Lord is the supreme worshipable object and the absolute controller of everything. He is elaborately glorified in the Vedic literature, but those who are bereft of intelligence do not like to hear about Him. They prefer to waste their time discussing their own mental concoctions, which inevitably deal with gross material sense gratification such as sex life and meat-eating.
Purport

In Bhagavad-gītā Lord Kṛṣṇa says, vedaiś ca sarvair aham eva vedyaḥ. The goal of all Vedic knowledge is to know the Lord, who is the Absolute Truth. Although this purpose of the Vedas is clearly revealed both in the Vedic literature itself and by the self-realized ācāryas, foolish people cannot grasp this simple truth. They prefer to cultivate knowledge of illicit sex by discussing their sexual partners and experiences. They also eagerly describe and recommend to their friends the best restaurants for eating meat, and they are fond of glorifying drugs and liquor by elaborately describing the drunken and hallucinogenic effects of their sinful experiences. The materialistic sense gratifiers eagerly call one another on the telephone, congregate in clubs and committees, and enthusiastically go on hunting, drinking and gambling excursions, thus filling their lives with the mode of ignorance. They have neither the time nor the inclination to discuss the Absolute Truth, Kṛṣṇa. Unfortunately, they neglect the Supreme Lord, who therefore severely punishes such foolish persons in order to bring them back to their senses. Everything belongs to the Lord, and everything is meant for the Lord’s enjoyment. When the living entity dovetails his activities for the pleasure of the Lord, he experiences unlimited happiness. Yena sattvaṁ śuddhyed yasmād brahma-saukhyaṁ tv anantam. There is actually no happiness in material affairs, and the Lord mercifully punishes the intoxicated conditioned soul to bring him back to his real life.

Unfortunately, materialistic persons do not heed the advice of the Supreme Lord in Bhagavad-gītā or that of the Lord’s representatives, who speak in allied literatures such as Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Instead, such sense gratifiers consider themselves to be most eloquent and learned. Every materialistic person actually feels that he is the most intelligent person, and thus he has no time to hear the actual truth. Nevertheless, the Personality of Godhead, as described in this verse, waits patiently within the heart of the conditioned soul, encouraging him to recognize the Lord, who is sitting beside him. Such recognition of the Personality of Godhead is the beginning of all auspiciousness and happiness for the conditioned soul.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
These unintelligent people do not hear about the Lord, worthy of worship and proclaimed in the Vedas, who is situated in all beings at all times, but who is not affected, like the sky. Instead they speak continuously about material topics. Though awaken strongly, they do not wake up. The Lord, like the sky which is not affected by what it pervades, is situated in all beings. He is worthy of worship, as well as being the punisher, and is glorified in the Vedas. However these people do not hear about him. Why? They speak predominantly of topics about women and eating meat, being thrown with force into material topics, though informed of topics about the Lord by devotees.

    His Holiness Bhakti Caitanya Swami· English
Personal Guidance
Kṛṣṇa sits within every heart like the sky inside a pot, yet the foolish ignore Him and squander life chattering about their own mental concoctions.
Practical Applications
  • Before speaking today, pause and ask whether what you are about to say glorifies Kṛṣṇa or merely advertises your own mano-ratha.
  • Replace one habitual conversation about food, entertainment, or relationships with a few minutes of hearing or speaking about the Lord.
  • Sit quietly for five minutes and acknowledge Kṛṣṇa seated in your heart as the īśvara, not as a vague inner light but as a person waiting to be recognized.
Reflection Questions
  • Which mental concoctions do I dress up as serious discussion so I can avoid hearing about Kṛṣṇa?
  • If the Lord has been patiently waiting in my heart all these years, what exactly have I been telling Him I am too busy with?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Audit one week of your group chats and social feeds and notice how many hours go to mano-ratha-vārtā — celebrities, crushes, food, gaming — versus anything about Kṛṣṇa.
  • In your friend group, be the first person willing to mention Kṛṣṇa or Bhāgavatam out loud, even once, instead of waiting for someone cooler to make it acceptable.
  • Before scrolling in bed tonight, spend two minutes acknowledging that the same Lord glorified in the Vedas is seated in your heart watching the scroll with you.
householder
  • Choose one family meal a week where the table conversation is steered, gently, away from work gossip and politics toward something about the Lord or the ācāryas.
  • Notice when career or financial planning has quietly become the whole content of your inner life, and reinsert hearing about Kṛṣṇa as a non-negotiable daily slot.
  • When raising your children, speak about Kṛṣṇa as a real person living in their heart, not as a vague value or cultural symbol they can take or leave.
senior
  • Refuse to let your remaining years be filled with reminiscing about old enjoyments — turn long conversations with peers toward the Lord whose company you are about to enter more fully.
  • Tell at least one younger person in your family, plainly, that you wasted years on mano-ratha and that hearing about Kṛṣṇa is the one thing you wish you had begun earlier.
  • Each evening, sit with the awareness that the īśvara has been seated in your heart through every decade of this life, and offer Him the day before sleep.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking the indwelling Lord for an impersonal "higher self" and congratulating yourself for spiritual insight while still ignoring Kṛṣṇa as a person.
  • Hearing about Kṛṣṇa intellectually while the actual airtime of your day is still given to sense-gratification talk with friends and colleagues.
  • Treating the verse's harsh word abudhāḥ as applying to other people — materialists, atheists, neighbors — and never to your own daily speech.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one topic you genuinely enjoy discussing with friends, and examine honestly whether it is hari-kathā or mano-ratha — and what that says about your association.
  • The verse calls those who will not hear about the Lord abudhāḥ, unintelligent. Do you accept that label for the educated, accomplished people you know who have no interest in Kṛṣṇa? Defend your answer.
  • If Kṛṣṇa is already seated in every heart, why is hearing from sādhus and śāstra still necessary — why can't we just turn inward?
Role Play

Two students are on a long car ride. One has just started chanting and reading Bhāgavatam; the other is a close old friend who wants to catch up on relationships, restaurants, and shows. Play out fifteen minutes of that drive — how does the practitioner stay warm and present without letting the entire conversation collapse into the very mano-ratha-vārtā this verse warns against?

Objections & Responses
  • Calling people who discuss food, sex, and entertainment "unintelligent" is arrogant and judgmental — these are normal human interests.
    The verse is not insulting people; it is diagnosing a tragedy. Someone with the Lord seated in their own heart, glorified by the entire Vedic literature, who instead spends decades discussing restaurants and partners, is objectively misusing a rare human birth. A doctor who tells a patient their lifestyle is killing them is not being arrogant — they are being honest. Gauḍīya teachers speak this way out of compassion, not contempt, and they apply it first to themselves.
  • If Kṛṣṇa is already within me as the verse says, I don't need scripture, temple, or guru — I can simply meditate and find Him directly.
    The same verse that says He is within also says He is vedopagītam — glorified by the Vedas — and condemns those who will not hear. The sky is everywhere, but you still need a window to see it; Kṛṣṇa within is realized through hearing about Kṛṣṇa without, from śāstra and sādhu. Bypassing that hearing is not a shortcut to the Lord in the heart, it is exactly the mano-ratha the verse warns against, dressed up as spirituality.
