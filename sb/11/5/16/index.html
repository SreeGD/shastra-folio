Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.16

ये कैवल्यमसम्प्राप्ता ये चातीताश्च मूढताम् । त्रैवर्गिका ह्यक्षणिका आत्मानं घातयन्ति ते ॥ १६ ॥

Those who have not achieved knowledge of the Absolute Truth, yet who are still beyond the darkness of complete ignorance, generally follow the threefold path of pious material life, namely religiosity, economic development and sense gratification. Not having time to reflect on any higher purpose, they become the killers of their own soul.
Purport

Those who are completely in the darkness of ignorance and thus bereft even of material pious life commit innumerable sinful activities and suffer greatly. Due to such intense suffering such persons sometimes seek the shelter of the devotees of the Lord and, being blessed by such transcendental association, are sometimes elevated to the highest perfectional stage of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

Those who are not completely sinful experience some mitigation of the miseries of material life and thus develop a false sense of well-being within the material world. Because those who are materially pious generally obtain worldly prosperity, bodily beauty and a pleasant family situation, they become falsely proud of their position and are not inclined to associate with or accept instructions from the devotees of the Lord. Unfortunately, all material activities, whether pious or impious, are inevitably contaminated by sinful activity. Those who are proud of their piety and do not like to hear about Kṛṣṇa sooner or later fall down from their artificial position. Every living entity is an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore, until we surrender to Kṛṣṇa, our position is actually always impious. The word akṣaṇikāḥ (“not having even a moment to reflect”) is significant in this verse. Materialistic persons cannot spare a single moment for their eternal self-interest. This is a symptom of misfortune. Such persons are considered to be killing their own souls because by their obstinacy they are preparing a dark future for themselves from which they will not escape for a very long time.

A sick man receiving medical treatment may be encouraged by the preliminary results of the doctor’s care. But if the patient becomes falsely proud of the preliminary progress in his treatment and prematurely gives up the doctor’s orders, thinking himself already cured, there will undoubtedly be a relapse. The words ye kaivalyam asamprāptāḥ in this verse clearly indicate that material piety is a long way from perfect knowledge of the Absolute Truth. If one gives up his spiritual progress before achieving the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, he will undoubtedly fall back down into the most unpleasant material situation, even if he has achieved impersonal realization of the Brahman effulgence. As stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, āruhya kṛcchreṇa paraṁ padaṁ tataḥ patanty adhaḥ.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Those who do not have jṣāna for liberation, and who are not utterly foolish, become absorbed in artha, dharma and kāma. Without any time for hearing about the Lord, they kill themselves. The ignorant, getting mercy from those who know the truth, are delivered. Those who know the truth are naturally delivered. But those who are not so ignorant, but do not know the truth, who exist between ignorance and knowledge, fall. That is explained in this verse. Those who do not have knowledge by which to attain liberation, but who are not foolish like animals, become attached to dharma, artha and kāma, not to jṣāna or bhakti. “Let that be. When an opportunity arises, they will hear about the Lord.” No, they will not obtain even a moment’s opportunity. They kill themselves.

Personal Guidance
Religiosity, wealth, and pleasure without time for Kṛṣṇa look respectable, but they quietly kill the soul by postponing its one real need.
Practical Applications
  • Audit today's calendar and name the single block of time you will protect for hearing about Kṛṣṇa, before any other "good" activity claims it.
  • Notice the moment piety makes you feel self-satisfied, and immediately seek out a devotee whose instruction will puncture that false comfort.
  • Refuse the excuse "I am too busy with my duties to chant today" — the verse names that exact busyness as ātma-ghāta, soul-killing.
Reflection Questions
  • Which of my dharma, artha, or kāma pursuits am I most proud of, and how is that very pride keeping me from surrendering to Kṛṣṇa?
  • Where in my week have I become akṣaṇika — a person who genuinely has no spare moment for the soul — and what bodily comfort am I protecting by staying that way?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice when a packed schedule of classes, internships, gym, and social plans leaves literally zero minutes for hearing about Kṛṣṇa, and reclaim ten of those minutes today.
  • Refuse the story that you will "get serious about spiritual life later, after placements / after marriage / after settling down" — the verse names exactly that postponement as soul-killing.
  • When peers praise you for being a "good kid" — disciplined, moral, ambitious — let that praise become a warning bell, not a finish line, and check whether Kṛṣṇa has shrunk in your day.
householder
  • Before adding one more responsibility — promotion, second home, another committee — ask whether it will leave you any kṣaṇa for the soul, and decline if it will not.
  • Build the family's daily rhythm so that hearing, chanting, and prasāda are the load-bearing beams, and let career and schooling fit around them, not the reverse.
  • When material life is going well — healthy children, rising income, respect in the community — treat that prosperity as the most dangerous moment Prabhupāda names here, and double your association with devotees.
senior
  • Use the loosening of duties in this stage to recover the moments your working years could not spare, and give them deliberately to hearing Bhāgavatam and chanting.
  • When younger family members admire your respectable, pious life, tell them honestly where you mistook trai-vargika success for spiritual arrival, so they do not repeat it.
  • Face the nearness of the next body without flinching — the verse warns that even impersonal realization can fall; deepen personal surrender to Kṛṣṇa now, while the mind can still be trained.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading the verse as a rebuke of "bad" worldly people while quietly exempting one's own respectable, pious, hard-working life from the same diagnosis.
  • Treating preliminary spiritual progress — some chanting, some study, a steadier family life — as proof of arrival, and easing off the very practices that produced it.
  • Using "I am still doing my dharma" as a permanent substitute for surrender, forgetting that trai-vargika life without Kṛṣṇa is still counted here as soul-killing.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a concrete area where your dharma, artha, or kāma is in good order — and then examine honestly whether that very order is making Kṛṣṇa feel less urgent.
  • The verse calls busy, pious people killers of their own soul. Where do you resist that language, and where does it land?
  • Compare a time you fell from gross sinful life into devotee association with a time you coasted on material piety. Which was harder to wake up from, and why?
Role Play

A successful, ethical professional — clean living, generous to charity, devoted to family, attends temple on festivals — tells a fellow student, "I am a good person, I do my duty, I do not need to chant sixteen rounds or hear Bhāgavatam daily; that is for renunciants." One student plays this person; another plays a friend who has read SB 11.5.16 and must gently show that this very stance is what the verse calls akṣaṇika and ātma-ghāta, without moralizing or shaming.

Objections & Responses
  • Calling decent, law-abiding, family-supporting people "killers of their own soul" is harsh and elitist — surely a moral life is enough.
    The verse is not denying that pious life is better than sinful life; Prabhupāda explicitly says the materially pious enjoy real mitigation of misery. The harshness is diagnostic, not insulting: even an excellent material life ends in another body, and a life that never spares a moment to ask whose servant I am has, in fact, wasted the only opportunity this human form was given. Naming that clearly is compassion, not elitism.
  • I am genuinely too busy — job, children, aging parents, bills. "No time for higher purpose" is just honest, not soul-killing.
    Honesty about busyness is good; the verse asks a sharper question. The word akṣaṇika means not having even a moment — and almost no one is literally that. We always find a moment for what we treat as non-negotiable. So the issue is not the schedule but the ranking: Kṛṣṇa has been quietly slotted below dharma, artha, and kāma. Begin with one fixed, small, daily act of hearing or chanting that cannot be moved, and let the rest of the busy life arrange itself around it.
