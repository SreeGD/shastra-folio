Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.6

कर्मण्यकोविदा: स्तब्धा मूर्खा: पण्डितमानिन: । वदन्ति चाटुकान् मूढा यया माध्व्या गिरोत्सुका: ॥ ६ ॥

Ignorant of the art of work, such arrogantly proud fools, enchanted and enlivened by the sweet words of the Vedas, pose as learned authorities and offer flattering entreaties to the demigods.
Purport

The words karmaṇy akovidāḥ refer to those who are ignorant of the art of performing work in such a way that there will be no future bondage. This art is described in Bhagavad-gītā: yajṣārthāt karmaṇo ’nyatra loko ’yaṁ karma-bandhanaḥ. Work must be performed for the satisfaction of Viṣṇu, otherwise work is the cause of future bondage in the cycle of repeated birth and death. The word stabdhāḥ, “puffed up by false pride,” indicates that although ignorant persons do not know the art of working properly, they do not inquire from learned devotees, nor do they accept the advice of the Lord’s own men. Being infatuated by the fruitive results offered in the Vedas, such mūrkhas, or fools, think, “We are learned Vedic scholars; we have understood everything perfectly.” Thus they are attracted to such Vedic statements as apāma somam amṛtā abhūma (“We have drunk the soma juice and now we are immortal”), akṣayyaṁ ha vai cāturmāsya-yājinaḥ sukṛtaṁ bhavati (“For one who executes the cāturmāsya sacrifice there is inexhaustible pious reaction”), and yatra noṣṇaṁ na śītaṁ syān na glānir nāpy arātayaḥ (“Let us go to that material planet where there is no heat, no cold, no diminution and no enemies”). Such foolish persons are unaware that even Lord Brahmā, the creator of the universe, will die at the end of universal time, what to speak of materialistic followers of the Vedas who jump like frogs to the different celestial planets, seeking the highest standard of sense gratification. Such bewildered Vedic scholars dream of frolicking with the Apsarās, the gorgeous society girls of the heavenly planets who are expert in singing, dancing and in general stimulating uncontrollable lusty desires. Thus, those who are carried away by the heavenly phantasmagoria offered in the karma-kāṇḍa section of the Vedas gradually develop an atheistic mentality. Actually, the entire universe is meant to be offered to Lord Viṣṇu as sacrifice. The conditioned soul can thereby gradually elevate himself to the eternal kingdom beyond the hallucination of material sense gratification. However, being puffed up by false pride, the materialistic followers of the Vedas remain perpetually ignorant of the supremacy and beauty of Lord Viṣṇu.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Those ignorant of how to act without accruing results since they are proud and foolish, and think themselves learned, speak sweet words among themselves because of being bewildered by the promising words of the Vedas. They are ignorant because they do not know how to do activities without creating bondage. Since they are proud, and since they are fools, thinking themselves learned, they do not ask from those who know. They become bewildered by sweet words such as “We became immortal by drinking soma,” or “Those who observe cāturmasya sacrifices become immortal” “Svarga is a place where there is no heat, cold, fatigue or enemies.” Consequently, they speak sweet words among themselves such as “We will enjoy with the Apsarās.”

Personal Guidance
Vedic flattery aimed at heavenly enjoyment is not learning but proud ignorance; real skill in work is offering it to Viṣṇu.
Practical Applications
  • Before starting any task today, name aloud whom it is for — and if the answer is not Kṛṣṇa, re-aim it before you begin.
  • Notice one place where you posture as the "knowledgeable one" and instead ask a sincere question of a devotee senior to you.
  • Refuse to chase any reward — promotion, praise, comfort — that you would be embarrassed to offer at Kṛṣṇa's feet.
Reflection Questions
  • Which sweet promises — career, retirement, status, even subtle "spiritual" perks — am I quietly working toward as if they were the real goal?
  • Where does my self-image as "learned" or "experienced" actually block me from hearing correction from Kṛṣṇa's devotees?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Catch yourself the next time you quote a teaching or post something "spiritual" online mainly to look wise to peers, and delete or rewrite it for Kṛṣṇa instead.
  • Pick one upcoming exam, audition, or application and consciously offer the effort to Kṛṣṇa beforehand, releasing your grip on the specific outcome.
  • When older devotees correct you, resist the reflex to defend your intelligence; sit with the correction for a full day before responding.
householder
  • Before the next big family decision — a job change, a move, a school for the children — write down honestly what "sweet promise" is driving it, and test it against Viṣṇu's satisfaction.
  • Stop using your professional or scriptural credentials to win arguments at home; let your spouse and children see you ask a more advanced devotee for guidance.
  • Choose one income stream or ambition and deliberately cap it, refusing further pursuit of comforts you would not want to defend before Kṛṣṇa.
senior
  • Audit the legacy you are quietly building — wealth, reputation, lineage — and ask which parts are still bids for a personal heaven rather than offerings to Viṣṇu.
  • When younger seekers come to you, refuse the role of unquestioned authority; speak as a fellow servant still being corrected, and point them past yourself to Kṛṣṇa.
  • Loosen one attachment this month — a possession, a position, a grievance — that you have been holding as your "earned" reward for a lifetime of duty.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as a critique of "those other people" (ritualists, materialists) while exempting one's own subtle bargaining with the Lord.
  • Concluding that scripture or ritual itself is the problem, rather than the proud, fruitive mood in which it is taken up.
  • Mistaking command of Sanskrit terms or doctrinal vocabulary for actual skill in work, which is yajña offered to Viṣṇu.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share a concrete "sweet promise" the modern world dangles before you — and discuss honestly whether your sādhana is partly a strategy to secure it.
  • When have you posed as the learned one in a spiritual conversation? What did that pride cost you, and who paid the price?
  • Where is the line between honoring varṇāśrama duties with their natural rewards and slipping into karma-kāṇḍa fruitiveness? Try to disagree.
Role Play

Two students pair up. One plays a confident, well-read seeker who quotes scripture to justify pursuing a high-paying career "as an offering," subtly attached to the lifestyle it will buy. The other plays a fellow devotee who must, without insulting them, help them see the karmaṇy akovidāḥ pattern in their reasoning and point them toward work performed for Viṣṇu's satisfaction. Switch roles after five minutes.

Objections & Responses
  • Isn't it unfair to call Vedic scholars "fools"? They studied for decades — surely their learning counts for something.
    The verse does not mock study; it mocks study divorced from its purpose. Vedaiś ca sarvair aham eva vedyaḥ — all the Vedas are meant to lead to Kṛṣṇa. A scholar who masters every branch but uses it to bargain for heavenly enjoyment has missed the one thing the texts exist to give. That is the precise sense in which he is called a fool — akovida in karma, ignorant of how work actually works.
  • I don't pray to demigods or chase heavenly planets. This verse has nothing to do with my life.
    The demigods and heavens are just the period's vocabulary for upgraded sense gratification. The structure is what matters: using a sacred framework to flatter a higher power into giving you a better material situation. Substitute career, health, family outcomes, or even a "peaceful spiritual life" — if that is what we are really after, we are the very person this verse is describing.
