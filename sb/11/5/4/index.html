Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.4

दूरे हरिकथा: केचिद् दूरे चाच्युतकीर्तना: । स्त्रिय: शूद्रादयश्चैव तेऽनुकम्प्या भवाद‍ृशाम् ॥ ४ ॥

There are many persons who have little opportunity to take part in discussions about the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Hari, and thus it is difficult for them to chant His infallible glories. Persons such as women, śūdras and other fallen classes always deserve the mercy of great personalities like yourself.
Purport

In the previous verse it was explained that some persons are ignorant of the glories of the Personality of Godhead ( na bhajanti ), whereas others, although aware of the Lord, ridicule Him or state that the Lord is also material ( avajānanti ). In this verse the first class, namely the ignorant, are described as fit candidates for the mercy of a pure devotee. The word dūre indicates those who have little opportunity to hear and chant the glories of the Lord. According to Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura they can be explained as ye sādhu-saṅga-bhāgya-hīnāḥ, those who are bereft of the association of saintly persons and pure devotees. Generally, those who are advanced in the spiritual science of Kṛṣṇa consciousness avoid contact with women and low-class men. In general, women are lusty, and śūdras and other low-class men are habitually addicted to materialistic habits such as smoking, drinking and woman hunting. Therefore, Caitanya Mahāprabhu warned the sādhus, or saintly persons, to avoid intimate connection with women and low-class men. The practical result of such a restriction is that women and lower-class men are often bereft of the opportunity to hear the glories of the Lord chanted by saintly persons; thus Śrī Camasa Muni instructs the King that he should especially give his mercy to such fallen persons.

Our spiritual master, His Divine Grace Śrīla Prabhupāda, was severely criticized in India for giving all classes of men and women the opportunity to take part in the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement. Indeed, the caste brāhmaṇas of India and other persons concerned only with ritualistic formalities were shocked that Śrīla Prabhupāda allowed women and persons born in low-class families to take part freely in the Vaiṣṇava culture and even become initiated as bona fide brāhmaṇas. However, Śrīla Prabhupāda could understand that in this age practically everyone is fallen. He saw that if spiritual life were to be limited to the so-called higher classes, there would be no possibility of spreading a genuine spiritual movement all around the world. The mercy of Caitanya Mahāprabhu is so great and the holy name of Kṛṣṇa so potent that any man, woman, child or even animal can become purified by chanting Kṛṣṇa’s name and taking prasādam, the sanctified remnants of Kṛṣṇa’s food. In Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s movement, no sincere person is restricted from achieving the highest perfection of self-realization. Whereas the impersonalists and yogīs tend to be selfishly concerned with their individual realization and achievement of mystic power, it has always been the custom of the Vaiṣṇavas to be merciful to all classes of living entities.

It is understood that the conversation between the nava-yogendras and King Nimi took place approximately at the time of Lord Rāmacandra, many hundreds of thousands of years ago. But in the Bhagavad-gītā, which was spoken only five thousand years ago, Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself also states that any person, despite his material situation of life, can become the most dear devotee of the Lord by full surrender to the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore, the fallen people of Kali-yuga should take advantage of the special mercy of the Vaiṣṇavas and join the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement to perfect their lives and go back home, back to Godhead.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Persons devoid of devotee association and who cannot appreciate glorification of the Lord, such as women and śūdras, deserve your mercy. You should be merciful to those who are ignorant. That is explained in this verse. Those who are distant from topics of the Lord—who are devoid of the good fortune of association with devotees, who are distant from kīrtana of the Lord—who are deaf to it--should receive your mercy first by instructions on bhakti and then by the dust of you lotus feet placed on their heads.
Personal Guidance
Those far from hari-kathā — women, śūdras, the fallen — are not to be avoided but mercifully drawn in; this is the Vaiṣṇava's distinguishing compassion.
Practical Applications
  • Identify one person in your circle who has no access to hari-kathā and speak Kṛṣṇa's name or a single verse to them today, plainly, without preaching at them.
  • Refuse the inner snobbery that ranks people by their outward qualification, and offer prasāda or kīrtana to someone you would normally screen out.
  • Notice when 'avoiding bad association' has quietly become contempt for the fallen, and correct it by serving one such person concretely this week.
Reflection Questions
  • Whom have I written off as too worldly, too lusty, too uninterested in Kṛṣṇa — and what would it cost me to extend mercy to that exact person?
  • Where does my 'discrimination' about association serve my own comfort rather than the upliftment of those far from hari-kathā?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Pick one classmate or coworker whom your friend group quietly looks down on — the 'party' one, the one who's 'not spiritual' — and share one specific thing about Kṛṣṇa with them this week without trying to fix them.
  • Refuse the social-media instinct to curate your spiritual circle to only the impressive practitioners; follow, message, or sit with someone obviously far from hari-kathā.
  • Notice when 'avoiding bad association' becomes a clean excuse to drop people you simply find uncool, and re-engage with one of them on Kṛṣṇa's terms.
householder
  • In your home, identify the family member — in-law, cousin, employee, household help — who is most 'far from hari-kathā,' and create one small, dignified opening for them this month: prasāda at a meal, a festival invitation, a verse spoken kindly.
  • When raising your children, refuse the temptation to gatekeep bhakti by their performance — let the struggling, distracted, or 'worldly' child receive the same kīrtana and prasāda as the obedient one.
  • In your workplace, treat the colleague whose lifestyle you most disapprove of as a candidate for Kṛṣṇa's mercy, not for your correction — let one act of genuine respect this week carry the verse.
senior
  • Use the authority of your age to mentor someone the community has written off — a younger relative tangled in material life, a neighbor no one visits — and bring them hari-kathā in language they can actually receive.
  • Loosen the lifelong attachment to 'standards' and 'qualification' that may have hardened in you, and spend time this week with one person whom your younger self would have avoided as unsuitable association.
  • As part of your legacy, deliberately give a book, a set of beads, or a recording of the holy name to someone outside the usual devotee circle — a caregiver, a worker, a distant relative — so that your final years widen the door Prabhupāda opened.
Common Pitfalls
  • Using Caitanya Mahāprabhu's warning about intimate contact with women and low-class men as license for caste-style disdain rather than careful, merciful outreach.
  • Mistaking material compassion — pitying people's bodily condition — for the real compassion of giving them the holy name.
  • Restricting bhakti to those who already look qualified, and thereby reproducing the very exclusivism Śrīla Prabhupāda dismantled when he opened the movement worldwide.
Discussion Prompts
  • Where is the line between Caitanya Mahāprabhu's caution about intimate contact with women and low-class men and Camasa Muni's instruction here to specifically extend mercy to them? Find a case where you would draw it differently from someone else in the group.
  • Śrīla Prabhupāda was attacked by caste brāhmaṇas for initiating women and people from low-born families. Where in your own life do you still carry the caste-brāhmaṇa instinct, and where do you carry Prabhupāda's?
  • Who counts as 'striyaḥ śūdrādayaḥ' in your city today — which groups are structurally far from hari-kathā — and what would concrete mercy toward them look like, beyond a slogan?
Role Play

A senior devotee in your temple objects that a newcomer — a young woman who works in nightlife and still smokes — should not be encouraged to attend the morning program because she will 'disturb the standard.' You are asked to respond on the spot, citing this verse, in a way that neither dismisses the concern about association nor abandons the newcomer.

Objections & Responses
  • This verse is openly demeaning — lumping women with śūdras and 'fallen classes' is exactly the kind of patriarchy modern readers cannot accept.
    Read the verse as Camasa Muni intends: he is rebuking the spiritual elite for hoarding hari-kathā, not ranking souls. The categories name social disadvantage of that era — those structurally cut off from sādhu-saṅga — and the whole thrust is that such persons especially deserve mercy. Prabhupāda enacted exactly this by initiating women and people from every background as bona fide brāhmaṇas, which the caste establishment found scandalous.
  • If we are supposed to avoid intimate contact with women and materialistic people, how can we simultaneously be told to give them special mercy? It sounds contradictory.
    The two instructions sit at different levels. The sādhu guards his own sādhana by not entangling himself in lust or materialistic intimacy; the same sādhu, from that protected position, freely distributes hari-nāma, prasāda, and kīrtana to exactly those people. Mercy is not intimacy — it is the gift of Kṛṣṇa's name to those who would otherwise never hear it.
