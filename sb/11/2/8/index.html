Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.2.8

अहं किल पुरानन्तं प्रजार्थो भुवि मुक्तिदम् । अपूजयं न मोक्षाय मोहितो देवमायया ॥ ८ ॥

In a previous birth on this earth, I worshiped the Supreme Lord, Ananta, who alone can award liberation, but because I desired to have a child, I did not worship Him for liberation. Thus I was bewildered by the Lord’s illusory energy.
Purport

According to Śrīdhara Svāmī the word kila (meaning “indeed, it is true,” “it is said” or “as is well known”) indicates that Vasudeva was remembering the words the Lord had spoken to him when the Lord had appeared as four-armed Viṣṇu in the prison of Kaṁsa. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī states that from the anxiety of Vasudeva, which is expressed in this verse by the words apūjayaṁ na mokṣāya mohito deva-māyayā, it is to be discerned that Vasudeva had heard of the curse of the brāhmaṇas at Piṇḍāraka against the Yadu dynasty and that he could understand from this curse that the Lord’s disappearance from the earth was imminent. Vasudeva understood that the Lord’s manifest pastimes within this universe were coming to an end, and he now lamented that he had not directly taken advantage of the opportunity to worship Kṛṣṇa to go back home, back to Godhead.

Significantly, Vasudeva has used the word mukti-dam to describe the Lord. Mukti-dam is a synonym of Mukunda, or that personality who can give liberation from the cycle of birth and death. It is stated in Vedic literature that even the demigods are bound to the cycle of birth and death, although their life span is inconceivably long by earthly calculations. It is the almighty God alone who can free the conditioned soul from the reactions to his previous sinful activities and award him an eternal life of bliss and knowledge.

Vasudeva lamented that he had desired Kṛṣṇa to come to him as a son instead of desiring to go back to Kṛṣṇa, back to Godhead, to serve the Lord in the spiritual sky. Commenting on this incident in the Tenth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Śrīla Prabhupāda has emphasized that we should desire to go back home, back to Godhead, rather than try to bring the Lord to this world as our son. Nor can we artificially imitate the severe penances Vasudeva and Devakī performed for thousands of heavenly years in their previous births as Sutapā and Pṛśni. In this connection Śrīla Prabhupāda states, “If we want to get the Supreme Personality of Godhead to become one of us in this material world, this requires great penance, but if we want to go back to Kṛṣṇa ( tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti so ’rjuna ), we need only understand Him and love Him. Through love only we can very easily go back home, back to Godhead.” Śrīla Prabhupāda goes on to explain that Caitanya Mahāprabhu freely bestows the benediction of love of Kṛṣṇa, which allows one to return to Kṛṣṇa’s abode, through the chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra. This chanting process is far more effective in this age than artificial attempts to perform severe penances and austerities. Śrīla Prabhupāda concludes, “Therefore, one need not undergo severe penances for many thousands of years. One need only learn how to love Kṛṣṇa and be always engaged in His service ( sevonmukhe hi jihvādau svayam eva sphuraty adaḥ ). Then one can very easily go back home, back to Godhead. Instead of bringing the Lord here for some material purpose, to have a son or whatever else, if we go back home, back to Godhead, our real relationship with the Lord is revealed, and we eternally engage in our eternal relationship. By chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, we gradually develop our eternal relationship with the Supreme Person and thus attain the perfection called svarūpa-siddhi. We should take advantage of this benediction and go back home, back to Godhead.” ( Bhāg. 10.3.38 purport)

Although Vasudeva and Devakī desired that Kṛṣṇa become their son, it should be understood that they are devotees eternally situated in love of Kṛṣṇa. As stated by the Lord Himself ( Bhāg. 10.3.39 ), mohitau devamāyayā: Vasudeva and Devakī, His pure devotees, were covered by His internal potency. In the Fourth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (4.1.20) the great sage Atri Muni prayed to the Lord, prajām ātma-samāṁ mahyaṁ prayacchatu: “Kindly be pleased to offer me a son exactly like You.” Atri Muni said he wanted a son exactly like the Lord, and therefore he was not a pure devotee, because he had a desire to be fulfilled and that desire was material. If he had desired the Supreme Personality of Godhead as his child, he would have been completely free of material desires because he would have wanted the Supreme Absolute Truth, but because he wanted a similar child, his desire was material. Thus Atri Muni cannot be counted among the pure devotees. Vasudeva and Devakī, however, did want the Lord Himself, and therefore they were pure devotees of the Lord. In this verse, therefore, Vasudeva’s statement apūjayaṁ na mokṣāya mohito deva-māyayā should be taken to mean that Kṛṣṇa’s internal potency bewildered Vasudeva so that he would desire Kṛṣṇa as his son. This paved the way for the Lord’s appearance as the son of His loving devotees.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
In a previous birth on this earth, I worshiped the Supreme Lord, Ananta, who alone can award liberation, but because I desired to have a child, I did not worship him for liberation, since I was bewildered by the Lord's illusory energy. “But you are successful, yes?” I wanted a child, not liberation.

Personal Guidance
Even worshiping the Lord for a child instead of for liberation is deva-māyā — aim worship at Kṛṣṇa Himself, not at the gifts He can give.
Practical Applications
  • Audit one prayer you repeated this week and name the material fruit you were really after — then re-offer the same prayer aiming only at Kṛṣṇa's service.
  • When chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra today, refuse to slot in any secondary request, and let the holy name be the entire petition.
  • Identify one 'good' desire (a child, a promotion, a healing) you have been asking the Lord to deliver, and consciously place going back to Godhead above it on your list.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I trying to pull Kṛṣṇa down into my situation as a useful presence, instead of letting Him pull me up into His service?
  • Which of my most respectable, dharmic desires — a child, a household, a legacy — is quietly standing between me and mukti, and how would I notice if it were?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Before your next exam or interview, notice if your prayer is 'Kṛṣṇa, give me this result' and rewrite it as 'Kṛṣṇa, let me serve You whether or not this works out.'
  • When you scroll social media tonight, catch the moment you start asking the universe for validation — and redirect that same wanting toward one round of japa instead.
  • Pick one ambition your family or friend group is pressuring you toward and ask honestly whether you want it for Kṛṣṇa's service or to secure a future apart from Him.
householder
  • In your next family decision — school, job change, a move — name out loud whether the choice is being made to support bhakti or to extract a material fruit, and let the answer reshape the decision.
  • When you pray for your children's health, marriage, or success, append a single line aloud: 'and above all, let them and us go back to Godhead' — and mean it.
  • Refuse the subtle householder trade of doing more sādhana so the Lord will fix a family problem; do the sādhana for Him, and let the family problem be handled as His arrangement.
senior
  • Review the prayers of your earlier life and identify which ones, like Vasudeva's, asked the mukti-da Lord for something less than mukti — and tell a younger devotee what you learned, without dressing it up.
  • As mortality comes closer, stop asking Kṛṣṇa to extend or improve this body's situation and start asking only for remembrance of Him at the time of leaving it.
  • Loosen one specific attachment this week — a possession, a role, an expectation of a grandchild — and offer the loosening itself as worship of the Lord who alone gives liberation.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading this verse as a condemnation of family life itself, instead of as a warning against worshiping the mukti-da Lord for anything less than Him.
  • Concluding that any material prayer is forbidden, when the real lesson is that Kṛṣṇa-centered devotees still get bewildered by deva-māyā and must keep returning to pure aim.
  • Imitating Vasudeva and Devakī's thousand-year penances as a spiritual project, when Caitanya Mahāprabhu has already given the harināma as the age's effective process.
Discussion Prompts
  • Vasudeva calls the Lord mukti-dam in the same breath as confessing he did not worship Him for mukti — what does that pairing teach us about our own bhajana?
  • Where in your life have you, like Vasudeva, asked the Supreme to come closer for a reason other than going back to Him? Be specific with each other, not abstract.
  • Is it possible to perform pure worship inside gṛhastha-āśrama, or does household life structurally tilt our prayers toward deva-māyā? Argue both sides honestly.
Role Play

Two students play a married couple who have been chanting daily and fasting on Ekādaśī specifically so that their next child will be healthy. A third student plays a senior devotee they consult. The couple must defend their prayer; the senior devotee must, without crushing them, redirect their worship toward the mukti-da Lord while honoring their gṛhastha duties.

Objections & Responses
  • If even Vasudeva — the Lord's own father — was bewildered by deva-māyā, what hope does an ordinary practitioner have of praying purely?
    Precisely because Vasudeva is shown bewildered, we learn that purity of aim is not a private achievement but a mercy. Prabhupāda points out that Caitanya Mahāprabhu freely gives love of Kṛṣṇa through the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra in this age; we are not asked to manufacture Vasudeva's penance, only to take the harināma seriously and let it gradually purify the desire underneath our prayer.
  • Wanting a child is natural and even dharmic — calling it bewilderment by the Lord's illusory energy sounds harsh and life-denying.
    The verse does not condemn wanting a child; it diagnoses worshiping the liberation-giving Lord for that fruit. Gṛhastha duties, including raising children, remain dharma. What Vasudeva regrets is using access to Ananta for a smaller request than Ananta Himself — and that diagnosis applies to any of us who treat Kṛṣṇa as a means to our preferred outcome instead of as the goal.
