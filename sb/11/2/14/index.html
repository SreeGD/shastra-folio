Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.2.14

अत्राप्युदाहरन्तीममितिहासं पुरातनम् । आर्षभाणां च संवादं विदेहस्य महात्मन: ॥ १४ ॥

To explain the devotional service of the Lord, sages have related the ancient history of the conversation between the great soul King Videha and the sons of Ṛṣabha.
Purport

The words itihāsaṁ purātanam, meaning “ancient historical account,” are significant here. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is nigama-kalpa-taror galitaṁ phalam, the ripe fruit of the desire tree of Vedic knowledge. In the pages of the Bhāgavatam we find true historical narrations relating to the Supreme Personality of Godhead and the liberation of the conditioned souls. These historical accounts are not fiction or mythology, but describe the wonderful activities of the Lord and His devotees that took place in yugas previous to the advent of this puny age. Although mundane scholars have foolishly tried to depict the Bhāgavatam as a mythological work or a recent creation, the actual fact is that Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is a perfect transcendental literature describing not only the entire situation of this universe but also that which lies far beyond this universe, in both the material and spiritual skies. If one seriously studies the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam he becomes the most learned intellectual man. Caitanya Mahāprabhu desires that all pious persons become highly learned by hearing Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and then preach the glories of the Lord in a scientific way all over the world. It is essential that we hear these historical narrations, such as the conversation between the nava-yogendras and King Videha, with complete faith and submission. Then, as stated in verse 12 of this chapter, even if our former life is filled with abominable activities, simply by hearing Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam we shall be promoted to the same transcendental status as the Lord and His pure devotees. This is the extraordinary power of bhāgavata history, as contrasted with useless, mundane historical accounts, which ultimately serve no purpose. Although mundane historians justify their work on the plea that we have to learn from history, we can practically see that the world situation is now rapidly deteriorating into unbearable conflict and chaos while so-called historians stand helplessly by. But the bhāgavata historians who have faithfully heard Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam can give perfect and potent instructions for the restoration of a peaceful and blissful world. Therefore those who are inclined to enrich their intellectual life through the study of history should educate themselves in the historical narrations of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. This will bring them the perfection of intellectual and spiritual life.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya

Personal Guidance
Bhāgavata history is not myth but transcendental fact — hear the nava-yogendras and King Videha with faith, and even an abominable past dissolves.
Practical Applications
  • Today, sit with one section of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and read it as literal history, not as allegory or motivational reading.
  • Replace fifteen minutes of news or social feed scrolling with hearing or reading the conversation between King Videha and the sons of Ṛṣabha.
  • When tempted to dismiss a Bhāgavata narration as 'mythology,' pause and re-approach it with submissive hearing instead of mental editing.
Reflection Questions
  • Which Bhāgavata narrations have I been quietly treating as symbolic because I am embarrassed to take them as fact, and what does that reveal about my surrender?
  • If mundane history leaves the world in chaos while bhāgavata history yields peace, why do I still give my best hours to the former?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a professor or friend frames Bhāgavatam as mythology, do not argue loudly — quietly keep hearing it as itihāsa and let the strength of that conviction show in your character.
  • Swap one binge-watched series this week for hearing the Videha–Ṛṣabha-putra conversation, and notice what shifts in your mood and focus before exams.
  • Refuse the campus reflex that treats only peer-reviewed sources as 'real' — train yourself to recognize śabda-pramāṇa as the strongest evidence about the soul and Kṛṣṇa.
householder
  • Read one Bhāgavata narration aloud to your children at dinner as true history, not as a bedtime fable, so they grow up without the myth-versus-fact confusion you had to unlearn.
  • When deciding how to spend a free evening with your spouse, choose hearing Bhāgavatam together at least once this week over another episode or news cycle.
  • In your work and parenting decisions, consult the conduct of the great souls in Bhāgavatam as actual precedent, not as inspirational quotation.
senior
  • Make submissive hearing of Bhāgavatam your central daily occupation now — the verse promises this gives the perfection of intellectual and spiritual life, which is precisely your stage's work.
  • Pass on specific itihāsas like the Videha–Ṛṣabha-putra dialogue to grandchildren and younger devotees as the inheritance that actually matters, more than property or advice.
  • As the body weakens, lean harder on the verse 12 assurance — even an abominable past dissolves through faithful hearing — and let go of the lingering shame that keeps you from full surrender.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading itihāsa for entertainment or general culture rather than as instruction for liberation.
  • Mining the narration for personal lessons while skipping the explicit glorification of Kṛṣṇa and His devotees at its center.
  • Hearing such accounts casually, without the faith and submission the verse calls for, and then wondering why no transformation follows.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one Bhāgavata narration you struggle to accept as literal history — what is at stake for you in either accepting or rejecting it?
  • Mundane historians say we must learn from history, yet the world worsens. What is bhāgavata history doing differently that produces a different result?
  • How would your week look if you treated submissive hearing of Bhāgavatam as more practically useful than reading the news?
Role Play

A college classmate tells your student, 'These Bhāgavatam stories are charming mythology, but you cannot seriously believe they actually happened — that is intellectually weak.' Have the student respond from this verse, defending itihāsaṁ purātanam as literal history without becoming defensive or condescending.

Objections & Responses
  • Calling these narrations 'history' is unscientific — there is no archaeological evidence for King Videha or the sons of Ṛṣabha.
    The verse uses itihāsaṁ purātanam deliberately — 'thus it happened, long ago.' Bhāgavata history records events from previous yugas, far outside the reach of artifact-based methods that cannot even reconstruct last century without dispute. The pramāṇa here is śabda, received through paramparā from Kṛṣṇa, Vyāsa, Śukadeva, down to Prabhupāda — a stronger and more consistent evidential chain than fragmentary excavation.
  • Even if it is history, why does hearing an old conversation purify me? That sounds like magical thinking.
    It is not the antiquity that purifies but the content — these are accounts of Kṛṣṇa and His devotees, and the holy name and pastimes of the Lord carry His own potency. Verse 12 of this same chapter promises that submissive hearing elevates even one with an abominable past to the transcendental status of the Lord's pure devotees. That is not magic; that is the specific śakti of bhāgavata-kathā.
