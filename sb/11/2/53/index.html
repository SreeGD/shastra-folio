Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.2.53

त्रिभुवनविभवहेतवेऽप्यकुण्ठ- स्मृतिरजितात्मसुरादिभिर्विमृग्यात् । न चलति भगवत्पदारविन्दा- ल्ल‍वनिमिषार्धमपि य: स वैष्णवाग्य्र: ॥ ५३ ॥

vaiṣṇava-agryaḥ
the foremost of devotees of Lord Viṣṇu.
The lotus feet of the Supreme Personality of Godhead are sought even by the greatest of demigods, such as Brahmā and Śiva, who have all accepted the Supreme Personality of Godhead as their life and soul. A pure devotee of the Lord can never forget those lotus feet in any circumstance. He will not give up his shelter at the lotus feet of the Lord for a single moment — indeed, not for half a moment — even in exchange for the benediction of ruling and enjoying the opulence of the entire universe. Such a devotee of the Lord is to be considered the best of the Vaiṣṇavas.
Purport

According to Śrīla Śrīdhara Svāmī one may ask, “If one were able to gain the opulence of the entire universe in exchange for leaving the Lord’s lotus feet for just half a moment, what would be the harm in leaving the Lord’s lotus feet for such an insignificant amount of time?” The answer is given by the word akuṇṭha-smṛti. It is simply impossible for a pure devotee to forget the lotus feet of the Personality of Godhead, since everything that exists is in fact an expansion of the Supreme Lord. Since nothing is separate from the Supreme Lord, a pure devotee of the Lord cannot think of anything but the Lord. Nor can a pure devotee contemplate ruling or enjoying universal opulence; even if given all the opulence of the universe, he would immediately offer it at the lotus feet of the Lord and return to his position of a humble servant of the Lord.

The words ajitātma-surādibhir vimṛgyāt are very significant in this verse. The lotus feet of Lord Kṛṣṇa are so opulent that even the lords of all material opulence, namely Brahmā and Śiva, as well as the other demigods, are always searching for a glimpse of the Lord’s lotus feet. The word vimṛgyāt indicates that the demigods are not actually able to see the lotus feet of the Lord, but are endeavoring to see them. An example of this is given in the Tenth Canto when Lord Brahmā offers prayers to Viṣṇu, begging the Lord to rectify the disturbances on the earth.

A similar verse is found elsewhere in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (11.14.14) :

na pārameṣṭhyaṁ na mahendra-dhiṣṇyaṁ na sārvabhaumaṁ na rasādhipatyam na yoga-siddhīr apunar-bhavaṁ vā mayy arpitātmecchati mad vinānyat

“The devotee who has offered his soul to Me does not want anything if it is separate from Me — not the position of the supreme demigod of the universe, Brahmā, nor that of Lord Indra, nor kingship over the entire earth or over the lower planetary systems, nor the mystic perfections of yoga, nor even freedom from the cycle of rebirth.”

According to Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, the word ajitātmā can also be taken to mean ajitendriyāḥ, or “those whose senses are uncontrolled.” Although the demigods are all to be respected as devotees of Lord Viṣṇu, the absence of gross material discomfort in the higher planetary systems makes them tend to become affected by the bodily concept of life, and sometimes they experience some spiritual difficulty due to the gorgeous material facilities available to them. Such disturbances, however, cannot exist within the mind of a pure devotee of the Lord, as indicated in this verse by the word akuṇṭha-smṛti. According to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, we can understand from this verse that since none of the material facilities available in any of the universal planetary systems can distract a pure devotee of the Lord, such a devotee can never possibly fall or become inimical to the Lord’s service.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
He, whose remembrance is not lured by dominion over the three worlds, and who does move for half a minute or half a second from the Lord’s lotus feet which are sought by the devatās who also meditate on the Lord, is the best of devotees. The best devotee’s remembrance cannot be lured even for gaining lordship over the three worlds. na pārameṣṭhyaṁ na mahendra-dhiṣṇyaṁ na sārvabhaumaṁ na rasādhipatyam na yoga-siddhīr apunar-bhavaṁ vā mayy arpitātmecchati mad vinānyat One who has fixed his consciousness on me desires neither the position or abode of Brahmā or Indra, nor an empire on the earth, nor sovereignty in the lower planetary systems, nor the eightfold perfection of yoga, nor liberation. Such a person desires me alone. SB 11.14.14 He does not move for half a second from the lotus feet of the Lord, which are hard to obtain and thus only sought by devatās who concentrate their minds on the Lord. Or another meaning is “The lord’s lotus feet are sought by the devatās who cannot control their senses, so that they can rule the three worlds.”
Personal Guidance
The best Vaiṣṇava will not leave Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet for half a moment, even for rulership of all three worlds — that is the measure.
Practical Applications
  • Name today the one 'three-worlds' offer — a promotion, a relationship, a windfall — you would currently trade half a moment of remembrance of Kṛṣṇa for, and refuse it inwardly.
  • Set a recurring check every hour: ask 'Have I forgotten His lotus feet in the last hour?' and re-anchor by chanting one round or one śloka before continuing.
  • Refuse to treat any task — even the most opulent or urgent — as outside Kṛṣṇa; offer it at His feet before you begin, the way the pure devotee returns every gift.
Reflection Questions
  • Where in my life have I quietly accepted that 'just for a moment' of forgetting Kṛṣṇa is a fair price for material gain — and what has that small trade actually cost my devotional life?
  • If even Brahmā and Śiva are still searching for these lotus feet, what is my relationship to the access I have already been given through this line of devotees?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the 'half-moment trades' you make on your phone — the scroll, the comparison, the late-night feed — and name what 'three-worlds' fantasy each one is selling you in exchange for forgetting Kṛṣṇa.
  • Before an exam, an interview, or a difficult conversation with friends, take thirty seconds to place the outcome at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet — then act fully, without bargaining your remembrance for the result.
  • Refuse to treat your peer group's approval as a 'universe' worth trading your sādhana for; pick one daily practice — a round, a śloka, a praṇāma — that you will not move for any social opportunity this term.
householder
  • When weighing a career move, a house, or a school decision for your children, ask explicitly: does this require me to trade even half a moment of remembrance — morning chanting, association, family kīrtana — and if so, redesign the decision before accepting it.
  • When unexpected money or recognition comes into the family, do what the pure devotee does in the purport: mentally offer it at the Lord's feet first, then deploy it as a servant, not an owner.
  • Set a household standard with your spouse: there is no professional or social occasion important enough to skip the family's non-negotiable devotional anchor — pick that anchor together this week.
senior
  • Audit the attachments you are still carrying — property, reputation, family outcomes — and identify which one you would, honestly, still accept 'the three worlds' for; begin loosening that grip now, while you still have time, by offering it daily at His feet.
  • When mentoring younger devotees or family members, speak less about your accomplishments and more about the moments you almost traded remembrance for opulence and what you learned — let your legacy be akuṇṭha-smṛti, not achievements.
  • As the body weakens, refuse the temptation to treat declining health as a license to drop sādhana 'just for now'; the verse's standard is half a moment — let your final years tighten, not loosen, your shelter at His lotus feet.
Common Pitfalls
  • Reading akuṇṭha-smṛti as a feeling to manufacture, rather than a natural consequence of seeing everything as Kṛṣṇa's expansion — and then despairing when the feeling does not come on demand.
  • Using this verse to condemn one's own forgetfulness into paralysis, instead of taking it as the standard of the vaiṣṇavāgrya we are gradually approaching through sādhana.
  • Misreading the verse as devaluing varṇāśrama duties or opulence themselves — the point is not to flee responsibilities but to never trade remembrance of Him for them.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share with your partner a recent moment when you knowingly accepted 'a half-moment of forgetting' for some material payoff — what was the payoff, and was it worth what you describe here?
  • The verse says even Brahmā and Śiva are still searching for these lotus feet. Does that inspire you or discourage you about your own access through this line — and why?
  • If a pure devotee, handed the opulence of the universe, would immediately offer it back at the Lord's feet, what is one form of opulence in your life right now that you are still holding rather than offering?
Role Play

Two students pair up. One is a working professional who has just been offered a career-defining role that will consume the next five years and quietly displace morning sādhana and association of devotees; the other is an older Vaiṣṇava friend who has been asked for counsel. The friend must speak from the standard of this verse — not forbidding the role, but pressing on the akuṇṭha-smṛti question: can the role be accepted without trading even half a moment at the Lord's lotus feet? After eight minutes, switch roles with a different scenario: a windfall inheritance.

Objections & Responses
  • This sets an impossibly high bar — not forgetting for half a moment. Realistically no modern practitioner can meet that. Isn't this verse just discouraging?
    The verse is describing the vaiṣṇavāgrya — the best of Vaiṣṇavas — as a definite standard, not as the entry ticket. Prabhupāda's purport, citing Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, frames it as the assurance that such a devotee can never fall. For us it functions as a true north: it tells us what we are walking toward through chanting and service, and it exposes the small trades we make daily so we can begin to refuse them. Discouragement comes from comparing oneself to the destination; encouragement comes from seeing that the line itself, through this very verse, is showing us the way.
  • Demigods like Brahmā and Śiva are great souls in their own right — saying they 'cannot see' Kṛṣṇa's feet while a chanting practitioner can sounds sectarian.
    The verse and purport are precise: ajitātma-surādibhir vimṛgyāt — the demigods are endeavoring, searching. Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura explains that the very opulence of the higher planets tends to draw even great souls into the bodily concept, which is why the pure devotee's akuṇṭha-smṛti is glorified above their position. It is not a denigration of the demigods — they are respected as devotees of Viṣṇu — but a sober statement that material facility, even celestial, is a real distraction, and shelter at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, not cosmic post, is the actual treasure.
