Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.10.3

सुप्तस्य विषयालोको ध्यायतो वा मनोरथ: । नानात्मकत्वाद् विफलस्तथा भेदात्मधीर्गुणै: ॥ ३ ॥

[suptasya vishayaaloko]

One who is sleeping may see many objects of sense gratification in a dream, but such pleasurable things are merely creations of the mind and are thus ultimately useless. Similarly, the living entity who is asleep to his spiritual identity also sees many sense objects, but these innumerable objects of temporary gratification are creations of the Lord’s illusory potency and have no permanent existence. One who meditates upon them, impelled by the senses, uselessly engages his intelligence.
Purport

Because the fruits of material work are temporary, it ultimately does not matter whether or not one obtains them; the final result is the same. Materialistic activities can never award the highest perfection of life, Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The material intelligence, impelled by the senses, strongly desires sense gratification. As stated here ( bhedātma-dhīḥ ), such intelligence actually separates one from one’s real self-interest. Thus the intelligence, absorbed in that which is materially favorable and unfavorable, becomes divided in pursuit of innumerable categories of material advancement. Such divided intelligence is impotent and cannot understand the Absolute Truth, the Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa. The devotees of the Lord, however, have their intelligence fixed on one point — Lord Kṛṣṇa. They meditate upon the Lord’s form, qualities, pastimes and devotees, and thus their intelligence is never separated from the Absolute Truth. As stated in Bhagavad-gītā (2.41) :

vyavasāyātmikā buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana bahu-śākhā hy anantāś ca buddhayo ’vyavasāyinām

“Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one. O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many branched.”

If one is not Kṛṣṇa conscious, he is uselessly dreaming without any understanding of his eternal situation. The material intelligence will always devise novel means of achieving happiness, and therefore one bounces from one fruitless program of sense gratification to another, ignoring the simple fact that all material things are temporary and will disappear. In this way one’s intelligence becomes infected with material lust and greed, and such infected intelligence cannot bring one to the true goal of life. One should hear from the bona fide spiritual master whose intelligence is pure, and then one will come to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the highest perfection of life.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
Just as objects seen in dreams or in the waking mind yield no spiritual results since they depend on material objects, intelligence depending on material objects using the material senses yields no spiritual results. Because material results are destructible, they are equivalent of their non-attainment. Spiritual results are not obtainable by material senses. That is explained in this verse. Because of taking support of many material objects, the perceptions in sleep have no spiritual result. Because the intelligence takes support of various material objects (bhedātma), by the material senses (guṇaiḥ), it yields no spiritual result. Intelligence simply by being individually aware of various objects made of the guṇas by means of the senses does not yield spiritual results. The results are like objects experienced in dreams, generated from the mind, since they take support of material objects either auspicious or inauspicious. But intelligence which takes support of the Lord alone yields spiritual results. There is oneness in this perception of intelligence, concerning the Lords’ form, qualities pastimes and devotees, because one cannot separate them since they are all spiritual and since they are all auspicious. It is said: vyavasāyātmikā buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana | bahu-śākhā hy anantāś ca buddhayo ’vyavasāyinām || Resolute intelligence is only one in this process, O son of the Kurus. The intelligence of those who are irresolute has many branches of action and unlimited desires. BG 2.41
Personal Guidance
Sense objects pursued by a soul asleep to Kṛṣṇa are dream-fruits — real-seeming, ultimately empty; fix one-pointed intelligence on Him.
Practical Applications
  • Name one "sense-object dream" you chased today — a scroll, a snack, a fantasy — and notice it left you exactly as empty as a dream upon waking.
  • Before any pleasurable pursuit, ask whether this engages bhedātma-dhīḥ (divided intelligence) or vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ fixed on Kṛṣṇa, and choose accordingly.
  • Hear something from a bona fide source about Kṛṣṇa's form, qualities, or devotees today, to retrain intelligence away from many branches toward one point.
Reflection Questions
  • Which "favorable" and "unfavorable" categories has my intelligence been splitting itself across this week, and what has that division cost my devotional life?
  • If the objects of my daydreaming are as unreal as last night's dream, what am I actually defending when I refuse to let them go?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • When a feed, a crush, or a future-career fantasy hijacks twenty minutes of your attention, name it as manoratha and notice how it leaves your mind more scattered, not more satisfied.
  • Pick one exam or project this week and do it with vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ — one resolved aim, offered to Kṛṣṇa — instead of the usual many-branched anxiety about results, status, and comparison.
  • Choose one source of hearing about Kṛṣṇa — a class, a recorded lecture, a senior devotee — and let it, not the algorithm, shape your intelligence this week.
householder
  • In the next family money or career decision, separate genuine duty toward dependents from bhedātma-dhīḥ chasing favorable and unfavorable categories of advancement, and act from the first only.
  • Notice one "if only we had X" daydream you and your spouse share, and test it against this verse before you spend another year of intelligence pursuing it.
  • Bring Kṛṣṇa explicitly into one ordinary householder act today — a meal, a bedtime, a work email — so your intelligence practices fixing on Him while embedded in saṃsāra.
senior
  • Take inventory of the material achievements and disappointments of decades and feel, soberly, how exactly they now resemble last night's dream — let that recognition loosen present grips.
  • When you find yourself rehearsing old regrets or unfulfilled plans, recognize manoratha in the past tense and redirect that same attention to hearing and remembering Kṛṣṇa.
  • Offer the next mentoring conversation as a transmission of vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ — point a younger person toward one-pointed devotion rather than toward your own former many-branched strategies.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating "the world is like a dream" as license for passivity or callousness, instead of as a call to reorient intelligence toward Kṛṣṇa.
  • Drifting into impersonalist language — "all is illusion," "the Self alone is real" — and forgetting that the antidote here is meditation on Kṛṣṇa personally.
  • Renouncing a few obvious sense objects while leaving the underlying bhedātma-dhīḥ intact, so the same divided intelligence simply finds subtler dreams.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one "manoratha" — a private mental scenario you replay — and let the group test whether it's strengthening or fragmenting your intelligence.
  • Where does this verse leave room for legitimate worldly responsibilities, and where does the group disagree about that line?
  • Compare a time you woke from a literal dream of pleasure with a time you "woke" from a long-cherished material plan — what was similar, what was different?
Role Play

One student plays a friend who just received a long-awaited promotion, salary, and recognition and is exhilarated; the other plays a Gauḍīya practitioner who must honor the friend's effort without flattering bhedātma-dhīḥ, gently pointing toward the dream-likeness of the reward while not preaching down.

Objections & Responses
  • If material objects are just dream-creations, this teaching seems to devalue real human suffering and joy and excuse withdrawal from the world.
    The verse does not deny that experiences feel vivid — dreams feel vivid too — it denies their permanence and their power to deliver the highest perfection. Prabhupāda's purport keeps the devotee engaged, only with intelligence fixed on Kṛṣṇa's form, qualities, pastimes, and devotees; that is not withdrawal but redirection.
  • Modern psychology shows that fulfilling desires and achieving goals actually does increase well-being, so calling sense pursuits "useless" overstates the case.
    The verse grants that such pursuits produce results; it calls them viphala — without the fruit that actually matters, namely the soul's eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa. Temporary well-being is real on its own scale and still leaves intelligence many-branched and unable to grasp the Absolute Truth, Śrī Kṛṣṇa.
