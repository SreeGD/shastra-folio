Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.10.7

One should see one’s real self-interest in life in all circumstances and should therefore remain detached from wife, children, home, land, relatives, friends, wealth and so on.
Purport

A devotee of the Lord recognizes that his wife, children, home, land, friends and money are meant to be engaged in the loving service of the Supreme Lord. Therefore, he does not frantically make arrangements for the sense gratification of his family and friends. He is not eager to enjoy the false prestige of being the master of his wife and the lord of his children, nor is he anxious to gain prestige from his friends and society. Thus he does not envy anyone and is not lazy in the matter of self-realization. He is free from the false sense of proprietorship and is always eager to develop his understanding of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. He is free from false egotism and automatically turns away from useless materialistic conversation. Thus he is steady and not whimsical, and he is always firmly situated in loving friendship at the lotus feet of the spiritual master.

The question may be raised as to how one can develop freedom from false proprietorship. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura has given the following example. An ordinary man is very eager to accumulate more and more money, and he maintains his wealth in the form of bank accounts, properties, gold, and so on. As long as these different assets are contributing to his financial well-being, he sees them equally and considers that they belong to him. But if some of his assets are taken by the government for taxes, or if they are lost in an unfortunate business arrangement, then he is forced to give up his sense of proprietorship. In the same way, everyone should be intelligent enough to observe that one’s sense of ownership over innumerable material objects is not permanent; therefore one should develop detachment from these things. If one does not cultivate a loving feeling of friendship for the Supreme Personality of Godhead and His pure devotee, the spiritual master, one will undoubtedly be entangled by the network of material society, friendship and love. One will then remain bound up on the material platform with no hope of permanent happiness.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
One should see one’s real self-interest in life in all circumstances and should therefore remain detached from wife, children, home, land, relatives, friends, wealth and other things. By what type of consideration does lack of possessiveness take place? One should neutrally see one’s possessions such as gold and silver. One should have possessiveness only until attaining what is necessary for survival, and not longer than that. One should view one’s wife and children similarly. One should not have possessiveness because one sees that possessiveness is an impediment to full surrender to the Lord. The son of Citraketu said: yathā vastūni paṇyāni hemādīni tatas tataḥ paryaṭanti nareṣv evaṁ jīvo yoniṣu kartṛṣu nityasyārthasya sambandho hy anityo dṛśyate nṛṣu yāvad yasya hi sambandho mamatvaṁ tāvad eva hi Just as commodities and coins pass among many people, the jīva wanders into various wombs with various fathers and mothers. It is seen that the relationships of one object with many people are temporary. As long as the relationship lasts, one has possessiveness of the object. SB 6.16.6-7 But one should not lack possessiveness of guru and the Lord. Thus it is said one should have firm attachment to them (dṛḍah-sauhṛdaḥ).

Personal Guidance
See your real self-interest in Krsna alone, and stay detached from spouse, children, home, land, kin, friends, and wealth as ends in themselves.
Practical Applications
  • Name three things you secretly call "mine" today, and consciously re-offer each one to Krsna as His property entrusted to your care.
  • Refuse one act of frantic arranging for a relative's sense gratification today, and instead invite them into one small act of devotional service.
  • Before any major decision about family, home, or money this week, ask whether it engages these things in the Lord's service or just inflates your role as proprietor.
Reflection Questions
  • Where am I still drawing my identity from being the "master" of my spouse, the "lord" of my children, or the provider over my friends?
  • Which specific possession or relationship would shake me most if it were suddenly taken, and what does that exposure reveal about where I have placed my real self-interest?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Notice the next time you measure yourself by your friend group, your follower count, or your partner's attention, and quietly remind yourself that none of these are actually "yours" to own.
  • Refuse one purchase or social move this week whose only purpose is to look like a proprietor of status, image, or relationships among your peers.
  • Speak to one family member today not as someone you are trying to manage or impress, but as a soul Krsna has placed in your life to serve.
householder
  • Before approving the next family expense or upgrade, pause and ask explicitly whether it is for Krsna's service through the family or for inflating your role as provider.
  • Refuse to negotiate, gossip, or strategize today purely to gain prestige from your spouse, children, or social circle, and notice how often that impulse appears.
  • Sit down once this week with your spouse and review one asset, decision, or worry as something held in trust for the Lord rather than something you personally own.
senior
  • Identify one possession, role, or relationship you are still gripping as "mine," and take one concrete step this month to loosen that grip while you still can choose freely.
  • Speak to a younger family member today not to assert your authority or legacy, but to point them, even subtly, toward the lotus feet of the spiritual master and Krsna.
  • When the body's losses or others' indifference sting, refuse self-pity and instead use the moment as the very training in detachment that the purport describes as forced upon the ordinary man.
Common Pitfalls
  • Mistaking detachment for neglect, and using this verse to excuse cold or careless treatment of one's family and dependents.
  • Performing outward indifference toward home and wealth while inwardly still calculating, hoarding, and craving prestige from them.
  • Treating "seeing equally" as a flat impersonal sameness rather than seeing each person and asset as connected to Krsna's service.
Discussion Prompts
  • Share one relationship or possession where you can honestly feel the difference between using it for Krsna and clinging to it as yours, and one where you cannot yet feel that difference.
  • Visvanatha Cakravartis example compares lost assets to taxed wealth: where in your life has a forced loss actually trained your detachment more than any deliberate practice did?
  • Is it possible to be a serious provider, parent, or earner in this world without slipping into false proprietorship, and where do members of the group genuinely disagree about that line?
Role Play

A devotee has just received a large bonus and a promotion. A relative pressures them to upgrade the house, the car, and the children's schooling immediately, saying "this is what a good husband and father does." Two students play the devotee and the relative; the devotee must respond from this verse, neither renouncing responsibility nor sliding into proprietorship, while engaging the new resources in the Lord's service.

Objections & Responses
  • This sounds like emotional abandonment of one's wife and children dressed up as spirituality.
    Prabhupada is explicit that the devotee engages wife, children, home, and wealth in Krsna's loving service; what is abandoned is the false ego of being their proprietor and the frantic arranging for their sense gratification, not genuine care, duty, or affection, which actually deepen when they are no longer driven by possessiveness.
  • In the real modern world I have to fight hard for my family's security and prestige; this kind of detachment is a luxury for monks.
    The verse is spoken in the Bhagavatam to householders, not renunciants; it asks not that you stop working or providing, but that you stop locating your real self-interest in those arrangements, because, as the purport warns, ownership over material objects is never permanent and only loving connection to Krsna and His devotee gives steady shelter.
