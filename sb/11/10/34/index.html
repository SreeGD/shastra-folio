Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.10.34

When there is agitation and interaction of the material modes of nature, the living entities then describe Me in various ways such as all-powerful time, the Self, Vedic knowledge, the universe, one’s own nature, religious ceremonies and so on.
Purport

One can experience the potency of the Personality of Godhead by observing how different species of life — demigods, human beings, animals, fish, birds, insects, plants, etc. — gradually evolve their natures and activities. Each species of life executes a particular process of sense gratification, and this function is called the dharma of the species. Lacking knowledge of the Personality of Godhead, ordinary persons catch a glimpse of the Lord’s potencies in the above-mentioned manifestations. Śrīla Madhvācārya has cited the following information from the Tantra-bhāgavata. The Lord is called kāla, or time, because He is the mover and controller of all material qualities. Because He is complete and perfect, He is called ātmā, or the Self; and He is the personification of all knowledge. The word svabhāva indicates that the Lord fully controls His own destiny; and as the maintainer of everyone He is called dharma. One on the liberated platform can achieve unlimited bliss by worshiping the Personality of Godhead, whereas those who are ignorant of the Lord try to find happiness by concocting other objects of worship. If one stubbornly imagines that anything is independent of the Lord, one will remain in the grip of the illusory network of the Lord’s potency. Seeing the inevitability of the destruction of material things, one is constantly fearful and perpetually laments in the darkness of ignorance. In such darkness there is no question of happiness. Therefore, one should never think that anything is independent of the Personality of Godhead. As soon as one considers anything to be independent of the Lord, one is immediately gripped by the Lord’s illusory network, called māyā. One should always remain humble and obedient to the Personality of Godhead, even when one is liberated, and thus one will achieve the supreme spiritual happiness.

— A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda
Gauḍīya Ācāryas
Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura Gauḍīya
Translation
When there is agitation and interaction of the material modes of nature, I take on various forms of māyā: as the time for enjoyment, the body suitable for enjoyment, as the scriptures proclaiming karma, as the places giving enjoyment, as causes of transformation, and as rules for creating enjoyment. Some accept Svarga, the time of enjoyment, the scriptures proclaiming karma, and the body attained by karma to be eternal and supreme. Svabhāva refers to the cause of transformation into devatās and other forms. Dharma, the code of rules, is the cause of enjoyment. When māyā is agitated, I take on many forms, which are products of my māyā-śakti. Therefore the conclusion is that the jīva should strive for freedom from the bondage of karma.
Personal Guidance
When the guṇas churn, people name the Lord as time, self, scripture, world, nature, or dharma — fragments of Kṛṣṇa mistaken for the whole.
Practical Applications
  • Notice today when you credit "time," "nature," or "the universe" for an event, and silently re-attribute it to Kṛṣṇa as its source.
  • Refuse to treat any object, system, or law as independent of Kṛṣṇa — when fear arises around health, money, or work, name it as forgetfulness of His proprietorship.
  • Speak to Kṛṣṇa personally in your evening sit-down, addressing Him by name rather than as "the Self" or "consciousness."
Reflection Questions
  • Which of these substitutes — time, nature, dharma, knowledge, the cosmos — do I most often worship in place of Kṛṣṇa, and what does that reveal about what I am unwilling to surrender?
  • Where in my life am I currently lamenting because I have imagined some thing or person to exist independently of the Lord?
For Each Age Group
youth
  • Catch yourself saying "the universe wanted this" about an exam result, a crush, or an internship, and re-phrase it inwardly as "Kṛṣṇa arranged this."
  • When friends speak of "vibes," "energy," or "manifesting," don't argue — but privately refuse to let those words replace Kṛṣṇa's name in your own thinking.
  • Before a stressful test or interview, instead of trusting your "own nature" or "timing," offer the attempt to Kṛṣṇa as its real controller and do your work without panic.
householder
  • When a family decision rides on market timing, kids' temperaments, or career luck, name those forces as Kṛṣṇa's potencies and consult Him in prayer before strategizing.
  • Refuse to treat your dharma as a householder — earning, raising children, maintaining elders — as a self-standing duty; perform it explicitly as service to Kṛṣṇa, the real dharma.
  • Notice when you lament a financial or family loss as if it were caused by impersonal time or bad luck, and bring that lament directly to Kṛṣṇa as its actual cause and remedy.
senior
  • As aging makes "time" feel like the dominant force in your life, deliberately re-personalize it — address Kṛṣṇa as the kāla who is bringing you home, not an impersonal clock.
  • When mentoring younger family or students, point past the language of "nature," "karma," and "the Self" to Kṛṣṇa as the Person behind them, without lecturing.
  • Loosen attachments by daily acknowledging that none of what you built — house, savings, reputation, even your own body — was ever independent of Kṛṣṇa, and offer each one back to Him in thought.
Common Pitfalls
  • Treating the verse as a list of valid alternative names for God, instead of as a diagnosis of guṇa-induced misperception.
  • Sliding into impersonalism by deciding "all paths name the same Absolute," and quietly dropping Kṛṣṇa as a Person.
  • Using "everything is Kṛṣṇa" as a slogan to bypass concrete humility and obedience to Him, which the purport says must continue even after liberation.
Discussion Prompts
  • Pick one of the six substitutes — kāla, ātmā, āgama, loka, svabhāva, dharma — and share where in your life you have actually been worshiping it instead of Kṛṣṇa.
  • Do modern frames like "the universe is guiding me" or "trust the process" qualify as the kind of partial naming this verse critiques, or are they something else? Disagree honestly.
  • The purport says even the liberated must remain humble and obedient to Kṛṣṇa — what does that say about spiritual movements that emphasize realization of the Self over personal surrender?
Role Play

A close friend, recently into yoga and self-help, tells you over coffee: "I don't need a personal God — I just trust the intelligence of nature and my own higher Self." Drawing on this verse and purport, respond in a way that honors the partial truth in their words while pointing them toward Kṛṣṇa as the Person behind kāla, ātmā, and svabhāva — without arguing or condescending.

Objections & Responses
  • If people are calling the same reality time, Self, nature, or dharma, isn't that just different cultures naming the one Absolute? Why insist on "Kṛṣṇa"?
    The verse itself frames these names as arising "when there is agitation of the guṇas" — they are partial glimpses of the Lord's potencies caught by people who lack knowledge of the Personality of Godhead. They are not equally valid labels; they are fragments mistaken for the whole. Honoring Him as a Person is not sectarian narrowness but accuracy: time, self, and nature are His features, not His substitutes.
  • Saying "nothing is independent of Kṛṣṇa" sounds like a thought-stopper that prevents serious engagement with science, law, or one's own agency.
    The purport does not deny that kāla, svabhāva, and dharma operate — it says Kṛṣṇa is their mover and controller. Studying them carefully is honoring His arrangement; imagining them as self-sufficient is what drops you into māyā's grip and into the constant fear the purport describes. Real engagement with the world deepens, rather than shrinks, when its Person is acknowledged.
