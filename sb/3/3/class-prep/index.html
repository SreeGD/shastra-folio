Chapter 3 · Class prep

BG 3.1

Śrī Uddhava said: Thereafter Lord Kṛṣṇa went to Mathurā City with Śrī Baladeva, and to please Their parents They dragged Kaṁsa, the leader of public enemies, down from his throne and killed him, pulling him along the ground with great strength.

Essence. Krsna and Balarama dragged the tyrant down so their parents could see the deed completed. When you act to relieve someone's long-standing suffering, finish the job so their fear can finally rest, not only the threat itself.

BG 3.2

The Lord learned all the Vedas with their different branches simply by hearing them once from His teacher, Sāndīpani Muni, whom He rewarded by bringing back his dead son from the region of Yamaloka.

Essence. Krsna learned Vedas in one hearing yet served His teacher as if dependent on him. Even when you can outpace your teacher, accept the discipline and offer guru-daksina. Service to the source is itself the curriculum.

BG 3.3

Attracted by the beauty and fortune of Rukmiṇī, the daughter of King Bhīṣmaka, many great princes and kings assembled to marry her. But Lord Kṛṣṇa, stepping over the other hopeful candidates, carried her away as His own share, as Garuḍa carried away nectar.

Essence. Rukmini wrote, declared her love for Krsna, and waited for Him to come. When something or someone truly belongs to Krsna, do not let convention marry them off elsewhere. Stake your claim through honest invitation, not through manipulation.

BG 3.4

By subduing seven bulls whose noses were not pierced, the Lord achieved the hand of Princess Nāgnijitī in the open competition to select her bridegroom. Although the Lord was victorious, His competitors asked the hand of the princess, and thus there was a fight. Well equipped with weapons, the Lord killed or wounded all of them, but He Himself was not hurt.

Essence. Subduing wild bulls and surviving the rivals' attack is what it took to marry Nagnijiti. Sometimes the right relationship or service is hidden behind multiple tests in series. Do not give up at the first bull; the second princess is on the other side.

BG 3.5

Just to please His dear wife, the Lord brought back the pārijāta tree from heaven, just as an ordinary husband would do. But Indra, the King of heaven, induced by his wives (henpecked as he was), ran after the Lord with full force to fight Him.

Essence. Even the Lord brings a small gift to please His wife. Greatness does not exempt anyone from ordinary tenderness toward their spouse. Doing small thoughtful things for the one you love is not beneath the spiritual life; it is part of it.

BG 3.6

Narakāsura, the son of Dharitrī, the earth, tried to grasp the whole sky, and for this he was killed by the Lord in a fight. His mother then prayed to the Lord. This led to the return of the kingdom to the son of Narakāsura, and thus the Lord entered the house of the demon.

Essence. Even a child born of good parents becomes a demon through bad association. Birth-status is no shield; daily company is destiny. Audit who you spend most hours with and what their influence is doing to you.

BG 3.7

There in the house of the demon, all the princesses kidnapped by Narakāsura at once became alert upon seeing the Lord, the friend of the distressed. They looked upon Him with eagerness, joy and shyness and offered to be His wives.

Essence. The Lord is the only friend of those whose options have run out. When you are 'too damaged' for ordinary acceptance, the door to Krsna is precisely open. He receives the rejected without making them earn it.

BG 3.8

All those princesses were lodged in different apartments, and the Lord simultaneously assumed different bodily expansions exactly matching each and every princess. He accepted their hands in perfect rituals by His internal potency.

Essence. Krsna expanded into many forms to give each princess full presence. The Lord is not stretched thin between His devotees; each receives the whole of Him. Trust that your bond with Him is not diluted by His attention to others.

BG 3.9

Just to expand Himself according to His transcendental features, the Lord begot in each and every one of them ten offspring with exactly His own qualities.

Essence. The Lord begets children with His own qualities. What you devote yourself to determines the qualities of what your life produces. Aligning with Krsna means even your outcomes start to carry His character.

BG 3.10

Kālayavana, the King of Magadha and Sālva attacked the city of Mathurā, but when the city was encircled by their soldiers, the Lord refrained from killing them personally, just to show the power of His own men.

Essence. The Lord refrained from killing certain enemies personally so His devotees could share the glory. He prefers to act through devotees. Do not insist God do everything directly; allow yourself to be His instrument and let others be too.

BG 3.11

Of kings like Śambara, Dvivida, Bāṇa, Mura, Balvala and many other demons, such as Dantavakra, some He killed Himself, and some He caused to be killed by others [Śrī Baladeva, etc.].

Essence. Some battles the Lord fought Himself, some He delegated to Baladeva. Discern which fights are yours to wage and which to entrust to those alongside you. Not every demon is yours to slay personally.

BG 3.12

Then, O Vidura, the Lord caused all the kings, both the enemies and those on the side of your fighting nephews, to be killed in the Battle of Kurukṣetra. All those kings were so great and strong that the earth seemed to shake as they traversed the warfield.

Essence. The Lord arranged for many great but corrupted leaders to fall together on one battlefield. He waits to clean house at once. If your time looks chaotic, suspect a larger arrangement is collapsing the dross all at the same time.

BG 3.13

Duryodhana was bereft of his fortune and duration of life because of the intricacy of ill advice given by Karṇa, Duḥśāsana and Saubala. When he lay on the ground with his followers, his thighs broken although he was powerful, the Lord was not happy to see the scene.

Essence. Bad advice from close associates can cost a strong man his fortune and life. The Lord takes no pleasure in punishing wrongdoers. Choose your council carefully; their words become the policies that determine your ending.

BG 3.14

[After the end of the Battle of Kurukṣetra, the Lord said:] The abatement of the earth’s great burden, eighteen akṣauhiṇīs, has now been effected with the help of Droṇa, Bhīṣma, Arjuna and Bhīma. But what is this? There is still the great strength of the Yadu dynasty, born of Myself, which may be a more unbearable burden.

Essence. The earth is burdened not by population but by miscreants who violate the Lord's order. Your worry about external scarcity may be misplaced; the real overload is moral, not material. Address dharma, and the rest balances.

BG 3.15

When they quarrel among themselves, influenced by intoxication, with their eyes red like copper because of drinking [madhu], then only will they disappear; otherwise, it will not be possible. On My disappearance, this incident will take place.

Essence. Even the Lord's family disappeared through quarrel under intoxication. Wine and pride can dismantle even the holy. Guard against intoxicants (literal and figurative) that turn brothers against each other.

BG 3.16

Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, thus thinking to Himself, established Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira in the position of supreme control of the world in order to show the ideal of administration on the path of piety.

Essence. Krsna installed Yudhisthira to model administration on the path of piety. Where you have authority, use it to show what dharma-led leadership looks like, not just to manage outcomes. Your style is itself a teaching.

BG 3.17

The embryo of Pūru’s descendant begotten by the great hero Abhimanyu in the womb of Uttarā, his wife, was burnt by the weapon of the son of Droṇa, but later he was again protected by the Lord.

Essence. The Lord personally entered the womb to protect a single embryo destined for service. Whatever vulnerable beginning you are nurturing, He can guard it from inside. Pray for His omnipresence in the unseen places of your life.

BG 3.18

The Supreme Lord induced the son of Dharma to perform three horse sacrifices, and Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira, constantly following Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, protected and enjoyed the earth, assisted by his younger brothers.

Essence. Yudhisthira ruled by constantly following Krsna, supported by his brothers. Authority works best when it is downstream from devotion and shared with kin. Govern your sphere as Krsna's representative, not as His replacement.

BG 3.19

Simultaneously, the Personality of Godhead enjoyed life in the city of Dvārakā, strictly in conformity with the Vedic customs of society. He was situated in detachment and knowledge, as enunciated by the Sāṅkhya system of philosophy.

Essence. Krsna lived in Dvaraka following Vedic customs, situated in knowledge and detachment. Even householders can live in full samkhya-like clarity. Eat, work, and enjoy, but stay anchored in the awareness that this is not your final identity.

BG 3.20

He was there in His transcendental body, the residence of the goddess of fortune, with His usual gentle and sweetly smiling face, His nectarean words and His flawless character.

Essence. Lakshmi resides where Krsna's gentle face is. The smile, the words, the spotless character; that is where fortune naturally clusters. If wealth seems to flee you, examine whether your home invites a gentle, gracious presence.

BG 3.21

The Lord enjoyed His pastimes, both in this world and in other worlds [higher planets], specifically in the association of the Yadu dynasty. At leisure hours offered by night, He enjoyed the friendship of conjugal love with women.

Essence. The Lord visited higher worlds and enjoyed friendship and conjugal love in this one. Devotion does not require monotone existence; it can include relationship, leisure, and joy when oriented around Him.

BG 3.22

The Lord was thus engaged in household life for many, many years, but at last His detachment from ephemeral sex life was fully manifested.

Essence. Krsna lived as a householder for many years, then His detachment from sex-life was fully manifest. Detachment ripens in time; do not force it prematurely or pretend it before it is real. Stay honest about your stage.

BG 3.23

Every living entity is controlled by a supernatural force, and thus his sense enjoyment is also under the control of that supernatural force. No one, therefore, can put his faith in Lord Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental sense activities but one who has become a devotee of the Lord by rendering devotional service.

Essence. Only one engaged in devotion can rightly understand Krsna's transcendental enjoyments. Without bhakti, His pastimes get reduced to scandal. Read His life from inside service, not from spectator distance.

BG 3.24

Once upon a time, great sages were made angry by the sporting activities of the princely descendants of the Yadu and Bhoja dynasties, and thus, as desired by the Lord, the sages cursed them.

Essence. Even the Lord's own descendants were cursed for offending sages. Status near God does not protect you from offending His devotees. Guard your tongue and your sport when saints are present.

BG 3.25

A few months passed, and then, bewildered by Kṛṣṇa, all the descendants of Vṛṣṇi, Bhoja and Andhaka who were incarnations of demigods went to Prabhāsa, while those who were eternal devotees of the Lord did not leave but remained in Dvārakā.

Essence. Some went to Prabhasa; the eternal devotees stayed at Dvaraka with Him. When a community begins to scatter under the Lord's bewildering arrangement, ask which group you belong to and whether you should follow or stay.

BG 3.26

After arriving there, all of them took bath, and with the water of this place of pilgrimage they offered their respects to the forefathers, demigods and great sages and thus satisfied them. They gave cows to the brāhmaṇas in royal charity.

Essence. Visiting holy places to honor forefathers and saints is meaningful, but the highest devotee finds devotional service everywhere. If you cannot reach a tirtha, do not despair; the service of the Lord wherever you are is itself the perfection.

BG 3.27

The brāhmaṇas were not only given well-fed cows in charity, but also gold, gold coins, bedding, clothing, animal-skin seats, blankets, horses, elephants, girls and sufficient land for maintenance.

Essence. When brahmanas were generously supported, society's spiritual backbone stood strong. Identify who in your community is genuinely living a brahmana role and ensure they have what they need. Their wellbeing is yours.

BG 3.28

Thereafter they offered the brāhmaṇas highly delicious foodstuffs first offered to the Personality of Godhead and offered their respectful obeisances by touching their heads to the ground. They lived perfectly by protecting the cows and the brāhmaṇas.

Essence. Offering food first to the Lord, then to brahmanas, and protecting cows is the three-fold backbone of cultured life. Try this week to eat nothing un-offered. The plate itself becomes a sadhana.
