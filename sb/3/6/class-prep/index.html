Chapter 6 · Class prep

BG 6.1

The Ṛṣi Maitreya said: The Lord thus heard about the suspension of the progressive creative functions of the universe due to the noncombination of His potencies, such as the mahat-tattva.

Essence. The verse describes layered cosmic machinery — modes, elements, governing demigods — to remind us that even our 'private' lives are arranged by larger hands.

BG 6.2

The Supreme Powerful Lord then simultaneously entered into the twenty-three elements with the goddess Kālī, His external energy, who alone amalgamates all the different elements.

Essence. The cosmology here is not abstract scenery — it locates the reader inside a vast, intelligent order. Seeing your tiny role in this design quietly cools the urge to make yourself the centre. Its centre of gravity is material existence.

BG 6.3

Thus when the Personality of Godhead entered into the elements by His energy, all the living entities were enlivened into different activities, just as one is engaged in his work after awakening from sleep.

Essence. The verse describes a universe that is not a random stage but an arrangement with a purpose. Our work is not to engineer that purpose but to find our seat within it. The verse keeps returning to modes of nature.

BG 6.4

When the twenty-three principal elements were set in action by the will of the Supreme, the gigantic universal form, or the viśvarūpa body of the Lord, came into existence.

Essence. The Lord is named here as the actual proprietor of everything we manage. The verse asks us to relax our grip on what was never ours and let Him hold it again.

BG 6.5

As the Lord, in His plenary portion, entered into the elements of the universal creation, they transformed into the gigantic form in which all the planetary systems and all movable and immovable creations rest.

Essence. The scale of the creation in this verse is not given to overwhelm us but to right-size us. Most of our anxiety shrinks when our self-importance shrinks. The verse keeps returning to cosmic manifestation.

BG 6.6

The gigantic virāṭ-puruṣa, known as Hiraṇmaya, lived for one thousand celestial years on the water of the universe, and all the living entities lay with Him.

Essence. The cosmology here is not abstract scenery — it locates the reader inside a vast, intelligent order. Seeing your tiny role in this design quietly cools the urge to make yourself the centre. The pivot here is cosmic manifestation.

BG 6.7

The total energy of the mahat-tattva, in the form of the gigantic virāṭ-rūpa, divided Himself by Himself into the consciousness of the living entities, the life of activity, and self-identification, which are subdivided into one, ten and three respectively.

Essence. Pride is not loud confidence here but a subtle, constant background hum. The verse asks us to notice it before we can hand it over. The verse keeps returning to false ego.

BG 6.8

The gigantic universal form of the Supreme Lord is the first incarnation and plenary portion of the Supersoul. He is the Self of an unlimited number of living entities, and in Him rests the aggregate creation, which thus flourishes.

Essence. The scale of the creation in this verse is not given to overwhelm us but to right-size us. Most of our anxiety shrinks when our self-importance shrinks.

BG 6.9

The gigantic universal form is represented by three, ten and one in the sense that He is the body and the mind and the senses, He is the dynamic force for all movements by ten kinds of life energy, and He is the one heart where life energy is generated.

Essence. The verse treats sense gratification as the path of least resistance, not as evil. The work is to introduce a little resistance — a small rule, kept — and let the soul re-emerge. The verse keeps returning to false ego.

BG 6.10

The Supreme Lord is the Supersoul of all the demigods entrusted with the task of constructing the cosmic manifestation. Being thus prayed to [by the demigods], He thought to Himself and thus manifested the gigantic form for their understanding.

Essence. The cosmology here is not abstract scenery — it locates the reader inside a vast, intelligent order. Seeing your tiny role in this design quietly cools the urge to make yourself the centre. Its centre of gravity is cosmic manifestation.

BG 6.11

Maitreya said: You may now hear from me how the Supreme Lord separated Himself into the diverse forms of the demigods after the manifestation of the gigantic universal form.

Essence. Devotional service is named here as the central fact of spiritual life, not a hobby alongside it. The verse asks us to make the Lord the actual recipient of our daily activity rather than a polite afterthought. The pivot here is devotional service to the Lord.

BG 6.12

Agni, or heat, separated from His mouth, and all the directors of material affairs entered into it in their respective positions. By that energy the living entity expresses himself in words.

Essence. The Lord is named here as the actual proprietor of everything we manage. The verse asks us to relax our grip on what was never ours and let Him hold it again.

BG 6.13

When the palate of the gigantic form was separately manifested, Varuṇa, the director of water in the planetary systems, entered therein, and thus the living entity has the facility to taste everything with his tongue.

Essence. The very ability to taste is on loan from Varuṇa, who entered the Lord's palate so living beings could enjoy flavor. The tongue is not ours alone — it is a sense maintained by a higher director. This invites reverence even for simple pleasures of food.

BG 6.14

When the Lord’s two nostrils separately manifested themselves, the dual Aśvinī-kumāras entered them in their proper positions, and because of this the living entities can smell the aromas of everything.

Essence. Smell is possible because the Aśvinī-kumāras entered the Lord's nostrils. Every scent we register is the work of higher beings empowering our nose. This dismantles the idea that the body is self-sufficient hardware.

BG 6.15

Thereafter, the two eyes of the gigantic form of the Lord were separately manifested. The sun, the director of light, entered them with the partial representation of eyesight, and thus the living entities can have vision of forms.

Essence. Sight depends on the sun's representative entering the Lord's eyes. The vision we take for granted is a partial extension of solar light through divine arrangement. Without this support, the eye would be a useless lump.

BG 6.16

When there was a manifestation of skin separated from the gigantic form, Anila, the deity directing the wind, entered with partial touch, and thus the living entities can realize tactile knowledge.

Essence. Touch — knowing the world through skin — is the gift of Anila, the wind deity, entering the universal form's skin. Tactile knowledge is not a mechanical reflex but a sustained empowerment from above.

BG 6.17

When the ears of the gigantic form became manifested, all the controlling deities of the directions entered into them with the hearing principles, by which all the living entities hear and take advantage of sound.

Essence. Talks about the Lord, repeated with attention, are described here as transformative in their own right. The verse implies that what we listen to all day is quietly shaping who we become.

BG 6.18

When there was a separate manifestation of skin, the controlling deities of sensations and their different parts entered into it, and thus the living entities feel itching and happiness due to touch.

Essence. Mind and senses, left ungoverned, will quietly run a life into the ground. The verse implies that small daily regulation is more powerful than occasional dramatic resolve.

BG 6.19

When the genitals of the gigantic form separately became manifest, then Prajāpati, the original living creature, entered into them with his partial semen, and thus the living entities can enjoy sex pleasure.

Essence. Sex pleasure exists because Prajāpati entered the Lord's genitals with a partial portion of his potency. Even the most private function of the body is sustained by an external director. This places conjugal life within a sacred frame, not a private domain.

BG 6.20

The evacuating channel separately became manifest, and the director named Mitra entered into it with partial organs of evacuation. Thus the living entities are able to pass stool and urine.

Essence. Even the body's most humble functions — passing stool and urine — are governed by Mitra, who entered the evacuating channel. Nothing about embodiment is too low to be administered by a demigod. Every function is a managed grace.

BG 6.21

Thereafter, when the hands of the gigantic form separately became manifested, Indra, the ruler of the heavenly planets, entered into them, and thus the living entity is able to transact business for his livelihood.

Essence. The hands work because Indra entered the universal form's hands. The capacity to earn livelihood and transact business is not raw human cleverness but a demigod-sustained faculty. Hard work is real, but the power behind it is borrowed.

BG 6.22

Thereafter the legs of the gigantic form separately became manifest, and the demigod named Viṣṇu [not the Personality of Godhead] entered with partial movement. This helps the living entity move to his destination.

Essence. Movement — walking, traveling, reaching a destination — comes from the demigod Viṣṇu (not the Supreme) entering the Lord's legs. The simple act of going somewhere is sustained by an unseen mover. We are carried even as we step.

BG 6.23

When the intelligence of the gigantic form separately became manifest, Brahmā, the lord of the Vedas, entered into it with the partial power of understanding, and thus an object of understanding is experienced by the living entities.

Essence. Brahmā entered the universal intelligence so that understanding could become possible. Our power to grasp meaning is partially Brahmā's power working in us. Intellect is therefore something to use carefully, not boast about.

BG 6.24

After that, the heart of the gigantic form separately manifested itself, and into it entered the moon demigod with partial mental activity. Thus the living entity can conduct his mental speculations.

Essence. The mind's churning — speculation, planning, mental travel — is sustained by the moon demigod entering the universal heart. What feels like our private thinking is partly a lunar function. The mind is a maintained instrument, not a free self.

BG 6.25

Thereafter the materialistic ego of the gigantic form separately manifested itself, and into it entered Rudra, the controller of false ego, with his own partial activities, by which the living entity transacts his objective actions.

Essence. The verse exposes the fiction of self-made independence. Behind every result we credit to ourselves stands a long chain of help we never planned. Its centre of gravity is false ego.

BG 6.26

Thereafter, when His consciousness separately manifested itself, the total energy, mahat-tattva, entered with His conscious part. Thus the living entity is able to conceive specific knowledge.

Essence. The capacity for specific knowledge arises when the total energy, mahat-tattva, enters the universal consciousness. Our ability to recognize and categorize is downstream of a cosmic process. Knowing anything at all is itself a gift of the structure of reality.

BG 6.27

Thereafter, from the head of the gigantic form, the heavenly planets were manifested, and from His legs the earthly planets and from His abdomen the sky separately manifested themselves. Within them the demigods and others also were manifested in terms of the modes of material nature.

Essence. The cosmos itself is the universal form's body — heavens from the head, earth from the legs, sky from the abdomen — populated by demigods and beings according to the modes of nature. The whole universe is one organism arranged from one Person.

BG 6.28

The demigods, qualified by the superexcellent quality of the mode of goodness, are situated in the heavenly planets, whereas the human beings, because of their nature in the mode of passion, live on the earth in company with their subordinates.

Essence. The verse describes a universe that is not a random stage but an arrangement with a purpose. Our work is not to engineer that purpose but to find our seat within it.

BG 6.29

Living entities who are associates of Rudra develop in the third mode of material nature, or ignorance. They are situated in the sky between the earthly planets and the heavenly planets.

Essence. The verse describes a universe that is not a random stage but an arrangement with a purpose. Our work is not to engineer that purpose but to find our seat within it. Its centre of gravity is modes of material nature.

BG 6.30

O chief of the Kuru dynasty, the Vedic wisdom became manifested from the mouth of the virāṭ, the gigantic form. Those who are inclined to this Vedic knowledge are called brāhmaṇas, and they are the natural teachers and spiritual masters of all the orders of society.

Essence. Inquiry here is not casual curiosity — it is a serious approach made by someone ready to be changed. The verse asks the reader to examine the quality of their own inquiries. The pivot here is Supreme Personality of Godhead.

BG 6.31

Thereafter the power of protection was generated from the arms of the gigantic virāṭ form, and in relation to such power the kṣatriyas also came into existence by following the kṣatriya principle of protecting society from the disturbance of thieves and miscreants.

Essence. Real knowledge in this verse is relational, not abstract — it grows by humility before a teacher and by service, not by argument. Its centre of gravity is transcendental knowledge.

BG 6.32

The means of livelihood of all persons, namely production of grains and their distribution to the prajās, was generated from the thighs of the Lord’s gigantic form. The mercantile men who take charge of such execution are called vaiśyas.

Essence. The verse describes varṇāśrama not as a cage but as a scaffolding. Each station is shaped so the soul can perform service without being crushed by it.

BG 6.33

Thereafter, service was manifested from the legs of the Personality of Godhead for the sake of perfecting the religious function. Situated on the legs are the śūdras, who satisfy the Lord by service.

Essence. The verse describes varṇāśrama not as a cage but as a scaffolding. Each station is shaped so the soul can perform service without being crushed by it. The verse keeps returning to material energy.

BG 6.34

All these different social divisions are born, with their occupational duties and living conditions, from the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Thus for unconditional life and self-realization one has to worship the Supreme Lord under the direction of the spiritual master.

Essence. The verse describes a universe that is not a random stage but an arrangement with a purpose. Our work is not to engineer that purpose but to find our seat within it. The verse anchors this in Supreme Personality of Godhead.

BG 6.35

O Vidura, who can estimate or measure the transcendental time, work and potency of the gigantic form manifested by the internal potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead?

Essence. The Lord is named here as the actual proprietor of everything we manage. The verse asks us to relax our grip on what was never ours and let Him hold it again. Its centre of gravity is Supreme Personality of Godhead.

BG 6.36

In spite of my inability, whatever I have been able to hear [from the spiritual master] and whatever I could assimilate I am now describing in glorification of the Lord by pure speech, for otherwise my power of speaking would remain unchaste.

Essence. Hearing about the Lord is treated here as daily medicine, not entertainment. Slowly it rewires what the heart finds attractive, until devotion stops feeling like effort. Its centre of gravity is spiritual master.

BG 6.37

The highest perfectional gain of humanity is to engage in discussions of the activities and glories of the Pious Actor. Such activities are so nicely arranged in writing by the greatly learned sages that the actual purpose of the ear is served just by being near them.

Essence. Chanting and hearing, in this verse, are not religious rituals but the actual means of changing what we love. The mouth and ear do what willpower alone cannot.

BG 6.38

O my son, the original poet, Brahmā, after mature meditation for one thousand celestial years, could know only that the glories of the Supreme Soul are inconceivable.

Essence. The verse defines life's success by surrender, not by accumulation. What looks like 'giving up' from outside is, from inside, finally arriving at one's natural position.

BG 6.39

The wonderful potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead is bewildering even to the jugglers. That potency is unknown even to the self-sufficient Lord, so it is certainly unknown to others.

Essence. The verse insists on the Lord's specific identity — not an idea of God but a person with name, form, and intent. Vague spirituality cannot reach what only personal address can. The pivot here is Supreme Personality of Godhead.

BG 6.40

Words, mind and ego, with their respective controlling demigods, have failed to achieve success in knowing the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore, we simply have to offer our respectful obeisances unto Him as a matter of sanity.

Essence. The scale of the creation in this verse is not given to overwhelm us but to right-size us. Most of our anxiety shrinks when our self-importance shrinks. The verse keeps returning to Supreme Personality of Godhead.
