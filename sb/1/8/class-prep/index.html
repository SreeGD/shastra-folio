Chapter 8 · Class prep

BG 8.1

Sūta Gosvāmī said: Thereafter the Pāṇḍavas, desiring to deliver water to the dead relatives who had desired it, went to the Ganges with Draupadī. The ladies walked in front.

Essence. The Pāṇḍavas, with Draupadī, went to the Ganges to offer water to their dead. Honoring the dead is a duty even of the victorious. Grief and gratitude flow together to the river.

BG 8.2

Having lamented over them and sufficiently offered Ganges water, they bathed in the Ganges, whose water is sanctified due to being mixed with the dust of the lotus feet of the Lord.

Essence. Lamenting and offering Ganges water, they bathed in waters sanctified by the dust of the Lord's lotus feet. Sacred water externalizes inner cleansing. Ritual gives the body a way to participate in the soul's release.

BG 8.3

There sat the King of the Kurus, Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira, along with his younger brothers and Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Gāndhārī, Kuntī and Draupadī, all overwhelmed with grief. Lord Kṛṣṇa was also there.

Essence. The whole family sat together overwhelmed with grief, and Kṛṣṇa was there too. The Lord doesn't avoid the room where grief is being felt. He sits with us.

BG 8.4

Citing the stringent laws of the Almighty and their reactions upon living beings, Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa and the munis began to pacify those who were shocked and affected.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa and the munis pacified the shocked by citing the stringent laws of the Almighty. Sometimes the most comforting thing is to be reminded of a larger order. Truth, not just sentiment, can console.

BG 8.5

The clever Duryodhana and his party cunningly usurped the kingdom of Yudhiṣṭhira, who had no enemy. By the grace of the Lord, the recovery was executed, and the unscrupulous kings who joined with Duryodhana were killed by Him. Others also died, their duration of life having decreased for their rough handling of the hair of Queen Draupadī.

Essence. Duryodhana cunningly usurped the kingdom; by the Lord's grace it was recovered, and the unscrupulous were killed. Justice may take long, and may look like ordinary politics, but the Lord's hand is at work.

BG 8.6

Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa caused three well-performed aśvamedha-yajṣas [horse sacrifices] to be conducted by Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira and thus caused his virtuous fame to be glorified in all directions, like that of Indra, who had performed one hundred such sacrifices.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa caused Yudhiṣṭhira's three horse sacrifices to be conducted, glorifying his fame. The Lord arranges opportunities for His devotees' glory. Our work is sometimes to receive what He sets up.

BG 8.7

Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa then prepared for His departure. He invited the sons of Pāṇḍu, after having been worshiped by the brāhmaṇas, headed by Śrīla Vyāsadeva. The Lord also reciprocated greetings.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa prepared to depart, was worshiped by the brāhmaṇas, and reciprocated greetings. Even cosmic departures are bordered by simple courtesies. Holiness honors etiquette.

BG 8.8

As soon as He seated Himself on the chariot to start for Dvārakā, He saw Uttarā hurrying toward Him in fear.

Essence. As Kṛṣṇa mounted His chariot, Uttarā ran toward Him in fear. Real crisis collapses ceremony. We are allowed to interrupt the Lord with urgent need.

BG 8.9

Uttarā said: O Lord of lords, Lord of the universe! You are the greatest of mystics. Please protect me, protect me, for there is no one else who can save me from the clutches of death in this world of duality.

Essence. Uttarā cried: protect me from death's clutches; no one else can. Real refuge is found only when we admit no one else can save us. Other rescuers are partial; Kṛṣṇa is total.

BG 8.10

O my Lord, You are all-powerful. A fiery iron arrow is coming towards me fast. My Lord, let it burn me personally, if You so desire, but please do not let it burn and abort my embryo. Please do me this favor, my Lord.

Essence. She begged: burn me personally if You wish, but spare the embryo. The mother's instinct is to take the burn herself. Love is willingness to absorb cost to protect another.

BG 8.11

Sūta Gosvāmī said: Having patiently heard her words, Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who is always very affectionate to His devotees, could at once understand that Aśvatthāmā, the son of Droṇācārya, had thrown the brahmāstra to finish the last life in the Pāṇḍava family.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa heard her words and understood Aśvatthāmā's brahmāstra was finishing the last Pāṇḍava line. The Lord, affectionate, sees the deeper threat behind the immediate prayer. He hears what we cannot fully say.

BG 8.12

O foremost among the great thinkers [munis] [Śaunaka], seeing the glaring brahmāstra proceeding towards them, the Pāṇḍavas took up their five respective weapons.

Essence. Seeing the brahmāstra approaching, the Pāṇḍavas took up their five weapons. Even surrendered souls do their part. Faith doesn't replace responsibility; it accompanies it.

BG 8.13

The almighty Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, having observed that a great danger was befalling His unalloyed devotees, who were fully surrendered souls, at once took up His Sudarśana disc to protect them.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, seeing danger to His unalloyed devotees, took up His Sudarśana disc to protect them. The Lord's protective response is proportional to our exclusive surrender. He shows up most fully where He is most needed.

BG 8.14

The Lord of supreme mysticism, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, resides within everyone’s heart as the Paramātmā. As such, just to protect the progeny of the Kuru dynasty, He covered the embryo of Uttarā by His personal energy.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa as Paramātmā within everyone's heart covered Uttarā's embryo with His personal energy. The same Lord who pervades all also acts intimately within one small womb. Cosmic and intimate are the same Person.

BG 8.15

O Śaunaka, glory of Bhṛgu’s family, although the supreme brahmāstra weapon released by Aśvatthāmā was irresistible and without check or counteraction, it was neutralized and foiled when confronted by the strength of Viṣṇu [Lord Kṛṣṇa].

Essence. Though the brahmāstra was supposedly irresistible, it was neutralized by Viṣṇu's strength. What is 'irresistible' to material forces is small before the Lord's will. Categories of inevitability shift around Him.

BG 8.16

O brāhmaṇas, do not think this to be especially wonderful in the activities of the mysterious and infallible Personality of Godhead. By His own transcendental energy He creates, maintains and annihilates all material things, although He Himself is unborn.

Essence. Don't think this especially wonderful: by His own transcendental energy He creates, maintains, and annihilates all things while remaining unaffected. Miracles are not exceptions for the Lord; they are His ordinary scale.

BG 8.17

Thus saved from the radiation of the brahmāstra, Kuntī, the chaste devotee of the Lord, and her five sons and Draupadī addressed Lord Kṛṣṇa as He started for home.

Essence. Saved from the radiation, Kuntī and the others addressed Kṛṣṇa as He departed. Gratitude is best expressed before the helper leaves. We should speak our thanks while the moment is fresh.

BG 8.18

Śrīmatī Kuntī said: O Kṛṣṇa, I offer my obeisances unto You because You are the original personality and are unaffected by the qualities of the material world. You are existing both within and without everything, yet You are invisible to all.

Essence. Kuntī begins: I bow to You as the original Person, unaffected by material qualities, existing within and without yet invisible. The Lord is hiddenness itself; sight comes only when He chooses to reveal.

BG 8.19

Being beyond the range of limited sense perception, You are the eternally irreproachable factor covered by the curtain of deluding energy. You are invisible to the foolish observer, exactly as an actor dressed as a player is not recognized.

Essence. He is beyond limited senses, hidden by deluding energy as an actor in costume. Fools don't recognize Him because the disguise is excellent. We must look past the costume of appearance.

BG 8.20

You Yourself descend to propagate the transcendental science of devotional service unto the hearts of the advanced transcendentalists and mental speculators, who are purified by being able to discriminate between matter and spirit. How then can we women know You perfectly?

Essence. He descends to propagate devotional service in the hearts of advanced transcendentalists. He is reachable by the purified — and the question implicit is: how can the rest of us recognize Him too?

BG 8.21

Let me therefore offer my respectful obeisances unto the Lord, who has become the son of Vasudeva, the pleasure of Devakī, the boy of Nanda and the other cowherd men of Vṛndāvana, and the enlivener of the cows and the senses.

Essence. Obeisances to the Lord who became son of Vasudeva, joy of Devakī, boy of Nanda, enlivener of cows and senses in Vṛndāvana. Cosmic divinity made itself a beloved boy. Intimacy is His chosen mode.

BG 8.22

My respectful obeisances are unto You, O Lord, whose abdomen is marked with a depression like a lotus flower, who are always decorated with garlands of lotus flowers, whose glance is as cool as the lotus and whose feet are engraved with lotuses.

Essence. Obeisances to the Lord whose lotus-marked feet, lotus-shaped abdomen, lotus garland and lotus glance crush devotees' miseries. Beauty itself is medicine. Contemplation of His form heals.

BG 8.23

O Hṛṣīkeśa, master of the senses and Lord of lords, You have released Your mother, Devakī, who was long imprisoned and distressed by the envious King Kaṁsa, and me and my children from a series of constant dangers.

Essence. Hṛṣīkeśa freed Devakī from long imprisonment, and Kuntī and her children from constant dangers. The Lord's track record with our family is the basis for confidence in His present care.

BG 8.24

My dear Kṛṣṇa, Your Lordship has protected us from a poisoned cake, from a great fire, from cannibals, from the vicious assembly, from sufferings during our exile in the forest and from the battle where great generals fought. And now You have saved us from the weapon of Aśvatthāmā.

Essence. Kuntī recounts protection from poisoned cake, fire, cannibals, vicious assembly, exile sufferings, and battlefield generals — and now from the brahmāstra. The list of God's saves grows long for those who keep count.

BG 8.25

I wish that all those calamities would happen again and again so that we could see You again and again, for seeing You means that we will no longer see repeated births and deaths.

Essence. I wish those calamities would happen again so we could see You again — for seeing You ends birth and death. Trouble that drives us toward the Lord is more valuable than ease that lets us forget Him.

BG 8.26

My Lord, Your Lordship can easily be approached, but only by those who are materially exhausted. One who is on the path of [material] progress, trying to improve himself with respectable parentage, great opulence, high education and bodily beauty, cannot approach You with sincere feeling.

Essence. You are easily approached only by the materially exhausted, not by those puffed with parentage, opulence, education and beauty. Spiritual receptivity often requires the collapse of worldly props.

BG 8.27

My obeisances are unto You, who are the property of the materially impoverished. You have nothing to do with the actions and reactions of the material modes of nature. You are self-satisfied, and therefore You are the most gentle and are master of the monists.

Essence. You are the property of the materially impoverished. The poor inherit Him; the rich rarely make the time. Aspirational privilege is a spiritual hazard.

BG 8.28

My Lord, I consider Your Lordship to be eternal time, the supreme controller, without beginning and end, the all-pervasive one. In distributing Your mercy, You are equal to everyone. The dissensions between living beings are due to social intercourse.

Essence. You are eternal time, the supreme controller without beginning or end, equal to all. Inequalities are social inventions; from the Lord's vantage, all souls are equal.

BG 8.29

O Lord, no one can understand Your transcendental pastimes, which appear to be human and so are misleading. You have no specific object of favor, nor do You have any object of envy. People only imagine that You are partial.

Essence. No one understands Your pastimes, which appear human and so mislead. People imagine You are partial when You are not. The Lord's apparent partiality is our projection.

BG 8.30

Of course it is bewildering, O soul of the universe, that You work, though You are inactive, and that You take birth, though You are the vital force and the unborn. You Yourself descend amongst animals, men, sages and aquatics. Verily, this is bewildering.

Essence. It is bewildering that You work though inactive, that You take birth though unborn. You descend among animals, men, sages, aquatics. Logical categories break against the Lord's actuality.

BG 8.31

My dear Kṛṣṇa, Yaśodā took up a rope to bind You when You committed an offense, and Your perturbed eyes overflooded with tears, which washed the mascara from Your eyes. And You were afraid, though fear personified is afraid of You. This sight is bewildering to me.

Essence. Yaśodā bound You with a rope, and Your eyes flooded with tears — You feared though fear personified fears You. Real love is more powerful than divine power; the Lord lets Himself be bound by it.

BG 8.32

Some say that the Unborn is born for the glorification of pious kings, and others say that He is born to please King Yadu, one of Your dearest devotees. You appear in his family as sandalwood appears in the Malaya hills.

Essence. Some say You are born for the glory of pious kings, others to please the Yadu dynasty. You appear in Yadu's family as sandalwood in Malaya hills. The Lord's reasons for appearing are many and overlapping.

BG 8.33

Others say that since both Vasudeva and Devakī prayed for You, You have taken Your birth as their son. Undoubtedly You are unborn, yet You take Your birth for their welfare and to kill those who are envious of the demigods.

Essence. Others say You took birth because Vasudeva and Devakī prayed. The Lord, unborn, is born for His devotees' welfare and to remove the envious. Prayer brings God to earth.

BG 8.34

Others say that the world, being overburdened like a boat at sea, is much aggrieved, and that Brahmā, who is Your son, prayed for You, and so You have appeared to diminish the trouble.

Essence. Others say the earth, overburdened, distressed; Brahmā prayed; You appeared to diminish trouble. The whole cosmos prays, and the Lord responds. Our small prayers are part of a larger chorus.

BG 8.35

And yet others say that You appeared for the sake of rejuvenating the devotional service of hearing, remembering, worshiping and so on in order that the conditioned souls suffering from material pangs might take advantage and gain liberation.

Essence. Still others say You appeared to rejuvenate devotional service of hearing, remembering, worshiping — so conditioned souls can take advantage. The Lord makes Himself accessible through ear and memory.

BG 8.36

O Kṛṣṇa, those who continuously hear, chant and repeat Your transcendental activities, or take pleasure in others’ doing so, certainly see Your lotus feet, which alone can stop the repetition of birth and death.

Essence. Those who continuously hear, chant and repeat Your transcendental activities certainly see Your lotus feet, which alone stop the repetition of birth and death. The path is simple and durable.

BG 8.37

O my Lord, You have executed all duties Yourself. Are you leaving us today, though we are completely dependent on Your mercy and have no one else to protect us, now when all kings are at enmity with us?

Essence. Kuntī asks: are You leaving us today, who are completely dependent on Your mercy? The devotee can speak in pure protest, openly grieving the Lord's withdrawal. Honest complaint is a form of love.

BG 8.38

As the name and fame of a particular body is finished with the disappearance of the living spirit, similarly if You do not look upon us, all our fame and activities, along with the Pāṇḍavas and Yadus, will end at once.

Essence. As fame ends with the soul's exit, so our fame and activities end if You don't look upon us. Without the Lord's gaze, even the Pāṇḍavas and Yadus collapse. Borrowed lustre is no lustre at all.

BG 8.39

O Gadādhara [Kṛṣṇa], our kingdom is now being marked by the impressions of Your feet, and therefore it appears beautiful. But when You leave, it will no longer be so.

Essence. Our kingdom is beautiful because it is marked by Your feet; when You leave, it will no longer be so. The land itself carries memory of presence. Beauty is an after-effect of God walking through.

BG 8.40

All these cities and villages are flourishing in all respects because the herbs and grains are in abundance, the trees are full of fruits, the rivers are flowing, the hills are full of minerals and the oceans full of wealth. And this is all due to Your glancing over them.

Essence. Cities flourish, herbs abundant, trees fruitful, rivers flowing, hills mineral-rich, oceans full — all because You look on us. Ecology and economy alike depend on divine glance.

BG 8.41

O Lord of the universe, soul of the universe, O personality of the form of the universe, please, therefore, sever my tie of affection for my kinsmen, the Pāṇḍavas and the Vṛṣṇis.

Essence. Sever my tie of affection for my kinsmen, the Pāṇḍavas and Vṛṣṇis. Even noble attachment can be a bond. The devotee asks for cutting, not preserving, those threads that compete with the Lord.

BG 8.42

O Lord of Madhu, as the Ganges forever flows to the sea without hindrance, let my attraction be constantly drawn unto You without being diverted to anyone else.

Essence. As the Ganges flows to the sea without hindrance, let my attraction flow to You without diversion. The river is one image of single-pointed devotion. Steady current, not violent leap.

BG 8.43

O Kṛṣṇa, O friend of Arjuna, O chief amongst the descendants of Vṛṣṇi, You are the destroyer of those political parties which are disturbing elements on this earth. Your prowess never deteriorates. You are the proprietor of the transcendental abode, and You descend to relieve the distresses of the cows, the brāhmaṇas and the devotees. You possess all mystic powers, and You are the preceptor of the entire universe. You are the almighty God, and I offer You my respectful obeisances.

Essence. Kṛṣṇa, destroyer of those political parties disturbing earth, proprietor of the transcendental abode — Your prowess never deteriorates. He is the one stable power in shifting times.

BG 8.44

Sūta Gosvāmī said: The Lord, thus hearing the prayers of Kuntīdevī, composed in choice words for His glorification, mildly smiled. That smile was as enchanting as His mystic power.

Essence. The Lord, hearing Kuntī's prayers, mildly smiled — that smile enchanting as His mystic power. A smile, freely given, can carry more divine reality than any miracle.

BG 8.45

Thus accepting the prayers of Śrīmatī Kuntīdevī, the Lord subsequently informed other ladies of His departure by entering the palace of Hastināpura. But upon preparing to leave, He was stopped by King Yudhiṣṭhira, who implored Him lovingly.

Essence. Accepting her prayers, He informed the other ladies of His departure but was stopped by Yudhiṣṭhira's loving plea. Sometimes love's request slows even God. Asking the Lord to stay is allowed.

BG 8.46

King Yudhiṣṭhira, who was much aggrieved, could not be convinced, despite instructions by great sages headed by Vyāsa and the Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself, the performer of superhuman feats, and despite all historical evidence.

Essence. Yudhiṣṭhira could not be consoled even by Vyāsa and Kṛṣṇa and historical evidence. Some griefs resist even sage counsel. Sitting with the inconsolable is its own form of holy work.

BG 8.47

King Yudhiṣṭhira, son of Dharma, overwhelmed by the death of his friends, was aggrieved just like a common, materialistic man. O sages, thus deluded by affection, he began to speak.

Essence. King Yudhiṣṭhira, deluded by affection, began to speak like a common materialistic man. Even great souls sometimes process loss like ordinary people. There is no shame in it; only in not eventually rising.

BG 8.48

King Yudhiṣṭhira said: O my lot! I am the most sinful man! Just see my heart, which is full of ignorance! This body, which is ultimately meant for others, has killed many, many phalanxes of men.

Essence. Yudhiṣṭhira: I am the most sinful, my heart full of ignorance, this body has killed many. Even righteous war leaves the warrior haunted. Moral injury is real.

BG 8.49

I have killed many boys, brāhmaṇas, well-wishers, friends, parents, preceptors and brothers. Though I live millions of years, I will not be relieved from the hell that awaits me for all these sins.

Essence. I have killed boys, brāhmaṇas, well-wishers, friends, parents, preceptors, brothers. Even millions of years won't relieve me from hell. The conscience of the righteous is more sensitive, not less.

BG 8.50

There is no sin for a king who kills for the right cause, who is engaged in maintaining his citizens. But this injunction is not applicable to me.

Essence. There is no sin for a king who kills for just cause and protects citizens — but this rule doesn't apply to me. He won't let himself off on a technicality. Maturity is being harder on ourselves than our role permits.

BG 8.51

I have killed many friends of women, and I have thus caused enmity to such an extent that it is not possible to undo it by material welfare work.

Essence. I have killed many friends of women, and caused enmity that material welfare work cannot undo. Some harms can't be repaired by good deeds. They require a different kind of resolution.

BG 8.52

As it is not possible to filter muddy water through mud, or purify a wine-stained pot with wine, it is not possible to counteract the killing of men by sacrificing animals.

Essence. You can't filter muddy water through mud or purify wine-stained pot with wine; killing men can't be counteracted by sacrificing animals. Symbolic atonement falls short of real reckoning.
