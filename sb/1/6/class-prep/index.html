Chapter 6 · Class prep

BG 6.1

Sūta said: O brāhmaṇas, thus hearing all about Śrī Nārada’s birth and activities, Vyāsadeva, the incarnation of God and son of Satyavatī, inquired as follows.

Essence. Hearing about a saint's birth and activities awakens deeper questions in a sincere seeker. Vyāsa, though himself an incarnation, models the humility of asking more. Inquiry itself is a sign of spiritual hunger.

BG 6.2

Śrī Vyāsadeva said: What did you [Nārada] do after the departure of the great sages who had instructed you in scientific transcendental knowledge before the beginning of your present birth?

Essence. Vyāsa, a disciple of Nārada, models sad-dharma-pṛcchā — sincere inquiry from the spiritual master. Initiation is not the end of the journey; the willingness to keep asking 'what next?' is essential to real progress.

BG 6.3

O son of Brahmā, how did you pass your life after initiation, and how did you attain this body, having quit your old one in due course?

Essence. Nārada was once an ordinary maidservant's son. His transformation into an eternal spiritual body shows that birth and circumstance do not limit spiritual destiny — sincere practice and association rewrite the story.

BG 6.4

O great sage, time annihilates everything in due course, so how is it that this subject matter, which happened prior to this day of Brahmā, is still fresh in your memory, undisturbed by time?

Essence. Spiritual consciousness developed in one body carries across lifetimes, untouched by time. What looks like 'long ago' to the material mind is fresh to the awakened spirit. Inner work compounds across births.

BG 6.5

Śrī Nārada said: The great sages, who had imparted scientific knowledge of transcendence to me, departed for other places, and I had to pass my life in this way.

Essence. After the sages departed, young Nārada was left to live with what he had received. Real teaching is tested not in the teacher's presence but in their absence. We must learn to walk the path alone.

BG 6.6

I was the only son of my mother, who was not only a simple woman but a maidservant as well. Since I was her only offspring, she had no other alternative for protection: she bound me with the tie of affection.

Essence. Nārada describes the simple, dependent circumstances of his early life — a single mother, an only son, bonds of affection. Even saints begin in small, vulnerable situations. There is no shame in humble origins.

BG 6.7

She wanted to look after my maintenance properly, but because she was not independent, she was not able to do anything for me. The world is under the full control of the Supreme Lord; therefore everyone is like a wooden doll in the hands of a puppet master.

Essence. His mother wanted to provide but could not — the world is run by the Supreme, and even loving caregivers are like wooden dolls in a puppet master's hands. Recognizing this loosens our grip on the illusion of control.

BG 6.8

When I was a mere child of five years, I lived in a brāhmaṇa school. I was dependent on my mother’s affection and had no experience of different lands.

Essence. Nārada lived a small, sheltered childhood — school, mother, no exposure to the wider world. Innocence and a narrow world are not failings; they are the soil in which simple faith first grows.

BG 6.9

Once upon a time, my poor mother, when going out one night to milk a cow, was bitten on the leg by a serpent, influenced by supreme time.

Essence. Nārada's mother died from a snakebite — an event devastating on the surface but driven by supreme time. The deepest losses can be the very means by which we are turned toward the Lord.

BG 6.10

I took this as the special mercy of the Lord, who always desires benediction for His devotees, and so thinking, I started for the north.

Essence. Instead of despairing at his mother's death, Nārada received it as the Lord's special mercy and walked north. Devotees read difficulty as direction, not as punishment. Orientation matters more than circumstance.

BG 6.11

After my departure, I passed through many flourishing metropolises, towns, villages, animal farms, mines, agricultural lands, valleys, flower gardens, nursery gardens and natural forests.

Essence. Nārada passed through cities, farms, mines, gardens, forests — the same kinds of human activity that fill our world today. Civilizations rise and fall, but the search for the eternal is what really matters in any age.

BG 6.12

I passed through hills and mountains full of reservoirs of various minerals like gold, silver and copper, and through tracts of land with reservoirs of water filled with beautiful lotus flowers, fit for the denizens of heaven, decorated with bewildered bees and singing birds.

Essence. He passed through landscapes rich with minerals, flowers, and bees — nature itself is the Lord's wealth, far surpassing any human treasury. Beauty is everywhere for those who travel light enough to notice.

BG 6.13

I then passed alone through many forests of rushes, bamboo, reeds, sharp grass, weeds and caves, which were very difficult to go through alone. I visited deep, dark and dangerously fearful forests, which were the play yards of snakes, owls and jackals.

Essence. Nārada walked alone through dangerous forests of snakes, owls, and jackals. The mendicant tradition trains fearlessness by sustained exposure to the unknown. Faith is built by walking, not just believing.

BG 6.14

Thus traveling, I felt tired, both bodily and mentally, and I was both thirsty and hungry. So I took a bath in a river lake and also drank water. By contacting water, I got relief from my exhaustion.

Essence. Tired, thirsty, hungry, Nārada bathed in a river and drank — nature itself met his needs without begging. A sincere seeker doesn't need to clutch at people; simplicity and trust are enough.

BG 6.15

After that, under the shadow of a banyan tree in an uninhabited forest I began to meditate upon the Supersoul situated within, using my intelligence, as I had learned from liberated souls.

Essence. Under a banyan tree, Nārada meditated on the Supersoul as the liberated sages had taught him. Meditation should not be invented privately — it must be received from a trustworthy source and then practiced steadily.

BG 6.16

As soon as I began to meditate upon the lotus feet of the Personality of Godhead with my mind transformed in transcendental love, tears rolled down my eyes, and without delay the Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, appeared on the lotus of my heart.

Essence. When transcendental love stirred, tears flowed and Kṛṣṇa appeared on the lotus of his heart. The Lord is reached through feeling, not technique. Sincerity of bhāva is the actual doorway.

BG 6.17

O Vyāsadeva, at that time, being exceedingly overpowered by feelings of happiness, every part of my body became separately enlivened. Being absorbed in an ocean of ecstasy, I could not see both myself and the Lord.

Essence. Ecstasy overwhelmed Nārada so completely that he could not perceive himself or the Lord — only the ocean of love. True spiritual experience dissolves the separation between subject and object. Self disappears in joy.

BG 6.18

The transcendental form of the Lord, as it is, satisfies the mind’s desire and at once erases all mental incongruities. Upon losing that form, I suddenly got up, being perturbed, as is usual when one loses that which is desirable.

Essence. The Lord's transcendental form satisfies the mind completely; losing it leaves a holy ache. Once you taste something truly satisfying, all substitutes feel hollow. Real spiritual desire is born of real spiritual contact.

BG 6.19

I desired to see again that transcendental form of the Lord, but despite my attempts to concentrate upon the heart with eagerness to view the form again, I could not see Him any more, and thus dissatisfied, I was very much aggrieved.

Essence. Nārada tried to recreate the vision and could not — and grew aggrieved. There is no mechanical method to summon God. The seeker must wait in faithful duty, not demand on schedule.

BG 6.20

Seeing my attempts in that lonely place, the Personality of Godhead, who is transcendental to all mundane description, spoke to me with gravity and pleasing words, just to mitigate my grief.

Essence. The Lord, beyond all mundane words, spoke gently to console Nārada's grief. By His mercy, the very capacity to hear Him is given. We don't earn the conversation; we are made fit for it.

BG 6.21

O Nārada [the Lord spoke], I regret that during this lifetime you will not be able to see Me anymore. Those who are incomplete in service and who are not completely free from all material taints can hardly see Me.

Essence. The Lord warned Nārada: those incomplete in service and tinged with materialism cannot easily see Him. Vision of the Lord is not a reward for cleverness but a fruit of pure-heartedness. The work is on us.

BG 6.22

O virtuous one, you have only once seen My person, and this is just to increase your desire for Me, because the more you hanker for Me, the more you will be freed from all material desires.

Essence. A single vision was given to increase Nārada's hunger. The more we long for the Lord, the more material desires fall away on their own. Hunger for the Eternal is the real cure for craving.

BG 6.23

By service of the Absolute Truth, even for a few days, a devotee attains firm and fixed intelligence in Me. Consequently he goes on to become My associate in the transcendental world after giving up the present deplorable material worlds.

Essence. Even a few days of sincere devotional service produce fixed, unwavering intelligence centered in the Lord. Stability of purpose grows surprisingly fast when the orientation is right. Quality, not quantity, of practice matters.

BG 6.24

Intelligence engaged in My devotion cannot be thwarted at any time. Even at the time of creation, as well as at the time of annihilation, your remembrance will continue by My mercy.

Essence. Intelligence engaged in devotion is never wasted; it survives creation and dissolution. Material achievements end; spiritual remembrance is carried forward by the Lord's mercy across all changes.

BG 6.25

Then that supreme authority, personified by sound and unseen by eyes, but most wonderful, stopped speaking. Feeling a sense of gratitude, I offered my obeisances unto Him, bowing my head.

Essence. The Lord spoke and was heard, not seen. Sound is no less real than sight — the Vedas, the Gītā, the holy name carry the Lord Himself. Persistent chanting is direct contact, not symbolic preparation.

BG 6.26

Thus I began chanting the holy name and fame of the Lord by repeated recitation, ignoring all the formalities of the material world. Such chanting and remembering of the transcendental pastimes of the Lord are benedictory. So doing, I traveled all over the earth, fully satisfied, humble and unenvious.

Essence. Nārada began chanting and traveling, ignoring social formalities, fully satisfied, humble and unenvious. The chanting devotee needs little outwardly because the inward fullness is real.

BG 6.27

And so, O Brāhmaṇa Vyāsadeva, in due course of time I, who was fully absorbed in thinking of Kṛṣṇa and who therefore had no attachments, being completely freed from all material taints, met with death, as lightning and illumination occur simultaneously.

Essence. Absorbed in Kṛṣṇa, free of attachment, Nārada met death like a flash of lightning — simultaneous with new illumination. For the devotee, death is not a wall but a doorway opening at the same instant as a new light.

BG 6.28

Having been awarded a transcendental body befitting an associate of the Personality of Godhead, I quit the body made of five material elements, and thus all acquired fruitive results of work [karma] stopped.

Essence. A transcendental body was awarded; karma stopped. The new body of the liberated devotee is free of material modes and fruitive reactions. The Lord's mercy ends the karmic loop, not our effort alone.

BG 6.29

At the end of the millennium, when the Personality of Godhead, Lord Nārāyaṇa, lay down within the water of devastation, Brahmā began to enter into Him along with all creative elements, and I also entered through His breathing.

Essence. At the end of the millennium, Nārada entered the Lord through Brahmā's breath. The liberated devotee returns to the source between cycles, carried not by his own effort but by the Lord's natural inhalation of all that is His.

BG 6.30

After 4,300,000,000 solar years, when Brahmā awoke to create again by the will of the Lord, all the ṛṣis like Marīci, Aṅgirā, Atri and so on were created from the transcendental body of the Lord, and I also appeared along with them.

Essence. When Brahmā woke to create again, the great ṛṣis — and Nārada — reappeared from the Lord's transcendental body. The eternal associates of the Lord recur in every cycle. Devotion gives one a permanent role in the cosmic drama.

BG 6.31

Since then, by the grace of the almighty Viṣṇu, I travel everywhere without restriction both in the transcendental world and in the three divisions of the material world. This is because I am fixed in unbroken devotional service of the Lord.

Essence. By the Lord's grace, Nārada moves freely across all worlds because he is fixed in unbroken devotion. Real freedom is not the absence of duty but unbroken connection. The inwardly anchored move unbound outwardly.

BG 6.32

And thus I travel, constantly singing the transcendental message of the glories of the Lord, vibrating this instrument called a vīṇā, which is charged with transcendental sound and which was given to me by Lord Kṛṣṇa.

Essence. Nārada travels everywhere singing the Lord's glories, vibrating a vīṇā given by Kṛṣṇa Himself. The instrument, the sound, and the singer are all transcendental. Devotional art carries the Lord directly into the world.

BG 6.33

The Supreme Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, whose glories and activities are pleasing to hear, at once appears on the seat of my heart, as if called for, as soon as I begin to chant His holy activities.

Essence. As soon as Nārada chants Kṛṣṇa's activities, the Lord appears in his heart — like switching on a spiritual television. The Lord is not separate from His name and pastimes; chanting them invites Him immediately.

BG 6.34

It is personally experienced by me that those who are always full of cares and anxieties due to desiring contact of the senses with their objects can cross the ocean of nescience on a most suitable boat — the constant chanting of the transcendental activities of the Personality of Godhead.

Essence. Those tossed by sense-craving can cross the ocean of nescience on the most suitable boat — constant chanting of the Lord's activities. The mind cannot be silenced, but it can be redirected to something that actually satisfies.

BG 6.35

It is true that by practicing restraint of the senses by the yoga system one can get relief from the disturbances of desire and lust, but this is not sufficient to give satisfaction to the soul, for this [satisfaction] is derived from devotional service to the Personality of Godhead.

Essence. Yoga restrains the senses but cannot satisfy the soul; only devotional service to the Lord does that. Self-control is necessary but not sufficient. The heart wants love, not just discipline.

BG 6.36

O Vyāsadeva, you are freed from all sins. Thus I have explained my birth and activities for self-realization, as you asked. All this will be conducive for your personal satisfaction also.

Essence. Nārada's story of his own life is offered for Vyāsa's satisfaction — and ours. Personal testimony from a realized soul is itself transformative. We learn not only doctrine but example.

BG 6.37

Sūta Gosvāmī said: Thus addressing Vyāsadeva, Śrīla Nārada Muni took leave of him, and vibrating on his vīṇā instrument, he left to wander at his free will.

Essence. Nārada takes his leave and wanders at his free will, vibrating his vīṇā. True freedom belongs to the soul rooted in devotion; conditioned souls only imagine themselves free. Anchoring in service is liberation in motion.

BG 6.38

All glory and success to Śrīla Nārada Muni because he glorifies the activities of the Personality of Godhead, and so doing he himself takes pleasure and also enlivens all the distressed souls of the universe.

Essence. Glory to Nārada — he glorifies the Lord, takes pleasure himself, and revives all distressed souls. True service multiplies joy: the singer is enlivened, and so are the listeners. Real happiness here is found only on this path.
