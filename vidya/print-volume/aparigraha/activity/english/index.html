About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Aparigraha

Aparigraha means NOT piling up more than I need.

Worksheet

Aparigraha Worksheet — Just Enough

Activity 1: Need It or Pile It? (Circle the Right Answer)

1. I have three pencils. My pencil box is full. A new pencil is on the shelf. NEED IT PILE IT

2. My shoes are too small. I am going to buy new ones. NEED IT PILE IT

3. I have twenty toys. The shop has a new toy. I already have one like it. NEED IT PILE IT

4. My shirt has a hole. I need one new shirt. NEED IT PILE IT

5. I have three teddies. I see a new teddy at the mela. It is cute. NEED IT PILE IT

Activity 2: My Tidy Shelf

Draw a small shelf. Inside, draw ONLY the things you actually use every week. Outside the shelf, draw the things you could share with someone else. Count: how many things do you truly need? How many can you pass on?

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Chant three times. On 'a-pa-ri', open your palms wide. On 'graha', close them gently — BUT only around something small. Not grabbing.

A-pa-ri-GRA-ha! A-pa-ri-GRA-ha! A-pa-ri-GRA-ha! *Just enough. Not more. My heart stays light.*

Activity 4: The Share Box (Paired or Family)

Find a medium cardboard box. Label it 'Krishna's Share Box.' Walk around your room. Put in ONE toy you no longer play with. ONE book you have outgrown. ONE piece of clothing that is too small. When the box is full, find a friend who needs these things, or take the box to a shelter.

Notice — does your room feel lighter? Does your heart feel lighter?

Activity 5: Take Home — The One-Helping Plate

For one week, at every meal, take ONE helping first. Eat slowly. If you are still hungry AFTER, ask for a little more. Notice: did you always need the big pile you used to take? Or was one helping enough?

Whisper before each meal: 'Krishna, I take just enough. Thank You.'

At the end of the week, tell Amma or Appa: 'I ate just enough every day.'

Home Practice

This week, set up your Share Box at home. Each day, walk around your room and find ONE thing you do not really use — a toy, a book, a piece of clothing that is too small, a sticker you never used. Put it gently in the Share Box. Also practice the One-Helping Plate at every meal — take just enough first, ask only if you are still hungry. At bedtime, whisper: 'Krishna, I kept just enough today. The rest is Yours to share.' At week's end, give the Share Box to someone who needs what is inside.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the Share Box has at least 5-7 items ready to donate, the child has eaten one helping first at most meals, and can name at least 2 specific things they DID NOT pile up (a toy not asked for at the shop, a second sweet not reached for).
