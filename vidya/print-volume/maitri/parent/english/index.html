About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Maitri

Maitri means being a friend to everyone. Not just to my 'best friend' or to the children who look like me. Krishna is happy when I say 'hi' to the new boy, when I include the quiet girl, when I give a smile to the child I don't know yet. Every living being is Krishna's child — so every living being is MY friend too.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 12.13: Krishna says: My dear devotee is friendly to every living being. Not to one or two — to ALL. No envy, no pride, only warmth.

What Maitri is NOT

Maitri is not only playing with my chosen group. It is not ignoring the new child or the lonely child. It is not laughing at someone who is different.

  • Playing only with 'best friends' and telling others 'you cannot join'
  • Saying 'ew, go away' to a new child
  • Ignoring the quiet child who sits alone at lunch

Stories Your Child Heard

Krishna's Forest Picnic with the Cowherd Boys — In the forest of Vrindavan, the cowherd boys were very hungry. They had been running after the calves all morning. They had called the birds. They had climbed the kadamba tree. 'I'm hungry!' said Sridama.

Rani Welcomes the New Girl — It was Tuesday morning. Teacher Priya-ji walked into class with a new girl. 'Children, this is Zara. Please make her feel welcome.' Zara was small. Her eyes were big. She wore a soft hijab on her head. She gave a tiny smile, then looked at her shoes.

The Sanskrit Word

maitrī (मैत्री) — MY-tree

Meaning: Friendly goodwill to EVERYONE — not only to a chosen few. Every child is Krishna's child, so every child is my friend.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

This week, every day at school, find ONE child you don't usually play with. Say hi. Include them in a game. Sit with them at lunch once. Keep a small notebook called My Friendship Circle. Each day, write (or draw) the name of ONE new child you included. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa one thing you learned about them. Whisper: 'Krishna, another petal in Your lotus.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By week's end, the child can name at least 5 classmates they now know beyond their original group, and can describe at least one new friendship moment with specificity.

Signs of Growth

By week's end, the child can name at least 5 classmates they now know beyond their original group, and can describe at least one new friendship moment with specificity.

When our friendship grows so gentle that it becomes soft like a flower touching soft cheeks, that gentleness has its own name — mārdava.
