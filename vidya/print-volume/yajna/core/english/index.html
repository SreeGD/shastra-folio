About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 3.9

यज्ञार्थात्कर्मणोऽन्यत्र लोकोऽयं कर्मबन्धनः।
तदर्थं कर्म कौन्तेय मुक्तसङ्गः समाचर॥

yajñārthāt karmaṇo 'nyatra loko 'yaṁ karma-bandhanaḥ tad-arthaṁ karma kaunteya mukta-saṅgaḥ samācara

Word-for-word: yajña-arthāt — for the sake of offering; karmaṇaḥ — of work; anyatra — otherwise; lokaḥ — world; ayam — this; karma-bandhanaḥ — bondage of work; tat-artham — for His sake; karma — work; kaunteya — O son of Kuntī; mukta-saṅgaḥ — free from attachment; samācara — do perfectly.

Translation: Do your work as an offering to Krishna. Then the work will not tie you down. Do everything for Him, with a free and happy heart.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.2.36

कायेन वाचा मनसेन्द्रियैर्वा
बुद्ध्यात्मना वानुसृतस्वभावात्।
करोति यद्यत्सकलं परस्मै
नारायणायेति समर्पयेत्तत्॥

kāyena vācā manasendriyair vā buddhyātmanā vānusṛta-svabhāvāt karoti yad yat sakalaṁ parasmai nārāyaṇāyeti samarpayet tat

Translation: Whatever you do — with your body, your voice, your mind, your senses, your thinking, or simply by your nature — offer every bit of it to Narayana (Krishna). Say, 'This is Yours.'

What is Yajna?

Yajna means offering. When we do something — a song, a meal, a drawing, even a chore — and we first offer it to Krishna, that action becomes yajna. It is not only fire and flowers in a temple. Every little thing we do can become sacred when we say, 'Krishna, this is for You.' Our whole life can slowly turn into one beautiful offering.

What it is NOT

Yajna is not only for priests or only in the temple. It is not doing something just to show off — 'Look how much I gave!' It is not being grumpy while we serve. It is not keeping the best for ourselves and offering the leftover. Yajna is quiet, joyful, and done with love — even when nobody is watching.

  • Eating your snack without remembering to share even a tiny bit with Krishna or someone else
  • Doing a chore with an angry face — 'I HAVE to do this!'
  • Singing a song loudly so everyone will clap for YOU, forgetting it was meant to be for Krishna

Sanskrit Word Study

yajña (यज्ञ)

Pronunciation: YAG-nyah

Meaning: Offering. Any action — big or small — that we do for Krishna first. When we offer it, the action becomes sacred.

Example: When I sang for Krishna before breakfast, that song became a yajna.

Story from the Scriptures

King Ambarisha's Quiet Offering — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 9, Chapter 4-5

Long ago, there was a gentle king named Ambarisha. He loved Krishna more than his crown. More than his palace. More than his food.

Every morning, King Ambarisha woke up and whispered, 'Krishna, today is Yours. My hands, my words, my thoughts — all for You.'

He sat to rule his kingdom. Each good decision — offered to Krishna. He walked to the temple. Each step — an offering. He cooked prasadam for the brahmins. Each grain of rice — for Krishna first.

One day, the great sage Durvasa came to visit. Durvasa was very powerful, but also very quick-tempered.

'Dear sage, please share our meal!' said Ambarisha warmly.

'Wait!' said Durvasa. 'First I must bathe in the river.' And off he went.

Ambarisha waited. And waited. The sun was going down. The king had been fasting all day for his special offering. The moment to break the fast had arrived. If he waited any longer, the offering would be spoiled.

The priests whispered, 'Maharaja, take just ONE sip of water. Water is both food and fasting. This will honour Durvasa AND complete your offering.'

Ambarisha sipped a tiny drop of water. 'Krishna, for You,' he whispered.

Just then Durvasa returned — very angry! 'You did not wait for me!'

Durvasa's anger made a fiery demon to attack the king. Ambarisha did not run. He did not fight. He stood still, hand on heart. 'Krishna, this day was Yours. I am in Your hands.'

Suddenly a beautiful shining wheel of Krishna — the Sudarshan — appeared. It protected the king. The fiery demon fled. Durvasa himself had to run!

Ambarisha had done nothing unusual. He had only turned his whole day into an offering. And Krishna Himself watched over him — because yajna brings Krishna very, very close.

This is yajna. Not fire. Not big rituals. A whole life, quietly offered.

A Story from Today

Meera and the Homework Offering

Meera was seven years old. She loved singing. She loved stories. But Maths — Maths made her cheeks go red.

It was Tuesday evening. Her Maths notebook lay open on the table. Two times seven. Three times eight. The numbers swam around like angry fish.

Meera pushed the book away. 'I HATE this!' Her fists were tight. Her eyes felt hot.

Amma came in quietly. She sat next to Meera. She did not scold. She just waited.

'Amma, I cannot do this. It is not fair. Why do we have to do boring things?'

Amma smiled a small smile. 'Meera, do you remember the word yajna?'

Meera nodded slowly. 'Offering?'

'Yes. And what can we offer to Krishna?'

'Flowers. Food. Songs.'

Amma tilted her head. 'What about homework?'

Meera's eyes went big. 'HOMEWORK?'

'Close your eyes, beta. Pick up your notebook. Hold it on your heart.'

Meera did. The notebook felt warm.

'Now whisper, "Krishna, this is for You. Every number I write is for You."'

Meera whispered. She felt something soften inside her chest. Like a knot untying.

She opened her eyes. She picked up her pencil. The numbers were still there. Two times seven. Fourteen. Three times eight. Twenty-four. They were still hard. But now she was not alone with them. Krishna was watching every number.

Halfway through the page, Meera was smiling. When she finished, she did not run away. She held the notebook up one more time. 'Krishna, there. That was for You.'

Amma kissed her head. 'Meera, that was yajna.'

Meera felt tall. Even Maths could be holy. This is yajna.

Core Teaching

  1. Yajna means offering. Any little thing — a song, a meal, a chore, even homework — becomes an offering when we first say, 'Krishna, this is for You.'
  2. Yajna is not only fire and flowers in a temple. Our whole day can become an offering — waking up for Krishna, eating for Krishna, helping at home for Krishna, studying for Krishna.
  3. When we offer what we do, Krishna becomes very close to us. The work may still be hard — but our heart becomes soft and steady, and we are never alone inside.
