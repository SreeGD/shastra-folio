About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Yajna

Yajna means offering.

Chapter Overview

Yajna means offering. When we do something — a song, a meal, a drawing, even a chore — and we first offer it to Krishna, that action becomes yajna. It is not only fire and flowers in a temple. Every little thing we do can become sacred when we say, 'Krishna, this is for You.' Our whole life can slowly turn into one beautiful offering.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 3.9 — Do your work as an offering to Krishna. Then the work will not tie you down. Do everything for Him, with a free and happy heart.

Core Teaching

  1. Yajna means offering. Any little thing — a song, a meal, a chore, even homework — becomes an offering when we first say, 'Krishna, this is for You.'
  2. Yajna is not only fire and flowers in a temple. Our whole day can become an offering — waking up for Krishna, eating for Krishna, helping at home for Krishna, studying for Krishna.
  3. When we offer what we do, Krishna becomes very close to us. The work may still be hard — but our heart becomes soft and steady, and we are never alone inside.

Five-Minute Introduction

Place a small bell and a flower on the altar. Ring the bell once. Say softly, 'Children, today we learn a very old word — yajna. It means OFFERING. When we do something for Krishna first, that action becomes sacred.' Demonstrate: pick up a pencil, hold it to your heart, ring the bell, and say, 'Krishna, this is for You.' Place it back. Invite 3-4 children to do the same. Then chant 'Yajna, yajna, yajna' three times together, palms joined and lifted. Ask: 'Can you think of one thing you did today that you could have offered to Krishna?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Altar bell + chant 'yajna'. 10 min — Read aloud King Ambarisha's story; pause and ring the bell at each moment he offers. 8 min — Read aloud Meera's Homework Offering; act out the notebook-on-heart gesture. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Yajna or Just Me) and Activity 2 (My Yajna Plate). 5 min — Closing Offering Bell (Activity 4, quick version) + introduce the Morning Offering take-home. 2 min — Line up in silence.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Altar bell, chant 'yajna yajna yajna', opening share. 15 min — King Ambarisha's story slowly, with bell rung at each offering moment. 15 min — Meera's Homework Offering with full role-play of the notebook-on-heart gesture. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 20 min — Offering Bell circle (full Activity 4) — every child offers a small item. 15 min — My Yajna Plate craft: decorate a real paper plate with 'yajna' and four drawings of offerings. 15 min — Whole-class Yajna Walk: go outside, find ONE natural thing (a leaf, a petal, a small stone), bring it back, place on the class altar with a whispered offering. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE action tomorrow they will offer. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children meet yajna through the BODY and small actions. Avoid abstract talk about 'sacrifice' or 'cosmic reciprocity' — that is for middle and senior levels. Instead, keep it concrete: 'What did you do this morning? Can you offer THAT?' Teach the gesture — palms joined and lifted, then pressed to heart, then whispered 'Krishna, for You.' Use it as a ritual before snacks, before class activities, before going home. The body learns yajna through repetition of the small gesture. Watch for children who rush through or make the gesture theatrical — gently slow them down and whisper, 'This is between you and Krishna — just a little secret.' The quiet offering is the deepest. Never praise a child publicly for their offering; instead, catch eyes, smile, and nod. This protects the purity of the gesture and distinguishes yajna from the anti-pattern dambha-yajna (offering for show).

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Yajna is only the big fire ritual with priests.' Response: The big fire is ONE kind of yajna. But Krishna says in the Gita that every action can become yajna. Our meals, our chores, our songs, even our homework — any action offered first becomes yajna. Primary children can begin with the smallest offerings.
  • Misconception: 'If I offer my work, it will become easy.' Response: No, beta. Offering does not remove the hardness of the work. Maths is still Maths. But our heart becomes soft and steady. We are not alone with the work anymore — Krishna is watching every step. The work may stay hard, but the doing becomes joyful.
  • Misconception: 'If nobody sees me offer, it does not count.' Response: Yajna is strongest when nobody sees. We do not offer so others will praise us. We offer because Krishna is in our heart. A quiet 'Krishna, this is for You' whispered before a chore is a complete yajna, even if only Krishna hears it.
