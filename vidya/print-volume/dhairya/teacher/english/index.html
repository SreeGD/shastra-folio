About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

धैर्य
Dhairya

Dhairya means staying calm and not giving up when things are hard.

Chapter Overview

Dhairya means staying calm and not giving up when things are hard. It means waiting with a happy heart and trying again when something doesn't work.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 18.43 — Being brave, strong, never giving up, being smart, not running away when things are hard, sharing with others, and being a good leader are special gifts that some people have.

Core Teaching

  1. When things are hard, we can stay calm and keep trying instead of getting upset.
  2. Good things take time to happen, so we learn to wait with happy hearts.
  3. Krishna helps us when we don't give up and keep doing our best.

Five-Minute Introduction

Start by asking children to raise their hands if they've ever had to wait for something they really wanted. Share that today we'll learn about dhairya - a special word that means staying calm and not giving up when things are hard or when we have to wait. Show them how to say 'dhairya' with a hand gesture - place both hands on heart and take a deep breath. Explain that just like plants need time to grow, good things in our lives also need time, and dhairya helps us wait happily.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

Opening circle and dhairya introduction (5 min) → Epic story with simple acting (10 min) → Modern story with discussion (10 min) → Craft activity - patience garden (10 min) → Closing circle with dhairya chant (5 min)

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

Welcome and dhairya introduction with movement (10 min) → Epic story with role-play (20 min) → Snack break (10 min) → Modern story with drawing activity (20 min) → Craft activity - patience garden (25 min) → Movement game - waiting game (15 min) → Story sharing circle (15 min) → Closing with dhairya song and take-home practice (5 min)

Discussion Guide

1. Question: What did Prahlada do when his father was mean to him? Sample response: He stayed calm and kept praying to Lord Vishnu even when scary things happened. 2. Question: How did Kenji show dhairya with his garden? Sample response: He kept watering his seeds every day even when they didn't grow right away, and he didn't give up. 3. Question: When is it hard for you to have dhairya? Sample response: When I have to wait for my turn on the swing, or when my homework is really hard. Guide children to share specific examples and help them think of one small thing they can do to practice dhairya this week.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Children think dhairya means never feeling sad or upset when waiting. Response: Explain that it's okay to feel disappointed sometimes - dhairya means we can feel sad but still choose to keep trying and stay calm.
  • Misconception: Some children believe dhairya means waiting forever without doing anything. Response: Show that dhairya includes taking action - like Kenji watering his seeds every day while waiting for them to grow.
