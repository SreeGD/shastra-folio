About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Dhairya

Dhairya means staying calm and not giving up when things are hard. It means waiting with a happy heart and trying again when something doesn't work.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 18.43: Being brave, strong, never giving up, being smart, not running away when things are hard, sharing with others, and being a good leader are special gifts that some people have.

What Dhairya is NOT

Dhairya is not crying and getting angry when we have to wait. It is not giving up right away when something is hard.

  • Throwing tantrums when things don't happen fast
  • Giving up on homework when it's hard
  • Getting mad when we have to wait our turn

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Prahlada's Strong Heart — Once there was a little boy named Prahlada. He was only five years old. Prahlada loved Lord Vishnu very much. Every day he would pray and sing songs for the Lord. But Prahlada's father was a mean king. He did not like Lord Vishnu. He got very angry when he saw Prahlada praying. 'Stop praying!' his father shouted. 'I am the only one you should love!'

Kenji's Garden Dream — Kenji lived in a small town in Japan. His teacher, Tanaka-sensei, gave the class a special project. 'Everyone will plant seeds and grow a garden,' she said. 'The best garden will be shown to the whole school!' Kenji was so excited! He picked sunflower seeds because they were his favorite. At home, he planted them in a small pot by his window. 'I can't wait to see my big yellow flowers!' he told his mother.

The Sanskrit Word

dhairya (धैर्य) — DHIGH-rya

Meaning: staying calm and not giving up when things are hard

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each morning when you wake up, water a plant or put water in a small cup by your window. While you pour the water, say 'I have dhairya like Kenji' three times. If you don't have a plant, you can pretend to water an imaginary seed.

Duration: Every morning for one week
Look for: You will notice that waiting feels easier, and you might feel more calm when things don't happen right away.

Signs of Growth

You will notice that waiting feels easier, and you might feel more calm when things don't happen right away.

Next time we will learn about kshama - how to forgive others when they hurt our feelings, which helps us keep our hearts peaceful like dhairya keeps us calm.
