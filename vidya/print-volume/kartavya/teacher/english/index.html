About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Kartavya

Kartavya means the work that is MINE to do.

Chapter Overview

Kartavya means the work that is MINE to do. As a child, my kartavya is to study, to help at home, to be kind to friends, to care for my body and my things. Doing the little job that belongs to me — well and happily — is kartavya. When I do my own job with a glad heart, I am serving Krishna.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 3.35 — It is better to do YOUR own work — even a little clumsily — than to do someone else's work perfectly. Do the job that is yours. That is the safe and happy path.

Core Teaching

  1. Kartavya is the work that is MINE to do. Not my friend's work. Not my sister's work. Just my own little job — to study, to help, to care for my things. When I do MY job, the world works beautifully.
  2. We do our kartavya without waiting to be reminded. We do it with a smile, not a grumpy face. We do it even when it is boring. Even Krishna Himself did His small morning job as a cowherd boy.
  3. Every small duty — picking up my plate, finishing my homework, feeding the dog — is a little puja for Krishna. When I do my job happily, everyone is happy, and Krishna smiles in my heart.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin in a circle. Hold up a simple apron or a small broom. Ask: 'Whose job is it to sweep the floor at home?' Take 2-3 answers. Then ask: 'Whose job is it to do YOUR homework?' All point at themselves, smiling. Say: 'The job that is MINE to do has a beautiful Sanskrit name — kartavya. Even little Krishna had His kartavya — He fed the baby calves every morning. Today we will find OUR own kartavyas.' Chant together, patting chests gently: 'kartavya, kartavya, kartavya.' Then ask: 'What is one job you did today — all by yourself — without being told twice?' Take 2-3 answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Opening circle + chant 'kartavya'. 10 min — Read aloud: Little Krishna and the Sleepy Calves; act out the waking up and running to the shed. 8 min — Read aloud: Meera and the Plate Tower; pause at the moment she puts down her crayon. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Whose Job? Match) and Activity 2 (Duty Wheel). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce the Daily Duty Chart. 2 min — Line up, each child says one duty they will do today.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, duty-drum chant, share one duty from home. 15 min — Little Krishna story with props — a yellow cloth for the dhoti, a small pot for milk; children re-enact walking baby calves. 15 min — Meera's Plate Tower with role-play of the moment she carries the plate. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2 (Whose Job Match + Duty Wheel). 20 min — The Boring Job Game (Activity 4) paired, rotate partners twice. 15 min — Duty Chart Craft: each child decorates their own 7-day chart with stickers and pictures. 15 min — Classroom Service Round: children each pick one classroom job (wipe board, stack chairs, water plant) and do it together. 10 min — Closing circle: each child names their kartavya for the week. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children understand kartavya through tiny, visible, age-appropriate jobs — NEVER through abstract talk about 'responsibility.' Keep examples physical: your plate, your shoes, your bag, your teeth, your bed, your homework. Avoid guilt. A child who forgets a job is not 'bad' — they are practising. Watch for the perfectionist child who does too much and becomes anxious — gently tell them that kartavya is their OWN small job, not everyone's. Watch for the resistant child — invite them to pick just ONE duty this week and celebrate when they do it, even once. Teachers: model kartavya visibly. Do your own small duties — wiping the board, closing the attendance book — in front of the class, with a calm smile, not a tired sigh. Children catch what they see.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Kartavya means I have to do ALL the jobs in the house.' Response: No. Kartavya is the job that is YOURS — small jobs fit for your age. A little child does little jobs. Amma, Papa, and big people do bigger jobs. Each person has their own kartavya. When everyone does their own, the whole family is happy.
  • Misconception: 'If I do my job only because Amma told me to, it still counts as kartavya.' Response: Doing is good, and being reminded is okay at the start. But the true beauty of kartavya is doing it BEFORE we are asked. We see what needs to be done, and we do it happily — like little Krishna running to the calves.
  • Misconception: 'Boring jobs cannot be special.' Response: The job may be small and plain, but HOW we do it is never boring. A tidy shoe-rack, a clean plate, a finished homework — these are tiny pujas for Krishna. The same hands that fold prasadam can fold a school uniform. Both are sacred when offered.
