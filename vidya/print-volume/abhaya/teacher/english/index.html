About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

CHAPTER 1
अभय
Abhaya

Abhaya means being brave when you need to do the right thing.

Chapter Overview

Abhaya means being brave when you need to do the right thing. It means Krishna protects you when you are good.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.1 — The Supreme Lord said: Fearlessness comes first among all good qualities. Then comes purity of heart, learning about God, giving to others, self-control, worship, study, discipline and honesty.

Core Teaching

  1. Krishna protects children who try to do good things, even when they feel scared.
  2. Being brave means doing the right thing, not being mean or showing off to others.
  3. When we help others and pray to God, our fear goes away and we feel strong inside.

Five-Minute Introduction

Start by asking children: 'Who can show me what being scared looks like?' Let them make scared faces. Then ask: 'Who can show me what being brave looks like?' Have them stand tall and strong. Explain that today we'll learn about abhaya - being brave in a good way. Show them the Sanskrit word and have them repeat 'ah-BHA-ya' three times. Tell them Krishna helps brave children who do right things.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min intro and Sanskrit word, 10 min epic story with actions, 10 min modern story discussion, 10 min courage shield craft, 5 min closing circle sharing shields

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min intro and warm-up games, 20 min epic story with role-play, 20 min modern story and discussion, 30 min courage shield craft, 15 min brave heart game, 10 min coloring activity, 15 min sharing and cleanup

Discussion Guide

1. Question: What made Dhruva brave in the scary forest? Sample response: He remembered Krishna protects good children and he chanted God's names. Guide children to see that prayer and remembering God helps us be brave. 2. Question: How did Yuki show good bravery with the kitten? Sample response: She asked for help even though she was scared because she wanted to help the kitten. Help children understand that asking for help when doing good things is brave. 3. Question: When do you need to be brave like Dhruva and Yuki? Sample response: When helping friends, telling the truth, or doing right things even when scared. Encourage children to share their own examples of when they need courage.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Being brave means never feeling scared. Response: Explain that even brave people feel scared sometimes, but they do the right thing anyway because they trust Krishna to help them.
  • Misconception: Being brave means being tough or mean to others. Response: Show that real bravery is being kind and helpful, like Dhruva praying peacefully or Yuki helping the kitten gently.
