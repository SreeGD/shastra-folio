About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Abhaya

Abhaya means being brave when you need to do the right thing. It means Krishna protects you when you are good.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.1: The Supreme Lord said: Fearlessness comes first among all good qualities. Then comes purity of heart, learning about God, giving to others, self-control, worship, study, discipline and honesty.

What Abhaya is NOT

Abhaya is not being mean or rough with others. It is not showing off or being rude.

  • Being a bully
  • Showing off
  • Being rude to others

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Dhruva's Journey to the Forest — Once there was a little prince named Dhruva. He was only five years old. His stepmother said mean things to him. She told him he could not sit on his father's lap because his mother was not the queen. Dhruva felt very sad. He ran to his mother crying. His mother hugged him and said, "Only God can help you now. Go to the forest and pray to Lord Vishnu."

Yuki and the Lost Kitten — Yuki was walking to school in Tokyo when she heard a tiny sound. "Meow, meow." She looked under a bush and found a small kitten. The kitten was wet from the rain and looked hurt. Yuki's heart felt heavy. The kitten needed help right away. But she was scared to ask grown-ups. What if her teacher said no? What if her parents got angry?

The Sanskrit Word

abhaya (अभय) — ah-BHA-ya

Meaning: fearlessness, being brave

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each night before bed, hold your courage shield and say 'Krishna protects me' three times. Then think of one brave, good thing you want to do tomorrow.

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: You will feel less scared about doing good things and more excited to help others.

Signs of Growth

You will feel less scared about doing good things and more excited to help others.
