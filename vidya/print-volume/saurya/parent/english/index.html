About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Saurya

Saurya means brave-heart. It is the big warm feeling in my chest that says 'I will stand up. I will help. I will do the right thing even when I am a little scared.' Saurya is not the same as having no fear. My tummy can still feel fluttery. My knees can still wobble. But my heart says STAND UP — and my feet go forward. Saurya is Krishna's own strength borrowed into my small body. When I protect a smaller friend, speak the truth, or try the scary thing because it is right, a little golden shield flashes inside me. That shield has a name — saurya.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 2.3: Krishna says to Arjuna: 'Dear friend, do not give in to this little weak-heart feeling. It does not fit a brave one like you. Stand up! Stand up, hero — stand up and shine!' When fear knocks on our door, Krishna Himself says, 'Stand up, little one. I am with you. Be brave.'

What Saurya is NOT

Saurya is not being rough, loud, or pushing people around. A bully is NOT brave — a bully is hiding fear behind a big voice. Saurya is also not showing off — jumping off high things or doing dangerous stunts to look cool is not bravery, it is silliness with no heart. And saurya is not being brave only when people are watching. If I help only when the teacher can see me, that is not true saurya. Real saurya is quiet, warm, and ready — whether anyone is watching or not.

  • Pushing smaller children and calling it 'being strong'
  • Standing by and watching while a classmate is teased — saying 'it is not my problem'
  • Doing dangerous stunts to show off to friends
  • Being brave only when the teacher is watching

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Angada Stands in Ravana's Court — Long ago, when Lord Rama was marching to Lanka to bring back Sita Ma, He needed to send one last message to the terrible ten-headed demon-king Ravana. Rama looked around His army of monkeys. Hanuman had already gone. Who should go this time?

Meera and the Lunch-Box Thief — Meera was in Standard Three in a school in Pune. She had long two plaits and a bright orange water bottle. Meera loved lunchtime — idli-chutney on Mondays, dal-rice on Tuesdays, chapati on Wednesdays. There was a small quiet girl in her class named Tanu. Tanu was in Standard Two, sometimes joining Meera's class for art. Tanu was thin. She did not speak much.

The Sanskrit Word

saurya (शौर्य) — SHAUR-ya

Meaning: Brave-heart. The little golden shield that shines inside my chest when I stand up for what is right — while my knees still wobble. Krishna's bravery borrowed into my small body.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Golden Shield Journal by your bed. Each evening, before sleep, press your small hand on your chest and whisper three times: 'Krishna, lend me Your bravery. I will stand up kindly for what is right.' Then draw a tiny golden shield in your book. Under it, write or draw ONE brave thing you did today — spoke the truth, stood up for a smaller friend, tried a scary new thing, said 'no' to something wrong, or stood up for a puppy or plant. Small shields count just as much as big shields. Tick it before you sleep.

Duration: Every evening for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments when they chose to 'stand up kindly' (protect a smaller child, speak the truth when hard, say no to something wrong, or try a scary new thing). They can demonstrate the plant-feet-hand-on-chest gesture while chanting 'saurya' with a smile. The journal has at least 5 little golden shields drawn. Parents report fewer moments of the child freezing or going along silently when something was wrong.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments when they chose to 'stand up kindly' (protect a smaller child, speak the truth when hard, say no to something wrong, or try a scary new thing). They can demonstrate the plant-feet-hand-on-chest gesture while chanting 'saurya' with a smile. The journal has at least 5 little golden shields drawn. Parents report fewer moments of the child freezing or going along silently when something was wrong.

When the little golden shield of saurya grows brighter and brighter — so bright that even the FEAR itself melts away in its warmth — that total fearlessness has its own beautiful name: abhaya.
