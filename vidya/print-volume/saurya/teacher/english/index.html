About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Saurya

Saurya means brave-heart.

Chapter Overview

Saurya means brave-heart. It is the big warm feeling in my chest that says 'I will stand up. I will help. I will do the right thing even when I am a little scared.' Saurya is not the same as having no fear. My tummy can still feel fluttery. My knees can still wobble. But my heart says STAND UP — and my feet go forward. Saurya is Krishna's own strength borrowed into my small body. When I protect a smaller friend, speak the truth, or try the scary thing because it is right, a little golden shield flashes inside me. That shield has a name — saurya.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 2.3 — Krishna says to Arjuna: 'Dear friend, do not give in to this little weak-heart feeling. It does not fit a brave one like you. Stand up! Stand up, hero — stand up and shine!' When fear knocks on our door, Krishna Himself says, 'Stand up, little one. I am with you. Be brave.'

Core Teaching

  1. Saurya means brave-heart — the little golden shield that lights up inside me when I stand up for what is right. My knees may wobble. My tummy may flip-flop. But I press my hand on my chest, whisper 'Krishna stand with me,' plant my feet, and say or do the brave thing. Real bravery is not having no fear — it is moving forward even while the fear is there.
  2. Saurya is always GENTLE and KIND. A bully is not brave — a bully is hiding fear behind a loud voice. A truly brave child uses a clear, not-loud voice. A truly brave child protects the smaller one, not pushes them. Like little Angada, who stood firm but spoke politely. Like Meera, who did not shout at Karan but invited him to share. Saurya carries a shield, never a stick.
  3. Saurya is not my own — it is borrowed from Krishna. In BG 2.3, Krishna Himself says, 'Stand up, little one! Don't give in to that weak-heart feeling.' When I whisper His name and plant my feet, His strength becomes my strength. That is why Angada said, 'The brave heart was not mine — it was Rama's, lent to me.' I am small. But my Krishna is big. His shield shines through me.

Five-Minute Introduction

Sit in a circle. Ask the children: 'Have you ever felt your tummy go FLIP-FLOP? Your knees go wobble-wobble? Your heart go BUMP-BUMP?' Let them mime the wobble. Say: 'Everyone feels that. Even the biggest warriors feel that. But there is a little GOLDEN SHIELD inside your chest. When you press your hand here' — (hand on chest) — 'and whisper Krishna's name, the shield wakes up. Your knees still wobble, but your feet go FORWARD. That is called SAURYA — brave-heart.' Chant together, stamping one foot on each beat: 'Sau-rya! Sau-rya! Sau-rya!' Ask: 'Has there been a time when your knees wobbled, but you did the right thing anyway?' Take 2-3 short answers with big smiles. Celebrate every wobble.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Brave-heart intro + hand-on-chest + plant-feet chant 'saurya' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud Angada in Ravana's court; children mime planting their little foot and whispering 'Rama, stand with me.' 8 min — Read aloud Meera and the Lunch-Box; pause at the FLIP-FLOP tummy and the shield flaring golden. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Stand Up or Walk Past) + Activity 2 (Draw Golden Shield). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce Golden Shield Journal take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with hand-on-chest and brave-heart chant. 15 min — Angada's story with props: each child gets a paper-foot to 'plant' on the floor when their turn comes; they whisper 'Rama, stand with me' before speaking one brave line. 15 min — Meera's story, re-enacted — children take turns being Meera, Tanu, and Karan; Meera practises a clear, not-loud voice. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2 (Stand-Up scenarios and Golden Shield drawing). 15 min — Paired Plant-Your-Feet Role-Play (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how the chest feels when feet are planted. 15 min — Golden Shield Craft: each child cuts a paper shield, decorates it with their name, Krishna's peacock feather, and three 'brave things I want to do'; attaches string to wear or hang. 10 min — Biographical story: Lachit Borphukan, the brave Assamese general who defended his land from invaders; children mime standing firm on the river bank. 10 min — Yoga: Angada's Planted-Foot Pose (Vrksasana / tree pose) for 5 breaths; Meera's Brave-Heart Pose (Virabhadrasana / warrior pose) for 5 breaths. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE kind, brave thing they will do this week; teacher hands out Golden Shield Journals. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children feel saurya in the BODY first — the planted feet, the hand-on-chest, the straight back, the clear voice. Use these physical cues every class. A good way to teach: have the children stamp one foot when they say 'Sau-rya!' They feel the groundedness physically. Distinguish saurya very carefully from three confusions: (1) bullying — the child who pushes others is NOT brave, they are hiding fear; respond with compassion, not shame, but clearly name the difference; (2) showing-off / daredevilry — jumping from high walls, pretending not to be scared to look cool — this is silliness, not saurya; (3) recklessness — running into danger without thinking. Real saurya is always THOUGHTFUL and KIND. Watch for the shy or anxious child who wants to stand up but feels frozen — do not push them into role-playing first. Begin with just the planted-feet gesture and the hand-on-chest. Name their tiny brave moments out loud — 'You asked for a pencil when you were nervous — that is saurya.' The shield grows with sunshine, not storm. Also watch for children who already show bravery in rough ways — never say 'you are bad at saurya.' Instead, show them gently: 'Your chest is already brave. Now let your voice also be kind. Brave-heart PLUS kind-voice equals saurya.' Most importantly, model saurya yourself. Stand up kindly when you see unfairness in the class. Say 'no' gently when something is wrong. Use a clear, not-loud voice. Let them catch you whispering 'Krishna, stand with me' before a hard moment. The classroom full of little shields begins with one teacher's steady shining one.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Saurya means I must have no fear at all.' Response: No, no. Even little Angada's knees wobbled in Ravana's court. Even Meera's tummy went flip-flop. Even the bravest warriors in the Mahabharata felt afraid. Saurya is NOT the absence of fear — it is standing up WHILE the fear is there. Krishna Himself says in BG 2.3, 'Stand up, little one' — He knows we feel weak-heart sometimes. His name is the shield that lets us move forward even with wobbly knees.
  • Misconception: 'Saurya means being loud, rough, or pushing people around.' Response: Exactly backwards! A bully is NOT brave. A bully is hiding their own fear behind a loud voice. True saurya is GENTLE and CLEAR — not loud, not mean. Angada spoke politely in Ravana's court. Meera spoke kindly to Karan and even invited him to share. A truly brave child does not hurt — they PROTECT. The hand that bullies is shaky inside. The hand that protects is warm and steady.
  • Misconception: 'I am too small to be brave. Saurya is for grown-ups and warriors.' Response: Angada was a small monkey prince. Meera is only 8. You — at 6, 7, 8 — have the SAME little golden shield as the biggest warrior. Saurya does not measure in centimetres. It measures in heart. Even standing up for a smaller classmate, telling the truth when it is hard, or trying a scary new thing is full-size saurya. Krishna lends the shield equally to everyone who asks.
  • Misconception: 'Saurya is only for when people are watching — to look brave.' Response: No — that is showing off, not saurya. True saurya is the same whether the teacher is watching or not. If I help a smaller child only when amma can see me, that is not saurya yet. If I do the brave thing even when no one will ever know — THAT is real saurya. The shield shines because of what is inside, not for claps and stickers. Krishna sees. That is enough.
