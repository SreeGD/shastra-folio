About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Svadhyaya

Svadhyaya means self-study — learning one little thing every single day.

Chapter Overview

Svadhyaya means self-study — learning one little thing every single day. A new word. A verse. A story about Krishna. A fact about the saints. My little library grows page by page. And then, at the end of the day, I sit quietly and look inside — 'What did I do well today? What can I do better tomorrow?' The book outside teaches my mind. The looking inside teaches my heart.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 4.34 — Krishna says: Learn a little every day. Bow your head. Ask a gentle question. Serve with love. Then the wise ones — the ones who have truly seen — will open up their knowledge and pour it into your heart.

Core Teaching

  1. Svadhyaya means I learn ONE new thing every day — one word of Sanskrit, one verse, one story about Krishna, one fact about the saints. Like a tiny plant that adds one leaf each week, my little library inside me grows page by page. Small and steady beats big and flashy.
  2. Svadhyaya has two sides — outside study AND inside study. The outside is the book — the Gita, the story of Prahlada, the sweet name of Krishna. The inside is the quiet looking-in at bedtime — 'Did I speak kind words? Did I tell the truth? Where can I grow tomorrow?' Both together make a full heart.
  3. The best study is happy, slow, and honest. I do not read fast to show off. I do not pretend I did everything perfectly. I learn because I love Krishna and I love to grow. Krishna says in the Gita — bow gently, ask with care, serve with love. Then the wise ones will pour their knowledge into my heart.

Five-Minute Introduction

Bring a small plant (curry leaf, tulasi, or even a drawing of one) to the centre of the circle. Say: 'Children, this plant grows ONE new leaf at a time. Slowly. Quietly. It does not try to grow ten leaves in one day.' Then hold up a tiny book. 'Our heart is like this plant. Every day we add ONE new thing — one word, one story, one verse. That is called svadhyaya — स्वाध्याय — self-study.' Open your palm like a book. 'And then at bedtime we close our eyes for two minutes and ask our own heart — what did I do well today? Where can I grow? That is ALSO svadhyaya.' Chant together with palms joined, bowing gently: 'Sva-DHYAA-ya! Sva-DHYAA-ya! Sva-DHYAA-ya!' Ask: 'What is ONE new thing you learned this week?' Take 2-3 short, smiling answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — One-leaf plant demo + chant 'svadhyaya' 3x with bow gesture. 10 min — Read aloud Little Prahlada Teaches His Classmates; pause at 'every day, one new flower; every day, one look inside.' 8 min — Read aloud Ishaan and His One-Page-A-Day Book; children mime slowly turning one page and closing eyes for the bedtime inside-look. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Real Study or Pretend Study) + Activity 2 (My Little Library drawing). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce One-Leaf-A-Day Book take-home. 2 min — Line up quietly.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with one-leaf plant, palms-at-heart bow, chant 'svadhyaya' 3x. 15 min — Little Prahlada's story with children sitting cross-legged in a circle like his classmates; teacher plays Prahlada softly teaching. 15 min — Ishaan and the One-Page-A-Day Book, re-enacted with a real small Gita and a real small plant on a table — the teacher 'reads' one page slowly and models a 2-minute bedtime inside-look. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Paired Inside-Look Circle (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how the heart feels when someone listens without laughing. 15 min — One-Leaf Book Craft: each child decorates the cover of their own small notebook (will become their One-Leaf-A-Day Book); draw a plant, a sun, a tiny Gita. 10 min — Biographical story: young Swami Chinmayananda reading scriptures under a lamp; children sit quietly as if reading their own book. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE thing they will study tomorrow (a Krishna name, a page, a verse) and ONE thing they will look for in their own heart at bedtime. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn svadhyaya through TWO simple habits placed side by side: (1) the morning 'one-leaf' habit — opening one book, one page, one new word, one little story about Krishna; and (2) the bedtime 'inside-look' habit — two quiet minutes to ask the heart 'What did I do well? What will I grow tomorrow?' Keep BOTH in every lesson. If a child only reads and never looks inside, svadhyaya has one wing. If a child only reflects and never reads, the well has no water. Use physical cues — one small book held up for the outside wing, one small hand-on-heart for the inside wing. Distinguish svadhyaya carefully from two anti-patterns: (1) SHOW-OFF study — the child who memorises ten verses to impress others. Gently redirect: 'Beautiful that you remember. Now — what did ONE of those verses teach your heart this week?' (2) SPEED-READING — the child who flips pages to finish fast. 'Stop, beta. Read this ONE sentence again. What picture does it make in your mind?' Watch for the honest-but-worried child who says 'I spoke badly today' — NEVER shame. Smile and say 'Your heart is already growing because you noticed. Tomorrow a softer word.' Watch for the child who says 'I did everything perfectly!' — gently probe: 'Think again, quietly. Everyone has one small thing to grow. What is yours?' Connect svadhyaya to bhakti — every verse we learn is Krishna speaking to us; every honest inside-look is Krishna's friend helping us grow. Most importantly — model svadhyaya yourself. Tell the children ONE thing YOU are studying this week. Tell them ONE thing you noticed about yourself at bedtime yesterday. Teachers who study in front of children raise children who study. The small book in YOUR hand becomes the small book in theirs.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Svadhyaya means reading a LOT — the more pages the better.' Response: No, dear one. Svadhyaya is not about speed or quantity. A child who reads ONE page slowly and thinks about it has full svadhyaya. A child who flips through ten pages fast has only pretend-study. Krishna's Gita grows in the heart, not on the clock. One leaf a week is how great trees grow.
  • Misconception: 'Svadhyaya is only outside reading — only books.' Response: Not at all. Svadhyaya has TWO wings — outside study (the book) and inside study (looking at our own day, our own heart). A bird cannot fly with one wing. When we read the Gita AND look honestly at our own actions, then svadhyaya becomes whole. Both together.
  • Misconception: 'I can pretend I had a perfect day during my inside-study time.' Response: Gently, no. Svadhyaya means HONEST looking-in. If we spoke rudely, we say 'Krishna, I was unkind today. Tomorrow I will try better.' That is not shame — it is the warm truth. A child who hides mistakes from herself cannot grow. Krishna loves the honest heart more than the perfect-looking heart.
  • Misconception: 'I need a grown-up to tell me what to study each day.' Response: A grown-up can guide us — Dadaji, Amma, the teacher. But svadhyaya means WE pick up the book. We open the page. We sit for our quiet minute. It is called SVA-dhyaya — 'self-study' — because the first step is ours. Teachers plant seeds. We water them every day ourselves.
