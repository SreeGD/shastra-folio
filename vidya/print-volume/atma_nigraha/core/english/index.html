About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Atma Nigraha

Self-control means being the boss of your own body and mind.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 6.5

उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् ।
आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ॥५॥

uddhared ātmanātmānaṁ nātmānam avasādayet ātmaiva hy ātmano bandhur ātmaiva ripur ātmanaḥ

Word-for-word: uddharet — one must deliver; ātmanā — by the mind; ātmānam — the conditioned soul; na — never; ātmānam — the conditioned soul; avasādayet — put into degradation; ātmā — mind; eva — certainly; hi — indeed; ātmanaḥ — of the conditioned soul; bandhuḥ — friend; ātmā — mind; eva — certainly; ripuḥ — enemy; ātmanaḥ — of the conditioned soul

Translation: We must help ourselves with our mind. We must not let our mind make us do bad things. Our mind can be our best friend or our worst enemy.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 3.9.42

अहमात्मात्मनां धात: प्रेष्ठ: सन् प्रेयसामपि ।
अतो मयि रतिं कुर्याद्देहादिर्यत्कृते प्रिय: ॥ ४२ ॥

aham ātmātmanāṁ dhātaḥ preṣṭhaḥ san preyasām api ato mayi ratiṁ kuryād dehādir yat-kṛte pṛyaḥ

Translation: Therefore I do not favor anyone nor am I envious of anyone. I see all beings equally. But those who worship Me with love and devotion are very dear to Me, and I am very dear to them. One who fixes his mind on My lotus feet with firm determination can control his mind.

What is Atma Nigraha?

Self-control means being the boss of your own body and mind. It means you can choose to do good things even when you want to do something else.

What it is NOT

Self-control is not being mean to yourself or never having fun.

  • Throwing tantrums when you don't get what you want
  • Eating too much candy and getting sick
  • Hitting others when you feel angry

Sanskrit Word Study

ātma-nigraha (आत्मनिग्रह)

Pronunciation: AHT-ma NI-gra-ha

Meaning: controlling yourself

Example: When I practice atma-nigraha, I can choose to be kind even when I feel angry.

Story from the Scriptures

Prahlada and the Demons' Temptations — Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5-7

Little Prahlada lived in a palace full of demons. His father was the demon king. But Prahlada loved Lord Vishnu with all his heart.

The demon teachers did not like this. They wanted Prahlada to be mean like them. So they tried many tricks.

First, they brought shiny toys. "Look, Prahlada! Play with these instead of thinking about Vishnu!" But Prahlada smiled and said, "No thank you. I am happy singing to Vishnu."

Then they brought sweet candy. "Eat all you want! Forget about Vishnu!" But Prahlada ate just a little bit. He said, "Vishnu gives me everything I need."

The demons got angry. They made scary faces and loud noises. "Stop thinking about Vishnu or we will hurt you!" they shouted.

But Prahlada did not cry or get angry. He closed his eyes and sang Vishnu's name quietly in his heart. "Vishnu, Vishnu, Vishnu." The song made him feel safe and calm.

Because Prahlada controlled his mind with Vishnu's name, the demons could not make him do bad things. His peaceful heart was stronger than all their tricks.

Soon, other children saw how happy Prahlada was. They wanted to learn his secret too.

A Story from Today

Maya's Morning Choice

Maya lived in a cozy house in Mumbai with her mother and little brother. Every morning was the same problem.

"Maya, time to wake up!" her mother would call. But Maya loved her warm bed. She would pull the blanket over her head and say, "No! Go away!"

Sometimes Maya would stomp her feet. Sometimes she would say mean things. Her mother looked sad. Her little brother would start crying too.

One morning, Maya's grandmother came to visit. She saw Maya being grumpy again.

"Maya," said Grandmother gently, "I know a magic trick. When you feel angry, count to ten and take three deep breaths. Try it."

Maya felt silly, but she tried it. "One, two, three..." She breathed in and out slowly. Something felt different inside her chest.

"The angry feeling is still there," said Maya. "But now I can choose what to do with it."

Because Maya learned to control her grumpy feelings, she could get up without making everyone sad. Her mother smiled. Her brother giggled.

"I am the boss of my feelings!" Maya said proudly. And mornings became much happier in their house.

Core Teaching

  1. Your mind can be your friend when you control it, or your enemy when you don't.
  2. Self-control means making good choices even when you want to do something else.
  3. When you control yourself, you feel happy and strong inside.
