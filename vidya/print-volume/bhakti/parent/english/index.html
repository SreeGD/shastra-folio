About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Bhakti

Bhakti means loving God with your whole heart. It means caring for Krishna like you care for your best friend. When you have bhakti, you want to make Krishna happy.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.34: Think of Me always. Love Me. Pray to Me. Bow to Me. If you do this, you will come to Me.

What Bhakti is NOT

Bhakti is not just wanting things for yourself. It is not being mean to others or only caring about toys and games.

  • Being selfish and not sharing
  • Only praying when you want something
  • Being mean to animals or friends

Stories Your Child Heard

Krishna and Sudama's Love — Long ago, there lived a man named Sudama. He and Krishna had been best friends when they were little boys at school. They studied together and played together every day. But now they were grown up. Krishna had become a great king in the beautiful city of Dvaraka. Sudama had become a poor teacher. His clothes were old. His family did not have enough food to eat.

Maya's Garden Gift — Maya lived in a small village in Kenya. She loved to plant seeds in her little garden behind her house. Every morning, she watered the tiny green shoots. After many weeks, beautiful yellow flowers bloomed. Maya clapped her hands. 'They're so pretty!' she said. 'I want to keep them all in my room.'

The Sanskrit Word

bhakti (भक्ति) — BHAK-tee

Meaning: love for God, caring with your whole heart

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each day, find one small thing you can give or share with someone in your family - a drawing, a flower, help with a job, or a kind word. Before you give it, think 'This is for Krishna' in your heart.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice that giving makes you feel warm and happy inside, and your family will smile more.

Signs of Growth

You will notice that giving makes you feel warm and happy inside, and your family will smile more.
