About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Ishvara Pranidhana

Ishvara-pranidhana means trusting Krishna with all our heart. We do our best work, then we say, 'Krishna, the rest is for You.' We offer our food, our games, our homework — everything — to Krishna first. When we trust God, our heart feels safe and light.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 18.66: Come to Me. I will take care of you. Do not worry.

What Ishvara Pranidhana is NOT

Ishvara-pranidhana is not being lazy and saying 'God will do it.' It is not making deals with Krishna: 'If You give me this, I will pray.' It is not pretending to pray while holding tight to our own plans inside.

  • Throwing a tantrum when things do not go our way
  • Not trying hard and saying 'God will take care of everything'
  • Praying only when we want something, never to say thank you

Stories Your Child Heard

Gajendra the Elephant Calls Krishna — Long ago there was a mighty elephant named Gajendra. He was the king of all elephants. His grey body was big like a mountain. His tusks were shining white. His trunk was strong as ten trees. One hot afternoon, Gajendra went with his family to a beautiful lake. The water was cool and silver. Lotuses were blooming pink and white.

Pooja and the Lost Key — Pooja came home from school. She was tired and hungry. She reached into her pocket for the key Amma had given her. But the key was GONE.

The Sanskrit Word

īśvara-praṇidhāna (ईश्वरप्रणिधान) — EESH-va-ra pra-ni-DHAA-na

Meaning: Offering our heart, our work, and our worry to Krishna. Doing our best and trusting God with the rest.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each night before bed, sit quietly with Amma or Appa for two minutes. Close your eyes. Place your hands at your heart. Think of ONE thing from the day — a game, a struggle, a kindness, a worry. Whisper: 'Krishna, this is for You.' Put a small drawing or note into your Offering Box. Also: before eating, pause and offer a morsel to Krishna with a small mantra or a soft 'Thank You, Krishna.'

Duration: Every day for two weeks
Look for: By the end of two weeks, the child naturally pauses before meals and bedtime, and can describe at least one moment where giving the worry to Krishna helped them feel calmer.

Signs of Growth

By the end of two weeks, the child naturally pauses before meals and bedtime, and can describe at least one moment where giving the worry to Krishna helped them feel calmer.

When we have truly trusted Krishna with our life, our heart becomes fearless — and that fearlessness has its own name, abhaya.
