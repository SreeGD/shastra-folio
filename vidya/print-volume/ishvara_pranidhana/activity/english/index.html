About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Ishvara Pranidhana

Ishvara-pranidhana means trusting Krishna with all our heart.

Worksheet

Ishvara-Pranidhana Worksheet — Trusting Krishna

Activity 1: Whose Hands Hold What? (Match)

Draw a line from each thing to the correct pair of hands.

*Things:* My effort in studies · The grade I get · Saying 'please' and 'thank you' · Whether friends like me · Eating healthy food · The weather tomorrow · Telling the truth · Whether it rains on our picnic

*Pairs of Hands:* MY HANDS (I can do this) · KRISHNA'S HANDS (I trust Him)

Activity 2: Draw Your Offering Plate

Draw a circle to make a plate. Inside the plate, draw or write one of each:

  • One thing you ate today
  • One thing you played
  • One kind thing you did
  • One hard thing you tried

At the top write: For You, Krishna.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Chant three times, palms together at your heart:

Krish-na, Krish-na, Krish-na — I do my best, the rest is Yours.

Activity 4: The Worry Basket (Paired)

With a partner, take turns:

1. Tell one thing you are worried about (lost toy? hard test? angry friend?).

2. Pretend to put the worry in a basket.

3. Say together, 'Krishna, please take this. I did my best.'

4. Open your hands and smile.

How does it feel inside after you hand the worry over?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Offering Box

At home, decorate a small box with Amma or Appa. Call it your Offering Box. Each night before bed, draw or write ONE thing from your day — big or small — and put it in the box. Say softly, 'Krishna, this is for You.'

At the end of the week, count all your offerings. Every one of them — big or small — reached Krishna.

Structured Activities

  • Whose Hands Hold What (whole_class, 8 min)

    Match each thing to MY HANDS (I can do this) or KRISHNA'S HANDS (I trust Him).

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • Offering Plate Drawing (solo, 10 min)

    Draw a plate with one thing you ate, one thing you played, one kind act, one hard try. Write 'For You, Krishna' at the top.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons

  • Say It With Me (whole_class, 3 min)

    Chant 'Krishna, I do my best, the rest is Yours' three times with palms at heart.

  • The Worry Basket (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, take turns placing a worry into a pretend basket and saying 'Krishna, please take this.'

    Materials: A real or imaginary basket

  • The Offering Box (solo, 5 min)

    At home, decorate a box. Every night put in one drawing or note. Whisper 'Krishna, this is for You.'

    Materials: Small box, Crayons, Paper slips

Home Practice

Each night before bed, sit quietly with Amma or Appa for two minutes. Close your eyes. Place your hands at your heart. Think of ONE thing from the day — a game, a struggle, a kindness, a worry. Whisper: 'Krishna, this is for You.' Put a small drawing or note into your Offering Box. Also: before eating, pause and offer a morsel to Krishna with a small mantra or a soft 'Thank You, Krishna.'

Duration: Every day for two weeks
Look for: By the end of two weeks, the child naturally pauses before meals and bedtime, and can describe at least one moment where giving the worry to Krishna helped them feel calmer.
