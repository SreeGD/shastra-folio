About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Sthitaprajna

Sthitaprajna means steady wisdom.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 2.55

प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान्।
आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते॥

prajahāti yadā kāmān sarvān pārtha mano-gatān ātmany evātmanā tuṣṭaḥ sthita-prajñas tadocyate

Word-for-word: prajahāti — gives up; yadā — when; kāmān — wishes; sarvān — all; pārtha — O son of Prithā (Arjuna); manaḥ-gatān — running in the mind; ātmani — in the self; eva — only; ātmanā — by the self; tuṣṭaḥ — satisfied; sthita-prajñaḥ — steady in wisdom; tadā — then; ucyate — is called.

Translation: When a person is happy inside — not because of things outside — we call that person sthitaprajna. Their mind is steady. Their heart does not jump up and down.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.14.13

प्रसन्नमनसो ह्याशु भगवद्भक्तियोगतः।
ज्ञानविज्ञानविरक्तिस्तथात्मा सम्प्रसीदति॥

prasanna-manaso hy āśu bhagavad-bhakti-yogataḥ jñāna-vijñāna-viraktis tathātmā samprasīdati

Translation: When the mind becomes peaceful through devotion to the Lord, knowledge, wisdom, and steadiness arise naturally, and the heart is deeply satisfied.

What is Sthitaprajna?

Sthitaprajna means steady wisdom. My heart stays calm whether I win or lose, whether I am praised or scolded. When something good happens, I do not jump too high. When something sad happens, I do not fall too low. I remember Krishna is always with me, so inside I stay steady, like a deep calm lake.

What it is NOT

Sthitaprajna is not being cold or not caring. It is not hiding our feelings or pretending to be a stone. It is also not being grumpy and silent. A steady heart still smiles, still cries a little, still hugs — but it does not get blown away by every little wind.

  • Jumping up and down for hours after winning, then crying for hours after losing
  • Being full of pride when the teacher praises, then being angry when scolded
  • Refusing to play again because I lost one game

Sanskrit Word Study

sthita-prajña (स्थितप्रज्ञ)

Pronunciation: STHEE-ta-prag-nya

Meaning: Steady wisdom. A person whose mind stays calm whether happy things or sad things happen. Their heart is full because Krishna is inside.

Example: When I smiled kindly at Priya after losing the race, I was practicing sthitaprajna.

Story from the Scriptures

Arjuna's Big Question on the Battlefield — Bhagavad-gita, Chapter 2, verses 54-57

Long ago, on the great battlefield of Kurukshetra, two armies stood face to face. Arjuna, the best archer in the world, sat in his silver chariot. His horses were white like jasmine flowers. But Arjuna was not happy. His bow slipped from his hand. His eyes filled with tears.

'Krishna,' he whispered, 'my heart is shaking. I am afraid.'

Krishna, his blue-skinned friend, sat beside him holding the reins. Krishna smiled gently. He began to teach Arjuna the greatest song of all — the Bhagavad-gita.

After Krishna spoke many beautiful things, Arjuna asked a big question.

'Krishna, please tell me — who is a truly wise person? How do they sit? How do they walk?'

Krishna's eyes shone like the full moon. 'O Arjuna, a wise person is called a sthitaprajna. Their mind is steady inside.'

'But how, Krishna? How is their mind steady?'

Krishna pointed to the far, far ocean. 'Look at the big sea. Many rivers run into it — some bringing sweet water, some muddy water, some monsoon floods. But the ocean does not become angry. It does not overflow. It does not shout. It takes everything in, and stays full, and stays calm.'

Arjuna's eyes grew wide. 'So the wise person is like the ocean?'

'Yes,' said Krishna softly. 'Good things come, bad things come. Praise, scolding. The sthitaprajna takes them all in — and stays steady. Not because their heart is hard. Because their heart is full of Me.'

Arjuna slowly picked up his bow. His hands did not shake anymore. A steady mind is a gift Krishna gives to those who trust Him.

A Story from Today

Meera and the Sports Day Race

Meera loved running. Every morning she ran around the school ground with her long braid flying behind her. All the teachers said, 'Meera is the fastest in Standard 3!'

Sports Day came. The big race was the 100-meter dash. Meera wore her crisp white shoes. Amma had tied a small red thread on her wrist for good luck.

'On your marks. Get set. GO!'

Meera ran like the wind. But today — today Priya, the new girl, ran a tiny bit faster. Priya's finger touched the finish ribbon first. Meera came second.

Meera did not say a word. She walked to a corner near the mango tree. Her silver medal felt cold and heavy. Her eyes burned. 'Everyone will laugh at me,' she thought. 'I was supposed to win!'

At home, Meera threw the medal on the bed. 'I never want to run again!'

Appa sat beside her quietly. He took her hand. 'Meera, do you remember the verse we read last Sunday? About the sthitaprajna?'

Meera sniffled. 'The one who has a steady heart?'

'Yes. Krishna said — happy or sad, praised or scolded, the wise heart stays steady. Not because winning and losing do not matter. Because Krishna is bigger than both.'

Meera wiped her tears. 'But Appa, it hurts.'

'Of course, my treasure. Feel the hurt. Then let it go — like a leaf floating down a river. Tomorrow, cheer for Priya. A steady heart can even be happy for the winner.'

The next morning, Meera walked up to Priya. 'You ran beautifully,' she said. Priya's face broke into a big smile. And inside Meera, something new and warm began to grow — steadier than any medal.

Core Teaching

  1. Sthitaprajna means my heart stays steady — whether I win or lose, whether I am praised or scolded. Inside me, a quiet friend is always there: Krishna.
  2. Feelings like happiness and sadness come and go, like clouds in the sky. We let them come. We let them go. We do not become the clouds — we are the sky underneath.
  3. When we are steady, we can be kind to the winner AND to ourselves, we can cheer AND console, we can try again without fear. This is the real gift of Krishna's friendship.
