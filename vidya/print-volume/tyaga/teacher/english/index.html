About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

त्याग
Tyaga

Tyaga means letting go with a peaceful heart.

Chapter Overview

Tyaga means letting go with a peaceful heart. I can give up my turn. I can give up the biggest piece. I can give up winning, so a smaller friend can smile. I can even let go of needing to be right. The hand that opens is bigger than the hand that grabs.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 18.2 — The Lord says: Letting go of selfish wanting is one thing. But the wisest teachers call it TYAGA when we do our work with love — and let go of what we get in return.

Core Teaching

  1. Tyaga means letting go with a peaceful, smiling heart. I give up my turn, my biggest piece, my need to be first — and I do it happily, not with a grumpy face.
  2. Tyaga is not running away from our duty. It is doing our work with full love, and letting go of the REWARD. We help because helping is right, not because we want a prize.
  3. When we let go of small things quietly, our heart gets bigger. The hand that opens holds more than the hand that grabs. Krishna smiles when we let go for the joy of someone else.

Five-Minute Introduction

Sit in a circle. Show the children your closed fist. Ask: 'If my hand is closed tight, can anything come in? Can anything go out?' Open your palm slowly. 'An open hand can give. An open hand can receive. An open hand can let go.' Say the word: 'tyaga' — त्याग. Chant together three times, making the fist-to-open-palm gesture with each chant. Ask: 'Was there a time you gave up something you wanted — and it felt GOOD inside?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Fist/open-palm intro + chant 'tyaga' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud Prince Rama's story; act out taking off silk, putting on bark cloth. 8 min — Read aloud Meera on the Swing. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Let Go or Grab) and Activity 2 (Open Hand Drawing). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce Letting-Go Stone. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with fist-to-open-palm gesture and chant. 15 min — Prince Rama's story with props: silk scarf + piece of bark or brown cloth; children take turns 'letting go' of the scarf. 15 min — Meera's story, re-enacted with a chair as the swing; children practice stepping back with a smile. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 20 min — Last Ladoo Role-Play (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how the heart feels each way. 15 min — Letting-Go Stone craft: each child decorates a small smooth stone with paints/markers. 15 min — Whole-class game: 'The Gentle Swap' — children trade one small thing with a partner and practice opening the hand happily. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE thing they will let go of this week. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn tyaga through the BODY — the fist that opens, the hand that gives, the foot that steps back. Keep it physical. Use the fist-to-open-palm gesture often. Distinguish tyaga from dana carefully: dana = giving a thing to another; tyaga = letting go of the wanting inside me. Both meet in action, but tyaga is the INNER work. Children at this age are strongly possessive (developmentally normal). Praise every small letting-go you see — 'Look how your hand opened. That is tyaga.' Avoid making tyaga feel like LOSS. Always connect it to the lightness that follows — the 'stone falls off the chest' feeling Meera experiences in the modern story. Never shame a child for grabbing; instead model the open palm yourself and offer them a second chance.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Tyaga means I must give up everything I love.' Response: Not at all. Tyaga is letting go of ONE small thing with a happy heart — a turn, a sweet, a need to be first. Krishna does not ask us to lose our joy. He asks us to find a bigger joy by opening our hand.
  • Misconception: 'If I let go grumpily, it still counts as tyaga.' Response: No — tyaga is in the HEART, not only in the hand. If we give up our turn while frowning and muttering, it is not tyaga. We practice the smile first. 'I let go' is the easy part. 'I let go with joy' is real tyaga.
  • Misconception: 'Tyaga means giving up my homework because I do not want to do it.' Response: That is laziness, not tyaga. Tyaga means doing our work WITH full love and then letting go of the reward. We do our homework carefully — not for stickers, but because it is our kartavya (duty). THAT is tyaga.
