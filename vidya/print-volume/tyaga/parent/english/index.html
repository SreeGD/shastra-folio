About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Tyaga

Tyaga means letting go with a peaceful heart. I can give up my turn. I can give up the biggest piece. I can give up winning, so a smaller friend can smile. I can even let go of needing to be right. The hand that opens is bigger than the hand that grabs.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 18.2: The Lord says: Letting go of selfish wanting is one thing. But the wisest teachers call it TYAGA when we do our work with love — and let go of what we get in return.

What Tyaga is NOT

Tyaga is not letting go while feeling angry inside. It is not pretending to share while secretly grumbling. It is not running away from our work because we feel lazy. True tyaga is a happy letting go — quiet and clean, like a flower dropping from a tree.

  • Giving up a turn while stamping your foot and muttering 'It's not fair!'
  • Refusing to do homework because you do not want to — calling it 'giving up'
  • Letting your sister pick first, then reminding everyone all day that you gave up your turn

Stories Your Child Heard

Prince Rama Smiles and Lets Go of the Crown — The whole kingdom of Ayodhya was bright with lamps. Flowers hung from every door. Tomorrow, Prince Rama would be crowned king. Everyone was smiling — the rich, the poor, even the little street children who loved Rama like a brother. Rama slept peacefully that night.

Meera's Last Turn on the Swing — In the evening, the park near Meera's apartment filled with children. There was ONE swing Meera loved — the blue one with the silver chain. It made the best WHOOSH in the wind. Meera had been waiting for twenty whole minutes. Her tummy felt wiggly with excitement. Finally the big boy hopped off.

The Sanskrit Word

tyāga (त्याग) — TYAH-ga

Meaning: Letting go with a happy heart. Giving up what I want, without feeling sad or angry. The open hand.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, carry your Letting-Go Stone. Every day, find ONE moment to let go with a smile — a turn, the biggest piece, a toy, your need to be first, your need to be right. Hold the stone and whisper 'Tyaga'. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa what you let go of today. Whisper, 'Krishna, I let this go for You.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments of happy letting-go, and can demonstrate the fist-to-open-palm gesture while saying 'tyaga' with a smile.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments of happy letting-go, and can demonstrate the fist-to-open-palm gesture while saying 'tyaga' with a smile.

When our hand stays open and our heart stays light, we begin to feel a quiet strength inside — and that strength has its own beautiful name, vairagya.
