About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Adambhitva

Adambhitva means NO showing off.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 13.8

अमानित्वमदम्भित्वमहिंसा क्षान्तिरार्जवम्।
आचार्योपासनं शौचं स्थैर्यमात्मविनिग्रहः॥

amānitvam adambhitvam ahiṁsā kṣāntir ārjavam ācāryopāsanaṁ śaucaṁ sthairyam ātma-vinigrahaḥ

Word-for-word: amānitvam — no big-head feeling; adambhitvam — no show-off, no making a big fuss about my own goodness; ahiṁsā — no hurting; kṣāntiḥ — patience; ārjavam — straight-honest; ācārya-upāsanam — honouring the teacher; śaucam — cleanness; sthairyam — steadiness; ātma-vinigrahaḥ — gentle self-control.

Translation: Krishna teaches Arjuna a list of things that make a heart truly wise. Very near the beginning He says: adambhitvam — do NOT show off. When you do a good thing, don't make a big fuss. When you know something, don't brag to prove you are better. Keep your goodness simple and quiet — like a flower that just blooms, without announcing it.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.19.33-35

अहिंसा सत्यमस्तेयमसङ्गो ह्रीरसञ्चयः।
आस्तिक्यं ब्रह्मचर्यं च मौनं स्थैर्यं क्षमाभयम्॥

ahiṁsā satyam asteyam asaṅgo hrīr asañcayaḥ āstikyaṁ brahmacaryaṁ ca maunaṁ sthairyaṁ kṣamābhayam

Translation: Krishna says to Uddhava: 'The godly heart wears many quiet ornaments — truth, no-stealing, no-clinging, hrīḥ, no-hoarding, faith, self-control, silence, steadiness, patience, fearlessness. These ornaments are not flashy. They are not shown off. They just glow, softly, inside the child whom I love.'

What is Adambhitva?

Adambhitva means NO showing off. When I do something good, I don't need to make a big big show. When I know something, I don't brag to prove I am better than my friends. When Amma praises me, I say 'thank you' and keep playing — I do not run around telling everyone. Adambhitva is the simple child inside me — the one who just DOES good things without needing claps and whistles. Krishna puts this word near the very top of His list in Gita 13.8, because He loves simple unshowy hearts.

What it is NOT

Adambhitva is NOT being sneaky or hiding what I did. I can still tell Amma honestly what I did. I can still show my drawing to teacher when she asks. I can still answer in class when I know the answer. Adambhitva only means I do not DRESS UP my goodness to look bigger than it is. I do not stand on a chair to say 'LOOK AT ME'. I do not keep saying 'I already know that!' to prove I am cleverer than my friends. Simple does NOT mean small — it means just real-size, no puffing up.

  • Standing on the bench at lunch yelling 'I got full marks! Who else got full marks? NO ONE!'
  • When teacher asks a question, waving my hand and shouting 'I know! I know! I KNEW it first!' even when others were going to answer
  • Wearing my best shiny clothes to school every day just so everyone says 'WOW'
  • Doing seva at the temple — but only when Appa is watching, so he will say how good I am

Sanskrit Word Study

adambhitva (अदम्भित्व)

Pronunciation: a-DAMBH-it-va

Meaning: NO show-off. No bragging to prove I am better. Doing good things simply — real-size, not puffed up. Keeping my powers folded, like Hanuman. The a- at the front means 'no', and dambha means 'big show' or 'puffing up'.

Example: When Kavya hid her medal behind Ganesha instead of showing it at school, that was adambhitva.

Story from the Scriptures

Hanuman Keeps His Big Powers Secret — Kishkindha Kanda of the Ramayana (Valmiki)

Long ago in Kishkindha forest lived a band of monkeys. Their minister was a small-looking monkey named Hanuman.

Hanuman had a BIG secret. As a baby, he had jumped UP to bite the sun (he thought it was a mango!). His father Vayu, the wind-god, had blessed him with super-strength. This little monkey could jump over MOUNTAINS, lift ELEPHANTS, run faster than wind.

But did Hanuman show these powers? NO. Every morning he said 'Ram Ram' softly. He carried fruits like the other monkeys. He walked — he did not fly. He sat in the BACK of the monkey-council.

Sometimes the monkeys teased him. 'Hanuman-bhai, you are so small! Useless!' Some boasted: 'I am the strongest!' 'I climb the tallest tree!' Hanuman just smiled. He did not say 'I once jumped to the sun.' He just said 'Ram Ram'.

His mother Anjani had taught him: 'Beta, your powers are for Ram — not for showing off. Keep them folded, like a flag folded away, until Ram needs you to wave it.' So Hanuman folded his powers and waited.

Years passed. Then one day — Lord Rama came. Sita had been stolen by Ravana. Rama needed a helper to leap across the ocean to Lanka.

Sugriva looked at all the loud bragging monkeys. NONE could do it. Their shouting was empty. Then Sugriva remembered — 'Hanuman! That quiet one?'

Hanuman came forward, head bowed. Old wise Jambavan touched his shoulder. 'Hanuman, my child. Now — unfold your flag.'

Hanuman closed his eyes and whispered 'Shri Ram'. He GREW. TALLER. As BIG as the mountain. Hanuman leaped — and flew across the sea to Lanka.

Because his heart had been SIMPLE all those years, his powers were FULL and fresh. When he returned, he sat at the back again, still saying 'Ram Ram'.

Lord Rama hugged him. 'The gift I loved most in you was never your strength. It was your adambhitva — your no-show-off heart. Because you never puffed yourself up, I could fill you all the way up.'

That quiet no-show-off heart has a name. Adambhitva.

A Story from Today

Kavya Hides Her Medal Behind the Ganesha

Kavya was in Standard Three. She loved running. Every morning before anyone woke up, she ran around the park three times. She did not tell her friends. She just ran.

One Saturday, her school sent her to an inter-school race. Kavya touched her heart at the start line. 'Krishna, I am just going to run. You run with me.'

BANG! Kavya ran. Her feet became light. She just felt the cool air whooshing past. And — she came FIRST. Of FORTY children.

A big man put a shiny gold medal around her neck. Amma's eyes filled with tears. 'Kavya-kanna!!'

On the way home the medal bounced on her chest. A little voice whispered: 'Wear this to school on Monday! Put it on Instagram! Tell everyone!'

But a quieter voice said: 'Your medal is YOURS. You ran for Krishna. Keep it for Krishna.'

At home, there was a brass Ganesha on the puja shelf — smiling, round tummy. Kavya unclipped the medal. She put it BEHIND Ganesha, in the shadow.

'Ganapati,' she whispered, 'you keep it. It was never mine.'

On Sunday, cousin Raghu came. 'Kavya-akka! SHOW me the medal!' Kavya almost went to the shelf. Then she smiled. 'The medal is sleeping. Let's play snakes-and-ladders.'

On Monday, Kavya went to school in her normal uniform. During assembly the headmistress announced, 'Kavya of Standard Three came first!' The hall went 'OOOOH!' Kavya's cheeks went pink. She stood, folded her hands, bowed small. 'Thank you, ma'am.' And sat down.

At recess, Meera ran up. 'Kavya! WHERE is the medal?'

Kavya smiled gently. 'Meera — it is safe with Ganapati. You can come see it at my house, quietly.' Meera nodded. 'I like that.'

That night, the medal glinted softly behind Ganesha. Kavya touched her heart.

'Krishna, I did not make a show. The medal is Yours. My heart is clean.'

She felt very light.

That no-show-off feeling has a name. Adambhitva.

Core Teaching

  1. Adambhitva means NO showing off. When I do something good, I don't need a big big drama. I don't need to stand on a chair and shout. I don't need to post a picture. I can just smile, say 'thank you,' and keep going. Krishna places this word near the top of His wise-heart list in Gita 13.8.
  2. Adambhitva means NO bragging to prove I am better. When I know something, I don't need to shout 'I ALREADY KNEW THAT!' to make my friends feel small. Knowing is nice. Making others feel small is NOT nice. A wise child keeps their knowing simple — like Hanuman keeping his super-strength folded away, only for when it is truly needed.
  3. Adambhitva does NOT mean hiding my goodness sneakily. I can still show my drawing to teacher. I can still tell Amma about my good day. I can still answer when the teacher asks. Adambhitva only means I do not puff myself up like a balloon. I stay real-size. Real-size children are the ones Krishna fills with the biggest love — because there is no puff taking up the space.
