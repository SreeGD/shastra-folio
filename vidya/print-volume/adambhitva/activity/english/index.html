About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Adambhitva

Adambhitva means NO showing off.

Worksheet

Adambhitva Worksheet — No Show-Off, Just Real-Size

Activity 1: Show-Off or Simple? (Circle the Right One)

1. I got a sticker for a clean notebook. Do I stand on the bench and yell, or smile and put the sticker on my book cover? SHOW-OFF (bench-yell) SIMPLE (smile)

2. Teacher asks a question. I know the answer. Do I wave wildly and shout 'I KNEW IT FIRST,' or raise my hand softly and wait? SHOW-OFF SIMPLE

3. I helped an old uncle cross the road yesterday. Do I tell EVERY person at school today, or keep it in my heart for Krishna? SHOW-OFF SIMPLE

4. My new shoes are SHINY. Do I put my feet up on the desk so everyone sees, or walk normally and smile? SHOW-OFF SIMPLE

5. I did seva at the temple. Appa is watching. Do I suddenly do it more nicely because he is watching, or the same as always? SHOW-OFF (only when watching) SIMPLE (same always)

Activity 2: Hanuman's Folded Flag

Draw a picture of little Hanuman. Inside his chest, draw a FOLDED UP flag — neatly tucked. In the corner of the page, draw a BIG UNFOLDED flag with the words 'FOR RAM ONLY'. Colour Hanuman in orange and cream. Colour the folded flag gold.

Under the drawing, write: 'Hanuman kept his powers folded up for Ram. I will keep my goodness folded up for Krishna — not for show-offs.'

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me (The Real-Size Chant)

Stand in a small circle. Make yourself SMALL (crouch). Say quietly: 'I am just me.' Stand NORMAL size. Say: 'Real-size. No puffing.' Puff out your cheeks and stretch your arms wide. Say: 'Puffy Kavya? NO!' Shrink back to normal. Smile.

Chant three times, gently:

A-dam-bhi-tva! A-dam-bhi-tva! A-dam-bhi-tva! *Real-size. No show. Just me.*

Activity 4: The Secret Good Deed Game (Paired)

With a partner, whisper ONE good thing you did this week that NO ONE else knows about. Your partner will NOT tell anyone — pinky-promise. Partner whispers one secret good deed back. Both of you bow your heads and say softly: 'Krishna saw. That is enough.'

Now talk: how did it feel to keep something good QUIET? Was it harder than telling? Was it more peaceful?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Invisible Good-Things Jar

At home, find a small empty jar or box. Make it your Invisible Jar. Every day this week, do ONE small good thing that NO ONE notices — tidy a sibling's shoe, water the tulasi without being told, say 'Ram Ram' when no one is listening. Drop a single dried chickpea (or a folded paper bit) into the jar.

Do NOT announce your jar. Do NOT post it. Do NOT compare with friends.

At the end of the week, count the chickpeas quietly — just you, and Krishna. Each one is a little adambhitva seed.

Structured Activities

  • Show-Off or Simple? (solo, 6 min)

    Circle SHOW-OFF or SIMPLE for each scenario. Notice which one Krishna loves.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • Hanuman's Folded Flag (solo, 10 min)

    Draw little Hanuman with a folded flag inside his chest. In the corner, draw a big unfolded flag with the words 'FOR RAM ONLY'. Write the adambhitva promise underneath.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Orange and gold markers

  • The Real-Size Chant (whole_class, 3 min)

    In a circle, shrink small, stand real-size, and puff big to show the three sizes. Chant 'adambhitva' softly three times, ending in real-size.

  • The Secret Good Deed Game (paired, 8 min)

    With a partner, whisper one invisible good thing you did this week. Pinky-promise not to tell anyone else. Both bow and say 'Krishna saw. That is enough.'

  • The Invisible Jar (solo, 5 min)

    Keep a secret jar at home. Each day, do one good thing no one notices; drop one chickpea in. Count quietly with Krishna at week's end.

    Materials: Small jar or box, Dried chickpeas or folded paper bits

Home Practice

For one week, keep an Invisible Good-Things Jar at home. Each day, do ONE small good thing that NO ONE notices — tidy a sibling's shoes without telling, water the tulasi plant quietly, pick up a dropped pencil, say a soft 'Ram Ram' when no one is listening, put your own plate in the sink without being asked. After each invisible good thing, drop ONE small dried chickpea (or a folded bit of paper) into your jar. Do NOT tell friends about the jar. Do NOT post it. Do NOT compete with siblings. Before bed, touch the jar gently and whisper: 'Krishna, You saw. That is enough.'

Duration: One invisible good thing per day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child has at least 5 chickpeas in the jar and can describe at least 3 specific invisible good deeds WITHOUT bragging about them. The child can demonstrate the 'folded flag' gesture (both hands folded together at chest) while saying 'adambhitva' softly. They can explain in their own words why Hanuman did not show his powers until Ram called.
