About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Achapalya

Achapalya means I stay with ONE thing at a time. When I pick up a book, I read a little of it before I put it down. When I start a puzzle, I finish at least one piece before I run away. When I begin a song, I sing it to the end. I do not flap and flit like a moth around a lamp. My heart learns to land — and STAY. Krishna's children are steady children. They are like small quiet trees, not like little falling leaves.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2: Krishna lists the beautiful qualities of divine children. One of them is acāpalam — steadiness. It means: when I start something, I stay. I do not hop from toy to toy, from game to game, from 'yes' to 'no' in one minute. My heart holds on. My hands finish what they begin.

What Achapalya is NOT

Achapalya is not being slow or being a sleepy stone. It is not refusing to play anything new. It is also not being STUCK — if Amma calls for dinner, of course I stop my game. The opposite of achapalya is CHAPALYA — jumping-jumping, flitting-flitting, picking up one toy and dropping it in ten seconds, saying 'yes' and then 'no' and then 'yes' again. A chapala heart is a tired heart. An achapala heart is a full, shining heart.

  • Picking up a crayon, scribbling one line, dropping it, picking up blocks, stacking two, dropping them, running to the window — all in one minute
  • Starting a jigsaw puzzle and quitting when the second piece does not fit
  • Saying 'I want to learn guitar!' then 'I want to learn tabla!' then 'I want to learn football!' in three days
  • Half-eating the apple, half-drinking the milk, half-reading the book — nothing finished, everything begun

Stories Your Child Heard

Hanuman's One-Pointed Leap Across the Ocean — Long ago, Sita-Mata was stolen by Ravana and locked in a garden on the island of Lanka. Lord Rama was very sad. A circle of monkey-warriors sat on the southern shore. 'One hundred yojanas of ocean!' they cried. 'No one can leap that far!'

Tara Finishes the Paper Boat — Tara was seven. She loved drawing, blocks, dolls, books, playdough — ALL AT ONCE. On Saturday morning, Tara woke BUZZING. Drawing book — one dragon-head. 'BORING!' Blocks — four stacks. 'BORING!' Doll — one shoe off. 'BORING!' Book — three words. 'BORING!' Playdough — one squish. 'BORING!'

The Sanskrit Word

acāpalya (अचापल्य) — ah-CHAH-pal-ya

Meaning: Not-flitting. Not-jumping-from-one-thing-to-another. Steadiness. The staying heart. When I begin, I stay. When I pick up, I finish.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your One-Thing Basket on your shelf. Each morning at breakfast, with Amma or Appa, choose ONE small task for the day — finish the puzzle, read the next chapter, water every plant, fold all your socks, colour one drawing fully. Just ONE. Write or draw it on a little card and drop it in the basket. At bedtime, if you FINISHED it, add a small stone or shell to the basket. If you flitted today — no stone, no shame. Try again tomorrow. Before sleeping, whisper: 'Krishna, today I stayed. Thank You for holding my heart.' If you flitted, whisper: 'Krishna, tomorrow I will stay. Help my heart land.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can point to at least 4 stones in the basket, name the 4 finished tasks specifically, and describe the feeling of 'fullness' after finishing (as opposed to the 'empty' feeling of flitting). The child can also demonstrate the small-tree sitting posture and chant 'achapalya' calmly.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can point to at least 4 stones in the basket, name the 4 finished tasks specifically, and describe the feeling of 'fullness' after finishing (as opposed to the 'empty' feeling of flitting). The child can also demonstrate the small-tree sitting posture and chant 'achapalya' calmly.

When our heart has learned to stay with one thing — and then stay with that thing even when it is a little bit hard — that deeper holding-on becomes its own beautiful quality. Krishna calls it dhṛti — the long, patient holding of the heart that carries us through long journeys.
