About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Achapalya

Achapalya means I stay with ONE thing at a time.

Worksheet

Achapalya Worksheet — My Staying Heart

Activity 1: Flit or Stay? (Circle the Right Answer)

1. I start colouring a tiger. After two lines I want to start a new drawing. FLIT STAY (finish the tiger)

2. I am reading a story. Amma calls for dinner. I close the book with a bookmark. FLIT STAY (good stopping)

3. My puzzle has 15 pieces. I have fit 3. I want to give up. FLIT STAY (one more piece)

4. I start a song, get bored at the second line, want a new song. FLIT STAY (finish this one)

5. I begin a letter to Grandmother. I write one line and then run to play. FLIT STAY (finish the letter)

Activity 2: One Thing, One Circle (Drawing Task)

Draw ONE big circle in the middle of this page. Inside the circle, draw ONE thing you will FINISH this week — one drawing, one puzzle, one small chore, one book chapter. Only ONE thing. Not five. Not three. ONE.

Colour it with care. Do not hop to a new idea. Stay with this one.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me (The Staying Chant)

Sit cross-legged, still as a little tree. Hands folded in your lap. Eyes soft.

Chant three times, slowly — ONE word per breath:

A-chā-pal-ya! A-chā-pal-ya! A-chā-pal-ya! *I stay. I stay. I stay.*

Activity 4: The Sitting-Tree Game (Paired)

With a partner, take turns:

  • Partner A sits perfectly still, like a little tree. No fidgeting, no looking around. Partner B counts slowly — one, two, three... up to twenty.
  • Can Partner A stay like a tree till twenty? No flitting eyes, no wiggling shoulders?
  • Switch. Now Partner B is the tree.

Notice: was it hard to stay still? Did your mind want to HOP? Did your eyes want to FLIT? Celebrate every 'twenty' reached. That is achapalya inside the body.

Activity 5: Take Home — The One-Thing Basket

Ask Amma for one small basket. Each morning, choose ONE task for the day — finish the puzzle, read one chapter, water all the plants, colour one picture fully, fold all your socks into pairs. Put a little card in the basket with the task written or drawn on it.

At the end of the day, if you FINISHED that one thing, put a small stone or shell into the basket. If you flitted — no stone today. No shame. Try again tomorrow.

By the end of the week, count your stones. Each stone is a tiny finished thing. Each stone is an achapalya jewel. Whisper to Krishna: 'I stayed today. Thank You for holding my heart steady.'

Structured Activities

  • Flit or Stay (solo, 6 min)

    Circle FLIT or STAY for each scenario to practice recognizing a staying heart versus a flitting heart.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • One Thing, One Circle (solo, 10 min)

    Draw one big circle. Inside, draw the ONE thing you will finish this week. Colour it with care — do not hop to a new idea.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons

  • The Staying Chant (whole_class, 4 min)

    Sit cross-legged like a little tree. Hands folded. Chant 'achapalya' three times, one word per breath.

  • The Sitting-Tree Game (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, take turns sitting perfectly still like a little tree while the other counts to twenty. No flitting eyes, no wiggling shoulders.

    Materials: Open floor space

  • The One-Thing Basket (solo, 5 min)

    Keep a small basket. Each day, choose ONE task. Finish it. Add a stone to the basket. Count the stones at week's end.

    Materials: Small basket or cup, Small stones or shells, Cards

Home Practice

For one week, keep your One-Thing Basket on your shelf. Each morning at breakfast, with Amma or Appa, choose ONE small task for the day — finish the puzzle, read the next chapter, water every plant, fold all your socks, colour one drawing fully. Just ONE. Write or draw it on a little card and drop it in the basket. At bedtime, if you FINISHED it, add a small stone or shell to the basket. If you flitted today — no stone, no shame. Try again tomorrow. Before sleeping, whisper: 'Krishna, today I stayed. Thank You for holding my heart.' If you flitted, whisper: 'Krishna, tomorrow I will stay. Help my heart land.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can point to at least 4 stones in the basket, name the 4 finished tasks specifically, and describe the feeling of 'fullness' after finishing (as opposed to the 'empty' feeling of flitting). The child can also demonstrate the small-tree sitting posture and chant 'achapalya' calmly.
