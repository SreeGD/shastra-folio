About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Raksha

Raksha means protecting — using my hands, my voice, and my love to keep someone smaller or weaker safe.

Worksheet

Raksha Worksheet — Krishna's Little Protector

Activity 1: Help or Walk By? (Circle the Right Answer)

1. A smaller child trips and cries in the playground. HELP WALK BY

2. I see a kitten stuck on a wall and I am late for dinner. HELP WALK BY

3. A plant is knocked over on the classroom shelf. HELP (stand it up) WALK BY

4. A classmate is being called a mean name by other kids. HELP (speak up) WALK BY

5. A baby bird has fallen from a nest onto the grass. HELP (tell a grown-up) WALK BY

Activity 2: Draw Little Krishna Lifting the Hill

In the box below, draw little Krishna (age 7, like you!) holding up Govardhan Hill with His little finger. Under the hill, draw cows, calves, children, and grandmothers — all safe. Draw YOURSELF under the hill too, smiling.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Chant three times, standing tall with your chest up like a little guard:

Rak-SHAA! Rak-SHAA! Rak-SHAA! *I protect the small. I am Krishna's helper!*

Activity 4: The Little Guardian Role-Play (Paired)

With a partner, practice protecting someone smaller. One of you is a 'big helper.' The other pretends to be:

  • A little puppy who is lost
  • A younger brother being teased
  • A plant that is about to be stepped on
  • A classmate who is sitting alone at lunch

The 'big helper' speaks kind, brave words: 'Come with me. You are safe now.' Then switch. Notice how your chest feels when you are the protector — big or small?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Protector Notebook

Get a small notebook. On the cover, draw Krishna's little finger holding up the hill. This is your Protector Notebook. Every night this week, write (or draw) ONE little life you helped today — a sibling, a pet, a plant, an insect, a friend. At bedtime, whisper: 'Krishna, I was Your hands today.'

Structured Activities

  • Help or Walk By (solo, 6 min)

    Circle HELP or WALK BY for each scenario.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • Draw Little Krishna Lifting the Hill (solo, 10 min)

    Draw seven-year-old Krishna holding Govardhan Hill on His little finger. Under the hill, draw cows, calves, children, grandmothers — and yourself.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons

  • Say It With Me (whole_class, 3 min)

    Chant 'raksha' three times standing tall with chest up, like a little guard.

  • The Little Guardian Role-Play (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, take turns protecting — a lost puppy, a bullied classmate, a plant about to be stepped on. Use kind, brave words.

    Materials: Small soft toy or stand-in props

  • The Protector Notebook (solo, 5 min)

    Keep a daily notebook of one little life you protected each day this week. Whisper 'I was Krishna's hands today' at bedtime.

    Materials: Small notebook, Pencil, Crayons

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Protector Notebook. Every day, find ONE little life to protect — a baby sibling, a pet, a plant, an insect, a classmate who is sitting alone. Help, speak up, tell a grown-up if needed. In your notebook, draw or write what you did. At bedtime, hold your little finger up like Krishna's Govardhan finger and whisper: 'Krishna, today I was Your hands. Thank You for sending me this little one.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments of protecting a smaller or weaker life, and can demonstrate the pinky-finger gesture while chanting 'raksha' with a brave smile. The notebook has at least 5 entries.
