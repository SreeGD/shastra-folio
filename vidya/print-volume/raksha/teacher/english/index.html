About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Raksha

Raksha means protecting — using my hands, my voice, and my love to keep someone smaller or weaker safe.

Chapter Overview

Raksha means protecting — using my hands, my voice, and my love to keep someone smaller or weaker safe. I can protect my baby sister. I can protect a little puppy. I can protect a plant. I can stand beside a friend who is being teased. When I protect, I become Krishna's helper in the world. Krishna protects everyone — and I can be one of His helpers.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 9.22 — Krishna says: Those who love Me and remember Me always — I Myself carry what they need and I Myself keep them safe. I am their Protector, and no harm can reach them without passing through Me.

Core Teaching

  1. Raksha means PROTECTING — using my hands, my voice, my body to keep someone smaller or weaker safe. When I see a younger child being left out, a hurt animal, a plant being broken, a friend being teased — I do not look away. I help. That is raksha.
  2. Raksha is doing, not just not-hurting. Ahimsa keeps my own hands gentle. Raksha makes my hands REACH OUT. A person who only says 'I did not hurt anyone' is good. A person who stops the hurt when they see it is a raksha-helper of Krishna.
  3. When little Krishna lifted Govardhan Hill on His tiny finger, He showed us that even a small one can protect a whole village with a big heart. I am small. But my heart can be big. Each time I protect, Krishna is smiling in my heart and lifting the hill with me.

Five-Minute Introduction

Sit in a circle. Hold up your little finger (the pinky). Ask: 'If I told you that with THIS tiny finger, a little boy lifted a whole mountain — would you believe me?' Pause. 'Today we learn about the biggest finger-story in all the world. It is the story of little Krishna, who was seven years old — just like you — and lifted a whole hill to keep His village dry. That word for keeping-safe is RAKSHA.' Chant together, each child holding up their little finger like a tiny mountain-lifter: 'Rak-shaa, Rak-shaa, Rak-shaa.' Then ask: 'Have you ever helped someone smaller than you?' Take 2-3 short answers. Celebrate every one.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Pinky-finger intro + chant 'raksha' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud Krishna and Govardhan Hill; let children stand and 'lift' an imaginary hill with their pinky fingers as you narrate. 8 min — Read aloud Arjun and the Puppy; pause at the moment Arjun chooses to kneel in the rain. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Help or Walk By) + Activity 2 (Draw Krishna lifting the hill). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce the Protector Notebook. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, pinky-finger lift-the-hill gesture, chant 'raksha'. 15 min — Govardhan story with a prop: a large cardboard hill that one child holds up while other children (cows, calves, villagers) shelter underneath. 15 min — Arjun's story, re-enacted with a soft toy puppy; children practice kneeling and lifting gently. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 20 min — Little Guardian Role-Play (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how the chest feels when protecting. 15 min — Protector Shield Craft: each child decorates a small cardboard shield with a picture of Krishna's pinky and the word RAKSHA. 15 min — Biographical story: Rani Lakshmibai as a brave child defending her fort, with pictures. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE little life they will protect this week; teacher seals it by placing the Protector Shield briefly on each child's heart. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children love the Govardhan story because the mathematics of it is impossible and the heart of it is so simple — a child with a tiny finger holds up a whole hill because He loves the cows. Let this image sink deep. Use the pinky-finger gesture often; each time they make it, remind them: 'This is the protector-finger. This is Krishna's finger. This is yours.' Distinguish raksha from ahimsa with the body: a closed gentle hand (ahimsa — not hurting) vs. a reaching-out hand (raksha — helping). Children understand the difference in seconds when shown physically. Watch carefully for the shy child who sees wrong things happening but does not speak — not because they agree, but because they are scared. Praise small acts of raksha they do notice (standing a fallen plant upright, picking up a worm from a puddle). Never shame a child for NOT protecting — the capacity grows slowly. Celebrate when a child simply NOTICES that someone needed help, even if they did not act — noticing is the first step of raksha. Important guideline: always tell children that if a situation feels too big — if someone is in real danger, if an adult is being mean — the bravest raksha is finding a trusted grown-up quickly. 'Running to a grown-up is not running away. It is raksha with two people instead of one.'

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Raksha means I must fight anyone who bothers my friend.' Response: Not at all. Raksha is brave, but not rough. Krishna did not hit Indra — He simply lifted the hill. Raksha is usually quiet — kneeling to help a puppy, speaking up with kind strong words, telling a grown-up when a friend is hurt. Fighting is a last and rare thing.
  • Misconception: 'I am too small to protect anyone.' Response: Look at little Krishna — seven years old, tiny hands, and He lifted a whole HILL. Look at you — you can protect an ant, a flower, a baby sister, a puppy. Size does not decide the heart. Krishna Himself lives inside your heart and helps you lift whatever needs lifting.
  • Misconception: 'Raksha and ahimsa are the same thing.' Response: They are related, but different. Ahimsa means my own hands do not hurt anyone. Raksha means my hands DO something when I see someone being hurt. Ahimsa is the quiet rule. Raksha is the brave action. Krishna's little finger was ahimsa (it did not hurt) AND raksha (it lifted the hill) — both at the same time.
