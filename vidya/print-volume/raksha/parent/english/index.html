About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Raksha

Raksha means protecting — using my hands, my voice, and my love to keep someone smaller or weaker safe. I can protect my baby sister. I can protect a little puppy. I can protect a plant. I can stand beside a friend who is being teased. When I protect, I become Krishna's helper in the world. Krishna protects everyone — and I can be one of His helpers.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.22: Krishna says: Those who love Me and remember Me always — I Myself carry what they need and I Myself keep them safe. I am their Protector, and no harm can reach them without passing through Me.

What Raksha is NOT

Raksha is not only about NOT hurting — it is about DOING something when someone needs help. It is not turning my face away. It is not saying 'not my problem.' It is not being bossy or squeezing someone in the name of keeping them safe. Raksha is gentle, brave, and ready.

  • Walking past a classmate being laughed at and saying nothing
  • Watching a friend step on a tiny kitten and not speaking up
  • Saying 'I am protecting you' but really controlling a younger sibling

Stories Your Child Heard

Krishna Lifts Govardhan Hill to Shelter the Whole Village — In a green village called Vrindavan, little Krishna was seven years old. He loved the cows and the cowherd boys with all His heart. One year, Krishna asked the villagers to do a special puja — not for Indra the rain-king, but for Govardhan Hill, who gave them grass, water, and shade.

Arjun and the Puppy in the Rain — Arjun was walking home from school. He was in Standard Two. It was starting to rain. The other children ran ahead, splashing. Arjun walked slower. He liked to look at things.

The Sanskrit Word

rakṣā (रक्षा) — RAHK-shaa

Meaning: Protection. Keeping someone smaller or weaker safe with brave, gentle hands. Being Krishna's helper in the world.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Protector Notebook. Every day, find ONE little life to protect — a baby sibling, a pet, a plant, an insect, a classmate who is sitting alone. Help, speak up, tell a grown-up if needed. In your notebook, draw or write what you did. At bedtime, hold your little finger up like Krishna's Govardhan finger and whisper: 'Krishna, today I was Your hands. Thank You for sending me this little one.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments of protecting a smaller or weaker life, and can demonstrate the pinky-finger gesture while chanting 'raksha' with a brave smile. The notebook has at least 5 entries.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments of protecting a smaller or weaker life, and can demonstrate the pinky-finger gesture while chanting 'raksha' with a brave smile. The notebook has at least 5 entries.

When our hands protect with love, we become brave without being rough — and that brave, gentle strength has its own beautiful name: saurya.
