About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

धृति
Dhriti

Dhriti is the quiet hand that keeps holding.

Chapter Overview

Dhriti is the quiet hand that keeps holding. When I started something bright and happy, and then the middle got long and tired — dhriti is the hand inside me that says, 'Keep going, little one. Just one more line. Just one more day.' Dhriti does not shout. Dhriti does not run. Dhriti only STAYS. Krishna's own hand is inside my dhriti, helping me hold on.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 18.33 — Krishna says: The steady hand that keeps holding — that holds the mind quiet, the breath calm, and the senses kind, without wandering — THAT steady hand is the good kind. Its name is dhṛti.

Core Teaching

  1. Dhriti is the quiet hand that keeps holding. Udyama is the bright beginning. But dhriti is what comes AFTER the sparkle — when the middle gets long and tired. A quiet hand that stays is stronger than a loud hand that runs.
  2. Krishna says in the Gita — sāttvikī dhṛti holds the mind quiet, the breath calm, and the senses kind, without wandering. This is not stubbornness. This is gentle, steady staying — because the work is good, because Krishna is watching, because love asks us to hold on.
  3. Dhriti grows one small day at a time. Five minutes on a tired day is more precious than thirty minutes on an excited day. Bharata held the sandals for 5,110 days. Kavya held the sitar for thirty mornings. Your quiet hand can hold anything — if you let Krishna hold it with you.

Five-Minute Introduction

Hold up a small candle with a steady flame. Ask: 'Children, which is stronger — a firework or a candle?' They will shout 'Firework!' Smile and say: 'The firework is LOUD and BRIGHT — for three seconds. Then it is gone. But this candle will still be shining in five minutes, in ten minutes, in thirty minutes. The candle has a quiet strength. We have a special name for that quiet strength — dhṛti — धृति.' Light a small diya if possible. Ask: 'Have you ever started something fun, and then it got long and boring, and you wanted to quit? What did you do?' Take 2-3 short answers with gentle smiles. Then say: 'Today we will meet the quiet hand inside us that keeps holding — not like a firework, but like this little flame.' Chant slowly, palms up on knees: 'Dhṛ-tih! Dhṛ-tih! Dhṛ-tih!' three times with breath.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Candle vs. firework demo + Dhriti chant 3x with open palms. 10 min — Read aloud Bharata and the Sandals; children mime bowing to the sandals at the throne. 8 min — Read aloud Kavya and the Thirty-Day Sitar; pause at day ten when she wants to quit; ask the class to suggest what she should do. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Keep Going or Give Up) + Activity 2 (Draw Bharata's Sandals). 5 min — Closing chant + hand out thirty-tick cards. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with a small diya placed in the centre. Chant Dhṛ-tih 3x with open palms; ask 'what is one thing you promised and kept?' 15 min — Bharata's story acted out with a small cushion 'throne' and two wooden slippers; each child takes a turn bowing to the sandals. 15 min — Kavya's story; pause at day ten; children discuss in pairs 'what would YOU tell Kavya?' 15 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Build a Classroom Candle Ring: each child lights (or colours) one paper candle; the ring stands for the whole class's dhriti. 15 min — Biographical story: Dr. Homi Bhabha's long research; children mime 'ten thousand patient steps.' 15 min — Hand out and personalise the thirty-tick cards; each child chooses ONE daily practice and writes it on the card. 15 min — Closing: the Sandal Diary instruction; each child names their 'altar object.' Buffer: 5 min.

Discussion Guide

Dhriti is the most important bridge value in the primary curriculum — it connects the BRIGHT start (udyama) with the CAREFUL practice (daksya) and the FINISHED work later in life. Primary children experience dhriti in their BODY — the slow breath, the open palms on the knees, the candle-flame steadiness. Use candles, flames, and gentle gestures as much as possible. Avoid abstract language. Never lecture about perseverance; show it. Model it yourself — arrive to class on the tired days with the same quiet smile. Notice two kinds of children: (1) the BRIGHT STARTER who loves day one but vanishes by day ten — for this child, celebrate the middle, never just the beginning; say 'Day eleven! Your dhriti is holding!' (2) the QUIET PLODDER who never sparkles but always shows up — this child already has dhriti; name it, honour it, let them hear the word for what they already do. Watch carefully for children confusing dhriti with stubbornness — the child who keeps doing a WRONG maths method for forty minutes because they 'promised.' Gently redirect: 'Little one, dhriti is for GOOD holding. If the ship is sinking, we get off the ship. If the song is wrong, we sing it a new way. Dhriti is kind and wise.' Do NOT shame the child who breaks a thirty-day promise. Say: 'Today your dhriti fell. Tomorrow your dhriti gets up again. That is also dhriti — the getting up.' Connect dhriti explicitly to bhakti — the quiet hand is Krishna's own hand inside ours. Whenever a child finishes a long practice, have them bow gently and whisper 'For You, Krishna.' That single gesture anchors the whole value.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Dhriti means I must never stop — even if my work is wrong or hurtful.' Response: No, that is stubbornness. Dhriti is different. Dhriti holds on to GOOD things — kind habits, daily prayer, promises we made to Amma, practising our music. Krishna says in the Gita — sāttvikī dhṛti holds the mind QUIET and the senses KIND. If our work is hurting someone or hurting us, we need viveka — wisdom — to know when to stop. Dhriti + wisdom = true strength.
  • Misconception: 'I must feel excited every day, or my dhriti is broken.' Response: The opposite is true. Excitement is udyama — the bright beginning. Dhriti is for the TIRED days. Bharata was tired too. Kavya was tired on day ten. Krishna is closest on the tired days — because that is when the quiet hand really shows itself. A five-minute practice on a tired day is WORTH MORE than a thirty-minute practice on an excited day.
  • Misconception: 'Dhriti means never resting — always pushing and pushing.' Response: No, no. Rest is sweet and good. Krishna is in our rest too. Dhriti is about RETURNING gently — sleeping well, waking up, and putting our quiet hand back on the work the next morning. A candle does not burn itself up in one second. It glows slowly, with oil and patience. That is dhriti.
  • Misconception: 'Dhriti is a grown-up word for grown-up problems. I am too small.' Response: Not true. Every time you finish a picture, water your plant three mornings in a row, or sing your bhajan even on a sleepy Sunday — that is dhriti. Small children have BEAUTIFUL dhriti — Bharata was only seventeen when he started holding Rama's sandals. You are practising right now, just by listening to the end of this story.
