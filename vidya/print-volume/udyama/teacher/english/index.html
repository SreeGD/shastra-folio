About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

CHAPTER 1
उद्यम
Udyama

Udyama means bright-eyed beginning.

Chapter Overview

Udyama means bright-eyed beginning. It is the happy hop inside me when I say 'Yes! I will try!' I do not wait to be pushed. I do not sit and sulk. I roll up my sleeves, I smile, and I begin. If I stumble, I get up and try again. If the work is big, I break it small and start with the first piece. Udyama is the sunrise inside my chest.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 6.24 — Krishna says: Begin your work with bright eyes and a strong wish (sankalpa). Step by step, with quiet determination (niścayena), keep going. Even if your feet are small, a strong sankalpa can climb a mountain.

Core Teaching

  1. Udyama means I begin my work with bright eyes and a happy heart. I do not wait to be pushed. I do not sit and sulk. When Amma says 'Let's do homework,' I say 'Yes!' When teacher says 'Let's line up,' I hop up. The first step is the most important step — and it is mine to take.
  2. Udyama is the one-more-try heart. When my arrow wobbles, I pick it up and try again. When my drawing goes wrong, I turn the paper over and start again. When I fall, I get up. The Gita says — niścayena — with quiet determination — step by step, udyama climbs every mountain.
  3. Udyama is not for a prize. It is because there is a little sun inside my chest that wants to shine. I work happily. I help happily. I begin happily. When the work is done, I do not need stickers — my heart is already lit up from inside. Krishna smiles when He sees that little sun in me.

Five-Minute Introduction

Stand in a circle. Ask the children: 'What happens in the morning when the SUN comes up?' Let them answer — the sky goes pink, birds sing, flowers open, we wake up. Now say: 'There is a little sun that comes up INSIDE us too. When we feel it, our eyes go bright. Our feet want to hop. Our hands want to DO something. That little inside-sun has a name — udyama.' Chant together, hopping on each beat, eyes wide: 'Ud-ya-MA! Ud-ya-MA! Ud-ya-MA!' Place one hand on the chest. Ask: 'Has your little sun ever woken you up in the morning? Did you hop out of bed — or did you snooze?' Take 2-3 short answers with smiles, never shame. Celebrate every hop.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Sun-inside intro + bright-eyes hopping chant 'udyama' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud Lava and Kusha's story; children mime pulling tiny bows and crying 'one more try!' after each imagined flop. 8 min — Read aloud Raghav and the Garden; pause at the tick-tick-tick drumming in Raghav's chest. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Begin or Wait) + Activity 2 (Little Sun Drawing). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce Sunrise Journal take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with sun-inside gesture and bright-eyes chant. 15 min — Lava and Kusha's story with props: a tiny twig 'bow' per child, a paper arrow; children practise 'wobble, flop, one more try' together. 15 min — Raghav's story, re-enacted with a classroom flowerpot; children take turns kneeling and pretending to dig with a spoon. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Paired One-More-Try Game (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how the heart feels when a friend cheers us on. 15 min — Seed-Planting Craft: each child plants one real coriander or methi seed in a paper cup (soil, seed, water, label with their name and a little sun). 10 min — Biographical story: Bachendri Pal climbing every step of Everest; children mime one step at a time in place. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE thing they will BEGIN this week without waiting to be told; teacher hands out Sunrise Journals. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn udyama through the BODY — the hop, the wide eyes, the raised hand, the rolled-up sleeve. Use these physical cues every class. Distinguish udyama carefully from two anti-patterns: (1) frantic ego-trying ('I must win!') — this is not udyama but rājasika over-striving, recognizable by the tight brow and loud voice; (2) robotic duty-doing without joy — this is work without a heart, recognizable by the slumped shoulders. True udyama has BOTH warmth and willingness. Watch for the shy or anxious child who does not begin because they are scared of failing. Do not push them into volunteering first; instead, praise the TINY starts — 'You opened your book — that is udyama!' 'You picked up the crayon — that is udyama!' The little sun grows with sunshine, not with storm. Be extra careful with children who carry 'lazy' as an identity — never repeat the label. Instead, say 'Your little sun was sleeping today. Tomorrow we will wake it together with a song.' Most importantly — let children see you model udyama yourself. Arrive early. Begin class cheerfully. Try a new thing aloud. Say 'one more try' when YOU slip. The classroom full of little suns begins with one steady adult sun.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Udyama means I must be FIRST — first in line, first to finish, first to win.' Response: No, no. Udyama is about the beginning INSIDE, not the winning outside. A quiet child who begins their work happily has udyama. A loud child who rushes to be first because they want a prize does not have udyama — they have worry. The little sun shines for its own joy, not for a medal.
  • Misconception: 'If I fall or fail, my udyama is broken.' Response: That is the BEST time to feel udyama! Udyama is the one-more-try heart. Lava and Kusha's first arrows fell after two steps. Raghav's first seed took five days to peek up. Every fall is a new chance for the little sun to say 'Let's begin AGAIN.' Udyama does not break when we stumble. It only breaks when we stop trying.
  • Misconception: 'I need a reward to have udyama — a sticker, a sweet, a clap.' Response: No. A reward is lovely, but udyama lives WITHOUT a reward. The Gita calls this sāttvika effort — we work because the work itself is good, because our heart wants to, because Krishna is watching and smiling. If the sticker is gone, the udyama stays. If no one sees, the udyama still shines. That is the REAL little sun.
  • Misconception: 'Lazy children just need to be scolded more.' Response: (For teacher/parent) Never shame a slow-starting child. Udyama is not grown by scolding — it is grown by showing. Let them see YOU begin with a smile. Let them see you say 'One more try!' to yourself. Let them catch you humming as you sweep. The little sun is CAUGHT, not taught.
