About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Titiksha

Titiksha means bearing little troubles with a quiet heart.

Worksheet

Titiksha Worksheet — My Quiet Brave Heart

Activity 1: Visitors at the Door (Pictures)

Krishna says that hot, cold, happy, sad are all little visitors. They come. They stay a little. They go.

Draw four doors. In each doorway, draw a tiny visitor:

1. Hot Sun Visitor — a yellow smiley sun

2. Cold Wind Visitor — a blue shivery cloud

3. Mean Word Visitor — a little grey cloud with a frown

4. Long Wait Visitor — a purple clock

Under each door, write: 'Welcome, visitor. You will go soon. I will bear you quietly.' That is titiksha!

Activity 2: The One-Step-More Walk

With your teacher's help, walk slowly in a line around the classroom or playground. Count your steps.

Every five steps, stop. Whisper: 'Ti-tik-sha. One more step.' Then take five more. Whisper again.

Do this ten times. Feel how a long walk becomes small when we break it into gentle steps.

(The Pandavas walked through forests for TWELVE YEARS like this — one step, one breath, one Krishna.)

Activity 3: The Titiksha Chant

Sit cross-legged. Place both hands on your knees. Close your eyes softly.

Inhale slowly: Ti-tik... (4 counts) Exhale slowly: ...sha (4 counts)

Repeat five times.

Then open your eyes and whisper three times: Ti-tik-sha. Ti-tik-sha. Ti-tik-sha.

*Quiet heart. Brave heart. Krishna-heart.*

Activity 4: Match the Small Trouble to the Titiksha Response (Paired)

With a partner, match these troubles to the right titiksha response:

| Small Trouble | Titiksha Response | |---|---| | 1. The bus is late | a. I sip some water, remember Krishna, and keep walking | | 2. Sun is hot on my walk | b. I feel hurt, but I stay kind and tell a grown-up | | 3. My friend says a mean word | c. I take a slow breath and wait quietly | | 4. I fell and scraped my knee | d. I let Amma clean it, and I don't shout |

(Answers: 1-c, 2-a, 3-b, 4-d)

Activity 5: Take Home — My Titiksha Jar

Ask Amma or Appa for a small empty jar. Beside your bed, keep a bowl of small dry lentils or pebbles.

Every evening, think about the day. For EACH small trouble you bore quietly — the hot class, the long wait, the scraped knee, the mean word — drop ONE pebble into the jar.

Whisper: 'Krishna, I bore this quietly for You.'

At the end of the week, count your pebbles. Each one is a tiny piece of titiksha. Krishna is smiling inside.

Structured Activities

  • Visitors at the Door (solo, 10 min)

    Draw four doors with tiny visitors — hot sun, cold wind, mean word, long wait. Under each, write 'Welcome, visitor. You will go soon.' Practice welcoming and releasing.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons

  • The One-Step-More Walk (whole_class, 10 min)

    Walk slowly around the classroom. Every five steps, pause and whisper 'Ti-tik-sha. One more step.' Feel how a long walk becomes small when broken into gentle steps.

  • The Titiksha Chant (whole_class, 5 min)

    Sit cross-legged, hands on knees. Inhale 4 counts on 'ti-tik', exhale 4 counts on 'sha'. Repeat five times. Then whisper 'Ti-tik-sha' three times.

  • Match the Small Trouble (Paired) (paired, 8 min)

    With a partner, match each small trouble (bus late, hot walk, mean word, scraped knee) to the titiksha response. Discuss: what makes it a SMALL trouble vs a big one?

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • The Titiksha Jar (Take Home) (solo, 5 min)

    Each evening, drop one pebble into your jar for every small trouble you bore quietly today. Whisper 'Krishna, I bore this quietly for You.' Count pebbles at week's end.

    Materials: Small jar, Small pebbles or dry lentils

Home Practice

For one week, keep a small Titiksha Jar beside your bed. Beside it, keep a little bowl with small pebbles or dry lentils. Every evening before sleep, think about the day. For EACH small trouble you bore quietly — a hot classroom, a long wait in the car, a mean word from a friend, a scraped knee, a tired walk — drop ONE pebble into your jar. Whisper: 'Krishna, I bore this quietly for You.' In the morning, touch the jar gently and whisper: 'Ti-tik-sha. Today I will bear small things with a quiet heart.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific 'small troubles' they bore quietly, the Titiksha Jar contains at least 5-7 pebbles, and the child spontaneously uses the word titiksha when meeting a small hardship ('That is okay, Amma — titiksha'). Parents notice fewer tantrums at transitions (waiting, heat, minor disappointments).
