About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

तितिक्षा
Titiksha

Titiksha means bearing little troubles with a quiet heart.

Chapter Overview

Titiksha means bearing little troubles with a quiet heart. When the sun is hot on my head, when my legs are tired from a long walk, when a friend says a mean word, when I must wait a long time for my turn — I can stay steady. I do not fall apart. I do not cry and shout over small things. I remember Krishna, take a slow breath, and say inside, 'This will pass. I can bear it.' Titiksha is a soft, brave patience that belongs to every child of Krishna.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 2.14 — Krishna says: O Arjuna, hot and cold, pleasure and pain — these touches come and go like little visitors. They never stay. They are not forever. So, My dear friend — just bear them quietly. This quiet bearing is called titiksha.

Core Teaching

  1. Titiksha means I can bear little troubles quietly. Hot sun, long walk, empty tummy, mean word, long wait — these come and go like visitors. They never stay. So I take a breath, remember Krishna, and keep going.
  2. Krishna says in the Bhagavad-gita — mātrā-sparśāḥ. The touches of the world come and go. They are not forever. A brave child does not fall apart at every small hard thing. A brave child says, 'This will pass. I can bear it. Krishna is with me.'
  3. Titiksha is not pretending nothing hurts. Feelings are real. But titiksha is a gentle inner strength — like a little tree bending in the wind and not breaking. When we bear small troubles with a quiet heart, Krishna comes close, rishis come close, and our own heart grows tall.

Five-Minute Introduction

Gather children in a circle. Show them a small cup of ice and a small cup of warm water. Let each child touch one finger to the ice, then to the warm water. Ask: 'Is the cold feeling forever? Is the hot feeling forever?' Children will say no — the feeling goes. Smile. 'Exactly! Krishna tells us in the Gita — hot and cold, happy and sad, they come and go like little visitors. They never stay. Today we learn a beautiful word — TITIKSHA. It means bearing small troubles with a quiet, brave heart.' Place palms at heart. Chant together slowly, three times: 'Ti-tik-sha. Ti-tik-sha. Ti-tik-sha.' Breathe in on ti-tik, breathe out on sha. Ask: 'Can you remember one time something was hard, but you stayed steady?' Take 2-3 short answers and celebrate each one.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Ice-and-warm-water demo + titiksha chant. 10 min — Read aloud Yudhishthira in the Forest; children mime walking through hot forest. 8 min — Read aloud Arnav and the Long Hot Afternoon; pause at 'one more step' and invite children to whisper along. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Visitors at the Door) + Activity 3 (Titiksha Chant breath practice). 5 min — One-Step-More Walk around the classroom + introduce the Titiksha Jar. 2 min — Line up quietly (itself a titiksha practice).

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with ice-warm-water demo and group chant. 15 min — Yudhishthira story with forest props (dry leaves, a bark-cloth piece to touch); children sit as 'quiet Pandavas' while teacher narrates. 15 min — Arnav's story re-enacted with paper sun-rays, children walk a pretend path around the room, whispering 'one more step'. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1, 2, and 4 (Visitors drawing + One-Step-More Walk + Matching). 15 min — Biographical story: Baba Raghav Das and the Plague Year; quiet reflection afterward. 15 min — Titiksha Craft: each child makes a small clay or paper Titiksha Jar to take home, decorates it with a sun, a cloud, and a tiny Krishna flute. 15 min — Paired Breath Practice (Activity 3) — children sit in pairs, back-to-back, breathing 'ti-tik-sha' together. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE small trouble they will bear quietly this week; teacher hands out pebbles for the home jar. 5 min — Final chant 'ti-tik-sha' three times with palms at heart, and bow.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn titiksha best through BODY experience, not lecture. Use the ice-and-warm-water demo. Let them feel the cold, feel the warm, then feel the room temperature — proving that sensations come and go. Use the 'one more step' walk often during transitions — to the lunch hall, to assembly, to the bus. Transitions are real moments for small titiksha. Watch for three types of children and their needs: (1) The tantrum-thrower who falls apart at every small hurt — this child needs soft compassion first ('Yes, it is hard, sweetheart') THEN the gentle invitation ('Can we take one deep breath together? Can we take one more step?'). Never shame. (2) The stoic-silent child who stuffs feelings down 'to be tough' — this child needs permission to name feelings ('It IS hot. It IS hard. That is okay to say.'). Titiksha is NOT stuffing. (3) The show-off child who loudly announces their own bravery — gently redirect: 'Krishna sees it, beta. You don't have to tell everyone. Quiet titiksha is the sweetest.' Always distinguish titiksha from danger. Use the word 'small troubles' repeatedly. For a scraped knee, titiksha; for a broken arm, see the doctor. For a mean word, titiksha; for repeated bullying, tell a grown-up. This clarity protects children. Most importantly, model titiksha yourself. If the AC breaks, if a child spills water, if the class is stuck waiting — let them see your quiet breath, your small smile, your 'okay, we can bear this.' Children catch titiksha from a steady teacher.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Titiksha means I must NEVER say anything hurts.' Response: Not true. Feelings are real. You CAN say 'I feel hot, I feel tired, I feel sad' — that is honest. Titiksha is not hiding feelings. It is feeling them AND staying steady, not falling into a tantrum. Arnav felt hot. Yudhishthira felt tired. They both said so — softly. Then they kept going with Krishna inside.
  • Misconception: 'Titiksha means I should stay in danger or let people hurt me.' Response: Absolutely NOT. If the sun is too hot, go to the shade. If someone is unkind, tell a grown-up. If you are hurt, ask for help. Titiksha is for SMALL troubles — the bearable ones. For big troubles and unsafe things, we ask for help. A brave heart knows the difference between a small visitor and real danger.
  • Misconception: 'Titiksha is showing off how tough I am.' Response: Proud-tough is not titiksha. Quiet-brave is titiksha. Krishna does not want 'Look how strong I am!' children. He wants 'Krishna is inside me, so I can bear this' children. The difference is in the heart. Show-off titiksha is really just pride wearing a brave coat. Real titiksha is soft — like warm stone, not cold stone.
  • Misconception: 'If I complained once, I have lost my titiksha forever.' Response: The opposite! Every time we feel the urge to shout and we choose to breathe instead, titiksha grows. If we slipped yesterday, today we try again. Titiksha is a little seed — it grows each time we water it with a small brave choice. One wilted day does not kill the seed.
