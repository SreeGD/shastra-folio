About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

विवेक
Viveka

Viveka means stopping to think before we act.

Chapter Overview

Viveka means stopping to think before we act. We ask our heart two small questions: 'Is this true?' and 'Is this good for me and others?' Viveka is the little voice inside that helps us tell real from pretend, and right from wrong. It is the swan inside us who can drink the milk and leave the water.

The Verse: Katha Upanishad 1.2.2 — Two things come to a child: what is GOOD and what is FUN-right-now. The wise child stops, thinks, and chooses the good. The hurrying child grabs the fun and forgets to think.

Core Teaching

  1. Viveka means STOP and THINK before we act. The fast-feeling wants us to grab and run. The wise-feeling says, 'Wait. Breathe. Then choose.' Between what we see and what we do — put a little quiet space.
  2. Ask two small questions: 'Is this TRUE?' and 'Is this GOOD for my heart?' If the answer is yes to both, we go ahead. If not, we choose again. Two questions, gentle and simple — that is our little swan inside.
  3. Sometimes shreyas (what is good) is harder than preyas (what is fun-now). The wise child chooses the good anyway. Krishna smiles inside the heart of every child who thinks before they act.

Five-Minute Introduction

Bring a small bowl of water mixed with milk (or a clear cup where you can point: 'milk' and 'water'). Gather the children in a circle. Say: 'Our grandmothers say the swan — hamsa — has a special power. When you give it milk mixed with water, the swan drinks ONLY the milk and leaves the water behind. Isn't that amazing?' Pause. 'Today we learn a word that means the same thing INSIDE our hearts. That word is viveka.' Chant together, one hand on heart: 'Viveka — stop, think, choose the true.' Three times. Then ask: 'When did YOU stop and think before doing something this week?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Swan story + chant. 10 min — Read Nachiketa and the Two Gifts; show two 'gift boxes' (one sparkly, one plain) and ask which is shreyas. 8 min — Read Meera and the Answer Sheet; pause at the moment of temptation and ask the class 'what would you do?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Shreyas or Preyas) and Activity 2 (Thinking Swan drawing). 5 min — Introduce the Pause Pebble take-home + closing chant. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle + milk-and-water swan demonstration + chant. 15 min — Nachiketa's story told slowly with three 'gift boxes' (one filled with gold paper, one with horses, one with a small bright diya); act out Nachiketa choosing. 15 min — Meera and the Answer Sheet with role-play — one child is the 'fast-feeling', one is the 'wise-feeling', one is Meera. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Paired Two Questions Game (Activity 4) with rotation through three partners. 15 min — Make Pause Pebbles — each child decorates a small stone with one word of viveka (STOP, THINK, CHOOSE, TRUE, KIND, SWAN). 15 min — The Swan Dance: slow movements mimicking a swan sorting milk from water; pair with the chant. 10 min — Closing: each child shares one situation where they will use viveka this week. 5 min — Line up and send home. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children build viveka through simple BINARY choices with clear moral weight — the fork-in-the-road image, the swan separating milk from water, the two gifts. Keep language concrete: 'shreyas = good for my heart' and 'preyas = fun right now.' Avoid abstract words like 'discrimination' (it confuses at this age). Use physical anchors: hand on heart, one breath, two questions. Watch for the impulsive child and for the over-anxious child. For the impulsive one, give the Pause Pebble as a gentle tool. For the over-anxious one, reassure that viveka is a friendly helper — not a judge. Never shame a child for making an unwise choice; instead invite: 'Let us run it through the two questions now, together.' Celebrate every pause, even when the choice afterward is wrong. The pause is the practice.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Viveka means always saying NO to fun things.' Response: Not at all. Viveka means asking 'is this true? is this good for my heart?' Many fun things pass both tests — playing, laughing, eating sweets in the right amount. Viveka is not a 'no' machine; it is a wise friend who helps us say yes to what is good and no only to what hurts.
  • Misconception: 'If I take time to think, everyone will think I am slow.' Response: The quickest hand is not the wisest hand. Real strength is in the pause. Even one breath is enough to check the two questions. Great heroes like Nachiketa and Arjuna took time to think — and became the wisest of all.
  • Misconception: 'Viveka is only for grown-ups.' Response: Your little swan is already inside you. Even a small child can ask 'is this true?' and 'is this good?' The muscle of viveka grows by using it. Each time you pause and choose the good, your little swan grows a brighter feather.
