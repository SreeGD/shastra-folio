About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Daya

Daya means caring when you see someone hurt or sad.

Chapter Overview

Daya means caring when you see someone hurt or sad. It makes you want to help them feel better.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.2 — Kindness, truth, no anger, giving, peace, not finding fault, caring for all living beings, not being greedy, being gentle, being shy, and staying strong.

Core Teaching

  1. When we see someone hurt or sad, our heart wants to help them feel better.
  2. Being kind to all living things makes us feel happy and warm inside.
  3. Helping others is more important than just having fun for ourselves.

Five-Minute Introduction

Show children a picture of someone crying or hurt. Ask: 'How do you think this person feels? What would you want to do?' Explain that the feeling that makes us want to help is called daya. It's like having a caring heart that feels sad when others are sad and wants to make them feel better. Tell them they'll hear two stories about children who showed daya and learn how they can show it too.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

Opening circle (5 min) → Epic story (8 min) → Discussion (7 min) → Modern story (8 min) → Craft activity (10 min) → Closing circle (2 min)

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

Opening circle (10 min) → Epic story with acting (20 min) → Discussion (15 min) → Modern story (15 min) → Craft activity (25 min) → Animal friends game (15 min) → Songs and movement (10 min) → Reflection and sharing (10 min)

Discussion Guide

1. Question: What made Krishna stop playing to help the bird? Sample response: He heard the bird crying and felt sad for it, so he wanted to help. Facilitation: Help children connect feelings to actions - when we feel sad for others, we want to help them. 2. Question: How did Maya feel after helping the butterfly? Sample response: She felt happy and warm inside, and her friends wanted to help too. Facilitation: Emphasize that helping others makes us feel good and can inspire others to be kind too. 3. Question: When is it hard to show daya? Sample response: When we're busy playing or when we're upset about something. Facilitation: Acknowledge that being kind isn't always easy, but it's always worth it.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Children think daya means you have to give away all your things. Response: Explain that daya is about caring and helping, not about giving away everything. Sometimes helping means sharing, but sometimes it just means being kind with words or actions.
  • Misconception: Some children think only grown-ups can show real daya. Response: Show them that children can show daya in many ways - comforting a friend, being gentle with animals, or helping someone who is hurt. Their caring actions matter just as much.
