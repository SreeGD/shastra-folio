About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Daya

Daya means caring when you see someone hurt or sad. It makes you want to help them feel better.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2: Kindness, truth, no anger, giving, peace, not finding fault, caring for all living beings, not being greedy, being gentle, being shy, and staying strong.

What Daya is NOT

Daya is not being mean to others when you are upset or angry.

  • Being mean when upset
  • Not caring when others cry
  • Hurting animals or friends

Stories Your Child Heard

The Kind Prince and the Crying Bird — Little Krishna was playing with his friends in the forest. They were having so much fun running and laughing together. Suddenly, Krishna heard a tiny sound. "Cheep! Cheep!" It was very sad. Because Krishna had a kind heart, he stopped playing to listen.

Maya and the Hurt Butterfly — Maya was playing in her garden when her friends called to her. "Maya! Come quick! We have a new game to play!" Maya started to run to her friends. But then she saw something that made her stop. A beautiful butterfly was on the ground. One of its wings was torn.

The Sanskrit Word

dayā (दया) — dah-YAH

Meaning: caring, kindness, wanting to help when someone is hurt

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each day, look for one person or animal that needs help. Do one small kind thing for them - give a hug, share food, or say kind words.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice that helping others makes you feel happy and warm in your heart, and others will smile more around you.

Signs of Growth

You will notice that helping others makes you feel happy and warm in your heart, and others will smile more around you.
