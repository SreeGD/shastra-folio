About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Mardava

Mārdava means GENTLENESS. It is soft hands — I hold my baby brother like he is a flower. It is a soft voice — I speak to my friend like I am singing a lullaby. It is a soft heart — when I see a little puppy shiver, my heart melts like butter in the sun. Mārdava is Krishna's own quality. When baby Krishna touched Yashoda's cheek, His tiny fingers were so soft that her heart melted. That melting is mārdava.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2: Krishna says: These soft and shining qualities belong to the children of light — not hurting, truthfulness, not getting angry, letting go, quiet peace, kind speech, kindness to every living being, no greed, and — mārdava — gentleness. Gentleness is soft hands, soft voice, and a soft heart.

What Mardava is NOT

Mārdava is not being weak or scared. A soft heart is a STRONG heart — soft like a pillow that holds everyone, not soft like wet paper that tears. Mārdava is not being quiet because I am afraid. It is being gentle because I love. Mārdava is not the same as being a pushover who lets people break toys — I can say 'please be gentle' in a soft voice, and that is still mārdava.

  • Slamming the door when I am cross
  • Grabbing the toy from my little sister's hand
  • Shouting at Amma when she cannot hear me
  • Pulling the cat's tail — even a little bit

Stories Your Child Heard

Baby Krishna's Soft Touch Melts Yashoda's Heart — In the village of Vrindavan, long long ago, Mother Yashoda was sitting on her cool stone floor. In front of her was a big clay pot full of fresh milk. She was churning butter. CHURN-CHURN-CHURN. Back and forth. The rope creaked. Her arms were aching. She had been churning since sunrise. Now the sun was high. She was very tired.

Ananya and the Shivering Puppy — It was raining in Bangalore. Not a big storm — just that steady pitter-patter rain that makes the evening grey and wet. Ananya was seven years old. She had just come home from school. Her shoes were splashy. Her hair was damp. As she walked up to her apartment building, she heard a tiny noise. A WHIMPER. Very small. Very sad.

The Sanskrit Word

mardava (मार्दव) — MAAR-da-va

Meaning: Gentleness. Softness of hand, voice, and heart. The melting quality — like butter in the sun. The way baby Krishna touched Yashoda's knee. The way we hold a flower or a baby bird.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep a small Gentleness Chart on the wall near your bed. Each day, do ONE gentle act with full mārdava — soft hands, soft voice, soft heart. For example: pat a pet with one soft finger; turn the pages of your storybook like butterfly wings; whisper 'good morning' to Amma when she is sleepy; water a plant without splashing; hug a grandparent slowly; hold a flower without crushing; help a younger sibling with very soft hands. Tick a box each day. Before sleep, whisper to Krishna: 'Dear Krishna, today my hands were soft. My voice was soft. My heart was soft. Like You.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can show the 'butterfly touch' gesture, can whisper 'mārdava' without prompting, and can describe at least 3 specific moments when they used soft hands, soft voice, or soft heart. The Gentleness Chart has at least 5 ticked boxes.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can show the 'butterfly touch' gesture, can whisper 'mārdava' without prompting, and can describe at least 3 specific moments when they used soft hands, soft voice, or soft heart. The Gentleness Chart has at least 5 ticked boxes.

When our soft heart pours its gentleness outward — caring for all who suffer, whether puppy or person — that flowing compassion has its own beautiful name: dayā.
