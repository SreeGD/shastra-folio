About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Daksya Worksheet — The Careful Hand

Activity 1: Quick-and-Messy or Slow-and-Careful? (Circle)

1. I stack the shoes in a tall, wobbly pile and rush outside. QUICK-MESSY SLOW-CAREFUL

2. I fold the napkin twice, carefully, matching the corners. QUICK-MESSY SLOW-CAREFUL

3. I scribble the homework so I can watch TV. QUICK-MESSY SLOW-CAREFUL

4. I water the tulsi plant gently — a few drops at the root, not a flood. QUICK-MESSY SLOW-CAREFUL

5. I draw Krishna's peacock feather slowly, colouring inside the lines. QUICK-MESSY SLOW-CAREFUL

Activity 2: One Careful Line

In the box below, write your name FIVE times. First line — as FAST as you can. Last line — as CAREFULLY as you can. Which line is more beautiful? Which one would you offer to Krishna?

[Writing space with 5 lines]

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Chant three times, slowly — one breath per word:

Dak-shya! Dak-shya! Dak-shya! *I do my work with CARE. Skill is my offering.*

Activity 4: The Tower-Building Challenge (Paired)

With a partner, take 10 small blocks or stones.

  • Round 1: Build a tower as FAST as you can. How tall before it falls? How does it look?
  • Round 2: Build a tower as SLOWLY and CAREFULLY as you can. How tall now? How does it FEEL?

Which tower is prouder? Which tower smiles more?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Careful-Work Diary

For one week, pick ONE task each day to do with special daksya — shoes in a row, folding one shirt, watering the plant, making your bed, colouring one picture. Take an extra minute. Do it carefully.

At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa: 'Today I did ____ with daksya.' Whisper: 'Krishna, this was for You.'

Structured Activities

  • Quick-and-Messy or Slow-and-Careful? (solo, 6 min)

    Circle QUICK-MESSY or SLOW-CAREFUL for each scenario.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • One Careful Line (solo, 8 min)

    Write your name five times — first as fast as you can, last as carefully as you can. Compare.

    Materials: Paper, Pencil

  • Say It With Me (whole_class, 3 min)

    Chant 'daksya' three times with slow, careful hand gestures — like carefully placing something.

  • The Tower-Building Challenge (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, build a tower of blocks first as fast as you can, then as carefully as you can. Which feels more steady?

    Materials: 10 wooden blocks or small stones per pair

  • The Careful-Work Diary (solo, 5 min)

    Each day this week pick one task and do it carefully for Krishna. Tell your family at bedtime what you did with daksya.

    Materials: A small notebook

Home Practice

For one week, keep a simple Careful-Work Diary. Pick ONE task each day — tidy your shoes, fold one shirt, water the tulsi plant, make your bed, colour one picture, wash one plate. Take one extra minute and do it with daksya. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa: 'Today I did ____ with care.' Whisper to Krishna: 'I did this for You.' Notice the warm feeling inside after careful work — that feeling IS Krishna's smile.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 5 specific careful tasks done this week, shows slower hands during at least one routine activity (shoes, folding, writing), and smiles quietly — not boastfully — when their work is noticed.
