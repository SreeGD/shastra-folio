About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

दक्ष्य
Daksya

Daksya means skillfulness — doing our work with CARE.

Chapter Overview

Daksya means skillfulness — doing our work with CARE. When I do something, I take the time to do it right. I fold the clothes neatly. I write my letters clearly. I build the tower carefully. Skill grows from two things — love and practice. Krishna smiles when we do small things with a big heart.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 2.50 — Krishna says: When we keep our mind peaceful and connected to Him, we do our work beautifully — without worrying about the result. Yoga is skill in action. Doing things with care, for Krishna — that is true skill.

Core Teaching

  1. Daksya means doing our work with CARE — taking the time to do it right. Neat letters, careful folding, gentle building — these small things become a beautiful offering to Krishna.
  2. Skill grows from two things — LOVE and PRACTICE. Like Abhimanyu listening in the dark, we begin with loving attention. Like Arav writing ONE letter at a time, we grow with patient practice. Hurry makes skill small. Care makes skill strong.
  3. True daksya is not showing off. It is doing even the smallest work — like stacking shoes, or writing a B — with a quiet, full heart. Krishna sees every careful line. Yogah karmasu kausalam — work done with love IS yoga.

Five-Minute Introduction

Bring a small pile of shoes to the centre of the circle. Ask: 'Who can stack these shoes the FASTEST?' One child rushes. Then ask: 'Who can stack them the most CAREFULLY?' A different child arranges them in a neat row. Both children sit down. Ask the class: 'Which pile would you offer to Krishna? Which pile looks like love?' The class will choose the careful row. Say: 'Today we learn a beautiful word — daksya — दक्ष्य. It means doing our work with CARE, because we love Krishna.' Chant together with a slow, careful hand gesture (like carefully placing something): 'Dak-shya! Dak-shya! Dak-shya!' Ask: 'What is ONE thing you usually rush through? Could you slow down today?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Shoe-stacking demo + chant 'daksya' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud: Little Abhimanyu Listens in the Dark; pause at 'he did not wiggle, he did not sleep, he was careful.' 8 min — Read aloud: Arav Writes Slowly; children practice one slow A in the air with their finger. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Quick-Messy vs. Slow-Careful) and Activity 2 (One Careful Line — five names). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce Careful-Work Diary. 2 min — Line up (slowly, with care).

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, shoe-stacking demo, chant 'daksya' with slow careful hand gesture. 15 min — Abhimanyu's story with soft classroom lights dimmed; teacher speaks like Arjuna's voice; children close eyes and 'listen with their whole heart'. 15 min — Arav's story, re-enacted at a desk with real pencil and paper; whole class writes ONE careful A in the air, then on paper. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 20 min — Tower-Building Challenge (Activity 4) with 10 wooden blocks or small stones per pair; rotate pairs once. 15 min — Careful-Folding Craft: each child folds a simple origami lotus (or a napkin into a triangle) — slowly, one crease at a time. 10 min — Class altar offering: each child places their folded lotus/napkin at the altar, whispering 'For You, Krishna.' 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE thing they will do with daksya this week. Buffer: 5 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn daksya through the BODY — slow hands, one line at a time, careful stacking, gentle folding. Never lecture them about excellence; instead, model careful work yourself. Children imitate what they see. When you place chalk on the tray, place it slowly. When you close a book, close it gently. Every careful act you do is a silent daksya lesson. Watch for two kinds of children — the HURRIED child who rushes to finish, and the PERFECTIONIST child who freezes because the A is not quite right. For the hurried child: 'Let us do ONE thing slowly together — just one.' For the perfectionist: 'Daksya is not perfect. Daksya is CARE. Your tired A is also a teacher — the next A will be a little rounder.' Celebrate EFFORT, not outcome. Say 'I see you took your time' more than 'Your work is beautiful.' Connect daksya to bhakti — every careful act is an offering. The child who writes one neat B for Krishna has prayed with her pencil. Also distinguish daksya from PRIDE — the child who says 'mine is BEST' has lost the lesson. Gently redirect: 'Yes, you worked carefully. And look — your friend also worked carefully in his own way. Daksya makes us grateful, not grand.'

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Daksya means I must be the BEST at everything.' Response: Not at all. Daksya is not a competition. It is CARE. A six-year-old who folds her napkin slowly and gently has full daksya, even if her folds are not perfect. Care is the heart of skill — not being better than others.
  • Misconception: 'Daksya means doing things SLOWLY forever, even when we need to hurry.' Response: Daksya means doing things WITH FULL ATTENTION. Sometimes that takes longer. Sometimes, once we have practised, we can do beautiful work quickly too — like Abhimanyu who became a fast warrior because he had listened carefully. Care first; speed comes naturally from practice.
  • Misconception: 'Daksya is showing off my skill to impress others.' Response: That is the opposite of daksya. True skill is quiet. When we do our work for Krishna, we do not need anyone to clap. The joy is IN the doing. 'Look how fast I did it!' is pride. 'I did this with care, for You' is daksya.
