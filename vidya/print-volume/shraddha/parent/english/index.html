About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Shraddha

Shraddha means faith — trusting Krishna, trusting our teachers, trusting the sacred stories with our whole heart. It is doing the practice before we can see the result. We plant the seed today, even though the mango comes much later. A shraddha-heart says, 'I trust. I try. I wait with love.'

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 4.39: The child who has faith, who loves what the teacher teaches, and who keeps the senses calm — that child quickly receives true knowledge. And with true knowledge, the highest peace comes into the heart.

What Shraddha is NOT

Shraddha is not demanding, 'Show me first, then I will believe.' It is not obeying blindly without listening with our heart. It is not giving up the second we do not see results. It is not trusting every shiny idea — real shraddha is in what Krishna and the great souls have taught us.

  • Refusing to chant because 'it does not feel like anything yet'
  • Demanding Krishna show Himself before we will pray
  • Stopping a new practice after only one or two days because it 'did not work'

Stories Your Child Heard

Nachiketa and the Three Nights — Long ago in Bharat, there lived a brave small boy named Nachiketa. One day his father was giving gifts to the brahmins. But the cows were old and thin. Nachiketa's heart felt shaky. He asked softly, 'Father, who will you give ME to?'

Ananya and the Tulasi Seeds — Ananya was staying with her grandmother in the village. Every morning Ammamma watered the tulasi plant and sang softly to Krishna. One day Ammamma opened her palm. On it were tiny black seeds, smaller than mustard.

The Sanskrit Word

śraddhā (श्रद्धा) — SHRAH-dhaa

Meaning: Faith. A heart that trusts Krishna, the teacher, and the sacred stories — and keeps practicing with love even before the result appears.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

The Faith Pot: plant a few methi, moong, or tulasi seeds with Amma or Appa. Every morning for 10 days in a row, water them. While watering, chant 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna' three times. Do NOT dig up the seeds to check. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa what you did today. On the day the first green leaf comes up, clap your hands and say, 'Krishna, I trusted — and You kept Your promise.' Also: if a teacher or grown-up you love asks you to try a practice you do not fully understand, try it for three full days before deciding anything.

Duration: Every day for two weeks
Look for: By the end of two weeks, the child can show a sprouted Faith Pot (or describe the waiting if no sprout yet), and can say one specific moment when they chose to 'keep watering' instead of giving up.

Signs of Growth

By the end of two weeks, the child can show a sprouted Faith Pot (or describe the waiting if no sprout yet), and can say one specific moment when they chose to 'keep watering' instead of giving up.

When our faith is planted deep and fed every day, it grows into a quiet, total offering of our heart to Krishna — and that offering has its own beautiful name, ishvara-pranidhana.
