About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Santosha

Santosha means being happy with what Krishna gives me.

Verse Anchor

Yoga Sutras 2.42

सन्तोषादनुत्तमसुखलाभः॥

santoṣād anuttamaḥ sukha-lābhaḥ

Word-for-word: santoṣāt — from contentment; anuttamaḥ — the very highest, unbeatable; sukha — happiness; lābhaḥ — the gaining, the finding. A related primary teaching — BG 12.14: santuṣṭaḥ satataṁ yogī — the yogi who is always content.

Translation: Patanjali says: From santosha — from being happy with what Krishna gives — we find the highest, sweetest happiness. There is nothing better. A little cup of contentment is fuller than a palace of wanting-more.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 8.19.25

यावद्भ्रियेत जठरं तावत्स्वत्वं हि देहिनाम्।
अधिकं योऽभिमन्येत स स्तेनो दण्डमर्हति॥

yāvad bhriyeta jaṭharaṁ tāvat svatvaṁ hi dehinām adhikaṁ yo 'bhimanyeta sa steno daṇḍam arhati

Translation: Little Vamanadeva says to the greedy king Bali: 'A person needs only enough to fill the belly. If someone grabs more than this and calls it MINE, that person is really a thief.' Santosha is the answer — take what is needed, be happy, do not grab.

What is Santosha?

Santosha means being happy with what Krishna gives me. I look at my plate and say 'This is enough.' I look at my toys and say 'These are enough.' I look at my family and say 'This is enough.' I do not grab for more. I enjoy what is here. My heart is a little cup — already full.

What it is NOT

Santosha is not pretending to be happy when my heart is really whining inside. It is not being lazy or saying 'I don't care about anything.' It is not giving up on trying new things. It is not dull like a sleepy turtle. Santosha is bright and awake — it just does not GRAB.

  • Looking at my friend's bigger tiffin and whining 'I wanted THAT one!'
  • Getting a new toy and crying ten minutes later because I want ANOTHER new toy
  • Pushing away my dinner because 'I wanted pasta, not rice!'
  • Saying 'boring, boring, boring' all day because nothing feels new enough

Sanskrit Word Study

santoṣa (सन्तोष)

Pronunciation: san-TO-sha

Meaning: Contentment. Being happy with what Krishna gives us. The quiet 'enough' inside the heart. The little cup that is already full. Not grabbing for more.

Example: When Ananya tasted her plain dosa slowly and said 'This is enough,' that was santosha.

Story from the Scriptures

Kholaveca Sridhara and the Banana-Leaf Offering — Chaitanya Charitamrita, Madhya-lila and Antya-lila — the story of Sridhara of Navadvipa

Long ago in old Bengal, a banana-seller named Sridhara lived in Navadvipa.

Sridhara was very poor. His hut was so small he could touch both walls. The roof had a hole. When it rained, rain came IN. Sridhara only laughed and set a little pot under the hole. 'Krishna sends rain AND the pot to catch it!'

Every morning, Sridhara carried banana stems to market. He earned half a coin a day.

And he divided this tiny half-coin into TWO halves. One half for rice. The other half — for Krishna. A flower, a drop of ghee, a whisper of incense.

His wife sighed, 'We are so poor. Why give half to Krishna?'

Sridhara smiled. 'Krishna gives EVERYTHING. This half-coin is nothing.'

In that same town, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu was walking and chanting. He could SEE whose heart was full of Krishna, even under a leaky roof.

One evening, Chaitanya came to Sridhara's hut. Sridhara's eyes went WIDE. 'My Lord! In MY hut? I have no silver plate!'

But Sridhara had a broken clay pot and the soft green of a banana stem. He washed the Lord's feet with shaking hands. He placed banana-stem on a fresh banana LEAF — no plate, just the leaf.

Chaitanya ate slowly and smiled. 'Sridhara! This is the sweetest meal in all Navadvipa!'

Sridhara wept. 'Lord, I have nothing.'

Chaitanya touched his head. 'Dear one, you have santosha. You do not grab for more. Your heart is already full. You are richer than any king.'

From that day, Sridhara's little hut shone like a golden palace. Half a coin a day, and the happiest life in Bengal.

That is santosha. The cup already full.

A Story from Today

Ananya and the One Plain Dosa

It was Sunday morning. The cousins had come for breakfast. Amma was making dosas — the smell of ghee was everywhere.

Amma brought the first plate — for Priya. A GOLDEN dosa with melty cheese.

Priya squealed. 'Oh WOW!'

The next plate came for Rohit — dosa with CHOCOLATE inside. The family went 'Ooooooh.'

The next had potato. Then sweet coconut. Then spicy masala.

Finally, Amma came to Ananya. On her plate was one plain dosa. A tiny bowl of chutney. Nothing else.

Ananya's face fell. Her lip pushed out. 'Amma... mine is PLAIN. I want chocolate too.'

Amma knelt beside her and whispered in her ear.

'Beta, taste it first. Just one bite. Slowly.'

Ananya pouted. But she took one tiny bite.

The dosa was warm. Crispy outside, soft inside. The chutney was cool. And — Ananya noticed — it tasted a little like AMMA. Like her Amma's hands and Amma's love.

Ananya took another bite. Slower. She closed her eyes.

Inside her chest, a quiet WARM feeling woke up. Not the excited kind. The gentle kind. Like a kitten curling up.

Amma whispered, 'This is santosha, beta. A quiet happy. Already enough.'

Ananya opened her eyes. She looked at Priya's cheesy dosa. She looked at Rohit's chocolate. They looked yummy. But her tummy did not grab for them anymore.

'Amma,' Ananya said. 'My dosa is enough.'

Amma kissed her forehead. 'Krishna made YOUR dosa the perfect one. Santosha sees that.'

Ananya finished every bite. She sat warm and quiet — small plate and small heart, both full.

Core Teaching

  1. Santosha means I am happy with what Krishna gives me. I look at my tiffin and say 'This is enough.' I look at my clothes and say 'These are enough.' I do not grab for more. My heart is a small cup — and Krishna has filled it.
  2. Santosha is a QUIET happy, not a loud happy. It is the gentle warmth of a dosa eaten slowly, a toy enjoyed fully, a family loved quietly. When I stop wishing for more, I finally SEE what is already here — and it is beautiful.
  3. Santosha is not being dull or lazy. I can still try new things. I can still learn and play. But inside, I am not GRABBING. Like Sridhara with his banana leaf, I know — Krishna gives me just what I need. And that is always, always enough.
