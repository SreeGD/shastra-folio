About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Santosha

Santosha means being happy with what Krishna gives me.

Worksheet

Santosha Worksheet — The Cup That Is Already Full

Activity 1: Enough or Grab? (Circle the Right Answer)

1. I have a blue crayon. My friend has a red one. I take my blue one and draw happily. ENOUGH GRAB

2. Amma gives me ONE laddoo. I ate it. I whine and ask for three more. ENOUGH GRAB

3. My tiffin has roti and dal. My friend has pizza. I eat my roti with a smile. ENOUGH GRAB

4. I see a new toy on TV. I cry until Papa buys it. ENOUGH GRAB

5. My old shirt still fits me. I wear it happily and say 'Thank you, Krishna.' ENOUGH GRAB

Activity 2: Draw Your Little Cup

In the box below, draw a small cup inside your chest. Fill the cup with things Krishna has ALREADY given you — your family, your food, your home, your toys, your friends, the sky, the trees. Colour each gift with a different colour. Write under your picture: 'My cup is already full.'

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me (The Enough-Chant)

Stand tall. Put both hands on your chest, like cupping a small bowl.

Chant three times, gently, with a soft smile:

San-TO-sha! San-TO-sha! San-TO-sha! *Enough! Enough! I am happy with what Krishna gives!*

Activity 4: The Slow-Bite Game (Paired or Solo)

Your teacher will give each child ONE tiny raisin (or one piece of roasted chana, or a small piece of apple).

  • Round 1: Put the raisin on your tongue. Do NOT chew yet.
  • Round 2: Close your eyes. Breathe slowly. Feel the raisin. Notice — is it sweet? Soft? Wrinkly?
  • Round 3: Chew it VERY slowly — twenty chews. Taste every bit.

Turn to your partner. Say: 'One raisin was enough!' Smile.

Discuss quietly: Did you want five more raisins, or was ONE enough when you really tasted it?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Enough Jar

At home, keep a small clean jar on your shelf. Every evening this week, before bed, drop in ONE small pebble. Before dropping, whisper ONE thing Krishna gave you today that was enough — the sunny morning, the warm bath, the hug from Dadi, the crunchy biscuit, the kind word from your teacher.

At the end of the week, pour the pebbles into your palm. Look at them. Say softly: 'Krishna, my cup is full.'

Home Practice

Set up an Enough Jar on your shelf. Every evening this week, before bed, sit quietly with Amma or Appa. Think of ONE thing Krishna gave you today that was enough — a warm meal, a sunny window, a hug, a friend, a kind word, a book. Drop a small pebble into the jar and whisper that one thing out loud. Then take three slow breaths with your hands cupped over your chest. Whisper: 'Santosha. My cup is full. Thank You, Krishna.' Also this week — at every meal, take your FIRST bite very, very slowly. Close your eyes. Really taste it. Whisper inside: 'This is enough.' Notice how the slow bite tastes sweeter than a fast one.

Duration: Every evening for one week, plus one slow bite at every meal
Look for: By the end of the week, the Enough Jar has at least 7 pebbles, and the child can name at least 3 specific moments from the week when they felt 'this is enough' without grabbing for more. The child can demonstrate the hands-cupped-over-chest gesture and say 'santosha' with a soft smile.
