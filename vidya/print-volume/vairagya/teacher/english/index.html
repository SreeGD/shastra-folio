About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

वैराग्य
Vairagya

Vairagya means not clinging too tightly to things.

Chapter Overview

Vairagya means not clinging too tightly to things. We can love our toys, our friends, our favourite food — but inside we remember that Krishna is the real owner. We enjoy what comes. We smile when it goes. Our happiness does not depend on ONE thing — it lives quietly inside us, always.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 6.35 — Krishna says: Yes, Arjuna, the mind is jumpy and hard to catch. But by practising every day, and by not clinging too tightly to things, even the jumpy mind can be held gently — like a little bird in cupped hands.

Core Teaching

  1. Vairagya means holding what we love with a SOFT hand — not squeezing, not pushing away. We enjoy the toy, the friend, the sweet — and we also remember that Krishna is the real owner. Things come. Things go. Krishna stays.
  2. Our happiness does not live inside ONE toy or ONE friend. It lives quietly inside our own heart — where Krishna is always smiling. When we remember this, we do not fall apart when something breaks or goes away.
  3. A soft hand can still give big hugs. A soft heart can still love deeply. Vairagya is not being cold — it is loving in a way that does not turn into pain when things change. Like King Bharata learned — and like Meera learned under the tulsi.

Five-Minute Introduction

Hold a small flower in your cupped hands. Show the class. Say: 'Look how gently I am holding this. If I squeeze — ' (squeeze and crush it a little) '— it breaks. If I throw it away — it is wasted. But if I hold it gently, I can enjoy it, and then when the time comes, I can place it on the altar for Krishna.' Do the flower offering right there. Then say: 'Today we learn a beautiful old word — vairagya. It means holding what we love with a SOFT hand.' Chant 'vairagya, vairagya, vairagya' three times, opening palms outward from the heart. Then ask: 'What is something you love a LOT? Can you love it with a soft hand?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Flower demo + chant 'vairagya' with palm-opening gesture. 10 min — Read aloud: King Bharata and the Little Deer; pause at the mother deer splashing into the river and at Bharata's heart going 'BUMP-BUMP-BUMP' with worry. 8 min — Read aloud: Meera and the Little Blue Elephant; whole class does the palm-on-heart breath with Meera. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Clinging or Soft Hand) and Activity 3 (Chant). 5 min — Closing: each child opens one palm and whispers 'For Krishna.' 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, flower demonstration, chant 'vairagya' three times with palm-opening gesture. 15 min — Bharata story with a small soft-toy deer prop; children take turns 'rescuing' the deer gently, then placing her back at the altar. 15 min — Meera story with role-play of the under-tulsi conversation between Amma and Meera. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1, 2, and 3. 20 min — The Soft-Hand Swap (Activity 4); rotate partners twice. 15 min — Letting-Go Shelf Craft: each child decorates a small paper box labelled 'Krishna's Things.' 15 min — Outdoor walk: find one flower, one leaf, one small stone. Enjoy each for 30 seconds, then gently return each to where it belongs. 'These are all Krishna's.' 8 min — Closing: each child names ONE thing they love AND can hold with a soft hand. 2 min — Chant 'vairagya' softly as goodbye. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children cannot understand abstract 'detachment' — they will hear 'do not love your toys' and feel confused or sad. Teach vairagya entirely through the SOFT HAND metaphor. Show with your own hands — cupped gently, not clenched, not empty. The phrase 'love with a soft hand' is the whole lesson. Use the flower-offering and the hand-opening gesture every session. When a child is upset because something broke, lost, or changed — never dismiss the feeling. Sit with them. Breathe together. Then, when the first wave passes, gently remind: 'Krishna is the owner. He is taking care of it.' The Bharata story is RICH — do not rush it. Let children feel sorry for the king whose love became clinging. Never moralize — let the story do its work. Watch for the possessive child (common) AND the withdrawn child (rare) — vairagya is the middle path, warm and free at once.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Vairagya means I have to give away all my toys and not love anything.' Response: Not at all! Vairagya is not cold or empty. It is WARM — like a hand that holds gently. You can love your teddy, your Amma, your best friend — vairagya just reminds you that Krishna is the real owner. Your heart stays full even when things come and go.
  • Misconception: 'If I cry when something breaks, I am failing at vairagya.' Response: It is okay to feel sad. Feelings come like clouds. Vairagya is not about stopping feelings — it is about remembering that the SUN (Krishna's love in our heart) is still there behind the clouds. Take three breaths. The sad feeling will pass, and the quiet warmth inside will return.
  • Misconception: 'Vairagya means pretending not to care.' Response: That is the opposite! Pretending-not-to-care hides our real heart. Vairagya is fully caring — and also fully trusting Krishna. Like Meera, we can feel sad AND remember Krishna is taking care of things. Both at the same time. That is the soft-hand secret.
