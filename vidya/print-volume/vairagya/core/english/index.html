About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Vairagya

Vairagya means not clinging too tightly to things.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 6.35

श्रीभगवानुवाच
असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम्।
अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते॥

śrī-bhagavān uvāca asaṁśayaṁ mahā-bāho mano durnigrahaṁ calam abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate

Word-for-word: śrī-bhagavān uvāca — the Supreme Lord said; asaṁśayam — without doubt; mahā-bāho — O mighty-armed one; manaḥ — the mind; durnigraham — hard to hold; calam — jumpy, restless; abhyāsena — by practice; tu — but; kaunteya — O son of Kunti; vairāgyeṇa — by not clinging; ca — and; gṛhyate — can be held.

Translation: Krishna says: Yes, Arjuna, the mind is jumpy and hard to catch. But by practising every day, and by not clinging too tightly to things, even the jumpy mind can be held gently — like a little bird in cupped hands.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.8.44

सुखमैन्द्रियकं राजन् स्वर्गे नरके च देहिनाम्।
दुःखमन्तरितं विद्वान् नेच्छेत् तदसमञ्जसम्॥

sukham aindriyakaṁ rājan svarge narake ca dehinām duḥkham antaritaṁ vidvān necchet tad asamañjasam

Translation: The happiness we get from outside things — in the best of places and the saddest of places — always has a little sadness hiding inside it. The wise person does not cling to such happiness, because it never stays the same.

What is Vairagya?

Vairagya means not clinging too tightly to things. We can love our toys, our friends, our favourite food — but inside we remember that Krishna is the real owner. We enjoy what comes. We smile when it goes. Our happiness does not depend on ONE thing — it lives quietly inside us, always.

What it is NOT

Vairagya is not hating nice things or throwing away our toys. It is not being cold or grumpy. It is not crying for days when something breaks. It is not saying 'I do not care' about everything. Vairagya is a soft, open hand — not a clenched fist, not an empty one.

  • Screaming and crying for one whole day when a favourite toy breaks
  • Refusing to share because 'this is MINE — I NEED it!'
  • Saying 'I do not like anything anymore' when one thing goes wrong

Sanskrit Word Study

vairāgya (वैराग्य)

Pronunciation: vai-RAHG-ya

Meaning: Not clinging. Holding what we love with a soft, open hand — enjoying it without squeezing, letting go without breaking. Remembering that Krishna is the real owner of everything.

Example: When Meera smiled at the sky and whispered 'Krishna is keeping my elephant safe,' that was vairagya.

Story from the Scriptures

King Bharata and the Little Deer — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 5, Chapters 7-8

Long ago, a great king named Bharata lived in India. He was kind and brave. But one day he said, 'I want to remember Krishna with my whole heart.' So he gave his kingdom to his son and walked into the forest to live a simple life.

Bharata made a small hut by a river. He chanted Krishna's names all day. His heart felt light, like a bird.

One morning Bharata was at the river. Suddenly — SPLASH! A frightened mother deer jumped into the water. A tiger had been chasing her. In her fear, she gave birth to a tiny baby deer right there. The mother was too tired and floated away in the water.

The little baby deer was all alone. Her big brown eyes looked up at Bharata. She was so small. She was shivering.

Bharata's heart melted. 'Poor little one,' he whispered. He picked her up gently and carried her home.

At first, it was kind. He fed her soft grass. He kept her warm. But slowly, Bharata began to LOVE the deer too much. When she ate, he watched. When she played, he smiled. When she was away even for a minute, his heart went BUMP-BUMP-BUMP with worry.

Bharata forgot to chant Krishna's name. He only thought, 'Where is my deer? Is she hungry? Is she safe?'

The little deer became his whole world. And when Bharata was very old and ready to leave his body, the last thing he thought of was — the deer. Not Krishna.

But Krishna is so kind. Krishna gave Bharata another chance, and another, until at last Bharata learned the gentle lesson.

Love the deer — yes! But hold her with a SOFT hand. She belongs to Krishna. You are only taking care of her for a little while. This is vairagya — loving without clinging.

A Story from Today

Meera and the Little Blue Elephant

Meera was seven years old. She had ONE special toy — a tiny blue elephant with a little gold saddle. Dadi had given it to her on her birthday. Meera carried it everywhere. It slept on her pillow. It came to school in her bag.

One Monday, the school bus went over a big bump. Meera's bag tipped over. The little blue elephant rolled out and fell somewhere on the bus floor.

When Meera got home, she opened her bag. Her heart FROZE. The elephant was GONE.

Meera ran back to the bus stop. She called the driver. She searched everywhere. No elephant.

Meera threw herself on her bed. She cried and cried. Amma brought her favourite mango. She pushed it away. Appa called her to play. She turned her face to the wall.

'Everything is ruined,' she whispered. 'I will never be happy again.'

That evening, Amma took her hand gently. 'Come, beta. Let us sit under the tulsi.'

They sat on the cool ground. Amma did not say 'Stop crying.' She just held Meera's hand.

After a while, Amma said softly, 'Meera, who gave you the little blue elephant?'

'Dadi,' Meera sniffled.

'And who gave the elephant to Dadi, and Dadi to you, and you to me?'

Meera looked up. 'Krishna?'

Amma smiled. 'Krishna lets us hold His beautiful things for a little while. Sometimes He comes and borrows them back. He never says sorry — because they were always His.'

Amma put Meera's hand on her own heart. 'The love you felt for the elephant? That lives HERE. Nobody can borrow THAT.'

Meera breathed in. She breathed out. Her heart was still a little sore. But something bigger was also there — a soft, steady warmth that did not need the elephant at all.

'Amma,' Meera whispered, 'I think Krishna is taking good care of my elephant.'

'Of course He is,' Amma said. 'That is vairagya — holding what we love with a soft hand. Now come. Let us offer a flower to Krishna together.'

Meera smiled for the first time that day. Her hands were empty. Her heart was full.

Core Teaching

  1. Vairagya means holding what we love with a SOFT hand — not squeezing, not pushing away. We enjoy the toy, the friend, the sweet — and we also remember that Krishna is the real owner. Things come. Things go. Krishna stays.
  2. Our happiness does not live inside ONE toy or ONE friend. It lives quietly inside our own heart — where Krishna is always smiling. When we remember this, we do not fall apart when something breaks or goes away.
  3. A soft hand can still give big hugs. A soft heart can still love deeply. Vairagya is not being cold — it is loving in a way that does not turn into pain when things change. Like King Bharata learned — and like Meera learned under the tulsi.
