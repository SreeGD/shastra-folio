About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

CHAPTER 1
अपैशुनम्
Apaishunam

Apaishunam means NOT finding fault in others and NOT whispering mean things about them behind their backs.

Worksheet

Apaishunam Worksheet — My Mouth Is a Safe Place

Activity 1: Join the Whisper or Walk Away? (Circle the Right One)

1. My friends make a whisper-circle about a girl who is eating alone. Do I lean in and giggle, or smile softly and say 'let's play instead'? JOIN WALK AWAY

2. Ravi drops his pencil box and everything falls out. Do I rush to tell the whole bus later, or help him pick up the pencils and say nothing? TELL EVERYONE HELP QUIETLY

3. A bigger boy is BULLYING my friend — hitting her. Do I stay quiet because 'apaishunam,' or tell a trusted teacher RIGHT AWAY? STAY QUIET TELL TEACHER

4. Priya came to class with her hair a little messy. Do I whisper 'did you SEE her hair?' to Sara, or just smile normally at Priya? WHISPER SMILE

5. At home, Ajji asks 'how was your cousin Meera today?' Do I make up a long story about Meera's mistakes, or say honestly 'she was fine'? MAKE STORY SAY FINE

(Numbers 1, 2, 4, 5 — WALK AWAY / HELP QUIETLY / SMILE / SAY FINE is apaishunam. Number 3 — TELL TEACHER is the correct answer because big bad things must be told to a grown-up.)

Activity 2: Draw Two Mouths

In the first box, draw a mouth that is a WHISPER-MOUTH — big lips, tongue poking out, little arrows of mean words flying out. Make the face a little frowny.

In the second box, draw a mouth that is a SAFE-MOUTH — gentle closed lips, a soft smile, a tiny bird resting on the lips, safe. Make the face kind and calm.

Under the drawings, write: 'My mouth is a safe place. Krishna lives inside every person I meet.'

[Box 1: Whisper-Mouth] [Box 2: Safe-Mouth]

Activity 3: The Closed-Hand Chant

Stand in a circle. Make a SOFT fist with your right hand — like you are holding a tiny sparrow inside, keeping it safe. Hold the fist near your heart.

Chant three times, slowly, breathing gently:

A-pai-SHU-nam! A-pai-SHU-nam! A-pai-SHU-nam! *My mouth is safe. The little bird is safe. Krishna sees.*

Open your hand at the end and gently let the 'sparrow' fly up — fingers spread to the sky.

Activity 4: The See-the-Good-First Game (Paired)

With a partner, take turns. Your partner says the name of ONE classmate. You must quickly say ONE GOOD thing about that classmate — no mean things, no buts. Then switch.

Example:

  • Partner A: 'Arjun.'
  • Partner B: 'Arjun draws the nicest spaceships.'
  • Partner A: 'Priya.'
  • Partner B: 'Priya always shares her sharpener.'

Rule: if a mean thing slips out, start the round over. See how fast you can name five classmates with FIVE good things.

Notice: did the see-the-good-first game make you feel LIGHTER or heavier than a whisper-circle would?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Safe-Mouth Week

For one week, keep a tiny 'Safe-Mouth Book' next to your bed. Each night before sleep, think about your day. Write or draw THREE things:

1. One time my mouth was SAFE today — I did NOT whisper a mean thing about someone.

2. One GOOD thing I noticed about someone today (no mean buts).

3. If any mean thing slipped out — a small heart with a tiny crack in it, and a note: 'Tomorrow I try again.'

At the end of the week, count the safe-mouths. Each one is a tiny feather in Krishna's peacock crown.

Structured Activities

  • Join the Whisper or Walk Away? (solo, 6 min)

    Circle JOIN or WALK AWAY for each scenario. Remember that big bad things must be told to a trusted grown-up — that is different from gossip.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • Draw Two Mouths (solo, 10 min)

    In one box, draw a Whisper-Mouth with mean little arrow-words flying out. In the other, draw a Safe-Mouth with a tiny bird resting on gentle closed lips. Write the apaishunam promise underneath.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Soft colours

  • The Closed-Hand Chant (whole_class, 4 min)

    Make a soft fist near your heart, as if holding a tiny sparrow safe inside. Chant 'a-pai-shu-nam' three times slowly. At the end, open your hand gently and let the 'sparrow' fly up.

  • The See-the-Good-First Game (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, take turns naming a classmate and quickly saying ONE good thing about them. No mean buts. If a mean thing slips out, restart. Try to reach five classmates with five good things.

  • The Safe-Mouth Book (solo, 5 min)

    Keep a small book by your bed. Each night, write or draw: one time today your mouth was safe, one good thing you noticed about someone, and a tiny cracked heart if a mean thing slipped (with 'tomorrow I try again').

    Materials: Small notebook, Pencil, Crayons

Home Practice

For one week, keep a Safe-Mouth Book beside your bed. Each night before sleep, with Amma or Appa, think back over the day and share three things: (1) ONE time today your mouth was SAFE — you did NOT whisper a mean thing about someone, even when it was tempting. (2) ONE GOOD thing you noticed about someone today — a classmate, a cousin, a sibling, anyone (no mean buts). (3) If any mean thing slipped out, draw a small heart with a tiny crack and write 'tomorrow I try again.' No shame — just a fresh start. Before sleeping, cup your hand softly at your heart (like you are holding a little sparrow safe inside) and whisper: 'Krishna, my mouth is safe tonight. The little bird inside is safe. Thank You.'

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments when they closed their mouth against a whisper-chance, and can name at least 5 good things about at least 5 different classmates or family members. They can demonstrate the 'soft-fist-at-heart' gesture while saying 'apaishunam' softly. The Safe-Mouth Book has at least 5 entries and any 'cracks' have been followed by a successful 'safe mouth' the next day.
