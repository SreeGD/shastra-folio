About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Brahmacharya

Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH. It means my mind stays focused on learning, on Krishna, and on doing what is right. My senses are my FRIENDS, not my bosses. I choose what my eyes see. I choose what my mouth says. I choose what my hands do. When my eyes want to peek at the TV before homework — I say 'not now.' When my tongue wants a second sweet — I say 'not now.' When my mind wants to wander during prayer — I call it gently back. That is brahmacharya. Self-mastery, for Krishna.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 17.14: Krishna says: Honouring Krishna and our wise teachers, keeping clean, being straight and honest, walking the student's path of brahmacharya, and not hurting any creature — THIS is the body's little fire. The word brahmacharya means: my eyes, my mouth, my hands, and my mind are all walking toward Krishna and toward learning. I am the master of my senses, not their servant.

What Brahmacharya is NOT

Brahmacharya is not being grumpy or sour. It is not saying NO to every small happiness. It is not showing off — 'Look how strict I am!' It is not punishing our body or starving ourselves. And it is not being a servant of our senses — running wherever our eyes, tongue, and hands pull us. True brahmacharya is quiet, warm, and steady. It keeps the mind pointed at Krishna like a compass points north.

  • Turning on cartoons the moment I come home — before homework, before prayer, before even washing my hands
  • Eating a sweet laddoo right before dinner because 'Amma is not looking'
  • Letting my mind jump to games and toys during the morning aarti, and not calling it back
  • Speaking rude words to my little sister whenever I feel like it, because 'that is just how I am'

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Dhruva's Unwavering Eyes — Long, long ago, there was a tiny prince named Dhruva. He was only five years old. One sad day, Dhruva's step-mother spoke harsh words to him. Dhruva ran to his own mother, Suniti, with big tears. Suniti held him and said softly, 'My little son, only Krishna can truly help us. Go into the forest. Sit. Think only of Him.'

Arjun and the Tall TV — Arjun was seven years old. He lived in Hyderabad with his Amma, his Papa, and his little sister Ishani. Every afternoon at 4 o'clock, Chhota Bheem came on TV. Arjun LOVED Chhota Bheem. He would count the minutes from the school bus ride home.

The Sanskrit Word

brahmacarya (ब्रह्मचर्य) — BRAHM-ah-char-ya

Meaning: The student's path. Walking with Krishna. Self-mastery — being the master of my eyes, my tongue, my hands, and my mind, so they stay with learning and with Krishna instead of pulling me away. My senses are my friends, not my bosses.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Master-of-Myself Diary beside your pillow. Each evening, before sleep, think about the day. For EACH moment you were the master of your senses — did homework before TV, said no to a second sweet, brought your mind back to Krishna during bhajan, kept kind words when your tongue wanted to shout, put away the tablet at prayer time — draw one tiny GOLDEN CROWN in your diary. For each moment the senses were your boss, draw a tiny UMBRELLA (gentle, no shame). Then put your hand on your heart and whisper: 'Krishna, I am Your little student. Tomorrow, more crowns. Tomorrow, I choose again.' In the morning, before rising, touch your eyes, lips, hands, and forehead and whisper: 'Eyes, tongue, hands, mind — today we walk together with Krishna.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific moments they were 'the master' of a sense; can demonstrate the four-friends gesture (touch eyes, touch lips, fold hands, touch forehead); and the diary contains at least 7 tiny crowns. The child uses the phrase 'my senses are my friends' or 'I am the master' spontaneously when describing a moment of self-control. The child can sit still for 2-3 minutes during family bhajan without needing reminders.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific moments they were 'the master' of a sense; can demonstrate the four-friends gesture (touch eyes, touch lips, fold hands, touch forehead); and the diary contains at least 7 tiny crowns. The child uses the phrase 'my senses are my friends' or 'I am the master' spontaneously when describing a moment of self-control. The child can sit still for 2-3 minutes during family bhajan without needing reminders.

When our little student-self stays steady even while the world calls and tugs and shines — that same steadiness, grown a little wider, becomes the warm calm mind that holds its ground in every weather. That peaceful holding has its own name: dhairya.
