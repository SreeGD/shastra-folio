About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Brahmacharya

Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 17.14

देवद्विजगुरुप्राज्ञपूजनं शौचमार्जवम्।
ब्रह्मचर्यमहिंसा च शारीरं तप उच्यते॥

deva-dvija-guru-prājña- pūjanaṁ śaucam ārjavam brahmacaryam ahiṁsā ca śārīraṁ tapa ucyate

Word-for-word: deva — Krishna and His devas; dvija — the twice-born wise ones; guru — the teacher; prājña — elders who see clearly; pūjanam — honouring them; śaucam — cleanness; ārjavam — straight-ness (honesty); brahmacaryam — the student's path (self-control and focus on learning); ahiṁsā — not hurting; śārīram tapaḥ — the body's little fire.

Translation: Krishna says: Honouring Krishna and our wise teachers, keeping clean, being straight and honest, walking the student's path of brahmacharya, and not hurting any creature — THIS is the body's little fire. The word brahmacharya means: my eyes, my mouth, my hands, and my mind are all walking toward Krishna and toward learning. I am the master of my senses, not their servant.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 4.8.71

ध्यायन्मनसा विद्याद्विश्वरूपं हरेस्ततः।
धिया त्यजेदसाराणि त्रिवर्गं पुरुषार्थकम्॥

dhyāyan manasā vidyād viśva-rūpaṁ hares tataḥ dhiyā tyajed asārāṇi tri-vargaṁ puruṣārthakam

Translation: Sage Narada tells the little boy Dhruva: 'Dear child, hold your mind steady on the beautiful form of Hari. Let your little senses come back to Him again and again. Set aside for now the short-lived toys of the world — and walk the student's path with both feet firm.'

What is Brahmacharya?

Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH. It means my mind stays focused on learning, on Krishna, and on doing what is right. My senses are my FRIENDS, not my bosses. I choose what my eyes see. I choose what my mouth says. I choose what my hands do. When my eyes want to peek at the TV before homework — I say 'not now.' When my tongue wants a second sweet — I say 'not now.' When my mind wants to wander during prayer — I call it gently back. That is brahmacharya. Self-mastery, for Krishna.

What it is NOT

Brahmacharya is not being grumpy or sour. It is not saying NO to every small happiness. It is not showing off — 'Look how strict I am!' It is not punishing our body or starving ourselves. And it is not being a servant of our senses — running wherever our eyes, tongue, and hands pull us. True brahmacharya is quiet, warm, and steady. It keeps the mind pointed at Krishna like a compass points north.

  • Turning on cartoons the moment I come home — before homework, before prayer, before even washing my hands
  • Eating a sweet laddoo right before dinner because 'Amma is not looking'
  • Letting my mind jump to games and toys during the morning aarti, and not calling it back
  • Speaking rude words to my little sister whenever I feel like it, because 'that is just how I am'

Sanskrit Word Study

brahmacarya (ब्रह्मचर्य)

Pronunciation: BRAHM-ah-char-ya

Meaning: The student's path. Walking with Krishna. Self-mastery — being the master of my eyes, my tongue, my hands, and my mind, so they stay with learning and with Krishna instead of pulling me away. My senses are my friends, not my bosses.

Example: When Arjun put the remote back on the table and did his homework first, that was brahmacharya.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Dhruva's Unwavering Eyes — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 4, Chapters 8-9 — the tapas and unwavering focus of child-prince Dhruva

Long, long ago, there was a tiny prince named Dhruva. He was only five years old.

One sad day, Dhruva's step-mother spoke harsh words to him. Dhruva ran to his own mother, Suniti, with big tears. Suniti held him and said softly, 'My little son, only Krishna can truly help us. Go into the forest. Sit. Think only of Him.'

And so little Dhruva, in his small yellow dhoti, walked bravely into the great forest of Madhuvana. On the way he met the wise sage Narada. Narada smiled gently at the tiny boy.

'Dear child,' said Narada, 'Krishna's path is the student's path — brahmacharya. Your eyes, your tongue, your hands, your mind — all must walk together toward Him. Can a little boy do this?'

Dhruva's big eyes looked up. 'I will try, Narada-ji.'

Narada whispered the holy name into Dhruva's ear. Then he blessed the child and went on his way.

Dhruva sat down under a banyan tree. He folded his little legs. He closed his eyes.

But OH — the forest was so full of things!

A parrot CAWED. Dhruva's little ears wanted to listen to the parrot. 'Come back, ears,' Dhruva whispered. 'Listen only to Krishna.'

A butterfly landed on his nose. Dhruva's eyes wanted to open and peek. 'Stay closed, eyes,' Dhruva whispered. 'See only Krishna inside.'

A cool breeze brought the smell of ripe mangoes. Dhruva's tongue wanted a mango. 'Quiet, tongue,' Dhruva whispered. 'Taste only the sweet name of Krishna.'

His tiny hands wanted to wiggle. His little feet wanted to run. His mind wanted to jump to games and toys and his mother's lap.

Each time, Dhruva gently, gently called them back.

'Eyes — here. Tongue — here. Hands — here. Mind — here.'

As the days passed, something beautiful happened. The parrot still cawed — but Dhruva's ears stayed with Krishna. The mango still smelled sweet — but Dhruva's tongue stayed with Krishna's name. The butterfly still landed — but Dhruva's eyes stayed shut, full of Krishna's blue smile.

Little Dhruva's senses had become his FRIENDS, not his bosses. His little self was the king of his own body.

One dawn, a great warm light filled the forest. Dhruva opened his eyes.

Krishna Himself stood before him — smiling. The Lord touched His conch gently to Dhruva's forehead. A river of sweet words came pouring from the little boy's mouth.

'My child,' Krishna whispered, 'you have walked the student's path. You have become the master of your little senses. That is brahmacharya.'

And even today, when we look up at the night sky, we see Dhruva — the North Star — shining quiet and steady. Never wavering. Always pointed at Krishna.

A Story from Today

Arjun and the Tall TV

Arjun was seven years old. He lived in Hyderabad with his Amma, his Papa, and his little sister Ishani.

Every afternoon at 4 o'clock, Chhota Bheem came on TV. Arjun LOVED Chhota Bheem. He would count the minutes from the school bus ride home.

On this Tuesday, the school bus dropped Arjun at 3:45. He ran inside. He threw his backpack on the sofa. His homework notebook peeked out. His TEN pages of maths homework. His HANDWRITING practice. Tomorrow was a class test.

But 4 o'clock! The opening song of Chhota Bheem was about to start!

Arjun's hand reached for the remote.

Then — quiet inside — he heard Amma's morning voice.

'Arjun beta, a student has a special path. It is called brahmacharya. It means YOU are the master of your eyes. YOU are the master of your hands. YOU are the master of your time. The TV does not rule you. You rule yourself.'

Arjun's fingers touched the remote. They froze.

On one shoulder, a small voice said — 'JUST ONE EPISODE! Homework later!'

On the other shoulder, a quieter voice said — 'Arjun. Master. Not slave.'

Arjun took a deep breath. Another. Another.

He put the remote back on the table.

His heart went THUMP-THUMP. It was HARD. Chhota Bheem's song was starting on TV somewhere in the world — but not in his house.

Arjun walked slowly to his notebook. He sat down. He opened it. The maths problems looked HUGE.

'One at a time, Arjun,' he whispered to himself. 'Brahmacharya.'

He did the first problem. Then the second. Then the third. His mind WANDERED three times — to Chhota Bheem, to the snack jar, to Ishani's dolls. Each time he gently called it back. 'Come back, mind. Sit with the maths.'

After forty minutes, his homework was DONE. His letters were neat. His numbers were clear.

He went to the altar. Amma was there arranging fresh marigolds. She smiled. Together they chanted three rounds of 'Hare Krishna Hare Krishna.' Arjun's eyes stayed on the little deity. His tongue stayed with the holy names. His hands stayed quiet in his lap.

Then — at 5:15 — Arjun picked up the remote. He turned on TV. A later episode of Chhota Bheem was just starting. He watched happily.

But inside his chest, something new was glowing. Soft. Warm. Proud.

At bedtime, Amma sat beside him. 'How was your afternoon, beta?'

Arjun said slowly, 'Amma, today I was the master of my remote. Not the other way.'

Amma's eyes filled with gentle tears. 'Arjun, that is brahmacharya. The tiny fire of the student's path. Krishna is very pleased.'

Arjun fell asleep with a small smile. His senses were his friends now — walking beside him, not in front of him.

Core Teaching

  1. Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH — the path of self-mastery. My eyes, my tongue, my hands, and my mind are my FRIENDS. They walk beside me, not in front of me. I choose what they do. When they try to pull me away from Krishna, from learning, or from what is right, I gently call them back — like Dhruva in the forest, like Arjun with the remote.
  2. Brahmacharya is NOT grumpy or sour. It is warm, steady, and quiet. I do not shout at my senses. I do not punish my body. I just say, kindly but firmly, 'Not now, little eyes. Not now, little tongue. First Krishna. First homework. First the right thing.' The little fire of brahmacharya grows by gentle practice, day by day.
  3. Krishna calls brahmacharya one of the body-tapas — a tiny brave fire of the student. In BG 17.14 He says it walks together with cleanness, honesty, and never-hurting. When I am the master of my senses, I am free to walk happily toward Krishna. When my senses are my bosses, I am tugged this way and that way, and I cannot find peace. The student chooses to be the master.
