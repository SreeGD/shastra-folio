About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

CHAPTER 1
ब्रह्मचर्य
Brahmacharya

Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH.

Chapter Overview

Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH. It means my mind stays focused on learning, on Krishna, and on doing what is right. My senses are my FRIENDS, not my bosses. I choose what my eyes see. I choose what my mouth says. I choose what my hands do. When my eyes want to peek at the TV before homework — I say 'not now.' When my tongue wants a second sweet — I say 'not now.' When my mind wants to wander during prayer — I call it gently back. That is brahmacharya. Self-mastery, for Krishna.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 17.14 — Krishna says: Honouring Krishna and our wise teachers, keeping clean, being straight and honest, walking the student's path of brahmacharya, and not hurting any creature — THIS is the body's little fire. The word brahmacharya means: my eyes, my mouth, my hands, and my mind are all walking toward Krishna and toward learning. I am the master of my senses, not their servant.

Core Teaching

  1. Brahmacharya is the STUDENT'S PATH — the path of self-mastery. My eyes, my tongue, my hands, and my mind are my FRIENDS. They walk beside me, not in front of me. I choose what they do. When they try to pull me away from Krishna, from learning, or from what is right, I gently call them back — like Dhruva in the forest, like Arjun with the remote.
  2. Brahmacharya is NOT grumpy or sour. It is warm, steady, and quiet. I do not shout at my senses. I do not punish my body. I just say, kindly but firmly, 'Not now, little eyes. Not now, little tongue. First Krishna. First homework. First the right thing.' The little fire of brahmacharya grows by gentle practice, day by day.
  3. Krishna calls brahmacharya one of the body-tapas — a tiny brave fire of the student. In BG 17.14 He says it walks together with cleanness, honesty, and never-hurting. When I am the master of my senses, I am free to walk happily toward Krishna. When my senses are my bosses, I am tugged this way and that way, and I cannot find peace. The student chooses to be the master.

Five-Minute Introduction

Gather the children in a circle. Hold up a small TV remote (real or pretend). Say: 'Children, WHO is the master of this remote? The remote? Or you?' They will laugh and say 'Us!' Then ask: 'Who is the master of your EYES? Your TONGUE? Your HANDS? Your MIND? Is it your eyes that drag you to the TV? Or do YOU choose?' Let them ponder. Say: 'Today we learn the beautiful word brahmacharya — ब्रह्मचर्य. It means the STUDENT'S PATH. When I am a student of Krishna, my senses are my FRIENDS — not my bosses. I choose what they see, what they say, what they do.' Touch each body part while saying softly: 'Brahmacharya means I choose — eyes (touch eyes), tongue (touch lips), hands (fold hands), mind (touch forehead).' Chant together three times, slowly: 'Brahma-charya! Brahma-charya! Brahma-charya!' Ask: 'What is ONE thing you could choose tomorrow — homework before TV, prayer before play, kind words before grumpy words?' Take 2-3 short answers with warm smiles.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Remote-and-chant intro + four-friends gesture chant (eyes, tongue, hands, mind). 10 min — Read aloud Little Dhruva's Unwavering Eyes; at 'come back, ears' children mime gently calling each sense back. 8 min — Read aloud Arjun and the Tall TV; pause at the remote-hesitation moment and ask 'What would YOU do?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Who is the Boss) + Activity 2 (Draw the Four Little Friends). 5 min — Closing Little-Dhruva Sitting Game (1 minute of silent sitting) + introduce Master-of-Myself Diary. 2 min — Line up, walking slowly like a small Dhruva.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with remote demo and four-friends chant. 15 min — Dhruva's story in the forest; children sit cross-legged like little Dhruvas while the teacher makes gentle forest sounds (bell for parrot, soft clap for butterfly); children whisper 'come back, ears/eyes/nose/mind' each time. 15 min — Arjun's story, re-enacted with a real (pretend) remote on a table; children take turns role-playing — reaching for remote, hesitating, putting it down, opening a pretend notebook. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Little-Dhruva Sitting Game as Activity 4, with gentle sense-tests: bell, clap, fruit-smell, soft feather. Debrief: which sense was hardest to hold? 15 min — Four-Friends Craft: each child makes a small paper crown with four little painted symbols — an eye, a tongue, folded hands, a thought-cloud — and writes ONE vow under each. 10 min — Biographical story: young Shri Krishnamacharya waking before dawn to study with his father; children stand and gently mime one of the boy's disciplined morning moves (fold hands, bow, sit still). 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE way they will be the master of their senses this week; teacher hands out Master-of-Myself Diaries. Buffer: 10 min for transitions.

Discussion Guide

Primary children meet brahmacharya FIRST as self-mastery — being the master of their own eyes, tongue, hands, and mind. Keep the framing concrete: 'My senses are my FRIENDS, not my BOSSES.' Avoid any adult-framed content — nothing about celibacy, marriage, or romance. That dimension belongs to middle, senior, and college levels. For primary, brahmacharya = the student's focused path. Use the four body-touch-points (eyes, lips, folded hands, forehead) throughout lessons; children need to FEEL the four little friends. Distinguish brahmacharya carefully from three anti-patterns at this age: (1) SENSE-SLAVERY — the child who is always yanked by the TV, snacks, or the latest game. Gently and firmly redirect: 'Who is the boss here — you or the remote?' (2) RIGID RULE-FOLLOWING WITHOUT WARMTH — the child who says 'I am better because I don't watch TV.' Redirect: 'Beautiful that you are choosing. But a brahmacari is warm and gentle, never proud.' (3) SHAME AFTER SLIPPING — the child who says 'I ate the sweet, I am bad.' Never shame. 'Tomorrow you get to choose again. The little fire is patient.' Watch for children who cannot sit still — brahmacharya trains the body through the Little-Dhruva Sitting Game. Start with 1 minute. Celebrate ANY stillness. Watch for the child who already self-regulates well — honour quietly, but do not single out; that feeds pride. Connect brahmacharya to bhakti EVERYWHERE: 'Why do I choose? Because Krishna is waiting for me at the end of the student's path.' Most importantly — model brahmacharya yourself. Put your own phone face-down during class. Begin lessons on time. Speak only kind words even when tired. Children catch the student's path from an adult who walks it.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Brahmacharya means I can never watch TV, eat sweets, or play games.' Response: Not at all! Brahmacharya does not say NEVER — it says NOT YET, and NOT AS THE BOSS. Arjun still watched Chhota Bheem — but AFTER homework and prayer. Tanvi still enjoyed the first laddoo — she just did not let her tongue command a second before the offering was done. Brahmacharya keeps happy things in their right place. It does not throw them away.
  • Misconception: 'Brahmacharya is for grown-up sadhus in the forest, not for a seven-year-old.' Response: Dhruva was FIVE when he walked the student's path in Madhuvana. Prahlada was also very small. Krishna's Gita (BG 17.14) names brahmacharya inside the BODY-TAPAS — the little fire every student can light. For a primary child it is simple: homework before cartoons, prayer before play, kind words instead of rude ones, bringing the mind back to Krishna during bhajan. These tiny choices ARE brahmacharya.
  • Misconception: 'If I slip — eat the sweet, shout the mean word, watch TV before homework — my brahmacharya is broken.' Response: The OPPOSITE. Every time the mind-monkey jumps, we gently call it back — and the CALLING-BACK is the tapas. If you watched TV first today, tomorrow you get to choose again. The little fire of brahmacharya is patient. Krishna is patient. We just keep coming back to the student's path, gently, kindly, day after day.
  • Misconception: 'Brahmacharya means being grumpy, strict, and never smiling.' Response: Never. Dhruva smiled when Krishna touched him. Arjun smiled with a warm chest. Krishna says in BG 17.16 that true tapas includes saumyatvam — gentleness — and manaḥ-prasādaḥ — a glad peaceful mind. A grumpy student is not a brahmacari, only a grumpy child. The student's path is WARM and STEADY. Keep the smile.
