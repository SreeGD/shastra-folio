About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Worksheet

Brahmacharya Worksheet — The Student's Path

Activity 1: Who is the Boss? (Circle)

In each scene, who was the boss — ME (brahmacharya) or my SENSES (they ruled me)?

1. I came home, dropped my bag, and did my homework BEFORE turning on the TV. ME MY-SENSES

2. I saw a laddoo and grabbed three before dinner, even though Amma said wait. ME MY-SENSES

3. My mind wandered during bhajan to my toys; I gently brought it back to Krishna. ME MY-SENSES

4. My friend called me a mean name, so I shouted back right away without thinking. ME MY-SENSES

5. I wanted to watch one more YouTube video, but I closed the tablet because it was prayer time. ME MY-SENSES

6. I was tired so I skipped brushing my teeth and just hopped into bed. ME MY-SENSES

Activity 2: Draw the Four Little Friends

Draw YOURSELF in the centre. Around you, draw your four little sense-friends:

  • EYES — two little eyes looking toward a picture of Krishna
  • TONGUE — a little mouth saying 'Hare Krishna'
  • HANDS — two little hands folded at the heart
  • MIND — a little thought-cloud with Krishna inside

Under each friend, write ONE thing you will ask it to do tomorrow. Example: 'Eyes — look at my Gita page before looking at TV.'

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me — The Four-Friends Chant

Stand tall with palms folded at your heart.

Chant three times — touching each body part softly:

Brah-ma-cha-rya! *(touch your eyes)* My eyes see Krishna. *(touch your lips)* My tongue chants Krishna. *(fold your hands)* My hands serve Krishna. *(touch your forehead)* My mind stays with Krishna.

Activity 4: The Little-Dhruva Sitting Game (Paired or Whole Class)

Everyone sits cross-legged. Eyes gently closed. Hands on the lap.

The teacher (or a partner) makes small gentle noises — a bell, a soft clap, a parrot-sound, a sweet-smell (like a fruit held near the nose).

The little Dhruvas try to stay still and keep their minds on Krishna.

  • If your eyes peek — smile and close them again. That is brahmacharya.
  • If your mind jumps to the parrot — gently call it back. That is brahmacharya.
  • If your nose wiggles for the fruit — whisper 'later' and return to Krishna. That is brahmacharya.

Play for 2 minutes. Slowly increase to 3, 4, 5 minutes over the week.

Activity 5: Take Home — The Master-of-Myself Diary

For one week, keep a small diary by your bed. Each night:

1. Draw a tiny CROWN for every moment you were the master of your senses today. (Did homework before TV? One crown. Said no to a second sweet? One crown. Brought your mind back to Krishna during bhajan? One crown. Chose kind words when tongue wanted to shout? One crown.)

2. Draw a tiny UMBRELLA for every moment your senses were your boss. (No shame — just gentle honest noticing.)

3. Whisper: 'Krishna, tomorrow more crowns, fewer umbrellas. I am the student. I walk with You.'

At the end of the week, count your crowns. Each crown is the little fire of brahmacharya glowing bright.

Structured Activities

  • Who is the Boss? (solo, 6 min)

    For each scenario, circle ME (brahmacharya, I chose) or MY-SENSES (they pulled me). Notice the pattern across six everyday moments.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • Draw the Four Little Friends (solo, 10 min)

    Draw yourself in the centre. Around you, draw your four sense-friends (eyes, tongue, hands, mind). Under each, write one thing it will do for Krishna tomorrow.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Pencils

  • The Four-Friends Chant (whole_class, 3 min)

    Stand tall. Touch eyes, lips, fold hands, touch forehead as you chant: Brahmacharya! My eyes see Krishna. My tongue chants Krishna. My hands serve Krishna. My mind stays with Krishna.

  • The Little-Dhruva Sitting Game (whole_class, 8 min)

    Sit cross-legged, eyes gently closed, hands on lap. Teacher makes small sense-tests (bell, smell, clap, feather). Gently call each sense back to Krishna. Start with 2 minutes; grow across the week.

    Materials: Small bell, A piece of fruit, A feather

  • The Master-of-Myself Diary (solo, 5 min)

    Each night, draw a tiny crown for each moment you were the master of your senses, and a tiny umbrella for moments the senses were your boss. Whisper a brahmacharya prayer to Krishna.

    Materials: Small notebook, Gold and brown pencils

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Master-of-Myself Diary beside your pillow. Each evening, before sleep, think about the day. For EACH moment you were the master of your senses — did homework before TV, said no to a second sweet, brought your mind back to Krishna during bhajan, kept kind words when your tongue wanted to shout, put away the tablet at prayer time — draw one tiny GOLDEN CROWN in your diary. For each moment the senses were your boss, draw a tiny UMBRELLA (gentle, no shame). Then put your hand on your heart and whisper: 'Krishna, I am Your little student. Tomorrow, more crowns. Tomorrow, I choose again.' In the morning, before rising, touch your eyes, lips, hands, and forehead and whisper: 'Eyes, tongue, hands, mind — today we walk together with Krishna.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific moments they were 'the master' of a sense; can demonstrate the four-friends gesture (touch eyes, touch lips, fold hands, touch forehead); and the diary contains at least 7 tiny crowns. The child uses the phrase 'my senses are my friends' or 'I am the master' spontaneously when describing a moment of self-control. The child can sit still for 2-3 minutes during family bhajan without needing reminders.
