About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Worksheet

Guru-Bhakti Worksheet — Loving My Teacher

Activity 1: Bow, Ask, Serve — Which One?

Krishna's verse tells us three ways to show guru-bhakti: bow down (praṇipāta), ask good questions (paripraśna), serve with love (sevā). Look at each picture-story. Circle the matching word.

1. Rani folds her hands and touches her teacher's feet when the teacher enters. BOW / ASK / SERVE

2. Dev raises his hand: 'Teacher, I did not understand — can you please show me again?' BOW / ASK / SERVE

3. Ananya quietly wipes the blackboard after class without being told. BOW / ASK / SERVE

4. Vikram brings one flower from his garden for his teacher's table. BOW / ASK / SERVE

5. Priya looks up with bright eyes and says 'Good morning, teacher!' BOW / ASK / SERVE

Activity 2: My Three Teachers (Draw)

Inside the three hearts below, draw three teachers from your life.

❤️ Heart 1 — My school teacher (write their name under the drawing) ❤️ Heart 2 — A grandparent, Amma, or Appa who taught me something ❤️ Heart 3 — Krishna — the teacher of ALL teachers

Under each heart, write ONE thing they taught you.

[Drawing space — three large hearts]

Activity 3: Say It With Me — the Praṇāma

Palms pressed together at the heart. Bow slightly. Chant with your class three times:

Guruve namaḥ! Guruve namaḥ! Guruve namaḥ! *(I bow to my guru. I bow to my guru. I bow to my guru.)*

Activity 4: The 'Thank-You Teacher' Card (Solo Craft)

Fold a sheet of paper in half to make a card.

  • On the front, draw your teacher with a bright smile.
  • Inside the card, write (or ask Amma to help write): 'Thank you, teacher, for _____.' Fill in ONE specific thing.
  • Decorate with flowers, stars, and one small tulsi leaf if you have one.
  • On Monday morning, give the card quietly — touch their feet or fold your hands and offer the card.

Activity 5: Take Home — The Sunday Guru-Bhakti Ritual

Every Sunday morning, do the Three-Step Guru Ritual at home:

1. Bow: Stand in front of Krishna's picture and a photo of your school teacher (or a photo of a teacher you love). Fold your hands. Bow your head.

2. Ask: Whisper one question you want to learn this week. ('Krishna, help me understand maths better.' OR 'Teacher, please help me write nicer letters.')

3. Serve: Do ONE small service for a teacher this week — carry books, wipe the board, water the class plant, say a clear 'Good morning'.

📿 🙏 🌸 — Take a coloured sticker each Sunday you do all three.

Structured Activities

  • Bow, Ask, Serve — Which One? (solo, 8 min)

    Read five little picture-stories. Circle whether the child is showing bowing (praṇipāta), asking (paripraśna), or serving (sevā) — the three steps from BG 4.34.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • My Three Teachers (solo, 10 min)

    Draw three teachers inside three hearts — school teacher, family teacher, and Krishna the teacher of teachers. Under each, write one thing they taught you.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Pencil

  • Say It With Me — the Praṇāma (whole_class, 4 min)

    Stand with palms pressed at the heart. Bow slightly. Chant 'Guruve namaḥ' three times together as a class.

  • The Thank-You Teacher Card (solo, 15 min)

    Make a card for a teacher. Draw their face on the front, write 'Thank you teacher for ___' inside, decorate with flowers and a tulsi leaf. Offer it on Monday with folded hands.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Markers, One tulsi leaf if available

  • The Sunday Guru Ritual (Take-Home) (solo, 10 min)

    Every Sunday for four weeks, do three steps: BOW to Krishna's picture plus a teacher photo, ASK one learning question aloud, and PLAN one small service for a teacher that week.

    Materials: Krishna picture, Teacher photo, Stickers for weekly tracking

Home Practice

The Sunday Three-Step Guru Ritual: every Sunday morning for four weeks, do three tiny actions. (1) BOW — stand in front of Krishna's picture plus a photo of your school teacher (or any teacher you love). Fold hands. Bow your head. (2) ASK — whisper aloud ONE question you want to learn this week. (3) SERVE — plan ONE small service for a teacher in the coming week: carry their books, wipe the board, water the plant, say a clear 'Good morning, teacher' with folded hands, or hand in your neatest work. On one special Sunday, take a small gift (one flower, one fruit, a hand-drawn card) to a teacher — especially an OLD teacher from last year or kindergarten — and simply say 'Thank you, teacher.' Touch their feet if your family practices it. Before bed each Sunday, tell Amma or Appa which of the three steps you did.

Duration: Every Sunday for four weeks, with one special 'thank-you visit' during the month
Look for: By the end of four weeks, the child can name three teachers and one specific thing each one gave them. The child voluntarily greets teachers with folded hands and offers their neatest work. Parents and old teachers report being surprised and moved by the child's unprompted thank-you.
