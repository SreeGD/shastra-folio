About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Gratitude Activities

1. Story Pictures

Look at these pictures and circle the ones that show gratitude:
  • [ ] A child saying "thank you" to their teacher
  • [ ] Someone throwing food away
  • [ ] A child hugging their parent
  • [ ] Someone grabbing toys without asking

2. Make a Gratitude Sun Catcher

Materials: Yellow paper, crayons, scissors, string

Steps:

1. Cut out a big yellow circle (like the sun)

2. Cut out 8 smaller circles for sun rays

3. On each small circle, draw something you are grateful for

4. Glue the small circles around the big circle

5. Make a hole at the top and put string through it

6. Hang your gratitude sun in the window

While you make it, think: What makes you feel warm and happy like sunshine?

3. Gratitude Dance Game

How to play:

1. Stand in a circle with your friends

2. When the music plays, dance around

3. When the music stops, the teacher calls out someone to thank (like "parents" or "trees")

4. Everyone must freeze and put their hands on their heart

5. Say "Thank you" together three times

6. Start dancing again!

Debrief questions:

  • How did it feel to say thank you together?
  • Which person or thing was hardest to remember to thank?

4. Color the Thank You Tree

Color this tree and draw fruits on it. In each fruit, draw a picture of something you want to thank God for.

Structured Activities

  • Gratitude Sun Catcher Craft (solo, 15 min)

    Create a sun catcher with 8 rays, drawing something you're grateful for on each ray. Hang it in a window to remind you of all your blessings.

    Materials: Yellow paper, crayons, scissors, string, hole punch

  • Gratitude Dance Game (whole_class, 10 min)

    Dance in a circle, freeze when music stops, and say thank you together for whatever the teacher calls out (parents, trees, food, etc.)

    Materials: Music player, space to move

  • Thank You Tree Drawing (solo, 8 min)

    Color a tree and draw fruits on it. In each fruit, draw a picture of something you want to thank God for.

    Materials: Coloring sheet, crayons

  • Daily Gratitude Sharing Circle (whole_class, 7 min)

    Sit in a circle and each person shares one thing they're grateful for today, using the talking stick format.

    Materials: Talking stick or special object

Home Practice

Before each meal this week, look at your food and say 'Thank you Krishna for this food' out loud. Then tell one person at the table something you are grateful for that day.

Duration: Before every meal for one week
Look for: You will notice that meal times feel more peaceful and happy, and your family will start sharing grateful thoughts too.
