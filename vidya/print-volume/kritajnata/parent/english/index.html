About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Kritajnata

Gratitude means saying thank you with your heart. It means being happy for all the good things you have. When someone helps you or gives you something, you feel thankful inside.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.26: If one offers Me with love a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

What Kritajnata is NOT

Gratitude is not just saying thank you with your mouth but not meaning it. It is not taking things without caring who gave them to you.

  • Forgetting to say thank you when someone helps you
  • Always asking for more things instead of being happy with what you have
  • Taking food, toys, and help for granted

Stories Your Child Heard

The Grateful Squirrel — Long ago, Lord Rama needed to cross the ocean to save Sita. The monkey army was building a big bridge to Lanka. Strong monkeys carried huge rocks and threw them into the water. A tiny squirrel watched from a tree. "I want to help Lord Rama too," she thought. "But I am so small. The monkeys are so big and strong."

Maya's Thank You Garden — Maya was seven years old and lived in a small town in Kenya. Every morning, her mother made her breakfast. Her teacher helped her read new words. Her friend Kesi shared crayons with her. But Maya always forgot to say thank you. When her mother gave her food, Maya just ate it. When her teacher helped her, Maya just nodded. When Kesi shared crayons, Maya just took them.

The Sanskrit Word

kṛtajñatā (कृतज्ञता) — kri-ta-GYAH-taa

Meaning: gratitude, being thankful, remembering kindness

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Before each meal this week, look at your food and say 'Thank you Krishna for this food' out loud. Then tell one person at the table something you are grateful for that day.

Duration: Before every meal for one week
Look for: You will notice that meal times feel more peaceful and happy, and your family will start sharing grateful thoughts too.

Signs of Growth

You will notice that meal times feel more peaceful and happy, and your family will start sharing grateful thoughts too.
