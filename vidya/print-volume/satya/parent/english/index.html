About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Satya

Satya means speaking the truth and living the truth. We say what really happened — even when it is hard. We keep the promises we make. When we do not know something, we say, 'I do not know.' Truth makes our heart light and our words strong.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2: Do not hurt anyone. Speak the truth. Do not get angry. Give up what you do not need. Be peaceful. Do not find fault. Be kind to every living being. Do not be greedy. Be gentle. Be humble. Do not give up.

What Satya is NOT

Satya is not making up stories so we do not get in trouble. It is not half-truths that trick others. It is not pretending we did something we did not do. When we hide the truth, a small stone sits on our heart.

  • Saying 'It wasn't me!' when you broke the cup
  • Telling Amma you brushed your teeth when you didn't
  • Blaming a friend for something you did

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Krishna and the Butter Pot — Long ago in the village of Vrindavan, a little blue boy named Krishna lived with his mother Yashoda. Krishna loved butter. He loved it more than anything else. Every morning, Mother Yashoda churned fresh butter in a big clay pot. She hung the pot high — very high — so little Krishna could not reach.

Anu and the Broken Flower Pot — Anu was playing with her red rubber ball. Amma had said, 'Not inside, Anu. Play outside.' But the sky looked cloudy. So Anu bounced the ball just a little. Once. Twice. Three times. On the fourth bounce — CRASH! — the ball hit Amma's favourite clay flower pot. The pot broke into six pieces. The jasmine plant lay on the floor.

The Sanskrit Word

satya (सत्य) — SAT-ya

Meaning: Truth. Saying what really happened. Keeping promises. Words that match the heart.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep a Truth Diary with Amma or Appa. Each night at bedtime, share three things: (1) one true thing you said today, (2) one promise you kept, and (3) one time you said 'I don't know' instead of guessing. Draw a small smile beside each one. Also: before sleeping, place your hand on your heart and ask quietly, 'Is there anything I am hiding today?' If yes, tell someone kind tomorrow morning.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 times they told a hard truth, and can demonstrate the 'stone on heart / light heart' gesture. Parents report the child volunteering truths without being asked.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 times they told a hard truth, and can demonstrate the 'stone on heart / light heart' gesture. Parents report the child volunteering truths without being asked.

When our words are always true, people begin to trust us — and from trust grows a deeper gift called shraddha, our faith.
