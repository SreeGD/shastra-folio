About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Samata

Samata means treating everyone with equal kindness — rich or poor, friend or stranger, the one who wins and the one who loses.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 6.32

आत्मौपम्येन सर्वत्र समं पश्यति योऽर्जुन।
सुखं वा यदि वा दुःखं स योगी परमो मतः॥

ātmaupamyena sarvatra samaṁ paśyati yo 'rjuna sukhaṁ vā yadi vā duḥkhaṁ sa yogī paramo mataḥ

Word-for-word: ātma — self; aupamyena — by comparison; sarvatra — everywhere; samam — equally; paśyati — sees; yaḥ — who; arjuna — O Arjuna; sukham — happiness; vā — or; yadi — if; vā — or; duḥkham — sorrow; saḥ — that; yogī — friend of Krishna; paramaḥ — the best; mataḥ — is considered.

Translation: O Arjuna, the one who sees every person the same way they see themselves — feeling another's joy and sadness as their own — that friend of Krishna is the very best.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 6.17.28

तितिक्षवः कारुणिकाः सुहृदः सर्वदेहिनाम्।
अजातशत्रवः शान्ताः साधवः साधुभूषणाः॥

titikṣavaḥ kāruṇikāḥ suhṛdaḥ sarva-dehinām ajāta-śatravaḥ śāntāḥ sādhavaḥ sādhu-bhūṣaṇāḥ

Translation: The true saints are patient, kind, and friendly to every living being. They make no enemies anywhere. They are peaceful, and their jewellery is their goodness itself.

What is Samata?

Samata means treating everyone with equal kindness — rich or poor, friend or stranger, the one who wins and the one who loses. We see the same shining soul inside every person. Our smile is the same for all.

What it is NOT

Samata is not being friends with only the popular kids. It is not changing our kindness based on who is watching. It is not being nice to people who give us things and mean to people who don't.

  • Playing only with the children who have new toys
  • Saying 'hello' loudly to the principal but not to the sweeper
  • Being kind to a friend when they win but cold when they lose

Sanskrit Word Study

samatā (समता)

Pronunciation: sa-ma-TAA

Meaning: Equal vision. Seeing and treating everyone with the same kindness, because the same soul shines in every heart.

Example: When Ananya sat next to Golu with the same smile she gave her best friend, she was practising samata.

Story from the Scriptures

Rama and the Old Woman of the Forest — Ramayana, Aranya Kanda (adapted for primary students)

Long ago, in the deep green forest, a prince named Rama walked with His brother Lakshmana. Their feet were dusty. The sun was hot.

Deep in the trees lived an old woman named Shabari. She was from a forest tribe. Her hair was white as cotton. Her hut was tiny. For many years she had waited — just to see Rama one day.

When Shabari heard Rama was coming, her hands shook. 'But I am just an old tribal woman,' she whispered. 'Rama is a prince. What will I give Him?'

She picked berries from the bushes. To make sure each berry was sweet, she took a tiny bite first. Only the sweetest ones went into her small wooden bowl.

Rama arrived. He bent low and came into the hut. Shabari fell at His feet, crying with joy.

'Please,' she said, holding out the berries. 'But I have tasted each one first. They are not pure. Please forgive me.'

Rama smiled the brightest smile. He sat down on her little mat as if it were a throne. He took the berries in His own hand and ate them one by one.

'Mother,' He said softly, 'these are the sweetest berries I have ever eaten. Because you tasted them with love — they are doubly sweet.'

Lakshmana watched with wonder. The same Rama who spoke to kings Vibhishana and Sugriva with honour — now spoke to this tribal mother with the very same love.

Shabari's eyes shone like stars. She understood. Rama's heart had no 'big' and 'small'. His love was the same for everyone.

That is samata — equal eyes, equal heart.

A Story from Today

Ananya's Lunchbox

Ananya was seven. She went to a bright school in Pune.

At lunch, Ananya sat with the 'pink-tiffin girls'. Their lunchboxes were bright pink. Their snacks were fancy. They laughed a lot.

In the corner sat Golu. His uniform was faded. His shoes had a small hole. Every day he opened a steel box with one plain roti and a little pickle. He ate alone.

One day, the pink-tiffin girls saw Golu's lunch. 'Eww, just a roti!' one giggled. 'Don't sit near him.'

Ananya laughed too. But something felt sharp inside her heart, like a thorn.

That evening, Ananya's Daddi told her a story. 'Rama once sat on an old tribal woman's mat and ate her berries. He saw only the soul inside.' Daddi touched Ananya's chest. 'Everyone has the same light in here. Samata.'

Next day, Ananya picked up her lunchbox. Her feet walked past the pink-tiffin girls.

She sat down next to Golu. 'Hi! Want to share? Amma made aloo paratha.'

Golu's eyes went wide. Slowly, a little smile crept up. He broke his plain roti in half. 'Want some? Amma makes the softest rotis.'

Ananya took a bite. 'Oh wow! It IS the softest.'

Then something wonderful happened. One by one, other children came over with their lunchboxes. They all sat in one big circle, sharing everything.

The pink-tiffin girls watched. One by one, they came too.

Ananya looked around. Everyone was eating. Everyone was smiling.

That is samata — the same kindness for every friend.

Core Teaching

  1. Samata means we give the same kindness to everyone — rich and poor, new and old, the child with many toys and the child with few. Every soul shines the same inside.
  2. When we see someone alone or different, we do not laugh or walk past. We sit down next to them with the same smile we give our best friend.
  3. Krishna sees every living being equally — the king and the sweeper, the tiger and the ant. When we love everyone equally, our heart becomes like Krishna's heart.
