About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Pratijna

Pratijna means a promise I truly keep.

Chapter Overview

Pratijna means a promise I truly keep. When I say I will do something, I do it — even when I don't feel like it. Even when it is hard. My word is my strength. A small promise kept makes my heart brave. A big promise kept makes my heart like a mountain.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 9.31 — Krishna says: O Arjuna, declare this as a solemn promise — My devotee is never lost. A person who walks in dharma quickly becomes peaceful. You can take this as My vow.

Core Teaching

  1. Pratijna means a promise I truly keep. When I say I will do something, I do it — even when I do not feel like it. My word becomes my strength.
  2. We should think BEFORE we promise. A small promise kept is bigger than ten big promises forgotten. If we are not sure, we say, 'Let me think first.' When we finally say YES, we mean it.
  3. Each kept promise makes the heart a little stronger — like stacking bricks in a tall wall. Krishna Himself makes promises, and never breaks them. When we keep our word, we become a little bit like Him.

Five-Minute Introduction

Stand in a circle. Ask the children: 'Who has made a promise this week?' Take 2-3 small answers. Then ask: 'And who KEPT the promise?' Celebrate each raised hand. Say: 'In our tradition, a promise kept is so sacred that it has its own beautiful name — pratijna.' Chant together with hand on heart: 'Pra-tij-ñā! Pra-tij-ñā! Pra-tij-ñā!' Then tell them, 'Today we meet Bhishma — a boy whose promise shook the earth. And we meet Arjun — a boy whose promise was small, but strong like a mountain.' Open the storybook.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Opening circle + pratijna chant with hand on heart. 10 min — Bhishma's story; children act out raising the hand to make the terrible vow. 8 min — Arjun's Rainy-Day story; children mime the sleepy-voice vs. the true-voice. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Kept/Broken) and Activity 2 (Promise Wall). 5 min — Closing chant; tie a Promise Ribbon on each child and explain the take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with 'who kept a promise this week?' + pratijna chant. 15 min — Bhishma story with a sky-blue scarf (Ganga) and a brass bell that rings when the vow is made; one child plays Devavrata raising his hand. 15 min — Arjun's story, acted with a real umbrella and a small basket of paper marigolds. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2 (Kept/Broken + Promise Wall drawing). 20 min — Two Voices Role-Play (Activity 4); rotate pairs twice; debrief 'which voice felt lighter?' 15 min — Promise Ribbon craft: each child decorates a simple cotton ribbon with marker and receives help tying it on the wrist. 15 min — Whole-class 'Class Pratijna' — the whole class makes ONE small shared promise for the week (e.g., 'We will say good morning to the guard.'). 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE promise they will keep tomorrow. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn pratijna through the BODY first — standing tall, hand on heart, feeling strength in the spine. Use the 'brick wall' metaphor throughout. Each kept promise = one brick. A wall built slowly is strong; a wall thrown up all at once falls down. This teaches patience. Distinguish pratijna from satya carefully: satya is the honesty of words; pratijna is the follow-through of actions. A child can SAY a true thing (satya) but still not DO what they promised (weak pratijna). Both must walk together. Also distinguish pratijna from stubbornness — a rash promise made in anger (like 'I will NEVER talk to you again!') is not real pratijna. Real pratijna comes from a quiet, thoughtful yes. Teach the pause before promising: 'Let me think first.' Celebrate EVERY small kept promise in class — praise the brick, not the whole wall. A child whose wall has three bricks is doing beautifully. Never shame a child for a broken promise; invite them to try again tomorrow. The Promise Ribbon is a gentle reminder, not a punishment.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'A promise is only real if I say the word I promise.' Response: Not at all. Every 'yes' we give is a small promise. 'Yes, I will bring the book.' 'Yes, I will come at five.' These are all promises. Pratijna teaches us to make every YES true — not only the big, loud ones.
  • Misconception: 'If it is hard, I can break my promise — I did not know it would be hard.' Response: A promise does not disappear when things become hard. That is exactly WHEN it matters. Bhishma's vow was hard for a hundred years — and he kept it. For us, a rainy morning or a boring chore is our small Bhishma-moment. That is when pratijna grows strong.
  • Misconception: 'Making a big promise makes me important.' Response: Making BIG promises is easy. KEEPING small promises is the real greatness. One kept promise is worth a hundred loud promises. Let us promise less — and do more.
