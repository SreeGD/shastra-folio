About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.1

अभयं सत्त्वसंशुद्धिर्ज्ञानयोगव्यवस्थितिः।
दानं दमश्च यज्ञश्च स्वाध्यायस्तप आर्जवम्॥

abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhir jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ dānaṁ damaś ca yajñaś ca svādhyāyas tapa ārjavam

Word-for-word: abhayam — fearlessness; sattva-saṁśuddhiḥ — a clean heart; jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ — sitting in wisdom; dānam — giving freely; damaḥ — calm control; yajñaḥ — offering; svādhyāyaḥ — studying holy words; tapaḥ — doing hard good things; ārjavam — being simple and straight.

Translation: Be brave. Keep your heart clean. Sit in wisdom. Give freely. Stay calm. Offer your work. Study holy words. Do hard good things. And be simple and straight — the way you feel inside is the way you show outside.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 4.8.22

यस्यानुग्रहमिच्छामि तस्य विध्नमि संपदाम्।
यथा प्रपद्यमानस्य भजन्ति सुतरां गुणाः॥

yasyānugraham icchāmi tasya vidhnami sampadām yathā prapadyamānasya bhajanti sutarāṁ guṇāḥ

Translation: Krishna says: the simple, surrendered heart of a child like Dhruva — without cleverness, without pride — attracts My favour most swiftly. All good qualities come naturally to such a one.

What is Arjava?

Arjava means being simple and straight. What is inside our heart is the same as what we say with our mouth and do with our hands. No hiding, no pretending, no two faces. Our heart is clear like fresh water — Krishna can see right through it, and so can our friends.

What it is NOT

Arjava is not telling little lies to escape trouble. It is not smiling outside when we feel angry inside and planning something sneaky. It is not acting good only when the teacher is watching. Being simple is brave — being tricky is never strong.

  • Saying 'I finished my homework' when really we have not
  • Saying sorry with your mouth but still feeling angry in your heart
  • Pretending to like a friend's drawing but whispering mean things behind them

Sanskrit Word Study

ārjava (आर्जव)

Pronunciation: AAR-ja-va

Meaning: Being simple and straight. Our heart, our words, and our actions all match — no hiding, no pretending.

Example: When I told Amma straight away that I broke the glass, I was practicing arjava.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Dhruva Asks Straight from the Heart — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 4, Chapters 8-9

Long ago there was a prince named Dhruva. He was five years old, with big eyes and a small, honest face.

One evening Dhruva ran to his father, King Uttanapada. He wanted to sit on his lap. But his stepmother was already there. She looked at Dhruva coldly. 'This lap is not for you. You were not born from me.'

Dhruva's heart hurt. He did not shout. He did not make up a clever story. He went straight to his mother Suniti. 'Mother, I want a lap where nobody can push me off. Where can I find it?'

Suniti hugged him. 'Only Lord Vishnu has such a lap, my son. But the forest is dark, and you are so small.'

Dhruva did not argue. He did not pretend to be brave. He just said, 'Then I will go find Him.'

The little boy walked into the forest. The sage Narada met him. 'Go home, little one. The forest is not for children.'

Dhruva looked straight at the sage. 'I am going to find Vishnu. My heart is already on the way.'

Narada smiled. This child was so simple, so straight, that even a tiger would not trouble him. He taught Dhruva one mantra: 'Om namo bhagavate vasudevaya.'

Dhruva sat under a tree. He closed his eyes. He chanted with a clean heart — no pretending, no asking for tricks. Just, 'Lord, I want You.'

After many days, light filled the forest. Lord Vishnu stood smiling before him. He touched Dhruva's cheek with His conch. The boy could now speak beautiful prayers — his simple heart had become the Lord's flute.

A straight heart always reaches Krishna first.

A Story from Today

The Blue Paint on Meera's Dress

Meera's Amma had stitched her a brand-new white dress for her seventh birthday, with tiny pink flowers at the hem. Meera loved it so much she wore it to school the very next day.

In art class, the teacher gave everyone pots of poster paint. Meera was painting a peacock. Right at the tail feathers — CRASH! Her elbow bumped the pot. A big blot of blue paint jumped onto her lovely dress.

Meera froze. Her classmate Radhika whispered quickly, 'Quick! Pull your sweater down — cover it! Tell Amma you got it dirty at lunch.'

Meera looked at the blue blot. It was shaped like a small lake.

All the way home, the blue lake seemed to grow bigger and bigger under her sweater. Meera tried to practice the story in her mind. 'At lunch… the dal spilled… no… a dog pushed me…'

Every story felt heavy. It was like stones in her tummy.

Amma opened the door. 'Welcome home, birthday girl!'

Meera looked at her mother's happy face. Suddenly all the practiced stories fell away. She took a big breath. She pulled up her sweater.

'Amma, I am sorry. I knocked over the blue paint in art class. I tried to think of a story, but — the dress is spoiled.'

Amma knelt down. She looked at the blot, then at Meera's eyes. She hugged her tight.

'My brave, simple girl. The dress can be washed. A hidden heart is harder to clean.'

They soaked the dress together. The blue did not all come out. But Meera's chest felt as light as a kite. That is arjava.

Core Teaching

  1. Arjava means what is inside is the same as what is outside. Our heart, our words, and our hands all say the same thing — together.
  2. Telling the simple truth feels scary for one minute. Hiding the truth feels heavy all day long. Krishna loves a clean, simple heart more than a clever, tangled one.
  3. When we are straight, Krishna can come close. A hidden heart has no door for Him. An honest heart is like an open window — the Lord walks right in.
