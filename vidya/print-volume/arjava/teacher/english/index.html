About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Arjava

Arjava means being simple and straight.

Chapter Overview

Arjava means being simple and straight. What is inside our heart is the same as what we say with our mouth and do with our hands. No hiding, no pretending, no two faces. Our heart is clear like fresh water — Krishna can see right through it, and so can our friends.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.1 — Be brave. Keep your heart clean. Sit in wisdom. Give freely. Stay calm. Offer your work. Study holy words. Do hard good things. And be simple and straight — the way you feel inside is the way you show outside.

Core Teaching

  1. Arjava means what is inside is the same as what is outside. Our heart, our words, and our hands all say the same thing — together.
  2. Telling the simple truth feels scary for one minute. Hiding the truth feels heavy all day long. Krishna loves a clean, simple heart more than a clever, tangled one.
  3. When we are straight, Krishna can come close. A hidden heart has no door for Him. An honest heart is like an open window — the Lord walks right in.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin with everyone standing. Ask: 'Can you walk in a straight line from here to the door?' Let two or three children try. Then say: 'A straight line is the shortest way. Today we learn a word from our tradition about a straight HEART. The word is arjava.' Draw a zig-zag on the board. Then draw a straight line below it. 'Which one is easier to follow?' Chant 'arjavam, arjavam, arjavam' three times together, palms at heart. Then ask: 'Is there a time you wanted to hide something small?' Take 2-3 short answers — but do not press. Thank any child who shares.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Intro: walk the straight line + chant arjavam. 10 min — Read aloud: Little Dhruva Asks Straight from the Heart. Pause and lift a pretend lotus to the sky together. 8 min — Read aloud: The Blue Paint on Meera's Dress. Act out 'pulling up the sweater' gesture. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Straight or Tangled) and Activity 2 (One Heart, One Face). 5 min — Closing chant and the Clean Heart Diary take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, walk-the-straight-line warm-up, chant 'arjavam'. 15 min — Little Dhruva story with a paper lotus children take turns lifting. 15 min — The Blue Paint story with a real sweater and a blue paint patch prop; children practice 'pulling up the sweater' gesture. 20 min — Activities 1 & 2 (Straight or Tangled + One Heart, One Face). 20 min — Paired Twisty Sentence Game (Activity 4) — rotate partners twice. 15 min — Clean Heart Craft: fold a paper heart in two; on one side draw 'feeling inside', on the other 'face outside' — then glue them so they match. 15 min — Circle story-sharing: each child says one true, simple thing about their day (no judgement, pass is allowed). 10 min — Closing: the Clean Heart Diary take-home; chant arjavam three times with palms at heart. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children understand arjava through the BODY of feelings — the heavy tummy when hiding, the light chest after telling the truth. Anchor in sensation, not in rules. Use the 'stones in the tummy' image often; it is unforgettable. Avoid shaming any child who confesses something — even late. The moment they are simple and straight is the moment they have already begun to practice arjava; reinforce that moment warmly. For the quiet child who rarely shares: never extract confession; just model arjava yourself ('Teacher forgot to bring the book today — I am sorry, friends') and let them absorb. Watch also for the 'performative good child' who is always pleasing — gently invite them to say one true thing, even if small ('Today I felt sleepy in math'). That moment of small honesty is more precious than a week of pretended perfection.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Arjava means I have to tell every single thing to everybody.' Response: No. Arjava means we do not pretend. Some things are private — that is different from hiding. Private means 'I am not ready to share yet.' Hiding means 'I am showing a false face.' Private is okay. False is not.
  • Misconception: 'If I am straight, I will hurt people's feelings.' Response: Arjava walks hand-in-hand with kindness (daya). We say true things in a gentle voice. 'I do not like this game' is better than 'You are a stupid game-maker.' Straight does not mean rough. Straight means honest AND kind — together.
  • Misconception: 'A small lie is okay if no one finds out.' Response: Somebody always finds out — our own heart. A small lie makes a small stone in our tummy. Ten small lies make a heavy bag we have to carry. Arjava puts the bag down. Krishna sees a clean heart and stays close.
