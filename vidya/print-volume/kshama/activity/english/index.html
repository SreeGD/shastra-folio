About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Kshama Worksheet — The Hot-Stone Let-Go

Activity 1: Hot Stone or Soft Heart? (Circle)

Your teacher will show or read each scene. If the child is HOLDING the hot stone (keeping the hurt tight), circle the closed fist ✊. If the child is LETTING GO with kshama, circle the open heart 💗.

1. Ravi's sister accidentally tore his drawing. He yells, 'I will throw HER drawings in the bin tomorrow!' ✊ 💗

2. Meera's friend forgot her birthday. Meera feels sad, breathes three times, and says 'It's okay, I forgive you.' ✊ 💗

3. Sam's classmate bumped him on the playground and said sorry. Sam says, 'Okay! Let's keep playing.' ✊ 💗

4. Little Tara's cousin ate her last laddoo. Tara has been angry for three whole days and will not speak to him. ✊ 💗

5. Arjun's friend called him a mean name. Arjun tells the teacher calmly, and then lets the hurt go. ✊ 💗

Activity 2: The Let-Go Stone Craft

Materials: One small smooth stone, a paper circle, crayons, glue, a shallow bowl

Steps:

1. Find one smooth pebble. Imagine it is a 'hot stone' — a hurt you are holding.

2. On the paper circle, draw a tiny picture of a hurt — someone pushing you, calling a name, breaking your toy. Glue the stone on top of the picture.

3. Sit quietly. Take three deep breaths. Whisper: 'Kshama. I let this go.'

4. Pick up the paper-and-stone. Walk to a bowl of soil or water or sand. Place the stone IN. Leave it there.

5. Come back with empty hands. Place your palms over your heart. Breathe. Say: 'My heart is clean. Krishna stays inside.'

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Stand tall. Hold your fists tight in front of your chest. Chant:

Kshama! Kshama! Kshama!

On each chant, slowly open your fists like flowers. On the third chant, blow gently across your open palms.

*I put the hurt down. My heart is free. Krishna holds the rest.*

Activity 4: Two Brave Stories (Paired)

With a partner, choose one scene and act it out. Both children take turns being each character.

Scene A: One child accidentally spills paint on the other's drawing. The hurt one first wants to shout — then remembers Prahlada and chooses kshama.

Scene B: One child has been teased at lunch. A friend comes to sit with them and helps them breathe three times and 'put the stone down.'

After you act, whisper to each other: 'Which heart felt lighter — the fist-heart or the open-heart?'

Activity 5: Take Home — The Forgiveness Garden

Every night this week, before bed, think of ONE tiny hurt from your day — a mean word, a broken promise, someone who didn't share. Whisper 'kshama' three times, and imagine placing a tiny flower on a garden.

Draw a flower in your home calendar each night. At the end of the week, count your flowers. That is your kshama garden — grown with every hurt you let go.

Structured Activities

  • Hot Stone or Soft Heart (whole_class, 6 min)

    Listen to each scene. Circle the closed fist if the child is holding the hurt tight; circle the open heart if the child is letting the hurt go with kshama.

    Materials: Worksheet, Crayons

  • The Let-Go Stone Craft (solo, 15 min)

    Hold a smooth pebble as a hot-stone hurt. Breathe three times, whisper 'kshama,' and walk the stone out to a bowl of soil or water. Return with empty hands and a clean heart.

    Materials: Small smooth stone per child, Paper circle, Crayons, Glue, Bowl of soil or water

  • Say It With Me (whole_class, 4 min)

    Chant 'ksha-MAA' three times, opening a tight fist a little more on each chant, and blowing gently across the open palm at the end.

  • Two Brave Stories (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, act out either a paint-spill scene or a lunchtime-teasing scene. Take turns being each character. Afterward, whisper which heart felt lighter — the fist or the open hand.

  • The Forgiveness Garden (solo, 5 min)

    Every night for one week, think of one hurt from the day. Whisper 'kshama' three times and draw a tiny flower on your calendar. Count your flowers as a family at week's end.

    Materials: Home calendar, Crayons

Home Practice

Every night before bed this week, do the Forgiveness Garden practice with Amma or Appa. Think of ONE small hurt from your day — a mean word, a forgotten promise, a broken thing, a pushing hand. Hold your fists tight for three seconds. Then take three slow breaths. On each breath, open your fingers a little more. On the third breath, whisper: 'Kshama. I let this go. My heart is clean.' Then draw one tiny flower in your home calendar. If there was no hurt that day, draw a flower anyway — as thanks to Krishna for a peaceful day. At the end of the week, count your flowers together as a family.

Duration: Once each night for one week (7 flowers minimum)
Look for: By the end of the week, the child's home calendar has at least 5 flowers drawn, the child can demonstrate the fist-open breathing practice without a reminder at least once, and Amma or Appa notices the child using a phrase like 'it's okay, I forgive' or 'I'm putting the stone down' at least once during an ordinary family moment.
