About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Tapas

Tapas is the little fire inside me.

Chapter Overview

Tapas is the little fire inside me. It is the warm, steady flame that helps me do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. When I say no to a second sweet, that is tapas. When I wake up early for prayer even though my bed is cosy, that is tapas. When my tongue wants to say something mean but I keep it soft — that is tapas too. Tapas is not big or scary. It is a tiny fire that grows brighter the more I feed it with small brave choices.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 17.14-16 — Krishna says there are three little fires inside us. The BODY-fire: keeping clean, standing straight, being gentle, being pure. The SPEECH-fire: saying only true, kind, and helpful words, and reading good books. The MIND-fire: a peaceful happy mind, a gentle feeling inside, and a self that we can hold like a sleepy puppy in our lap. When these three fires burn together, that is tapas.

Core Teaching

  1. Tapas is the little fire inside me. It grows when I do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. Saying no to a second sweet. Waking up on time for morning prayer. Putting my toys away when I would rather play more. Every small choice feeds the little fire.
  2. Krishna teaches THREE tapas — body, speech, and mind. Body-tapas: be clean, sit straight, be gentle. Speech-tapas: only true, kind, helpful words. Mind-tapas: a quiet happy mind, and bringing the monkey-mind back to Krishna when it jumps. Three little fires together make a lamp.
  3. Tapas is not for showing off. It is not to get prizes. It is not to punish ourselves. It is a quiet gift for Krishna. Even when no one is watching, the little fire burns for Him. And when it burns steady, Krishna Himself smiles inside our heart.

Five-Minute Introduction

Gather children in a circle. Hold up a small battery diya or an unlit diya. Say: 'Children, inside each of your chests, there is a tiny flame. Not a hot fire — a WARM fire. A SOFT fire. A friendly fire.' Put your hand on your chest. 'Can you feel it? Press gently.' Let them press their own chests. 'That little fire has a Sanskrit name — TAPAS. Every time you say NO to something easy-wrong, the fire glows brighter. Every time you say YES to something hard-right, the fire grows warmer.' Ask: 'Who can tell me ONE easy-wrong thing — like eating too many sweets, or shouting at a sister, or staying in bed when it's time to wake?' Take 3 answers. Then: 'And what is the HARD RIGHT in each case?' Celebrate every answer. Chant together, with hand on chest: 'TA-pas. TA-pas. TA-pas.' Three times, softly — because the little fire is quiet.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Press-the-chest intro + three-fire chant (body-fire, speech-fire, mind-fire). 10 min — Read aloud Shukadeva's story; children mime the monkey-mind jumping, then gently coming back. 8 min — Read aloud Tanvi and the Second Laddu; pause at the hand-reaching-out moment and ask 'What would YOU do?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Easy Wrong or Hard Right) + Activity 2 (Three Little Fires drawing). 5 min — Closing Monkey-Mind Game in pairs + introduce Little-Fire Diary. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with hand-on-chest and three-fire chant. 15 min — Shukadeva's story with props: a small peepal leaf for each child; children sit still as 'little Shukadevas' while teacher calls the names of forest creatures coming near. 15 min — Tanvi's story, re-enacted with a real (empty) laddu plate; children take turns reaching-stopping-pulling back with a deep breath. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Monkey-Mind paired game; debrief how gentleness feels versus scolding. 15 min — Three-Fire Craft: each child makes a small paper lantern with three painted flames (orange tummy-flame, gold lip-flame, rose forehead-flame) and writes a tiny vow under each. 10 min — Biographical story: young Milkha Singh running in the hot Punjabi sun; children run-in-place for 60 seconds 'like Milkha'. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE hard-right choice they will make this week; teacher hands out Little-Fire Diaries. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children feel tapas in their BODIES first — the warm glow after saying no to the easy wrong, the cold slap of giving in. Use the 'little fire' metaphor everywhere. Let them press their chests when they feel the fire glow. Distinguish tapas carefully from THREE anti-patterns at this age: (1) self-harm disguised as discipline — watch for children who skip meals or deny themselves water 'to be holy'; gently correct this — tapas BUILDS, never BREAKS; (2) show-off discipline — the child who loudly announces 'I did not eat any sweets today!'; redirect toward quiet practice — 'The fire glows best in the dark'; (3) grumpy-duty — the child who practises sadhana with a pinched face; remind them that tapas leads to JOY. Watch for children who feel they have 'broken' their tapas and given up — gently teach that the calling-back IS the tapas. Use the monkey-mind game often. Primary children LOVE the image of the monkey-mind and will self-regulate when they recognize their own jumpy thoughts as the monkey. When the child says — 'My monkey jumped to mangoes in bhajan today, teacher' — celebrate the NOTICING. That noticing is already the little fire. Most importantly — let your own discipline be QUIET and JOYFUL. If children see you arrive early, speak gently, and smile through small difficulties, they will catch the fire from you. A classroom of little fires is lit from one adult flame burning steady.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Tapas means hurting my body — skipping food, staying in the cold, pinching myself to be spiritual.' Response: No, no. Krishna Himself in BG 17.5-6 warns that fake-austerity which harms the body is tāmasika (ignorant). True tapas strengthens the body so it can serve Krishna better. A child doing tapas eats simple healthy food, sleeps enough, keeps clean — and makes many small brave choices. We never pinch. We never starve. We never punish.
  • Misconception: 'Tapas is for showing off — so everyone will say I am holy.' Response: That is rājasika tapas (BG 17.18), and it actually PUTS OUT the little fire. Krishna's tapas is quiet. It happens when no one is watching. When Tanvi said no to the second laddu, nobody saw. But the fire grew anyway. If we only do hard-right things for claps and stickers, we are not growing tapas — we are growing vanity. The little fire burns best in the dark.
  • Misconception: 'If I fail once, my tapas is broken forever.' Response: The OPPOSITE! Every time the mind-monkey jumps, we gently call it back. The CALLING-BACK is the tapas. Shukadeva's monkey-mind jumped a thousand times — he just kept gently bringing it back to Krishna. If you eat the second laddu today, tomorrow you get to try again. The little fire is patient. It waits for you.
  • Misconception: 'Tapas means I have to be grumpy and serious all the time.' Response: Not at all! True tapas leads to santosha — sweet inner contentment. Shukadeva was joyful. Tanvi felt warmth and sweetness after her no. Krishna says in BG 17.16 that mind-tapas includes saumyatvam (gentleness) and manaḥ-prasādaḥ (a glad, peaceful mind). Grumpy discipline is not tapas — it is just grumpiness wearing a spiritual coat. Keep the smile.
