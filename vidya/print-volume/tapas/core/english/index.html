About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 17.14-16

देवद्विजगुरुप्राज्ञपूजनं शौचमार्जवम्।
ब्रह्मचर्यमहिंसा च शारीरं तप उच्यते॥
अनुद्वेगकरं वाक्यं सत्यं प्रियहितं च यत्।
स्वाध्यायाभ्यसनं चैव वाङ्मयं तप उच्यते॥
मनः प्रसादः सौम्यत्वं मौनमात्मविनिग्रहः।
भावसंशुद्धिरित्येतत्तपो मानसमुच्यते॥

deva-dvija-guru-prājña- pūjanaṁ śaucam ārjavam brahmacaryam ahiṁsā ca śārīraṁ tapa ucyate anudvega-karaṁ vākyaṁ satyaṁ priya-hitaṁ ca yat svādhyāyābhyasanaṁ caiva vāṅ-mayaṁ tapa ucyate manaḥ-prasādaḥ saumyatvaṁ maunam ātma-vinigrahaḥ bhāva-saṁśuddhir ity etat tapo mānasam ucyate

Word-for-word: śārīraṁ tapaḥ — body-fire; vāṅ-mayaṁ tapaḥ — speech-fire; mānasam tapaḥ — mind-fire; śaucam — cleanness; ārjavam — straightness; ahiṁsā — not hurting; satyam — truthful; priya-hitam — kind and helpful; svādhyāya — reading good books; manaḥ-prasādaḥ — happy-peaceful mind; saumyatvam — gentle-ness; ātma-vinigrahaḥ — keeping the self in hand.

Translation: Krishna says there are three little fires inside us. The BODY-fire: keeping clean, standing straight, being gentle, being pure. The SPEECH-fire: saying only true, kind, and helpful words, and reading good books. The MIND-fire: a peaceful happy mind, a gentle feeling inside, and a self that we can hold like a sleepy puppy in our lap. When these three fires burn together, that is tapas.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 5.5.1

नायं देहो देहभाजां नृलोके
कष्टान्कामानर्हते विड्भुजां ये।
तपो दिव्यं पुत्रका येन सत्त्वं
शुद्ध्येद्यस्माद्ब्रह्मसौख्यं त्वनन्तम्॥

nāyaṁ deho deha-bhājāṁ nṛloke kaṣṭān kāmān arhate viḍ-bhujāṁ ye tapo divyaṁ putrakā yena sattvaṁ śuddhyed yasmād brahma-saukhyaṁ tv anantam

Translation: Rishabhadeva says to his sons: 'My dear children, this human body is not for chasing little sweet things that even pigs and dogs can enjoy. The body is for divine tapas — a tiny brave fire — which makes the heart pure and opens a joy that never ends. So feed your little fire, my sons, and Krishna will feed you.'

What is Tapas?

Tapas is the little fire inside me. It is the warm, steady flame that helps me do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. When I say no to a second sweet, that is tapas. When I wake up early for prayer even though my bed is cosy, that is tapas. When my tongue wants to say something mean but I keep it soft — that is tapas too. Tapas is not big or scary. It is a tiny fire that grows brighter the more I feed it with small brave choices.

What it is NOT

Tapas is not punishing ourselves. It is not starving or hurting our bodies to feel holy. Tapas is not showing off — 'Look how strict I am!' It is not being grumpy or sour while we practise. And tapas is not for getting prizes — stickers, sweets, praise. The real little fire burns quietly, for Krishna, even when no one is watching.

  • Skipping lunch so everyone will say 'Wow, you are so spiritual'
  • Waking up early and then being grumpy and mean to my sister all morning
  • Doing a hard thing only because Amma promised me ice cream
  • Hurting my body — like pinching myself or not drinking water — and calling it 'tapas'

Sanskrit Word Study

tapas (तपस्)

Pronunciation: TA-pas

Meaning: The little fire inside. A small steady warmth that helps me do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. The warm glow when I say no to a second sweet, or wake up early for prayer, or keep my words kind when my tongue wants to shout. Krishna's fire inside me.

Example: When Tanvi pulled her hand back from the second laddu, the little fire inside her chest glowed warmly — that was tapas.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Shukadeva and the Still Little Fire — Srimad Bhagavatam — Canto 1, Chapter 2 and Canto 2, Chapter 1 (traditional accounts of Shukadeva's youthful tapasya)

On the banks of a quiet river, Rishi Vyasa had a little son named Shukadeva. He was only eight.

Shukadeva loved Krishna. When other children chased butterflies, he sat still and whispered, 'Krishna. Krishna.'

One morning he said, 'Father, I want my mind on Krishna ALL day. But it RUNS away! My mind is a MONKEY — it jumps to mangoes, to stones. How do I catch it?'

Vyasa smiled. 'Shuka, inside you is a tiny fire — like a diya flame. Its name is tapas. Feed it small brave choices three ways — body, speech, and mind.'

So little Shukadeva began.

**Body first.** Every dawn, he slipped from his warm blanket. The forest was cold. His toes went SHIVER. But he walked to the river, bathed, and sat under the old peepal — back straight. Even when an ant crossed his foot, he did not move.

**Speech next.** Before speaking, he asked: 'TRUE? KIND? HELPFUL?' If one answer was no, he kept the words inside.

**Mind last — hardest.** The monkey jumped and jumped. Each time, Shukadeva gently said, 'Come back, monkey. Sit with Krishna.' He never scolded. He just called it back.

Days passed. His little fire grew.

One morning Vyasa came looking. He found Shukadeva under the peepal. A soft deer rested on the boy's lap. A green parrot sat on his shoulder. A squirrel sat still on his knee. Nobody was afraid.

Shukadeva's lips whispered — 'Krishna. Krishna.'

Vyasa's eyes filled with tears. 'My son — your little fire has become a lamp.'

Shukadeva smiled. 'Father, the monkey does not jump much anymore. He has become a friend. He sits with Krishna now.'

Tapas.

A Story from Today

Tanvi and the Second Laddu

It was Janmashtami. Tanvi was seven, at her Nani's house in Vrindavan. The house smelled like GHEE and FRESH LADDUS.

Nani had made a plate of besan laddus. Golden. Round. Each with a pistachio on top.

After the bhajan, Nani said, 'Children, ONE laddu each. We are offering Krishna first.'

Tanvi bit her laddu. Oh! WARM. SWEET. It melted on her tongue.

Then the laddu was gone.

Tanvi's tongue began to SHOUT. 'MORE!' Many laddus remained. Nobody was watching. Nani was in the kitchen.

Her hand started to REACH OUT.

But then — quietly, inside her chest — a tiny voice whispered, soft and warm.

'Tanvi. Just one. Krishna is watching.'

Tanvi's hand stopped. Her tongue still shouted MORE. But the little voice was steady.

Tanvi took a deep breath and pulled her hand back.

'No thank you,' she whispered to the laddus.

It was HARD. Her eyes felt a little wet. But something else happened. Inside her chest, a tiny warmth began to glow. Soft. Sweet. Happy.

Nani came out. She knelt. 'What is in your chest, beta?'

Tanvi whispered, 'Nani, I wanted another laddu. But I said no.'

Nani put her hand on Tanvi's chest. 'Feel a little warmth here?'

'Yes, Nani. Like a tiny fire.'

Nani smiled. 'That is TAPAS, my little one. Every no to the easy wrong — the fire grows. Krishna's fire. Forever.'

That night Tanvi fell asleep happy. Her tummy was not full of extra laddus. But her chest was full of something sweeter — a tiny, steady, warm little fire.

Tapas.

Core Teaching

  1. Tapas is the little fire inside me. It grows when I do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. Saying no to a second sweet. Waking up on time for morning prayer. Putting my toys away when I would rather play more. Every small choice feeds the little fire.
  2. Krishna teaches THREE tapas — body, speech, and mind. Body-tapas: be clean, sit straight, be gentle. Speech-tapas: only true, kind, helpful words. Mind-tapas: a quiet happy mind, and bringing the monkey-mind back to Krishna when it jumps. Three little fires together make a lamp.
  3. Tapas is not for showing off. It is not to get prizes. It is not to punish ourselves. It is a quiet gift for Krishna. Even when no one is watching, the little fire burns for Him. And when it burns steady, Krishna Himself smiles inside our heart.
