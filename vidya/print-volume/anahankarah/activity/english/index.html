About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Anahankarah

Anahankarah is when I do something good — and I do NOT puff up and say 'I did it! Me! Me! Look at ME!' Krishna is the REAL doer.

Worksheet

Anahankara Worksheet — I Am Krishna's Little Helper

Activity 1: Puff-Puff or Helper-Heart? (Circle the Right One)

1. I won the running race. Teacher says 'Well done!' Do I shout 'I AM THE FASTEST!' or fold my hands and say 'Krishna ran with me'? PUFF-PUFF HELPER-HEART

2. I drew a nice picture. A friend asks 'who did this?' Do I shout 'I DID IT ALL BY MYSELF!' or say softly 'Krishna painted through my hand'? PUFF-PUFF HELPER-HEART

3. I helped Nani carry the rice bag. Do I run to Amma and shout 'I DID IT! ME!' or quietly smile and say 'Krishna's hands helped Nani today'? PUFF-PUFF HELPER-HEART

4. I got full marks in my spelling test. Do I think 'I am so clever, nobody helped me' or say 'thank You Krishna for my mind and my hand'? PUFF-PUFF HELPER-HEART

5. I won a school medal. Do I keep saying 'MY medal, MY medal' or put it first before Krishna's picture on the puja shelf? PUFF-PUFF HELPER-HEART

Activity 2: Draw Your Helper-Hands

Draw your two little hands big on a page. Inside each palm, draw a tiny flute — because these are Krishna's hands, and Krishna loves His flute. On one hand write: 'My hands are Krishna's helper-hands.' On the other hand write: 'Krishna does the work. I only help.' Colour the hands soft pink and gold. Draw a tiny peacock feather floating above the hands.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: The Helper-Hand Chant

Stand in a circle. Hold up BOTH hands in front of you, palms facing up, like an empty bowl. Say softly:

A-na-haṅ-kā-ra! A-na-haṅ-kā-ra! A-na-haṅ-kā-ra! *Not me. Not me. Krishna. Krishna. Krishna.*

After each chant, touch your open palms to your heart and bow the head gently.

Activity 4: The Paired Thank-You Game

With a partner, take turns praising each other softly. Partner A: 'You are very kind.' Partner B: Instead of saying 'yes I know!' or 'no no, you are kinder!', fold hands, bow head, say: 'Thank you. Krishna is kind through me.' Swap. Try again with different praises:

  • 'Your drawing is beautiful.'
  • 'You run so fast!'
  • 'You are so good at singing.'
  • 'You are so helpful.'

Each time, the receiver answers: 'Thank you. Krishna does it through me.' Notice — is your heart lighter than when you say 'I know I'm the best'?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Helper-Hand Diary

Keep a small notebook by your bed. Each night, draw TWO tiny hands. Under the hands, whisper (or write with a crayon):

  • ONE thing your hands did today that was GOOD
  • ONE soft 'thank You, Krishna' for doing it THROUGH your hands

At the end of the week, look at all your helper-hands. Each pair is a little anahankara flower — blooming quietly for Krishna.

PS — Do not show this diary to anyone except Amma. It is a secret between your hands, Krishna's hands, and the little diya of your heart.

Structured Activities

  • Puff-Puff or Helper-Heart? (solo, 6 min)

    Circle PUFF-PUFF or HELPER-HEART for each scenario. Notice which one Krishna loves.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • Draw Your Helper-Hands (solo, 10 min)

    Trace both your hands on paper. Inside each palm, draw a tiny flute. Write 'My hands are Krishna's helper-hands.' Colour in soft pink and gold.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Pink and gold pastels

  • The Helper-Hand Chant (whole_class, 3 min)

    In a circle, hold both palms up like bowls. Chant 'anahankara' three times softly. After each chant, bring palms to heart and bow.

  • The Paired Thank-You Game (paired, 10 min)

    With a partner, practise receiving praise. Fold hands, bow head, whisper 'Thank you. Krishna does it through me.' Notice how the heart feels lighter than when we say 'I know I'm the best!'

  • The Helper-Hand Diary (solo, 5 min)

    Keep a secret nightly diary of two small hands. Under them, write one good thing the hands did today and a soft 'thank You, Krishna.' Do not show anyone except Amma.

    Materials: Small notebook, Pencil, Pink crayon

Home Practice

For one week, keep a Helper-Hand Diary by your bed. Every night, before sleep, draw two small hands on a page. Under the hands, whisper (or write with a crayon): (1) one thing your hands did today that was GOOD — helped Amma, wrote a nice letter, drew a picture, tidied a shoe — and (2) a soft 'thank You, Krishna' for working through your hands today. Do NOT show the diary to friends or siblings — it is a secret between you, Krishna, and Amma. Before closing the book, fold your hands on your chest and whisper: 'Krishna, tomorrow also please use my hands. I am Your little helper.'

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific good things their hands did and give soft credit to Krishna (not themselves) without being prompted. They can demonstrate the helper-hand gesture (both palms up, then brought to heart with a bow) while whispering 'anahankara.' The diary has at least 5 pairs of hands drawn inside. When praised during the week, the child responds with 'Krishna did it through me' at least twice, naturally.
