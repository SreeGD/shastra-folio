About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Anahankarah

Anahankarah is when I do something good — and I do NOT puff up and say 'I did it! Me! Me! Look at ME!' Krishna is the REAL doer. My hands are just little helper-hands. My feet are Krishna's helper-feet. My mouth says His songs. When teacher praises me, I smile and whisper, 'Krishna, You did it through me.' I am happy to be His little helper — not the big hero. The 'a' at the start means 'NO,' 'aham' means 'I,' 'kara' means 'doer.' So anahankara = NO-I-did-it feeling. Just happy helper-feeling.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 13.8: Krishna tells Arjuna the qualities of a truly wise heart. In the very next breath after this verse, He says anahaṅkāra — do NOT feel 'I DID IT! I am the big doer!' When Krishna helps your hand, your foot, your mouth, your mind — say softly, 'Krishna did it through me. I am only His little helper.'

What Anahankarah is NOT

Anahankara does NOT mean being lazy or saying 'Krishna will do it, so I will not try.' NO — I still try my very best. I still practise. I still work hard. My hands are Krishna's helper-hands only when they are WORKING. Anahankara also does not mean pretending I did nothing. If Amma asks 'did you clean your room?' I say honestly, 'Yes, Amma — Krishna helped me clean it.' Honest AND not puffy. That is the real anahankara heart.

  • Winning the drawing prize and shouting 'I DID IT ALL BY MYSELF! ME! I am the best artist!'
  • Helping a friend, then running to teacher — 'Teacher, I helped! ME! I did it!'
  • Getting a good mark and thinking 'I am so clever — nobody helped me, not Amma, not Krishna, not teacher'
  • Winning a race and refusing to bow before the altar — 'These are MY legs, MY victory!'

Stories Your Child Heard

Hanuman After the Ocean Leap — Long ago, wicked Ravana stole Mother Sita across the ocean to Lanka. Lord Rama needed a helper to leap the ocean and find her. A small brown monkey stood up — Hanuman. He bowed. He did not say 'I am strong.' He whispered, 'Prabhu, YOUR name will carry me.'

Arnav and the Big Poster — Arnav, age 8, loved painting Krishna. One week Sarla-ji clapped. 'For Janmashtami we will paint a BIG poster of baby Krishna on Kaliya. Whoever paints the most beautiful Krishna — their name goes in BIG GOLD LETTERS!'

The Sanskrit Word

anahaṅkāra (अनहङ्कार) — an-a-han-KAA-ra

Meaning: NO 'I did it!' feeling. The letter 'a' means 'NO.' 'Aham' means 'I.' 'Kara' means 'doer.' So anahankara = NO-I-am-the-doer feeling. When I know my hands are Krishna's helper-hands, my heart has anahankara. I am happy to be His little helper — not the big hero.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep a Helper-Hand Diary by your bed. Every night, before sleep, draw two small hands on a page. Under the hands, whisper (or write with a crayon): (1) one thing your hands did today that was GOOD — helped Amma, wrote a nice letter, drew a picture, tidied a shoe — and (2) a soft 'thank You, Krishna' for working through your hands today. Do NOT show the diary to friends or siblings — it is a secret between you, Krishna, and Amma. Before closing the book, fold your hands on your chest and whisper: 'Krishna, tomorrow also please use my hands. I am Your little helper.'

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific good things their hands did and give soft credit to Krishna (not themselves) without being prompted. They can demonstrate the helper-hand gesture (both palms up, then brought to heart with a bow) while whispering 'anahankara.' The diary has at least 5 pairs of hands drawn inside. When praised during the week, the child responds with 'Krishna did it through me' at least twice, naturally.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific good things their hands did and give soft credit to Krishna (not themselves) without being prompted. They can demonstrate the helper-hand gesture (both palms up, then brought to heart with a bow) while whispering 'anahankara.' The diary has at least 5 pairs of hands drawn inside. When praised during the week, the child responds with 'Krishna did it through me' at least twice, naturally.

When the helper-hand heart of anahankara grows steady, my work itself becomes an offering — done fully with love, but without clinging to the reward. That next beautiful quality has its own name: niṣkāma karma.
