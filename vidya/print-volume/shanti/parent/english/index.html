About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Parent Book helps you reinforce at home what your child is learning at school. You do not need to read the full stories — your child will tell you! Instead, this book gives you conversation starters, signs to look for, and simple practices to do together as a family.

For each value, you will find:

  • Value at a Glance — What the value means, in plain language
  • The Verse — The scriptural foundation, in your language
  • Stories Your Child Heard — Brief summaries so you can ask about them
  • The Sanskrit Word — Ask your child to teach it to you!
  • Home Practice — The same action your child was given at school
  • Dinner Table Questions — 3–4 conversation starters for family discussion
  • Signs of Growth — Observable behaviours showing your child is internalizing the value

The most powerful teaching happens not in the classroom but at the dinner table.

Shanti

Shanti means peace inside our heart. It is the quiet feeling when our hands are calm, our voice is soft, and our heart is still. Even when everything outside is noisy — cars honking, friends shouting, thunder roaring — shanti is the small, quiet place inside us that stays steady. We touch it by breathing slowly and remembering Krishna.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 2.66: When the mind is not quiet, we cannot think clearly. When we cannot think clearly, we cannot be happy. First the mind becomes still. Then peace comes. Then happiness.

What Shanti is NOT

Shanti is not running away and hiding. It is not being grumpy and silent. It is not pretending we are not upset when our heart is racing. And it is not just going to sleep to forget our troubles. Shanti is awake, soft, and alive — like a candle flame in a glass jar.

  • Shouting back when someone shouts at us
  • Hiding in a corner and refusing to speak
  • Stamping our feet when we cannot have what we want

Stories Your Child Heard

Prahlada in the Burning Fire — Long ago there was a tiny prince named Prahlada. He was only five years old. His eyes were full of love for Krishna. But Prahlada's father was an angry king named Hiranyakashipu. He did not love Krishna.

Ravi and the Loudest Wedding — Ravi was seven years old. His cousin Anjali was getting married. The whole family had come to the big tent in the garden. There were drums — DHOOM! DHOOM! A shehnai blew loud. Uncles shouted with joy. Aunties laughed. Little cousins ran everywhere.

The Sanskrit Word

śānti (शान्ति) — SHAHN-tee

Meaning: Peace inside the heart. The still, quiet feeling when our hands, our voice, and our heart are all calm together.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each evening, sit in your Shanti Corner for TWO MINUTES with Amma or Appa. Place your hand on your heart. Breathe slowly — in for 3 counts, out for 3 counts. Whisper 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna.' Notice how your heart feels after. Also: whenever you feel a big feeling (angry, sad, too-excited), remember the three calms — calm hands, calm voice, calm heart — and take three slow breaths before you do anything else.

Duration: Every evening for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can sit still for two full minutes, describes their Shanti Corner with affection, and can demonstrate the three-breath pause when asked — and at least once initiates it on their own during a loud moment.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can sit still for two full minutes, describes their Shanti Corner with affection, and can demonstrate the three-breath pause when asked — and at least once initiates it on their own during a loud moment.

When shanti grows inside us, we can listen carefully to what is really true around us — and truth-listening is the beginning of the next value, satya.
