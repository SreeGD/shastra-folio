About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Seva Worksheet — My Hands for Krishna

Activity 1: Seva or Not Seva? (Circle the Right Answer)

1. I water the tulasi plant without being asked. SEVA NOT SEVA

2. I clean my room only because Amma promises me ice-cream. SEVA NOT SEVA

3. I carry Dadi's bag slowly so she does not get tired. SEVA NOT SEVA

4. I sweep the altar loudly so my teacher sees me. SEVA NOT SEVA

5. I feed the street puppy quietly every morning. SEVA NOT SEVA

Activity 2: My Seva Hands

In the box below, trace BOTH your hands on paper. Inside each finger, draw or write ONE seva you will do this week. Examples: sweep the altar, water tulasi, help Dadi, feed the puppy, wash my plate, pick up a fallen flower, help my little brother tie his shoe. Colour your hands warm gold.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Chant three times, pressing both palms together in a Namaste:

Se-VAA! Se-VAA! Se-VAA! *My hands are for Krishna!*

Activity 4: The Tulasi Watering Practice (Paired)

With a partner, practice giving seva to the tulasi plant (or any classroom plant). One child holds a small cup of water. The other child pretends to be the tulasi plant. The first child:

  • Walks slowly (not hurried)
  • Bows first with Namaste
  • Pours water very gently at the base, not a drop spilled
  • Whispers: 'Tulasi-mata, for Krishna'
  • Bows again

Switch. Notice: does the hand move differently when we remember 'for Krishna'? Does the heart?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Secret Seva Notebook

Get a small notebook. On the cover, write: MY SECRET SEVA BOOK. Every night this week, draw or write ONE seva you did today — that NO ONE ELSE KNOWS. Only you and Krishna. At the end of the week, look at your page. Do not show anyone. Just smile quietly and whisper: 'Krishna, these are Yours.'

Structured Activities

  • Seva or Not Seva? (solo, 6 min)

    Circle SEVA or NOT SEVA for each scenario, noticing the heart behind the helping.

    Materials: Worksheet, Pencil

  • My Seva Hands (solo, 10 min)

    Trace both your hands. In each finger, draw or write one seva you will do this week.

    Materials: Paper, Crayons, Pencil

  • Say It With Me (whole_class, 3 min)

    Chant 'seva' three times, pressing palms together in Namaste each time.

  • The Tulasi Watering Practice (paired, 12 min)

    With a partner, practice watering the tulasi plant slowly, gently, with Namaste before and after.

    Materials: Small watering can or cup, Tulasi or any classroom plant

  • The Secret Seva Notebook (solo, 5 min)

    Keep a small notebook. Each day, draw or write ONE quiet seva no one else saw. Do not show it — just smile at Krishna.

    Materials: Small notebook, Pencil or crayons

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Secret Seva Notebook. Every day, find ONE tiny seva — water tulasi, help Dadi or Thatha, carry Amma's bag, feed the street puppy, sweep the altar, help a younger sibling. Do it QUIETLY. Do not announce it. Draw or write it in your Secret Seva Book each night. At bedtime, press your palms in Namaste and whisper: 'Krishna, these are Your hands today.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific seva moments done without being asked or rewarded, and can demonstrate the Namaste gesture while chanting 'seva' with a quiet smile. The Secret Seva Notebook has at least 5 entries.
