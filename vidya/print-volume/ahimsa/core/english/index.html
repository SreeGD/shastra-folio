About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English
  • Srimad Bhagavatam Verse — A second scriptural anchor
  • What is — What the value is, and what it is NOT
  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize
  • Language Practice — Vocabulary and reading comprehension

Ahimsa

Ahimsa means not hurting anyone — not with hands, not with words, not even with angry thoughts.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम्।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम्॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Word-for-word: ahiṁsā — non-violence; satyam — truthfulness; akrodhaḥ — free from anger; tyāgaḥ — giving up what is not needed; śāntiḥ — peacefulness; apaiśunam — not finding fault; dayā — kindness; bhūteṣu — to all living beings; aloluptvam — not being greedy; mārdavam — being gentle; hrīḥ — being humble; acāpalam — being steady.

Translation: Do not hurt anyone. Speak the truth. Do not get angry. Give up what you do not need. Be peaceful. Do not find fault. Be kind to every living being. Do not be greedy. Be gentle. Be humble. Do not give up.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 7.14.9

सर्वभूतेषु यः पश्येद्भगवद्भावमात्मनः।
भूतानि भगवत्यात्मन्येष भागवतोत्तमः॥

sarva-bhūteṣu yaḥ paśyed bhagavad-bhāvam ātmanaḥ bhūtāni bhagavaty ātmany eṣa bhāgavatottamaḥ

Translation: One who sees the Lord in every living being, and every living being in the Lord — that person is the best devotee.

What is Ahimsa?

Ahimsa means not hurting anyone — not with hands, not with words, not even with angry thoughts. We are gentle with people, animals, plants, and even small insects. Every living being has a soul inside, just like you.

What it is NOT

Ahimsa is not hitting, pushing, or calling names. It is not laughing when someone is hurt. It is not hurting little creatures on purpose. When we are angry, we still use gentle hands and kind words.

  • Pushing a classmate to grab a toy
  • Calling someone a mean name when you are upset
  • Stepping on an ant just for fun

Sanskrit Word Study

ahiṁsā (अहिंसा)

Pronunciation: ah-HIM-sa

Meaning: Not hurting. Being gentle with every living being — people, animals, plants, insects.

Example: When I walk around the ants instead of on them, I am practicing ahimsa.

Story from the Scriptures

The Little Swan and the Brave Prince — Jataka Tales / Buddhist tradition

Long ago in India, a little prince named Siddhartha walked in his garden. The sky was blue. The birds were singing.

Suddenly, a white swan fell from the sky. Thud! It landed in the soft grass. An arrow was in its wing. The swan was crying softly.

Siddhartha ran over. He knelt down. His hands were very gentle. He pulled the arrow out slowly. He used a clean cloth to cover the wound.

Just then, his cousin came running. His cousin had a bow. He said loudly, 'That is MY swan! I shot it! Give it to me!'

Siddhartha held the swan close. He looked at his cousin. In a calm voice he said, 'The one who saves a life is the true owner. Not the one who takes a life.'

The grown-ups heard this. They said, 'Siddhartha is right. The swan belongs to the one who is kind.'

Siddhartha took the swan home. He gave it water and soft food. When the wing was strong again, the swan flew back into the sky.

Siddhartha smiled. Being gentle felt much better than being rough. This is ahimsa.

A Story from Today

Arun and the Ant Line

Arun was playing in the courtyard with his friend Kiran. The afternoon sun was warm. A piece of chapati had fallen on the ground.

Suddenly, Kiran shouted, 'Look, Arun! Ants!'

A long line of tiny black ants was marching toward the chapati. Each ant was carrying a small crumb. They were going home to feed their family.

Kiran raised his foot. 'I'm going to stamp on them!'

'No!' said Arun quickly. 'Don't!'

Arun gently pulled Kiran back. 'Amma says ants have little lives too. They are just hungry, like us.'

Kiran thought about it. 'But they are so small.'

'Small lives are still lives,' said Arun. He picked up a piece of white chalk. He drew a big circle around the ants. 'Now no one will walk on them.'

The two boys sat down. They watched the ants carry crumbs across the circle. One ant seemed to wave its tiny feeler, as if saying thank you.

Kiran smiled. 'You are right, Arun. It feels better to protect than to hurt.'

That is ahimsa. Gentle hands. Gentle heart.

Core Teaching

  1. Ahimsa means do not hurt anyone — not with hands, not with words, not even with angry thoughts. All living beings have a soul, just like you.
  2. When we are angry, we still use gentle hands and kind words. Hurting others is easy, but being gentle is strong.
  3. Every life matters — even the tiniest ant. When we protect life, Krishna is happy with us.
