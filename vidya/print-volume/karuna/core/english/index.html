About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the reading volume. The companion Activity Workbook contains the doing material — worksheets, crafts, games, and home practice. Read this book first, do the activities second.

Each chapter contains:

  • Verse Anchor — The scriptural foundation in Sanskrit and English

  • Sanskrit Word Study — The Sanskrit term, its meaning, and use
  • Story from the Scriptures — From the scriptures, from history, and from modern life
  • Core Teaching — Three principles to internalize

Karuna

Karuna means a mercy-heart that reaches out to help, especially to those who cannot help themselves — or even to those who may have hurt us.

Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 12.13

अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च।
निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी॥

adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṁ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī

Word-for-word: adveṣṭā — never hating; sarva-bhūtānām — of all living beings; maitraḥ — a friend; karuṇaḥ — full of mercy; eva — indeed; ca — and; nirmamaḥ — with no 'mine'; nirahaṅkāraḥ — with no pride; sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ — same in sadness and happiness; kṣamī — forgiving.

Translation: One who never hates any creature, who is a friend to all, who is full of mercy, who has no 'mine' and no pride, who stays the same in sorrow and in joy, and who forgives — such a one is very dear to Me, says Krishna.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 6.2.14

साङ्केत्यं पारिहास्यं वा स्तोभं हेलनमेव वा।
वैकुण्ठनामग्रहणमशेषाघहरं विदुः॥

sāṅketyaṁ pārihāsyaṁ vā stobhaṁ helanam eva vā vaikuṇṭha-nāma-grahaṇam aśeṣāgha-haraṁ viduḥ

Translation: Even one who chants the holy name of the Lord casually, in joking, for play, or even by accident — that holy name has the power to wipe away every wrong. This is the mercy of the Lord's name.

What is Karuna?

Karuna means a mercy-heart that reaches out to help, especially to those who cannot help themselves — or even to those who may have hurt us. Daya feels sad when we see someone suffer; karuna is the hand that MOVES to lift them up. Karuna does not ask, 'Do they deserve it?' It asks, 'Do they need it?'

What it is NOT

Karuna is not pity that looks down on others. It is not being mean back when someone is mean to us. It is not helping only the people we like.

  • Laughing at a classmate who fell, because they teased us yesterday
  • Walking past a stray dog because 'it's not my pet'
  • Feeling sorry for someone but doing nothing to help

Sanskrit Word Study

karuṇā (करुणा)

Pronunciation: ka-ru-NAA

Meaning: Mercy — the mercy-heart that reaches to help, especially those who cannot help themselves or do not deserve it.

Example: When Nisha tied Bittu's sandal even though he had teased her, that was karuna — the mercy-heart in action.

Story from the Scriptures

The Merciful Name That Saved Ajamila — Srimad Bhagavatam 6.2 (simplified for primary children)

Long ago in India, there lived a man named Ajamila. When he was a boy, he was very kind. He chanted Krishna's names. He helped the temple priest. Everyone said, 'What a good boy.'

But when Ajamila grew up, something sad happened. He forgot all the good things. He lied. He took things that were not his. He was unkind. Years passed. Ajamila became old and sick.

Ajamila had a tiny baby son. He had named the baby NARAYANA, which is one of Krishna's holy names. He loved this little boy more than anything.

One day, Ajamila became very, very sick. He was lying on his bed. His eyes were closing. He felt afraid. He looked around for his little boy.

With his last breath, Ajamila cried out, 'NARAYANA! Narayana, come to me!' He was only calling his baby son.

But in the sky, something beautiful happened.

Krishna — whose other name is Narayana — heard His own holy name spoken with love. He did not wait to ask, 'Does Ajamila deserve help?' He did not say, 'Look at all his mistakes!' Krishna's heart is a mercy-heart. A karuna-heart.

Krishna sent four bright messengers, called Vishnudutas. They flew down like lightning. They had golden skin and soft eyes. Their hearts were full of karuna.

They stood beside Ajamila's bed. Their voices were gentle. 'Do not be afraid, Ajamila. You have called the holy Name. Even without knowing, you have touched the Lord. He has sent us for you.'

Ajamila was so surprised. Tears rolled down his face. 'I do not deserve this mercy,' he whispered.

'Mercy is not for the deserving,' said the Vishnudutas gently. 'Mercy is for those who need it. That is karuna.'

Ajamila woke up. He stood up. He bowed his head. From that day, he lived a holy life and went to Krishna forever.

And this is why Krishna's name is called the merciful name. It reaches anyone — anyone at all — who calls.

A Story from Today

Nisha and the Boy Who Teased Her

Nisha was in Class 2. She had long braids and a bright lunchbox with a picture of Hanuman on it. She was happy most days. But one boy made her sad.

His name was Bittu. Bittu sat two benches behind her. Every morning, Bittu pulled her braid. At lunch, he laughed at her idli and sambar. 'Eww, white soft balls!' he would shout. His friends laughed too.

Nisha told her Amma. Amma said, 'Ignore him, kanna. A mean heart is a sad heart.' But it was hard.

One Thursday, it rained very hard after school. The courtyard was muddy. Nisha was waiting for her auto under the big neem tree. She opened her umbrella.

Then she saw something.

Bittu was sitting on the wet cement step near the gate. His schoolbag was next to him. He was crying — really crying, with his face in his hands. His left sandal had broken. The strap was hanging off. Rain was dripping on his head.

Nisha's first thought was, 'Good. Now HE knows how it feels.'

But then — something warm moved inside her chest. A little voice said, 'He is crying. He needs help.'

Nisha stood still. She thought about her Amma's words. Mean heart is a sad heart. Maybe Bittu was sad inside, and that was why he was mean.

Slowly, Nisha walked over. She held her umbrella over Bittu's head.

Bittu looked up. His eyes went wide. 'Why are YOU here?' he sniffed.

'Your sandal,' Nisha said softly. She pulled a pink hairband off her wrist. 'Let me try.'

She knelt down in the mud. She tied the broken strap around his foot with the hairband. It was not perfect, but it would hold.

Bittu did not say anything. His nose was running.

'My auto comes at four,' said Nisha. 'Walk to the stand with me. I have the umbrella.'

They walked together in silence. The rain made a soft music on the umbrella. When they reached the stand, Bittu whispered, 'Thank you, Nisha.'

His voice was different. It was the first gentle voice Nisha had ever heard from Bittu.

The next Monday, Bittu did not pull her braid. At lunch, he did not laugh at her idli. After a few days, he shyly asked, 'Can I try one?'

Nisha smiled. She gave him one idli with extra sambar.

Her Amma was right. A mean heart IS a sad heart. And karuna is the hand that reaches out — even to someone who did not deserve it.

That is how mercy works.

Core Teaching

  1. Karuna is mercy — the hand that reaches out to help. Daya is the feeling inside; karuna is the action outside. When we see suffering, our feet move and our hands give.
  2. Karuna does not ask, 'Do they deserve my help?' It asks only, 'Do they need it?' Krishna's mercy rescued Ajamila, who did not deserve it. Our mercy can rescue someone who teased us — and their heart may change.
  3. When we show karuna, we become Krishna's little helpers. He sees every mercy-act we do, even the tiniest one. A pink hairband tying a broken sandal is as holy as a temple bell.
