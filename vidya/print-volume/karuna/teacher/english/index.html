About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

CHAPTER 1
करुणा
Karuna

Karuna means a mercy-heart that reaches out to help, especially to those who cannot help themselves — or even to those who may have hurt us.

Chapter Overview

Karuna means a mercy-heart that reaches out to help, especially to those who cannot help themselves — or even to those who may have hurt us. Daya feels sad when we see someone suffer; karuna is the hand that MOVES to lift them up. Karuna does not ask, 'Do they deserve it?' It asks, 'Do they need it?'

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 12.13 — One who never hates any creature, who is a friend to all, who is full of mercy, who has no 'mine' and no pride, who stays the same in sorrow and in joy, and who forgives — such a one is very dear to Me, says Krishna.

Core Teaching

  1. Karuna is mercy — the hand that reaches out to help. Daya is the feeling inside; karuna is the action outside. When we see suffering, our feet move and our hands give.
  2. Karuna does not ask, 'Do they deserve my help?' It asks only, 'Do they need it?' Krishna's mercy rescued Ajamila, who did not deserve it. Our mercy can rescue someone who teased us — and their heart may change.
  3. When we show karuna, we become Krishna's little helpers. He sees every mercy-act we do, even the tiniest one. A pink hairband tying a broken sandal is as holy as a temple bell.

Five-Minute Introduction

Start by asking children to look at their own hands. Say: 'Show me what a mercy-hand looks like.' Let them try — a hand reaching out, palm open. Explain: 'In our tradition we have a beautiful Sanskrit word for the mercy-heart that reaches to help. That word is karuna. Last week we learned daya — the FEELING when we see someone sad. Karuna is the ACTION. Daya stays in the chest. Karuna moves the feet and the hands.' Chant 'ka-ru-NAA' three times with palms open and forward. Then ask: 'Who has someone in their life who always reaches out with a mercy-hand?' Take 2-3 quick answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Opening circle, show your hands, chant 'karuna' with open palms. 10 min — Read aloud: The Merciful Name That Saved Ajamila. Slow down on the moment when Krishna sends the messengers. 8 min — Brief discussion: What is the difference between daya and karuna? Who in the story showed karuna? 8 min — Read aloud: Nisha and the Boy Who Teased Her. Pause at 'Nisha's first thought was good. Now HE knows how it feels' — discuss what happened next. 7 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Mercy-Moment) and Activity 2 (Mercy-Hand Craft start). 2 min — Closing chant and Take-Home instruction.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, hands exercise, chant 'karuna' with open palms. 15 min — The Ajamila story with a puppet or drawing on the board; let a child act out calling 'Narayana' and another child act out the Vishnuduta arriving with soft gestures. 15 min — Nisha's story; pause for children to share whether they have ever helped someone who was mean to them. 20 min — Mercy-Hand Craft (Activity 2) — trace hand, decorate, each finger a mercy-act. 15 min — Turn-Around Role Play (Activity 4) paired; rotate partners once. 15 min — Singing the bhajan 'Krishna Karuna-Sindhu' (if available) or a simple 'Mercy Hand' song with gestures. 15 min — Talking circle: name one person you will reach your mercy-hand toward this week. 10 min — Quiet reflection and drawing of one imagined mercy-moment. 5 min — Closing chant, palms together, and take-home instruction.

Discussion Guide

Primary children know daya from last week — build on that. The key teaching-move: help them FEEL the difference between the inside-feeling (daya) and the outside-action (karuna). Use body language: touch your heart (daya), then stretch your hand out (karuna). Ask every child: 'What can your mercy-hand DO this week?' Concrete actions are the point — a shared snack, a held umbrella, a picked-up pencil. Watch for the child who says 'but they were mean to me.' Honor that feeling fully — 'yes, that hurt. And karuna is what makes us BIGGER than the hurt.' Never force children to forgive; instead, invite them to notice one moment when a mercy-act felt right to them. With very young children, make it about animals first (easier than humans who have hurt them), then extend to people.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Karuna is only for people who deserve it — good people, nice people.' Response: Exactly the opposite. Karuna is MOST powerful when we reach to help those who have not earned our help — someone who teased us, a stranger, a tiny creature. Lord Krishna saved Ajamila, who had done many wrong things. Krishna did not ask 'does he deserve it?' That is karuna.
  • Misconception: 'Karuna is the same as daya — just different words.' Response: They are sisters, but different. Daya is the FEELING inside — the sad, tender feeling when we see someone hurt. Karuna is the DOING — the hand that actually moves to help. Daya without karuna is just a sigh. Karuna without daya can be cold. Both together are beautiful.
  • Misconception: 'If I help someone who was mean to me, I am weak.' Response: It takes the biggest heart to help someone who hurt you. Lord Chaitanya embraced people who insulted Him. Gandhi forgave people who jailed him. When you show karuna to someone mean, you are not weak — you are strong like Krishna. Mean hearts can change when they meet a mercy-hand.
