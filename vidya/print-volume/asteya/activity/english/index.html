About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This is the doing volume. Use it together with the Core Values reading book — read about a value first, then come here to live it.

Each chapter offers:

  • Worksheet — Drawing, writing, and reflection prompts
  • Visual Activities — Colouring pages, comic strips
  • Cultural Arts — Bhajans and art projects
  • Devanagari Practice — Hands-on Sanskrit script writing
  • Home Practice — Daily actions with observable results
  • 30-Day Challenge — A month-long journey
  • Abhyasa & Yoga — Embodied practice
  • Quiz — Self-check questions with a compact answer key

Asteya means we do not take what is not ours.

Worksheet

Asteya Worksheet — Honest Hands, Honest Heart

Activity 1: Ask First! (Yes or No)

Look at each picture your teacher shows. If the child is ASKING first, circle the smiling face 🙂. If the child is TAKING without asking, circle the sad face 😢.

1. A girl saying, 'May I please use your crayon?' 🙂 😢

2. A boy slipping a toy car into his pocket at a friend's house. 🙂 😢

3. A child returning a borrowed book next day. 🙂 😢

4. A girl grabbing the last laddu from her brother's plate. 🙂 😢

5. A boy telling teacher, 'I found this pencil. Whose is it?' 🙂 😢

Activity 2: Mine, Yours, or Ours?

Draw three circles on your paper. Label them MINE, YOURS, and OURS.

  • MINE: Write or draw 3 things that belong only to you (your toothbrush, your shoes, your notebook).
  • YOURS: Write or draw 3 things that belong to someone else (Amma's phone, Appa's chair, your sister's doll).
  • OURS: Write or draw 3 things that everyone shares (the playground swing, the classroom books, the water tap).

Before using anything from the YOURS circle, we always ASK.

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Chant three times, palms together at your heart:

As-te-ya! As-te-ya! As-te-ya! *Honest hands! Honest hands! Honest hands!*

Activity 4: The Magic Words Role-Play (Paired)

With a partner, practice turning a GRABBING sentence into an ASKING sentence:

  • Grabbing: 'Give me that!' → Asking: 'May I please have a turn?'
  • Grabbing: (silent — just take it) → Asking: 'Is this yours? I would like to borrow it.'
  • Grabbing: 'I found it, it is mine now!' → Asking: 'I found this. Who has lost it?'

Take turns being the ASKER and the OWNER. Use a gentle voice.

Activity 5: Take Home — The Honest Hands Diary

For one week, each night before bed, think of two things:

1. Did I ASK before I took anything today? Draw a little 🙂 for yes.

2. Did I return what I borrowed? Draw a little 🌸 for yes.

Show your diary to Amma or Appa every night. At the end of the week, count your smiling faces and flowers. Krishna is counting too.

🌸 🪔 ✋ 🤝 🎁

Home Practice

For one week, do the Honest Hands Diary. Every day at school and at home, practice ASKING before you borrow anything — a crayon, a toy, a snack, a hug. At bedtime, share with Amma or Appa one time you asked, and one time you returned something. If you find something that is not yours, hand it to a grown-up right away. Also: every night before sleep, place your hands at your heart and whisper, 'Krishna, my hands were honest today.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 times they asked before taking, and at least 2 times they returned something that was borrowed. They can also name one thing that 'belongs to everyone' and should be shared.
