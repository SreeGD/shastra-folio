About This Book

This book is part of the Vidyā Karaṇa (विद्या करण) Value Education Series, designed for the Primary (Ages 6-9) age group.

Each chapter explores one of the values from the daivī sampad (divine qualities) described in the Bhagavad Gītā verses 16.1–3. The values are presented in a carefully sequenced order, where foundational values are taught before those that build upon them.

This Teacher Guide is designed to be used alongside the Student Book. It does not repeat the stories or activities — instead, it equips you to deliver each chapter with confidence.

For each value, you will find:

  • Chapter Overview — Value summary, verse reference, core principles, and MI targets at a glance
  • Five-Minute Introduction — An opening hook to start the lesson
  • 40-Minute Lesson Plan — Ready-to-use timing for 40-minute and 2-hour sessions
  • Skit Script — A classroom drama bringing the value to life
  • Role-Play Scenarios — Age-appropriate situations for practice
  • Discussion Guide — Questions with sample responses and facilitation tips
  • Quiz Bank + Answer Key — 10 MCQs with explanations for each chapter
  • Assessment Tools — Rubrics, pre/post surveys, portfolio prompts, and observation logs
  • Common Misconceptions — What children often get wrong and how to address it

The LLM is the karaṇa (instrument); you are the teacher. Trust your instincts.

Asteya

Asteya means we do not take what is not ours.

Chapter Overview

Asteya means we do not take what is not ours. We ASK first. If something belongs to someone else, we say, 'May I please borrow this?' We return it the same way we got it. Honest hands feel light and happy inside.

The Verse: Yoga Sutras 2.30 — Do not hurt. Speak the truth. Do not take what is not yours. Be pure in heart. Do not grab and hoard. These are the five gentle rules.

Core Teaching

  1. Asteya means we do not take what is not ours. We ASK before we borrow. We return what we borrow. Honest hands feel light and happy inside.
  2. If we find something that is not ours, we try to give it back. A found thing is not a free thing. Someone is looking for it.
  3. Everything in the whole world belongs to Krishna. When we share and ask politely, Krishna is pleased with us. Honest children are Krishna's dearest friends.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin by placing three small objects in the middle of the circle — a crayon, a toy, and a book. Say to the children: 'These things belong to me.' Then ask: 'What should you say if you want to use one?' Wait for the answer. Praise the child who says 'May I please...' Then say: 'Today we learn a very old word from our tradition. It teaches us to always ask before we take, and to give back what we borrow. The word is ASTEYA.' Chant 'asteya, asteya, asteya' three times together, palms at heart. Ask one more question: 'Have you ever had something taken from you without asking? How did it feel?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Intro circle + 'ask first' demo + chant. 10 min — Read aloud: Hanuman Brings Back the Ring. Use a prop ring; pass it around with each child saying, 'This is Rama's.' 8 min — Read aloud: Meera and the Found Wallet. Use a prop wallet. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Ask First) and Activity 2 (Mine/Yours/Ours). 5 min — Closing chant and the Honest Hands Diary take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, ring-passing game, chant 'asteya'. 15 min — Hanuman Brings Back the Ring with prop ring; each child carries the ring carefully to the next. 15 min — Meera and the Found Wallet; use prop wallet with fake notes; stage the finding and returning. 20 min — Activity 1 (Ask First) and Activity 2 (Mine/Yours/Ours circles). 20 min — Paired Role-Play (Activity 4) — grabbing-to-asking sentences; rotate partners twice. 15 min — Honest Hands Craft: each child traces their hand on paper and writes one thing those hands promise to ASK for this week. 15 min — Mini drama: 'The Lost Crayon Returns Home' — one child is the crayon, one is the asker, one is the owner; rotate. 10 min — Closing: each child names one thing that is MINE, one that is YOURS, one that is OURS. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children understand asteya through CONCRETE ownership first. They need to clearly know: 'This is mine. This is not mine.' Use visible, touchable examples. Hold up a pencil and say 'This is mine.' Hold up the child's water bottle and say 'This is yours.' Pause between. Children learn asteya by being TRUSTED to hold something precious and return it. Try this: give each child a small object (a polished stone, a leaf, a bead) and say 'This is mine. Please take care of it for five minutes and give it back.' Watch their little faces as they hold honest care in their palms. The child who is tempted to take things will benefit most — never shame them; give them a role as 'keeper of the class pencil box' and let them practice honest return. Asteya is built through repeated small trust, not through warning or threat.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'If I find something, it is mine.' Response: A found thing is not a free thing. Someone lost it — maybe an old grandfather, maybe a little friend. Our first job is to try to return it. If no one claims it, we ask a grown-up what to do. Asteya means thinking of the person who is looking.
  • Misconception: 'It is okay to take something small, like one biscuit.' Response: Small and big are not the difference. Asked and not-asked is the difference. One biscuit taken without asking is still taking. One biscuit given with a smile is a gift. The size does not matter — the ASKING matters.
  • Misconception: 'I can take from my family because we share everything.' Response: Families share kindly, but even in a family, we ASK. 'Amma, may I have an apple?' 'Bhaiya, may I use your pencil?' Asking shows respect. Respect is the seed of asteya at home.
  • Misconception: 'If my friend is not looking, I can peek at her test answers.' Response: Peeking is taking. Taking the answer is taking the work. Krishna sees all, even the little peek. Our own answer, even if wrong, is a gift. A copied answer, even if right, is not ours at all.
