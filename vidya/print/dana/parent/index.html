Value at a Glance

Dana means giving with a happy heart. When we see someone without something we have, we share. We give food, toys, clothes, time, or a kind word. Krishna is the real owner of everything — we are only His helpers, passing gifts from His hands to His children.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.1: The Supreme Lord says: Be brave. Keep your heart pure. Learn about God. Give to others. Control yourself. Worship. Read good books. Be disciplined. Be honest.

What it is NOT

Dana is not giving only to get thank-yous. It is not giving the broken toy while keeping the good one. It is not making noise about our giving. It is not giving with a grumpy heart.

  • Saying 'I gave the biggest piece — everyone clap for me!'
  • Refusing to share because 'this is MINE'
  • Giving the smallest, oldest thing and keeping the best for ourselves

Stories Your Child Heard

Sudama's Pouch of Puffed Rice — Long ago, a poor brahmin named Sudama lived in a tiny hut. He had studied at the gurukul with Krishna when they were both boys. But now Sudama was very poor. Sometimes his children went to bed hungry. Sudama's kind wife said, 'Go visit your old friend Krishna. He is a king now. He will be happy to see you.'

Ravi and the Birthday Bag — Ravi was turning seven. His Amma had given him a bright red bag. It was full of ladoos, jalebi, and colourful toffees. Ravi hugged the bag close to his heart. As he walked out of the sweet shop, he saw a small boy standing near the gate. The boy was about Ravi's age. His clothes were torn. His feet had no slippers. His eyes were big and quiet.

The Sanskrit Word

dāna (दान) — DAH-na

Meaning: Giving with a happy heart. Sharing what we have so others can be happy too.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each day this week, do ONE act of dana — big or small. Give a crayon to a friend. Help Amma with a chore without being asked. Share your snack. Say a kind word. Drop a pebble in your Giving Jar for each act. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa what you gave today. Whisper, 'Krishna, I gave this for You.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the Giving Jar has at least 7 pebbles, and the child can tell one specific story of giving that made someone else happy.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the Giving Jar has at least 7 pebbles, and the child can tell one specific story of giving that made someone else happy.

When we keep giving from a pure heart, our whole life becomes an offering — and that offering has its own beautiful name, yajna.
