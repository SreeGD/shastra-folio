Chapter Overview

Dana means giving with a happy heart. When we see someone without something we have, we share. We give food, toys, clothes, time, or a kind word. Krishna is the real owner of everything — we are only His helpers, passing gifts from His hands to His children.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.1 — The Supreme Lord says: Be brave. Keep your heart pure. Learn about God. Give to others. Control yourself. Worship. Read good books. Be disciplined. Be honest.

Core Teaching

  1. Dana means sharing what we have with a happy heart. Food, toys, clothes, time, a kind word — everything we give, we give to Krishna through His children.
  2. When we give, we give the BEST — not the leftover. We give quietly — not for applause. We give happily — not grumpily. This is the way of Sudama and Krishna.
  3. Giving does not make us smaller. Giving makes our heart bigger. The more we share, the more Krishna fills us with love.

Five-Minute Introduction

Place a small bowl of sweets or raisins on the altar. Invite: 'Today we learn a beautiful word — dana, which means giving.' Pass the bowl around. Each child takes ONE and then places it BACK on the altar saying 'For You, Krishna.' Then chant together: 'Dana, dana, dana — I give with joy.' Ask: 'When did someone give something to YOU that made you happy?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Altar offering + chant. 10 min — Read aloud Sudama's story; use a small cloth and rice grains as props. 8 min — Read aloud Ravi's Birthday Bag. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Share or Keep) and Activity 2 (Giving Tree). 5 min — Closing Giving Circle (Activity 4, small version) + introduce the Giving Jar. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Altar offering, chant, and opening share-story. 15 min — Sudama's story with puppet/cloth/rice grains; children re-enact Krishna eating the puffed rice. 15 min — Ravi's Birthday Bag with a role-play of the moment of sharing. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 20 min — Giving Circle (full Activity 4) with reflection afterwards. 15 min — Giving Jar craft: decorate a small clear jar each. 15 min — Whole-class giving project: pack a small bag of extra classroom supplies for a school that needs them. 10 min — Closing: each child names one thing they will give this week. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children connect to dana through CONCRETE acts — a shared crayon, a ladoo given away, a toy passed. Keep it physical and specific. Avoid abstract discussions of 'helping the world.' Use the Sudama story as a touchstone — children love that a torn handful of puffed rice becomes a golden palace. Watch for the possessive child (common at this age) and gently invite them to practice ONE small share per day. Praise the giving itself, not the giver — say 'what a happy thing you did' rather than 'what a good boy you are.' Giving should grow the act, not the ego.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Dana means giving my things away so others have more than me.' Response: Dana is not about having less. It is about sharing joyfully. When we give with love, Krishna fills us back — often more than before. The cup that pours is never empty.
  • Misconception: 'I must give to everyone who asks or I am being bad.' Response: True dana is wise. Giving should go to those who really need, at the right time, in the right way. Ask Amma or a teacher if you are unsure. Saying 'I cannot today, but next time' is also honest.
  • Misconception: 'If I give, people will remember me as kind.' Response: The most beautiful dana is quiet. We give and we forget. We do not wait for thank-yous. When no one knows but Krishna knows — that is the sweetest giving.
