Bhagavad-gita 16.1

श्रीभगवानुवाच
अभयं सत्त्वसंशुद्धिर्ज्ञानयोगव्यवस्थितिः।
दानं दमश्च यज्ञश्च स्वाध्यायस्तप आर्जवम्॥

śrī-bhagavān uvāca abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhir jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ dānaṁ damaś ca yajñaś ca svādhyāyas tapa ārjavam

Word-for-word: śrī-bhagavān uvāca — the Supreme Personality of Godhead said; abhayam — fearlessness; sattva-saṁśuddhiḥ — purification of one's existence; jñāna — in knowledge; yoga — of linking up; vyavasthitiḥ — the situation; dānam — charity; damaḥ — controlling the mind; ca — and; yajñaḥ — performance of sacrifice; ca — and; svādhyāyaḥ — study of Vedic literature; tapaḥ — austerity; ārjavam — simplicity

Translation: The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Fearlessness; purification of one's existence; cultivation of spiritual knowledge; charity; self-control; performance of sacrifice; study of the Vedas; austerity and simplicity;

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 7.5.23

श्रीप्रह्लाद उवाच
न केवलं मे भवतश्च राजन्
स योऽयं काल: सर्वभूतानां
जगद्गुरोर्विष्णोरवेहि वीर्यं
यदेष कालो जगतो जीवन श्च ॥

Translation: Prahlada Maharaja said: My dear King, not only you but also all the other kings and all living entities, including me, are controlled by the supreme time of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore be enlightened and try to understand that this time is Krishna, the Supreme Lord, under whose control are the time factor and we ourselves.

What is Abhaya?

Abhaya is the courage that comes from knowing Krishna protects us. It means facing challenges with a brave heart because we trust in divine help. True fearlessness grows when we remember our spiritual nature and do what is right.

What it is NOT

Abhaya is not reckless behavior without thinking. It is not showing off or trying to prove how tough you are. It is not ignoring real dangers or being careless with safety.

  • Recklessness - acting without thinking of consequences
  • False bravado - pretending to be brave to impress others
  • Stubbornness - refusing to listen to wise advice

Sanskrit Word Study

abhaya (अभय)

Pronunciation: ah-BHA-ya

Meaning: fearlessness, courage, freedom from fear

Example: When we chant Krishna's names with devotion, abhaya naturally develops in our hearts.

Story from the Scriptures

Abhimanyu's Test of Courage — Mahabharata, Drona Parva

Thirteen-year-old Abhimanyu stood at the edge of the battlefield. Before him stretched the Chakravyuha - a deadly wheel formation of enemy warriors. His father Arjuna was trapped far away. His uncles needed someone to break through this formation, but it seemed impossible.

Abhimanyu's heart pounded as he looked at the greatest warriors of the enemy army. They were all waiting to destroy whoever dared enter their trap. Because he was young, doubt crept into his mind. "What if I fail? What if I cannot find my way out?"

But then Abhimanyu remembered Krishna's words: "The soul is eternal. The body may be hurt, but the real you cannot be destroyed." Since Krishna was his guide, Abhimanyu felt his fear melting away. Although the odds were against him, he knew his duty as a warrior.

Abhimanyu touched his bow and whispered a prayer to Krishna. "I offer this battle to You. Please give me strength to do what is right." Because he surrendered his fear to the Divine, courage filled his young heart.

With a war cry that shook the battlefield, Abhimanyu charged into the formation. His arrows flew like lightning. So fearless was his attack that even veteran warriors stepped back in amazement. Although he knew the danger, his spiritual courage never wavered.

Abhimanyu fought with such divine fearlessness that his name became legendary. His courage showed that when we trust in Krishna and do our duty, no challenge is too great. The young warrior's bravery inspired his family and proved that spiritual strength conquers all fear.

A Story from Today

Kenji's Stand Against Bullying

Kenji Tanaka watched from across the Tokyo schoolyard as three older boys surrounded his friend Hiroshi. They were pushing him and calling him names because his family was poor. Hiroshi's lunch money scattered on the ground as he tried to protect himself.

Kenji's stomach twisted with fear. Because these bullies were bigger and stronger, he worried they would hurt him too if he interfered. "What if they make me their next target?" he thought, his hands shaking. Although he wanted to help, his feet felt frozen to the spot.

Then Kenji remembered his grandmother's words from that morning: "Kenji-kun, when you see someone suffering, your heart knows what is right. Trust that feeling." Since his grandmother was very wise, her words gave him strength. Although his heart was still pounding, he knew he had to act.

Kenji took a deep breath and walked toward the group. "Hey, leave Hiroshi alone," he called out, his voice steadier than he expected. Because he spoke with quiet confidence, the bullies looked surprised. Although they were bigger, something in Kenji's calm courage made them hesitate.

"This isn't your business," the biggest bully said, but his voice sounded less sure than before.

"Yes, it is," Kenji replied firmly. "Hiroshi is my friend, and what you're doing is wrong." Since other students had started to gather and watch, the bullies realized they couldn't continue. Although they grumbled, they walked away.

Hiroshi looked at Kenji with grateful tears in his eyes. "Thank you," he whispered. "I was so scared."

Kenji helped his friend pick up the scattered coins. Although his heart was still beating fast, he felt a warm glow inside. Because he had chosen to do what was right despite his fear, he discovered that true courage grows when we care more about helping others than protecting ourselves.

Core Teaching

  1. True courage comes from trusting in Krishna's protection and knowing our spiritual nature is eternal.
  2. Fearlessness grows when we focus on doing what is right rather than worrying about what might happen to us.
  3. Spiritual courage means acting with wisdom and devotion, not rushing into danger without thinking.
