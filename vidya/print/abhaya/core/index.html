Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.1

श्रीभगवानुवाच
अभयं सत्त्वसंशुद्धिर्ज्ञानयोगव्यवस्थितिः।
दानं दमश्च यज्ञश्च स्वाध्यायस्तप आर्जवम्॥

śrī-bhagavān uvāca abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhir jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ dānaṁ damaś ca yajñaś ca svādhyāyas tapa ārjavam

Word-for-word: śrī-bhagavān uvāca — the Supreme Personality of Godhead said; abhayam — fearlessness; sattva-saṁśuddhiḥ — purification of one's existence; jñāna — in knowledge; yoga — of linking up; vyavasthitiḥ — the situation; dānam — charity; damaḥ — controlling the mind; ca — and; yajñaḥ — performance of sacrifice; ca — and; svādhyāyaḥ — study of Vedic literature; tapaḥ — austerity; ārjavam — simplicity

Translation: The Supreme Lord said: Fearlessness comes first among all good qualities. Then comes purity of heart, learning about God, giving to others, self-control, worship, study, discipline and honesty.

श्रीप्रह्राद उवाच

अर्चनं वन्दनं दास्यं सख्यमात्मनिवेदनम् ॥ २३ ॥

śrī-prahrāda uvāca śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṁ pāda-sevanam arcanaṁ vandanaṁ dāsyaṁ sakhyam ātma-nivedanam iti puṁsārpitā viṣṇau bhaktiś cen nava-lakṣaṇā kṛyeta bhagavaty addhā tan manye ’dhītam uttamam

Translation: Prahlada Maharaja said: My dear King, not only you but also all the other kings and all living entities, including me, are controlled by the supreme time of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Therefore be enlightened and try to understand that this time is Krishna, the Supreme Lord, under whose control are the time factor and we ourselves.

What is Abhaya?

Abhaya means being brave when you need to do the right thing. It means Krishna protects you when you are good.

What it is NOT

Abhaya is not being mean or rough with others. It is not showing off or being rude.

  • Being a bully
  • Showing off
  • Being rude to others

Sanskrit Word Study

abhaya (अभय)

Pronunciation: ah-BHA-ya

Meaning: fearlessness, being brave

Example: When we pray to Krishna, we feel abhaya in our hearts.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Dhruva's Journey to the Forest — Srimad Bhagavatam 4.8-9

Once there was a little prince named Dhruva. He was only five years old. His stepmother said mean things to him. She told him he could not sit on his father's lap because his mother was not the queen.

Dhruva felt very sad. He ran to his mother crying. His mother hugged him and said, "Only God can help you now. Go to the forest and pray to Lord Vishnu."

Dhruva was scared. The forest was dark and full of wild animals. But because he wanted to find God, he walked into the forest alone.

At night, Dhruva heard scary sounds. Wolves howled. Owls hooted. He felt cold and hungry. His little heart was beating fast with fear.

But then Dhruva remembered what his mother taught him. "Krishna protects good children," she had said. So Dhruva closed his eyes. He started chanting God's holy names.

As he chanted, something wonderful happened. Warm light filled his heart. The fear went away. Because Dhruva was brave and kept praying, Lord Vishnu himself came to see him.

"My dear child," said Lord Vishnu with a kind smile. "You are very brave. I will give you a special place in the sky. Everyone will see your star and remember your courage."

Dhruva became the Pole Star. Even today, when children look up at night, they can see Dhruva's star shining bright. It reminds us that Krishna helps brave children who do the right thing.

A Story from Today

Yuki and the Lost Kitten

Yuki was walking to school in Tokyo when she heard a tiny sound. "Meow, meow." She looked under a bush and found a small kitten. The kitten was wet from the rain and looked hurt.

Yuki's heart felt heavy. The kitten needed help right away. But she was scared to ask grown-ups. What if her teacher said no? What if her parents got angry?

Yuki stood there in the rain, looking at the poor kitten. Her hands were shaking. She wanted to help, but fear made her feet feel stuck.

Then she remembered her grandmother's words: "When you help others, the gods smile on you. Be brave and do what is right."

Because Yuki knew helping was the right thing to do, she picked up the kitten gently. She ran to school, even though her heart was beating fast.

"Sensei," she said to her teacher, "I found a hurt kitten. Can we help it?"

Her teacher smiled. "Yuki, you are very kind and brave. Let's call the animal doctor right now."

So they called the vet together. The doctor came and took care of the kitten. Because Yuki was brave enough to ask for help, the kitten got better.

Later, Yuki's family adopted the kitten. They named him Yuki, which means "courage." Every day when Yuki saw her kitten playing happily, she felt warm inside. She learned that being brave to help others always brings good things.

Core Teaching

  1. Krishna protects children who try to do good things, even when they feel scared.
  2. Being brave means doing the right thing, not being mean or showing off to others.
  3. When we help others and pray to God, our fear goes away and we feel strong inside.
