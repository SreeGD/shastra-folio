Value at a Glance

Abhaya is the courage that comes from knowing Krishna protects us. It means facing challenges with a brave heart because we trust in divine help. True fearlessness grows when we remember our spiritual nature and do what is right.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.1: The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Fearlessness; purification of one's existence; cultivation of spiritual knowledge; charity; self-control; performance of sacrifice; study of the Vedas; austerity and simplicity;

What it is NOT

Abhaya is not reckless behavior without thinking. It is not showing off or trying to prove how tough you are. It is not ignoring real dangers or being careless with safety.

  • Recklessness - acting without thinking of consequences
  • False bravado - pretending to be brave to impress others
  • Stubbornness - refusing to listen to wise advice

Stories Your Child Heard

Abhimanyu's Test of Courage — Thirteen-year-old Abhimanyu stood at the edge of the battlefield. Before him stretched the Chakravyuha - a deadly wheel formation of enemy warriors. His father Arjuna was trapped far away. His uncles needed someone to break through this formation, but it seemed impossible. Abhimanyu's heart pounded as he looked at the greatest warriors of the enemy army. They were all waiting to destroy whoever dared enter their trap. Because he was young, doubt crept into his mind. "What if I fail? What if I cannot find my way out?"

Kenji's Stand Against Bullying — Kenji Tanaka watched from across the Tokyo schoolyard as three older boys surrounded his friend Hiroshi. They were pushing him and calling him names because his family was poor. Hiroshi's lunch money scattered on the ground as he tried to protect himself. Kenji's stomach twisted with fear. Because these bullies were bigger and stronger, he worried they would hurt him too if he interfered. "What if they make me their next target?" he thought, his hands shaking. Although he wanted to help, his feet felt frozen to the spot.

The Sanskrit Word

abhaya (अभय) — ah-BHA-ya

Meaning: fearlessness, courage, freedom from fear

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each evening before bed, think of one small thing you were afraid to do that day. It could be raising your hand in class, talking to someone new, or admitting a mistake. Say a short prayer asking Krishna for courage, then plan to try that one thing the next day. Keep a simple courage journal - write down what you tried and how it felt.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice that small acts of courage become easier with practice, and you will feel more confident about doing what is right even when it feels scary.

Signs of Growth
