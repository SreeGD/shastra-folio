Value at a Glance

Guru-bhakti means loving my teacher. I listen carefully when my teacher speaks. I say 'thank you, teacher' from my heart. I offer my best work, not my sloppy work. My teacher is Krishna's own helper — a kind lamp who lights the path for my small feet. When I honour my teacher, I am honouring Krishna.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 4.34: Krishna says: Learn from your teacher by three happy steps — bow to them, ask them good questions, and serve them with love. Then the wise ones will open the door of true knowledge for you.

What it is NOT

Guru-bhakti is not rolling my eyes when the teacher speaks. It is not doing my work the easy-fast-lazy way. It is not forgetting my teacher the minute I walk home. It is not only being polite when the teacher is watching and rude when they are not.

  • Yawning loudly and looking bored while teacher is speaking
  • Handing in a crumpled, half-done page when I know I can do better
  • Making fun of a teacher behind their back with other children

Stories Your Child Heard

Ekalavya and the Statue of Drona — Long ago in Bharat, there was a forest boy named Ekalavya. His hair was wild. His feet were bare. But his heart carried one big wish — to learn archery from Guru Drona, the greatest teacher of all. One morning Ekalavya walked through the forest to where Guru Drona was teaching the young princes. The princes had silk clothes and gold arrows. Ekalavya had only a wooden bow.

Aarav's Two Mangoes for Lakshmi-Teacher — Aarav lived in a small town in Andhra Pradesh. Behind his house stood an old mango tree. This summer it gave exactly two mangoes — golden yellow, sweet-smelling, shining like little suns. Aarav's mouth watered. 'Both are MINE,' he whispered.

The Sanskrit Word

guru-bhakti (गुरुभक्ति) — GOO-roo BHUK-tee

Meaning: Loving devotion to the teacher. Listening with a bright heart, offering our best work, and saying a true thank-you. The teacher is Krishna's own helper guiding our small feet on the path.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

The Sunday Three-Step Guru Ritual: every Sunday morning for four weeks, do three tiny actions. (1) BOW — stand in front of Krishna's picture plus a photo of your school teacher (or any teacher you love). Fold hands. Bow your head. (2) ASK — whisper aloud ONE question you want to learn this week. (3) SERVE — plan ONE small service for a teacher in the coming week: carry their books, wipe the board, water the plant, say a clear 'Good morning, teacher' with folded hands, or hand in your neatest work. On one special Sunday, take a small gift (one flower, one fruit, a hand-drawn card) to a teacher — especially an OLD teacher from last year or kindergarten — and simply say 'Thank you, teacher.' Touch their feet if your family practices it. Before bed each Sunday, tell Amma or Appa which of the three steps you did.

Duration: Every Sunday for four weeks, with one special 'thank-you visit' during the month
Look for: By the end of four weeks, the child can name three teachers and one specific thing each one gave them. The child voluntarily greets teachers with folded hands and offers their neatest work. Parents and old teachers report being surprised and moved by the child's unprompted thank-you.

Signs of Growth

By the end of four weeks, the child can name three teachers and one specific thing each one gave them. The child voluntarily greets teachers with folded hands and offers their neatest work. Parents and old teachers report being surprised and moved by the child's unprompted thank-you.

When our heart bows to the teacher every morning, a sweet new quality grows silently inside us — the quiet, steady trust called śraddhā.
