Value at a Glance

Vairagya means not clinging too tightly to things. We can love our toys, our friends, our favourite food — but inside we remember that Krishna is the real owner. We enjoy what comes. We smile when it goes. Our happiness does not depend on ONE thing — it lives quietly inside us, always.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 6.35: Krishna says: Yes, Arjuna, the mind is jumpy and hard to catch. But by practising every day, and by not clinging too tightly to things, even the jumpy mind can be held gently — like a little bird in cupped hands.

What it is NOT

Vairagya is not hating nice things or throwing away our toys. It is not being cold or grumpy. It is not crying for days when something breaks. It is not saying 'I do not care' about everything. Vairagya is a soft, open hand — not a clenched fist, not an empty one.

  • Screaming and crying for one whole day when a favourite toy breaks
  • Refusing to share because 'this is MINE — I NEED it!'
  • Saying 'I do not like anything anymore' when one thing goes wrong

Stories Your Child Heard

King Bharata and the Little Deer — Long ago, a great king named Bharata lived in India. He was kind and brave. But one day he said, 'I want to remember Krishna with my whole heart.' So he gave his kingdom to his son and walked into the forest to live a simple life. Bharata made a small hut by a river. He chanted Krishna's names all day. His heart felt light, like a bird.

Meera and the Little Blue Elephant — Meera was seven years old. She had ONE special toy — a tiny blue elephant with a little gold saddle. Dadi had given it to her on her birthday. Meera carried it everywhere. It slept on her pillow. It came to school in her bag. One Monday, the school bus went over a big bump. Meera's bag tipped over. The little blue elephant rolled out and fell somewhere on the bus floor.

The Sanskrit Word

vairāgya (वैराग्य) — vai-RAHG-ya

Meaning: Not clinging. Holding what we love with a soft, open hand — enjoying it without squeezing, letting go without breaking. Remembering that Krishna is the real owner of everything.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Set up a Letting-Go Shelf at home — a small corner or box called 'Krishna's Shelf.' Each day this week, place ONE thing on it that you are ready to release gently — a toy you have outgrown, a drawing to gift, a sweet to share, a kind word for someone. At the end of the week, give each thing away with a smile. Also: whenever something you love breaks, gets lost, or has to go, take three slow breaths, put your hand on your heart, and whisper: 'Krishna, this was Yours. I return it with love.' Notice the soft warmth that stays inside.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the Letting-Go Shelf has at least 5 items ready to give, and the child can demonstrate the soft-hand gesture and the three-breath whisper when something small does not go their way — at least once without a reminder.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the Letting-Go Shelf has at least 5 items ready to give, and the child can demonstrate the soft-hand gesture and the three-breath whisper when something small does not go their way — at least once without a reminder.

When our hand becomes soft enough to let things go gently, our heart is free — and that free heart is ready to love Krishna with everything it has. That love has its own beautiful name: bhakti.
