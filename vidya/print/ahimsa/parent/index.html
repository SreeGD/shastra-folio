Parent Guide

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Value at a Glance

Ahimsa means not hurting anyone — not with hands, not with words, not even with angry thoughts. We are gentle with people, animals, plants, and even small insects. Every living being has a soul inside, just like you.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2: Do not hurt anyone. Speak the truth. Do not get angry. Give up what you do not need. Be peaceful. Do not find fault. Be kind to every living being. Do not be greedy. Be gentle. Be humble. Do not give up.

What it is NOT

Ahimsa is not hitting, pushing, or calling names. It is not laughing when someone is hurt. It is not hurting little creatures on purpose. When we are angry, we still use gentle hands and kind words.

  • Pushing a classmate to grab a toy
  • Calling someone a mean name when you are upset
  • Stepping on an ant just for fun

Stories Your Child Heard

The Little Swan and the Brave Prince — Long ago in India, a little prince named Siddhartha walked in his garden. The sky was blue. The birds were singing. Suddenly, a white swan fell from the sky. Thud! It landed in the soft grass. An arrow was in its wing. The swan was crying softly.

Arun and the Ant Line — Arun was playing in the courtyard with his friend Kiran. The afternoon sun was warm. A piece of chapati had fallen on the ground. Suddenly, Kiran shouted, 'Look, Arun! Ants!'

The Sanskrit Word

ahiṁsā (अहिंसा) — ah-HIM-sa

Meaning: Not hurting. Being gentle with every living being — people, animals, plants, insects.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, do the Ant Walk. Every time you walk outside, look down. Step AROUND ants, worms, and small creatures. Tell Amma or Appa each night how many creatures you saved. Also: each time you feel angry, breathe three times before you speak or touch anyone.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 times they chose gentle action over rough, and can demonstrate the three-breath pause when upset.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 times they chose gentle action over rough, and can demonstrate the three-breath pause when upset.

When we stop hurting others, we naturally feel kindness in our hearts — and that warm feeling is called daya.
