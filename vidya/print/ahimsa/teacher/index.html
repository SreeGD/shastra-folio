Ahimsa

अहिंसा · ah-HIM-sa

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Ahimsa means not hurting anyone — not with hands, not with words, not even with angry thoughts. We are gentle with people, animals, plants, and even small insects. Every living being has a soul inside, just like you.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.2 — Do not hurt anyone. Speak the truth. Do not get angry. Give up what you do not need. Be peaceful. Do not find fault. Be kind to every living being. Do not be greedy. Be gentle. Be humble. Do not give up.

Core Teaching

  1. Ahimsa means do not hurt anyone — not with hands, not with words, not even with angry thoughts. All living beings have a soul, just like you.
  2. When we are angry, we still use gentle hands and kind words. Hurting others is easy, but being gentle is strong.
  3. Every life matters — even the tiniest ant. When we protect life, Krishna is happy with us.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin with a circle. Ask: 'Show me your hands.' Let children raise them. Then say: 'Our hands can do two things — they can help, or they can hurt. Today we learn a very old word from our tradition that teaches us to always use our hands gently. That word is ahimsa.' Chant 'ahimsa, ahimsa, ahimsa' three times together, palms at heart. Then ask: 'Can you think of a time someone was gentle with you?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Intro circle + chant. 10 min — Read aloud: The Little Swan. Pause and act out pulling the arrow gently. 8 min — Read aloud: Arun and the Ant Line. Use a chalk to draw a circle on the floor. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Circle-the-Face) and Activity 2 (Gentle Hands Drawing). 5 min — Closing chant and the Ant Walk take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, hand-song, chant 'ahimsa'. 15 min — The Little Swan story with puppet or soft-toy swan; children take turns gently 'healing' the wing. 15 min — Arun and the Ant Line story; go outside (if possible) and do a real ant walk together. 20 min — Activity 1 (Kind/Unkind Sorting) and Activity 2 (Gentle Hands Drawing). 20 min — Paired Role-Play (Activity 4) — practice angry-to-gentle sentences; rotate partners twice. 15 min — Gentle Hands Craft: each child traces their hand on paper and writes one kind thing those hands will do this week. 15 min — Nature walk or mindful classroom circle: notice the plants and small creatures in our space. 10 min — Closing: each child names one living thing they will protect. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children understand ahimsa through the BODY first — gentle hands, soft voice, careful steps. Keep examples visible and physical. Use your own hands to demonstrate 'gentle holding' (two hands cupped as if holding a butterfly) vs 'rough grabbing' (one hand clenched). Avoid abstract moral discussion. Watch for the child who is rough with others — never shame them; instead invite them to help care for a class plant or pet as a gentle-hands practice. Children learn ahimsa by BEING trusted to protect something small.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Ahimsa means I have to let anyone hurt me.' Response: Not at all. Ahimsa means WE do not hurt others. If someone hurts you, go to a grown-up for help. Being gentle does not mean being unsafe.
  • Misconception: 'Ants are so small, it does not matter if I step on them.' Response: Small lives are still lives. Every ant has a family. Every worm has a home. Size does not decide who deserves kindness. Krishna sees every living being.
  • Misconception: 'If I am angry, I cannot be gentle.' Response: Being angry is normal — everyone feels angry sometimes. But what we DO when angry is our choice. Ahimsa is the practice of using gentle hands and kind words even when we feel upset inside.
