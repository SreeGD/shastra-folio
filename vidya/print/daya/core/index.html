Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम् ।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम् ॥२॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Word-for-word: ahiṁsā — nonviolence; satyam — truthfulness; akrodhaḥ — freedom from anger; tyāgaḥ — renunciation; śāntiḥ — tranquillity; apaiśunam — aversion to faultfinding; dayā — mercy; bhūteṣu — towards all living entities; aloluptvam — freedom from greed; mārdavam — gentleness; hrīḥ — modesty; acāpalam — determination

Translation: Kindness, truth, no anger, giving, peace, not finding fault, caring for all living beings, not being greedy, being gentle, being shy, and staying strong.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

GS 6.10.4

यो मां सर्वेषु भूतेषु सन्तमात्मानमीश्वरम्। हित्वार्चां भजते मौढ्यादभाषो गुणदोषदृक्॥

dvārakāṁ nagarīṁ dṛśṭvā naro naṛāyaṇo bhavet dvārakāyāṁ mṛtaḥ ko 'pi gardabho 'pi catur-bhujaḥ

Translation: Anyone who worships Me in the temple but does not know that I am present in the hearts of all living beings is in ignorance. He who sees differences between living beings and does not see Me equally present in all beings is a fool who is unaware of spiritual knowledge.

What is Daya?

Daya means caring when you see someone hurt or sad. It makes you want to help them feel better.

What it is NOT

Daya is not being mean to others when you are upset or angry.

  • Being mean when upset
  • Not caring when others cry
  • Hurting animals or friends

Sanskrit Word Study

dayā (दया)

Pronunciation: dah-YAH

Meaning: caring, kindness, wanting to help when someone is hurt

Example: When Maya saw the hurt butterfly, daya made her want to help it feel better.

Story from the Scriptures

The Kind Prince and the Crying Bird — Srimad Bhagavatam 10.11.45-50

Little Krishna was playing with his friends in the forest. They were having so much fun running and laughing together.

Suddenly, Krishna heard a tiny sound. "Cheep! Cheep!" It was very sad. Because Krishna had a kind heart, he stopped playing to listen.

There on the ground was a baby bird. It had fallen from its nest high up in a tree. The little bird was crying for its mama.

"Come on, Krishna!" called his friends. "Let's keep playing! The bird will be fine."

But Krishna felt sad when he saw the crying bird. Because he cared about all living things, he knew he had to help.

"Wait for me," Krishna told his friends. "This little bird needs help first."

Krishna gently picked up the tiny bird. It was so small and scared. Then he looked up at the tall tree. The nest was very high up.

Although the tree was hard to climb, Krishna started going up. Branch by branch, he climbed higher. The bird's mama was waiting in the nest, worried about her baby.

When Krishna reached the nest, he carefully put the baby bird back with its mama. The mama bird chirped happily. The baby bird stopped crying.

Krishna climbed back down with a big smile. Because he had helped the bird family, his heart felt warm and happy.

His friends saw how happy Krishna looked. "You did a good thing," they said. "Now let's play!"

And they all played together, feeling good because they had learned that helping others makes everyone happy.

A Story from Today

Maya and the Hurt Butterfly

Maya was playing in her garden when her friends called to her. "Maya! Come quick! We have a new game to play!"

Maya started to run to her friends. But then she saw something that made her stop. A beautiful butterfly was on the ground. One of its wings was torn.

The butterfly was trying to fly, but it couldn't. It looked so sad.

"Maya! Hurry up!" her friends called again. "The game is really fun!"

Maya looked at her friends. Then she looked at the hurt butterfly. Because she had a caring heart, she felt sad for the little butterfly.

"I'll be there in a minute," Maya called back. "I need to help someone first."

Maya knelt down next to the butterfly. "Don't worry, little butterfly," she said softly. "I will help you."

She ran to get a small box and put soft leaves inside. Although the butterfly was scared, Maya was very gentle. She carefully moved the butterfly into the box.

Maya put the box in a quiet, safe place in the garden. She gave the butterfly some sugar water on a tiny spoon.

"There," she said. "Now you can rest and get better."

When Maya's friends saw what she was doing, they came over. "Oh! The poor butterfly," they said. "Can we help too?"

So all the friends took turns caring for the butterfly. They brought it flowers and kept it safe.

After a few days, the butterfly's wing got better. When they let it go, it flew in a happy circle around them before flying away.

"That felt good," said Maya's friend Sam. "Helping made me feel warm inside."

Maya smiled. Because she had chosen to help when someone was hurt, everyone learned that caring for others makes the world more beautiful.

Core Teaching

  1. When we see someone hurt or sad, our heart wants to help them feel better.
  2. Being kind to all living things makes us feel happy and warm inside.
  3. Helping others is more important than just having fun for ourselves.
