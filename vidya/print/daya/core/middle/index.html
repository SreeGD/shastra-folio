Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम् ।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम् ॥२॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Word-for-word: ahiṁsā — nonviolence; satyam — truthfulness; akrodhaḥ — freedom from anger; tyāgaḥ — renunciation; śāntiḥ — tranquillity; apaiśunam — aversion to faultfinding; dayā — mercy; bhūteṣu — towards all living entities; aloluptvam — freedom from greed; mārdavam — gentleness; hrīḥ — modesty; acāpalam — determination

Translation: Nonviolence, truthfulness, freedom from anger, renunciation, peace, not finding fault with others, compassion for all living beings, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty, and steady determination.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 3.29.21

यो मां सर्वेषु भूतेषु सन्तमात्मानमीश्वरम्। हित्वार्चां भजते मौढ्यादभाषो गुणदोषदृक्॥

Translation: Anyone who worships Me in the temple but does not know that I am present in the hearts of all living beings is in ignorance. He who sees differences between living beings and does not see Me equally present in all beings is a fool who is unaware of spiritual knowledge.

What is Daya?

Daya is the divine quality of compassion that naturally flows from the heart when we see others suffering. It is the feeling that moves us to help and heal, recognizing that all living beings deserve kindness and care. True compassion sees beyond differences and responds with love.

What it is NOT

Daya is not mere pity that looks down on others, nor is it emotional weakness that makes us cry without helping. It is not selective kindness only for friends and family, ignoring the suffering of strangers or those different from us.

  • Cruelty - deliberately causing pain to others
  • Indifference - not caring when others suffer
  • Selfishness - helping only when it benefits us

Sanskrit Word Study

dayā (दया)

Pronunciation: dah-YAH

Meaning: Compassion, mercy, kindness toward all living beings

Example: When we feel dayā in our hearts, we naturally want to help those who are suffering.

Story from the Scriptures

Yudhishthira's Test of Equal Love — Mahabharata, Vana Parva

Young Prince Yudhishthira walked through the forest with his four brothers, searching for water during their exile. The sun blazed overhead, and their throats burned with thirst. When they reached a beautiful lake, Yudhishthira felt relief wash over him.

"Wait!" called a mysterious voice as his brother Nakula bent to drink. "Answer my questions first, or face the consequences!"

But Nakula was too thirsty to listen. He drank deeply and immediately collapsed. One by one, Sahadeva, Arjuna, and even mighty Bhima ignored the warning. Each brother drank and fell motionless by the water's edge.

Yudhishthira's heart shattered as he knelt beside his brothers. "Please," he whispered to the invisible Yaksha. "They are everything to me."

"I am the guardian of this lake," the voice replied. "Because you showed respect, I will answer your questions and then offer you a choice. I can bring back only one brother. Choose wisely."

After answering the Yaksha's questions about dharma, Yudhishthira faced the terrible decision. His mind raced. Bhima was his closest companion, strong and loyal. Arjuna was the greatest archer in the world. Both were sons of his own mother, Kunti.

But then Yudhishthira looked at Nakula and Sahadeva, sons of his step-mother Madri. Although they shared the same father, they came from a different mother. Many people would choose their own blood first.

"I choose Nakula," Yudhishthira said quietly.

The Yaksha's voice filled with surprise. "Why not Bhima, who is strong like a thousand elephants? Why not Arjuna, whose arrows never miss their mark?"

Yudhishthira's eyes filled with tears, but his voice remained steady. "Because my father had two wives - Kunti and Madri. If only I survive from Kunti's sons, then Madri's line will be completely lost. That would not be dharma. Both mothers should have at least one son living."

Silence filled the forest. Then the Yaksha's form shimmered into view - tall, radiant, and smiling with deep approval.

"You have shown true compassion, young prince," the Yaksha said. "You chose not from attachment, but from equal love for all. Because your heart sees no difference between your own brothers and your step-brothers, I will restore all four."

One by one, Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna, and Bhima opened their eyes and sat up, alive and well. Yudhishthira embraced each brother with joy, but the lesson remained in his heart forever. True compassion treats all beings with equal care, not based on how close they are to us, but because all deserve love.

A Story from Today

Kenji's Choice

Kenji counted the coins in his jar for the hundredth time. After eight months of saving every yen from his allowance and part-time job helping at his uncle's shop, he finally had enough to buy the new gaming console he'd been dreaming about.

"Tomorrow," he whispered excitedly to himself in his small Tokyo apartment. "Tomorrow I'll have it."

But the next morning at school, Kenji noticed his classmate Hiroshi sitting alone, looking worried. Hiroshi usually smiled and joked with everyone, but today his eyes were red and he kept staring at his phone.

"What's wrong?" Kenji asked, sitting beside him.

Hiroshi hesitated, then showed Kenji a message from his mother. "My grandmother is in the hospital. She needs medicine that costs exactly 45,000 yen, but we don't have enough money. The doctor says without it, she might not get better."

Kenji's stomach dropped. His gaming console cost exactly 45,000 yen.

"Can't your parents borrow money?" Kenji asked hopefully.

Hiroshi shook his head. "My father lost his job last month. My mother works two jobs already. We've asked everyone we know." His voice cracked. "My grandmother raised me when my parents worked. She taught me origami, told me stories, made me feel brave when I was scared. I don't know what I'll do if something happens to her."

All day, Kenji couldn't concentrate. During math class, he kept thinking about his gaming console. During lunch, he imagined Hiroshi's grandmother lying in a hospital bed. After school, he walked slowly toward the electronics store, his jar of coins heavy in his backpack.

At the store entrance, Kenji stopped. He could see the gaming console through the window, gleaming under the bright lights. He'd worked so hard for this moment. But then he remembered Hiroshi's face - the fear, the love for his grandmother, the helplessness.

Kenji's hands shook as he turned away from the store. Although his heart felt like it was breaking, he walked back to school where Hiroshi was waiting for his mother to pick him up.

"Hiroshi," Kenji said, pulling out his coin jar. "I want to help your grandmother."

Hiroshi's eyes widened. "Kenji, no! This is your gaming console money. You've been saving for months!"

"Your grandmother is more important than any game," Kenji said, though his voice wavered. "She helped raise you. Now I want to help save her."

Tears filled Hiroshi's eyes as he accepted the jar. "I promise I'll pay you back somehow."

"Just help her get better," Kenji replied.

Two weeks later, Hiroshi bounded up to Kenji with a huge smile. "She's coming home tomorrow! The medicine worked perfectly. And Kenji..." He handed him an envelope. "My grandmother wanted to give you this."

Inside was a hand-written note: "Dear Kenji, because of your kindness, I will see many more sunrises. Your compassion has given me the greatest gift - more time with my family. I have asked everyone in our neighborhood about your sacrifice, and we all want to thank you."

That afternoon, Kenji's doorbell rang. Outside stood Hiroshi, his parents, his grandmother (looking healthy and smiling), and a dozen neighbors - all holding a large box.

"We all contributed," Hiroshi's father explained. "When we heard what you did, we knew we had to show you that kindness creates more kindness."

Inside the box was the gaming console Kenji had wanted, along with three games and a note signed by everyone: "For a young man who shows us what true compassion looks like."

As Kenji looked at all the smiling faces around him, he realized something amazing. The warm feeling in his chest from helping Hiroshi's grandmother felt even better than any video game ever could. He had discovered that when we open our hearts to others' suffering, we don't lose something - we gain a joy that multiplies when shared.

Core Teaching

  1. True compassion sees all beings as deserving of kindness, not just those close to us or those we like.
  2. When we feel moved to help someone who is suffering, that feeling is daya calling us to act with love.
  3. Compassionate actions create a circle of kindness that comes back to bless us in unexpected ways.
Next time we will learn about seva (selfless service) - when our compassion naturally flows into action to help others without expecting anything in return.
