Worksheet

Worksheet: Daya (Compassion)

Section 1: Story Analysis

Analyze the connections between the epic and modern stories:

1. Parallel Struggles: How are Karna's and Arjun's moral dilemmas similar? What internal conflict does each face when choosing between self-interest and compassion?

2. Wisdom in Action: Both protagonists could have chosen easier paths. Explain how each character's decision demonstrates that true compassion requires both emotional response and practical wisdom.

3. Universal Principles: What does each story teach about the relationship between personal sacrifice and genuine care for others? How do both characters show that daya transcends cultural and historical boundaries?

Section 2: Collaborative Design Challenge

Time: 20 minutes | Group Size: 3-4 students

Challenge: Design a "Compassion in Action" board game that teaches younger students (ages 10-12) about the balance between kindness and wisdom.

Materials: Large paper, colored markers, dice, small objects for game pieces

Requirements:

  • Create a board path with 20-25 spaces
  • Include scenario cards that present moral dilemmas requiring compassionate choices
  • Design "Wisdom Check" spaces where players must explain their reasoning
  • Include both positive consequences (move forward) and learning moments (reflect and try again)
  • Write clear, simple rules that a 10-year-old could follow

Reflection Questions (discuss while creating):

  • How can a game help someone practice making compassionate choices?
  • What's the difference between scenarios that require "soft compassion" versus "tough love"?

Section 3: Ethical Dilemma Strategy Game

Format: Class debate with rotating perspectives

Scenario: Your school's student council has ₹10,000 to spend on one of these options:

  • A) Fund free lunch for 20 students from low-income families for one month
  • B) Buy equipment for a new peer counseling program to help students with mental health struggles
  • C) Create a scholarship fund for one student to attend a coding bootcamp
  • D) Install water purifiers in all classrooms to improve everyone's health

Game Rules:

1. Class divides into four teams, each advocating for one option

2. Each team gets 3 minutes to present their case

3. Teams then rotate and must argue for a different option (builds perspective-taking)

4. Final round: individuals vote based on compassionate reasoning, not team loyalty

Debrief Questions:

  • Which choice would help the most people? Which would have the deepest impact?
  • How do we balance immediate needs versus long-term benefits?
  • When resources are limited, how do we make compassionate choices without leaving anyone behind?

Section 4: Extended Reflection Essay

Prompt: "The Complexity of Compassion"

Write a 400-500 word essay exploring this question: *When does compassion require us to say "no" or set boundaries, and how do we distinguish between enabling harmful behavior and showing genuine care?*

Structure your essay around:

  • Introduction: Define what you mean by "complex compassion" with a specific example
  • Body Paragraph 1: Describe a situation where unlimited kindness might actually cause harm
  • Body Paragraph 2: Explain how wisdom and discernment work together with emotional compassion
  • Body Paragraph 3: Give an example (real or hypothetical) of "tough love" that demonstrates true care
  • Conclusion: Reflect on how this understanding might change how you show compassion in your own relationships

Consider these scenarios as inspiration:

  • A friend who constantly asks to copy your homework
  • A family member struggling with addiction who asks for money
  • A classmate who uses emotional manipulation to avoid consequences
  • Someone who refuses help but continues to complain about their problems

Section 5: Real-World Application Challenge

This Week's Practice: "Compassionate Problem-Solving"

Choose one ongoing problem in your school, family, or community where people are suffering or struggling. Instead of just feeling bad about it or offering quick fixes, apply the three-step compassionate response:

Step 1: Deep Listening (Days 1-2)

  • Talk to at least two people affected by this problem
  • Ask: "What does this feel like for you?" and "What would actually help?"
  • Listen without trying to solve or give advice

Step 2: Root Cause Analysis (Days 3-4)

  • Research or reflect: What underlying factors contribute to this problem?
  • Consider: What are the immediate symptoms versus deeper causes?
  • Ask: What would address the source, not just the effects?

Step 3: Wise Action (Days 5-7)

  • Design one small, practical action you can take
  • Ensure your action respects people's dignity and doesn't create dependency
  • Take the action and observe the results

Reflection Questions (journal daily):

  • What surprised you about what people actually wanted versus what you assumed they needed?
  • How did focusing on root causes change your understanding of the problem?
  • What did you learn about the difference between helping and fixing?

Home Practice

Each day this week, practice the 'Pause and Ask' technique when you encounter someone who is struggling or upset. Instead of immediately offering advice or trying to fix their problem, pause and ask one genuine question: 'What would be most helpful for you right now?' or 'How are you feeling about this?' Listen to their full response before taking any action. If they ask for advice, offer it gently. If they just need to be heard, simply listen with full attention.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice that people respond differently when they feel truly heard versus when they feel rushed toward solutions. You may discover that what people actually need is often different from what you assumed they needed. By the end of the week, you should feel more confident in your ability to offer compassion that truly serves others rather than just making yourself feel helpful.
