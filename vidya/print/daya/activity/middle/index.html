Worksheet

Daya (Compassion) - Worksheet

Section 1: Story Understanding

1. Why did Yudhishthira choose to revive Nakula instead of his own brother Bhima?

2. What made Kenji's decision to help Hiroshi so difficult?

3. How did both Yudhishthira and Kenji show that compassion means treating all people equally?

4. What happened because both characters chose compassion over their own desires?

5. Describe a time when you felt moved to help someone who was suffering. What did you do?

Section 2: What Would You Do?

Read each situation and write what a compassionate person would do:

Scenario A: Your classmate forgot their lunch money and looks embarrassed. You have enough money to buy lunch for both of you, but you were saving it for a special snack you wanted.

Scenario B: You see a younger student being teased by older kids in the playground. The older kids are popular and you don't want them to start picking on you too.

Scenario C: Your neighbor's cat is injured and needs to go to the vet, but they don't have a car. Your family was planning to use the car for a fun trip to the mall.

Section 3: Craft Activity - Compassion Web

Materials needed: Colored yarn or string, cardboard circle (paper plate works), scissors, markers

Instructions:

1. Cut a circle from cardboard about 8 inches across

2. Write "DAYA" in the center and decorate it

3. Make 8 small holes around the edge of the circle

4. Think of 8 different ways you can show compassion (helping animals, being kind to new students, etc.)

5. Write each way near a hole around the edge

6. Take your yarn and create a web by connecting each compassion action to the center and to each other

7. As you weave each strand, say: "When I show compassion by _____, I strengthen the web of kindness"

Reflection while making: Notice how each strand makes the web stronger. This is how acts of compassion connect all living beings.

Section 4: Game Activity - Circle of Care

Players: 6-20 people Time: 10-15 minutes Setup: Everyone sits in a circle

Rules:

1. One person starts by saying: "I feel compassion when I see..." and describes a situation (like "a lost dog" or "someone crying")

2. The next person says: "I can help by..." and suggests one specific action

3. The third person says: "This creates..." and describes the positive result

4. Continue around the circle with new situations

5. If someone can't think of something in 10 seconds, the group helps them

Debrief Questions:

  • Which situations made you feel the strongest urge to help?
  • What surprised you about the different ways people wanted to help?
  • How did it feel to imagine helping in each situation?

Section 5: Personal Reflection

Write one paragraph (5-7 sentences) about this question: "Describe someone you know who shows great compassion. What do they do that makes you think of them as compassionate? How does being around them make you feel?"

Be specific about their actions, not just their personality. Instead of writing "they are nice," write "they always ask how I'm feeling when I look sad."

Section 6: Discussion Preparation

Complete this sentence to share with the class: "One question I want to ask the class about compassion is: _______________"

Some ideas to get you thinking:

  • Is it possible to be too compassionate?
  • How do we show compassion to people we don't like?
  • What's the difference between feeling sorry for someone and feeling compassion?

Home Practice

Create a 'Compassion Detective' journal. Each day, look for three acts of compassion - one you witness, one you receive, and one you give. Write a short sentence about each one, noting how it made people feel.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice compassion happening all around you and feel more connected to others. Your heart will feel warmer and you'll find yourself naturally looking for ways to help.
