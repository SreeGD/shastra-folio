Worksheet

Daya (Compassion) - College Level Worksheet

Section 1: Integrative Analysis

Compare and contrast the compassion demonstrated by King Shibi and Priya Sharma:

1. Structural Analysis: How do both protagonists face a choice between personal cost and protecting others? What are the specific stakes for each character?

2. Motivational Depth: What drives each character's decision to act compassionately despite significant personal risk? How do their motivations reflect different aspects of daya?

3. Systemic Impact: Analyze how individual acts of compassion in both stories create broader changes. What does this suggest about the relationship between personal virtue and social transformation?

Section 2: Design Thinking Challenge

Create a Compassion Training Program

Design a 6-week program to develop sustainable compassionate practices for young professionals in high-stress careers (medicine, law, social work, etc.). Your program should address:

  • Week 1-2: Foundation building (understanding compassion vs. sympathy, self-care practices)
  • Week 3-4: Skill development (active listening, difficult conversations, boundary setting)
  • Week 5-6: Integration and sustainability (preventing burnout, systemic thinking)

Include specific activities, discussion topics, and assessment methods. Consider how to balance emotional engagement with professional effectiveness.

Section 3: Ethical Dilemma Analysis

Case Study: The Whistleblower's Choice

Dr. Sarah Chen, a psychiatric resident, discovers that her hospital is systematically over-medicating elderly patients to reduce staffing needs. The practice is not immediately life-threatening but significantly impacts patients' quality of life. Speaking up would likely result in her dismissal and difficulty finding another residency position, effectively ending her medical career.

Analysis Framework:

1. Stakeholder Impact: Who benefits and who suffers from the current situation vs. potential changes?

2. Competing Values: How do compassion, professional loyalty, personal ambition, and systemic reform conflict here?

3. Action Options: Generate at least 5 different approaches Dr. Chen could take, analyzing the ethical implications of each.

4. Daya Application: How would the principle of daya guide decision-making in this situation? What would authentic compassion require?

Section 4: Philosophical Reflection

Write a 750-word reflective essay addressing:

"The Bhagavad-gita states that compassion toward all living entities is a divine quality (daivi sampat). In our contemporary world of global interconnectedness and systemic inequalities, how can individuals cultivate and express daya in ways that address both immediate suffering and root causes? Draw from the chapter's teachings, your personal experience, and current social challenges."

Consider:

  • The relationship between individual transformation and social change
  • How technology and globalization affect our capacity for authentic compassion
  • The balance between local service and global awareness
  • Practical strategies for avoiding compassion fatigue while maintaining engagement

Section 5: Research and Application Project

Independent Study Options (Choose one):

Option A: Historical Analysis Research a historical figure known for compassionate leadership (Gandhi, Mother Teresa, Martin Luther King Jr., Thich Nhat Hanh, etc.). Analyze how they balanced personal spiritual practice with social action, examining both their successes and limitations. Present findings in a 10-minute presentation.

Option B: Contemporary Innovation Identify a current organization or movement addressing suffering through innovative approaches (medical missions, restorative justice programs, environmental activism, etc.). Conduct interviews with participants and analyze how their work embodies or challenges traditional concepts of daya. Create a multimedia report.

Option C: Personal Practice Design Develop and implement a 30-day personal daya practice, documenting your experience through daily reflection journals. Design practices that stretch your comfort zone while remaining sustainable. Conclude with a synthesis paper analyzing insights gained and plans for continued development.

Reflection Questions for Discussion

1. How does the concept of daya as "spiritual discipline" differ from conventional approaches to compassion training in secular contexts?

2. What are the potential dangers of compassion without wisdom (viveka)? How can we avoid enabling harmful behavior while maintaining genuine care?

3. In what ways might systemic oppression limit individuals' capacity to express daya? How can compassionate action address structural as well as individual suffering?

Home Practice

Choose one person in your daily life who triggers irritation or judgment in you (a difficult classmate, family member, coworker, or even a public figure you dislike). Each morning for one week, spend 3 minutes sending them genuine good wishes—not trying to like them, but sincerely hoping for their happiness and freedom from suffering. Notice what happens to your own mental state and interactions with this person.

Duration: 3 minutes each morning for one week
Look for: You will notice either a softening in your own attitude toward this person, a shift in how they respond to you, or increased clarity about healthy boundaries needed in the relationship. The practice works when you feel less emotionally reactive and more spacious around this person's behavior.
