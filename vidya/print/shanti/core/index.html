Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 2.66

नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना।
न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम्॥

nāsti buddhir ayuktasya na cāyuktasya bhāvanā na cābhāvayataḥ śāntir aśāntasya kutaḥ sukham

Word-for-word: na asti — there is not; buddhiḥ — clear thinking; ayuktasya — of one whose mind is not quiet; na — not; ca — and; ayuktasya — of the restless one; bhāvanā — steady feeling; na — not; ca — and; abhāvayataḥ — without steady feeling; śāntiḥ — peace; aśāntasya — of one without peace; kutaḥ — where?; sukham — happiness.

Translation: When the mind is not quiet, we cannot think clearly. When we cannot think clearly, we cannot be happy. First the mind becomes still. Then peace comes. Then happiness.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.19.36

शमो मन्निष्ठता बुद्धेर्दम इन्द्रियसंयमः।
तितिक्षा दुःखसम्मर्षो जिह्वोपस्थजयो धृतिः॥

śamo man-niṣṭhatā buddher dama indriya-saṁyamaḥ titikṣā duḥkha-sammarṣo jihvopastha-jayo dhṛtiḥ

Translation: True peace of mind comes when the intelligence is firmly fixed on Me. Self-control is holding the senses gently. Patience is bearing difficulties with a still heart. Steadiness means staying calm through all things.

What is Shanti?

Shanti means peace inside our heart. It is the quiet feeling when our hands are calm, our voice is soft, and our heart is still. Even when everything outside is noisy — cars honking, friends shouting, thunder roaring — shanti is the small, quiet place inside us that stays steady. We touch it by breathing slowly and remembering Krishna.

What it is NOT

Shanti is not running away and hiding. It is not being grumpy and silent. It is not pretending we are not upset when our heart is racing. And it is not just going to sleep to forget our troubles. Shanti is awake, soft, and alive — like a candle flame in a glass jar.

  • Shouting back when someone shouts at us
  • Hiding in a corner and refusing to speak
  • Stamping our feet when we cannot have what we want

Sanskrit Word Study

śānti (शान्ति)

Pronunciation: SHAHN-tee

Meaning: Peace inside the heart. The still, quiet feeling when our hands, our voice, and our heart are all calm together.

Example: When I close my eyes and whisper Krishna's name, shanti comes into my heart.

Story from the Scriptures

Prahlada in the Burning Fire — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 7, Chapter 5

Long ago there was a tiny prince named Prahlada. He was only five years old. His eyes were full of love for Krishna.

But Prahlada's father was an angry king named Hiranyakashipu. He did not love Krishna.

'Stop chanting Krishna's name!' the king shouted.

Prahlada looked up with gentle eyes. His hands were calm. His voice was soft. 'Father,' he said, 'Krishna lives in my heart. I cannot stop loving Him.'

The king grew more and more angry. His face turned red. The whole palace trembled. But Prahlada sat still. Inside him was a quiet place, like a little lake with no waves.

The king called his sister Holika. Holika had a special cloak that could not burn. 'Sit in the fire with Prahlada on your lap!' the king said. 'The fire will eat the boy. You will be safe.'

A huge fire was lit. Orange flames leapt up high. Holika laughed. She took Prahlada on her lap and sat down in the middle of the fire.

Prahlada did not shout. He did not cry. He closed his eyes and whispered softly, 'Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna.' His heart was so full of shanti that even the roaring fire could not disturb it.

Then — something wonderful happened. The special cloak flew away from Holika and wrapped itself around little Prahlada. The fire could not touch him at all. Prahlada walked out of the flames smiling, his chant still soft on his lips.

The whole kingdom gasped. Shanti is stronger than fire, stronger than anger, stronger than any loud thing. When Krishna is in our heart, nothing can take our peace away.

A Story from Today

Ravi and the Loudest Wedding

Ravi was seven years old. His cousin Anjali was getting married. The whole family had come to the big tent in the garden.

There were drums — DHOOM! DHOOM! A shehnai blew loud. Uncles shouted with joy. Aunties laughed. Little cousins ran everywhere.

Ravi loved weddings. But today his ears hurt. His head felt too full. Someone bumped his elbow and his plate fell — CLANG! Everyone laughed. Ravi's eyes filled with tears.

He ran out of the tent. Behind the house was a quiet old mango tree. Ravi sat down under it. His hands were shaking.

Nani came out slowly. She was small and soft and smelled like jasmine. She sat next to Ravi and held his hand.

'Too loud, Nani,' Ravi whispered. 'My head is full.'

Nani smiled gently. 'The noise is outside, beta. But inside you, there is a quiet room. Krishna stays there. Shall I show you?'

Ravi nodded.

'Close your eyes,' Nani said softly. 'Put one hand on your heart. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly. Whisper with me — Krishna, Krishna, Krishna.'

Ravi closed his eyes. The drums still played outside. But with each breath, a cool stillness came inside him. Like a cold glass of water on a hot day.

After some minutes, Ravi opened his eyes. He was smiling. The wedding was still loud. His heart was calm.

'It worked, Nani!' he whispered. 'The noise is outside. I am inside.'

Nani patted his head. 'Calm hands. Calm voice. Calm heart. That is shanti. Now let us go back and dance.'

Core Teaching

  1. Shanti means a quiet heart. Even when the world is loud, we can find a small still place inside us. We get there by breathing slowly and whispering Krishna's name.
  2. When we feel upset, we can practice three things together — calm hands, calm voice, calm heart. First the body settles. Then the voice softens. Then the heart becomes still.
  3. Shanti is not hiding or running away. It is standing steady inside while the world moves around us. Krishna lives in that still place — and we can visit Him anytime.
When shanti grows inside us, we can listen carefully to what is really true around us — and truth-listening is the beginning of the next value, satya.
