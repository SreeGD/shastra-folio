Teacher Guide

Middle 9–14 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Dhriti is the inner strength that helps you keep going when things get difficult. It means staying steady with your good choices and not giving up on what is right, even when you face problems or feel like quitting.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.3 — Power, forgiveness, determination, cleanliness, freedom from envy and pride — these are the divine qualities, O son of Bharata, of those born with a divine nature.

Core Teaching

  1. True strength comes from continuing good actions even when you feel like stopping, not from never feeling tired or discouraged.
  2. Dhriti means staying steady with what is right, but it also means being wise enough to change your methods when they are not working.
  3. When you practice dhriti, you build inner confidence that comes from knowing you can trust yourself to keep going when things get difficult.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin by asking students: 'Who here has ever wanted to quit something but kept going anyway?' Let 2-3 students share briefly. Then say: 'Today we are learning about dhriti (DHRI-ti), which is the inner strength that helps us keep doing what is right even when it is hard. It is not about never feeling tired or frustrated - it is about continuing anyway. The verse we will study lists dhriti as one of the divine qualities we can develop. Let's see how Arjuna and a modern student named Priya both showed this quality.'

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min: Opening discussion and verse introduction. 10 min: Read and discuss Arjuna story. 10 min: Read and discuss Priya story. 10 min: Core teaching and anti-patterns discussion. 5 min: Assign worksheet and home practice.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

15 min: Opening circle and verse study with chanting. 25 min: Arjuna story with detailed discussion and role-play. 25 min: Priya story with student sharing of similar experiences. 20 min: Core teaching discussion with examples from student lives. 15 min: Begin worksheet in pairs. 20 min: Sanskrit word study and pronunciation practice.

Discussion Guide

Question 1: What is the difference between dhriti and stubbornness? Sample response: Dhriti listens to good advice and changes methods when needed, but stubbornness refuses to listen or change even when clearly wrong. Facilitate by asking: 'Can you think of examples from your own life?' Question 2: Why do you think Arjuna practiced alone in the early morning instead of just during regular class time? Sample response: Because it was harder to practice alone when tired and cold, it built stronger character and skills. The difficulty itself made him stronger. Facilitate by connecting to their own experiences of doing hard things. Question 3: How did both characters deal with the feeling of wanting to quit? Sample response: They acknowledged the feeling but chose to act differently. They did not pretend they felt confident - they acted with dhriti despite their doubts. Use this to reinforce that dhriti includes feeling the difficulty.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Students think dhriti means you never feel like giving up or never get tired. Response: Explain that dhriti is not about not having difficult feelings - it is about continuing good actions even when you have those feelings. Both Arjuna and Priya felt frustrated but kept going.
  • Misconception: Students confuse dhriti with stubbornness, thinking you should never change your approach. Response: Help them see that dhriti includes wisdom - you stay steady with your good goals but you can change your methods when they are not working. Priya changed her volcano design four times.
  • Misconception: Students think dhriti means doing everything alone without help. Response: Point out that Priya asked her brother for help and watched videos to learn. Dhriti includes being humble enough to learn from others.
