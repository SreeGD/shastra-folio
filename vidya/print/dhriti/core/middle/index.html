Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.3

तेजः क्षमा धृतिः शौचमद्रोहो नातिमानिता । भवन्ति सम्पदं दैवीमभिजातस्य भारत ॥

tejaḥ kṣamā dhṛtiḥ śaucam adroho nāti-mānitā bhavanti sampadaṁ daivīm abhijātasya bhārata

Word-for-word: tejaḥ — power/radiance; kṣamā — forgiveness; dhṛtiḥ — determination/steadfastness; śaucam — cleanliness; adrohaḥ — freedom from envy; na — not; ati-mānitā — expectation of honour; bhavanti — are; sampadam — qualities; daivīm — divine; abhijātasya — of one born with; bhārata — O son of Bharata

Translation: Power, forgiveness, determination, cleanliness, freedom from envy and pride — these are the divine qualities, O son of Bharata, of those born with a divine nature.

What is Dhriti?

Dhriti is the inner strength that helps you keep going when things get difficult. It means staying steady with your good choices and not giving up on what is right, even when you face problems or feel like quitting.

What it is NOT

Dhriti is not stubbornness, where you refuse to listen to good advice or change when you are clearly wrong. It is also not pretending you do not feel scared or sad when you actually do.

  • Stubbornness - refusing to change a failing study method
  • Emotional suppression - pretending you are not worried about exams
  • Reckless endurance - continuing harmful activities without thinking

Sanskrit Word Study

dhṛti (धृति)

Pronunciation: DHRI-ti

Meaning: Determination, steadfastness, inner strength that sustains right action

Example: When Arjuna showed dhṛti by practicing every morning, he developed the skill and character of a true warrior.

Story from the Scriptures

Arjuna's Dawn Practice — Mahabharata, Adi Parva

In the royal school of Dronacharya, all the princes learned archery together. Arjuna was not the strongest or the tallest among them. His cousin Duryodhana was bigger, and his brother Bhima was more powerful. But Arjuna had something different in his heart.

Every morning, while the other students slept in their warm beds, Arjuna would wake up before the sun rose. The air was cold and the ground was wet with dew. His body felt heavy with sleep, and his mind whispered, 'Just sleep a little more. You can practice later.' But Arjuna would get up anyway.

He would take his bow and arrows to the practice field. In the dim light, he could barely see the targets. His fingers were stiff from the cold, and his first few arrows often missed. Some mornings, he felt frustrated and wanted to go back to bed. But he stayed.

Day after day, week after week, Arjuna continued this practice. Because he practiced when it was hard, his skills grew stronger than anyone else's. His aim became perfect even in poor light. His hands learned to work even when they were cold.

One day, Dronacharya decided to test all his students. He hung a wooden bird from a tree and asked each prince to aim at its eye. One by one, the other students tried and missed. When Arjuna's turn came, his arrow flew straight and true, hitting exactly where it should.

Dropacharya smiled and declared Arjuna the finest archer among all his students. But Arjuna knew the real reason for his success. It was not just talent - it was the choice he made every morning to get up and practice, even when everything in him wanted to stay comfortable.

A Story from Today

Priya's Science Project

Priya had chosen the most ambitious project for the school science fair - a working volcano model that would demonstrate how eruptions happen. She had big dreams of amazing her classmates and teachers.

But when she first tested her volcano, everything went wrong. Instead of a controlled eruption, the baking soda and vinegar mixture overflowed messily all over her desk. The papier-mâché mountain collapsed, and red food coloring stained her clothes and the floor.

Some of her classmates saw the disaster and started laughing. 'Priya's volcano exploded!' they said. Her face burned with embarrassment. That night, she wanted to tell her teacher she was switching to something simple, like a poster about the solar system.

But something inside her said, 'No, try again.' So Priya spent her weekend researching what went wrong. She discovered she had used too much vinegar and made the mountain walls too thin. Because she was willing to learn from her mistakes, she started over.

The second attempt was better but still not right. The eruption was too weak this time. Some classmates asked, 'Are you still working on that volcano? Why don't you just give up?' But Priya kept going.

She asked her older brother for help with the measurements. She watched online videos to understand the chemical reaction better. Because she was not too proud to ask for help, she learned new techniques.

On her fourth try, everything worked perfectly. The volcano erupted with just the right force, the lava flowed realistically, and the mountain stayed intact. When she presented it at the science fair, her classmates were genuinely impressed.

But the best part was not the praise. It was the feeling inside Priya that she had not given up on herself. She had learned that failing does not mean you are a failure - it means you are learning.

Core Teaching

  1. True strength comes from continuing good actions even when you feel like stopping, not from never feeling tired or discouraged.
  2. Dhriti means staying steady with what is right, but it also means being wise enough to change your methods when they are not working.
  3. When you practice dhriti, you build inner confidence that comes from knowing you can trust yourself to keep going when things get difficult.
