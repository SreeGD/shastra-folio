Dhriti Worksheet — The Quiet Hand That Keeps Holding

Activity 1: Keep Going or Give Up? (Circle the Right Answer)

1. My colouring is only half done and my hand is tired. KEEP GOING (five more minutes) GIVE UP

2. I promised to water the tulsi every day. Today I am sleepy. KEEP GOING (a small drink) GIVE UP

3. My friend wanted to teach me a game. I lost the first try. I am sad. KEEP GOING (one more try) GIVE UP

4. I am learning a new song. Today I forgot a line. KEEP GOING (sing from the start) GIVE UP

5. Amma asked me to read ten pages. I have read only four and I am bored. KEEP GOING (six more, slowly) GIVE UP

Activity 2: Draw Bharata's Sandals

In the box below, draw a golden throne. On the throne, draw TWO little wooden sandals (padukas). Below the throne, draw Bharata sitting on the floor in simple cloth, quietly bowing. Under your picture, write in big careful letters:

Dhriti = The Quiet Hand That Keeps Holding.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: The Quiet-Hand Chant

Sit cross-legged, both palms facing up on your knees. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.

Chant three times, very softly — one breath per chant:

Dhṛ-tih! Dhṛ-tih! Dhṛ-tih! *Quiet hand. Keep holding. Krishna is here.*

Activity 4: The Thirty-Tick Card (Paired — Teacher Hands Out)

With your partner, each of you will choose ONE small daily practice for the next thirty mornings. Good choices: water the tulsi, make my bed, one page of drawing, ten minutes of reading, learning one bhajan line, one pose of Surya Namaskar.

Draw thirty little squares on your card. Each morning you do the practice — however small — you tick ONE square. Even on tired days, do five minutes. Even five minutes = one tick. No skipping. No lies.

Compare cards with your partner every Friday. Cheer each other with: 'The quiet hand is holding!'

Activity 5: Take Home — The Sandal Diary

Like Bharata who kept Rama's sandals on the throne, put ONE little thing on your home altar (or quiet shelf) that belongs to something you have promised to keep doing. Maybe a small plant you will water. Maybe a flashcard with one Sanskrit word you will learn. Maybe the green thirty-tick card.

Every morning, bow to it and whisper: 'Krishna, my quiet hand will hold today.'

Every night, tell Amma or Appa: *'Today my dhriti held / wobbled / fell — and tomorrow I will hold again.'*

Home Practice

For thirty mornings, pick ONE small kind practice and keep it. Good choices: water the tulsi, make your own bed, draw one page, read for ten minutes, sing one bhajan line, do one Surya Namaskar, tidy one shelf. Use the thirty-tick card from class. On tired days do only five minutes — still tick. Before bed, tell Amma or Appa: 'Today my dhriti held / wobbled / fell — and tomorrow it will hold again.' Place one small object on your home altar (the card, a flower, a flashcard) and bow each morning, whispering: 'Krishna, my quiet hand will hold today.'

Duration: Every day for thirty days
Look for: By the end of thirty days, the child has ticked at least 25 squares on the card (with honesty about wobbles), can describe at least 3 tired days when they held on with five small minutes, and can recite the Dhriti chant with open palms. Parents notice the child returning to unfinished work without being pushed.
