Bhakti

भक्ति · BHAK-tee

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Bhakti means loving God with your whole heart. It means caring for Krishna like you care for your best friend. When you have bhakti, you want to make Krishna happy.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 9.34 — Think of Me always. Love Me. Pray to Me. Bow to Me. If you do this, you will come to Me.

Core Teaching

  1. Love means giving, not just taking. When we share with others, our hearts feel happy.
  2. God loves us when we love others. Being kind to people and animals makes God smile.
  3. Small gifts given with love are better than big gifts given without caring.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin by asking children: 'Who do you love most in your family?' Let them share. Then say: 'Today we'll learn about a special kind of love called bhakti. It means loving God and showing that love by being kind to everyone around us. Just like you love your family, we can love Krishna by caring for others.' Show them the verse picture and explain: 'Krishna says if we think of Him and love Him, He will always be with us.'

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

Opening circle (5 min) → Story 1: Sudama (8 min) → Story 2: Maya (7 min) → Discussion (10 min) → Craft activity (10 min)

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

Opening circle (10 min) → Story 1 with acting (20 min) → Story 2 with discussion (20 min) → Craft activity (30 min) → Sharing circle game (15 min) → Nature walk for gifts (20 min) → Closing circle (5 min)

Discussion Guide

1. Question: 'How did Sudama show love to Krishna?' Sample response: 'He brought rice flakes and visited his friend even though he was nervous.' Guide children to see that love means making an effort to care. 2. Question: 'How did Maya feel after giving her flowers away?' Sample response: 'She felt happy and warm inside.' Help children understand that giving with love makes us feel good. 3. Question: 'What are some ways we can show bhakti every day?' Sample response: 'Being nice to our pets, helping mom, sharing our snacks.' Encourage practical, daily actions.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Children think bhakti means only praying in a temple. Response: Explain that bhakti means showing love through actions - being kind to animals, sharing with friends, helping at home. These are all ways to love Krishna.
  • Misconception: Children think they need expensive things to show bhakti. Response: Use Sudama's story to show that Krishna loved the simple rice flakes because they were given with love. A drawing, a flower, or a kind word are perfect gifts.
