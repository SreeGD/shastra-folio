Worksheet

Bhakti (Devotion) - Worksheet

Section 1: Story Analysis

Compare Prahlada's unwavering devotion with Arjun's musical journey:

1. What motivated each character's initial actions, and how did their motivation deepen or clarify under pressure?

2. How did both characters demonstrate that bhakti is about the heart's intention rather than external circumstances?

3. Prahlada faced an ethical conflict between duty to his father and duty to truth. How did his ananya-bhakti resolve that tension, and what does this teach us about the foundation of devotional life?

Section 2: Devotional Design Challenge

Team Activity (20 minutes)

Work in groups of 3-4 to design a "Bhakti Transformation Board" - a visual map showing how ordinary daily activities can become devotional service.

Materials: Large poster paper, colored markers, sticky notes, magazines for cutting

Instructions:

1. Draw a large tree trunk in the center labeled "Pure Intention"

2. Create branches for different life areas: Study, Relationships, Work, Recreation, Challenges

3. On each branch, attach leaves (sticky notes) showing specific ways to transform activities in that area into devotional service

4. Add flowers representing the fruits of such transformed activities

5. Decorate with symbols from various spiritual traditions showing universal nature of devotion

6. Present your board to the class, explaining one transformation from each branch

Reflection while creating: "As you add each leaf, consider: How would this activity feel different if done as an offering rather than for personal gain?"

Section 3: The Bhakti Debate Game

Whole Class Activity (15 minutes)

Setup: Class divides into two teams. Teacher presents scenarios where someone must choose between ego-driven action and devotional service.

Rules:

  • Teams alternate presenting arguments for the devotional choice
  • Each argument must reference either the verse, stories, or core principles
  • Points awarded for philosophical depth and practical wisdom
  • No team "wins" - the goal is collaborative exploration

Sample Scenarios:

  • A student can either study to get the highest grade or study to serve others with knowledge
  • An athlete can train for personal glory or to inspire others through disciplined example
  • A leader can make decisions to look good or to genuinely serve their community

Debrief Questions:

  • When did you feel most convinced by a devotional argument?
  • What makes the difference between selfish and selfless motivation?
  • How can we check our own intentions in daily life?

Section 4: Philosophical Reflection Essay

Individual Writing (30 minutes)

Choose one of these prompts and write a thoughtful response (400-500 words):

A) "Bhakti is the topmost yoga system" - Analyze this claim by comparing devotional service with other spiritual paths you know about. What makes bhakti unique?

B) Prahlada's bhakti remained steady through serpents, fire, and mountains. Explore what it means for devotion to be "independent of circumstance," and where you see (or fail to see) such constancy in your own inner life.

C) Arjun discovered that offering his talents as service brought more fulfillment than seeking recognition. Reflect on a skill or talent you have - how might approaching it as devotional service change your experience?

Writing Guidelines:

  • Include specific examples from the stories or your own observations
  • Address potential counterarguments to your position
  • Connect philosophical insights to practical applications
  • Conclude with a personal commitment or question for further exploration

Section 5: Real-World Application Challenge

This Week's Practice: Choose one daily activity (eating, studying, exercising, helping at home) and consciously transform it into devotional service.

Specific Instructions:

  • Before beginning the activity, pause and mentally offer it as service to the Supreme
  • Perform the activity with full attention and care, as if it were sacred
  • Notice any changes in your experience, motivation, or results
  • Keep a brief daily log of insights

Observable Results to Watch For:

  • Increased sense of purpose and meaning in the activity
  • Less anxiety about outcomes and results
  • Greater patience and care in execution
  • Natural desire to serve others through this activity

Reflection Questions for Week's End:

  • How did offering this activity as service change your relationship to it?
  • What obstacles did you encounter in maintaining devotional consciousness?
  • What insights emerged about the difference between selfish and selfless action?

Home Practice

Each morning before breakfast, spend 5 minutes offering your day's activities to Krishna. Mentally go through your planned activities (school, meals, interactions, study) and consciously dedicate each one as devotional service. End by expressing gratitude for the opportunity to serve.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice increased mindfulness and purpose in daily activities, less anxiety about results, and a growing sense that ordinary moments can be sacred.
