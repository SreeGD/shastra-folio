Chapter Overview

Self-control means being the boss of your own body and mind. It means you can choose to do good things even when you want to do something else.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 6.5 — We must help ourselves with our mind. We must not let our mind make us do bad things. Our mind can be our best friend or our worst enemy.

Core Teaching

  1. Your mind can be your friend when you control it, or your enemy when you don't.
  2. Self-control means making good choices even when you want to do something else.
  3. When you control yourself, you feel happy and strong inside.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin by having children sit quietly and notice their breathing for 30 seconds. Ask: 'Who was the boss of your body just now? You were! That's called self-control - being the boss of yourself.' Show them how they can choose to sit still, speak softly, or be kind even when they feel different inside. Explain that today's stories will show them how other children learned to be the boss of their feelings and actions.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

Opening circle (5 min) → Epic story with actions (10 min) → Modern story discussion (8 min) → Shield craft activity (15 min) → Closing song about being strong (2 min)

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

Opening circle (10 min) → Epic story with role-play (20 min) → Modern story and discussion (15 min) → Shield craft (30 min) → Stop and Breathe game (15 min) → Tree coloring (20 min) → Share shields in circle (8 min) → Closing song and quiet time (2 min)

Discussion Guide

1. Question: What helped Prahlada stay calm when the demons tried to scare him? Sample response: He sang Vishnu's name in his heart and remembered that Vishnu loved him. 2. Question: How did Maya change her grumpy mornings? Sample response: She learned to count to ten and breathe deeply instead of hiding under blankets. 3. Question: When is it hard for you to control yourself? Sample response: When I'm tired, when someone takes my toy, when I have to wait for something I really want. Use these responses to practice the 'count to ten and breathe' technique together.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: Self-control means never feeling angry or sad. Response: Explain that all feelings are okay to have - self-control means choosing what to do with those feelings.
  • Misconception: Self-control means being perfect all the time. Response: Emphasize that self-control is something we practice every day, and it's okay to make mistakes while learning.
