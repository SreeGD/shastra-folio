Value at a Glance

Seva means loving service. It is giving my TIME and my HANDS to help someone — my grandmother, my teacher, a hungry puppy, a tired tree. When I sweep the altar for Krishna, water the tulasi, wash my dadi's feet, or carry the heavy bag for my mother, I am doing seva. Seva is a small action done with a big love. The hands do the work. The heart does the seva.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 12.10: Krishna says: If you cannot sit still and meditate, do not worry. Just do MY work. Help others for My sake. Sweep the floor for Me. Feed the puppy for Me. Even a small, simple work done for Me will make your life complete.

What it is NOT

Seva is not helping only to be seen. It is not helping when someone is watching and stopping when no one is. It is not rushing through a chore to finish fast and get praise. It is not helping for a chocolate or a sticker. Seva is quiet, careful, and full of love. If the heart is grumpy, the hands are not really doing seva — they are just moving.

  • Helping Amma only when she promises a treat
  • Cleaning the classroom loudly so the teacher notices ME
  • Sweeping the altar in a hurry while thinking about recess

Stories Your Child Heard

Shabari's Berries for Sri Rama — Deep in the Dandaka forest lived an old tribal woman named Shabari. Her hair was white. Her back was bent. But her eyes still shone like two little lamps. When Shabari was a young girl, a holy sage named Matanga told her, 'One day, Lord Rama Himself will walk through this forest. He will come to your small hut.'

Ananya and Dadi's Morning Walk — Ananya was seven years old. She lived with her Amma, her Appa, and her Dadi. Dadi was her grandmother. Dadi's knees hurt. She could not walk fast any more. Each morning, Dadi wanted to visit the tulasi plant in the courtyard. She wanted to water it, ring the little bell, and say a small prayer. But the three steps down to the courtyard were hard.

The Sanskrit Word

sevā (सेवा) — say-VAH

Meaning: Loving service. Giving my time and my hands to help someone — for Krishna. Small work, big love. The quiet helper's path.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Secret Seva Notebook. Every day, find ONE tiny seva — water tulasi, help Dadi or Thatha, carry Amma's bag, feed the street puppy, sweep the altar, help a younger sibling. Do it QUIETLY. Do not announce it. Draw or write it in your Secret Seva Book each night. At bedtime, press your palms in Namaste and whisper: 'Krishna, these are Your hands today.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific seva moments done without being asked or rewarded, and can demonstrate the Namaste gesture while chanting 'seva' with a quiet smile. The Secret Seva Notebook has at least 5 entries.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific seva moments done without being asked or rewarded, and can demonstrate the Namaste gesture while chanting 'seva' with a quiet smile. The Secret Seva Notebook has at least 5 entries.

When my hands serve quietly for Krishna, my heart learns to stay close to Him in all that I do — and that loving closeness has its own beautiful name, bhakti.
