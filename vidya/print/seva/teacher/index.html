Seva

सेवा · say-VAH

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Seva means loving service. It is giving my TIME and my HANDS to help someone — my grandmother, my teacher, a hungry puppy, a tired tree. When I sweep the altar for Krishna, water the tulasi, wash my dadi's feet, or carry the heavy bag for my mother, I am doing seva. Seva is a small action done with a big love. The hands do the work. The heart does the seva.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 12.10 — Krishna says: If you cannot sit still and meditate, do not worry. Just do MY work. Help others for My sake. Sweep the floor for Me. Feed the puppy for Me. Even a small, simple work done for Me will make your life complete.

Core Teaching

  1. Seva means loving service. It is giving my hands and my TIME to help someone — a grandparent, a teacher, a pet, a plant, the altar, a classmate. The work may be small, but the love is big. That love is the seva.
  2. Seva is different from dana. Dana means giving a THING — a sweet, a toy, a coin. Seva means giving MYSELF — my time, my hands, my care. Dana empties my bag. Seva fills my heart.
  3. The best seva is quiet. No clapping, no stickers, no telling everyone. Just the broom sweeping, the water being poured, the hand being held. Krishna sees it. That is enough. When seva is secret and sweet, our heart becomes a small temple.

Five-Minute Introduction

Sit in a circle. Show the children both your hands. Turn them palm-up. Ask: 'What can my hands do?' Let them call out — write, eat, clap, hug, help. Pause. 'Today we learn a beautiful word. It is SEVA. सेवा. It means using my hands to help someone — with love. Not for a sticker. Not for a chocolate. Just with love.' Chant together, pressing palms into Namaste: 'Se-VAH! Se-VAH! Se-VAH!' Then ask: 'Who can think of ONE tiny seva you did this week — that no one saw?' Celebrate every answer with a warm smile and a quiet nod (not loud applause — seva's praise is quiet).

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Hands-up Namaste intro + chant 'seva' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud Shabari's story; let children mime the 'swish-swish' sweeping with one hand. 8 min — Read aloud Ananya and Dadi. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Seva or Not Seva) and Activity 2 (Trace My Seva Hands). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce Secret Seva Notebook. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with palms-Namaste gesture and chant. 15 min — Shabari's story with a twig broom and a small cup of berries (raisins or small dried fruit) as props; each child takes a turn to 'sweep' the altar and place one fruit in a leaf-cup offering. 15 min — Ananya and Dadi story, re-enacted with one child as Dadi (walking slowly with a 'stick') and another as Ananya (helping with a small watering can); rotate. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Tulasi Watering Practice (Activity 4) with the classroom tulasi or any plant; children take real turns with a tiny cup. 15 min — Dr. Kotnis biographical story with world map — show China on the map, trace Dr. Kotnis's journey, let the children see that seva crosses borders. 15 min — Make the Secret Seva Notebook: each child gets 4 sheets folded and stapled into a little booklet with 'MY SECRET SEVA' on the cover, decorated. 15 min — Closing: each child whispers (not shouts) ONE tiny seva they will try this week; teacher presses palms in Namaste back. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn seva through the BODY — the hand that holds, the broom that sweeps, the cup that waters. Keep it physical. Use the palms-together Namaste gesture often. Distinguish seva from dana carefully at this age — dana = thing given; seva = time and hands given. Both meet in love, but they are not the same. Children at six to nine are naturally helpful if the helping is framed as a chance to be BIG, not as a chore. Frame every seva invitation as 'would you like to be Krishna's hands for a minute?' Watch for the praise-hungry child who helps only when watched — gently invite them to try ONE secret seva this week. Praise quietly. 'I noticed your hand went to Dadi without being asked. Krishna saw.' Avoid loud group applause for seva acts — it turns seva into performance. Model seva yourself — sweep the altar, water the classroom plant, wipe a smudge, and let children notice. Never force seva as punishment ('since you misbehaved, sweep the floor') — this confuses the heart. Seva should always feel like a gift from the child to Krishna, never a penalty.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Seva and dana are the same thing — both are helping.' Response: They are cousins, but not twins. Dana means giving a THING — a sweet, a coin, a toy. Seva means giving MY HANDS and MY TIME. When I give my crayon to a friend — that is dana. When I sit and help the friend colour with that crayon — that is seva. Dana empties my bag; seva fills my heart.
  • Misconception: 'Seva is only done in a temple.' Response: Oh no! The temple is ONE place for seva. But Krishna lives everywhere — in Dadi, in the tulasi plant, in the hungry puppy, in the tired teacher. Every hand that helps ANY living being, with love, is doing seva. Your home is a temple. Your school is a temple. The street where the puppy sleeps is a temple.
  • Misconception: 'If my seva is not seen, it does not count.' Response: The OPPOSITE is true. The seva that no one sees is the SWEETEST. When we help for applause, the seva is small. When we help quietly — just because — the seva is very big. Krishna sees. Krishna's seeing is bigger than all the stickers in the world.
