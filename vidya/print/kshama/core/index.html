Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.3

तेजः क्षमा धृतिः शौचम् अद्रोहो नातिमानिता।
भवन्ति सम्पदं दैवीम् अभिजातस्य भारत॥

tejaḥ kṣamā dhṛtiḥ śaucam adroho nāti-mānitā bhavanti sampadaṁ daivīm abhijātasya bhārata

Word-for-word: tejaḥ — spiritual shine; kṣamā — forgiveness, letting go of hurt; dhṛtiḥ — steady holding on; śaucam — clean heart; adrohaḥ — no wish to hurt back; na-ati-mānitā — no big pride; bhavanti — these shine in; sampadam daivīm — the divine treasure-chest; abhijātasya — in the one born for goodness; bhārata — O Arjuna.

Translation: Krishna tells Arjuna: shining courage, forgiveness that lets go of hurt, steady holding on, a clean heart, no wish to hurt back, and no puffed-up pride — these beautiful qualities shine in the child who is born for goodness.

naivodvije para duratyaya-vaitaraṇyās tvad-vīrya-gāyana-mahāmṛta-magna-cittaḥ śoce tato vimukha-cetasa indriyārtha- māyā-sukhāya bharam udvahato vimūḍhān

Translation: O Lord, I am not afraid of the river of suffering, for my mind is deeply dipped in the sweet nectar of Your glories. I only feel sorrow for the foolish ones who still carry heavy stones of hurt and anger for the sake of tiny passing pleasures. (Prahlada's prayer — offered after he forgave his father.)

What is Kshama?

Kshama means forgiveness. When someone hurts me — with words, with hands, with a broken promise — I do not hold the hurt tight inside my heart like a burning stone. I let it go. Forgiving does not mean saying 'it was okay' or that they can do it again. It means freeing MY OWN heart from the sting. The sting is not the gift Krishna wants me to keep. A clean, soft heart is.

What it is NOT

Kshama is not pretending nothing happened. It is not saying 'it was fine' when it was not fine. It is not letting someone hurt you again and again. It is not weakness. Kshama is the STRONGEST thing a small heart can do — put down the hot stone before it burns the hand holding it.

  • Holding on to a mean word for days, growing grumpier and grumpier
  • Hitting back the person who hit you — 'now you know how it feels'
  • Secretly planning to make the person who hurt you feel bad tomorrow

Sanskrit Word Study

kṣamā (क्षमा)

Pronunciation: ksha-MAA

Meaning: Forgiveness — letting go of the hurt that burns inside my own heart when someone wrongs me. Not 'it was okay'; rather, 'my heart will not stay on fire.'

Example: When little Prahlada asked Lord Narasimha to forgive his own father, that was kshama — the strongest small heart in the whole world.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Prahlada Forgives His Father — Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5 (simplified for primary children)

Long ago there was a tiny boy named Prahlada. He was only five years old. But his heart was as big as the sky, because it was full of love for Krishna.

Prahlada's father was the strongest king in the world. His name was Hiranyakashipu. But his heart was dark. He hated Krishna, and he hated anyone who loved Krishna.

One day the king found out that his own little son was chanting, 'Narayana, Narayana, Narayana!' all day long. He became VERY angry.

'Stop saying that name!' he shouted.

But Prahlada smiled softly. 'Father, Krishna is the Lord of all. I cannot stop loving Him.'

The king did many cruel things to his tiny son. He threw him into a deep river with a big stone tied to him. But Krishna held Prahlada safe. He made his own soldiers point their sharp spears at Prahlada. But the spears felt like soft flower petals. He even sat Prahlada on a burning fire. But the fire felt like cool moonlight.

Each time, Prahlada did not cry. He did not shout back at his father. He kept chanting, 'Narayana, Narayana, Narayana.'

Inside his tiny chest was something beautiful. It was kshama — forgiveness. He did not hold the hurt tight.

At last Krishna Himself came in His half-lion form, Lord Narasimha. Roaring like thunder, He saved Prahlada. The bad deeds of the father ended.

Then Lord Narasimha, still as huge as a mountain, looked gently at little Prahlada. 'Dear child,' He rumbled, 'ask for anything. A kingdom. A treasure. Anything at all.'

The whole world waited.

Prahlada folded his small hands. 'My Lord,' he whispered, 'please forgive my father. He hurt me, but I have already let it go. Please let his heart go too.'

Krishna's mighty eyes filled with love. He blessed the father. He blessed the boy.

And every child since then has known: when someone hurts you, the biggest thing you can do is let it go. That is kshama.

A Story from Today

Anya and the Broken Elephant

Anya was seven. She had one most-special thing in the whole world. It was a tiny wooden elephant her Thatha had carved for her. The elephant had soft-painted eyes and a curly trunk. Anya called him Chotu.

One Sunday her best friend Kavya came over to play. They built a block-castle for Chotu. Kavya picked up Chotu to put him on the castle — and FIZZ-OOPS! — Chotu slipped from her fingers. He fell on the stone floor. When Anya picked him up, his tiny trunk had snapped off.

Anya's chest went HOT. Her face went HOT. A burning stone-feeling filled her heart.

'YOU BROKE HIM!' Anya shouted. 'Chotu was my Thatha's! I will NEVER play with you again!'

Kavya's eyes filled with tears. 'I am so sorry, Anya. It was an accident. Please—'

'GO HOME!' said Anya.

Kavya walked home crying.

All evening Anya sat on her bed. She would not eat. She held the broken trunk in one hand and Chotu in the other. But the burning stone in her chest only got bigger. She thought it would make her feel stronger. It only made her tired.

Amma came and sat beside her. Amma did not say 'Stop it.' Amma just held her hand. After a long quiet, Amma whispered, 'Beta, show me the hurt.'

Anya opened her fist and showed the broken trunk.

'That is the small hurt,' said Amma gently. 'Now show me the big hurt.'

Anya blinked. 'Where?'

Amma placed her palm over Anya's chest. 'Here, kanna. It feels like a hot stone. You are holding it tight because you think it will punish Kavya. But whose hand is burning right now?'

Anya looked down. Her own little palm was red from squeezing.

'Our ancestors have a beautiful word,' said Amma softly. 'Kshama. It means — when someone hurts us, even by mistake, we do not hold the hurt tight. We let it go. Not because the hurt was okay. But because WE need our heart free.'

Amma kissed her forehead. 'Can Chotu be glued tomorrow? Yes. Can your friendship be glued back? Only kshama can do that.'

That night Anya dreamed of Prahlada saying 'Please forgive my father.' She smiled in her sleep.

The next morning, Anya walked to Kavya's house with Chotu in her pocket. Her heart was light — the hot stone was gone.

'Kavya,' Anya said at the door, 'I am sorry I shouted. Chotu can be fixed. I forgive you.'

Kavya's face opened like a flower. The two friends hugged. That evening, Thatha glued Chotu's trunk back with gentle hands. 'Now he has a story,' Thatha smiled. 'And so do you.'

Anya touched her chest. It felt soft. It felt clean. That was kshama.

Core Teaching

  1. Kshama means forgiveness — letting go of the hurt that lives inside my own heart. When someone hurts me, I do not have to keep the hot stone. I can put it down. My heart is happier without it.
  2. Forgiving is NOT saying 'it was okay.' It is not letting someone hurt me again and again. Forgiving just means my own heart is free. The hurt stays outside; Krishna stays inside.
  3. Kshama is strong, not weak. Little Prahlada forgave a father who had hurt him again and again. That tiny boy had a bigger heart than a giant king. When we forgive, we become just a tiny bit like Prahlada — and Krishna smiles.
When our heart learns to let go of hurt — again and again — it becomes clean and quiet inside. And a clean, quiet heart hears Krishna's gentle voice more easily. That listening heart has its own beautiful name: shanti — the peace we find after kshama has done its work.
