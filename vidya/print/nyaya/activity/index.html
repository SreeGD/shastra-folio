Nyaya Worksheet — Fair Hands, Fair Heart

Activity 1: Fair or Not Fair? (Circle the Scale)

Your teacher will read each scene. If the child is being FAIR (nyaya), circle the balanced scale ⚖. If they are being UNFAIR, circle the tilted scale 📉.

1. Rohan gives the biggest mango piece to his little brother who is hungriest. ⚖ 📉

2. Tina's two friends had a fight. She decides her best friend is right without asking the other girl. ⚖ 📉

3. At snack time, Ravi shares one biscuit with each child — even the new boy. ⚖ 📉

4. Priya's brother accidentally spilled water. Priya shouts before asking what happened. ⚖ 📉

5. On the playground, a big boy takes little Anu's ball. Sara walks up and says, 'That is Anu's. Please give it back.' ⚖ 📉

Activity 2: My Fair Plate

Draw a round plate. Inside the plate, draw THREE pieces of food you must share between:

  • A baby sister (very small tummy)
  • You (normal tummy)
  • Big brother (very hungry after sports)

Show with your drawing how you would share fairly — NOT the same size for all, but the right size for each. Write under it: 'Fair is not always equal.'

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Stand tall. Hold out your hands like a scale — one palm up on the left, one palm up on the right. Chant three times:

Nyā-ya! Nyā-ya! Nyā-ya!

*Listen to both. See what is needed. Give what is right.*

On each chant, tip your palms like a balance — left a little, right a little, then steady in the middle.

Activity 4: The Two-Side Role-Play (Paired)

With a partner, practise being a fair monitor. One child pretends to be upset ('He took my pencil!'). The other child becomes the Fair Monitor and says:

  • 'Wait. Let me listen. First you tell me. Then I ask him.'
  • (Turn to the other pretend child) 'Now you tell your side.'
  • 'I heard both. Here is what is fair: ____.'

Swap roles. Try a new small problem each time — a dropped lunch, a turn on the swing, a missing eraser.

Activity 5: Take Home — The Fair Helper Week

For one week, be the fair helper at home. Every day:

1. Once a day, share something (a snack, a toy, a turn) — and share by NEED, not equally. The hungrier gets a bit more. The one already satisfied gets a bit less.

2. If there is a small argument at home, do not take sides. Say 'Wait. Let me hear both.'

3. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa one fair thing you did today. Draw a tiny scale ⚖ on your calendar.

At week's end, count your scales together.

Home Practice

For one week, be the Fair Helper at home. Every day do three small things: (1) Share ONE thing — a snack, a toy, a turn — by NEED, not equally. Give the smaller child a softer piece, the hungrier child a bit more. (2) If a small argument starts between you and a sibling or cousin, do not shout first. Say 'Wait — let me tell you my side; then you tell yours.' Use a calm voice. (3) If you notice something unfair at home (someone forgotten, someone given less), speak up gently to Amma or Appa: 'I think this is not fair; can we look?' At bedtime, tell your parent ONE fair thing you did and draw a tiny scale ⚖ on your calendar. At week's end, count your scales together as a family.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By week's end, the child's home calendar has at least 5 scale marks, the child can describe two moments when they listened to both sides before speaking, and parents notice the child saying 'Wait, let me hear' or 'That would be more fair' in an ordinary family moment.
