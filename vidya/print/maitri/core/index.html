Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 12.13

अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च।
निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी॥

adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṁ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī

Word-for-word: adveṣṭā — one who is not envious; sarva-bhūtānām — of all living beings; maitraḥ — friendly; karuṇaḥ — merciful; eva — certainly; ca — and.

Translation: Krishna says: My dear devotee is friendly to every living being. Not to one or two — to ALL. No envy, no pride, only warmth.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 3.25.21

तितिक्षवः कारुणिकाः सुहृदः सर्वदेहिनाम्।
अजातशत्रवः शान्ताः साधवः साधुभूषणाः॥

titikṣavaḥ kāruṇikāḥ suhṛdaḥ sarva-dehinām ajāta-śatravaḥ śāntāḥ sādhavaḥ sādhu-bhūṣaṇāḥ

Translation: The saintly person is patient, merciful, a friend (suhrit) of all living beings. They have no enemies. They are peaceful.

What is Maitri?

Maitri means being a friend to everyone. Not just to my 'best friend' or to the children who look like me. Krishna is happy when I say 'hi' to the new boy, when I include the quiet girl, when I give a smile to the child I don't know yet. Every living being is Krishna's child — so every living being is MY friend too.

What it is NOT

Maitri is not only playing with my chosen group. It is not ignoring the new child or the lonely child. It is not laughing at someone who is different.

  • Playing only with 'best friends' and telling others 'you cannot join'
  • Saying 'ew, go away' to a new child
  • Ignoring the quiet child who sits alone at lunch

Sanskrit Word Study

maitrī (मैत्री)

Pronunciation: MY-tree

Meaning: Friendly goodwill to EVERYONE — not only to a chosen few. Every child is Krishna's child, so every child is my friend.

Example: When I welcomed Zara to my lunch, that was maitri.

Story from the Scriptures

Krishna's Forest Picnic with the Cowherd Boys — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 10, Chapter 13

In the forest of Vrindavan, the cowherd boys were very hungry. They had been running after the calves all morning. They had called the birds. They had climbed the kadamba tree.

'I'm hungry!' said Sridama.

'Me too!' said Subala.

'Let's eat right here!' said Krishna, smiling.

The boys pulled out their little cloth bundles. Each mother had packed something different. Some had roti. Some had butter. Some had fruits. Some had only a little bit.

Then something beautiful happened.

The boys did not sit in rows. They did not sit by 'best friends'. They all sat around Krishna — like the petals of a lotus flower, Krishna in the middle.

Krishna laughed His beautiful laugh. He took a bite of Sridama's roti. He shared His butter with Subala. He broke His sweet in half and gave part to a new boy who had never played with them before.

The boys followed His example. They mixed their food. They ate from each other's bundles. They tickled each other. They wiped butter off each other's faces.

At one point, a shy boy sat a little apart. He had very little food. Krishna saw him. Krishna beckoned him closer. 'Come! Sit here, between Sridama and Me!'

The shy boy came. Krishna shared His own food with him. The shy boy's cheeks grew pink with joy.

Not one boy ate alone. Not one boy felt left out. Every single bundle became everyone's bundle.

This is maitri. The lotus-circle friendship. Where Krishna is in the middle and every child is equally close.

A Story from Today

Rani Welcomes the New Girl

It was Tuesday morning. Teacher Priya-ji walked into class with a new girl. 'Children, this is Zara. Please make her feel welcome.'

Zara was small. Her eyes were big. She wore a soft hijab on her head. She gave a tiny smile, then looked at her shoes.

Most of the children stared. No one said hello.

At lunch break, everyone rushed outside. Rani and her best friends — Meera and Ankita — sat on their favourite bench. Zara stood alone, holding her tiffin.

Meera whispered, 'Don't sit with her, Rani. She's new. She's... different.'

Ankita nodded. 'She doesn't look like us.'

Rani looked at Zara. Zara was now sitting on the ground, alone, trying hard not to cry.

Rani remembered what her Nani had said just last Sunday. 'Beta, maitri means every child is Krishna's child. Every one.'

Rani stood up. 'I'll be back,' she said to Meera and Ankita.

She walked over to Zara and sat right next to her. 'Hi,' Rani said. 'I'm Rani. What did your Ammi pack for you?'

Zara's face lit up slowly. 'Parathas. And... and a boiled egg.'

'I have idlis,' Rani said. 'Let's share.'

Meera and Ankita watched from far. After a few minutes, they came over too.

By the end of lunch, all four girls were laughing. Zara's parathas were shared. The idlis were shared. Even the boiled egg was shared.

On the way home, Rani told Nani. Nani hugged her. 'THAT is maitri, Rani. The lotus-circle where everyone belongs.'

Core Teaching

  1. Maitri means being a friend to EVERY child — not just my group, not just those who look like me. Krishna treats everyone with the same warm smile. So do we.
  2. When someone is new, quiet, or alone, that is maitri's moment. We walk over. We say hi. We share our tiffin. We include them. Their heart lights up — and so does ours.
  3. Maitri is Krishna in the middle and every child in the circle. No 'best' friend who makes others feel 'lesser'. Just a warm circle where everyone belongs.
When our friendship grows so gentle that it becomes soft like a flower touching soft cheeks, that gentleness has its own name — mārdava.
