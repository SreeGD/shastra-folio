मैत्री · MY-tree

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Maitri means being a friend to everyone. Not just to my 'best friend' or to the children who look like me. Krishna is happy when I say 'hi' to the new boy, when I include the quiet girl, when I give a smile to the child I don't know yet. Every living being is Krishna's child — so every living being is MY friend too.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 12.13 — Krishna says: My dear devotee is friendly to every living being. Not to one or two — to ALL. No envy, no pride, only warmth.

Core Teaching

  1. Maitri means being a friend to EVERY child — not just my group, not just those who look like me. Krishna treats everyone with the same warm smile. So do we.
  2. When someone is new, quiet, or alone, that is maitri's moment. We walk over. We say hi. We share our tiffin. We include them. Their heart lights up — and so does ours.
  3. Maitri is Krishna in the middle and every child in the circle. No 'best' friend who makes others feel 'lesser'. Just a warm circle where everyone belongs.

Five-Minute Introduction

Have the children stand in a big circle, holding hands. Say: 'Look around. Who is in this circle?' Let them name each classmate. Then say: 'Today we learn the word maitri. It means FRIEND. But not just for some. For EVERYONE in this circle — and even children who are not here yet.' Chant 'maitri, maitri, maitri' together while gently swinging joined hands. Ask: 'Is there anyone — anywhere — you don't yet consider a friend?' Take 2-3 honest answers gently, without judgment.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Circle intro + chant. 10 min — Krishna's Forest Picnic story; act out the lotus-circle eating arrangement. 8 min — Rani Welcomes Zara story; discuss Meera and Ankita's initial response honestly. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 + 2. 5 min — Welcome Role-Play + introduce Friendship Circle take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Circle opening + chant + story-sharing. 15 min — Krishna's Forest Picnic with actual shared snacks (children bring bundles, eat in lotus-circle formation). 15 min — Rani and Zara story with deeper role-play — including the 'my friends said don't sit with her' moment. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 20 min — Paired Welcome Role-Play with every child being 'new' once. 15 min — Lotus-Circle Mural craft (see extended). 15 min — Whole-class discussion: 'Is there a child in our school we sometimes don't invite? What can we do tomorrow?' 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE person they will include tomorrow.

Discussion Guide

Primary children naturally form tight groups — and that's normal. Do NOT try to dissolve 'best friends'. Instead, teach them to EXPAND the circle. Maitri is additive, not substitutive. Watch for exclusion patterns in the classroom. Address them gently through stories, not punishment. The Krishna-lotus-circle image is powerful — children remember it. When a new child arrives, assign a 'maitri buddy' for the first week. Rotate the role so every child practices it. Praise inclusive action ('You welcomed her — that is maitri') more than sharing generally ('Good sharing'). The target is specifically WIDENING the circle.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Maitri means everyone must be my best friend.' Response: No. You can still have CLOSE friends. But maitri means EXCLUDING NO ONE. You can have a best friend AND still welcome a new child to your table. The circle grows without shrinking.
  • Misconception: 'If someone is unkind to me, I don't have to be friendly.' Response: Maitri doesn't mean accepting unkindness. It means not HATING anyone. You can protect yourself AND still send warm thoughts. Krishna was friendly even to those who attacked Him.
  • Misconception: 'I should feel friendly toward everyone right away.' Response: Maitri grows. Start with a smile. Then a hi. Then sharing a tiffin. The feeling catches up with the action. Don't wait for the feeling — do the action.
