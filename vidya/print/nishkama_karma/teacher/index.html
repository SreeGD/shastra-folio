Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Nishkama karma means doing the right thing because it is right — not because we want a prize, a sticker, or a clap. We help because someone needs help. We study because learning is good. We are kind because kindness is beautiful. Whether anyone sees or not, we keep doing our work with a happy heart.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 2.47 — Krishna says: Your work is yours to do. The results are not. So do your best, little one — but do not do it FOR the prize. And do not stop doing good just because nobody is watching.

Core Teaching

  1. Nishkama karma means doing our work with full love — and letting go of the reward. We do the kind thing because it is RIGHT, not because we want a prize, a sticker, or a clap.
  2. The work is ours to do. The fruit is Krishna's to give. We study fully. We help fully. We share fully. Then we leave the result in Krishna's hands and sleep with a peaceful heart.
  3. A lamp does not light a room to get praise. It lights the room because that is its nature. When we live like a quiet lamp — shining whether people watch or not — our whole life becomes a beautiful offering.

Five-Minute Introduction

Light a small diya at the altar (or hold up a battery tealight). Ask: 'Children, does this lamp shine for a prize? Does it wait for someone to clap before it lights up the room?' Take answers. 'A lamp shines because it is a LAMP. Nobody teaches it. Nobody watches it. It just shines.' Slowly trace the shape of the flame with your finger. 'Today we learn a beautiful old Sanskrit phrase — nishkama karma. It means doing our work with love, like this little lamp — not waiting for a prize, not waiting for a clap.' Chant together, hands on heart: 'Nish-kaa-ma Kar-ma! Nish-kaa-ma Kar-ma! Nish-kaa-ma Kar-ma!' Ask: 'Can you think of one kind thing you could do today that NOBODY would see?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Diya intro + chant 'nishkama karma' 3×. 10 min — Read aloud King Janaka's story; pause at the milk bowl and let children imagine carrying it without spilling. 8 min — Read aloud Meera and the Missing Paintbrushes; pause at the moment she decides, alone, in the empty room. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (For the Prize or For the Right) and Activity 2 (My Quiet Lamp). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce the Quiet Lamp Jar take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Altar diya lighting + opening chant with hands-on-heart. 15 min — King Janaka's story with a real small bowl of water (substitute for milk); children walk in turns around the classroom carrying the bowl, noticing what their eyes DO and DO NOT see. 15 min — Meera's Paintbrushes story with props (dry brushes, a cloth); re-enact the moment of choosing to help. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2 (Circle + Quiet Lamp drawing). 15 min — Secret Kindness Role-Play (Activity 4) with rotated partners. 15 min — Decorate Quiet Lamp Jars: each child decorates a small glass jar with stickers and a paper label. 15 min — Sister Nivedita biographical story with a map of India showing Kolkata. 10 min — Closing: each child names ONE secret kindness they will try this week; whisper together 'Krishna, these are Yours.' Buffer: 5 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children will quickly confuse nishkama karma with 'not caring' or 'being lazy.' Guard carefully against this — the work itself must stay WHOLEHEARTED. Use the lamp image every session: 'We shine fully. We just do not ask for claps.' Praise children when you catch them doing something kind quietly — but praise them PRIVATELY, not in front of the class. Public praise trains a child to perform for applause. A quiet smile, a nod, a squeeze of the shoulder — these honor the child without hooking them to the reward. Distinguish nishkama karma from tyaga carefully: tyaga = letting go of a THING (a turn, a sweet); nishkama karma = letting go of the WANTING-TO-BE-SEEN inside our work. Children at this age are strongly motivated by stickers, stars, and public praise — that is developmentally fine. Our job is to gently plant the seed that a DEEPER joy exists — the quiet warmth Meera felt after cleaning the art room. Story-based learning is key: let Janaka's milk-bowl and Meera's brushes do the teaching. Never shame a child for wanting praise. Instead model, again and again, the quiet lamp.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Nishkama karma means I should not care about doing a good job.' Response: The opposite! Nishkama karma means we do our work with FULL love — even more carefully than if we wanted a prize. We care about the WORK, not the reward. A lamp does not shine halfway. It shines fully. We too do our work fully — and then let Krishna hold the results.
  • Misconception: 'If I do not want any reward, does that mean I should feel sad when nobody thanks me?' Response: No, beta. Nishkama karma is a HAPPY practice. We do not need a clap to be happy. The work itself is the joy. If someone thanks us, we smile and say thank you. If they do not, we still smile — because we did not do it for the thanks.
  • Misconception: 'Nishkama karma means being lazy — if I do not care about results, I do not need to try hard.' Response: That is laziness, not nishkama karma. Krishna's verse says: 'YOUR WORK IS YOURS TO DO.' We still finish our homework carefully. We still help at home fully. The giving up is only of the REWARD — not the work. King Janaka ruled a whole kingdom without clinging. That is the secret.
