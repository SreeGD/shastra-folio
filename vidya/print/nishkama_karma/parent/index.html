Parent Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Value at a Glance

Nishkama karma means doing the right thing because it is right — not because we want a prize, a sticker, or a clap. We help because someone needs help. We study because learning is good. We are kind because kindness is beautiful. Whether anyone sees or not, we keep doing our work with a happy heart.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 2.47: Krishna says: Your work is yours to do. The results are not. So do your best, little one — but do not do it FOR the prize. And do not stop doing good just because nobody is watching.

What it is NOT

Nishkama karma is not being lazy. It is not skipping our chores because 'it does not matter.' It is not being sad when nobody notices. It is also not doing good things ONLY so that people will praise us. The work itself — done with love — is the real reward.

  • Helping Amma only when she promises ice cream — and refusing the moment the treat is gone
  • Doing a kind deed, then running to tell five people so they will say 'What a good child!'
  • Stopping halfway through homework because no one is watching, and starting again when Amma walks in

Stories Your Child Heard

King Janaka — The King Who Ruled But Did Not Hold — Long, long ago in the kingdom of Mithila, there ruled a very special king. His name was Janaka. He had a golden crown. He had a big palace. He had elephants and horses and many wise ministers. But Janaka was not like other kings.

Meera and the Missing Paintbrushes — It was Saturday morning. Meera came to art class fifteen minutes early because she loved the smell of paint and paper. She pushed open the classroom door. The room was a mess.

The Sanskrit Word

niṣkāma karma (निष्काम कर्म) — nish-KAH-ma KAR-ma

Meaning: Doing the right work without wanting a prize. 'Nish' means 'without'; 'kama' means 'selfish wish'; 'karma' means 'action.' Together: work done without the 'what will I get?' feeling — done with love, offered to Krishna.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Quiet Lamp Jar on your desk or shelf. Every day, do ONE kind thing that NOBODY knows about — tidy a shoe, fold a napkin, refill the water jug, help a sibling silently, put away a toy that is not yours. For each secret kindness, drop ONE pebble or button into the jar. Do not count in front of others. Do not tell. At bedtime, close your eyes and whisper: 'Krishna, these pebbles are Yours. The work was for You.' At the end of the week, empty the jar by pouring the pebbles back into the garden — they belonged to Krishna, not to you.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific quiet kindnesses performed without announcing them, demonstrates the hands-on-heart gesture while saying 'nishkama karma', and has not asked for praise or acknowledgment for the jar's contents.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific quiet kindnesses performed without announcing them, demonstrates the hands-on-heart gesture while saying 'nishkama karma', and has not asked for praise or acknowledgment for the jar's contents.

When we learn to work without grabbing the reward, our heart becomes steady and balanced — and that beautiful balance has its own name, samata.
