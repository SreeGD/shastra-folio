Vinaya Worksheet — The Bow Inside

Activity 1: Bow or Stand Tall? (Circle the Right One)

1. Teacher corrects my spelling. Do I argue or bow my head and say 'please show me'? ARGUE BOW

2. Ajji is telling me a story I've heard before. Do I interrupt or listen quietly? INTERRUPT LISTEN

3. I walk into the temple. Do I run around shouting or bow before Krishna? RUN BOW

4. Guru-ji is speaking to the class. Do I chat with my friend or sit still with palms folded? CHAT FOLD HANDS

5. Amma asks me to help. Do I say 'I'm BUSY!' or bow and say 'Yes, Amma'? REFUSE BOW YES

Activity 2: Draw the Four Bows

In the box below, draw YOURSELF bowing in FOUR places:

1. Before Krishna on the altar at home

2. Before your teacher at school

3. Before your grandmother when she gives you something

4. Before your guru-ji (or the sacred picture on the wall)

Show your HEAD bowed, your HANDS folded, your FACE soft. Colour your family members' faces warm and kind.

Activity 3: The Bowing Chant (Soft and Slow)

Stand tall. Breathe in — hands above head. Breathe out — bring palms together to chest. Then bend forward gently and touch your forehead toward your folded hands.

Chant three times — SLOWLY, one chant per full bow:

Vi-na-YA! Vi-na-YA! Vi-na-YA! *My head bows. My heart opens. I am here to learn.*

Activity 4: The Please-Show-Me Game (Paired)

With a partner, take turns:

  • Partner A (teacher): 'Your drawing has a mistake here.'
  • Partner B (student): Chin down. Hands folded. Soft voice — 'Please show me.'
  • Partner A shows. Partner B listens.
  • Partner A (grandmother): 'Long ago, when I was a little girl...'
  • Partner B: Sits still. Listens. Does not interrupt.
  • Partner A (guru-ji): 'Listen carefully to this teaching.'
  • Partner B: Palms folded. Eyes soft. Head slightly bowed.

Switch. Notice: does your heart feel BIGGER or smaller when you bow and listen?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Morning Bow Book

Keep a small notebook by the family altar. Each morning, before breakfast, bow before Krishna (or whichever deity your family keeps). Put your forehead to the floor if you can, or just bow your head if you cannot.

Then draw ONE tiny folded-hands namaste in your book. Under it, whisper one name — the name of ONE person you will bow to today: 'Asha-ma'am,' 'Ajji,' 'Amma,' 'Papa,' 'Guru-ji.'

At night, tick it if you bowed to that person.

At the end of the week, count your little namastes. Each one is a tiny diya of vinaya burning in your heart.

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Morning Bow Book by the family altar. Every morning before breakfast, stand in front of Krishna (or the family deity). Ring a small bell if there is one — tinkle, tinkle. Then bow. If you can, go all the way down — knees, forehead to the floor, like Satyakama did. If you cannot, just bow your head with folded hands. Do this three times. Then draw ONE tiny folded-hands namaste in your book. Under it, write or whisper ONE name — the name of ONE elder (Ajji, Amma, Papa, teacher, guru-ji) you will bow to today. At bedtime, tick it if you bowed. Before sleeping, whisper: 'Krishna, my head bowed today. Thank You for filling my cup.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 mornings when they did the full morning bow at the altar, and at least 3 times they bowed before an elder during the day (touching feet, folded hands, or soft 'please show me' when corrected). They can demonstrate the dandavat (log-bow) position on the floor while softly saying 'vinaya.' The book has at least 5 little namastes drawn.
