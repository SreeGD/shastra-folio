Teacher Guide

Chapter Overview

Vinaya is the bow inside me. It is what makes my head go DOWN when I see my teacher, my grandmother, my guru, or Krishna. It is the quiet voice that says 'I have so much to learn. Please teach me.' When I do vinaya, I put my hands together. I bow low. I listen before I speak. I know I am small — not sad-small, just happy-small, like a little lamp waiting to be lit by a bigger lamp.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 13.8 — Krishna says: First, my child, learn amānitvam — no-pride. Learn to bow your head before those who know more. Sit close to your teacher. That is where real learning begins. A head that bows is a head that can fill with wisdom.

Core Teaching

  1. Vinaya is the bow inside me. When I see my teacher, my grandmother, my guru, or Krishna — something in me goes DOWN. My head bows. My hands come together. My voice goes soft. I am not scared — I am RECEIVING. A head that bows is a head that fills with wisdom. A cup held low catches more water than a cup held high.
  2. Vinaya is the listening-first heart. When teacher corrects me, I do not say 'I ALREADY KNOW.' I say 'Please show me.' When Ajji tells a story, I do not interrupt with my own. I listen. I breathe. I wait. When Krishna speaks to my heart in the quiet, I listen before I speak. Krishna says — amānitvam — no-pride — this is where real learning begins.
  3. Vinaya is NOT feeling bad about myself. Krishna made me lovely. Vinaya is not hiding behind a wall of false shyness. Vinaya only means I know — truly, deeply — 'I have SO much to learn.' My teacher knows more than me. My grandmother has lived longer than me. My guru has walked the path before me. Krishna is the Big Teacher of ALL. Before them, my head goes DOWN. And that is where I grow TALL.

Five-Minute Introduction

Stand at the front. Place a small empty brass cup on the table. Hold a jug of water above it. Slowly pour — but tilt the cup UP, rim-first, so the water splashes out onto the table. Say: 'A cup held high catches nothing.' Now set the cup down the right way. Pour again. The cup fills. Smile. 'A cup held low fills up. Children, our HEAD is like that cup. If we hold it HIGH — if we say I already know, I am the best, don't teach me — then nothing can fill us. But if we hold our head LOW, if we bow before those who know more, then wisdom pours IN. This bowing has a name. Krishna teaches it first on the path of real learning. The name is VINAYA.' Chant together, bowing on each beat, palms folded at chest: 'Vi-na-YA. Vi-na-YA. Vi-na-YA.' Ask: 'To whom do you bow every morning at home?' Take 2-3 short answers — Krishna, Amma, Ajji, guru-ji, the family altar. Celebrate each one with a small soft bow back to the child.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Cup-and-water demonstration + bowing chant 'vinaya' 3x. 10 min — Read aloud Satyakama's story; children mime 'walk, fold hands, dandavat-bow' at the right moment. 8 min — Read aloud Rohan's story; pause at the 'chin went UP' moment — ask 'what should Rohan do?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Bow or Stand Tall) + Activity 2 (Draw the Four Bows). 5 min — Closing chant with slow full bow + introduce Morning Bow Book take-home. 2 min — Soft line-up, with a small bow to the teacher at the door.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, cup-and-water demo, bowing chant. 15 min — Satyakama's story with physical reenactment: each child practises the dandavat (log-bow) on a soft mat; teacher gently guides 'forehead to floor, arms outstretched, whisper vinaya.' 15 min — Rohan's story, acted out in pairs — one child as Asha-ma'am pointing at the sum, the other bringing the chin DOWN and saying 'please show me.' 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Paired Please-Show-Me Game (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how the heart feels lighter after bowing than after arguing. 15 min — Small Altar Craft: each child decorates a small cardboard frame with glitter and flowers; inside they will place a picture of Krishna (or their family deity) for their own tiny home-altar where they will practise the morning bow. 10 min — Biographical story: Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan bowing before his teachers all his life; children discuss 'what does it mean that his birthday is Teachers' Day?' 10 min — Closing: children line up in pairs; each child bows to their partner (namaste-style), receives a small soft 'bless you, kanna' back; teacher hands out Morning Bow Books. Buffer: 10 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn vinaya through the BODY — the folded hands, the bowed head, the touching of the floor. This is a value that LIVES in physical ritual before it becomes an inner attitude. Use bowing cues every single class. Make the BODY bow, and the heart will follow. Distinguish vinaya carefully from two things children confuse it with. (1) Hrih (soft modesty) — hrih is about holding my OWN goodness and OWN mistakes gently; vinaya is specifically about my posture before someone BIGGER than me (teacher, elder, Krishna). Hrih is inward. Vinaya is relational. They are cousins, not twins. (2) Fear — some children freeze in silence and LOOK bowed, but inside they are scared. That is not vinaya. Vinaya is warm — the child WANTS to learn, WANTS to be corrected, WANTS to listen. Watch for the child who always argues with correction — the chin-up child. Do not shame them. Simply model the bow yourself, praise the children who bow, and slowly the chin-up child will try it. Never force a bow; a forced bow is no bow. Also watch for the child with shame who never lifts their head AT ALL — that is not vinaya either; that is pain. Gently, warmly invite their head UP when they are not before an elder — 'Kanna, when we are playing, we stand tall and meet eyes. We only bow before our teachers and our elders.' Most importantly, model vinaya in yourself as the teacher. Bow to the altar at the start of class. Bow to your own guru's picture. Say 'I do not know that — let me ask someone who does' in front of the children. The child catches vinaya from the adult who still bows. Also: be gentle with multi-faith families. The gesture of folded hands and bowed head is universal. Let children bow to whatever their family holds sacred — no judgment.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Vinaya means I am weak or scared.' Response: No, no. Vinaya is INNER STRENGTH, not weakness. Think of Satyakama — he was BRAVE enough to walk alone into the forest, to stand before the great rishi, to tell the truth even when he thought he might be sent away. That is not fear. That is mighty courage. The bow is strong. The bow says 'I know I have much to learn, and I am brave enough to say it out loud.'
  • Misconception: 'If I bow before everyone, people will walk all over me.' Response: Vinaya does not mean we let others hurt us. We bow before those who have WISDOM to give us — our teacher, our grandmother, our guru, Krishna. We do NOT bow before bullies. We do not bow before bad things. Vinaya is choosing WHERE to put our head down — so the right people can place their hand on it and bless us.
  • Misconception: 'Vinaya is just touching the feet — it is only a ritual.' Response: The outside bow is lovely, but the real vinaya is the INSIDE bow. When Rohan's chin came down before Asha-ma'am, that was vinaya. Even if he never touched her feet, his HEART bowed. Touching feet without a bowed heart is just exercise. A bowed heart without touching feet is the real thing. But when BOTH happen together — that is most beautiful of all.
  • Misconception (for teacher/parent): 'My child will become a pushover if I teach them to bow.' Response: The opposite is true. Children who learn vinaya early become SAFER and STRONGER adults. They know who to turn to for wisdom. They do not waste their years arguing with everyone. They grow FAST because they fill with knowledge from many elders. The cocky child stays small. The bowing child grows tall. Please model vinaya yourself — bow before your guru, your parents, the altar — and your child will catch it naturally.
