Value at a Glance

Karuna means a mercy-heart that reaches out to help, especially to those who cannot help themselves — or even to those who may have hurt us. Daya feels sad when we see someone suffer; karuna is the hand that MOVES to lift them up. Karuna does not ask, 'Do they deserve it?' It asks, 'Do they need it?'

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 12.13: One who never hates any creature, who is a friend to all, who is full of mercy, who has no 'mine' and no pride, who stays the same in sorrow and in joy, and who forgives — such a one is very dear to Me, says Krishna.

What it is NOT

Karuna is not pity that looks down on others. It is not being mean back when someone is mean to us. It is not helping only the people we like.

  • Laughing at a classmate who fell, because they teased us yesterday
  • Walking past a stray dog because 'it's not my pet'
  • Feeling sorry for someone but doing nothing to help

Stories Your Child Heard

The Merciful Name That Saved Ajamila — Long ago in India, there lived a man named Ajamila. When he was a boy, he was very kind. He chanted Krishna's names. He helped the temple priest. Everyone said, 'What a good boy.' But when Ajamila grew up, something sad happened. He forgot all the good things. He lied. He took things that were not his. He was unkind. Years passed. Ajamila became old and sick.

Nisha and the Boy Who Teased Her — Nisha was in Class 2. She had long braids and a bright lunchbox with a picture of Hanuman on it. She was happy most days. But one boy made her sad. His name was Bittu. Bittu sat two benches behind her. Every morning, Bittu pulled her braid. At lunch, he laughed at her idli and sambar. 'Eww, white soft balls!' he would shout. His friends laughed too.

The Sanskrit Word

karuṇā (करुणा) — ka-ru-NAA

Meaning: Mercy — the mercy-heart that reaches to help, especially those who cannot help themselves or do not deserve it.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Do the One-Mercy-Day practice. Every day for one week, reach your mercy-hand to ONE person or creature who needs it — and try at least twice to help someone you do not usually help (a shy child, a stray animal, a person who was rough with you). At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa: 'Today my karuna-hand reached ______.' Draw a tiny heart on your calendar.

Duration: Once daily for one week (7 mercy-acts minimum)
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 5 specific mercy-acts they did, can describe one moment when it was HARD to help but they did it anyway, and shows less hesitation to reach out to a struggling peer.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can name at least 5 specific mercy-acts they did, can describe one moment when it was HARD to help but they did it anyway, and shows less hesitation to reach out to a struggling peer.

When our mercy-hand reaches others again and again, it teaches our whole self to move with love — and that loving self naturally wants to SERVE. That is our next beautiful word: seva.
