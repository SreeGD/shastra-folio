Worksheet

Karuna Worksheet — The Mercy-Heart

Activity 1: Does It Need Help? (Circle the Mercy-Moment)

Circle the picture where someone's karuna-heart would move. Put an X if no one needs help.

1. An old beggar sitting in the rain with no umbrella. 🤲 ❌

2. A friend winning first prize in a race. 🤲 ❌

3. A stray puppy limping with a hurt paw. 🤲 ❌

4. A classmate who teased you yesterday, now crying alone. 🤲 ❌

5. A thirsty sparrow on a hot day with no water nearby. 🤲 ❌

Activity 2: The Mercy-Hand Craft

Materials: Paper, pencil, crayons, scissors, glue

Steps:

1. Trace your own hand on paper — spread your fingers wide.

2. On each finger, write or draw ONE person or creature your mercy-hand can reach: a stray dog, a little sister, a friend who fell, an old grandmother, a tired ant.

3. In the palm, write the Sanskrit word करुणा (karuṇā).

4. Color your mercy-hand bright and warm. Cut it out.

5. Tape it beside your bed. Every night, look at one finger and remember one kindness you did.

Activity 3: Say It With Me

Stand up. Hold your palms out, open, like a bowl ready to give. Chant three times together:

Ka-ru-NAA! Ka-ru-NAA! Ka-ru-NAA! *I reach! I help! I care!*

Activity 4: The Turn-Around Story (Paired)

With a partner, act out this scene twice:

Scene: Someone who was mean to you last week is crying because they dropped their lunchbox.

First time: Walk past. Shrug. Say 'Not my problem.' Second time: Kneel. Pick up the lunchbox. Share your own lunch. Sit with them.

After both turns, whisper to each other: 'Which heart was lighter? Which heart felt more like Krishna?'

Activity 5: Take Home — The One-Mercy-Day

Every day for one week, do ONE tiny mercy-act — AND especially to someone you do not usually help (a shy classmate, a stray animal, someone who was rough with you). At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa: 'Today my karuna-hand reached ______.' Draw it on your calendar with a tiny heart.

🤲 ❤️ 🌼

Home Practice

Do the One-Mercy-Day practice. Every day for one week, reach your mercy-hand to ONE person or creature who needs it — and try at least twice to help someone you do not usually help (a shy child, a stray animal, a person who was rough with you). At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa: 'Today my karuna-hand reached ______.' Draw a tiny heart on your calendar.

Duration: Once daily for one week (7 mercy-acts minimum)
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 5 specific mercy-acts they did, can describe one moment when it was HARD to help but they did it anyway, and shows less hesitation to reach out to a struggling peer.
