Value at a Glance

Svadhyaya means self-study — learning one little thing every single day. A new word. A verse. A story about Krishna. A fact about the saints. My little library grows page by page. And then, at the end of the day, I sit quietly and look inside — 'What did I do well today? What can I do better tomorrow?' The book outside teaches my mind. The looking inside teaches my heart.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 4.34: Krishna says: Learn a little every day. Bow your head. Ask a gentle question. Serve with love. Then the wise ones — the ones who have truly seen — will open up their knowledge and pour it into your heart.

What it is NOT

Svadhyaya is not reading fast just to finish. It is not showing off how many shlokas I know. It is not reading only when teacher is watching. And it is not hiding from my own mistakes — pretending I did everything perfectly. True svadhyaya is slow, warm, happy learning — and honest, gentle looking-inside.

  • Flipping the pages of the Gita quickly without reading one word carefully
  • Memorising a verse only to win a prize at school
  • Saying 'I don't want to think about my day — I was perfect!' when I secretly was not
  • Refusing to open a book at home because 'reading is only for class'

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Prahlada Teaches His Classmates — Long ago, in the palace of the demon-king Hiranyakashipu, a little boy was born. His name was Prahlada. He was only five years old. His father was a fierce king. But Prahlada's heart was soft and full of love for Krishna. One day, the king sent Prahlada to school. Two stern teachers — Shanda and Amarka — were in charge. They taught the children of the kingdom only three things: how to grab power, how to get rich, and how to defeat enemies.

Ishaan and His One-Page-A-Day Book — Ishaan was seven years old. He lived in Pune with his Amma, his Papa, and his grandfather — Dadaji. On his seventh birthday, Dadaji gave him a small brown book. It was a children's Bhagavad Gita. It had simple pictures and simple words.

The Sanskrit Word

svādhyāya (स्वाध्याय) — svaa-DHYAA-ya

Meaning: Self-study. Learning one new thing every day from sacred books. And looking inside one's own heart with honest, gentle eyes. Outside study and inside study — both together.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your One-Leaf-A-Day Book by your bed. Every morning, AFTER brushing your teeth, open a small book — a children's Gita, a Krishna story, or even a page Amma reads to you. Read or listen to ONE little thing. Every evening, before sleep, draw a tiny leaf in your book. On the leaf, write or draw: (1) ONE new thing I learned today, (2) ONE thing my heart did well, (3) ONE thing I will do better tomorrow. Sit quietly for two minutes. Whisper: 'Krishna, my library grew one leaf today. Thank You for teaching me inside and outside.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child has drawn at least 5 leaves in their One-Leaf-A-Day Book, can name at least 3 new things they learned that week (a Krishna name, a story, a verse, a fact), and can share at least 2 honest inside-look reflections without shame or boasting. When our daily learning and honest inside-looking grow steady — day after day, leaf after leaf — that steady, warm trust in Krishna and the sacred path has its own beautiful name: śraddhā.
