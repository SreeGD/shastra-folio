Value at a Glance

Tapas is the little fire inside me. It is the warm, steady flame that helps me do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. When I say no to a second sweet, that is tapas. When I wake up early for prayer even though my bed is cosy, that is tapas. When my tongue wants to say something mean but I keep it soft — that is tapas too. Tapas is not big or scary. It is a tiny fire that grows brighter the more I feed it with small brave choices.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 17.14-16: Krishna says there are three little fires inside us. The BODY-fire: keeping clean, standing straight, being gentle, being pure. The SPEECH-fire: saying only true, kind, and helpful words, and reading good books. The MIND-fire: a peaceful happy mind, a gentle feeling inside, and a self that we can hold like a sleepy puppy in our lap. When these three fires burn together, that is tapas.

What it is NOT

Tapas is not punishing ourselves. It is not starving or hurting our bodies to feel holy. Tapas is not showing off — 'Look how strict I am!' It is not being grumpy or sour while we practise. And tapas is not for getting prizes — stickers, sweets, praise. The real little fire burns quietly, for Krishna, even when no one is watching.

  • Skipping lunch so everyone will say 'Wow, you are so spiritual'
  • Waking up early and then being grumpy and mean to my sister all morning
  • Doing a hard thing only because Amma promised me ice cream
  • Hurting my body — like pinching myself or not drinking water — and calling it 'tapas'

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Shukadeva and the Still Little Fire — On the banks of a quiet river, Rishi Vyasa had a little son named Shukadeva. He was only eight. Shukadeva loved Krishna. When other children chased butterflies, he sat still and whispered, 'Krishna. Krishna.'

Tanvi and the Second Laddu — It was Janmashtami. Tanvi was seven, at her Nani's house in Vrindavan. The house smelled like GHEE and FRESH LADDUS. Nani had made a plate of besan laddus. Golden. Round. Each with a pistachio on top.

The Sanskrit Word

tapas (तपस्) — TA-pas

Meaning: The little fire inside. A small steady warmth that helps me do the hard right thing instead of the easy wrong thing. The warm glow when I say no to a second sweet, or wake up early for prayer, or keep my words kind when my tongue wants to shout. Krishna's fire inside me.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Little-Fire Diary beside your pillow. Each evening, before sleep, think about the day. For EACH time you chose the hard-right over the easy-wrong — say no to a second sweet, woke up on time, kept kind words when you wanted to shout, brought your mind back to Krishna during bhajan — draw one tiny orange flame in your diary. Then put your hand on your chest and whisper: 'Krishna, my little fire is for You. Please keep it burning.' In the morning, before rising, touch your chest and whisper: 'Body-fire. Speech-fire. Mind-fire. Three tapas for Krishna today.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific 'hard-right' choices they made, can demonstrate the three-fire gesture (hand on tummy, touch lips, fold on forehead), and the diary contains at least 7 tiny flames. The child can use the phrase 'my little fire' spontaneously to describe a moment of discipline.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific 'hard-right' choices they made, can demonstrate the three-fire gesture (hand on tummy, touch lips, fold on forehead), and the diary contains at least 7 tiny flames. The child can use the phrase 'my little fire' spontaneously to describe a moment of discipline.

When our little fire burns steady enough that we even control the words on our tongue and the thoughts in our mind — that mastery has its own beautiful name: ātma-nigraha.
