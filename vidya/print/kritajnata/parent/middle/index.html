Value at a Glance

Kritajnata is deep thankfulness for all we have received. It means seeing that everything we have comes from God, our parents, teachers, and nature. When we feel real gratitude, we want to give back and help others.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.26: If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

What it is NOT

Kritajnata is not just saying 'thank you' as good manners. It is not thinking we earned everything by ourselves. It is not feeling grateful only when we get what we want.

  • Entitlement - thinking we deserve everything without effort from others
  • Taking for granted - ignoring the work parents and teachers do for us
  • Fake politeness - saying thanks without really meaning it

Stories Your Child Heard

Vidura's True Loyalty — In the great palace of Hastinapura lived Vidura, the wise advisor to King Dhritarashtra. Because Vidura was born to a servant woman, many nobles looked down on him. But Dhritarashtra had always treated Vidura like a brother. He gave him education, respect, and an important position in the court. Vidura felt deep gratitude for these gifts. As years passed, Dhritarashtra began making terrible decisions. He allowed his son Duryodhana to cheat the Pandavas in a rigged dice game. He watched silently as Draupadi was insulted in the royal court. Each wrong choice pushed the kingdom closer to war.

The Recipe Book — Thirteen-year-old Kenji lived in a small apartment in Kyoto with his parents and grandmother, Obaa-chan. Every day after school, he would rush home to find her in the tiny kitchen, preparing amazing meals. The smell of miso soup and fresh rice always made him smile. Obaa-chan had taught herself to cook during hard times after the war. She never used recipes - everything was in her memory. 'Cooking is love made visible,' she would say, watching Kenji eat with joy.

The Sanskrit Word

kṛtajñatā (कृतज्ञता) — kri-ta-GYAH-taa

Meaning: Gratitude, thankfulness, grateful recognition of help received

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each evening before dinner, tell one family member about someone who helped you that day and how it made you feel. Then ask them to share about someone who helped them. Keep a simple list of all the helpers you both mention.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, you will have a list of at least 14 different people who helped your family, and you'll notice helpers you used to overlook.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, you will have a list of at least 14 different people who helped your family, and you'll notice helpers you used to overlook.
