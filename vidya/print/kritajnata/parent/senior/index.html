Value at a Glance

Kṛtajñatā is the deep recognition and appreciation of all that one has received from the Divine, parents, teachers, nature, and all living beings. It is not mere politeness but a heartfelt awareness that everything we possess — life, knowledge, food, shelter — flows from sources beyond ourselves. This gratitude naturally leads to humility, service, and a desire to give back, forming the foundation of all healthy relationships and spiritual growth.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.26: If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

What it is NOT

Kṛtajñatā is not transactional thankfulness expressed only to gain future favors, nor is it mere intellectual acknowledgment of blessings without heartfelt transformation. It is not the self-made delusion that all achievements are entirely self-earned, denying the contributions of God, teachers, and community.

  • Entitlement — assuming one deserves everything received without acknowledging others' sacrifice
  • Kṛtaghnatā (ingratitude) — actively forgetting or denying help received
  • Transactional thankfulness — expressing gratitude only as social formality or to extract favors
  • Self-made delusion — believing all achievements are entirely self-earned

Stories Your Child Heard

Prahlada's Divine Gratitude — In the golden palace of Hiranyakashipu, sixteen-year-old Prince Prahlada knelt in his chambers, hands folded in prayer. Although his father ruled as the most powerful demon king in the universe, Prahlada's heart overflowed with gratitude to Lord Vishnu for every breath, every moment of existence. "How can you thank that Vishnu who is my greatest enemy?" roared Hiranyakashipu, bursting into the room. "Everything you have comes from me — your royal birth, your education, your very life!"

The Weight of Dreams — Rohan stared at the acceptance letter in his hands, his heart racing. Full scholarship to Oxford University. Everything he had worked for since childhood was finally within reach. But across the small apartment in Mumbai, his father Suresh coughed violently, the sound echoing off the thin walls. "Beta, this is wonderful news!" Suresh wheezed, trying to smile despite his obvious pain. "All those years of tutoring students after my factory shift... it was worth it to see this day."

The Sanskrit Word

kṛtajñatā (कृतज्ञता) — kri-ta-GYAH-taa

Meaning: Gratitude, thankfulness, grateful acknowledgment of benefits received

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each evening before sleep, write down three specific contributions others made to your day that you might normally overlook — not just family and friends, but people like the bus driver, cafeteria worker, or person who stocked the grocery store. Include one sentence about how their contribution affected your life.

Duration: Daily for one week
Look for: You will notice yourself becoming more aware of the 'invisible' network of people supporting your daily life, and may find yourself naturally expressing appreciation more often throughout the day.

Signs of Growth
