Kṛtajñatā (Gratitude) — College Level Worksheet

Section 1: Integrative Analysis

Compare and contrast the expressions of gratitude in the epic story of Sudama and the modern story of Dr. Elena Vasquez. In your analysis, address:

1. Recognition Patterns: How does each protagonist move from taking things for granted to recognizing the web of contributions that shaped their lives?

2. Transformation Mechanisms: What specific internal shifts occur in each character, and how do these shifts manifest in their external actions?

3. Cultural Contexts: How do the ancient Vedic and contemporary academic contexts shape the expression of gratitude differently, yet reveal universal principles?

*Write 400-500 words, drawing specific examples from both stories.*

Section 2: Philosophical Research Project

Design a comparative study of gratitude practices across three wisdom traditions of your choice (e.g., Vedantic, Buddhist, Stoic, Indigenous, Sufi, etc.). Your research should include:

Framework Development:

  • Create a matrix comparing how each tradition defines gratitude
  • Identify the philosophical foundations underlying each approach
  • Analyze the practical methods each tradition recommends for cultivating gratitude

Synthesis Component:

  • Design an integrated gratitude practice that draws from all three traditions
  • Explain how this integrated approach might address the limitations of any single tradition
  • Consider how this practice could be adapted for different life circumstances

Presentation Format: Create a visual infographic or digital presentation that could teach this comparative framework to younger students.

Section 3: Applied Ethics Case Study

You are serving as an ethics consultant for a multinational corporation that has been criticized for environmental damage and labor practices. The CEO genuinely wants to transform the company culture but asks: "How can gratitude be more than just corporate social responsibility theater?"

Your Task: Develop a comprehensive proposal that addresses:

1. Diagnostic Phase: How would you help the organization recognize what it has truly received from employees, communities, and the natural environment?

2. Structural Changes: What specific policies and practices would embody authentic corporate gratitude rather than mere public relations?

3. Measurement Metrics: How would you assess whether the organization's gratitude practices are genuine transformation or performative compliance?

4. Stakeholder Integration: How would you involve employees, communities, and environmental groups in co-creating this gratitude-based transformation?

*Present your proposal as a detailed memo (800-1000 words) that demonstrates both philosophical depth and practical applicability.*

Section 4: Personal Integration Essay

Reflect on your own relationship with gratitude through the lens of the five debts (pañca-ṛṇa) mentioned in the Vedic tradition:

1. Deva-ṛṇa (debt to the divine/cosmic forces)

2. Pitṛ-ṛṇa (debt to ancestors and parents)

3. Ṛṣi-ṛṇa (debt to teachers and knowledge-givers)

4. Manuṣya-ṛṇa (debt to society and fellow humans)

5. Bhūta-ṛṇa (debt to all living beings and nature)

Writing Prompts:

  • Which of these debts do you find easiest to acknowledge? Which is most challenging?
  • How has your understanding of gratitude evolved throughout your educational journey?
  • What specific practices might help you live with greater awareness of these interconnections?
  • How might recognizing these debts change your career choices, relationships, or daily habits?

*Write a reflective essay of 600-800 words that demonstrates both personal honesty and philosophical sophistication.*

Section 5: Mentorship Practice Design

Drawing on the principle that gratitude naturally flows into service, create a structured plan for mentoring someone in developing authentic gratitude practices.

Your Plan Should Include:

  • Assessment Tools: How would you help someone recognize their current gratitude patterns and blind spots?
  • Progressive Practices: Design a 6-week curriculum of increasingly sophisticated gratitude exercises
  • Integration Challenges: What obstacles might arise, and how would you address resistance or superficiality?
  • Evaluation Methods: How would you and your mentee assess genuine transformation versus mere behavioral compliance?

Reflection Component: How might the process of teaching gratitude deepen your own understanding and practice?

*Present this as a detailed curriculum guide (600-800 words) that you could actually implement.*"

Home Practice

Each evening before sleep, write down three specific contributions others made to your day that you initially took for granted — not obvious ones like meals or transportation, but hidden ones like the person who designed your phone's interface, the researcher who developed the medicine you take, or the janitor who cleaned your workspace. Then choose one person from your list and find a concrete way to acknowledge their contribution within the next 24 hours.

Duration: Daily for two weeks
Look for: You will notice an increasing awareness of the invisible network supporting your daily life, and you may find yourself naturally expressing appreciation more frequently and specifically to people around you. Several people may comment on feeling more valued or appreciated by you.
