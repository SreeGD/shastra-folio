Worksheet

Kṛtajñatā (Gratitude) — Senior Level Worksheet

Section 1: Story Analysis

Connect the epic and modern stories by answering these questions:

1. Parallel Recognition: Both Prahlada and Rohan faced situations where gratitude required sacrifice. Compare how each character chose to honor their relationships despite personal cost. What similar principle guided both decisions?

2. Transformation Through Gratitude: Analyze how gratitude changed not just the protagonists but also the people around them. How did Prahlada's gratitude affect his father's ultimate destiny? How did Rohan's gratitude impact his family's healing?

3. Gratitude in Adversity: Both stories show gratitude practiced during difficulty. Explain how each character maintained thankfulness even when circumstances seemed to work against their immediate desires. What does this teach us about the true nature of kṛtajñatā?

Section 2: Collaborative Design Challenge

Time: 30 minutes | Team Size: 3-4 students

Challenge: Create a "Gratitude Web" installation for your school that makes visible the network of people who contribute to student success.

Materials: Large sheets of paper, colored yarn/string, sticky notes, markers

Process:

1. Map out all the people who contribute to a typical student's day (teachers, janitors, cafeteria workers, bus drivers, parents, farmers who grow food, etc.)

2. Create a visual web showing these connections using yarn between different roles

3. Add specific appreciation notes for often-overlooked contributors

4. Design a way for other students to add their own gratitude connections

Debrief Questions:

  • Which contributors were you surprised to discover?
  • How does seeing this web change your awareness of interdependence?
  • What would happen if any part of this web was missing?

Section 3: Philosophical Dialogue Game

Time: 20 minutes | Players: 6-12 students

Setup: Students sit in a circle. One person poses a gratitude dilemma, others offer perspectives.

Sample Scenarios:

  • "I'm grateful for my privileges, but I feel guilty that others don't have them. How do I practice gratitude without ignoring injustice?"
  • "My parents made sacrifices for me that I didn't ask for. Am I obligated to live my life according to their expectations out of gratitude?"
  • "I want to be grateful for challenges as 'learning opportunities,' but sometimes that feels like I'm dismissing real suffering. Where's the line?"

Rules:

  • Each response must begin with "I appreciate that perspective, and I also think..."
  • No one can repeat a point already made
  • The goal is understanding, not winning

Debrief: Which dilemmas felt most relevant to your life? What insights emerged from hearing multiple perspectives?

Section 4: Extended Reflection Essay

Length: 500-750 words

Prompt: The Bhagavad Gita teaches that Krishna accepts even the smallest offering made with love and devotion. This suggests that gratitude is reciprocal — we offer thanks, and the Divine graciously receives it.

Write a reflective essay exploring this question: "How does understanding gratitude as a reciprocal relationship (rather than a one-way obligation) change the way you approach thankfulness in your daily life?"

Consider:

  • The difference between gratitude as debt versus gratitude as relationship
  • How this perspective might affect your interactions with parents, teachers, and friends
  • Whether seeing the Divine as grateful for your offerings changes your spiritual practice
  • Examples from your own experience where gratitude felt more like connection than obligation

Section 5: Real-World Application Challenge

Duration: This week

Challenge: Practice "hidden gratitude" — express appreciation to three people who contribute to your life but rarely receive acknowledgment.

Guidelines:

  • Choose people whose work often goes unnoticed (school support staff, local service workers, family members who handle "invisible" tasks)
  • Express gratitude in a way that's meaningful to them (a handwritten note, a small gift, public recognition, offering to help)
  • Observe their response and your own feelings during the interaction

Reflection Questions (to discuss next class):

  • How did expressing gratitude to "hidden contributors" affect your awareness of interdependence?
  • What did you learn about the difference between formal "thank you" and heartfelt appreciation?
  • How might making this a regular practice change your relationships and community?

Home Practice

Each evening before sleep, write down three specific contributions others made to your day that you might normally overlook — not just family and friends, but people like the bus driver, cafeteria worker, or person who stocked the grocery store. Include one sentence about how their contribution affected your life.

Duration: Daily for one week
Look for: You will notice yourself becoming more aware of the 'invisible' network of people supporting your daily life, and may find yourself naturally expressing appreciation more often throughout the day.
