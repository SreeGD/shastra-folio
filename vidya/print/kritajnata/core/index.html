Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 9.26

पत्रं पुष्पं फलं तोयं
यो मे भक्त्या प्रयच्छति ।
तदहं भक्त्युपहृतम्
अश्नामि प्रयतात्मनः ॥

patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati tad ahaṁ bhakty-upahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ

Word-for-word: patram — a leaf; puṣpam — a flower; phalam — a fruit; toyam — water; yaḥ — whoever; me — unto Me; bhaktyā — with devotion; prayacchati — offers; tat — that; aham — I; bhakti-upahṛtam — offered in devotion; aśnāmi — accept; prayata-ātmanaḥ — from one in pure consciousness

Translation: If one offers Me with love a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.3.21

तस्माद् गुरुं प्रपद्येत जिज्ञासु: श्रेय उत्तमम् ।
शाब्दे परे च निष्णातं ब्रह्मण्युपशमाश्रयम् ॥ २१ ॥

tasmād guruṁ prapadyeta jijṣāsuḥ śreya uttamam śābde pare ca niṣṇātaṁ brahmaṇy upaśamāśrayam

Translation: Any person who seriously desires real happiness must seek a bona fide spiritual master and take shelter of him by initiation. The qualification of the bona fide guru is that he has realized the conclusions of the scriptures by deliberation and is able to convince others of these conclusions. Such great personalities, who have taken shelter of the Supreme Godhead, leaving aside all material considerations, should be understood to be bona fide spiritual masters.

What is Kritajnata?

Gratitude means saying thank you with your heart. It means being happy for all the good things you have. When someone helps you or gives you something, you feel thankful inside.

What it is NOT

Gratitude is not just saying thank you with your mouth but not meaning it. It is not taking things without caring who gave them to you.

  • Forgetting to say thank you when someone helps you
  • Always asking for more things instead of being happy with what you have
  • Taking food, toys, and help for granted

Sanskrit Word Study

kṛtajñatā (कृतज्ञता)

Pronunciation: kri-ta-GYAH-taa

Meaning: gratitude, being thankful, remembering kindness

Example: When Maya showed kṛtajñatā by making thank you flowers, everyone felt happy.

Story from the Scriptures

The Grateful Squirrel — Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda

Long ago, Lord Rama needed to cross the ocean to save Sita. The monkey army was building a big bridge to Lanka. Strong monkeys carried huge rocks and threw them into the water.

A tiny squirrel watched from a tree. "I want to help Lord Rama too," she thought. "But I am so small. The monkeys are so big and strong."

The squirrel felt sad because she could not carry big rocks like the monkeys. She sat on a branch and watched them work.

Then the squirrel had an idea. "I may be small, but I can still help!" She ran down to the beach.

The squirrel picked up tiny pebbles in her mouth. She carried them to the bridge. She put the small stones in the gaps between the big rocks.

Some monkeys laughed at her. "Your tiny stones will not help," they said. "Go away, little squirrel."

But the squirrel did not give up. She kept working. Because she was grateful to Lord Rama, she wanted to help however she could.

Lord Rama saw the squirrel working hard. He picked her up gently. "Thank you, little friend," he said. "Your small stones are very important. They fill the gaps and make the bridge strong."

Lord Rama blessed the squirrel. Three white stripes appeared on her back. "Everyone will remember your grateful service," he said.

The squirrel was so happy. She learned that when you are thankful, even small help is precious. Because of her gratitude, Lord Rama blessed her forever.

A Story from Today

Maya's Thank You Garden

Maya was seven years old and lived in a small town in Kenya. Every morning, her mother made her breakfast. Her teacher helped her read new words. Her friend Kesi shared crayons with her.

But Maya always forgot to say thank you. When her mother gave her food, Maya just ate it. When her teacher helped her, Maya just nodded. When Kesi shared crayons, Maya just took them.

One day, Maya's grandmother got very sick. Maya felt scared and sad. "Grandma always tells me stories," Maya thought. "She always hugs me when I am sad. What if she cannot do that anymore?"

That night, Maya could not sleep. She thought about all the people who helped her every day. Her mother cooked for her. Her father walked her to school. Her teacher was patient when she made mistakes.

"I never say thank you," Maya realized. "But they do so much for me every day."

The next morning, Maya had an idea. She took her crayons and paper. She drew a big flower and wrote "THANK YOU MAMA" on it. She gave it to her mother.

Her mother smiled so big. "This is beautiful, Maya! Thank you for thanking me."

Maya felt warm inside. Because saying thank you made her mother happy, Maya wanted to do it more.

At school, Maya drew another flower for her teacher. "Thank you for helping me read," she said.

Her teacher hugged her. "You are so sweet, Maya."

Maya drew flowers for Kesi, for the school cook, and for her father. Soon her room had a whole garden of thank you flowers on the wall.

When Grandma got better, she saw Maya's thank you garden. "My dear Maya," she said, "your grateful heart has made the most beautiful garden of all."

Core Teaching

  1. Everything good in your life is a gift from God, your family, and other people who care about you.
  2. When you say thank you with your heart, it makes both you and others feel happy and loved.
  3. Even small acts of gratitude, like a smile or a drawing, can show how much you care about someone's kindness.
