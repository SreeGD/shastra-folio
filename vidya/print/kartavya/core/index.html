Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 3.35

श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात्।
स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः॥

śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt sva-dharme nidhanaṁ śreyaḥ para-dharmo bhayāvahaḥ

Word-for-word: śreyān — better; sva-dharmaḥ — one's own work; viguṇaḥ — done imperfectly; para-dharmāt — than another's work; su-anuṣṭhitāt — done perfectly; sva-dharme — in one's own work; nidhanam — finishing; śreyaḥ — is better; para-dharmaḥ — another's work; bhaya-āvahaḥ — brings fear.

Translation: It is better to do YOUR own work — even a little clumsily — than to do someone else's work perfectly. Do the job that is yours. That is the safe and happy path.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.3.46

शमो दमस्तपः शौचं सन्तोषः क्षान्तिरार्जवम्।
मद्भक्तिश्च दया सत्यं ब्रह्मचर्यमथापि च॥

śamo damas tapaḥ śaucaṁ santoṣaḥ kṣāntir ārjavam mad-bhaktiś ca dayā satyaṁ brahmacaryam athāpi ca

Translation: Peacefulness, self-control, doing one's own small work, cleanliness, contentment, patience, simplicity, devotion to Me, kindness, and truthfulness — these are the duties of every good person.

What is Kartavya?

Kartavya means the work that is MINE to do. As a child, my kartavya is to study, to help at home, to be kind to friends, to care for my body and my things. Doing the little job that belongs to me — well and happily — is kartavya. When I do my own job with a glad heart, I am serving Krishna.

What it is NOT

Kartavya is not doing only the fun parts and running away from the boring parts. It is not waiting to be asked three times. It is not copying what another child is doing so I can skip my own job. Kartavya is not saying 'but I don't feel like it' when I already know what to do.

  • Leaving my shoes in the middle of the room because 'someone else will pick them up'
  • Not finishing my homework because my friend does not finish hers
  • Pretending not to hear when Amma calls me to help

Sanskrit Word Study

kartavya (कर्तव्य)

Pronunciation: kar-ta-vya

Meaning: The work that is MINE to do. My own little job — studying, helping, caring — done happily and on time.

Example: Putting my plate in the sink is my kartavya, and I do it with a smile.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Krishna and the Sleepy Calves — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 10 (Gopala-lila), adapted for primary students

Long ago in beautiful Vrindavan, little Krishna lived with Mother Yashoda. He was only seven years old. His skin was the colour of a monsoon cloud. His eyes were full of laughter.

Every morning before the sun rose, Krishna had a job. His father Nanda Maharaja had said, 'My son, you are a cowherd boy. The baby calves are YOUR friends. It is your kartavya to care for them.'

One cool morning, Krishna woke up and blinked. The blanket was soft. The bed was warm. Outside, the stars were still twinkling.

'Oh,' Krishna yawned. 'I will sleep a little more. The calves can wait.'

He closed His eyes.

But far away in the cow shed, Krishna heard a tiny sound. One small calf was crying — 'Maaa... Maaa...' Another joined in. Then another. They were cold. They were hungry. They were waiting for their morning friend.

Krishna sat up. 'They are waiting for ME.'

He did not grumble. He did not ask to be asked twice. He put on His little yellow dhoti. He tied the peacock feather in His hair. He picked up His small pot of warm milk.

Krishna ran to the shed. He sang softly, 'Gopala is coming, little ones.'

The calves jumped up with happy tails. Krishna washed their noses with a soft cloth. He gave each one a little drink of warm milk. He walked them gently to their mothers — one by one.

Mother Yashoda watched from the doorway. Her heart was full.

When Krishna came back, she asked, 'Was it hard to get up, my son?'

Krishna smiled. 'A little. But the calves are my kartavya. If I do my job happily, everyone is happy — even Krishna.'

And that morning, the whole of Vrindavan shone brighter — because the Lord Himself had cheerfully done His small morning duty.

A Story from Today

Meera and the Plate Tower

Meera was seven years old. She loved two things most in the world — drawing with her big box of crayons, and eating Amma's crispy dosas.

There was one thing Meera did NOT love. After every meal, she was supposed to carry her plate to the kitchen sink. Every time.

Meera thought plates were boring. Plates had no colour. Plates made her fingers sticky. So every day after lunch, she jumped up and ran to her crayons. The plate stayed on the table, like a lonely little island.

'Meera!' Amma would call. 'Your plate.'

'Coming!' Meera would shout. But she never came.

One Saturday, Amma came home from the market. Her shopping bag was heavy. Her face looked tired. She sat down on the sofa and closed her eyes for a moment.

Meera was drawing a peacock. She looked up. She saw Amma's tired face. She looked at the lunch table. Her plate was still there from the morning — a tiny tower of forgotten rice.

Something in Meera's chest felt funny. Not guilty-bad. Just — awake.

Her teacher's words came back to her. 'Children, even a small job, done for your family with love, is a small puja. Krishna sees it.'

Meera put down her crayon. She tiptoed to the table. Quietly, she picked up her plate. She carried it carefully — like it was a flower for the altar — all the way to the sink.

She washed it with soap. She wiped it with a cloth. She placed it gently on the rack.

No one saw. Amma was still resting.

But inside Meera, something sparkled. It was a clean, happy, strong feeling. Like she had grown a little bigger.

She whispered, 'Krishna, this plate is for You.'

From that day, Meera's plate was never lonely again. And every time she carried it, the sparkly feeling came back — because doing her own small job had become her daily kartavya.

Core Teaching

  1. Kartavya is the work that is MINE to do. Not my friend's work. Not my sister's work. Just my own little job — to study, to help, to care for my things. When I do MY job, the world works beautifully.
  2. We do our kartavya without waiting to be reminded. We do it with a smile, not a grumpy face. We do it even when it is boring. Even Krishna Himself did His small morning job as a cowherd boy.
  3. Every small duty — picking up my plate, finishing my homework, feeding the dog — is a little puja for Krishna. When I do my job happily, everyone is happy, and Krishna smiles in my heart.
When we do our small jobs joyfully, a steady strength grows inside us — and that steady strength that helps us keep going is called dhriti.
