Value at a Glance

Kartavya means the work that is MINE to do. As a child, my kartavya is to study, to help at home, to be kind to friends, to care for my body and my things. Doing the little job that belongs to me — well and happily — is kartavya. When I do my own job with a glad heart, I am serving Krishna.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 3.35: It is better to do YOUR own work — even a little clumsily — than to do someone else's work perfectly. Do the job that is yours. That is the safe and happy path.

What it is NOT

Kartavya is not doing only the fun parts and running away from the boring parts. It is not waiting to be asked three times. It is not copying what another child is doing so I can skip my own job. Kartavya is not saying 'but I don't feel like it' when I already know what to do.

  • Leaving my shoes in the middle of the room because 'someone else will pick them up'
  • Not finishing my homework because my friend does not finish hers
  • Pretending not to hear when Amma calls me to help

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Krishna and the Sleepy Calves — Long ago in beautiful Vrindavan, little Krishna lived with Mother Yashoda. He was only seven years old. His skin was the colour of a monsoon cloud. His eyes were full of laughter. Every morning before the sun rose, Krishna had a job. His father Nanda Maharaja had said, 'My son, you are a cowherd boy. The baby calves are YOUR friends. It is your kartavya to care for them.'

Meera and the Plate Tower — Meera was seven years old. She loved two things most in the world — drawing with her big box of crayons, and eating Amma's crispy dosas. There was one thing Meera did NOT love. After every meal, she was supposed to carry her plate to the kitchen sink. Every time.

The Sanskrit Word

kartavya (कर्तव्य) — kar-ta-vya

Meaning: The work that is MINE to do. My own little job — studying, helping, caring — done happily and on time.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each day this week, pick THREE small duties that are yours — for example, making your bed, putting shoes on the rack, and clearing your plate. Do all three WITHOUT being asked. Colour a square on your Daily Duty Chart each night. Also: when Amma or Appa asks you to help with something, say 'Yes, coming!' and come right away — not after three reminders. At bedtime, whisper: 'Krishna, my little jobs today were for You.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child has coloured at least 5 of the 7 squares, can name their three daily duties without looking at the chart, and the parent reports at least one moment of unreminded helping.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child has coloured at least 5 of the 7 squares, can name their three daily duties without looking at the chart, and the parent reports at least one moment of unreminded helping.

When we do our small jobs joyfully, a steady strength grows inside us — and that steady strength that helps us keep going is called dhriti.
