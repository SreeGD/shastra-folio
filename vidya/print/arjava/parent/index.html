Value at a Glance

Arjava means being simple and straight. What is inside our heart is the same as what we say with our mouth and do with our hands. No hiding, no pretending, no two faces. Our heart is clear like fresh water — Krishna can see right through it, and so can our friends.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.1: Be brave. Keep your heart clean. Sit in wisdom. Give freely. Stay calm. Offer your work. Study holy words. Do hard good things. And be simple and straight — the way you feel inside is the way you show outside.

What it is NOT

Arjava is not telling little lies to escape trouble. It is not smiling outside when we feel angry inside and planning something sneaky. It is not acting good only when the teacher is watching. Being simple is brave — being tricky is never strong.

  • Saying 'I finished my homework' when really we have not
  • Saying sorry with your mouth but still feeling angry in your heart
  • Pretending to like a friend's drawing but whispering mean things behind them

Stories Your Child Heard

Little Dhruva Asks Straight from the Heart — Long ago there was a prince named Dhruva. He was five years old, with big eyes and a small, honest face. One evening Dhruva ran to his father, King Uttanapada. He wanted to sit on his lap. But his stepmother was already there. She looked at Dhruva coldly. 'This lap is not for you. You were not born from me.'

The Blue Paint on Meera's Dress — Meera's Amma had stitched her a brand-new white dress for her seventh birthday, with tiny pink flowers at the hem. Meera loved it so much she wore it to school the very next day. In art class, the teacher gave everyone pots of poster paint. Meera was painting a peacock. Right at the tail feathers — CRASH! Her elbow bumped the pot. A big blot of blue paint jumped onto her lovely dress.

The Sanskrit Word

ārjava (आर्जव) — AAR-ja-va

Meaning: Being simple and straight. Our heart, our words, and our actions all match — no hiding, no pretending.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, each night before bed, sit for one minute with Amma or Appa. Place your hand on your heart. Ask yourself one question: 'Did I say and do today only what I really felt inside?' If yes, draw a clean heart in your diary. If there is one thing you hid or pretended, gently say it out loud to Amma or Appa — OR just whisper it to Krishna and let it go. Also: any time you feel 'stones in your tummy' during the day, stop and ask, 'Is something hidden here?'

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least two moments they chose to speak straight instead of tangle, and can describe the difference between the 'heavy tummy' feeling of hiding and the 'light chest' feeling of simplicity.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can name at least two moments they chose to speak straight instead of tangle, and can describe the difference between the 'heavy tummy' feeling of hiding and the 'light chest' feeling of simplicity.

When our heart is clean and straight, the true words that come out of it have a special name — and that word is satya.
