Samata

समता · sa-ma-TAA

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Samata means treating everyone with equal kindness — rich or poor, friend or stranger, the one who wins and the one who loses. We see the same shining soul inside every person. Our smile is the same for all.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 6.32 — O Arjuna, the one who sees every person the same way they see themselves — feeling another's joy and sadness as their own — that friend of Krishna is the very best.

Core Teaching

  1. Samata means we give the same kindness to everyone — rich and poor, new and old, the child with many toys and the child with few. Every soul shines the same inside.
  2. When we see someone alone or different, we do not laugh or walk past. We sit down next to them with the same smile we give our best friend.
  3. Krishna sees every living being equally — the king and the sweeper, the tiger and the ant. When we love everyone equally, our heart becomes like Krishna's heart.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin with a circle. Hold up two objects — a bright new pencil and an old pencil-stub. Ask: 'Can both pencils write?' Yes! Then ask: 'Which one deserves a gentle hand?' Let children say 'both'. Now say: 'Children are like pencils — new ones, old ones, bright, simple. But every single one has the same soul shining inside. That is called samata — equal eyes, equal heart. Today we learn how to see with these equal eyes.' Chant 'samata, samata, samata' three times together, palms at heart. Then ask: 'Can you think of one person you sometimes forget to smile at?' Take 2-3 short answers gently.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Intro circle + two-pencil demo + chant. 10 min — Read aloud: Rama and the Old Woman of the Forest. Pause and let one child mime 'tasting a berry before giving'. 8 min — Read aloud: Ananya's Lunchbox. Ask 'who was alone at lunch in YOUR class last week?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Same Kindness Circle) and Activity 2 (Equal Eyes drawing). 5 min — Closing chant and the Same-Smile Challenge take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, two-pencil demo, chant 'samata'. 20 min — Rama and Shabari story with a small berry bowl prop; children take turns 'offering' a berry to a class Rama doll. 20 min — Ananya's Lunchbox; act it out as a short skit with volunteers. 20 min — Activity 1 (Same Kindness Circle) and Activity 2 (Equal Eyes drawing). 20 min — Paired Sit-Beside Role-Play (Activity 4); rotate partners twice so every child partners with someone new. 15 min — The Equal-Sun Craft: each child draws a big yellow sun; under it, draws five different children (tall, short, many colours, many abilities) all smiling. 10 min — Circle of Names: go around, each child says one kind thing about the classmate on their LEFT (not best friend — left). 5 min — Closing: each child names one person they will smile at tomorrow.

Discussion Guide

Primary children experience samata through EVERYDAY moments — who they sit with, who they smile at, who they do not notice. Keep examples concrete and visible: the child with old shoes, the child who eats alone, the visiting cousin. Use the 'sunshine test': 'The sun shines the same on every head — tall, short, dusty, clean. Can our smile be like the sun?' Avoid discussions of caste or economic class as abstractions — instead focus on observable classroom behaviours. Watch for the child who sits alone — never spotlight them; instead build pair-and-share activities that quietly include them. Children learn samata by BEING partnered with everyone in turn — when the teacher herself models random, fair pairings. If a child is excluded at lunch, the teacher models the move: sits beside the child themselves, cheerfully, without explanation. Other children follow.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Samata means I must like everyone the same amount.' Response: Not at all. You can have best friends. Samata means we give the same KINDNESS to everyone — same smile, same hello, same seat to share. Favourites inside your heart are fine; unequal kindness is not.
  • Misconception: 'If I am kind to everyone, bad people will also hurt me.' Response: Samata means kindness, not danger. We can be kind to everyone AND go to a grown-up if someone hurts us. Kindness and safety travel together.
  • Misconception: 'Some people are more important than others because they have more money or bigger houses.' Response: Rama the prince sat on an old tribal mother's little mat as if it were a throne. Krishna loves a rich king and a barefoot sweeper exactly the same. Money does not change the soul inside.
