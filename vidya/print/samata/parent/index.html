Value at a Glance

Samata means treating everyone with equal kindness — rich or poor, friend or stranger, the one who wins and the one who loses. We see the same shining soul inside every person. Our smile is the same for all.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 6.32: O Arjuna, the one who sees every person the same way they see themselves — feeling another's joy and sadness as their own — that friend of Krishna is the very best.

What it is NOT

Samata is not being friends with only the popular kids. It is not changing our kindness based on who is watching. It is not being nice to people who give us things and mean to people who don't.

  • Playing only with the children who have new toys
  • Saying 'hello' loudly to the principal but not to the sweeper
  • Being kind to a friend when they win but cold when they lose

Stories Your Child Heard

Rama and the Old Woman of the Forest — Long ago, in the deep green forest, a prince named Rama walked with His brother Lakshmana. Their feet were dusty. The sun was hot. Deep in the trees lived an old woman named Shabari. She was from a forest tribe. Her hair was white as cotton. Her hut was tiny. For many years she had waited — just to see Rama one day.

Ananya's Lunchbox — Ananya was seven. She went to a bright school in Pune. At lunch, Ananya sat with the 'pink-tiffin girls'. Their lunchboxes were bright pink. Their snacks were fancy. They laughed a lot.

The Sanskrit Word

samatā (समता) — sa-ma-TAA

Meaning: Equal vision. Seeing and treating everyone with the same kindness, because the same soul shines in every heart.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, play the Same-Smile Game. Every morning, choose THREE people at home, at school, or in your colony — one person you know well, one you know a little, and one you hardly know. Give each one the SAME warm smile and a 'good morning'. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa who the three people were today. Also: when you meet a new child, sit next to them at least once and share something from your bag — a crayon, a snack, a sticker.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name 21 different people (three per day) they greeted with the same warm smile, and can describe at least one new friendship they made by sitting beside someone they had not noticed before.

Signs of Growth
