Samata Worksheet — Equal Eyes, Equal Heart

Activity 1: Same Kindness Circle (Sort-and-Stick)

Your teacher will give you picture cards. Look at each child in the picture and put the card in the SAME basket — because every child deserves the SAME kindness.

1. A child with a big new bicycle. 🙂

2. A child walking barefoot. 🙂

3. A child in a wheelchair. 🙂

4. A child laughing with many friends. 🙂

5. A child sitting alone at lunch. 🙂

(All cards go in the SAME basket! That is samata.)

Activity 2: My Equal Eyes

Draw TWO children side by side. One should look very different from the other — maybe different clothes, or a different ability, or a different colour. Above BOTH children, draw the SAME sunshine. Inside BOTH hearts, draw the SAME tiny star.

[Drawing space]

Write: 'Both have the same light inside.'

Activity 3: Chant With Me

Chant three times with your teacher, palms at your heart:

Sa-ma-tā! Sa-ma-tā! Sa-ma-tā! *Equal eyes, equal heart, equal love.*

Activity 4: The Sit-Beside Role-Play (Paired)

With a partner, practise sitting beside someone at lunch who is alone.

  • You approach: 'Hi! Can I sit here?'
  • They say: 'Okay.'
  • You: 'Want to share? I have ____.'
  • They: 'Thanks! Want some of my ____?'

Take turns. Use a warm voice.

Activity 5: Take Home — The Same-Smile Challenge

For one week, at school or in your colony, give the SAME warm smile to everyone you meet — the teacher, the cook, the watchman, the new child, the old child. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa three people you smiled at today. Count them together.

🙂 🙂 🙂 🙂 🙂

Home Practice

For one week, play the Same-Smile Game. Every morning, choose THREE people at home, at school, or in your colony — one person you know well, one you know a little, and one you hardly know. Give each one the SAME warm smile and a 'good morning'. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa who the three people were today. Also: when you meet a new child, sit next to them at least once and share something from your bag — a crayon, a snack, a sticker.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name 21 different people (three per day) they greeted with the same warm smile, and can describe at least one new friendship they made by sitting beside someone they had not noticed before.
