Parent Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Value at a Glance

Aparigraha means NOT piling up more than I need. I take just enough — one helping, not three. I keep what I truly use, and I share the rest. I do not want everyone's things to become mine. My shelf stays neat. My heart stays light. Krishna is the real owner — I am only His small helper with a small, tidy corner.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 2.71: Krishna says: The one who quietly sets down endless wanting, who stops saying 'mine, mine, mine', and who walks through life with a light heart — that person reaches real peace.

What it is NOT

Aparigraha is not having nothing. It is not throwing everything away in one big dramatic pile. It is not being stingy with what we DO have. It is not bragging about how little we own. True aparigraha is QUIET. It is just — enough. Not more. Not less.

  • Asking for the new toy on the shelf when I already have twenty toys at home that I do not play with
  • Keeping every single pencil, sticker, and paper scrap in a drawer 'just in case', while a friend has none
  • Crying because a cousin got a gift and saying 'But I WANT one TOO!' when I already have plenty

Stories Your Child Heard

The Rishi Who Owned Only a Bowl — Long ago, in a green forest by a small river, a kind old rishi lived in a tiny hut. He had only three things. One cotton cloth to wear. One mat to sleep on. And ONE round wooden bowl. Every morning he walked to the river, washed his bowl, and filled it with a little rice and some fruit. He ate, drank water, and sang Krishna's names all day. His eyes were bright. His heart was light.

Aarav's Crowded Shelf — Aarav was seven years old. His toy shelf was FULL. So full that toys fell off every time he opened it. There were three red cars. Four blue trucks. Two teddy bears with missing ears. A pile of blocks. A puzzle with lost pieces. A robot whose battery had been dead for a year. And somewhere — deep down — his favourite: a bright red fire engine.

The Sanskrit Word

aparigraha (अपरिग्रह) — ah-pa-ree-GRA-ha

Meaning: Not piling up. Taking just enough, not more. Keeping what I truly use, and sharing the rest. The small tidy corner.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

This week, set up your Share Box at home. Each day, walk around your room and find ONE thing you do not really use — a toy, a book, a piece of clothing that is too small, a sticker you never used. Put it gently in the Share Box. Also practice the One-Helping Plate at every meal — take just enough first, ask only if you are still hungry. At bedtime, whisper: 'Krishna, I kept just enough today. The rest is Yours to share.' At week's end, give the Share Box to someone who needs what is inside.

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the Share Box has at least 5-7 items ready to donate, the child has eaten one helping first at most meals, and can name at least 2 specific things they DID NOT pile up (a toy not asked for at the shop, a second sweet not reached for).

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the Share Box has at least 5-7 items ready to donate, the child has eaten one helping first at most meals, and can name at least 2 specific things they DID NOT pile up (a toy not asked for at the shop, a second sweet not reached for).

When our hand takes only what we need, we naturally feel quietly full inside — and that peaceful, satisfied feeling has its own beautiful name: santosha.
