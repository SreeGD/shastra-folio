Apaishunam

अपैशुनम् · a-pai-SHU-nam

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Apaishunam means NOT finding fault in others and NOT whispering mean things about them behind their backs. When I see someone's mistake, I do not run to tell the whole class. I do not make a circle with my friends and whisper 'did you SEE what she did?' I close my mouth kindly. I see the good FIRST. If something is really important, I go straight to a grown-up I trust — I do not gossip. Krishna loves the child whose mouth is a safe place for other people's little mistakes.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 16.2 — Krishna tells Arjuna the shining marks of a divine heart. One of them is apaiśunam — NO fault-finding. When I see someone's mistake, I do not run to tell everyone. I do not whisper behind their back. I see the good first. My mouth keeps the little faults of others safe, like a closed hand keeps a small bird safe.

Core Teaching

  1. Apaishunam means NOT whispering mean things about other people behind their back. When I hear my friends making a whisper-circle about someone who is not there, I do not join. I say softly, 'Let us not talk about them.' I see the good in the other person FIRST. Krishna names apaishunam among the shining gifts of a divine heart in Gita 16.2 — right next to truth and non-hurting.
  2. Apaishunam means my mouth is a SAFE PLACE for other people's little mistakes. When Priya spills her water, I do not rush to tell Mala 'did you SEE Priya?!' When Ravi forgets his lunch, I do not giggle about it at the bus stop. Like Vidura, I keep their tiny wrongs safe inside me. Even the bird knows it is safe in a gentle closed hand — so a friend's small mistake is safe in a gentle closed mouth.
  3. Apaishunam does NOT mean hiding big bad things. If someone is being bullied, or something is really wrong, I go STRAIGHT to a grown-up I trust — not in whispers, but quietly and directly. That is different from gossip. Gossip is when we share someone's small mistake for fun with people who do not need to know. Apaishunam only closes the mouth against gossip — never against the truth that keeps a friend safe.

Five-Minute Introduction

Bring two props: a soft feather (or small paper bird) and a thin spool of red thread. Hold up the feather. 'Children, imagine this feather is something PRIVATE about a friend — a small mistake they made, a silly trip, a wrong answer. I hold it gently in my closed hand. Nobody sees. The feather stays safe. This is apaishunam — the SAFE MOUTH.' Now take the red thread. Pull a long piece out and hold both ends. 'Now imagine this thread is a whispered mean story. One friend holds this end. Another friend holds that end. Then THEY pass it to another child. And another. And another.' Walk through the circle pretending to pass the thread from child to child. 'The thread grows LONGER. And LONGER. Until the mean story has travelled through the WHOLE school. And the person it was about? Their heart is pricked by every single passing of the thread.' Tangle the thread into a messy knot. 'And now try to pick up the thread and put it back. Can we? No. Whispered words are THREADS we cannot take back.' Now return to the feather. 'So — a safe mouth holds the feather gently. It does not start the thread.' Chant softly together, hands cupped near the heart: 'a-pai-shu-nam, a-pai-shu-nam, a-pai-shu-nam.' Tell the children — 'Today's word is one of Krishna's favourites. He lists it in Gita 16.2 with truth and kindness. It is called apaishunam — the mouth that does not whisper faults.'

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Feather + red-thread demonstration + 'a-pai-shu-nam' chant 3x with soft fist-at-heart. 10 min — Read aloud Vidura's story; children practise the 'finger on the lips, smile softly' gesture at the moment Vidura declines gossip. 8 min — Read aloud Meera's story; pause at the whisper-circle moment — ask 'what should Meera do?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Join the Whisper or Walk Away) + Activity 2 (Two Mouths drawing). 5 min — See-the-Good-First paired game; introduce the Safe-Mouth Book take-home. 2 min — Silent line-up — each child whispers the name of ONE classmate and one good thing about them, very softly, to themselves only.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, feather + red-thread demo, closed-fist chant. 15 min — Vidura's story with small prop re-enactment (children sit in pairs — one 'whisperer' comes to 'Vidura' with a pretend gossip, Vidura puts gentle finger to lips, 'speak to them, not about them'). 15 min — Meera's story, acted out — one child as the whisper-circle of four, one child as Meera; debrief the brave walk across the lunch room. 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2. 15 min — Paired See-the-Good-First Game (Activity 4) with rotated partners; count how many classmate-names they could see-the-good for in three minutes. 15 min — Safe-Mouth Craft: each child makes a small paper 'mouth-pocket' — a folded cardboard mouth they decorate with flowers and hearts, with a tiny paper bird tucked safely inside. 10 min — Biographical story: Guru Gobind Singh's refusal to speak ill of those who opposed him. 10 min — Closing: whole class in a silent circle, each child holds their mouth-pocket at heart level, and together they whisper — only once, only softly — 'a-pai-shu-nam' before bowing and leaving. Buffer: 10 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn apaishunam best through BODY CUES and SAFETY SIGNS, because the value is about not speaking. Use the soft fist-at-heart gesture (the 'little bird safe') every time the class closes a mouth. Use the 'zip it with love' gesture — finger crossing lips gently with a smile — when someone catches themselves before joining a whisper. Most primary children already know the difference between telling and tattling intuitively — they just need the Sanskrit name and the kind vocabulary. Be careful with TWO kinds of children. (1) The Whisper-Starter child — the one who enjoys power by pulling others into whisper-circles. Do not shame publicly. Instead, catch their mouth SAFE even once, and name it warmly — 'I saw you did not tell anyone about Ravi's mistake this morning. Your mouth was safe. That was apaishunam. I saw.' They slowly learn that the safe mouth earns more warmth than the whisper-circle. (2) The Silenced-Bully child — one who is being whispered ABOUT. Do not call out their name; just talk to the class about safe mouths. Then privately, check in with the child, and if bullying is real, intervene as a safeguarding matter, NOT as 'apaishunam teaching'. Apaishunam is for the speakers, not as a reason to silence the hurt. Very important: be scrupulous yourself — NEVER discuss one child in front of another teacher or parent within the child's hearing. Children catch apaishunam from the adult mouths around them. If YOUR mouth spreads faults, no lesson will land. Finally — distinguish apaishunam clearly from two cousins: satya (truth) and ahimsa (non-hurting). Satya says 'tell the truth when asked.' Apaishunam adds 'AND do not carry small truths about others from mouth to mouth as entertainment.' Ahimsa says 'do not hurt.' Apaishunam adds 'AND know that whispers hurt — the invisible hurt of the mouth.' Apaishunam sits between the two, like a soft quiet sibling.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Apaishunam means I should NEVER tell a grown-up anything about anyone — that would be tattling.' Response: No, no. Apaishunam is against GOSSIP — sharing someone's small mistake for fun with friends who do not need to know. It is NOT against safety-telling. If a friend is being bullied, hurt, or scared, go STRAIGHT to a trusted grown-up and tell them. That is not gossip — that is keeping your friend safe. The test is — AM I sharing this to help, or to giggle? If to help, speak up. If to giggle, close the mouth. Apaishunam is kindness, not silence about real harm.
  • Misconception: 'Whispering is just fun — it is not mean.' Response: Let us think of the feather and the thread. A whisper feels small in YOUR mouth. But it travels. It lands in the other person's heart like a tiny splinter they cannot pull out. Would you want a whisper-circle to form about YOU? If not, then no whisper-circle about anyone else. Fun-whispers are tiny splinters. Apaishunam keeps the splinters out of every heart in the class.
  • Misconception: 'But what if the mean thing I want to say is TRUE? It really happened!' Response: Yes, sometimes the thing is true. Arjun DID trip. Priya's hair WAS messy. But here is the test — are we sharing the true thing to HELP or to LAUGH? If we laugh about Arjun's trip behind his back, that is apaishunam gone — even if it is true. If Arjun needs his shoelace tied, we help him tie it, face to face, kindly. Truth that helps face-to-face is dharma. Truth that giggles behind-the-back is paishuna — the opposite of our value. A true thing becomes mean the moment it travels by whisper.
  • Misconception (for teacher/parent): 'Children just whisper — it is harmless.' Response: Actually, the primary years are when the WHISPERING HABIT either takes root or gets pruned. Children who practise apaishunam young become teenagers who do not cyberbully, who do not start WhatsApp gossip chains, who do not pull others down to feel big. Children who are allowed to whisper freely at seven grow into adults who cannot stop spreading faults at thirty. Please model apaishunam at home: never discuss other parents' failings in front of the child, never criticise a teacher by name at the dinner table, never carry a neighbour's mistake into the living room. The child catches the safe mouth from the safe mouths around them. This is one of the most POWERFUL values to teach by example, because children are listening to everything we say about everyone else.
