Worksheet

Prema Worksheet — The Sweetest Love

Activity 1: Who Lives in Your Heart? (Draw)

Hanuman had Lord Rama and Mother Sita inside his heart. Who lives inside YOUR heart?

Draw a BIG heart on a sheet of paper. Inside the heart, draw all the ones you love with prema — Amma, Papa, didi, bhaiya, your dog, your plant, your grandma, your best friend, your teacher, Krishna, the moon… draw as many as you want!

Colour the heart soft pink. Around the outside, draw little sun-rays to show the love shining out.

[Drawing space]

Activity 2: The Whisper Game (Paired)

Prema is quiet love. Sit with a partner. Take turns whispering ONE thing you love about the other — softly, gently.

Examples:

  • 'I love the way you share your crayons.'
  • 'I love your quiet laugh.'
  • 'I love that you helped me yesterday.'

Do NOT whisper anything silly. Whisper with a warm heart. Notice — does your chest feel warmer when you speak softly or loudly?

Activity 3: The Gopi Garland Craft

The gopis of Vrindavan loved Krishna so much that they wove garlands of forest flowers for Him every day — and each flower was a little whisper of 'I love you, Krishna.'

You will make a tiny prema-garland.

What you need:

  • 10 small flower shapes cut from coloured paper (ask a grown-up to help)
  • A piece of string or wool, about two hand-lengths long
  • A pencil or crayons
  • A little hole-puncher or adult help

How to make it:

1. On each flower, draw or write ONE person/creature you love.

2. Punch a small hole in the centre of each flower.

3. Thread them onto the string.

4. Tie the ends to make a loop.

5. Hang your garland near your study table or your little altar.

While you make it, think: 'Each flower is a prema-whisper. I am giving it to Krishna.'

Activity 4: The Ant and the Sugar Grain

Go outside with a grown-up. Find an ant. Place ONE tiny grain of sugar (or a crumb of bread) near it. Sit quietly. Watch the ant find it. Watch it carry it home.

While you watch, say softly in your heart: 'Little ant, I love you. Take this. Share with your ant-family.'

Answer these:

1. Did the ant know you gave the sugar? ________________

2. Did it say thank you? ________________

3. Did you need a thank-you to feel good? ________________

4. How did your heart feel while watching? ________________

(This is a tiny taste of prema — loving without needing to be thanked.)

Activity 5: The Softest Song

Sit cross-legged. Close your eyes. Place both hands on your chest.

Sing softly, three times, like a slow lullaby:

Ra-dhe Ra-dhe, Krish-na Krish-na, My little heart is Yours.

Feel the warmth under your hands. That warmth has a name — prema. It was always there. It just needed to be remembered.

Home Practice

Pick ONE living thing that needs your quiet love this week — a house plant, a pet, a neighbourhood tree, a stray kitten, a younger sibling, or your grandma. Every single day, for one week, spend 5 gentle minutes with this one being. Water the plant slowly. Stroke the pet softly. Press your palm on the tree's bark. Read a story to the baby. Massage Ajji's feet. While you do it, whisper quietly in your heart: 'I love you. Krishna loves you. I am sending you warmth.' Do NOT tell anyone you are doing this practice. Prema grows best in the quiet dark — like a seed in soil. Keep a small Prema Heart Diary: each night, draw a tiny pink heart, and inside write/draw the one you loved today. At the end of the week, you will have seven pink hearts — a little garland of prema.

Duration: 5 minutes daily for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name the creature they loved, describe how their own heart felt during those minutes (warm, full, soft, peaceful), and show 7 pink hearts in the Prema Heart Diary. Parents/teachers may notice the child being gentler with small creatures, speaking more softly to siblings, or showing unprompted small kindnesses (tucking in a sibling, watering a plant without being asked, pausing to pet a stray).
