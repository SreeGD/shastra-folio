Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Prema is the sweetest, purest love in the whole world. It is the love that gives and gives and gives — and does not ask for anything back. Prema is the love the gopis had for Krishna. It is the love Hanuman had for Rama. It is the love that makes your heart so full, so warm, so soft, that you want to share it with everyone — your mother, your pet, the little ant on the floor, and even the big tree in the garden. Prema is love that has melted into sweetness. When prema wakes up, your heart forgets itself.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 12.13-14 — Krishna says: The one who hates no one, who is a friend to every little creature, who is kind, who does not say 'mine-mine,' who is happy in pain and joy, who forgives, and who gives his whole heart to Me — that dear one is very, very dear to Me. That is the heart of prema.

Core Teaching

  1. Prema is love that GIVES and gives and gives — and does not ask for anything back. When I love Amma without wanting a chocolate in return, that is a tiny seed of prema. When I love my pet, my tree, my friend — with no 'if' and no 'because' — my little heart is practicing the biggest love in the world.
  2. Prema flows from Krishna. Krishna loves every leaf, every ant, every river, every child. When we let a little bit of that love come through us — like a window lets in sunlight — we feel SO full. Krishna says in the Gita: 'The one who is a friend to every creature, who hates no one, is very dear to Me.' When I am kind to a little ant, Krishna smiles.
  3. Prema is quiet. It does not say 'LOOK AT ME! I am loving!' It just sits, strokes the bark of a tree, hums to a sleeping baby, gives the last biscuit to the hungry dog. The loudest love is often not the realest love. The real one is soft — but oh, it is STRONG. Like Hanuman's heart. Like Anya's hand on the neem.

Five-Minute Introduction

Sit the children in a circle. Speak softly — prema is a quiet value, do not use a loud voice today. Ask: 'Who do you love very, very much?' Let each child name one person, one pet, one thing. Accept every answer with a warm smile — Amma, doll, dog, Ajji, bicycle. Then say: 'Today we will learn a very, very special word. It is the sweetest word in the whole Sanskrit book. The word is PREMA (प्रेम). Prema is the love that gives and gives and gives — and does not ask for anything back.' Place your own hand on your chest and say slowly: 'Pre-ma. The love in our hearts for Krishna, and through Krishna, for everyone.' Have the children place their hand on their own chest. Whisper — do not shout — 'Pre-ma. Pre-ma. Pre-ma.' Tell them today's first story is about a great hero — Hanuman — and what was found inside his chest. Keep the voice tender throughout the session. Prema is CAUGHT from the teacher's heart, not taught.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Soft opening circle; whisper-chant 'prema' three times with hand on chest. 10 min — Read aloud Hanuman's Open Heart; pause at the moment Hanuman opens his chest, let children gasp. 8 min — Read aloud Anya and the Old Neem Tree; ask one question: 'Did Anya win with loud words or with quiet love?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Who Lives in Your Heart) with coloured crayons. 5 min — Closing: each child whispers to the class ONE creature they will be extra kind to this week. 2 min — Hand out materials for home practice.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, soft lighting, whisper chant. 15 min — Hanuman's Open Heart, read with puppet or shadow play if possible; children mime Hanuman opening his chest. 15 min — Anya and the Old Neem Tree; pause to discuss quiet love versus loud love. 15 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Draw the Heart) with careful colouring. 15 min — Worksheet Activity 3 (Gopi Garland Craft) — thread flowers, name a loved one on each. 10 min — Short walk outside to meet the school's oldest tree; each child places a palm on the bark and whispers 'I love you, Tree-didi.' 10 min — Biographical story: Andal, the little flower-girl who loved Krishna with her whole heart. 15 min — The Ant and the Sugar Grain activity outside; children sit quietly and watch. 10 min — Closing soft-song (Radhe Radhe Krishna Krishna), hand-on-chest, slow swaying. 5 min — Hand out Prema Heart Diary for home practice.

Discussion Guide

Primary children feel prema before they can name it. Your job is to help them NOTICE what is already in their chests. Use a soft voice, soft lighting if possible, soft sitting. Never shame a child for 'not loving enough' — prema cannot be pushed; it opens like a flower. Be careful with three things: (1) Do NOT force children to say 'I love you' to specific people. Let the naming stay theirs. (2) When a child shares a complicated love — 'I love my dog, but my dog died' or 'I love Appa but he is far away' — receive it warmly. Do not rush to fix. Say 'Your love is real. It goes to him even from here.' (3) Gently distinguish prema from possession-love. If a child says 'I love my doll SO much that I won't let anyone else touch her' — smile and say 'That is a warm love. Let us also try the love that WANTS to share, not only to keep.' Model prema yourself — care for the classroom plants, speak softly to the class guinea pig, thank the cleaning uncle by name. Children absorb prema by watching your heart move. For children who seem hard-hearted or reluctant, never push. Praise ANY tiny giving — a pencil shared, a smile offered, a seat moved. The little flower opens when it is safe, not when it is shouted at. Finally — this chapter's home practice (loving a plant, a pet, or a small creature daily) is deliberately chosen so shy or lonely children can practice prema without any interpersonal risk. Celebrate these quiet practitioners as much as the outgoing ones.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Prema means I must love only the cute things — my dog, my best friend. Not the ant, not the stray cat, not the grumpy uncle.' Response: No, no, little one. True prema is like the sunlight — it falls on everyone, without choosing. Krishna loves the ant AND the elephant AND the grumpy uncle. When we love only the cute and the nice, that is a small love. Prema is the BIG love — it tries to love even the hard ones. Start with your own pet if you like, but let the love GROW outwards.
  • Misconception: 'If I love someone, they MUST love me back. Otherwise it is unfair.' Response: Prema is the love that does not count. The gopis gave their whole hearts to Krishna. Hanuman gave his whole chest. Anya loved her neem tree — and a tree cannot even say 'thank you.' The WARMTH you feel while loving IS the gift. Prema is its own reward. Krishna says this Himself in the Gita — the friend to every creature is very dear to Me.
  • Misconception: 'Love means I hug and kiss and say I love you all the time, loud.' Response: Loud love can be real — but so can quiet love. Sometimes the quietest love is the biggest. Anya did not shout. She sat. She stroked. She sang softly. That was prema. A mother's hand on a sleeping baby's head — that is prema. A grandpa's slow pat on the family dog — that is prema. Real love has a soft centre. Noise is not the measure.
  • Misconception: 'Children are too small for prema — it is for big saints and gopis only.' Response: (For the teacher to hold close) This is the biggest mistake of all. A child's heart is the MOST natural home for prema. They have not yet learned to calculate, 'what do I get in return?' When a little girl gives her whole sandwich to a hungry dog, when a little boy kisses the sleeping face of his baby brother, when a toddler pats the head of a flower — that IS prema, pure and whole. Our job as adults is not to teach prema. It is not to LET the world teach children to calculate. The prema is already there. We only need to name it, honour it, and water it.
