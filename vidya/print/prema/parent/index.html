Value at a Glance

Prema is the sweetest, purest love in the whole world. It is the love that gives and gives and gives — and does not ask for anything back. Prema is the love the gopis had for Krishna. It is the love Hanuman had for Rama. It is the love that makes your heart so full, so warm, so soft, that you want to share it with everyone — your mother, your pet, the little ant on the floor, and even the big tree in the garden. Prema is love that has melted into sweetness. When prema wakes up, your heart forgets itself.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 12.13-14: Krishna says: The one who hates no one, who is a friend to every little creature, who is kind, who does not say 'mine-mine,' who is happy in pain and joy, who forgives, and who gives his whole heart to Me — that dear one is very, very dear to Me. That is the heart of prema.

What it is NOT

Prema is not 'I love you IF you give me a toffee.' It is not 'I love you because you are mine.' It is not loving only your things and your people while being rude to others. Prema is not jealous love that wants to keep the beloved all for itself. And prema is not the pretend-love we show to get praise. Real prema is not loud. It is quiet, it is warm, and it wants only the happiness of the one it loves.

  • Saying 'I love you' only because I want a new toy
  • Being sweet to my own dog but throwing stones at a stray dog
  • Hugging Amma in front of guests but sulking when no one is watching
  • Loving only the friends who give me sweets

Stories Your Child Heard

Hanuman's Open Heart — After the great war, Lord Rama and Mother Sita came home to Ayodhya. The whole city was sparkling with little lamps. Flowers fell from every balcony. Everyone was SO happy. Lord Rama sat on His golden seat. He smiled. 'My dear ones, I want to give each friend a gift.' To Lakshmana — a ring. To Bharata — a hug. To Sugriva — a bright shawl. To every monkey and bear — sweets and shining cloths.

Anya and the Old Neem Tree — Anya lived in a small house in Pune. Right outside her bedroom window stood a big, big neem tree. It was older than Anya's grandmother. Its leaves were tiny and green. Its branches reached up to the sky like a thousand soft hands. Every morning, Anya said hello to the neem tree. 'Good morning, Neem-didi!' Every evening, she said goodnight. On hot days, she sat under its shade and read her book. When she was sad, she pressed her cheek against its rough bark. And when the wind blew, the tree rustled back like it was whispering — 'I love you too, little one.'

The Sanskrit Word

prema (प्रेम) — PRAY-ma

Meaning: The sweetest, purest love — love that only gives, love that asks nothing back, love for Krishna that flows out to every creature. The love inside Hanuman's heart. The love inside Anya's hand on the neem tree.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Pick ONE living thing that needs your quiet love this week — a house plant, a pet, a neighbourhood tree, a stray kitten, a younger sibling, or your grandma. Every single day, for one week, spend 5 gentle minutes with this one being. Water the plant slowly. Stroke the pet softly. Press your palm on the tree's bark. Read a story to the baby. Massage Ajji's feet. While you do it, whisper quietly in your heart: 'I love you. Krishna loves you. I am sending you warmth.' Do NOT tell anyone you are doing this practice. Prema grows best in the quiet dark — like a seed in soil. Keep a small Prema Heart Diary: each night, draw a tiny pink heart, and inside write/draw the one you loved today. At the end of the week, you will have seven pink hearts — a little garland of prema.

Duration: 5 minutes daily for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name the creature they loved, describe how their own heart felt during those minutes (warm, full, soft, peaceful), and show 7 pink hearts in the Prema Heart Diary. Parents/teachers may notice the child being gentler with small creatures, speaking more softly to siblings, or showing unprompted small kindnesses (tucking in a sibling, watering a plant without being asked, pausing to pet a stray).

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can name the creature they loved, describe how their own heart felt during those minutes (warm, full, soft, peaceful), and show 7 pink hearts in the Prema Heart Diary. When prema flows out of our hearts and moves our hands to help and serve others, it has another beautiful name — seva.
