Anahankarah

अनहङ्कार · an-a-han-KAA-ra

Teacher Guide

Primary 6–9 · English

A chapter from Vidyā Karaṇa · Values for Life

Govardhan Eco Village

Chapter Overview

Anahankarah is when I do something good — and I do NOT puff up and say 'I did it! Me! Me! Look at ME!' Krishna is the REAL doer. My hands are just little helper-hands. My feet are Krishna's helper-feet. My mouth says His songs. When teacher praises me, I smile and whisper, 'Krishna, You did it through me.' I am happy to be His little helper — not the big hero. The 'a' at the start means 'NO,' 'aham' means 'I,' 'kara' means 'doer.' So anahankara = NO-I-did-it feeling. Just happy helper-feeling.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 13.8 — Krishna tells Arjuna the qualities of a truly wise heart. In the very next breath after this verse, He says anahaṅkāra — do NOT feel 'I DID IT! I am the big doer!' When Krishna helps your hand, your foot, your mouth, your mind — say softly, 'Krishna did it through me. I am only His little helper.'

Core Teaching

  1. Anahankara means NO 'I did it! Me!' feeling. When I do something good, I remember — Krishna is the real doer. My hands are His helper-hands. My feet are His helper-feet. My mouth sings His songs. I do not puff up. I bow my head and whisper, 'Krishna, thank You for using me today.' Krishna puts this word in His Gita list (13.8) right next to adambhitva and amānitva — they are sisters.
  2. Anahankara does NOT mean being lazy. It does NOT mean saying 'Krishna will do it, so I will not try.' I STILL try my very best. I still practise. I still work hard. A helper-hand must keep WORKING — not sit still. The only thing I leave behind is the puff-puff 'I did it!' feeling. The work stays. The puff-puff goes.
  3. Anahankara makes me happy, not sad. When I say 'I did it all by myself,' my heart fills with puff-puff — and puff-puff gets empty fast, like a balloon with a tiny hole. When I say 'Krishna did it through me,' my heart stays full and soft and bright — like a little butter-lamp that keeps glowing. Hanuman chose the lamp. Arnav chose the lamp. Krishna loves the lamp-children best.

Five-Minute Introduction

Have all the children hold up their two hands, palms up, like little bowls. 'Children — look at these hands. Whose hands are these?' Children will say 'mine!' Smile. 'Yes — AND — who made these hands? Who gave them bones? Who gave them skin? Who put the little fingers on?' Wait. Let a quiet voice come: 'Krishna.' Nod. 'Yes. Krishna made these hands. So when these hands do something good — who is really doing the work? YOU — or Krishna, THROUGH you?' Touch your own hand. 'These are helper-hands. Krishna is the doer. We are the helpers.' Now chant together softly, hands still held up: 'a-na-haṅ-kā-ra, a-na-haṅ-kā-ra, a-na-haṅ-kā-ra.' Ask: 'Who won a race lately? Who drew a nice picture? Who helped Amma?' Take 2-3 short answers — and each time the child finishes, gently ask: 'Whose hands really did the work?' Let them find the answer: 'Krishna's helper-hands — through me.' Finish by saying: 'Krishna put this word in His Gita list — anahankara. It means NO me-me-me feeling. Just happy-helper feeling. Today we will practise being happy helpers, just like little Hanuman.'

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Hands-up intro; chant 'anahankara' 3× softly. 10 min — Read aloud Hanuman's ocean-leap story; children mime 'leap — return — bow at Rama's feet.' 8 min — Read aloud Arnav and the Big Poster; pause at the principal's question and ask children 'what would YOU say?' 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Puff-Puff or Helper-Heart) and Activity 2 (Draw Your Helper-Hands). 5 min — Paired Thank-You Game in practice. 2 min — Closing bow: hands at heart, whisper 'Krishna did it through me,' introduce Helper-Hand Diary take-home.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle, hands-up intro, soft 'anahankara' chant. 15 min — Hanuman's ocean-leap story with mime (children 'leap' across the classroom, touch the far wall as Lanka, return, bow at the altar as Rama). 15 min — Arnav and the Big Poster; children mime holding a paintbrush, whisper 'Krishna, paint through my hand.' 20 min — Worksheet Activities 1 & 2 plus Helper-Hand chant practice. 15 min — Paired Thank-You Game (Activity 4) with rotated partners; debrief how different replies felt. 15 min — Art: Helper-Hand Tracing. Each child traces their two hands on paper, decorates each palm with a tiny peacock feather and the letters 'K' (for Krishna) — takes home to stick by their bed. 15 min — Biographical story: A R Rahman and his Oscar wins (or substitute). 10 min — Closing circle: each child folds hands, bows head, whispers one thing they did this week that Krishna helped them do. Buffer: 5 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children often confuse anahankara with three other values — adambhitva (no show-off), amānitva (no big-head), and vinaya (humility). These are all neighbours, but anahankara is specific. The key phrase to teach is 'helper-hands.' Adambhitva says 'don't make a big show.' Amānitva says 'don't feel big.' Vinaya says 'behave humbly with elders.' Anahankara says something deeper: 'you are not even the doer — Krishna is.' This is the metaphysical heart of the Gita. Teach it with the HANDS metaphor: 'Look at your hand. Wiggle the fingers. Who is making them wiggle? You? Or the electricity in the nerves that Krishna put in your body?' Children will giggle, and they will GET it. Watch for three classroom patterns. (1) The High Achiever who always says 'I did it!' — do NOT shame; gently ask 'and who gave you your hands / mind / Amma who feeds you?' They will slowly see. (2) The Shy Child who says 'I am nothing, Krishna is everything' as a way to hide — gently correct: 'Krishna works THROUGH you — so you are not nothing. You are precious. You are Krishna's own chosen helper.' (3) The Competitive child who can't stand sharing credit — this is where the HANUMAN story does its work; Hanuman was the strongest monkey in the world AND the most humble. Not because he was weak. Because he knew the source. Finally — TEACHERS and PARENTS must model. When YOU win praise, say aloud in front of the child: 'Krishna helped me today.' When you cook a beautiful dish, say 'Krishna's recipe.' When you solve a hard problem at work, say 'Krishna gave me the answer.' Children catch anahankara more than they are taught it. Never weaponize — 'you did nothing, Krishna did it' used to shame a child is cruel and wrong. The lesson is JOYFUL — 'Krishna and I worked TOGETHER today!'

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'Anahankara means I should never say I did anything. I must always say Krishna did it.' Response: That is a little too extreme for primary children. Say honestly what you did — but keep it un-puffed. If Amma asks 'did you clean your room?' answer 'Yes, Amma — Krishna helped me clean it.' You are not LYING. You are telling the truth AND you are acknowledging Krishna. Both honest and un-puffy. That is the real anahankara.
  • Misconception: 'If I have anahankara, I should not try hard — Krishna will do it all.' Response: NO — this is LAZY, not anahankara. Hanuman STILL leaped the ocean. He STILL burned Lanka. He STILL worked with all his strength. Arnav STILL practised painting for a whole week. The hands must WORK — the puffing must stop. Work hard, try your best, AND give all credit to Krishna. Both, not either.
  • Misconception: 'If I don't claim I did it, nobody will give me a prize.' Response: This is why Krishna places anahankara so high on His list. Because it is HARD. We are used to a world of big gold letters and everyone shouting 'ME!' But Krishna says — the quiet helper-heart is more beautiful than any gold letters. Hanuman did not want gold letters. Arnav did not want gold letters. And look — Krishna Himself wrote their names in His heart. That is the biggest gold letter of all.
  • Misconception (for teacher/parent): 'My child needs to learn self-confidence — anahankara will make them doubt themselves.' Response: Anahankara BUILDS deep confidence, not doubts. A child who knows Krishna works through her hand is NEVER alone. She does not need public applause to know she did well. She has a quiet inner partnership with the Divine. That is a far stronger confidence than any 'I am the best!' boasting. Watch Hanuman — he leapt the ocean without a single boast. His confidence came from Ram inside, not from his own big muscles.
