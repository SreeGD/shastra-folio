Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 13.8

अमानित्वमदम्भित्वमहिंसा क्षान्तिरार्जवम्।
आचार्योपासनं शौचं स्थैर्यमात्मविनिग्रहः॥

amānitvam adambhitvam ahiṁsā kṣāntir ārjavam ācāryopāsanaṁ śaucaṁ sthairyam ātma-vinigrahaḥ

Word-for-word: amānitvam — no big-head feeling; adambhitvam — no show-off; ahiṁsā — no hurting; kṣāntiḥ — patience; ārjavam — straight-honest; ācārya-upāsanam — honouring the teacher; śaucam — cleanness; sthairyam — steadiness; ātma-vinigrahaḥ — gentle self-control. (And a little later in this same verse-cluster, Krishna says ANAHAṄKĀRA — no 'I am the doer' feeling.)

Translation: Krishna tells Arjuna the qualities of a truly wise heart. In the very next breath after this verse, He says anahaṅkāra — do NOT feel 'I DID IT! I am the big doer!' When Krishna helps your hand, your foot, your mouth, your mind — say softly, 'Krishna did it through me. I am only His little helper.'

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 11.29.34

मर्त्योऽयं देहमाहिताच्यो
विनियुक्तश्चिदात्मनः।
हस्तौ पादौ च जिह्वा च
श्रोत्रं चक्षुर्नरस्य च॥

martyo 'yaṁ deham āhitā cyo viniyuktaś cid-ātmanaḥ hastau pādau ca jihvā ca śrotraṁ cakṣur narasya ca

Translation: Krishna tells Uddhava: 'This little body — hands and feet and tongue and ears and eyes — all of it is given to the soul so that the soul may serve Me. Let your hands be My hands. Let your feet walk to My temple. Let your tongue sing My name. Do not think I am the doer. Think — Krishna is the doer, I am His happy little helper.'

What is Anahankarah?

Anahankarah is when I do something good — and I do NOT puff up and say 'I did it! Me! Me! Look at ME!' Krishna is the REAL doer. My hands are just little helper-hands. My feet are Krishna's helper-feet. My mouth says His songs. When teacher praises me, I smile and whisper, 'Krishna, You did it through me.' I am happy to be His little helper — not the big hero. The 'a' at the start means 'NO,' 'aham' means 'I,' 'kara' means 'doer.' So anahankara = NO-I-did-it feeling. Just happy helper-feeling.

What it is NOT

Anahankara does NOT mean being lazy or saying 'Krishna will do it, so I will not try.' NO — I still try my very best. I still practise. I still work hard. My hands are Krishna's helper-hands only when they are WORKING. Anahankara also does not mean pretending I did nothing. If Amma asks 'did you clean your room?' I say honestly, 'Yes, Amma — Krishna helped me clean it.' Honest AND not puffy. That is the real anahankara heart.

  • Winning the drawing prize and shouting 'I DID IT ALL BY MYSELF! ME! I am the best artist!'
  • Helping a friend, then running to teacher — 'Teacher, I helped! ME! I did it!'
  • Getting a good mark and thinking 'I am so clever — nobody helped me, not Amma, not Krishna, not teacher'
  • Winning a race and refusing to bow before the altar — 'These are MY legs, MY victory!'

Sanskrit Word Study

anahaṅkāra (अनहङ्कार)

Pronunciation: an-a-han-KAA-ra

Meaning: NO 'I did it!' feeling. The letter 'a' means 'NO.' 'Aham' means 'I.' 'Kara' means 'doer.' So anahankara = NO-I-am-the-doer feeling. When I know my hands are Krishna's helper-hands, my heart has anahankara. I am happy to be His little helper — not the big hero.

Example: When Hanuman said 'Prabhu, YOUR name crossed the ocean — I am only Your little helper,' that was anahankara.

Story from the Scriptures

Hanuman After the Ocean Leap — Sundara Kanda of the Ramayana (Valmiki)

Long ago, wicked Ravana stole Mother Sita across the ocean to Lanka. Lord Rama needed a helper to leap the ocean and find her.

A small brown monkey stood up — Hanuman. He bowed. He did not say 'I am strong.' He whispered, 'Prabhu, YOUR name will carry me.'

Hanuman climbed a tall mountain. Hand on heart. 'Shri Ram. Shri Ram.'

Then he leaped.

WHOOSH! He flew like an arrow. He found Mother Sita crying under a tree, gave her Ram's ring, and burned parts of Ravana's city with his fiery tail.

Then — WHOOSH! — he leaped back. The monkeys ran to meet him.

'HANUMAN! YOU are the hero! YOU are the greatest monkey ever!'

They lifted him high. They wanted him to shout 'YES! I did it!' But Hanuman's heart was too soft for those words.

He slid down and walked to Rama. He fell on his knees and pressed his forehead to the earth.

'Prabhu,' he whispered, 'I did not cross the ocean. YOUR name crossed it. I did not find Sita-Ma. YOUR ring found her. I am only a little monkey. My hands are YOUR helper-hands.'

Rama's eyes filled with tears. He picked up little Hanuman and hugged him.

'Hanuman,' Rama said softly, 'you did the biggest thing — and you do not say I did it. That is anahankara. That is the heart I love most.'

Even today, in every picture of Hanuman-ji, his hands are folded. His eyes look UP at Ram. His lips whisper 'Ram, Ram, Ram.'

Children, your hands are also helper-hands. Do not say 'I did it.' Say 'Krishna did it through me.'

That no-I-did-it heart has a name. Anahankara.

A Story from Today

Arnav and the Big Poster

Arnav, age 8, loved painting Krishna.

One week Sarla-ji clapped. 'For Janmashtami we will paint a BIG poster of baby Krishna on Kaliya. Whoever paints the most beautiful Krishna — their name goes in BIG GOLD LETTERS!'

Arnav's heart went thump-thump.

At home he painted frantically. Too small. Too big. Too red.

Nani came. 'Kanna, why so worried?'

'Nani, I want my name in gold!'

'Arnav, whose hands hold that brush?'

'Mine.'

'And who made your hands?'

'Krishna made them.'

'So whose hands are really painting?'

Arnav's eyes went soft. 'Krishna's hands. Through me.'

Nani kissed his head. 'Paint with a happy heart. Forget the gold letters.'

All week Arnav painted calmly. A soft blue baby Krishna came out — dancing on Kaliya.

On Janmashtami the principal stopped before Arnav's Krishna.

'Whose work is this?'

The children pointed. 'Arnav's!'

The puffy voice inside shouted — 'MINE!' But Nani's voice spoke: 'Whose hands held the brush?'

Arnav folded his hands. He bowed his head. Cheeks pink.

'Sir,' he said softly, 'Krishna painted Himself through my hand. I only held the brush.'

The principal's eyes went shiny. 'Son, that answer is worth more than the whole poster.'

He did not write Arnav's name in gold. At the bottom, in small neat letters, he wrote: 'Painted by Krishna, through Arnav's hand.'

That night Arnav folded his hands before the puja shelf. 'Krishna, thank You for using my hand today.'

The diya-flame wiggled. Arnav's heart felt light — like a butter-lamp on a river.

That no-I-did-it heart has a name. Anahankara.

Core Teaching

  1. Anahankara means NO 'I did it! Me!' feeling. When I do something good, I remember — Krishna is the real doer. My hands are His helper-hands. My feet are His helper-feet. My mouth sings His songs. I do not puff up. I bow my head and whisper, 'Krishna, thank You for using me today.' Krishna puts this word in His Gita list (13.8) right next to adambhitva and amānitva — they are sisters.
  2. Anahankara does NOT mean being lazy. It does NOT mean saying 'Krishna will do it, so I will not try.' I STILL try my very best. I still practise. I still work hard. A helper-hand must keep WORKING — not sit still. The only thing I leave behind is the puff-puff 'I did it!' feeling. The work stays. The puff-puff goes.
  3. Anahankara makes me happy, not sad. When I say 'I did it all by myself,' my heart fills with puff-puff — and puff-puff gets empty fast, like a balloon with a tiny hole. When I say 'Krishna did it through me,' my heart stays full and soft and bright — like a little butter-lamp that keeps glowing. Hanuman chose the lamp. Arnav chose the lamp. Krishna loves the lamp-children best.
When the helper-hand heart of anahankara grows steady, my work itself becomes an offering — done fully with love, but without clinging to the reward. That next beautiful quality has its own name: niṣkāma karma.
