Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 9.22

अनन्याश्चिन्तयन्तो मां ये जनाः पर्युपासते।
तेषां नित्याभियुक्तानां योगक्षेमं वहाम्यहम्॥

ananyāś cintayanto māṁ ye janāḥ paryupāsate teṣāṁ nityābhiyuktānāṁ yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham

Word-for-word: ananyāḥ — only Me; cintayantaḥ — always thinking; mām — of Me; ye — those; janāḥ — people; paryupāsate — love and serve; teṣām — of them; nitya — always; abhiyuktānām — who are close to Me; yoga-kṣemam — what they need and their safety; vahāmi — I carry; aham — I.

Translation: Krishna says: Those who love Me and remember Me always — I Myself carry what they need and I Myself keep them safe. I am their Protector, and no harm can reach them without passing through Me.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 10.25.19

तदोपनिन्ये तद्गोपा वर्षे मासे च विह्वलाः।
कृष्णस्य शक्तिमखिलां निरीक्ष्य तमगायन्॥

tadopaninye tad-gopā varṣe māse ca vihvalāḥ kṛṣṇasya śaktim akhilāṁ nirīkṣya tam agāyan

Translation: When the cowherd people saw how Krishna — small, smiling, seven years old — had sheltered them all under Govardhan for days, they sang of His wonderful protecting power with wide-open eyes and full hearts.

What is Raksha?

Raksha means protecting — using my hands, my voice, and my love to keep someone smaller or weaker safe. I can protect my baby sister. I can protect a little puppy. I can protect a plant. I can stand beside a friend who is being teased. When I protect, I become Krishna's helper in the world. Krishna protects everyone — and I can be one of His helpers.

What it is NOT

Raksha is not only about NOT hurting — it is about DOING something when someone needs help. It is not turning my face away. It is not saying 'not my problem.' It is not being bossy or squeezing someone in the name of keeping them safe. Raksha is gentle, brave, and ready.

  • Walking past a classmate being laughed at and saying nothing
  • Watching a friend step on a tiny kitten and not speaking up
  • Saying 'I am protecting you' but really controlling a younger sibling

Sanskrit Word Study

rakṣā (रक्षा)

Pronunciation: RAHK-shaa

Meaning: Protection. Keeping someone smaller or weaker safe with brave, gentle hands. Being Krishna's helper in the world.

Example: When Arjun lifted the shivering puppy out of the drain, that was raksha.

Story from the Scriptures

Krishna Lifts Govardhan Hill to Shelter the Whole Village — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 10, Chapters 24-25

In a green village called Vrindavan, little Krishna was seven years old. He loved the cows and the cowherd boys with all His heart.

One year, Krishna asked the villagers to do a special puja — not for Indra the rain-king, but for Govardhan Hill, who gave them grass, water, and shade.

Indra became very angry. 'How dare they worship a hill instead of ME!'

He sent a terrible storm. Black clouds rolled over the village. Rain fell like spears. Lightning crashed. Hail the size of coconuts dropped from the sky. Rivers overflowed. The little calves began to slip. The babies in the arms of the mothers began to cry.

'Krishna!' the villagers cried. 'Krishna, save us!'

Little Krishna did not run. Little Krishna smiled.

He walked to the foot of Govardhan Hill. He placed His little left hand under the hill — the hand of a seven-year-old child. And He lifted.

The whole hill came up! Up and up it rose, like a giant green umbrella.

'Everyone — come under!' Krishna called. 'Bring the cows! Bring the calves! Bring Grandma and the little ones!'

Every cow, every calf, every child, every grown-up, every grandmother ran under Govardhan Hill. The rain beat on top. Not one drop reached them.

For seven days and seven nights, Krishna held up the whole hill on His tiny finger. He did not tire. He smiled the whole time.

Finally Indra understood. He bowed at Krishna's feet. 'Forgive me, Lord. You are the true Protector.'

Krishna gently placed Govardhan back. Every cow, every child walked out — dry and safe.

This is raksha. When the small and afraid cry out — the one with a big heart lifts a whole mountain for them.

A Story from Today

Arjun and the Puppy in the Rain

Arjun was walking home from school. He was in Standard Two. It was starting to rain.

The other children ran ahead, splashing. Arjun walked slower. He liked to look at things.

That is when he heard it. A tiny, shaky sound — like a baby crying, but smaller.

Arjun stopped. The sound came from the big concrete drain pipe at the edge of the road.

He bent down and peered inside. Two little brown eyes looked back. A tiny puppy was stuck — soaking wet, shivering, paws slipping on the pipe.

Arjun's heart went BUMP-BUMP. In a few minutes the drain would fill with water.

'Arjun, hurry up!' his friend Vikram shouted from far ahead.

Arjun looked at the puppy. He looked at his friends running away.

'Just a minute, Vikram!' Arjun called back.

He knelt in the wet road. 'It's okay, little one,' he whispered. 'I am here.'

The puppy's tail gave one tiny wag.

Arjun rolled up his sleeves. He reached his small hand into the pipe. Very, very slowly — so as not to scare him — Arjun lifted the puppy out.

He pulled off his school sweater and wrapped the puppy inside. Arjun walked home in his wet shirt, the puppy warm against his chest.

'Amma!' he called at the door. 'Please — he needs help.'

Amma saw Arjun — wet, muddy, cold. She saw the small bundle in his arms. She saw his face — so careful, so brave.

'Oh, my protector-boy,' Amma said softly. 'Come in. Let us dry you both.'

That night, the puppy slept on an old shawl by the kitchen, warm and full of milk. Arjun lay in bed smiling.

'Krishna,' he whispered. 'I helped one little one today. Thank You for sending him to me.'

This is raksha. Stopping when others hurry past. Kneeling down. Being Krishna's hands.

Core Teaching

  1. Raksha means PROTECTING — using my hands, my voice, my body to keep someone smaller or weaker safe. When I see a younger child being left out, a hurt animal, a plant being broken, a friend being teased — I do not look away. I help. That is raksha.
  2. Raksha is doing, not just not-hurting. Ahimsa keeps my own hands gentle. Raksha makes my hands REACH OUT. A person who only says 'I did not hurt anyone' is good. A person who stops the hurt when they see it is a raksha-helper of Krishna.
  3. When little Krishna lifted Govardhan Hill on His tiny finger, He showed us that even a small one can protect a whole village with a big heart. I am small. But my heart can be big. Each time I protect, Krishna is smiling in my heart and lifting the hill with me.
When our hands protect with love, we become brave without being rough — and that brave, gentle strength has its own beautiful name: saurya.
