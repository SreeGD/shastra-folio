Value at a Glance

Viveka means stopping to think before we act. We ask our heart two small questions: 'Is this true?' and 'Is this good for me and others?' Viveka is the little voice inside that helps us tell real from pretend, and right from wrong. It is the swan inside us who can drink the milk and leave the water.

The Verse

Katha Upanishad 1.2.2: Two things come to a child: what is GOOD and what is FUN-right-now. The wise child stops, thinks, and chooses the good. The hurrying child grabs the fun and forgets to think.

What it is NOT

Viveka is not grabbing the first shiny thing we see. It is not copying what everyone else does without thinking. It is not choosing only what is fun-right-now and forgetting what comes later. When the fast-feeling says 'GO!', viveka whispers 'Wait. Think. Then choose.'

  • Eating all the sweets before dinner because they LOOK so yummy
  • Copying a friend's answer on a worksheet because it is faster
  • Saying 'yes' to a dare without asking 'is this safe? is this kind?'

Stories Your Child Heard

Nachiketa and the Two Gifts — Long, long ago, a boy named Nachiketa lived in a small hut with his father. Nachiketa was only eight, but his eyes were bright and his heart was brave. One day Nachiketa went on a special journey and came before Yama, the great lord of time. Yama was tall and kind. He looked at the small boy and smiled.

Meera and the Answer Sheet — Meera's class was writing a maths test. The room was very quiet. The fan was spinning. Meera's pencil tapped on her desk. She was stuck on question four. 3 × 7. She knew 3 × 6, but her mind went blank.

The Sanskrit Word

viveka (विवेक) — vi-VAY-ka

Meaning: Stopping to think and choosing wisely. Telling real from pretend. Telling good from fun-now. The little swan inside who drinks milk and leaves water.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Carry your Pause Pebble in your pocket every day for one week. Whenever you feel the fast-feeling (wanting to grab, shout, copy, or hurry), touch the pebble, take one slow breath, and ask: 'Is this TRUE? Is this GOOD for my heart?' Then choose. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa about ONE moment today when the pebble helped you pause. Whisper to your swan inside, 'Thank you for helping me see.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific moments when they paused before acting, and can demonstrate the two-question routine (hand on heart, one breath, two questions) without prompting.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific moments when they paused before acting, and can demonstrate the two-question routine (hand on heart, one breath, two questions) without prompting.

When we learn to stop and choose the true, we stop running after shiny things that do not last — and that gentle letting-go is called vairagya.
