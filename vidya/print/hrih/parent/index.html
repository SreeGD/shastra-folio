Value at a Glance

Hrih is the soft quiet inside me. When I do something good, I don't SHOUT about it — I hold it like a little diya inside my chest, gently, so it keeps shining. When I make a mistake, I feel gentle-sorry — not hiding, not lying — just 'oh, I did that, I am sorry.' Hrih is the hush-hush voice, the little bow of the head, the pink cheeks when Amma praises me. Krishna's name for it is hrīḥ, and He loves this quality in a child.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2: Krishna names many pretty qualities of the godly heart — kindness, truth, gentleness — and right in the middle, He places hrīḥ. Hrīḥ is the soft quiet inside. It is the hush-hush smile when I do something good. It is the gentle-sorry feeling when I make a mistake. Krishna loves a child whose heart has hrīḥ.

What it is NOT

Hrih is not hiding when I have done wrong — that is a different kind of hiding, the scared-hiding. Hrih is not being so quiet that no one can see my good work at all — we can still do good, we just don't need to BRAG. And hrih is NOT feeling bad about who I am — Krishna made me lovely. Hrih is only the soft-quiet-pink-cheek feeling about MY OWN goodness and MY OWN mistakes.

  • Running around the class shouting 'I got 10 on 10! I got 10 on 10!'
  • Pretending I did not spill the milk when Amma is standing right there
  • Saying 'I am the BEST dancer in the whole class' very loudly at lunch
  • Making fun of a friend who made a mistake, instead of feeling gentle for them

Stories Your Child Heard

Young Arjuna Bows Before Dronacharya — Long ago, in the city of Hastinapura, there was a royal school. A wise teacher named Dronacharya taught the princes how to shoot arrows. There were a hundred Kaurava princes and five Pandava princes. They all came to practise every morning. One of the Pandava princes was a little boy named Arjuna. He was only eight years old. He was small. He had dark hair and bright clever eyes. And he LOVED archery. He loved the way the bow felt in his hand. He loved the whispering sound the arrow made when it flew.

Priya Wins the Drawing Prize — Priya was in Standard Two. She loved to draw. Every evening she sat in the corner of her grandmother's kitchen with a small box of crayons and drew — flowers, birds, little cows, and her favourite: Krishna standing under a tree playing His flute. One week, teacher Meena-ji announced, 'Children, our school is having an art show. Each of you may bring ONE drawing. The judges will pick the best three.'

The Sanskrit Word

hrīḥ (ह्रीः) — hree

Meaning: The soft quiet inside. The little diya in the chest. The pink-cheek feeling when I do something good. The gentle-sorry feeling when I make a mistake. A sweet quality Krishna lists among the godly heart's treasures.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Quiet Diya Diary by your bed. Every night, after Amma turns off the light, draw ONE tiny diya in your book. Under the diya, whisper (or write with a crayon): (1) one good thing you did today that you did NOT tell anyone about, and (2) one gentle-sorry you said today when you made a small mistake. If there was nothing to say sorry for, draw a little smile. Do NOT show the diary to friends — this is a secret between you, Amma, and Krishna. Before sleep, touch your chest and whisper: 'Krishna, my diya is soft tonight. Thank You for keeping it gentle.'

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 2 good things they kept quietly private and at least 2 gentle-sorry moments without shame or hiding. They can demonstrate the pink-cheek gesture (hand to cheek, small bow) while saying 'hrīḥ' softly. The diary has at least 5 little diyas drawn.

Signs of Growth

By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 2 good things they kept quietly private and at least 2 gentle-sorry moments without shame or hiding. They can demonstrate the pink-cheek gesture (hand to cheek, small bow) while saying 'hrīḥ' softly. The diary has at least 5 little diyas drawn.

When the soft quiet of hrih meets the world with a clean bright face that never pretends, that honest-shining-quietness has its own beautiful name: ārjava.
