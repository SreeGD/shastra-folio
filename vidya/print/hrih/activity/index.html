Hrih Worksheet — The Soft Quiet Inside

Activity 1: Shout or Soft? (Circle the Right One)

1. I got 10 on 10 in spelling. I feel proud. Do I shout in the corridor or whisper to Amma at home? SHOUT SOFT (whisper)

2. I spilt the water jug. Amma is in the kitchen. Do I hide under the bed or say 'Amma, I spilt it, I am sorry'? HIDE GENTLE-SORRY

3. My friend's drawing is nicer than mine. Do I sulk or say 'Wow, your Krishna is so blue!'? SULK SOFT (praise)

4. Teacher says 'Priya, your handwriting is the best in class.' Do I jump and shout or smile pink and say 'thank you'? JUMP/SHOUT PINK SMILE

5. I fell down during the race. A boy laughs at me. Do I hit him or say 'haha, I fell, I will try tomorrow'? HIT SOFT LAUGH

Activity 2: Draw Your Diya

In the box below, draw your CHEST with a soft pink diya glowing inside it. Around the diya, draw FOUR good things about you — your smile, your hands that help, your voice that sings, your feet that run. Color the diya soft pink and gold. Do NOT draw rays shooting out like fireworks. Draw the glow gentle, like a small candle in a quiet room.

[Drawing space]

Activity 3: The Pink-Cheek Chant (Gentle and Soft)

Stand in a circle. Put BOTH hands gently on your cheeks. Close your eyes a little. Chant three times — SOFTLY, like a secret:

Hrīḥ... Hrīḥ... Hrīḥ... *Soft and quiet. Soft and quiet. Soft and quiet.*

Open your eyes. Bow your head just a little. Smile a small smile.

Activity 4: The Gentle-Sorry Game (Paired)

With a partner, take turns:

  • Partner A: 'I spilt the milk.' Partner B: 'That's okay! Say gentle-sorry.' Partner A: 'I am gentle-sorry.' Big hug.
  • Partner A: 'I took your pencil without asking.' Partner B: 'Say gentle-sorry.' Partner A: 'I am gentle-sorry.' Pencil returned.
  • Partner A: 'I stepped on your foot.' Partner B: 'Say gentle-sorry.' Partner A: 'I am gentle-sorry.' Both smile.

Switch. Notice: does your heart feel WARMER after gentle-sorry than after hiding?

Activity 5: Take Home — The Quiet Diya Diary

Keep a little notebook by your bed. Each night, draw ONE tiny diya.

Under the diya, write or draw:

  • ONE good thing you did today that you did NOT brag about
  • ONE gentle-sorry you said today when you made a mistake

At the end of the week, count your diyas. Each one is a tiny pink flame of hrih.

PS — Do not show the notebook to ANYONE except Amma or Ajji. The diya shines brightest when it is yours alone.

Home Practice

For one week, keep your Quiet Diya Diary by your bed. Every night, after Amma turns off the light, draw ONE tiny diya in your book. Under the diya, whisper (or write with a crayon): (1) one good thing you did today that you did NOT tell anyone about, and (2) one gentle-sorry you said today when you made a small mistake. If there was nothing to say sorry for, draw a little smile. Do NOT show the diary to friends — this is a secret between you, Amma, and Krishna. Before sleep, touch your chest and whisper: 'Krishna, my diya is soft tonight. Thank You for keeping it gentle.'

Duration: Every night for one week
Look for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 2 good things they kept quietly private and at least 2 gentle-sorry moments without shame or hiding. They can demonstrate the pink-cheek gesture (hand to cheek, small bow) while saying 'hrīḥ' softly. The diary has at least 5 little diyas drawn.
