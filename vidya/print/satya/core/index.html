Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम्।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम्॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Word-for-word: ahiṁsā — non-violence; satyam — truthfulness; akrodhaḥ — free from anger; tyāgaḥ — giving up what is not needed; śāntiḥ — peacefulness; apaiśunam — not finding fault; dayā — kindness; bhūteṣu — to all living beings; aloluptvam — not being greedy; mārdavam — being gentle; hrīḥ — being humble; acāpalam — being steady.

Translation: Do not hurt anyone. Speak the truth. Do not get angry. Give up what you do not need. Be peaceful. Do not find fault. Be kind to every living being. Do not be greedy. Be gentle. Be humble. Do not give up.

Srimad Bhagavatam Verse

SB 1.17.24

तपः शौचं दया सत्यमिति धर्माः पदा विभोः।
निर्मिताः सत्ययुगादौ वदन्ति मुनयो मखे॥

tapaḥ śaucaṁ dayā satyam iti dharmāḥ padā vibhoḥ nirmitāḥ satya-yugādau vadanti munayo makhe

Translation: Austerity, cleanliness, compassion, and truthfulness — these are the four legs of dharma, the sacred path. Of these, truthfulness is one of the strongest.

What is Satya?

Satya means speaking the truth and living the truth. We say what really happened — even when it is hard. We keep the promises we make. When we do not know something, we say, 'I do not know.' Truth makes our heart light and our words strong.

What it is NOT

Satya is not making up stories so we do not get in trouble. It is not half-truths that trick others. It is not pretending we did something we did not do. When we hide the truth, a small stone sits on our heart.

  • Saying 'It wasn't me!' when you broke the cup
  • Telling Amma you brushed your teeth when you didn't
  • Blaming a friend for something you did

Sanskrit Word Study

satya (सत्य)

Pronunciation: SAT-ya

Meaning: Truth. Saying what really happened. Keeping promises. Words that match the heart.

Example: When I told Amma I broke the pot, I was practicing satya.

Story from the Scriptures

Little Krishna and the Butter Pot — Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 10, Chapter 8-9

Long ago in the village of Vrindavan, a little blue boy named Krishna lived with his mother Yashoda. Krishna loved butter. He loved it more than anything else.

Every morning, Mother Yashoda churned fresh butter in a big clay pot. She hung the pot high — very high — so little Krishna could not reach.

But Krishna was clever. He called his friends. 'Come, let us make a tower!' One boy stood on another. Krishna climbed on top. He reached the pot. He ate the soft yellow butter with both hands.

That afternoon, Mother Yashoda came home. She saw broken pots on the floor. Butter was on the walls. Butter was on the cat. Butter was even on the baby monkey!

Yashoda found Krishna sitting quietly. His little face looked too clean. But there was one spot of butter on his cheek.

'Krishna,' she said softly, 'did you eat the butter?'

Krishna looked at the floor. He wanted to say 'No.' A lie was on his tongue. But his heart felt heavy. It felt like a stone.

Krishna looked up at his mother. Her eyes were kind. He opened his mouth wide.

And there, inside his little mouth, was yellow butter. Fresh butter. His tongue could not hide the truth.

'Yes, Amma,' he whispered. 'I ate the butter.'

Mother Yashoda tried to look stern. But her heart was melting. She picked Krishna up and held him close.

'My honest boy,' she said, 'a thousand butter pots are nothing. But your truth — your truth is everything.'

Krishna smiled. His heart was light again. This is satya.

A Story from Today

Anu and the Broken Flower Pot

Anu was playing with her red rubber ball. Amma had said, 'Not inside, Anu. Play outside.' But the sky looked cloudy. So Anu bounced the ball just a little. Once. Twice. Three times.

On the fourth bounce — CRASH! — the ball hit Amma's favourite clay flower pot. The pot broke into six pieces. The jasmine plant lay on the floor.

Anu's stomach felt funny. Her face felt hot. Nobody had seen.

Just then, their grey cat Moti walked in. Anu had an idea. 'I can say Moti did it,' she thought. 'Moti jumps on things all the time.'

But when Anu tried to say the words in her mouth, they would not come. A small stone sat inside her chest. It was heavy.

She tried to play. She tried to eat a biscuit. She tried to watch cartoons. The stone did not go away.

At dinner, Amma noticed. 'Anu, are you okay? You look sad.'

Anu put down her spoon. Her eyes filled with tears. Quietly she said, 'Amma, I was bouncing the ball inside. The pot broke. It was me. Not Moti.'

Amma was quiet for a moment. Then she opened her arms. 'Come here, my brave girl.'

Anu ran to her. 'You are not angry?' she asked.

'The pot can be fixed,' Amma said gently. 'But hiding the truth breaks something inside YOU. I am so proud you told me.'

Anu felt the stone lift off her chest. Her heart was light again. That is satya.

Core Teaching

  1. Satya means saying what really happened — even when it is hard. When we tell the truth, our heart feels light. When we hide the truth, a small stone sits on our heart.
  2. A true friend, a true student, a true child of Krishna — all have one gift: their words match what really happened. We can trust them.
  3. When we keep a promise, we speak truth with our hands and feet too. A promise kept is satya in action.
When our words are always true, people begin to trust us — and from trust grows a deeper gift called shraddha, our faith.
