Worksheet

Dhairya (Composure) Worksheet

Section 1: Story Understanding

Answer these questions about Dhruva and Maya's stories:

1. What made Dhruva decide to go to the forest, and how did he stay focused during his meditation?

2. How did Maya break down the impossible mountain climb into manageable steps?

3. Both Dhruva and Maya faced moments when they wanted to give up. What helped them continue?

4. What was the difference between the reward Dhruva originally wanted and what he actually received?

5. How did helping Priya actually help Maya find more strength for herself?

Section 2: What Would You Do?

Read each situation and choose what shows dhairya:

Situation 1: Your friend group is planning to cheat on tomorrow's math test. They want you to join them.

  • A) Go along with them so you don't lose your friends
  • B) Tell them cheating is wrong and study harder instead
  • C) Report them to the teacher immediately

Situation 2: You've been practicing violin for six months but still can't play your favorite song perfectly.

  • A) Quit violin and try something easier
  • B) Keep practicing a little bit each day, focusing on one difficult part at a time
  • C) Practice for hours every day until your fingers hurt

Situation 3: Your younger sibling keeps interrupting while you're doing homework.

  • A) Yell at them to leave you alone
  • B) Take three deep breaths, then calmly explain you need quiet time to study
  • C) Give up on homework and watch TV instead

Section 3: Craft Activity - The Steady Mountain

Materials needed: Cardboard or thick paper, colored pencils/markers, scissors, glue

Instructions:

1. Cut out a large triangle for your mountain base

2. Cut out 5-7 smaller triangular "steps" going up the mountain

3. On each step, write one challenge you're currently facing (like "learning guitar" or "making new friends")

4. On the back of each step, write one small action you can take this week toward that goal

5. Decorate your mountain with colors that make you feel calm and strong

6. At the peak, draw or write your biggest dream or goal

While making, think about: How does breaking a big mountain into smaller steps make it feel less scary?

Section 4: Game Activity - The Patience Challenge

Players: 4-8 people Time: 10-15 minutes

Setup: Everyone sits in a circle. One person is the "Mountain Guide."

Rules:

1. The Mountain Guide gives everyone a simple task (like "count to 30 in your head" or "balance on one foot")

2. While doing the task, other players try to distract you by making funny faces or telling jokes

3. If you laugh, get distracted, or stop your task, you become a "distractor" too

4. The last person still doing their task becomes the new Mountain Guide

5. Play 3-4 rounds with different tasks

Debrief questions:

  • What made it hard to stay focused?
  • What helped you stay steady when others were trying to distract you?
  • How is this like real life when we're trying to reach a goal?

Section 5: Personal Reflection

Write one paragraph (4-5 sentences) about a time when you had to show dhairya. Use these questions to guide you:
  • What was the situation that required patience and steadiness?
  • What made you want to give up?
  • What helped you keep going?
  • What did you learn about yourself?

If you can't think of a personal example, write about someone you know who shows dhairya and what you admire about them.

Section 6: Discussion Prep

Complete this sentence to share with your class: "One question I want to ask the class about dhairya is: ________________________________"

Some ideas to get you started:

  • How do you know the difference between dhairya and stubbornness?
  • What's the hardest part about staying patient when you really want something?
  • How can we help each other show more dhairya?

Home Practice

Each morning this week, choose one thing that requires patience (like practicing an instrument, helping a family member, or studying a difficult subject). Before you start, take three deep breaths and remind yourself: 'I can stay steady, one step at a time.' If you feel like giving up, count to twenty and take three more deep breaths before deciding what to do next.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice that taking those breathing breaks helps you feel calmer and more able to continue. You might also discover that things you thought were too hard become possible when you break them into smaller steps.
