Worksheet

Dhairya Worksheet - Senior Level

Section 1: Story Analysis

Compare Bhishma's choice and Kenji's journey by answering these questions:

1. Motivation Analysis: What drove each protagonist to make their difficult choice? How were their motivations similar or different?

2. Sacrifice and Gain: What did each character sacrifice, and what did they ultimately gain? Consider both material and spiritual dimensions.

3. Patience vs. Passivity: How did both characters demonstrate active patience rather than passive resignation? Give specific examples from each story.

Section 2: Collaborative Design Challenge

Time: 25 minutes | Materials: Large paper, markers, sticky notes

Working in teams of 3-4, design a "Dhairya Training Program" for your age group:

Your Challenge: Create a practical system that helps teenagers develop genuine patience without becoming passive or losing their drive for positive change.

Include:

  • 3 specific practices (daily, weekly, monthly)
  • Methods for distinguishing healthy patience from harmful passivity
  • Ways to maintain motivation during long-term goals
  • Support systems for when patience feels impossible

Present: Each team has 3 minutes to share their most innovative idea with the class.

Section 3: The Patience Strategy Game

Players: 4-6 per group | Time: 15 minutes

Setup: Each player receives a "Life Goal Card" (examples: "Get into dream college," "Heal a friendship," "Master a skill," "Start a social cause").

Round 1: Players face "Obstacle Cards" (setbacks, delays, criticism, competition). For each obstacle, choose:

  • React Immediately (roll dice - high risk, high reward)
  • Wait and Observe (steady progress, lower risk)
  • Pivot Strategy (change approach while keeping goal)

Round 2: New obstacles appear. Players must now help each other's goals while managing their own.

Debrief Questions:

  • When did patience serve you better than immediate action?
  • How did helping others affect your own patience levels?
  • What strategies emerged for maintaining long-term vision during setbacks?

Section 4: Personal Reflection Essay

Length: 1-2 pages

Prompt: "Describe a current situation in your life where you need dhairya. This could be a long-term goal, a difficult relationship, a challenging skill you're developing, or a personal struggle you're facing.

Analyze your situation using the three principles from this chapter:

1. How can you balance patient endurance with purposeful action?

2. What would it mean to trust divine timing in your specific circumstances?

3. How might you reframe your challenges as opportunities for character development?

Conclude with one specific practice you will implement this week to cultivate dhairya in this situation."

Section 5: Real-World Application Challenge

Duration: One week

Choose one of these dhairya practices to implement:

Option A - The Long View: Identify one goal that will take 6+ months to achieve. Create a weekly check-in system where you assess both your practical progress and your internal state of patience.

Option B - Relationship Patience: Choose one relationship where you tend to be impatient (family member, friend, or even yourself). Practice responding rather than reacting for one week, keeping a brief daily log.

Option C - Skill Mastery: Commit to practicing a challenging skill (musical instrument, sport, art, academic subject) for 20 minutes daily, focusing on the process rather than immediate improvement.

Reflection: At week's end, write a paragraph about what you discovered about the relationship between patience and progress.

Home Practice

Create a 'Dhairya Journal' using any notebook or digital app. Each evening, write 2-3 sentences about one situation that day where you needed patience. Note: (1) What you were waiting for or working toward, (2) How you felt internally, (3) What action (if any) you took. Focus on observing your patterns without judgment.

Duration: Daily for one week
Look for: By week's end, you'll notice recurring themes in what triggers your impatience and begin to see the difference between situations requiring active patience versus passive waiting. You may also discover that naming your feelings reduces their intensity.
