Value at a Glance

Dhairya is the inner strength to stay calm and steady when facing difficult situations. It means keeping your courage and not giving up, even when things get hard or take longer than expected.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 18.43: Heroism, power, determination, resourcefulness, courage in battle, generosity and leadership are the natural qualities of work for the kshatriyas.

What it is NOT

Dhairya is not stubbornness or refusing to change when you should. It is not being lazy and calling it patience, or giving up and pretending you are being calm about it.

  • Stubbornness - refusing to listen to good advice
  • Laziness disguised as patience - avoiding work and calling it calmness
  • Giving up too easily - quitting when things get challenging

Stories Your Child Heard

Dhruva's Forest Test — Five-year-old Prince Dhruva felt his heart burning with hurt. His stepmother had told him he could not sit on his father's lap because he was not born from the right mother. Although his own mother Suniti tried to comfort him, Dhruva decided he would find Lord Vishnu himself and ask for a kingdom greater than his father's. Suniti watched her young son with worried eyes. 'My dear child,' she said, 'only Lord Vishnu can help us now. If you truly want to see Him, you must go to the forest and meditate with complete focus.'

Maya's Mountain Challenge — Maya stared up at the steep mountain trail ahead, her backpack feeling heavier with each passing minute. The school trekking group had been walking for three hours through the misty hills of Nepal, and her legs already ached. Although she had been excited about this adventure trip, now she wondered if she had made a mistake. 'I can't do this,' whispered her friend Priya, who was breathing heavily beside her. 'My feet are killing me, and we're not even halfway up.'

The Sanskrit Word

dhairya (धैर्य) — DHIGH-rya

Meaning: Composure, patience, fortitude, steady courage in the face of difficulties

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Each morning this week, choose one thing that requires patience (like practicing an instrument, helping a family member, or studying a difficult subject). Before you start, take three deep breaths and remind yourself: 'I can stay steady, one step at a time.' If you feel like giving up, count to twenty and take three more deep breaths before deciding what to do next.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Look for: You will notice that taking those breathing breaks helps you feel calmer and more able to continue. You might also discover that things you thought were too hard become possible when you break them into smaller steps.
