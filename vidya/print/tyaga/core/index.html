Verse Anchor

Bhagavad-gita 18.2

श्रीभगवानुवाच
काम्यानां कर्मणां न्यासं संन्यासं कवयो विदुः।
सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं प्राहुस्त्यागं विचक्षणाः॥

śrī-bhagavān uvāca kāmyānāṁ karmaṇāṁ nyāsaṁ sannyāsaṁ kavayo viduḥ sarva-karma-phala-tyāgaṁ prāhus tyāgaṁ vicakṣaṇāḥ

Word-for-word: śrī-bhagavān uvāca — the Lord said; kāmyānām — done for selfish wish; karmaṇām — of actions; nyāsam — leaving off; sannyāsam — renunciation; kavayaḥ — the wise; viduḥ — know; sarva — all; karma — of action; phala — fruits; tyāgam — letting go; prāhuḥ — say; tyāgam — tyaga; vicakṣaṇāḥ — those who see clearly.

Translation: The Lord says: Letting go of selfish wanting is one thing. But the wisest teachers call it TYAGA when we do our work with love — and let go of what we get in return.

न तथा मे प्रियतम आत्मयोनिर्न शङ्करः।
न च सङ्कर्षणो न श्रीर्नैवात्मा च यथा भवान्॥

Translation: Krishna says: The devotee who has let go of selfish wanting — even Brahma, Shiva, or My own brother are not as dear to Me as that devotee is.

What is Tyaga?

Tyaga means letting go with a peaceful heart. I can give up my turn. I can give up the biggest piece. I can give up winning, so a smaller friend can smile. I can even let go of needing to be right. The hand that opens is bigger than the hand that grabs.

What it is NOT

Tyaga is not letting go while feeling angry inside. It is not pretending to share while secretly grumbling. It is not running away from our work because we feel lazy. True tyaga is a happy letting go — quiet and clean, like a flower dropping from a tree.

  • Giving up a turn while stamping your foot and muttering 'It's not fair!'
  • Refusing to do homework because you do not want to — calling it 'giving up'
  • Letting your sister pick first, then reminding everyone all day that you gave up your turn

Sanskrit Word Study

tyāga (त्याग)

Pronunciation: TYAH-ga

Meaning: Letting go with a happy heart. Giving up what I want, without feeling sad or angry. The open hand.

Example: When Meera let the little girl have her turn on the swing, that was tyaga.

Story from the Scriptures

Prince Rama Smiles and Lets Go of the Crown — Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda

The whole kingdom of Ayodhya was bright with lamps. Flowers hung from every door. Tomorrow, Prince Rama would be crowned king. Everyone was smiling — the rich, the poor, even the little street children who loved Rama like a brother.

Rama slept peacefully that night.

But before the sun came up, a messenger ran to Rama's room. 'My prince, come quickly. Your father needs you.'

Rama went. His father, King Dasharatha, was crying. Queen Kaikeyi stood near him. Her face was hard.

She said, 'Rama, the king promised me two wishes long ago. Now I ask for them. First — my son Bharata will be crowned king, not you. Second — you will go to the forest for fourteen years.'

Fourteen years! In bark clothes! No throne. No palace.

Rama did not shout. Rama did not cry. Rama did not even look sad.

He bowed to Kaikeyi. 'Mother, if this makes you happy, it makes me happy too. I will go today.'

He walked back to his room. He took off his silk robes. He folded them neatly. He put on simple bark cloth like a forest sage. He smiled at his wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana.

'Come,' he said softly. 'The forest is calling.'

The people of Ayodhya ran behind his chariot, weeping. But Rama's face stayed peaceful.

He had let go of a crown — and his heart was lighter than ever.

This is tyaga. Letting go with a smile, not a frown.

A Story from Today

Meera's Last Turn on the Swing

In the evening, the park near Meera's apartment filled with children. There was ONE swing Meera loved — the blue one with the silver chain. It made the best WHOOSH in the wind.

Meera had been waiting for twenty whole minutes. Her tummy felt wiggly with excitement. Finally the big boy hopped off.

'My turn!' Meera called, running toward the blue swing.

Then she stopped.

A very small girl was standing near the swing, crying softly. She was only about four years old. Her tiny fingers held the silver chain. Her eyes were big and wet.

'Didi,' the little girl whispered, 'I waited so long. But every time I come, the big children go first.'

Meera looked at the swing. Her favourite blue swing. Her turn at last.

She thought of her Nani's words — 'Meera-beta, sometimes the biggest thing we can give is the thing we wanted most. And we give it with a smile, not with a sad face.'

Meera took a deep breath. She stepped back.

'You go first,' she said softly. 'I will push you.'

The little girl's face cracked into a sunshine-smile. 'Really, didi? Really?'

Meera lifted her onto the swing. She pushed gently. The little girl squealed, 'WHOOOOSH!'

Meera laughed. She had given up her turn. But something heavy — like a little stone in her chest — had dropped away. She felt light. Free. Bigger than the swing, bigger than the park.

That night Meera told Nani. Nani hugged her. 'This is tyaga, my child. Letting go with a happy heart.'

Meera fell asleep still smiling.

Core Teaching

  1. Tyaga means letting go with a peaceful, smiling heart. I give up my turn, my biggest piece, my need to be first — and I do it happily, not with a grumpy face.
  2. Tyaga is not running away from our duty. It is doing our work with full love, and letting go of the REWARD. We help because helping is right, not because we want a prize.
  3. When we let go of small things quietly, our heart gets bigger. The hand that opens holds more than the hand that grabs. Krishna smiles when we let go for the joy of someone else.
When our hand stays open and our heart stays light, we begin to feel a quiet strength inside — and that strength has its own beautiful name, vairagya.
