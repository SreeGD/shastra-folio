Value at a Glance

Sthitaprajna means steady wisdom. My heart stays calm whether I win or lose, whether I am praised or scolded. When something good happens, I do not jump too high. When something sad happens, I do not fall too low. I remember Krishna is always with me, so inside I stay steady, like a deep calm lake.

The Verse

Bhagavad-gita 2.55: When a person is happy inside — not because of things outside — we call that person sthitaprajna. Their mind is steady. Their heart does not jump up and down.

What it is NOT

Sthitaprajna is not being cold or not caring. It is not hiding our feelings or pretending to be a stone. It is also not being grumpy and silent. A steady heart still smiles, still cries a little, still hugs — but it does not get blown away by every little wind.

  • Jumping up and down for hours after winning, then crying for hours after losing
  • Being full of pride when the teacher praises, then being angry when scolded
  • Refusing to play again because I lost one game

Stories Your Child Heard

Arjuna's Big Question on the Battlefield — Long ago, on the great battlefield of Kurukshetra, two armies stood face to face. Arjuna, the best archer in the world, sat in his silver chariot. His horses were white like jasmine flowers. But Arjuna was not happy. His bow slipped from his hand. His eyes filled with tears. 'Krishna,' he whispered, 'my heart is shaking. I am afraid.'

Meera and the Sports Day Race — Meera loved running. Every morning she ran around the school ground with her long braid flying behind her. All the teachers said, 'Meera is the fastest in Standard 3!' Sports Day came. The big race was the 100-meter dash. Meera wore her crisp white shoes. Amma had tied a small red thread on her wrist for good luck.

The Sanskrit Word

sthita-prajña (स्थितप्रज्ञ) — STHEE-ta-prag-nya

Meaning: Steady wisdom. A person whose mind stays calm whether happy things or sad things happen. Their heart is full because Krishna is inside.

Ask your child to teach you this word!

Home Practice

Keep your Calm Jar on your bedside table. Every time you feel a BIG feeling — very happy, very sad, very angry, very scared — pick up the Calm Jar. Shake it. Sit cross-legged. Watch the glitter settle. Breathe slowly in and out. When the water is clear, your heart is clear too. Then decide what to say or do. Also: before bed, share ONE steady-heart moment with Amma or Appa — 'Today something happened and I stayed steady.'

Duration: Every day for two weeks
Look for: By the end of two weeks, the child can describe at least 3 moments where they felt a big emotion, used the Calm Jar or steady breath, and chose a kind response. Parents notice shorter tantrums and quicker recovery from upsets.

When our heart is steady, we stop grabbing and pulling — and we start giving freely from that full, calm place. That happy giving has a beautiful name: dana.
