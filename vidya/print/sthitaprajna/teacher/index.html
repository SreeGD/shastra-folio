Chapter Overview

Sthitaprajna means steady wisdom. My heart stays calm whether I win or lose, whether I am praised or scolded. When something good happens, I do not jump too high. When something sad happens, I do not fall too low. I remember Krishna is always with me, so inside I stay steady, like a deep calm lake.

The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 2.55 — When a person is happy inside — not because of things outside — we call that person sthitaprajna. Their mind is steady. Their heart does not jump up and down.

Core Teaching

  1. Sthitaprajna means my heart stays steady — whether I win or lose, whether I am praised or scolded. Inside me, a quiet friend is always there: Krishna.
  2. Feelings like happiness and sadness come and go, like clouds in the sky. We let them come. We let them go. We do not become the clouds — we are the sky underneath.
  3. When we are steady, we can be kind to the winner AND to ourselves, we can cheer AND console, we can try again without fear. This is the real gift of Krishna's friendship.

Five-Minute Introduction

Begin with a clear jar of water and a teaspoon of glitter. Shake it. Say: 'Children, look! This is our heart when something big happens — a win, a loss, a scolding, a surprise.' Set the jar down. 'Watch — if we sit still, the glitter settles. The water becomes clear again. Today we learn a special word from Krishna: sthitaprajna — steady wisdom. It is the clear-water heart.' Chant 'sthita-prajña, sthita-prajña, sthita-prajña' three times slowly, palms at heart, breathing deeply. Then ask: 'Can you remember one time your heart was very shaky? What happened?' Take 2-3 short answers.

40-Minute Lesson Plan

40-Minute Lesson Plan

5 min — Calm Jar demo + chant. 10 min — Read aloud: Arjuna's Big Question; show ocean picture; practice the 'steady breath' together. 8 min — Read aloud: Meera and the Sports Day Race; ask children to mime Meera cheering for Priya. 10 min — Worksheet Activity 1 (Heart Weather) and Activity 2 (Steady Breath). 5 min — Closing chant + introduce Calm Jar take-home. 2 min — Line up.

Two-Hour Lesson Plan

10 min — Opening circle with Calm Jar demo; chant sthita-prajña with palm-at-heart. 15 min — Arjuna's story; draw the ocean on the board; children take turns being 'rivers of feelings' and 'the ocean.' 15 min — Meera's story; class discussion: 'Has a medal ever disappointed you?' 20 min — Activities 1 & 2 (Heart Weather drawing + Steady Breath practice). 15 min — Paired Win-Lose Role-Play (Activity 3); rotate partners once. 15 min — Praise-Scold Mirror (Activity 4) — each child in turn, with gentle teacher presence. 15 min — Make individual Calm Jars: small clear bottle + water + glitter + seal. 10 min — Circle closing: each child shares one moment this week they will try to stay steady. 5 min — Final chant and bow. Buffer: 0 min.

Discussion Guide

Primary children learn sthitaprajna through concrete contrasts — the Calm Jar before and after settling, the big-reaction vs. small-steady-smile in role-play, the ocean vs. little puddle. Keep the BODY involved: breathing with hand on tummy, rooted sitting posture, the palm-at-heart gesture. Avoid philosophical discussion of 'transcendence' or 'detachment.' Watch for the perfectionist child who crashes when they fail — their work is to feel and then LET GO, not to stuff feelings down. Watch also for the dramatic child who loves big reactions — invite them to perform the small-steady-smile as a new acting challenge. The sthitaprajna heart is not cold; it is warm AND steady — cultivate BOTH in the classroom.

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: 'A sthitaprajna has no feelings.' Response: Not true. A steady person still feels happy, sad, scared — everyone does. The difference is they do not get swept away. Feelings are like clouds. The sthitaprajna is the sky underneath — big enough to hold every cloud without becoming it.
  • Misconception: 'Being steady means I should not try to win.' Response: We still try our best! We still run fast, study hard, play well. Being steady means the TRYING is joyful, and whatever happens after — win or lose — we stay peaceful inside. The effort is ours to give; the result is Krishna's to hold.
  • Misconception: 'If I am steady, no one will praise me because I will look boring.' Response: A steady heart is not a boring heart. It is a STRONG heart. People feel safe near a steady friend. Krishna said the sthitaprajna is the most beautiful person in the world — because their peace cannot be stolen.
