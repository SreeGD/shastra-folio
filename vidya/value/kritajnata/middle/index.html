Vinaya

Kritajnata

कृतज्ञता · kri-ta-GYAH-taa

Primary 6–9 Middle 9–14 Senior 14–18 College 17+
বাংলা EN ગુજરાતી हिन्दी ಕನ್ನಡ मराठी ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ES தமிழ் తెలుగు

What it is

Kritajnata is deep thankfulness for all we have received. It means seeing that everything we have comes from God, our parents, teachers, and nature. When we feel real gratitude, we want to give back and help others.

What it is not

Kritajnata is not just saying 'thank you' as good manners. It is not thinking we earned everything by ourselves. It is not feeling grateful only when we get what we want.

Word study

Word
कृतज्ञता (kṛtajñatā)
Say it
kri-ta-GYAH-taa
Meaning
Gratitude, thankfulness, grateful recognition of help received
Example
The student showed kṛtajñatā by helping her teacher organize books after receiving free tutoring.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.26

पत्रं पुष्पं फलं तोयं यो मे भक्त्या प्रयच्छति। तदहं भक्त्युपहृतमश्नामि प्रयतात्मनः॥

patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati | tad ahaṁ bhakty-upahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ ||

If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

Vidura's True Loyalty

Mahabharata, Sabha Parva

In the great palace of Hastinapura lived Vidura, the wise advisor to King Dhritarashtra. Because Vidura was born to a servant woman, many nobles looked down on him. But Dhritarashtra had always treated Vidura like a brother. He gave him education, respect, and an important position in the court. Vidura felt deep gratitude for these gifts.

As years passed, Dhritarashtra began making terrible decisions. He allowed his son Duryodhana to cheat the Pandavas in a rigged dice game. He watched silently as Draupadi was insulted in the royal court. Each wrong choice pushed the kingdom closer to war.

Vidura's heart was heavy. Other advisors stayed quiet because they feared the king's anger. But Vidura remembered all that Dhritarashtra had given him. Because he was truly grateful, he knew he had to protect his benefactor from making mistakes that would destroy everything.

'My lord,' Vidura said one day, 'these actions will bring ruin to our family. Please reconsider.' Dhritarashtra's face turned red with anger. 'How dare you question me!' he shouted. 'Leave my palace!'

Although his heart broke, Vidura left without complaint. He knew that real gratitude sometimes means doing what is right, not what is easy. Because Vidura truly loved the king, he was willing to face exile to try to save him.

Years later, when the great war ended and the kingdom lay in ruins, Dhritarashtra finally understood. Vidura had been the only one who truly cared enough to speak the truth. The king wept, realizing that Vidura's courage came from genuine gratitude and love.

The Recipe Book

Thirteen-year-old Kenji lived in a small apartment in Kyoto with his parents and grandmother, Obaa-chan. Every day after school, he would rush home to find her in the tiny kitchen, preparing amazing meals. The smell of miso soup and fresh rice always made him smile.

Obaa-chan had taught herself to cook during hard times after the war. She never used recipes - everything was in her memory. 'Cooking is love made visible,' she would say, watching Kenji eat with joy.

Then Obaa-chan got sick. Her hands shook too much to hold a knife safely. She spent most days resting in her chair, looking sad and tired. The family started buying prepared food from the store. It tasted fine, but something was missing.

Kenji's friends wanted him to join the soccer team that met every afternoon. 'Come on, Kenji!' they called. 'It'll be fun!' He wanted to go, but he kept thinking about Obaa-chan sitting alone, and all those recipes that might disappear forever.

One afternoon, instead of going out, Kenji sat beside his grandmother. 'Obaa-chan, will you teach me to make your chicken teriyaki?' Her face lit up like sunshine. Although she couldn't cook anymore, she could still remember every step.

'First, we marinate the chicken,' she said, guiding his hands. 'Not too much soy sauce - just enough to honor the chicken.' As they worked together, she told him stories about learning to cook as a young mother, about the neighbors who shared ingredients during difficult times.

Day after day, Kenji came home to learn. He wrote down each recipe carefully, but more importantly, he listened to the stories that came with each dish. Because he spent this time with Obaa-chan, he discovered that she had given him much more than food all these years - she had given him a connection to his family's history.

Months later, when Kenji cooked his first complete meal for the family, Obaa-chan cried happy tears. 'Now you can feed your own family someday,' she whispered. Kenji realized that learning her recipes was his way of saying thank you for all the love she had cooked into every meal.

Core teaching

  • Real gratitude sees how much effort others put into helping us grow and succeed.
  • When we truly appreciate what we've received, we naturally want to give back and help others.
  • Gratitude is not just good manners - it's recognizing that everything we have comes from sources beyond ourselves.

Try this at home

Each evening before dinner, tell one family member about someone who helped you that day and how it made you feel. Then ask them to share about someone who helped them. Keep a simple list of all the helpers you both mention.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, you will have a list of at least 14 different people who helped your family, and you'll notice helpers you used to overlook.
