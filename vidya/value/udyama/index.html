Karma

Udyama

उद्यम · UD-ya-ma

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Udyama means bright-eyed beginning. It is the happy hop inside me when I say 'Yes! I will try!' I do not wait to be pushed. I do not sit and sulk. I roll up my sleeves, I smile, and I begin. If I stumble, I get up and try again. If the work is big, I break it small and start with the first piece. Udyama is the sunrise inside my chest.

What it is not

Udyama is not running around madly just to look busy. It is not the worried kind of trying — 'I must win! I must be first!' It is not doing our work angrily or with a frowny face. And it is not only trying for a sticker or a sweet. True udyama is cheerful work — eyes bright, hands busy, heart light.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 6.24

सङ्कल्पप्रभवान्कामांस्त्यक्त्वा सर्वानशेषतः।
मनसैवेन्द्रियग्रामं विनियम्य समन्ततः॥

saṅkalpa-prabhavān kāmāṁs tyaktvā sarvān aśeṣataḥ manasaivendriya-grāmaṁ viniyamya samantataḥ

Krishna says: Begin your work with bright eyes and a strong wish (sankalpa). Step by step, with quiet determination (niścayena), keep going. Even if your feet are small, a strong sankalpa can climb a mountain.

Lava and Kusha Practice Their Arrows at Sunrise

Uttara Kanda of the Ramayana (Valmiki)

Deep in the forest, Rishi Valmiki had a small ashram. Two little boys lived there with their mother Sita. Their names were Lava and Kusha. They were twins. They were only seven years old.

One evening, Rishi Valmiki gave them each a tiny bow. 'Boys,' he said, 'today you begin to learn archery.'

Lava's eyes went SHINE. Kusha's eyes went SHINE-SHINE. They hopped up and down. 'Yes! Yes, Guruji!'

That night, before anyone else woke up, Lava sat up in his little bed. He shook Kusha gently. 'Brother. Sunrise is coming. Let us go.'

Kusha rubbed his eyes. He YAWNED very big. Then he smiled. 'Yes! Let us go!'

The two little boys picked up their tiny bows. They tip-toed past their sleeping mother. They walked through the cool dark forest to the river.

The first arrow WOBBLED. It flopped onto the ground after two steps.

Lava laughed. 'Mine too!' Kusha's arrow fell at his own feet.

Did they cry? No. Did they say 'We cannot do this'? No. They picked up the arrows. They tried again. WOBBLE. Flop. Try again. WOBBLE. Flop. Try again.

The sun came up. Birds began to sing. The boys were still shooting.

By the time Rishi Valmiki walked down to the river, the boys' cheeks were pink, their hair was sweaty, and their arrows were starting to fly STRAIGHTER.

Valmiki smiled. 'Children, you did not wait for me to wake you. You came by yourselves.'

Lava said, 'Guruji, my eyes would not stay shut! There was a little sun inside my chest!'

Kusha said, 'And when I fell down, I thought — one more try, one more try, one more try!'

Valmiki touched their foreheads. 'My sons, this is UDYAMA. The little sun inside. The one-more-try. The bright beginning. Keep this, and one day your arrows will sing.'

Months passed. Every single morning, Lava and Kusha were at the river before sunrise. Their arrows began to fly like birds. When they grew a little bigger, their songs and their arrows would one day reach Lord Rama's own palace. But it all began with two sleepy little boys who got up — on their own — because a little sun was burning inside their chests.

That little sun has a name. Udyama.

न तथा विन्दते क्षेमं मुकुन्दचरणाम्बुजम्॥

tat-prayāso na kartavyo yata āyur-vyayaḥ param na tathā vindate kṣemaṁ mukunda-caraṇāmbujam

Little Prahlada says to his friends: 'Do not waste your energy on small, passing things. Put your bright effort (udyama) into what truly matters — loving Krishna, learning, serving, and doing good work. THAT effort is never lost.'

Raghav Starts the Classroom Garden

Raghav was in Standard Two in a little school in Hyderabad. His classroom had a window. Outside the window was a PATCH of brown empty dirt. Just dirt. No grass. No flowers. Just dirt.

One day, teacher Meena-ji said, 'Children, one day we must plant a garden there. That patch is sad.'

Raghav's eyes went BIG. 'Yes! A garden!'

But days went by. No one brought seeds. No one brought a spoon. The patch stayed brown.

Raghav's feet started to wiggle. His fingers started to itch. Inside his chest, there was a little TICK-TICK-TICK like a tiny drum. It was saying — 'Begin! Begin! Begin!'

At home, Raghav told Amma. Amma smiled. 'If your heart is tick-tick-ticking, Raghav-beta, that is udyama knocking. Do not wait. Begin with what you have.'

'But Amma, I am only seven.'

Amma handed him a small steel spoon from the kitchen drawer. She gave him three coriander seeds from her masala box. She gave him a little plastic cup. 'That is what you have. Begin.'

Monday morning, Raghav arrived early. No one else was there. The patch was still brown. Raghav did not wait. He knelt in the dirt. He scratched with the spoon. One hole. Two holes. Three holes. He dropped in one seed per hole. He poured water.

'Grow, my friends,' he whispered. 'Grow slow or grow fast. Just grow.'

Tuesday, his friend Aditi saw him. 'Raghav, what are you DOING?'

'Starting,' said Raghav. 'Want to help?'

Aditi brought a spoon on Wednesday.

Wednesday, two more children brought seeds. Thursday, five children came. Friday, the WHOLE CLASS was kneeling in the dirt, scratching, planting, watering, laughing.

On Friday afternoon, Raghav saw it first. He pointed with a shaking finger.

One tiny green shoot. Thinner than a thread. Peeking up through the brown dirt.

The whole class cheered.

Teacher Meena-ji knelt beside Raghav. 'Raghav, you did not wait. You began.'

Raghav smiled. 'My heart was going tick-tick-tick, teacher. So I began.'

Meena-ji touched his shoulder. 'This is udyama, child. The bright hop. The happy start. The little sun inside the chest. Keep it — and even the driest patch of dirt will turn green.'

Core teaching

  • Udyama means I begin my work with bright eyes and a happy heart. I do not wait to be pushed. I do not sit and sulk. When Amma says 'Let's do homework,' I say 'Yes!' When teacher says 'Let's line up,' I hop up. The first step is the most important step — and it is mine to take.
  • Udyama is the one-more-try heart. When my arrow wobbles, I pick it up and try again. When my drawing goes wrong, I turn the paper over and start again. When I fall, I get up. The Gita says — niścayena — with quiet determination — step by step, udyama climbs every mountain.
  • Udyama is not for a prize. It is because there is a little sun inside my chest that wants to shine. I work happily. I help happily. I begin happily. When the work is done, I do not need stickers — my heart is already lit up from inside. Krishna smiles when He sees that little sun in me.

Try this at home

For one week, keep your Sunrise Journal by your bed. Each morning, try to wake yourself before Amma calls — the little sun should wake you. Sit up, draw a tiny sun in your book, and write or draw ONE thing you will BEGIN today — brush teeth by yourself, help Papa with breakfast, start homework without being asked, water a plant, tidy your toys. At bedtime, tick it if you did it. Before sleeping, whisper: 'Krishna, my little sun shone today. Thank You for lighting me up.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 mornings when they woke themselves or began a task without being told, and can demonstrate the bright-eyes-hop gesture while chanting 'udyama' with a smile. The journal has at least 5 little suns drawn.

Activities

  • Begin or Wait · 6 min

    Circle BEGIN or WAIT for each scenario.

  • Draw Your Little Sun · 10 min

    Draw yourself with a golden sun shining inside your chest. Around the sun, draw three things you will begin this week.

  • The Bright-Eyes Chant · 3 min

    Stand tall, open your eyes wide, and chant 'udyama' three times while hopping — hand on chest.

  • The One-More-Try Game · 10 min

    With a partner, take turns trying small tasks (balance a book, hop to the door). When one falls, the other cheers 'One more try!' Notice how the heart feels.

  • The Sunrise Journal · 5 min

    Keep a daily journal by your bed. Each morning, wake yourself, draw a tiny sun, and name one thing you will begin. Tick it at night.
