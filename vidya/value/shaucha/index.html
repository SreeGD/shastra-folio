Dharma

Shaucha

शौच · SHOW-cha

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Shaucha means keeping clean — our body, our clothes, our room, AND our heart. Outside shaucha is washing our hands, brushing our teeth, and tidying our space. Inside shaucha is keeping our thoughts kind and our words true. Krishna loves a clean body and a clean heart — both.

What it is not

Shaucha is not just a show — clean hands outside with mean words inside is not shaucha. It is not looking down on anyone because our clothes are new. It is not being clean only when Amma is watching.

Word study

Word
शौच (śaucha)
Say it
SHOW-cha
Meaning
Cleanliness and purity. Keeping the body, clothes, room, and heart bright and clear. Outside clean and inside clean — both together.
Example
When I bathe in the morning AND speak kindly to my sister, I am practicing shaucha.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.3

तेजः क्षमा धृतिः शौचमद्रोहो नातिमानिता।
भवन्ति सम्पदं दैवीमभिजातस्य भारत॥

tejaḥ kṣamā dhṛtiḥ śaucam adroho nātimānitā bhavanti sampadaṁ daivīm abhijātasya bhārata

Shine bright. Forgive. Be patient. Stay clean — inside and outside. Do not be jealous. Do not be too proud. These are the divine treasures, Arjuna.

Prahlada's Clean Heart in the Asura Palace

Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 7, Chapters 4-5

Long ago, a little prince named Prahlada lived in a huge palace. His father was a mighty demon-king. The palace was big and shiny, but inside, many people had angry faces and shouting voices.

Prahlada was only five years old. But he loved Krishna with his whole heart.

Every morning before sunrise, Prahlada slipped down to the clear river. He splashed cool water on his face. He rubbed his little arms and legs clean. He rinsed his mouth. Then he wrapped a fresh white cloth around his body.

Softly, he whispered, 'Krishna, my body is clean. Please make my heart clean too.'

Prahlada's teachers tried to teach him bad things. They said, 'Only the strong win. Be proud. Be angry.' But Prahlada did not let those dirty thoughts in. He kept his heart tidy, like a clean temple waiting for Krishna.

One day his father shouted, 'Why do you love Krishna? Forget Him!'

Little Prahlada folded his hands. In a clear voice he said, 'Father, I cannot forget Him. When my body is clean, my mind is clear. When my mind is clear, I see Krishna everywhere.'

The demon-king became furious. He tried to harm little Prahlada. He threw him from a high wall. He pushed him into fire. He sent big elephants.

But Prahlada's body stayed safe, and his heart stayed calm. Krishna stood between Prahlada and every danger — because Prahlada's inside and outside were both clean and full of love.

At last, Krishna Himself came as Narasimha and set everything right. Little Prahlada smiled and bowed.

This is shaucha. A clean body. A clean heart. Krishna walks in both.

Meera's Morning Temple

Meera was seven. She hated two things — bathing and tidying up.

Her socks lived on the fan. Her books slept on the floor. Her hair was a bird's nest. One day Amma opened her school bag and a squashed banana fell out.

'Meera!' Amma cried. 'A tidy room is a tidy mind.'

Meera rolled her eyes. 'Krishna sees my heart, Amma. He does not care about my socks!'

That Saturday, Dadi took Meera's hand. 'Come with me to the temple, beta.'

It was still dark when they reached the stone temple. Meera was sleepy. But something was happening inside.

The priest was sweeping the floor with a soft broom. He gathered every little leaf. Then he carried a brass pot of water. Gently, gently, he bathed the small Krishna deity — head, arms, feet. He dried Krishna with a yellow cloth, dressed Him in fresh clothes, lit a clean lamp, and offered a tulasi leaf.

Meera watched with big eyes. She had never seen anyone be so careful, so slow, so loving.

'Dadi,' she whispered, 'why does he clean so much?'

Dadi smiled. 'Because Krishna likes to come where it is clean. The priest is making a bright house for Him. First outside. Then inside.'

Meera thought about her own bedroom. Her own hair. Her own hands.

On the way home, Meera said, 'Dadi, tomorrow I will bathe first. My body is Krishna's little temple too, na?'

Dadi kissed her forehead. 'That is shaucha, beta.'

The next morning, Meera washed, brushed, and tied her hair. She folded her clothes. Then she whispered, 'Good morning, Krishna. Your little temple is ready.'

Core teaching

  • Shaucha means being clean — outside AND inside. A clean body, clean clothes, a clean room — and clean thoughts and true words. Both together make shaucha.
  • Our body is Krishna's little temple. When we bathe, brush, and tidy, we are preparing a bright place for Him. The outside cleaning helps the inside stay bright too.
  • Shaucha is not showing off. It is a quiet, daily love — a fresh bath, a swept floor, a kind word. Every clean moment is an offering to Krishna.

Try this at home

Each morning this week, do the Morning Three BEFORE breakfast: (1) bath or wash face-hands-feet, (2) brush teeth, (3) tidy your bed. After the third thing, touch your heart and say softly, 'Krishna, Your little temple is ready.' Then each night at bedtime, tell Amma or Appa ONE kind word you spoke today and ONE true word you said. This is your inside shaucha.

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can do the Morning Three independently (without being reminded) and can name at least 3 kind-and-true words they spoke during the week.

Activities

  • My Daily Shaucha Checklist · 5 min

    Circle Yes or No for six daily shaucha actions — three outside (brush, wash, clean clothes) and three inside (tidy space, kind words, folded bed).

  • Inside and Outside Sort · 8 min

    Sort picture cards into two buckets — outside shaucha and inside shaucha. Discuss which cards could belong to both.

  • Shaucha Chant · 3 min

    Chant shaucha three times with hands moving from head to heart, singing 'clean body, clean heart, Krishna is near'.

  • The Temple-of-Me Drawing · 10 min

    Draw a small temple with yourself inside. Around the temple draw a toothbrush, soap, broom, and folded cloth. Title the page 'My body is Krishna's little temple.'

  • The Morning Three · 5 min

    For one week, do three things every morning before breakfast — bath, brush, tidy bed. Each night also share one kind word and one true word you spoke.
