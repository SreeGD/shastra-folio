Prema

Bhakti

भक्ति · BHAK-tee

Primary 6–9 Middle 9–14 Senior 14–18 College 17+
বাংলা EN ગુજરાતી हिन्दी ಕನ್ನಡ मराठी ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ES தமிழ் తెలుగు

What it is

Bhakti means loving God with your whole heart. It means caring for Krishna like you care for your best friend. When you have bhakti, you want to make Krishna happy.

What it is not

Bhakti is not just wanting things for yourself. It is not being mean to others or only caring about toys and games.

Word study

Word

Meaning

Maya showed bhakti when she gave her flowers with love.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.34

मन्मना भव मद्भक्तो मद्याजी मां नमस्कुरु ।
मामेवैष्यसि युक्त्वैवमात्मानं मत्परायणः ॥

man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ namaskuru mām evaiṣyasi yuktvā evam ātmānaṁ mat-parāyaṇaḥ

Think of Me always. Love Me. Pray to Me. Bow to Me. If you do this, you will come to Me.

Krishna and Sudama's Love

Srimad Bhagavatam 10.80-81

Long ago, there lived a man named Sudama. He and Krishna had been best friends when they were little boys at school. They studied together and played together every day.

But now they were grown up. Krishna had become a great king in the beautiful city of Dvaraka. Sudama had become a poor teacher. His clothes were old. His family did not have enough food to eat.

One day, Sudama's wife said, 'Why don't you visit your dear friend Krishna? He is so kind. He will be happy to see you.'

Sudama felt shy. 'But I have nothing to give him,' he said. 'Krishna is a king. What can a poor man like me bring?'

His wife smiled. She went to the neighbours and asked for some flat rice. She tied four handfuls in a torn cloth. 'Give this to Krishna,' she said. 'He will love anything given with a loving heart.'

Sudama walked a long way to Dvaraka. His clothes were torn and his feet were dusty. The palace guards looked at him strangely. 'I am Krishna's friend,' Sudama said softly.

When Krishna saw Sudama coming, He jumped up from His throne and ran to meet him! 'Sudama! My dear friend!' Krishna hugged him tightly. His eyes filled with happy tears. He did not care about Sudama's old clothes.

Krishna washed Sudama's tired feet with His own hands. He gave Sudama the best seat and the best food. They sat together and talked about their happy days at school.

'What did you bring me?' Krishna asked with a big smile. Sudama felt shy about his small gift of flat rice. But Krishna took the rice and ate it with so much joy! 'This tastes wonderful because you brought it with love!' Krishna said.

When Sudama walked home, he thought he had nothing new. But when he reached his village, his little house had become a beautiful home! Krishna had quietly blessed his family with everything they needed.

Sudama's eyes filled with tears. The best gift was not the new house. It was knowing that Krishna loved him — not because he was rich or poor, but because of the love in his heart.

SB 11.14.21

भक्त्याहमेकया ग्राह्य: श्रद्धयात्मा प्रिय: सताम् ।
भक्ति: पुनाति मन्निष्ठा श्वपाकानपि सम्भवात् ॥ २१ ॥

bhaktyāham ekayā grāhyaḥ śraddhayātmā pṛyaḥ satām bhaktiḥ punāti man-niṣṭhā śva-pākān api sambhavāt

The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: My dear Uddhava, only by devotional service can one understand Me as I am, standing before you. By such bhakti, or devotional service, which is unmixed with fruitive activities and knowledge, one can understand that I, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, in My original spiritual body, can be understood only by devotional service. That bhakti, or devotional service, purifies even dog-eaters, what to speak of brāhmaṇas.

Maya's Garden Gift

Maya lived in a small village in Kenya. She loved to plant seeds in her little garden behind her house. Every morning, she watered the tiny green shoots.

After many weeks, beautiful yellow flowers bloomed. Maya clapped her hands. 'They're so pretty!' she said. 'I want to keep them all in my room.'

That afternoon, Maya's grandmother came to visit. She walked slowly with her walking stick. 'Oh, Maya,' she said, 'your flowers are the most beautiful I've ever seen.'

Maya felt torn inside. She loved her flowers. But she also loved her grandmother. She remembered how her grandmother always shared her chapati with Maya when she was hungry.

Maya looked at her grandmother's kind, wrinkled face. Then she looked at her bright yellow flowers. Slowly, Maya picked the biggest, prettiest flowers.

'These are for you, Grandma,' Maya said, putting them in her grandmother's hands.

Her grandmother's eyes filled with happy tears. 'Thank you, my dear child. These flowers will make my house bright and cheerful.'

As Maya watched her grandmother smell the flowers, something warm grew in Maya's heart. She felt happier than when she was keeping the flowers for herself.

Because Maya chose to give with love, her heart felt full of joy.

Core teaching

  • Love means giving, not just taking. When we share with others, our hearts feel happy.
  • God loves us when we love others. Being kind to people and animals makes God smile.
  • Small gifts given with love are better than big gifts given without caring.

Try this at home

Each day, find one small thing you can give or share with someone in your family - a drawing, a flower, help with a job, or a kind word. Before you give it, think 'This is for Krishna' in your heart.

Duration: Once daily for one week
Watch for: You will notice that giving makes you feel warm and happy inside, and your family will smile more.

Activities

  • Love Garden Craft · 20 min

    Create a paper plate garden with flowers showing different ways to express love and kindness

  • Sharing Circle Game · 15 min

    Sit in a circle and take turns sharing how you can give to others, passing a flower token

  • Krishna's Name Song · 10 min

    Learn and sing a simple song with Krishna's names, clapping to the rhythm

  • Nature's Gifts Hunt · 15 min

    Find natural items (leaves, flowers, stones) that you could offer as gifts, discussing how nature gives freely
