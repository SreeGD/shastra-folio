Vinaya

Apaishunam

अपैशुनम् · a-pai-SHU-nam

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Apaishunam means NOT finding fault in others and NOT whispering mean things about them behind their backs. When I see someone's mistake, I do not run to tell the whole class. I do not make a circle with my friends and whisper 'did you SEE what she did?' I close my mouth kindly. I see the good FIRST. If something is really important, I go straight to a grown-up I trust — I do not gossip. Krishna loves the child whose mouth is a safe place for other people's little mistakes.

What it is not

Apaishunam is NOT hiding a really big bad thing that hurts someone. If a friend is being bullied, or something scary is happening, we DO tell a grown-up we trust — straight to the grown-up, not in whispers. Apaishunam is also NOT pretending to like someone's mistake — we can quietly help them fix it, or just leave it alone. Apaishunam only means we do not make other people's mistakes into a FUN STORY to share with our friends. A mistake is not a snack to pass around.

Word study

Word
अपैशुनम् (apaiśunam)
Say it
a-pai-SHU-nam
Meaning
NO fault-finding. NO whispering mean things about others behind their back. NO gossip. The a- at the start means 'no', and paiśuna means 'tale-bearing, slander, the carrying of mean stories from mouth to mouth'. A safe mouth — a mouth that does not spread other people's little wrongs.
Example
When Meera closed her mouth instead of joining the whisper-circle about Arjun, that was apaishunam.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम्।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम्॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Krishna tells Arjuna the shining marks of a divine heart. One of them is apaiśunam — NO fault-finding. When I see someone's mistake, I do not run to tell everyone. I do not whisper behind their back. I see the good first. My mouth keeps the little faults of others safe, like a closed hand keeps a small bird safe.

Vidura Keeps His Mouth a Safe Place

Mahabharata — Udyoga Parva and Sabha Parva (retold for children)

Long ago, in the grand palace at Hastinapura, lived a wise uncle named Vidura — the king's younger brother. He lived simply: a small house, a soft voice. And he had one rule. Vidura's mouth was a SAFE PLACE for everybody's little mistakes.

The palace was a WHISPERY place. Prince Duryodhana and his brothers whispered mean things about their cousins the Pandavas all day. 'Yudhishthira is slow!' 'Bhima is just a big belly!' They made it a game.

People rushed to Vidura with hot stories. 'Did you HEAR what Duryodhana said today?' A minister would huff, 'That so-and-so spoke SO badly of YOU!'

Vidura only smiled and put a finger to his lips.

'My friend,' he said softly, 'speak to THEM — not about them to me. My ears are not a basket. My mouth is not a drum.'

Even when the blind king asked his advice, Vidura never said 'wicked Shakuni' or 'greedy Duryodhana.' He named the WRONG, never the name.

Slowly, everyone began to TRUST Vidura. Palace children ran to him when they broke something — because his mouth was safe.

One day Lord Krishna came to Hastinapura. Duryodhana prepared a HUGE feast. 'Krishna must stay with ME!'

But Krishna smiled. 'No. I will stay at Vidura-uncle's house.'

The palace gasped. 'But he has only a mud floor!'

Krishna laughed. 'Exactly. At Vidura's house, no one whispers. The spinach there is sweeter than your sweets here.'

And so the Lord of all the worlds sat on Vidura's mud floor, eating plain rice — because the non-whispering mouth was the real feast.

That gentle closed-mouth heart has a name. Apaishunam.

Verse

SB 11.28.1

परस्वभावकर्माणि न प्रशंसेन्न गर्हयेत्।
विश्वमेकात्मकं पश्यन्प्रकृत्या पुरुषेण च॥

para-svabhāva-karmāṇi na praśaṁsen na garhayet viśvam ekātmakaṁ paśyan prakṛtyā puruṣeṇa ca

Krishna tells Uddhava: 'Children, do not praise and do not find fault with the natures and doings of other people. See the whole world as ONE — held together by Me, Krishna. When you see Me in every person, your mouth will stop whispering, because you will know — whatever I say about anyone, I am saying about Krishna sitting inside them.'

Meera Does Not Join the Whisper-Circle

Meera was in Standard Three. She had four friends — Priya, Sara, Naina, and Kavya.

One Tuesday at lunch, Arjun — a quiet boy from class — carried his tiffin with an untied shoelace. He TRIPPED. His dal went SPLASH. His face went TOMATO RED. Arjun sat alone with one small roti.

Meera's heart hurt for him.

But her friends pulled into a whisper-circle. Priya giggled, 'Did you SEE how he tripped?!' Sara whispered, 'His dal went EVERYWHERE!' Naina laughed, 'His face went SO red!' Kavya added, 'SO clumsy!'

They turned to Meera — 'Your turn! Say something funny!'

A little joke was on her tongue. But something felt heavy inside — a sticky ball in her tummy. Arjun sat alone, shoulders small.

Meera remembered Amma's words. 'Krishna lives inside every person. When you speak badly, even in a whisper, Krishna hears it from INSIDE them.'

Meera took a slow breath. She closed her mouth. The joke went back inside.

'His shoelaces come untied,' she said softly. 'Mine do too. Let's not whisper. Let's play jump-rope instead.'

Then she walked straight to Arjun. 'Can I eat with you? I have extra chapati.'

Arjun's face went from sad to shining. Meera tore her chapati in half. Arjun giggled.

After lunch, all four friends came over. 'Sorry, Arjun,' whispered Priya.

That evening at the puja shelf, Meera whispered — 'Krishna, my mouth wanted to say a mean thing. I closed it. My heart feels clean, like a window wiped with a cloth.'

That closed-mouth, clean-heart feeling has a name. Apaishunam.

Core teaching

  • Apaishunam means NOT whispering mean things about other people behind their back. When I hear my friends making a whisper-circle about someone who is not there, I do not join. I say softly, 'Let us not talk about them.' I see the good in the other person FIRST. Krishna names apaishunam among the shining gifts of a divine heart in Gita 16.2 — right next to truth and non-hurting.
  • Apaishunam means my mouth is a SAFE PLACE for other people's little mistakes. When Priya spills her water, I do not rush to tell Mala 'did you SEE Priya?!' When Ravi forgets his lunch, I do not giggle about it at the bus stop. Like Vidura, I keep their tiny wrongs safe inside me. Even the bird knows it is safe in a gentle closed hand — so a friend's small mistake is safe in a gentle closed mouth.
  • Apaishunam does NOT mean hiding big bad things. If someone is being bullied, or something is really wrong, I go STRAIGHT to a grown-up I trust — not in whispers, but quietly and directly. That is different from gossip. Gossip is when we share someone's small mistake for fun with people who do not need to know. Apaishunam only closes the mouth against gossip — never against the truth that keeps a friend safe.

Try this at home

For one week, keep a Safe-Mouth Book beside your bed. Each night before sleep, with Amma or Appa, think back over the day and share three things: (1) ONE time today your mouth was SAFE — you did NOT whisper a mean thing about someone, even when it was tempting. (2) ONE GOOD thing you noticed about someone today — a classmate, a cousin, a sibling, anyone (no mean buts). (3) If any mean thing slipped out, draw a small heart with a tiny crack and write 'tomorrow I try again.' No shame — just a fresh start. Before sleeping, cup your hand softly at your heart (like you are holding a little sparrow safe inside) and whisper: 'Krishna, my mouth is safe tonight. The little bird inside is safe. Thank You.'

Duration: Every night for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific moments when they closed their mouth against a whisper-chance, and can name at least 5 good things about at least 5 different classmates or family members. They can demonstrate the 'soft-fist-at-heart' gesture while saying 'apaishunam' softly. The Safe-Mouth Book has at least 5 entries and any 'cracks' have been followed by a successful 'safe mouth' the next day.

Activities

  • Join the Whisper or Walk Away? · 6 min

    Circle JOIN or WALK AWAY for each scenario. Remember that big bad things must be told to a trusted grown-up — that is different from gossip.

  • Draw Two Mouths · 10 min

    In one box, draw a Whisper-Mouth with mean little arrow-words flying out. In the other, draw a Safe-Mouth with a tiny bird resting on gentle closed lips. Write the apaishunam promise underneath.

  • The Closed-Hand Chant · 4 min

    Make a soft fist near your heart, as if holding a tiny sparrow safe inside. Chant 'a-pai-shu-nam' three times slowly. At the end, open your hand gently and let the 'sparrow' fly up.

  • The See-the-Good-First Game · 10 min

    With a partner, take turns naming a classmate and quickly saying ONE good thing about them. No mean buts. If a mean thing slips out, restart. Try to reach five classmates with five good things.

  • The Safe-Mouth Book · 5 min

    Keep a small book by your bed. Each night, write or draw: one time today your mouth was safe, one good thing you noticed about someone, and a tiny cracked heart if a mean thing slipped (with 'tomorrow I try again').
