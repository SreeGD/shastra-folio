Dharma

Dana

दान · DAH-na

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Dana means giving with a happy heart. When we see someone without something we have, we share. We give food, toys, clothes, time, or a kind word. Krishna is the real owner of everything — we are only His helpers, passing gifts from His hands to His children.

What it is not

Dana is not giving only to get thank-yous. It is not giving the broken toy while keeping the good one. It is not making noise about our giving. It is not giving with a grumpy heart.

Word study

Word
दान (dāna)
Say it
DAH-na
Meaning
Giving with a happy heart. Sharing what we have so others can be happy too.
Example
When I gave my biggest sweet to the hungry boy, that was dana.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.1

श्रीभगवानुवाच
अभयं सत्त्वसंशुद्धिर्ज्ञानयोगव्यवस्थितिः।
दानं दमश्च यज्ञश्च स्वाध्यायस्तप आर्जवम्॥

śrī-bhagavān uvāca abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhir jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ dānaṁ damaś ca yajñaś ca svādhyāyas tapa ārjavam

The Supreme Lord says: Be brave. Keep your heart pure. Learn about God. Give to others. Control yourself. Worship. Read good books. Be disciplined. Be honest.

Sudama's Pouch of Puffed Rice

Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 10, Chapter 80-81

Long ago, a poor brahmin named Sudama lived in a tiny hut. He had studied at the gurukul with Krishna when they were both boys. But now Sudama was very poor. Sometimes his children went to bed hungry.

Sudama's kind wife said, 'Go visit your old friend Krishna. He is a king now. He will be happy to see you.'

Sudama was shy. 'But I cannot go empty-handed.'

His wife smiled. She ran to the neighbours. She borrowed a tiny handful of puffed rice. She tied it in an old torn cloth.

Sudama walked many days to Dwarka. The guards saw his torn clothes. They almost stopped him. But Krishna saw Sudama and came running down the palace steps!

Krishna hugged His old friend. Krishna washed Sudama's tired feet with His own hands.

Then Krishna asked, 'Friend, what did you bring for Me?'

Sudama blushed. He tried to hide the little cloth. But Krishna snatched it happily and ate a handful of puffed rice with the biggest smile.

'Nothing is more precious than what a friend brings with love,' Krishna said.

Sudama never asked for anything. He walked home.

When he reached his village, there was no hut — only a shining golden palace. His wife and children were dressed in silk, laughing.

Sudama understood. 'Krishna, You returned my little gift as a mountain.'

This is dana. When we give to Krishna with a pure heart, Krishna gives back a thousandfold.

Ravi and the Birthday Bag

Ravi was turning seven. His Amma had given him a bright red bag. It was full of ladoos, jalebi, and colourful toffees. Ravi hugged the bag close to his heart.

As he walked out of the sweet shop, he saw a small boy standing near the gate. The boy was about Ravi's age. His clothes were torn. His feet had no slippers. His eyes were big and quiet.

Ravi stopped. He held his bag tighter. 'These are MINE. Amma bought them for MY birthday.'

But something inside him felt uncomfortable.

Ravi remembered what Amma had told him. 'Everything we have is Krishna's. We are only His helpers. When someone needs something, Krishna is asking through them.'

Ravi looked at the boy again. The boy was not begging. He was just standing quietly in the sun.

Slowly, Ravi opened his bag. He picked out the biggest, freshest ladoo — the one HE wanted most. He walked to the boy.

'Here,' Ravi said. 'For you. Happy day.'

The boy's face lit up like a hundred diyas. 'Thank you!'

Ravi walked home. The bag felt lighter. But something else felt FULLER inside — a warm, sparkly feeling in his chest.

That night, Ravi told Amma. She smiled and kissed his forehead. 'The one who gives is the one who is truly full.'

Ravi went to sleep smiling. This is dana.

Core teaching

  • Dana means sharing what we have with a happy heart. Food, toys, clothes, time, a kind word — everything we give, we give to Krishna through His children.
  • When we give, we give the BEST — not the leftover. We give quietly — not for applause. We give happily — not grumpily. This is the way of Sudama and Krishna.
  • Giving does not make us smaller. Giving makes our heart bigger. The more we share, the more Krishna fills us with love.

Try this at home

Each day this week, do ONE act of dana — big or small. Give a crayon to a friend. Help Amma with a chore without being asked. Share your snack. Say a kind word. Drop a pebble in your Giving Jar for each act. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa what you gave today. Whisper, 'Krishna, I gave this for You.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the Giving Jar has at least 7 pebbles, and the child can tell one specific story of giving that made someone else happy.

Activities

  • Share or Keep · 6 min

    Circle the right answer for each picture scenario.

  • My Giving Tree · 10 min

    Draw a tree. On each leaf, write one thing you can give this week.

  • Say It With Me · 3 min

    Chant 'dana' three times with palms cupped outward as if offering.

  • The Giving Circle · 10 min

    Place one small thing in the centre. Take turns picking up someone else's gift with a 'Thank you Krishna'.

  • The Giving Jar · 5 min

    Drop a pebble in the jar for each act of giving this week. Count at week's end.
