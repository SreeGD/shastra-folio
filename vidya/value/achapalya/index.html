Vinaya

Achapalya

अचापल्य · ah-CHAH-pal-ya

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Achapalya means I stay with ONE thing at a time. When I pick up a book, I read a little of it before I put it down. When I start a puzzle, I finish at least one piece before I run away. When I begin a song, I sing it to the end. I do not flap and flit like a moth around a lamp. My heart learns to land — and STAY. Krishna's children are steady children. They are like small quiet trees, not like little falling leaves.

What it is not

Achapalya is not being slow or being a sleepy stone. It is not refusing to play anything new. It is also not being STUCK — if Amma calls for dinner, of course I stop my game. The opposite of achapalya is CHAPALYA — jumping-jumping, flitting-flitting, picking up one toy and dropping it in ten seconds, saying 'yes' and then 'no' and then 'yes' again. A chapala heart is a tired heart. An achapala heart is a full, shining heart.

Word study

Word
अचापल्य (acāpalya)
Say it
ah-CHAH-pal-ya
Meaning
Not-flitting. Not-jumping-from-one-thing-to-another. Steadiness. The staying heart. When I begin, I stay. When I pick up, I finish.
Example
When Tara folded the paper boat from beginning to end without running to her blocks, that was acāpalya.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम्।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम्॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Krishna lists the beautiful qualities of divine children. One of them is acāpalam — steadiness. It means: when I start something, I stay. I do not hop from toy to toy, from game to game, from 'yes' to 'no' in one minute. My heart holds on. My hands finish what they begin.

Hanuman's One-Pointed Leap Across the Ocean

Sundara Kanda of the Ramayana (Valmiki)

Long ago, Sita-Mata was stolen by Ravana and locked in a garden on the island of Lanka. Lord Rama was very sad.

A circle of monkey-warriors sat on the southern shore. 'One hundred yojanas of ocean!' they cried. 'No one can leap that far!'

Then the brave monkey-boy Hanuman stood up. His eyes shone. 'I will go.'

He bowed to Rama in his heart. He thought only one thought — 'Sita-Mata. Sita-Mata.' Then — WHOOOSH! — he leapt into the sky like a golden arrow.

Soon a BIG golden mountain rose from the water. 'Hanuman!' it called sweetly. 'Sit on me. Eat sweet fruits. Rest your legs!'

The fruits smelled DELICIOUS. Hanuman's legs were tired. But Hanuman did not stop. 'Thank you, dear Mainaka,' he said, touching it gently with one foot. 'But my heart is on Sita-Mata. I must go!'

And he flew on.

Then the demoness Surasa rose up. Her mouth opened BIGGER than the sky. 'Come INTO my mouth!' Hanuman shrank to the size of a thumb, zipped in, zipped out, bowed, and kept flying.

Then the demoness Simhika grabbed his shadow. Hanuman dived through her and flew out — in one clean minute.

NOTHING could make Hanuman hop or flit. His heart held ONE thing — Sita-Mata's face — and his body did ONE thing — flying to Lanka.

That is achapalya. The ocean had ten tricks. Hanuman had ONE thought.

Finally, green trees appeared below. Lanka! He landed softly, like a leaf. And there, under the ashoka tree, sat Sita-Mata. Her face lit up like the moon.

She would not have been found if Hanuman had stopped on the golden mountain. A steady heart can cross any distance.

Verse

SB 5.5.14

न कुर्यात्कर्हिचित्सङ्गं तमस्तीव्रं तितीरिषुः।
धर्मार्थकाममोक्षाणां यदत्यन्तविघातकम्॥

na kuryāt karhicit saṅgaṁ tamas tīvraṁ titīriṣuḥ dharma-artha-kāma-mokṣāṇāṁ yad atyanta-vighātakam

Lord Rishabhadeva says: A child who wants to cross the great ocean of flitting should not keep flitting from thing to thing. Let the heart rest on ONE good thing — and that one thing will carry it across.

Tara Finishes the Paper Boat

Tara was seven. She loved drawing, blocks, dolls, books, playdough — ALL AT ONCE.

On Saturday morning, Tara woke BUZZING. Drawing book — one dragon-head. 'BORING!' Blocks — four stacks. 'BORING!' Doll — one shoe off. 'BORING!' Book — three words. 'BORING!' Playdough — one squish. 'BORING!'

By lunch-time, her floor was a STORM. A half-dragon. A roofless castle. A doll with one bare foot.

Tara sat in the middle of the storm. Suddenly she felt TIRED — not the good tired, the sad tired. Her heart felt wobbly, like jelly.

Amma came in and sat cross-legged beside her.

'Tara, my flitter-butterfly. How many things did you start today?'

'Five, Amma.'

'And how many did you finish?'

Tara looked at her fingers. 'None.'

Amma smiled softly. 'That is chapalya, beta — the flitting heart. Krishna teaches a different word — acāpalam — the staying heart.'

Amma pulled out a paper-folding book. 'Just ONE paper boat. First fold to last. Only the boat.'

Tara took the paper. Fold one. Fold two. Her eyes wandered to the dragon. She pulled them back. Fold three. Fold four. Slow, careful. Fold five. Fold six.

A tiny paper boat sat in her palm — pointed, neat, ready to sail.

Amma filled a small bucket. Tara placed the boat on the water. It floated.

And Tara felt — for the first time all day — a calm, FULL, shining feeling inside her chest. Like a lamp finally lit.

'That is achapalya, my treasure,' Amma said. 'Finishing feels BIGGER than starting.'

That night, Tara fell asleep with the boat on her bedside. 'Krishna,' she whispered, 'tomorrow I will stay.'

Core teaching

  • Achapalya means I stay with ONE thing at a time. When I start a book, I read it to a stopping place. When I start a puzzle, I finish at least the corners. When I start a song, I sing to the end. My hands learn to hold on. My heart learns to land.
  • Krishna teaches in the Gita — acāpalam — steadiness. Hanuman had one thought — Sita-Mata — and crossed the whole ocean. Tara had one task — the paper boat — and her wobbly heart became calm and full. When we finish ONE small thing well, a little lamp lights up inside. That lamp is Krishna smiling.
  • A flitting heart is a tired heart. Ten games started and none finished leaves my hands empty and my room messy. But ONE thing done — slowly, carefully, to the last fold — fills my heart like warm milk. Achapalya is not slow. It is steady. It is Krishna's gift to children who want a quiet, full, shining heart.

Try this at home

For one week, keep your One-Thing Basket on your shelf. Each morning at breakfast, with Amma or Appa, choose ONE small task for the day — finish the puzzle, read the next chapter, water every plant, fold all your socks, colour one drawing fully. Just ONE. Write or draw it on a little card and drop it in the basket. At bedtime, if you FINISHED it, add a small stone or shell to the basket. If you flitted today — no stone, no shame. Try again tomorrow. Before sleeping, whisper: 'Krishna, today I stayed. Thank You for holding my heart.' If you flitted, whisper: 'Krishna, tomorrow I will stay. Help my heart land.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can point to at least 4 stones in the basket, name the 4 finished tasks specifically, and describe the feeling of 'fullness' after finishing (as opposed to the 'empty' feeling of flitting). The child can also demonstrate the small-tree sitting posture and chant 'achapalya' calmly.

Activities

  • Flit or Stay · 6 min

    Circle FLIT or STAY for each scenario to practice recognizing a staying heart versus a flitting heart.

  • One Thing, One Circle · 10 min

    Draw one big circle. Inside, draw the ONE thing you will finish this week. Colour it with care — do not hop to a new idea.

  • The Staying Chant · 4 min

    Sit cross-legged like a little tree. Hands folded. Chant 'achapalya' three times, one word per breath.

  • The Sitting-Tree Game · 10 min

    With a partner, take turns sitting perfectly still like a little tree while the other counts to twenty. No flitting eyes, no wiggling shoulders.

  • The One-Thing Basket · 5 min

    Keep a small basket. Each day, choose ONE task. Finish it. Add a stone to the basket. Count the stones at week's end.
