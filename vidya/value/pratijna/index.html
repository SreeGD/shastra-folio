Karma

Pratijna

प्रतिज्ञा · pra-TIG-nyaa

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Pratijna means a promise I truly keep. When I say I will do something, I do it — even when I don't feel like it. Even when it is hard. My word is my strength. A small promise kept makes my heart brave. A big promise kept makes my heart like a mountain.

What it is not

Pratijna is not making big promises to look important and then forgetting them. It is not saying 'I promise!' when we already know we will not do it. It is not breaking our word the moment it becomes hard. A promise spoken but not kept is like a flower with no smell — pretty to look at, but empty inside.

Word study

Word

Meaning
A promise I truly keep. A solemn word. A vow. When I say it, I do it — even when it is hard.

When Arjun walked Diya to the temple in the rain, he was keeping his pratijna.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 9.31

क्षिप्रं भवति धर्मात्मा शश्वच्छान्तिं निगच्छति।
कौन्तेय प्रतिजानीहि न मे भक्तः प्रणश्यति॥

kṣipraṁ bhavati dharmātmā śaśvac-chāntiṁ nigacchati kaunteya pratijānīhi na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati

Krishna says: O Arjuna, declare this as a solemn promise — My devotee is never lost. A person who walks in dharma quickly becomes peaceful. You can take this as My vow.

Devavrata Becomes Bhishma — The Boy Who Made a Terrible Promise

Mahabharata, Adi Parva

Long ago, a young prince named Devavrata lived in the great kingdom of Hastinapur. His father was King Shantanu. His mother was the sacred river Ganga herself. Devavrata was strong, kind, and loved by everyone. The whole kingdom said, 'One day he will be our king.'

But one evening, Devavrata saw his father sitting sadly by the river.

'Father,' he asked, 'why are you sad?'

The king tried to smile, but his eyes were heavy. At last he said, 'I met a good young woman named Satyavati. I wish to marry her. But her father says — his grandson must be king, not you. I cannot promise that. So I have come away.'

Devavrata went straight to Satyavati's father, the fisherman chief.

'I give you my word,' he said. 'I will never become king. My half-brother — Satyavati's son — will be king.'

The fisherman shook his head. 'But YOU may have children one day. THEY may fight for the throne.'

Devavrata stood very still. Then he raised his hand to the sky.

'Then hear me! I take a terrible vow. I will never marry. I will never have children. I will serve the throne of Hastinapur all my life — but I will never sit on it.'

The earth trembled. Flowers fell from the sky. Gods leaned from the clouds and whispered, 'Bhishma! Bhishma!' which means — the one whose vow is terrible.

From that day, Devavrata was called Bhishma.

He lived a very long life. He became the grandfather of the Pandavas and the Kauravas. Many times life was hard. Many times it would have been easier to break his promise. But Bhishma never did. Not once. Not in a hundred years.

His word was his true crown — and it was bigger than any throne.

This is pratijna.

साधुभिर्ग्रस्तहृदयो भक्तैर्भक्तजनप्रियः॥

ahaṁ bhakta-parādhīno hy asvatantra iva dvija sādhubhir grasta-hṛdayo bhaktair bhakta-jana-priyaḥ

Krishna says to the sage Durvasa: 'I have made a promise, O brahmana — I belong to My devotees. I am not free on My own; My heart is held by the saintly ones who love Me.' Thus Krishna Himself keeps His vow to protect those who trust Him.

Arjun and the Rainy-Day Promise

It was Saturday evening. Arjun was eight years old. His little sister Diya was turning five on Sunday.

Diya tugged his sleeve. 'Bhaiya, will you come with me to the temple tomorrow morning? Before school? Just you and me. I want to put my birthday flowers at Krishna's feet.'

Arjun smiled. The temple was only ten minutes away. Easy.

'I promise,' he said. 'Pakka. Seven o'clock sharp.'

Diya's eyes sparkled. She went to sleep hugging her little basket of marigolds.

But in the morning — oh, the morning was bad.

Rain drummed on the roof. The wind was cold. Arjun's blanket was thick and warm. His eyes did not want to open.

His mother peeked in. 'Arjun-beta, Diya is already waiting at the door. Are you coming?'

'Mmm,' said Arjun. 'It is SO cold, Amma. Tell her tomorrow.'

But then he heard Diya's small voice in the hallway — 'I will wait. Bhaiya promised.'

Something moved inside Arjun's chest. A promise was a promise. His teacher had told him the story of Bhishma — the boy who kept a terrible vow his whole life. Arjun's was not terrible. Arjun's was tiny. Just a walk.

He sat up. He put on his sweater. He grabbed the big black umbrella.

'Ready!' he called.

Diya's face lit up like a little lamp.

They walked together through the rain. Arjun held the umbrella high so Diya would not get wet. The marigolds bounced in her basket. At the temple, Diya placed every flower gently at Krishna's feet. She closed her eyes and folded her tiny hands.

On the way home, Arjun noticed something strange. The cold did not bother him anymore. His heart felt tall. Quiet. Strong — like a mountain.

He had kept his word.

This is pratijna.

Core teaching

  • Pratijna means a promise I truly keep. When I say I will do something, I do it — even when I do not feel like it. My word becomes my strength.
  • We should think BEFORE we promise. A small promise kept is bigger than ten big promises forgotten. If we are not sure, we say, 'Let me think first.' When we finally say YES, we mean it.
  • Each kept promise makes the heart a little stronger — like stacking bricks in a tall wall. Krishna Himself makes promises, and never breaks them. When we keep our word, we become a little bit like Him.

Try this at home

For one week, wear your Promise Ribbon. Every morning, make ONE small promise to yourself or your family — 'Today I will water the tulsi.' 'Today I will greet Dadi kindly.' 'Today I will finish my homework before play.' Touch the ribbon and whisper 'pratijna'. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa which promise you kept today. If you forgot, say 'Tomorrow I will try again.' On Sunday, count how many promises you kept — and whisper to Krishna, 'My word is Yours.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can name at least 3 specific small promises they kept. They begin to pause before promising — saying 'Let me think first' — and can demonstrate the hand-on-heart pratijna gesture with quiet strength.

Activities

  • Promise Kept or Promise Broken · 6 min

    Read each scenario and circle KEPT or BROKEN.

  • My Promise Wall · 10 min

    Draw a wall of bricks. On each brick, write or draw one small promise you will keep this week.

  • Say It With Me · 3 min

    Stand tall with hand on heart. Chant 'pratijna' three times with a strong, kind voice.

  • The Two Voices Role-Play · 8 min

    With a partner, act out the Sleepy Voice and the True Voice. Which voice feels lighter in the chest?

  • The Promise Ribbon · 5 min

    Wear a small ribbon. Touch it each time you make a promise. Tell Amma each evening which promise you kept.
