Dharma

Asteya

अस्तेय · ah-STAY-ya

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Asteya means we do not take what is not ours. We ASK first. If something belongs to someone else, we say, 'May I please borrow this?' We return it the same way we got it. Honest hands feel light and happy inside.

What it is not

Asteya is not slipping something into our pocket when no one is looking. It is not keeping what we found without trying to return it. It is not taking the biggest piece of cake before anyone else, or copying a friend's answer.

Word study

Word
अस्तेय (asteya)
Say it
ah-STAY-ya
Meaning
Not-stealing. Asking before we take. Returning what we borrow. Honest hands and an honest heart.
Example
When I asked before using my sister's crayons, I was practicing asteya.

Anchor verse

Yoga Sutras 2.30

अहिंसासत्यास्तेयब्रह्मचर्यापरिग्रहा यमाः॥

ahiṁsā-satyāsteya-brahmacaryāparigrahā yamāḥ

Do not hurt. Speak the truth. Do not take what is not yours. Be pure in heart. Do not grab and hoard. These are the five gentle rules.

Hanuman Brings Back the Ring

Ramayana, Sundara Kanda

Long ago, Lord Rama had a little helper named Hanuman. Hanuman was strong like a mountain, and his heart was soft like a flower.

One day, Mother Sita was taken far away to the island of Lanka. Rama was very sad. He called Hanuman and said, 'Please go find Sita. Take this ring from my finger. When she sees it, she will know you are my friend.'

The ring was gold. It had Rama's name shining on it. It was the most beautiful ring in the world.

Hanuman bowed. 'Yes, my Lord.' He put the ring gently in his small pouch.

Hanuman jumped across the big blue sea! Whoosh! He flew past clouds and birds. In his pouch, the little ring felt warm.

Hanuman could have thought, 'This ring is so pretty. Maybe I will keep it.' But no! Hanuman did not think that way. The ring belonged to Rama. His job was only to carry it.

In Lanka, Hanuman found Mother Sita under a tree. She was weeping. Hanuman took out the ring and placed it carefully in her hand.

Sita's eyes filled with happy tears. 'Oh, this is my Rama's ring!' She gave Hanuman her own hair-jewel to take back.

Hanuman flew home with the jewel held tight. He did not peek at it. He did not keep even one shiny bead.

He gave the jewel straight to Rama. Rama smiled. 'Dear Hanuman, you are the best helper. What is Mine, you return to Me. This is true service.'

That is asteya. Honest hands. Honest heart.

Verse

SB 8.1.10

ईशावास्यमिदं सर्वं यत्किञ्च जगत्यां जगत्।
तेन त्यक्तेन भुञ्जीथा मा गृधः कस्यस्विद्धनम्॥

īśāvāsyam idaṁ sarvaṁ yat kiñca jagatyāṁ jagat tena tyaktena bhuñjīthā mā gṛdhaḥ kasya svid dhanam

Everything in this whole world belongs to the Lord. Take only what He gives you with a kind heart. Do not grab what is not yours.

Meera and the Found Wallet

Meera was walking home through the little park. The neem leaves were dancing. A koel was singing on the branch.

As she passed the red bench, something caught her eye.

'A wallet!' said Meera.

It was a small brown wallet. She opened it gently. Inside were three crisp hundred-rupee notes. A bus ticket. A small photo of a smiling old man holding a baby.

Her friend Rohan came running. His eyes grew big.

'Wow, Meera! You found it. Finders keepers! Let us buy ice cream!'

Meera held the wallet tight. Her tummy wanted ice cream. Her mouth watered.

But then she remembered Amma's words. 'If it is not ours, it is not ours. Even if no one sees.'

Meera closed the wallet. 'No, Rohan. This is someone's money. Maybe the grandfather in the photo. He must be very worried.'

Rohan frowned. 'But no one will know!'

'I will know,' said Meera softly. 'And Krishna will know.'

Meera walked to the park gate. The old security uncle was there.

'Uncle, I found this on the bench.'

The uncle's eyes shone. 'Good girl, Meera! Just now a grandfather came asking. Come, we will give it to him.'

The grandfather was the man from the photo. His hands trembled as he counted. Every note was there.

He placed his palm on Meera's head. 'Beta, you have given me back more than money. You have given me faith in little children.'

Meera walked home. Her pocket was empty, but her heart was full and light. That is asteya.

Core teaching

  • Asteya means we do not take what is not ours. We ASK before we borrow. We return what we borrow. Honest hands feel light and happy inside.
  • If we find something that is not ours, we try to give it back. A found thing is not a free thing. Someone is looking for it.
  • Everything in the whole world belongs to Krishna. When we share and ask politely, Krishna is pleased with us. Honest children are Krishna's dearest friends.

Try this at home

For one week, do the Honest Hands Diary. Every day at school and at home, practice ASKING before you borrow anything — a crayon, a toy, a snack, a hug. At bedtime, share with Amma or Appa one time you asked, and one time you returned something. If you find something that is not yours, hand it to a grown-up right away. Also: every night before sleep, place your hands at your heart and whisper, 'Krishna, my hands were honest today.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 times they asked before taking, and at least 2 times they returned something that was borrowed. They can also name one thing that 'belongs to everyone' and should be shared.

Activities

  • Ask First · 6 min

    Look at each picture and circle the smiling face if the child asked first, or the sad face if they took without asking.

  • Mine, Yours, or Ours · 10 min

    Draw three circles — MINE, YOURS, OURS — and place things from your life into each circle.

  • Say It With Me · 3 min

    Chant 'asteya' three times with palms at your heart.

  • The Magic Words Role-Play · 8 min

    With a partner, practice turning a grabbing sentence into an asking sentence.

  • The Honest Hands Diary · 5 min

    For one week, each night draw a 🙂 if you asked before taking and a 🌸 if you returned what you borrowed. Show your diary to Amma or Appa.
