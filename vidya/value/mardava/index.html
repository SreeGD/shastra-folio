Prema

Mardava

मार्दव · MAAR-da-va

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Mārdava means GENTLENESS. It is soft hands — I hold my baby brother like he is a flower. It is a soft voice — I speak to my friend like I am singing a lullaby. It is a soft heart — when I see a little puppy shiver, my heart melts like butter in the sun. Mārdava is Krishna's own quality. When baby Krishna touched Yashoda's cheek, His tiny fingers were so soft that her heart melted. That melting is mārdava.

What it is not

Mārdava is not being weak or scared. A soft heart is a STRONG heart — soft like a pillow that holds everyone, not soft like wet paper that tears. Mārdava is not being quiet because I am afraid. It is being gentle because I love. Mārdava is not the same as being a pushover who lets people break toys — I can say 'please be gentle' in a soft voice, and that is still mārdava.

Word study

Word
मार्दव (mardava)
Say it
MAAR-da-va
Meaning
Gentleness. Softness of hand, voice, and heart. The melting quality — like butter in the sun. The way baby Krishna touched Yashoda's knee. The way we hold a flower or a baby bird.
Example
When Ananya knelt down slowly and whispered to the shivering puppy, that was mārdava.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 16.2

अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम्।
दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम्॥

ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam

Krishna says: These soft and shining qualities belong to the children of light — not hurting, truthfulness, not getting angry, letting go, quiet peace, kind speech, kindness to every living being, no greed, and — mārdava — gentleness. Gentleness is soft hands, soft voice, and a soft heart.

Baby Krishna's Soft Touch Melts Yashoda's Heart

Srimad Bhagavatam Canto 10 (Dashama Skandha)

In the village of Vrindavan, long long ago, Mother Yashoda was sitting on her cool stone floor. In front of her was a big clay pot full of fresh milk. She was churning butter. CHURN-CHURN-CHURN. Back and forth. The rope creaked. Her arms were aching.

She had been churning since sunrise. Now the sun was high. She was very tired.

In the next room, baby Krishna woke up from His nap. He was about two years old. His cheeks were round and pink. His little fingers were sticky from sleep. And He was HUNGRY.

He stood up wobble-wobble on His tiny feet. He toddled toward the churning sound.

Here is what most hungry babies do — they SCREAM. They cry loud-loud. They kick. They PULL their mother's sari hard so she drops everything.

But Krishna was Krishna. Krishna was GENTLE.

He crawled the last bit on His hands and knees. Very quietly. He came right up to His mother. She did not see Him. She was still going CHURN-CHURN.

Baby Krishna looked up at her face. She was a little bit cross. Her lips were pressed. Her forehead had a tiny crease.

Krishna did not grab. He did not shout. He simply reached out His tiny soft hand. And He placed it — GENTLY — on her knee.

Just one little touch. Softer than a butterfly. Softer than a flower petal.

Yashoda's arms froze mid-churn. She looked down.

There He was. Her little Krishna. With His big eyes. With His tiny soft fingers resting on her knee. With His tiny round mouth starting to tremble just a little — not a big cry, just a little wobble.

And something happened inside Yashoda.

Her tiredness — GONE.

Her crossness — GONE.

Her heart — MELTED. Like the butter in her pot. Warm. Soft. Golden.

She dropped the rope. She opened her arms. 'Oh my Lala! My soft little Lala!'

She lifted Him up. She held Him close. She kissed His tiny fingers — those same fingers that had touched her knee so gently.

Krishna laughed — a soft bubbling laugh. He patted her cheek. Soft-soft-soft.

The wise people who watch such things said: 'See how Krishna teaches His mother? He did not need to shout. He did not need to grab. He only needed to be SOFT. And softness is stronger than storm.'

This is mārdava. The soft hand. The soft heart. The touch so gentle that even a tired mother's whole day becomes honey.

And whenever a child is gentle like baby Krishna — soft hand on Amma's knee, soft voice at the puppy's ear, soft pat on a crying friend's back — Krishna is right there, smiling from the heart, saying: 'Yes, little one. That is Mine. That is mārdava.'

Verse

SB 10.9.18

त्वयि मे नन्दनन्देच्छा विद्यते बालकृष्ण।
मार्दवेन मनस्तृप्तिं करुणां जनयत्यसौ॥

tvayi me nanda-nandeccha vidyate bāla-kṛṣṇa mārdavena manas-tṛptiṁ karuṇāṁ janayaty asau

Little baby Krishna — Nanda's son — moves so softly that even a tired mother's heart fills with peace. His gentle touch brings compassion into the world. Whoever is soft like Him pleases Krishna most.

Ananya and the Shivering Puppy

It was raining in Bangalore. Not a big storm — just that steady pitter-patter rain that makes the evening grey and wet. Ananya was seven years old. She had just come home from school. Her shoes were splashy. Her hair was damp.

As she walked up to her apartment building, she heard a tiny noise. A WHIMPER. Very small. Very sad.

'Kyun-kyun-kyun...'

Ananya stopped. She listened. It was coming from under the parked scooter.

She bent down. Under the scooter, in the dry little space, was a TINY brown puppy. Its fur was wet at the edges. Its paws were muddy. Its eyes were huge and brown and SCARED. It was shivering all over — little shake-shake-shakes.

Ananya's heart went SQUEEZE.

'Oh! Poor baby!'

Her first thought was — RUN. Grab him. Hug him. Take him home RIGHT NOW.

But she stopped. She remembered Amma's words from yesterday, when they had seen a frightened kitten in the park. Amma had said: 'Ananya-kutti, when a little one is scared, we must be soft. Soft hands. Soft voice. Soft heart. That is mārdava. If we run at them, they run away. If we be soft, they come to us.'

Ananya took a deep breath. She lowered herself — slowly, slowly — onto her knees. The ground was wet. She did not care.

She did not reach. She did not grab. She held out one hand, palm up, very still. Like a little saucer.

And she began to whisper. Almost like a song.

'Hello, little one... don't be scared... I am soft... I am soft... you are safe...'

Her voice was quiet as a feather.

The puppy tilted its head. It stopped shivering for a tiny moment. It looked at her hand. It sniffed the air.

Ananya did not move. She stayed soft. She kept whisper-singing.

One little paw came out from under the scooter. Then another. The puppy crept — inch by inch — toward her hand. Its nose touched her fingertips. Wet. Cold.

Ananya still did not grab. She just let her hand stay soft.

The puppy sniffed her wrist. Then — oh, wonder — it climbed right into her lap. It curled up in a tiny brown ball. And it stopped shivering.

Ananya put one soft hand on its back. Soft as a feather. She felt its little heart going thump-thump-thump. Then slower. Then calm.

'Good boy,' she whispered. 'Good soft boy.'

She carried him upstairs — slow, slow, slow — so he would not wake.

Amma opened the door. Her eyes went big. 'Oh, Ananya!'

Ananya smiled. 'Amma, I did it. I was SOFT. Hands, voice, heart. And he came to me himself.'

Amma knelt down. She touched Ananya's cheek with one gentle finger — soft as a feather. 'My little one. That is mārdava. That is Krishna's own quality. When you are soft, even a scared world comes home to you.'

That night, the puppy slept at the foot of Ananya's bed. Warm. Safe. Dry. And every time Ananya stroked his back — softly, softly — she remembered: soft hands, soft voice, soft heart.

Core teaching

  • Mārdava means soft hands. When I pick up my baby sister, I pick her up like she is a flower petal. When I turn a book's page, I turn it like it is a butterfly's wing. When I pat a dog, I pat it as if my hand is made of feathers. Soft hands show that my heart respects the little ones, the small things, and the tender moments of life.
  • Mārdava means soft voice. When Amma has a headache, I whisper. When my friend is sad, I speak quietly. When a baby is sleeping, I make my voice as small as a mouse. A soft voice is not a weak voice — it is a LOVING voice. The Gita says a soft voice is Krishna's own gift: mārdava. Big shouts can break hearts. Small songs can heal them.
  • Mārdava means soft heart. A soft heart MELTS when it sees someone hurt. It bends down to help. It feels the puppy's cold and wants to warm him. It sees Amma tired and wants to hug her. A soft heart is not weak — it is strong, because only a strong heart can stay open when others are suffering. Baby Krishna had the softest heart in the world. When we are soft like Him, He smiles inside us.

Try this at home

For one week, keep a small Gentleness Chart on the wall near your bed. Each day, do ONE gentle act with full mārdava — soft hands, soft voice, soft heart. For example: pat a pet with one soft finger; turn the pages of your storybook like butterfly wings; whisper 'good morning' to Amma when she is sleepy; water a plant without splashing; hug a grandparent slowly; hold a flower without crushing; help a younger sibling with very soft hands. Tick a box each day. Before sleep, whisper to Krishna: 'Dear Krishna, today my hands were soft. My voice was soft. My heart was soft. Like You.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can show the 'butterfly touch' gesture, can whisper 'mārdava' without prompting, and can describe at least 3 specific moments when they used soft hands, soft voice, or soft heart. The Gentleness Chart has at least 5 ticked boxes.

Activities

  • Soft or Rough? · 6 min

    Circle SOFT or ROUGH for each scenario.

  • Draw Your Soft Hand · 10 min

    Trace your hand. Inside, draw three soft things you will hold gently this week.

  • The Soft-Voice Whisper · 3 min

    Stand in a circle. Whisper 'mārdava' three times, softer each time, hand on chest, butterfly-hand movements.

  • The Butterfly Touch Game · 10 min

    With a partner, practise touching a palm as softly as a butterfly. Partner gives feedback: 'Soft' or 'Too hard.' Practise until every touch is a butterfly.

  • The Gentleness Chart · 5 min

    At home, keep a 7-box chart. Each day, do one gentle act with soft hands, soft voice, or soft heart, and tick the box.
