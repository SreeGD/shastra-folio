Karma

Seva

सेवा · say-VAH

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Seva means loving service. It is giving my TIME and my HANDS to help someone — my grandmother, my teacher, a hungry puppy, a tired tree. When I sweep the altar for Krishna, water the tulasi, wash my dadi's feet, or carry the heavy bag for my mother, I am doing seva. Seva is a small action done with a big love. The hands do the work. The heart does the seva.

What it is not

Seva is not helping only to be seen. It is not helping when someone is watching and stopping when no one is. It is not rushing through a chore to finish fast and get praise. It is not helping for a chocolate or a sticker. Seva is quiet, careful, and full of love. If the heart is grumpy, the hands are not really doing seva — they are just moving.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 12.10

अभ्यासेऽप्यसमर्थोऽसि मत्कर्मपरमो भव।
मदर्थमपि कर्माणि कुर्वन्सिद्धिमवाप्स्यसि॥

abhyāse 'py asamartho 'si mat-karma-paramo bhava mad-artham api karmāṇi kurvan siddhim avāpsyasi

Krishna says: If you cannot sit still and meditate, do not worry. Just do MY work. Help others for My sake. Sweep the floor for Me. Feed the puppy for Me. Even a small, simple work done for Me will make your life complete.

Shabari's Berries for Sri Rama

Ramayana, Aranya Kanda (Valmiki) and Ramacharitamanas (Tulsidas)

Deep in the Dandaka forest lived an old tribal woman named Shabari. Her hair was white. Her back was bent. But her eyes still shone like two little lamps.

When Shabari was a young girl, a holy sage named Matanga told her, 'One day, Lord Rama Himself will walk through this forest. He will come to your small hut.'

From that day, Shabari began her seva.

Every morning before the sun came up, she swept her little courtyard with a twig broom. Swish — swish — swish. 'The Lord may come today,' she whispered to the dust.

Every morning she walked to the forest. She collected flowers. She picked sweet wild berries — jujube, or 'ber'. She laid them in a leaf-cup on a flat stone. 'For You, Rama.'

Every evening she lit a tiny oil lamp at her doorway. 'So You will see the path, Rama.'

Years passed. Then decades. Shabari grew old. Her hair turned white. Her hands became wrinkled. But still — every morning — swish, swish, swish. Every morning — fresh berries in a leaf-cup. Every evening — a tiny lamp.

Other sages laughed softly. 'Poor Shabari. She is still waiting. Her Rama will never come.'

Shabari only smiled. 'He will come. And my hands will be ready.'

One golden morning — after many, many years — Shabari heard footsteps. Two tall figures walked toward her hut. A dark, handsome prince with kind eyes. Beside Him, His brother.

'Mother,' the prince said gently. 'I am Rama. I have come.'

Shabari's heart nearly stopped. She fell at His feet. Tears rolled down her cheeks.

Then — because old habits of seva are strong — she ran and brought her little leaf-cup of berries. Before offering each berry, she bit into it FIRST — to make sure it was sweet, not sour. Only the best for her Lord.

Rama smiled. 'Mother, these are the sweetest berries I have ever tasted. Your love has made them sweeter than nectar.'

He ate every berry from her hands. He did not mind that her teeth had touched them first. The seva was bigger than any rule.

And Shabari — after a whole life of quiet seva — sat at her Lord's feet and became completely, completely happy.

This is seva. Swish of a broom. A leaf-cup of berries. A small life given with big love.

yathā taror mūla-niṣecanena tṛpyanti tat-skandha-bhujopaśākhāḥ prāṇopahārāc ca yathendriyāṇāṁ tathaiva sarvārhaṇam acyutejyā

Just as watering the root of a tree makes every branch, twig, and leaf happy — and just as feeding the stomach makes every part of the body strong — in the same way, when we serve Krishna (Acyuta), we are really serving and loving every single living being.

Ananya and Dadi's Morning Walk

Ananya was seven years old. She lived with her Amma, her Appa, and her Dadi. Dadi was her grandmother.

Dadi's knees hurt. She could not walk fast any more. Each morning, Dadi wanted to visit the tulasi plant in the courtyard. She wanted to water it, ring the little bell, and say a small prayer. But the three steps down to the courtyard were hard.

One Sunday morning, Ananya was reading her favourite comic in bed. She heard Dadi's slow shuffle. She heard the bell. She heard Dadi cough.

Ananya turned the page. The comic was at the most exciting part.

Then she heard Dadi sigh — a small, tired sigh.

Ananya stopped. She closed the comic.

'Dadi,' she called, running out. 'Wait! I am coming.'

She took Dadi's wrinkly hand. Very, very slowly, they went down the three steps. Ananya held the small watering can. She poured the water carefully at the base of tulasi. She did not spill even one drop. Dadi rang the little bell — TING — and said a prayer.

'Dadi,' Ananya whispered, 'tomorrow, I will come with you again.'

Dadi's old eyes got a little wet. She squeezed Ananya's hand. 'My little helper.'

From that day, every single morning, Ananya closed her comic. She walked slowly beside Dadi. She poured the water. She rang the bell. Sometimes she even swept the tulasi-mandap with a tiny broom.

No one clapped. No one gave her a sticker. Amma did not even know for a whole week.

But Ananya noticed something. Each morning, as she helped Dadi down the steps, something inside her chest grew WARMER. Like a tiny sun, rising.

One night Amma found out. 'Ananya, why did you not tell me?'

Ananya smiled shyly. 'Because — it is for Krishna and Tulasi-mata. Not for telling.'

Amma hugged her tight.

This is seva. A comic closed. A slow hand held. A tiny watering can. A sun rising in the heart.

Core teaching

  • Seva means loving service. It is giving my hands and my TIME to help someone — a grandparent, a teacher, a pet, a plant, the altar, a classmate. The work may be small, but the love is big. That love is the seva.
  • Seva is different from dana. Dana means giving a THING — a sweet, a toy, a coin. Seva means giving MYSELF — my time, my hands, my care. Dana empties my bag. Seva fills my heart.
  • The best seva is quiet. No clapping, no stickers, no telling everyone. Just the broom sweeping, the water being poured, the hand being held. Krishna sees it. That is enough. When seva is secret and sweet, our heart becomes a small temple.

Try this at home

For one week, keep your Secret Seva Notebook. Every day, find ONE tiny seva — water tulasi, help Dadi or Thatha, carry Amma's bag, feed the street puppy, sweep the altar, help a younger sibling. Do it QUIETLY. Do not announce it. Draw or write it in your Secret Seva Book each night. At bedtime, press your palms in Namaste and whisper: 'Krishna, these are Your hands today.'

Duration: Every day for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can describe at least 3 specific seva moments done without being asked or rewarded, and can demonstrate the Namaste gesture while chanting 'seva' with a quiet smile. The Secret Seva Notebook has at least 5 entries.

Activities

  • Seva or Not Seva? · 6 min

    Circle SEVA or NOT SEVA for each scenario, noticing the heart behind the helping.

  • My Seva Hands · 10 min

    Trace both your hands. In each finger, draw or write one seva you will do this week.

  • Say It With Me · 3 min

    Chant 'seva' three times, pressing palms together in Namaste each time.

  • The Tulasi Watering Practice · 12 min

    With a partner, practice watering the tulasi plant slowly, gently, with Namaste before and after.

  • The Secret Seva Notebook · 5 min

    Keep a small notebook. Each day, draw or write ONE quiet seva no one else saw. Do not show it — just smile at Krishna.
