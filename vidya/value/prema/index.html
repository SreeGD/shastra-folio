Prema

Prema

प्रेम · PRAY-ma

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Prema is the sweetest, purest love in the whole world. It is the love that gives and gives and gives — and does not ask for anything back. Prema is the love the gopis had for Krishna. It is the love Hanuman had for Rama. It is the love that makes your heart so full, so warm, so soft, that you want to share it with everyone — your mother, your pet, the little ant on the floor, and even the big tree in the garden. Prema is love that has melted into sweetness. When prema wakes up, your heart forgets itself.

What it is not

Prema is not 'I love you IF you give me a toffee.' It is not 'I love you because you are mine.' It is not loving only your things and your people while being rude to others. Prema is not jealous love that wants to keep the beloved all for itself. And prema is not the pretend-love we show to get praise. Real prema is not loud. It is quiet, it is warm, and it wants only the happiness of the one it loves.

Word study

Word

Meaning
The sweetest, purest love — love that only gives, love that asks nothing back, love for Krishna that flows out to every creature. The love inside Hanuman's heart. The love inside Anya's hand on the neem tree.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 12.13-14

अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च।
निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी॥
सन्तुष्टः सततं योगी यतात्मा दृढनिश्चयः।
मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः॥

adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṁ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī santuṣṭaḥ satataṁ yogī yatātmā dṛḍha-niścayaḥ mayy arpita-mano-buddhir yo mad-bhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ

Krishna says: The one who hates no one, who is a friend to every little creature, who is kind, who does not say 'mine-mine,' who is happy in pain and joy, who forgives, and who gives his whole heart to Me — that dear one is very, very dear to Me. That is the heart of prema.

Hanuman's Open Heart

Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda

After the great war, Lord Rama and Mother Sita came home to Ayodhya. The whole city was sparkling with little lamps. Flowers fell from every balcony. Everyone was SO happy.

Lord Rama sat on His golden seat. He smiled. 'My dear ones, I want to give each friend a gift.' To Lakshmana — a ring. To Bharata — a hug. To Sugriva — a bright shawl. To every monkey and bear — sweets and shining cloths.

Then Rama's eyes fell on Hanuman. Hanuman was sitting very quietly at Rama's feet. His eyes were full of love. His tail was curled up. He was not looking at the gifts. He was looking only at Rama.

Mother Sita stepped forward. She held out a beautiful pearl necklace. The pearls glowed like little moons. 'For you, brave Hanuman,' she said kindly.

Hanuman bowed. He took the necklace with soft fingers. The whole court watched.

Then something strange happened. Hanuman held one pearl up close to his eyes. He turned it. He smelled it. He rolled it on his cheek. Then — CRACK — he bit it open with his teeth. He looked inside. His face fell.

He took the next pearl. CRACK. He looked inside. He shook his head.

The courtiers started to giggle. 'What is this monkey doing?'

One by one, Hanuman cracked open every pearl. He looked inside every one.

Now the laughter grew loud. A wise old minister spoke up. 'Hanuman! Why are you breaking such a precious gift?'

Hanuman looked up. His eyes were shining with tears. 'Sir, I was searching. I was looking inside each pearl for my Rama and my Mother Sita. But they are not there. If Rama is not inside, the pearl is just a stone.'

The court went silent.

The minister spoke again, a little teasingly. 'Hanuman, by that logic, even YOUR body is just a thing. Is Rama inside YOU?'

Hanuman smiled. A soft, sweet smile. He placed both his paws on his chest. Then — with his own sharp nails — he gently tore open the skin of his chest.

And oh! Oh, what the whole court saw!

Inside Hanuman's heart, seated together, shining like two golden suns, were Lord Rama and Mother Sita — smiling, hand in hand, their feet resting on the soft heart of their dearest servant.

Every courtier fell to the floor. The queens wept. Lord Rama himself rose from His throne and came down. He embraced Hanuman so tightly that the chest-wound closed in an instant.

'My Hanuman,' Rama whispered. 'Your love is the biggest gift of all. I cannot ever leave your heart — you have made it My only home.'

And that, children, is prema. When you love someone SO much that they live inside your heart forever — and nothing else, no gift, no pearl, no prize, can take their place — that is prema.

Lord Brahma speaks to Krishna: 'Dear Krishna, those gentle souls who give up the struggle of dry knowledge and simply sit humbly — listening to Your sweet stories, singing Your sweet name, offering their body, words, and heart to You wherever they are — such loving ones conquer You, the Unconquerable One. That is the power of prema.'

Anya and the Old Neem Tree

Anya lived in a small house in Pune. Right outside her bedroom window stood a big, big neem tree. It was older than Anya's grandmother. Its leaves were tiny and green. Its branches reached up to the sky like a thousand soft hands.

Every morning, Anya said hello to the neem tree. 'Good morning, Neem-didi!' Every evening, she said goodnight. On hot days, she sat under its shade and read her book. When she was sad, she pressed her cheek against its rough bark. And when the wind blew, the tree rustled back like it was whispering — 'I love you too, little one.'

One afternoon, big loud trucks came. Men with orange helmets stepped out. They were going to build a new wall for the colony. The wall had to go exactly where the neem tree stood.

'This tree must come down tomorrow,' one man said loudly.

Anya's heart went COLD. Then it went HOT. She ran to Amma. 'Amma, they can't! They can't cut her!'

Amma held Anya tight. 'Beta, sometimes grown-ups make these decisions.'

'But Amma — I LOVE her.'

That night, Anya could not sleep. Her chest hurt. But inside her hurt, something else was stirring — something soft and very, very warm. It was not angry. It was not loud. It was just full.

In the morning, Anya took her water bottle, her little stool, and her favourite picture book. She sat down at the foot of the neem tree. She put both her hands on its rough brown bark. And she began.

She talked softly. 'Neem-didi, today they want to cut you. But I am here. I am not going.'

She sang the lullaby Amma used to sing her. She read her picture book aloud to the tree. She hummed. She stroked the bark. She put her cheek against it.

People from the colony came to watch. The men with orange helmets came. They stopped. They stared.

Anya did not look up. She just kept singing.

By afternoon, Amma brought her lunch. Papa came home from work early. 'Anya, beta…' he said gently. 'Shall we go home?' Anya shook her head. 'Papa, I am not leaving Neem-didi alone.'

By evening, something had changed in the colony. Uncle-ji from next door brought a glass of water. An auntie brought a flower garland and placed it on the trunk. Even the man with the orange helmet walked over and stood quietly. He watched the little girl with her hand still on the bark.

'Baby,' he said softly. 'Do you love this tree so much?'

Anya looked up. Her face was calm. 'She is my big sister. She has loved me since I was small.'

The man's eyes went wet. He rubbed them quickly. Then he went to the colony meeting. He told them what he had seen. He said, 'Let the wall bend around the tree. The child's love has asked, and I cannot say no.'

The next morning, the trucks came back. But they did not cut the neem tree. The new wall had a beautiful curve in it — a round bump, right where the neem tree stood, so the branches could still reach for the sky.

Anya stood beside her Amma. Her hand rested on the warm bark. She did not smile loudly. She just whispered, 'Thank you, Neem-didi.' And the wind moved through the leaves, whispering back.

That soft, full, warm, whole-hearted love — that is prema.

Core teaching

  • Prema is love that GIVES and gives and gives — and does not ask for anything back. When I love Amma without wanting a chocolate in return, that is a tiny seed of prema. When I love my pet, my tree, my friend — with no 'if' and no 'because' — my little heart is practicing the biggest love in the world.
  • Prema flows from Krishna. Krishna loves every leaf, every ant, every river, every child. When we let a little bit of that love come through us — like a window lets in sunlight — we feel SO full. Krishna says in the Gita: 'The one who is a friend to every creature, who hates no one, is very dear to Me.' When I am kind to a little ant, Krishna smiles.
  • Prema is quiet. It does not say 'LOOK AT ME! I am loving!' It just sits, strokes the bark of a tree, hums to a sleeping baby, gives the last biscuit to the hungry dog. The loudest love is often not the realest love. The real one is soft — but oh, it is STRONG. Like Hanuman's heart. Like Anya's hand on the neem.

Try this at home

Pick ONE living thing that needs your quiet love this week — a house plant, a pet, a neighbourhood tree, a stray kitten, a younger sibling, or your grandma. Every single day, for one week, spend 5 gentle minutes with this one being. Water the plant slowly. Stroke the pet softly. Press your palm on the tree's bark. Read a story to the baby. Massage Ajji's feet. While you do it, whisper quietly in your heart: 'I love you. Krishna loves you. I am sending you warmth.' Do NOT tell anyone you are doing this practice. Prema grows best in the quiet dark — like a seed in soil. Keep a small Prema Heart Diary: each night, draw a tiny pink heart, and inside write/draw the one you loved today. At the end of the week, you will have seven pink hearts — a little garland of prema.

Duration: 5 minutes daily for one week
Watch for: By the end of the week, the child can name the creature they loved, describe how their own heart felt during those minutes (warm, full, soft, peaceful), and show 7 pink hearts in the Prema Heart Diary. Parents/teachers may notice the child being gentler with small creatures, speaking more softly to siblings, or showing unprompted small kindnesses (tucking in a sibling, watering a plant without being asked, pausing to pet a stray).

Activities

  • Who Lives in Your Heart · 12 min

    Draw a big heart. Inside, draw all the people, creatures, and Krishna that live inside your heart with prema. Colour the heart soft pink with sun-rays around it.

  • The Whisper Game · 8 min

    Sit with a partner. Take turns whispering ONE warm thing you love about them. Notice whether the chest feels warmer when love is whispered or shouted.

  • The Gopi Garland Craft · 15 min

    Make a small garland of 10 paper flowers. On each flower, draw or name one person or creature you love. Thread them together and hang near your altar or study.

  • The Ant and the Sugar Grain · 10 min

    Go outside with a grown-up. Offer one grain of sugar to an ant. Sit quietly and watch. Notice what your heart feels when you give without expecting thanks.

  • The Softest Song · 5 min

    Sit cross-legged. Hands on chest. Sing softly three times: 'Ra-dhe Ra-dhe, Krish-na Krish-na, my little heart is Yours.' Feel the warmth under your palms.
