Ishvara Pranidhana

ईश्वरप्रणिधान · EESH-va-ra pra-ni-DHAA-na

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Ishvara-pranidhana means trusting Krishna with all our heart. We do our best work, then we say, 'Krishna, the rest is for You.' We offer our food, our games, our homework — everything — to Krishna first. When we trust God, our heart feels safe and light.

What it is not

Ishvara-pranidhana is not being lazy and saying 'God will do it.' It is not making deals with Krishna: 'If You give me this, I will pray.' It is not pretending to pray while holding tight to our own plans inside.

Word study

Word
ईश्वरप्रणिधान (īśvara-praṇidhāna)
Say it
EESH-va-ra pra-ni-DHAA-na
Meaning
Offering our heart, our work, and our worry to Krishna. Doing our best and trusting God with the rest.
Example
When I offered my drawing to Krishna before starting, I was practicing ishvara-pranidhana.

Gajendra the Elephant Calls Krishna

Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 8, Chapter 2-3

Long ago there was a mighty elephant named Gajendra. He was the king of all elephants. His grey body was big like a mountain. His tusks were shining white. His trunk was strong as ten trees.

One hot afternoon, Gajendra went with his family to a beautiful lake. The water was cool and silver. Lotuses were blooming pink and white.

Gajendra stepped into the water. He sprayed his baby elephants and trumpeted happily. 'Whoosh! Whoosh!' The water flew high.

Suddenly — SNAP! A huge crocodile caught Gajendra's leg. The crocodile was old and very strong. He pulled and pulled.

Gajendra pulled back. For many days and nights they fought. Gajendra became thin and tired. Still the crocodile did not let go.

Gajendra thought, 'Oh! I am the king of elephants. But my strength is not enough. My family cannot help me. My friends cannot help me.'

Then he remembered something beautiful. 'Lord Vishnu! Only You can save me. I surrender to You. I am Yours.'

Gajendra broke a big pink lotus with his trunk. He lifted it high to the sky. In a soft voice he called, 'Vishnu! Vishnu!'

The moment Gajendra called out with a surrendered heart, Lord Vishnu heard. He flew down right away on Garuda the great bird. In one second, the Lord's chakra (spinning disc) cut the crocodile. Gajendra was free.

Vishnu lifted Gajendra gently. The elephant cried happy tears. 'Krishna, I am Yours forever.'

This is ishvara-pranidhana. When we truly surrender, Krishna never fails to come.

Pooja and the Lost Key

Pooja came home from school. She was tired and hungry. She reached into her pocket for the key Amma had given her.

But the key was GONE.

Pooja checked her other pocket. Empty. She opened her school bag. No key. She shook her water bottle upside down. Still no key.

Pooja sat down on the step. Her heart was beating fast. 'Amma will be upset. Appa will be upset. I have lost the key!'

She ran back along the path. She looked under every bush. She asked the security uncle. She asked the sweeping-auntie. Nobody had seen a key.

The sun was going down. Pooja sat on the step again. Tears came to her eyes.

Then Pooja remembered what Amma had taught her: 'When we are worried, we give our worry to Krishna.'

Pooja closed her eyes. She placed her hands at her heart. She whispered, 'Krishna, I do not know what to do. I tried my best. The key is Yours. Please help me.'

She sat still for a moment. Slowly, her heart stopped racing. A small peace came inside.

And then — like a soft voice in her mind — Pooja remembered. 'The gulmohar tree! I bent down to pick up a red flower!'

Pooja ran to the tree. She looked under the flowers. There — gleaming in the golden light — was her silver key!

Pooja picked it up and held it tight. 'Thank You, Krishna,' she whispered.

That evening, Pooja told Amma the whole story. Amma smiled. 'Panic never helps us think. Surrender always helps us remember.'

Core teaching

  • Ishvara-pranidhana means: first we try our very best. Then we say, 'Krishna, the result is Yours.' We work hard AND we trust God. Both together.
  • When we are worried or scared, we can give the worry to Krishna. Our little heart is small. Krishna's heart is big enough for all our troubles.
  • Every day, we offer our food, our games, our homework, and our love to Krishna. This turns every moment into worship. This is called the life of a devotee.

Try this at home

Each night before bed, sit quietly with Amma or Appa for two minutes. Close your eyes. Place your hands at your heart. Think of ONE thing from the day — a game, a struggle, a kindness, a worry. Whisper: 'Krishna, this is for You.' Put a small drawing or note into your Offering Box. Also: before eating, pause and offer a morsel to Krishna with a small mantra or a soft 'Thank You, Krishna.'

Duration: Every day for two weeks
Watch for: By the end of two weeks, the child naturally pauses before meals and bedtime, and can describe at least one moment where giving the worry to Krishna helped them feel calmer.

Activities

  • Whose Hands Hold What · 8 min

    Match each thing to MY HANDS (I can do this) or KRISHNA'S HANDS (I trust Him).

  • Offering Plate Drawing · 10 min

    Draw a plate with one thing you ate, one thing you played, one kind act, one hard try. Write 'For You, Krishna' at the top.

  • Say It With Me · 3 min

    Chant 'Krishna, I do my best, the rest is Yours' three times with palms at heart.

  • The Worry Basket · 10 min

    With a partner, take turns placing a worry into a pretend basket and saying 'Krishna, please take this.'

  • The Offering Box · 5 min

    At home, decorate a box. Every night put in one drawing or note. Whisper 'Krishna, this is for You.'
