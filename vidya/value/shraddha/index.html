Jnana

Shraddha

श्रद्धा · SHRAH-dhaa

EN हिन्दी

What it is

Shraddha means faith — trusting Krishna, trusting our teachers, trusting the sacred stories with our whole heart. It is doing the practice before we can see the result. We plant the seed today, even though the mango comes much later. A shraddha-heart says, 'I trust. I try. I wait with love.'

What it is not

Shraddha is not demanding, 'Show me first, then I will believe.' It is not obeying blindly without listening with our heart. It is not giving up the second we do not see results. It is not trusting every shiny idea — real shraddha is in what Krishna and the great souls have taught us.

Word study

Word
श्रद्धा (śraddhā)
Say it
SHRAH-dhaa
Meaning
Faith. A heart that trusts Krishna, the teacher, and the sacred stories — and keeps practicing with love even before the result appears.
Example
When Ananya kept watering her tulasi seeds for fourteen days before the leaf came, that was shraddha.

Anchor verse

Bhagavad-gita 4.39

श्रद्धावाँल्लभते ज्ञानं तत्परः संयतेन्द्रियः।
ज्ञानं लब्ध्वा परां शान्तिमचिरेणाधिगच्छति॥

śraddhāvāṁl labhate jñānaṁ tat-paraḥ saṁyatendriyaḥ jñānaṁ labdhvā parāṁ śāntim acireṇādhigacchati

The child who has faith, who loves what the teacher teaches, and who keeps the senses calm — that child quickly receives true knowledge. And with true knowledge, the highest peace comes into the heart.

Nachiketa and the Three Nights

Katha Upanishad, Chapter 1 (adapted for primary students)

Long ago in Bharat, there lived a brave small boy named Nachiketa.

One day his father was giving gifts to the brahmins. But the cows were old and thin. Nachiketa's heart felt shaky. He asked softly, 'Father, who will you give ME to?'

His father was tired. He did not mean it. But he said, 'I give you to Yama — the lord of death!'

Nachiketa became quiet. Grown-ups must keep their word. With a trusting heart, he walked alone to Yama's big stone house.

Yama was not at home. Nachiketa sat by the door. He folded his hands. He did not cry. He did not run.

One day passed. Two days. Three days and three nights — no food, no water. Just a small boy whispering, 'Krishna, I trust You.'

On the third night Yama came home. His eyes grew wide. 'A brahmin-boy at my door for three days!' He bowed to Nachiketa. 'Dear child, because you waited with such faith, I will give you three gifts. Ask!'

First Nachiketa asked that his father not be angry anymore. Second, he asked for the sacred fire that lights the way home.

Then he asked the deepest question. 'What happens to the soul when the body stops? Is there an atma that never dies?'

Yama tried to change his mind. He offered elephants, gold, a thousand years of play. Nachiketa shook his head. 'No, great one. Only the truth. My faith brought me here.'

Yama's stone heart melted. He taught the beautiful secret. 'The atma is never born. The atma never dies. It is Krishna's own light inside you.'

Nachiketa came with only faith. He went home with wisdom.

I am known by devotion with faith. To those who love Me with shraddha, I am their own dear Self. Faith-filled devotion purifies even the humblest heart.

Ananya and the Tulasi Seeds

Ananya was staying with her grandmother in the village. Every morning Ammamma watered the tulasi plant and sang softly to Krishna.

One day Ammamma opened her palm. On it were tiny black seeds, smaller than mustard.

'These are for you, kanna. Plant them in this clay pot. Every morning, water them. While you water, chant Krishna three times. Do this every day — even when nothing seems to happen.'

Ananya pressed the seeds into the soft soil. 'Bye seeds. Grow fast!'

The next morning — nothing. Ananya watered. 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna.'

Day two — nothing. Day three, four, five — still just brown soil. Ananya began to frown.

'Ammamma, these seeds are bad! Nothing is coming!'

Ammamma only smiled. 'Keep watering, little one. The seed is doing its work in the dark, where we cannot see.'

Day seven. Nothing. Ananya almost did not water. But she remembered Ammamma's gentle face. She lifted the lota. 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna.' Splash.

Day ten. Day twelve. Ananya was tired of trusting.

Then — on the fourteenth morning — Ananya ran to the pot and stopped short. Two tiny green leaves had pushed up through the soil! They were bright like baby fingers waving hello.

'Ammamma! Come, come, COME!'

Ammamma knelt beside her, eyes crinkling with joy.

'You see, kanna. Shraddha means we water even when we see nothing. Krishna is always working under the soil of our heart — just like under this soil.'

Ananya held her grandmother's hand tight. 'I will water every day,' she whispered.

Core teaching

  • Shraddha means trusting Krishna, our teachers, and the sacred stories with our whole heart. We chant, we pray, we help — even before we can see the result. Faith comes first; knowing comes after.
  • Like a seed in the soil, our practices work in the dark where we cannot see. We water every day. We do not dig up the seed to check. Krishna takes care of the growing.
  • A shraddha-heart is brave like Nachiketa and patient like Ananya with her tulasi pot. The Gita says — the one who has faith quickly finds peace.

Try this at home

The Faith Pot: plant a few methi, moong, or tulasi seeds with Amma or Appa. Every morning for 10 days in a row, water them. While watering, chant 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna' three times. Do NOT dig up the seeds to check. At bedtime, tell Amma or Appa what you did today. On the day the first green leaf comes up, clap your hands and say, 'Krishna, I trusted — and You kept Your promise.' Also: if a teacher or grown-up you love asks you to try a practice you do not fully understand, try it for three full days before deciding anything.

Duration: Every day for two weeks
Watch for: By the end of two weeks, the child can show a sprouted Faith Pot (or describe the waiting if no sprout yet), and can say one specific moment when they chose to 'keep watering' instead of giving up.

Activities

  • Seed or No Seed · 6 min

    Read each little story and circle the seed if the child is showing shraddha, or the question mark if not.

  • Draw Your Faith-Seed · 8 min

    Draw a pot with a seed inside. Write on top ONE practice you will do every day for ten days.

  • Say It With Me · 3 min

    Chant shraddha three times with palms pressed at the heart.

  • The Trust Walk · 8 min

    With a partner, take turns leading each other three steps with eyes closed. Feel the trust on both sides.

  • The Faith Pot · 5 min

    Plant a few seeds. Water and chant Krishna three times every morning for 10 days. Do not dig up to peek. Draw what grows.
